# 46. Homosexuality

> Status line: BSS (brahmacari training) · 120 min · drafted · **SENSITIVE** (sexuality and celibacy). The head reviews this page after the build. It gives Srila Prabhupada's purports and BIKC in their own words, as they are (head, 30 Oct 2026), without political comment. Builds on bss-21 to bss-24 (the nature of lust and its remedy), which are not repeated here. Items I could not source are marked "Requires source review".

## Note to the preacher before you begin
1. **Read the sources as they are.** Prabhupada's purports (SB 3.20.23-26) and BIKC (ch. 46) are given below in their own words, strong words included. Do not soften them and do not add explanations of your own. The same purports and BIKC also say how a devotee who struggles is to be treated (BG 9.30 purport; BIKC: "sympathetic and understanding with such sincere souls").
2. **A group is not the place for anyone to talk about himself.** Never ask anyone to say, by a show of hands or in a question, whether he has such feelings. If someone tells you privately, thank him calmly and see C.4 and section H.3 below.
3. **You are not a counsellor.** Spiritual guidance and professional help can go together. If someone is distressed, anxious or thinking of harming himself, he should be helped to speak with a trusted senior devotee and with a qualified counsellor. In India, Tele-MANAS (the government mental-health helpline) is **14416**. Elsewhere, use the local crisis service. (This is pastoral guidance from me, not a statement from a scripture.)
4. **This page does not discuss laws, politics, civil rights or public debate.** Those are outside the topic and outside the speaker's role.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level
The BIKC (Bhakti Vikasa Swami, *Brahmacarya in Krishna consciousness*) has a short chapter on this topic. IYF's BSS audience is "maintaining strictly the initiation vows" and preparing to live as celibate brahmacaris (research/batch-criteria.md), so some of them, in a community of men, will have these feelings or meet someone who does. They need to know what the teaching is, and they need to know how to treat each other.

## C. Central message
Lust in every form is the common enemy of all living beings, and the cure for it is the same for everyone: hearing, chanting and service to Krishna under the guidance of a senior devotee (BG 3.37; BG 12.9). Srila Prabhupada's teaching sets one standard, no sex outside religious marriage, for everybody. He also teaches that each living being is a soul, and that a sincere devotee who struggles is to be treated as saintly and not mocked (BG 9.30).

## D. Learning outcomes
By the end, participants can:
1. State, in the purport's own words, what Prabhupada's purport to SB 3.20.26 says, and what the purports to SB 3.20.23-24 say about sex desire and demoniac life.
2. Explain from BG 7.11 and NOI 3 that the standard of celibacy outside religious marriage applies to every person equally.
3. Explain from BG 5.18 and BG 9.30 why every devotee must be treated with respect.
4. Describe the remedy from BG 12.9 and CC Madhya 22.107, and why it takes time (SB 1.2.17).
5. Say where a person can find help: a trusted senior devotee, a qualified counsellor, Tele-MANAS 14416 or the local crisis service.

- **Purport (3.20.23, whole):** "Sex life is the background of material existence. Here also it is repeated that demons are very fond of sex life. The more one is free from the desire for sex, the more he is promoted to the level of the demigods; the more one is inclined to enjoy sex, the more he is degraded to the level of demoniac life."
- **Purport (3.20.24, whole):** "Sexually inclined demons have no respect even for their father, and the best policy for a saintly father like Brahmā is to leave such demoniac sons."

**SB 3.20.26**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/20/26/
> "Lord Brahmā, approaching the Lord, addressed Him thus: My Lord, please protect me from these sinful demons, who were created by me under Your order. They are infuriated by an appetite for sex and have come to attack me."
- **Context (SB 3.20.23-28):** Brahma has created the demons from his own body (3.20.23). He runs away in fear (3.20.24). He prays to the Lord for protection (3.20.26-27), and the Lord tells him to "cast off this impure body of yours" (3.20.28).

Prabhupada says this appetite "is demoniac and is not for any sane male in the ordinary course of life", and the purport to 3.20.23 gives the reason: "the more one is inclined to enjoy sex, the more he is degraded to the level of demoniac life."

## F. Philosophical explanation
**F.1 One enemy, one standard.** BG 3.37 names lust, in general, as the enemy of every living being. In Prabhupada's teaching, the only sexual union that is consistent with dharma is within religious marriage and for the sake of children who will be Krishna conscious (BG 7.11 purport). Everything outside it, whether the desire is for a person of the opposite sex or of the same sex, is "illicit sex", which the four regulative principles forbid to every devotee (NOI 3 purport). So the standard of the brahmacari (no sex life at all) and the standard of the householder (sex only within religious marriage) are both standards for everyone. BIKC (ch. 46): "heterosexual desires can be accommodated within the gṛhastha-āśrama, but there is no scope for accommodating homosexual desires."

**F.2 What SB 3.20.23-26 adds.** Prabhupada writes that "the homosexual appetite of a man for another man is demoniac and is not for any sane male in the ordinary course of life" (3.20.26 purport), that it "is created in this episode of the creation of the demons by Brahmā", and why the word is "demoniac": "the more one is free from the desire for sex, the more he is promoted to the level of the demigods; the more one is inclined to enjoy sex, the more he is degraded to the level of demoniac life" (3.20.23 purport). BIKC (ch. 46) says: "Homosexuality means that the lust has increased to an abnormally high degree." The story ends with Brahma taking shelter of the Lord and the Lord protecting him (SB 3.20.27-28).

**F.3 The soul is not the body.** BG 5.18 says the learned see all with equal vision, because, as the purport says, the Lord is present as Paramatma in everyone's heart and the differences of body "are meaningless from the viewpoint of a learned transcendentalist". BG 9.30 purport: a devotee engaged in service "should be considered to be in the transcendental position, even if by chance or accident he is found to have fallen."

**F.4 The remedy.** There is one remedy for lust of every kind: BG 12.9 says to follow the regulative principles of bhakti-yoga, and CC Madhya 22.107 says the heart is purified by hearing and chanting. SB 1.2.17's purport adds two things for the one who is struggling. The first is that restlessness is "not an astonishment" and it takes time to recover from this foreign nature. The second is that, if he keeps hearing the Lord's glories, "gradually all disturbing elements are eliminated from his mind." The purport does not promise how or when feelings will change. I do not promise it either (Application (preacher's)).

**F.5 Respect, patience and help.** BG 9.30's purport forbids deriding a devotee for an accidental falldown, and also forbids a devotee from taking advantage of it. Together with BG 4.34 (approach a spiritual master submissively and serve him), this gives the pastoral shape: the person is welcome; the standard is clear; the person has a senior to talk to; and nobody mocks. Chaitanya Charan Das (a supporting source; his words come from an AI transcript, "What is the Vedic perspective of homosexuality?", 2013) says in his own words that everyone deserves respect, that a person should first of all be seen as a soul and as a devotee, that discrimination against a devotee because of a bodily feature is a materialistic mentality that can even be offensive, and that a person with such feelings should speak to a mature, practical senior devotee. He also says that the person, like every devotee, needs self-control and should not act out his desires with others. I use only those points.

**F.6 What BIKC adds (ch. 46, its own words).** "Due to the influence of Kali-yuga, homosexuality is now a common problem. As Kali-yuga advances we will have to accommodate more and more people with past perverse lives and give them the opportunity for purification." "Anyway, homosexuals coming to Kṛṣṇa consciousness will need special guidance from senior devotees. The homosexual must be understood as an individual person and be given proper facility after frank discussion. He should understand his condition to be especially fallen, but should be confident that by Kṛṣṇa consciousness, all difficulties can be overcome. And other devotees should be sympathetic and understanding with such sincere souls." On the ashram: "sheltering homosexuals in the āśrama is a risk. As with a heterosexual, we shall first have to see if a homosexual is sufficiently self-controlled before he may be allowed to stay in the āśrama"; "the homosexual is surrounded by them. We must be compassionate, but we cannot sacrifice our standards of purity." These are BIKC's statements, cited as BIKC. IYF's own policy is not in the documents I have (Requires source review).

**F.7 Misunderstandings.**
- "The purport's word 'demoniac' means a devotee with such desires may be derided." BG 9.30 purport: "No one should deride a devotee for some accidental falldown from the ideal path." BIKC (ch. 46): "other devotees should be sympathetic and understanding with such sincere souls."
- "Only some people have to struggle with lust." Every living being is associated with lust from time immemorial (SB 1.2.17 purport).
- "Chanting will change my feelings by a certain date." The sources promise the purification of the heart and the growth of love. They give no date and no promise about a particular feeling.
- "It is not our business to help." BIKC and BG 9.30 say that it is, with sympathy.

## H. Opening and engagement
**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes).**
1. Offer a short prayer or a verse of kirtana. State the title once and say: "We will read what Srila Prabhupada's purports and BIKC say on this subject, in their own words. Nobody will be asked to say anything about himself."
2. Give the ground rules: "Everything said here stays here. If anyone wishes to talk privately, I and the senior devotees are available."
3. Read BG 5.18 slowly, in English only.
4. Say: "The purport to BG 9.30 says that no one should deride a devotee for an accidental falldown, and BIKC says other devotees should be 'sympathetic and understanding with such sincere souls'. That is how we speak today."

**H.2 Opening questions (general, with no personal disclosure).**
1. "What does it mean that a person is a soul, not a body? How should that change the way we treat each other in the ashram?"
2. "Lust is called the greatest enemy in BG 3.37. Why do you think Krishna calls it an enemy of everyone and not only of some people?"
3. "What does a young devotee need from his seniors when he is struggling with any desire?" (Take answers without commenting. Praise answers about kindness and about patience.)

**H.3 What to do if someone speaks to you privately.** (These steps are my pastoral suggestion, not from a scripture.)
1. Listen without showing shock, disgust or advice. Say: "Thank you for trusting me. You are a devotee and you are welcome."
2. Do not promise to keep secrets that concern his safety. If he is in danger or thinks of harming himself, say that you will help him find immediate help.
3. Tell him what the sources say, as they say it. BIKC (ch. 46): "He should understand his condition to be especially fallen, but should be confident that by Kṛṣṇa consciousness, all difficulties can be overcome." The standard of conduct is the same for every devotee (NOI 3), and a devotee engaged in service is not to be derided (BG 9.30 purport).
4. Encourage him to speak regularly with a trusted senior devotee. Encourage him to see a qualified counsellor if he feels distressed, anxious or low. Spiritual practice and professional help are compatible.
5. If he is in India, Tele-MANAS is 14416. Elsewhere, tell him how to reach the local crisis service.
6. Do not repeat what he said to others without his agreement, and do not tell his family or his authorities unless safety requires it.
7. Tell your own senior devotee that you needed guidance, without naming the person, so that you are supported too.

## I. Core teaching points
**I.1 One enemy and one standard (BG 3.37; BG 7.11; NOI 3).** Read BG 3.37 and the line of the purport that lust is the greatest enemy. Then read BG 7.11 and its purport: sex life is acceptable to Krishna only within religious principles and for children. Show from the NOI 3 purport that the four regulative principles are for every devotee. Explain that the brahmacari takes the highest form of this standard, and the householder follows the standard within marriage. Example (preacher's, labelled): the speed limit on a road is the same for every driver, whatever each driver would like to do. Point: the standard does not ask who feels what; it asks for conduct and offers the same help to everyone. Next question for the group: what does Prabhupada say about the specific appetite BIKC discusses?

**I.2 What Prabhupada says (SB 3.20.23-26) and what BIKC says.** Tell the Brahma story (J.1). Read the translations and the whole purports of SB 3.20.23, 3.20.24 and 3.20.26 aloud, as they stand: "the homosexual appetite of a man for another man is demoniac and is not for any sane male in the ordinary course of life", and the reason, "the more one is inclined to enjoy sex, the more he is degraded to the level of demoniac life". Then read BIKC's sentences (E and F.6): "Neither heterosexuality nor homosexuality are 'natural'"; "homosexual or heterosexual, the disease is lust. Homosexuality means that the lust has increased to an abnormally high degree"; "there is no scope for accommodating homosexual desires" in the grhastha-ashrama. Say that BIKC says Srila Prabhupada recommended marriage to a woman for such a disciple, and that this is BIKC's report without a reference. Add nothing of your own. Next question: how do we treat a devotee who struggles?

**I.3 Every devotee is a soul (BG 5.18; BG 9.30).** BG 5.18: the learned see with equal vision, because the Lord is in every heart. BG 9.30's purport: no devotee is to be mocked for a slip, and no devotee should use it as an excuse. Apply to behaviour: no jokes, no nicknames, no remarks about anyone's manner or friendships (BG 9.30 purport: "No one should deride a devotee"). BIKC (ch. 46): "other devotees should be sympathetic and understanding with such sincere souls."

**I.4 The remedy and patience (BG 12.9; CC Madhya 22.107; SB 1.2.17).** The practice is the same as for any desire: hearing, chanting, service, association, a senior to guide. BG 12.9: if you cannot fix the mind, follow the regulative principles. CC Madhya 22.107: the heart is purified by hearing and chanting. SB 1.2.17's purport: restlessness is not astonishing and takes time to recover from. Application (preacher's): the first step for anyone who is struggling is to speak with a senior he trusts and to keep up his chanting and hearing; there is no need to hide.

**I.5 Where to find help.** Name them plainly: a trusted senior devotee or mentor (BG 4.34: approach a spiritual master submissively and serve him); a qualified counsellor, if the person feels distress, anxiety, depression, or has difficulty living; Tele-MANAS 14416 in India; the local crisis service elsewhere. Say that asking for professional help is compatible with spiritual life. Say that nobody is expected to carry this alone.

## J. Stories and illustrations
**J.1 Brahma, the demons and the Lord's shelter (SB 3.20.23-28).** Retell in order, using only the verses. Brahma "gave birth to the demons from his buttocks, and they were very fond of sex. Because they were too lustful, they approached him for copulation" (3.20.23). He "first laughed at their stupidity, but finding the shameless asuras close upon him, he grew indignant and ran in great haste out of fear" (3.20.24). He went to the Lord, who removes the distress of those who take shelter (3.20.25), and prayed: "please protect me from these sinful demons" (3.20.26) and "You are the only one capable of ending the affliction of the distressed" (3.20.27). The Lord, who sees the minds of all, told him to "cast off this impure body of yours" (3.20.28). Following the purport, this means that Brahma gave up a particular mentality. **Devotional lesson:** when overwhelmed by lust, even Brahma does not rely on himself; he takes shelter of the Lord, and the Lord protects him and shows him the way out. The lesson for us is not to be ashamed to turn to the Lord and to a senior devotee for help.

**J.2 Invented illustration (labelled).** A traveller is lost on a mountain path in fog. He hears the voice of a guide and shouts: "I am lost!" The guide does not scold him for being lost. He says: "Stay where you are; I will come; follow the rope." The Krishna conscious point is that the fog is the material energy; the guide is the senior devotee and the rope is the holy name. This is why BG 4.34 tells us to approach a spiritual master submissively.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections
1. *Is this person a bad devotee?* No. BG 9.30 says that a devotee engaged in service is to be considered saintly, and that no one should deride him for an accidental falldown. The conduct standard (NOI 3) still applies to everyone.
2. *Why does Prabhupada use the word "demoniac"?* Read the purports. SB 3.20.26 purport: the appetite "is created in this episode of the creation of the demons by Brahmā. In other words, the homosexual appetite of a man for another man is demoniac and is not for any sane male in the ordinary course of life." SB 3.20.23 purport gives the reason: "the more one is inclined to enjoy sex, the more he is degraded to the level of demoniac life."
3. *Can a person with such feelings join the ashram?* BIKC (ch. 46): "we shall first have to see if a homosexual is sufficiently self-controlled before he may be allowed to stay in the āśrama"; "We must be compassionate, but we cannot sacrifice our standards of purity." IYF's own policy is not in the documents I have (Requires source review); ask the head or the ashram leaders.
4. *Can chanting change the feeling?* The sources teach that hearing and chanting purify the heart (CC Madhya 22.107). BIKC (ch. 46): he "should be confident that by Kṛṣṇa consciousness, all difficulties can be overcome." SB 1.2.17 purport: "it takes time to recover from this foreign nature." The sources give no date; do not give one.
5. *Is this a sin?* Illicit sex is one of the four sinful activities (SB 5.5.4 purport, quoted in bss-21; NOI 3 purport), and the standard is the same for every devotee. BIKC (ch. 46): "He should understand his condition to be especially fallen, but should be confident that by Kṛṣṇa consciousness, all difficulties can be overcome." If a person is troubled by low mood, he should speak with a senior devotee and a qualified counsellor (H.3).
6. *What if a devotee says he cannot control himself?* He should speak with his senior devotee soon. If he feels distressed or unsafe, he should also contact a counsellor or Tele-MANAS (14416 in India) or the local crisis service.
7. *What about a friend who is being teased?* Stop the teasing at once (BG 9.30 purport). Tell a senior. Treat him as you want to be treated.
8. *What about the laws or debates in my country?* This page does not cover them. Answer: "That is outside the topic of this class; I teach what Srila Prabhupada teaches about conduct and about respect for each soul."

## L. Case studies
**L.1 Invented case for discussion.** In an ashram, some brahmacaris begin to joke about a quiet devotee, Raman, because of the friends he keeps. Raman begins to avoid the programme. *Principles:* BG 9.30 (no deriding), BG 5.18 (equal vision), NOI 3 (conduct standard for all). *Questions:* What should the senior say to the jokers? What should he say to Raman? *Analysis:* the senior stops the jokes, privately asks Raman how he is, assures him of his welcome, and offers help (a regular talk with him and, if Raman wishes, a qualified counsellor). He does not ask Raman about his feelings in front of anyone. *Takeaway:* the community's first duty is to stop the mockery, and the second is to welcome Raman back into the programme.

**L.2 Invented case for discussion (extension).** A devotee, Sunil, tells his senior privately that he is attracted to another devotee in the ashram, who is a man. He is worried and ashamed. *Principles:* NOI 3 (conduct), BG 9.30 (no deriding), SB 1.2.17 (it takes time), BG 4.34 (approach a senior). *Questions:* What should the senior say? What should he not say? *Analysis:* the senior thanks him for trusting him, does not express shock, and says he is welcome; he reads him the purport to SB 3.20.26 and BIKC's sentences (F.6) as they stand, explains that the standard of conduct applies to all brahmacaris, and that Sunil should not act on his feelings and should keep to the programme, speak to the senior regularly, and see a counsellor if the distress continues. The senior considers whether Sunil should work or live apart from the person for a time and discusses it with him. *Takeaway:* welcome, clarity about conduct, practice, and help.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary
Srila Prabhupada teaches one standard of conduct for every devotee. Sex life is acceptable to Krishna only within religious principles, in marriage, and for children who will be raised in Krishna consciousness (BG 7.11). Outside that, a devotee is celibate. The four regulative principles forbid illicit sex, meat-eating, gambling and intoxication, and every devotee follows them (NOI 3). Krishna calls lust, in every form, the greatest enemy of living beings (BG 3.37).

In Srimad-Bhagavatam 3.20.23, Brahma creates the demons, who "were very fond of sex", and Srila Prabhupada's purport says: "The more one is free from the desire for sex, the more he is promoted to the level of the demigods; the more one is inclined to enjoy sex, the more he is degraded to the level of demoniac life." In his purport to SB 3.20.26 he writes: "It appears here that the homosexual appetite of males for each other is created in this episode of the creation of the demons by Brahmā. In other words, the homosexual appetite of a man for another man is demoniac and is not for any sane male in the ordinary course of life." BIKC (ch. 46) says: "homosexual or heterosexual, the disease is lust. Homosexuality means that the lust has increased to an abnormally high degree." In that story, when Brahma was troubled by the lust of those he had created, he went to the Lord and took shelter, and the Lord helped him.

Every person is a soul, and the wise see all with equal vision (BG 5.18). A devotee who is engaged in service and slips must not be mocked (BG 9.30, purport). The remedy for every desire is the same: follow the practice of bhakti, and purify the heart by hearing and chanting (BG 12.9; CC Madhya 22.107). It takes time, because every living being has been with lust for a very long time (SB 1.2.17, purport). Nobody should struggle alone. A devotee can talk to a trusted senior devotee. If he feels distress, anxiety or low mood, he can also speak with a qualified counsellor. In India he can call Tele-MANAS on 14416. Elsewhere he can contact the local crisis service.

## S2. Key ideas
- Prabhupada teaches the same standard for every devotee: no sex life outside religious marriage.
- Lust of every kind is the common enemy, and the remedy for every devotee is hearing, chanting and service.
- Srila Prabhupada writes that this appetite "is demoniac and is not for any sane male in the ordinary course of life" (SB 3.20.26 purport).
- Every person is a soul (BG 5.18). A devotee who slips while serving must not be mocked (BG 9.30 purport); BIKC says other devotees "should be sympathetic and understanding with such sincere souls".
- A person who struggles should speak with a trusted senior and, if needed, a qualified counsellor.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **BG 5.18** · The wise see all with equal vision. · I treat every devotee with respect.
- **BG 9.30** · A devotee engaged in service is to be considered saintly, even if he slips. · I never mock a struggling devotee.
- **CC Madhya 22.107** · "When the heart is purified by hearing and chanting, this love naturally awakens." · The same practice helps every person.

## S4. Words to know
- **Brahmacari:** a celibate student of spiritual life.
- **Lust:** a strong desire for sense pleasure, especially sexual pleasure.
- **Regulative principles:** the four rules: no illicit sex, meat-eating, gambling or intoxication.
- **Illicit sex:** sex outside religious marriage.
- **Counsellor:** a trained person who helps others with feelings and problems.
- **Tele-MANAS:** India's free mental-health helpline, 14416.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *Does the same standard apply to everyone?* Yes. Sex life is acceptable only within religious marriage, and the four regulative principles are for every devotee (BG 7.11; NOI 3).
2. *Is a person with such feelings a bad devotee?* No. A devotee engaged in service is to be considered saintly, and no one should deride him (BG 9.30, purport).
3. *What does Prabhupada say about this appetite?* In the purport to SB 3.20.26: "the homosexual appetite of a man for another man is demoniac and is not for any sane male in the ordinary course of life." The purport to SB 3.20.23 gives the reason: "the more one is inclined to enjoy sex, the more he is degraded to the level of demoniac life." BIKC (ch. 46) says the person "should understand his condition to be especially fallen, but should be confident that by Kṛṣṇa consciousness, all difficulties can be overcome."
4. *What helps?* The same practice that helps for every desire: hearing, chanting, service and the guidance of a senior (BG 12.9; CC Madhya 22.107).
5. *Who can I talk to?* A trusted senior devotee, a qualified counsellor, Tele-MANAS (14416 in India) or the local crisis service.
