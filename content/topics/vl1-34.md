> **VL1 · 45–60 min · review: sources checked, awaiting senior review.** IYF's deck (*Yoga for modern age*) runs: life in the fast lane → what yoga

> experiment and asks for no commitment; the practice itself is VL3-04.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

"Yoga" is the one Sanskrit word every person in the room already uses, and uses to mean something the Gita does
not mean. That gap is a gift: the preacher can start from what they know (mats, breathing, apps) and move,
without any demand for faith, to what the texts say the word means and which form of it fits a short, busy,
distracted life. The Gita makes the argument itself: it describes the classical method (6.11–14), records
Arjuna's protest that the mind is too restless (6.34), answers "by practice" (6.35), and crowns the yogi who
"always abides in Me" (6.47). The Bhagavatam then names the practice for this age (12.3.51–52).

**Builds on vl1-33:** [vl1-33](vl1-33.md) already taught BG 2.48 (evenness of mind) as the answer to stress. Here it appears only as the Gita's measure of yoga; do not repeat the stress material.

## C. Central message

Yoga means to link with the Supreme, and the Gita measures it by evenness of mind, not by flexibility of body.
The classical meditation path asks for a secluded sacred place, celibacy and a mind steadier than the wind,
which almost nobody today can give it. The same texts therefore prescribe for this age a yoga that needs no
mat, no place and no qualification: hearing and chanting the names of God. It can be tried for ten minutes
tonight.

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. Say what the Gita means by yoga (6.46 purport; 2.48) and how that differs from the common use of the word.
2. List what BG 6.11–14 requires of a meditator, and say honestly which of those conditions they could meet.
3. Retell Arjuna's objection (6.34) and Krsna's answer (6.35) in their own words.
4. Explain why the texts recommend chanting for this age (SB 12.3.51–52; CC Adi 17.21).
5. Describe what the highest yogi does according to BG 6.47, and try ten minutes of mantra meditation this week.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** BG 6.46–47 and 6.11–14 carry the talk (what yoga is; what the classical path demands). 6.34–35 and
SB 12.3.51–52 make the turn to chanting. 2.48, 6.16–17, 2.59 and CC Adi 17.21 support. SB 12.3.51–52 are in
Canto 12: *BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples*.

**BG 6.46**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/46/
> "A yogī is greater than the ascetic, greater than the empiricist and greater than the fruitive worker. Therefore, O Arjuna, in all circumstances, be a yogī."
- **Context:** The end of the Gita's chapter on meditation.
- **Teaching:** Krsna tells Arjuna to be a yogi in all circumstances; a yogi ranks above ascetic, scholar and
  worker.
- **Purport point:** "When we speak of yoga we refer to linking our consciousness with the Supreme Absolute
  Truth." The purport names karma-yoga, jnana-yoga and bhakti-yoga by what predominates in the linking, and says
  "Bhakti-yoga, or Kṛṣṇa consciousness, is the ultimate perfection of all yogas".
- **Application (preacher's):** This is the definition the deck uses ("linking with the Supreme"), and it is the
  purport's. Say that the word's root meaning is "to join", and ask: join what to what?
- **Use:** in the talk (the definition).

**BG 6.47**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/47/
> "And of all yogīs, the one with great faith who always abides in Me, thinks of Me within himself and renders transcendental loving service to Me – he is the most intimately united with Me in yoga and is the highest of all. That is My opinion."
- **Teaching:** The highest yogi is defined by faith, remembrance and loving service, not by posture or trance.
- **Purport point:** "The culmination of all kinds of yoga practices lies in bhakti yoga. All other yogas are
  but means to come to the point of bhakti". The purport traces the path: karma-yoga → jnana-yoga →
  astanga-yoga → bhakti-yoga.
- **Application (preacher's):** The deck's climax, and rightly. Read the verse slowly: nothing in it is about the
  body. "thinks of Me within himself" is the door to mantra meditation.
- **Use:** in the talk (climax).

**BG 2.48**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/48/
> "Perform your duty equipoised, O Arjuna, abandoning all attachment to success or failure. Such equanimity is called yoga."
- **Teaching:** Equanimity in success and failure is called yoga.
- **Purport point:** "Yoga means to concentrate the mind upon the Supreme by controlling the ever-disturbing
  senses."
- **Application (preacher's):** The Gita's first definition of the word, two chapters before the meditation
  chapter, and it is about how you work on a Tuesday. Use it to show that yoga is measured in the mind.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 6.11–12**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/11-12/
> "To practice yoga, one should go to a secluded place and should lay kuśa grass on the ground and then cover it with a deerskin and a soft cloth. The seat should be neither too high nor too low and should be situated in a sacred place…"
- **Teaching:** The classical method begins with a secluded, sacred place and a prescribed seat.
- **Purport point:** "The so-called yoga societies in big cities may be successful in earning material benefit,
  but they are not at all suitable for the actual practice of yoga." The purport then quotes the
  Brhan-naradiya Purana: in Kali-yuga "the best means of spiritual realization is chanting the holy name of the
  Lord", with the *harer nama* verse (CC Adi 17.21 below).
- **Application (preacher's):** Srila Prabhupada himself makes the deck's turn inside this purport: the
  conditions, then the age, then chanting. Read the verse, ask "who has a secluded sacred place?", and let the
  purport carry you to the answer.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 6.13–14**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/13-14/
> "One should hold one's body, neck and head erect in a straight line and stare steadily at the tip of the nose. Thus, with an unagitated, subdued mind, devoid of fear, completely free from sex life, one should meditate upon Me within the heart and make Me the ultimate goal of life."
- **Teaching:** Posture, gaze, an unagitated mind, complete celibacy, and meditation on the Lord within the
  heart, as the goal of life.
- **Purport point:** "The yoga process is practiced in order to discover and see this localized form of Viṣṇu,
  and not for any other purpose." "One cannot enjoy sex life daily at home or elsewhere and attend a so-called
  yoga class and thus become a yogī." The purport adds that the bhakti school admits a regulated householder,
  "because the cult of bhakti-yoga is so powerful".
- **Application (preacher's):** Handle the celibacy line with care at VL1 (see K 3): state it as the text's
  condition for *this* method, not as a demand on the room. The point is the deck's: "how many of us are
  willing?" The honest answer makes the next verse land.
- **Use:** in the talk, with care.

**BG 6.34**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/34/
> "The mind is restless, turbulent, obstinate and very strong, O Kṛṣṇa, and to subdue it, I think, is more difficult than controlling the wind."
- **Context:** Arjuna's objection after hearing the method.
- **Purport point:** "such practice is never practical for a worldly person like Arjuna. And what can we say of
  modern man?" "The easiest way to control the mind, as suggested by Lord Caitanya, is chanting 'Hare Kṛṣṇa,'
  the great mantra for deliverance, in all humility."
- **Application (preacher's):** Arjuna says what the room is thinking, five thousand years earlier and with
  better conditions. The purport's "what can we say of modern man?" is the talk's title.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 6.35**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/35/
> "…it is undoubtedly very difficult to curb the restless mind, but it is possible by suitable practice and by detachment."
- **Purport point:** "In the present age no one can observe the strict rules and regulations of placing oneself
  in a sacred place… By the practice of Kṛṣṇa consciousness, however, one engages in nine types of devotional
  service to the Lord. The first and foremost of such devotional engagements is hearing about Kṛṣṇa."
- **Application (preacher's):** Krsna does not deny the difficulty; He names a practice. The purport says the
  practice for now is hearing. That is why a Sunday talk, and a mantra, are themselves yoga.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 6.16–17**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/16/ · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/17/
> 6.17: "He who is regulated in his habits of eating, sleeping, recreation and work can mitigate all material pains by practicing the yoga system."
- **Teaching:** Regulation of eating and sleeping is part of yoga; extremes disqualify.
- **Application (preacher's):** For the audience that came for wellness: the Gita agrees that routine matters,
  and puts it inside yoga. A bridge, not a lecture on diet.
- **Use:** in the talk, one minute.

**BG 2.59**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/59/
> "…ceasing such engagements by experiencing a higher taste, he is fixed in consciousness."
- **Purport point:** Sense restriction by astanga-yoga's steps "is recommended for less intelligent persons who
  have no better knowledge"; one "who has tasted the beauty of the Supreme Lord Kṛṣṇa… no longer has a taste
  for dead, material things."
- **Application (preacher's):** Why chanting works where restriction fails: it offers a taste, not a ban. Use
  for the "benefits" segment: the texts promise a higher taste, which the listener can test.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 12.3.51**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/12/3/51/ · *BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples*
> "My dear King, although Kali-yuga is an ocean of faults, there is still one good quality about this age: Simply by chanting the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra, one can become free from material bondage and be promoted to the transcendental kingdom."
- **Teaching:** This age has one great quality: chanting works.
- **Purport point:** "Just as one powerful king can kill innumerable thieves, one brilliant spiritual quality can
  destroy all the contamination of this age."
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 12.3.52**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/12/3/52/ · *BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples*
> "Whatever result was obtained in Satya-yuga by meditating on Viṣṇu, in Tretā-yuga by performing sacrifices, and in Dvāpara-yuga by serving the Lord's lotus feet can be obtained in Kali-yuga simply by chanting the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra."
- **Teaching:** Each age has its method; for this age it is chanting.
- **Purport point:** The purport quotes parallel verses from the Visnu, Padma and Brhan-naradiya Puranas, and
  Jiva Gosvami on why austerity, yoga meditation and sacrifice "are not properly carried out, even by the most
  expert embodied souls" in this age.
- **Application (preacher's):** The deck's four-yuga table rests on this verse. Present it as the texts' own
  account of their method, which the room is free to test.
- **Use:** in the talk (the turn).

**CC Adi 17.21**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/adi/17/21/
> "'In this Age of Kali there is no other means, no other means, no other means for self-realization than chanting the holy name, chanting the holy name, chanting the holy name of Lord Hari.'"
- **Context:** The *harer nama* verse of the Brhan-naradiya Purana, which Lord Caitanya "explained very
  elaborately" (CC Adi 17.20); the same verse Prabhupada quotes in the BG 6.11–12 purport. No purport on 17.21.
- **Application (preacher's):** The triple repetition is the text's own emphasis. At VL1, read it once and let
  it stand; do not argue exclusivity (K 7).
- **Use:** in the talk, one line.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** Yoga is linking the consciousness with the Supreme (6.46 purport), measured by equanimity
   (2.48) and perfected in loving remembrance and service (6.47). The classical meditation method is real and
   is described (6.11–14), but its conditions (seclusion, a sacred place, celibacy, a steady mind) are out of
   reach for most people now, as Arjuna already protested (6.34). Krsna's answer is practice (6.35), and the
   practice the texts name for this age is hearing and chanting the holy name (SB 12.3.51–52; CC Adi 17.21;
   the 6.11–12, 6.34 and 6.35 purports), which works by giving a higher taste (2.59).
2. **Why it matters.** The audience is sold a yoga that is either fitness or a paid app. The Gita's yoga costs
   nothing, needs no place and reaches the thing the apps promise and cannot deliver: a mind at rest in
   something lasting. Saying so honestly, with the texts' own argument, respects their intelligence.
3. **Connections.** The restless mind (VL1-16); equanimity under stress (VL1-33); happiness (VL1-01). The
   practice itself is VL3-04; the yoga ladder is argued at VL2.
4. **Consequences.** A listener who hears this well will (a) stop equating yoga with asana without despising
   asana, (b) know what the texts say the word means, (c) have a fair picture of why the classical path is hard,
   and (d) have tried ten minutes of mantra meditation as an experiment, with no one asking for more.
5. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - **"Yoga is exercise."** Exercise is good; the Gita's word means linking and evenness (6.46 purport, 2.48).
   - **"Real yoga is meditation in the Himalayas; chanting is for the simple."** The texts rank the yogi who
     remembers and serves the Lord as highest (6.47) and name chanting as this age's method (SB 12.3.52).
   - **"You're saying I must become celibate."** No. BG 6.13–14 states the condition for the classical method;
     the purport notes the bhakti path admits a regulated householder. The talk draws the opposite conclusion:
     this is why another method is given.
   - **"Chanting is a magic fix."** It is a practice (6.35); its results grow with practice and attention. Offer
     it as an experiment, report what practitioners say, promise nothing.
   - **"Other religions don't have this."** Not claimed. Many traditions value repeating God's names; this talk
     does not compare or rank them.
6. **Pitch for VL1.** Sanskrit: *yoga* (to join), *mantra* (mind-deliverer, from the deck), *japa* (quiet
   repetition). Nothing else. Tone: curious, a little playful about the apps, respectful of the asana
   practitioners in the room.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet, give your name, and say the talk is about what a word means, with no demand to believe or to chant.
2. Ask H.2 question 1; write six answers on the board.
3. Say that every answer is a real meaning of the word in everyday use.
4. Tell the room that at the end there is an optional ten-minute experiment; nobody has to join.
5. Give the transition line below and go to segment 2.

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the room's own life)
1. What do you think of when you hear the word "yoga"?
2. When did your mind last refuse to settle (before sleep, in class, at work)? Hands only.
3. What have you tried, if anything, to calm or focus the mind?

- **Opening:** "Finish this sentence: yoga is…" Collect six answers and write them up. Then: "Every one of
  these is in the dictionary. None of them is what the Gita means by the word. Let's find out what it does
  mean, and whether it fits a life with a 9 a.m. lecture and a phone that never sleeps."
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** Vikram downloaded a meditation app in January. He paid for the year. He has
  used it eleven times. He says his mind "just won't sit still". Is Vikram unusual, or is he Arjuna?
- **Transition:** "The Gita's hero made Vikram's complaint, in almost his words. Let's hear it."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** Everyone here wants a steadier mind; the market knows it and sells it.
  The Gita offers it free, with a method made for exactly this age.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Yoga means to join.** Linking the consciousness with the Supreme (6.46 purport); evenness in success and
   failure is the first thing the Gita calls yoga (2.48). *Example:* the audience's six answers, none about the
   mind. *Transition:* so how does one join?
2. **The classical method is real, and demanding.** Eight steps; a secluded sacred place, a prescribed seat
   (6.11–12); posture, gaze, an unagitated mind, complete celibacy (6.13–14). *Example:* "Who here could meet
   even the first condition this week?" *Transition:* Arjuna could not either.
3. **Arjuna objects; Krsna answers.** "more difficult than controlling the wind" (6.34); "possible by suitable
   practice" (6.35), and the purport names the practice: hearing. *Example:* Vikram's eleven sessions.
   *Transition:* which practice, for which age?
4. **Each age has its method; ours is chanting.** SB 12.3.52; the one good quality of this age (12.3.51); "no
   other means" (CC Adi 17.21). *Example:* a method that works on a crowded metro is the method for a crowded
   age. *Transition:* what does the highest yogi actually do?
5. **The highest yogi remembers and serves.** 6.47: faith, abiding in Him, thinking of Him within, loving
   service; nothing about the body. *Example:* the mantra as a way to fulfil "thinks of Me within himself".
   *Transition:* how, in one minute.
6. **The mantra, and the experiment.** Hare Krsna, Hare Krsna, Krsna Krsna, Hare Hare / Hare Rama, Hare Rama,
   Rama Rama, Hare Hare. Audibly, distinctly, attentively; any time, best early; ten minutes. Routine helps
   (6.16–17). The texts promise a higher taste (2.59); test it. *Transition:* to the takeaway.

## J. Stories and illustrations

- **Arjuna's objection** (BG 6.33–35). Tell it from the verses: the method is described, Arjuna says the mind is
  harder to hold than the wind, Krsna agrees it is hard and prescribes practice and detachment. Add nothing.
  *Connection:* the room's own objection, answered in the text.
- **Haridasa Thakura** (named in the BG 6.17 purport as one who would not eat or sleep before finishing his daily
  chanting). One line only, as the text gives it; the full story is for later levels. *Connection:* chanting as
  the yoga of a whole life, for those who want to see where it leads.
- **Contemporary (invented, labelled):** Vikram and the app (H).
- **IYF's slides on Fritjof Capra and the meditation-app business:** not used (B1). That meditation is popular
  proves neither the Gita nor the apps. If asked, say that attention training is widely valued, and that the
  Gita's yoga aims past calm at connection.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

> **Before you teach:** the room may include committed asana practitioners, people from other faiths, and
> people for whom "celibacy" is a word that closes doors. Respect all three. Never mock yoga studios or apps;
> never rank religions; state BG 6.13–14 as the text's condition for the classical method and nothing more.

1. **"I do yoga every morning. Are you saying it isn't yoga?"**
   - Asana and breathing are two of the eight steps and are good for the body. The Gita uses the word for the
     whole process of linking with the Supreme (6.46 purport) and measures it by evenness of mind (2.48). Keep
     the mat; add the meaning.
2. **"Why can't I just meditate in silence like the Gita describes?"**
   - You can try; the text gives the conditions honestly (6.11–14), and Arjuna, with every advantage, said the
     mind was too restless (6.34). Krsna's answer was practice, and the purports name hearing and chanting as
     the practice for this age. Many find that a sound to return to is what silent sitting lacked.
3. **"The verse says 'completely free from sex life'. Is that what you want from us?"**
   - No. BG 6.13–14 states what the classical meditation path requires; the purport notes the bhakti path accepts
     a regulated married life. The talk's point is the opposite of a demand: *because* those conditions are
     beyond most people, the texts give another method. Nobody here is being asked to change their life tonight.
4. **"Isn't chanting just repetition? How can repeating words do anything?"**
   - The texts say the name of God is not an ordinary sound but is the Lord present as sound (that claim is argued
     at VL2/VL3). At VL1, the honest answer is the experiment: ten minutes, attentively, for a week, and see what
     your mind does.
5. **"Is this relevant to my daily problems, or only for worship?"** (the deck's misconceptions)
   - BG 2.48 defines yoga by how you handle success and failure at work; 6.17 puts eating and sleeping inside
     it. The practice is meant for ordinary life, not instead of it.
6. **"Meditation apps make millions. Doesn't that prove it works?"**
   - It proves people want a quieter mind. Whether any method works is tested in your own experience, not by
     revenue. The Gita's method costs nothing; test it the same way.
7. **"'No other means' sounds exclusive. What about other religions?"**
   - The verse (CC Adi 17.21) is about this age's method for self-realisation. Many traditions value repeating
     God's names; this talk does not compare or rank them, and you are not asked to leave anything.
8. **"How much should I chant?"**
   - For now, ten minutes, attentively. If you want a routine and a number, VL3-04 and a facilitator are the
     place for that; no one here will press you.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Most of us use "yoga" to mean stretching, breathing or an app. The Bhagavad-gita (an ancient Indian text) means something else: to join the consciousness with the Supreme, and it measures yoga by evenness of mind in success and failure (BG 2.48), not by flexibility.

The Gita does describe the classical meditation method: a secluded sacred place, a prescribed seat, erect posture, an unagitated mind, complete celibacy (the text's condition for that method), and meditation on the Lord within (BG 6.11–14). Arjuna heard it and protested that the mind is "more difficult than controlling the wind" (BG 6.34), and Krsna agreed it is hard but "possible by suitable practice" (BG 6.35). The Srimad-Bhagavatam (a later scripture) says each age has its own method and that for this age it is chanting the names of God (SB 12.3.51–52), which works by giving the mind a better taste rather than a ban (BG 2.59).

The highest yogi, Krsna says, is the one "who always abides
in Me, thinks of Me within himself and renders transcendental loving service to Me" (BG 6.47); nothing in that
verse is about the body. Mantra meditation is a way to think of Him within, and it can be tried for ten minutes
tonight. Keep your mat if you have one; add the meaning.

## S2. Key ideas

- Yoga means to join with the Supreme (BG 6.46 purport); the Gita measures it by evenness of mind (BG 2.48).
- The classical meditation path has strict conditions (BG 6.11–14); Arjuna said the mind was too restless (BG 6.34).
- Krsna's answer: practice (BG 6.35). The purports say the practice for now begins with hearing.
- Each age has its method; for this age it is chanting (SB 12.3.51–52; CC Adi 17.21).
- The highest yogi remembers and serves the Lord (BG 6.47).
- Chanting works by a higher taste, not a ban (BG 2.59). Routine (eating, sleeping) is part of yoga too (BG 6.16–17).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **BG 2.48:** "…abandoning all attachment to success or failure. Such equanimity is called yoga." *For me:*
  yoga is measured in my mind, not my hamstrings.
- **BG 6.34:** "The mind is restless, turbulent, obstinate and very strong, O Kṛṣṇa, and to subdue it, I think,
  is more difficult than controlling the wind." *For me:* my complaint is Arjuna's; I am normal.
- **BG 6.47:** "…the one with great faith who always abides in Me, thinks of Me within himself and renders
  transcendental loving service to Me – he is… the highest of all." *For me:* the goal is remembering and
  serving, and a mantra is a way to begin.

## S4. Words to know

- **Yoga:** "to join"; linking the consciousness with the Supreme; evenness of mind in action (BG 2.48).
- **Astanga-yoga:** the eight-step meditation path (posture, breath, concentration, trance…) described in the Gita's sixth chapter.
- **Mantra:** *man* (mind) + *tra* (deliver): a sacred sound that frees the mind (the deck's gloss).
- **Japa:** quiet repetition of a mantra, usually on beads.
- **Maha-mantra:** Hare Krsna, Hare Krsna, Krsna Krsna, Hare Hare / Hare Rama, Hare Rama, Rama Rama, Hare Hare.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **I do yoga every morning. Is that not yoga?**
   Asana and breathing are steps of the classical path and are good for you. The Gita's word means the whole
   process of joining with the Supreme (BG 6.46 purport). Keep the mat; add the meaning.
2. **Why not just meditate in silence?**
   You can try; the Gita gives the conditions (BG 6.11–14), and Arjuna found the mind too restless (BG 6.34).
   Krsna's answer was practice (BG 6.35); a sound to return to is what many find silent sitting lacks.
3. **Does the verse about celibacy apply to me?**
   BG 6.13–14 states what the classical meditation path requires. Nobody is asking you to change your life
   tonight; the point is that because those conditions are hard, the texts give another method.
4. **How can repeating words do anything?**
   The texts say the name of God is not an ordinary sound. The honest test is the experiment: ten minutes,
   attentively, for a week.
5. **Is this for daily life or only for worship?**
   BG 2.48 defines yoga by how you handle success and failure at work. It is for Tuesday.
6. **How much should I chant?**
   Ten minutes a day, attentively, for now. If you want a routine and a number, ask a facilitator (VL3-04).

## S6. Check your understanding

1. According to BG 2.48, what is "called yoga"? (a) a posture, (b) equanimity in success and failure, (c) breathing.
2. Name two conditions for the classical meditation path from BG 6.11–14.
3. What did Arjuna compare the mind to in BG 6.34?
4. According to SB 12.3.52, what is the method for this age?
5. In your own words: what does the highest yogi do, according to BG 6.47?

**Answers:** 1. (b) (BG 2.48). 2. Any two of: a secluded, sacred place; a prescribed seat; erect posture; a steady
gaze; an unagitated mind; complete celibacy (BG 6.11–14). 3. The wind: harder to subdue than to control the wind
(BG 6.34). 4. Chanting the Hare Krsna maha-mantra (SB 12.3.52). 5. Has faith, always abides in Krsna, thinks of
Him within and renders loving service (BG 6.47).
