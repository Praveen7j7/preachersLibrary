# Relishing Krishna's sweetness without imitation

> **TRD · up to 180 min · review: drafted · `sensitive: true` (touches rasa and the gopis' mood; the head reviews).**
> The head's fourth aim for residents is "relishing transcendental sweetness and happiness" while serving. This page
> asks how that relish comes, from Srila Prabhupada's books, and marks the one road that looks like it and is not:
> imitation (sahajiya). Giving up sahajiyaism is SPS-15; spontaneous devotion is DPS-30; the gopi-bhava club controversy
> is DPS-43; raganuga as Prabhupada taught it is TRD-38; the sahajiya danger in depth is TRD-39; madhurya rasa is TRD-44.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on SPS-15 (why nectar-shopping is a disease) and DPS-30 (what spontaneous devotion is): this page does not re-argue against sahajiyaism or define raganuga; it treats the positive question, how a resident actually comes to taste Krishna, and keeps the warning beside it.

Two residents sit in the same room. One reads the tenth canto's confidential chapters in private and feels he has found the real thing; the other avoids every mention of Krishna's sweetness for fear of being a sahajiya, and his service has become dry. Srila Prabhupada's books speak to both: Krishna's sweetness is the goal, it is tasted by those who have become qualified, the road to it is the ordinary road of hearing and service, and the shortcut is a trap.

## C. Central message

Krishna's sweetness is real and is the goal: "Even a fractional understanding of Kṛṣṇa's beauty can merge all three worlds in the ocean of love" (CC Madhya 21.102). It "cannot be tasted by means of fruitive activity, yogic austerities, speculative knowledge, regulative devotional service, mantra-yoga or meditation" (CC Madhya 21.119); it is tasted by the pure devotee whose heart has been cleansed. The road there is the one we already walk: hearing from devotees, chanting, service, and the stages in order. Imitating the fruit before the heart is clean is "mere imitation and has no value" (NOD 16). The resident relishes most by giving the sweetness to others, as the Lord did (CC Madhya 8.280).

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Describe Krishna's sweetness from CC Madhya 21.99–120 and CC Adi 4.138–146, and say who tastes it.
2. Give the Lord's order of approach in the Ramananda-samvada (CC Madhya 8.57–90) and what He called "external".
3. Quote NOD 16 and CC Madhya 22.153 purport on when following the Vrindavana devotees becomes possible, and name the sign of imitation that NOD 16 gives.
4. State from *Madhurya-kadambini* when "mādhurya is realized" and the two kinds of taste (Fifth Shower, texts 1 and 3).
5. Say how a preacher relishes Krishna while preaching (CC Madhya 8.280; BG 10.9) and choose one practice for the month.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**CC Madhya 21.101–102, 21.104, 21.110, 21.119**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/21/102/
> 21.102: "My dear Sanātana, the sweet, attractive transcendental form of Kṛṣṇa is so nice. Just try to understand it. Even a fractional understanding of Kṛṣṇa's beauty can merge all three worlds in the ocean of love. He attracts all living entities within the three worlds."
> 21.119: "The transcendental mellows generated from the dealings between the gopīs and Kṛṣṇa cannot be tasted by means of fruitive activity, yogic austerities, speculative knowledge, regulative devotional service, mantra-yoga or meditation."
- **Context:** Lord Caitanya instructing Sanatana Gosvami. "His form as a human being is the supreme transcendental form. In this form He is a cowherd boy. He carries a flute in His hand, and His youth is new" (21.101). "The wonderful form of Kṛṣṇa in His personal feature is so great that it attracts even Kṛṣṇa to taste His own association" (21.104). "Such sweetness is the quintessence of His qualities" (21.110).
- **Teaching:** sweetness (madhurya) is Krishna's own quality, not a devotee's feeling; and the highest tasting of it is not reached by any method, including "regulative devotional service" by itself (21.119).
- **Use:** in the talk, as the description of the goal.

**CC Adi 4.138, 4.143**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/adi/4/143/
> 4.143: "My sweetness is always newer and newer. Devotees taste it according to their own respective love."
- **Context:** Krishna's own reflection before descending as Lord Caitanya: "My sweetness is wonderful, infinite and full. No one in the three worlds can find its limit" (4.138).
- **Teaching:** the measure of tasting is love, "according to their own respective love". Taste is not acquired by reading further; it is given to love.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 1.1.3 with purport**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/1/3/. "Relish Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, the mature fruit of the desire tree of Vedic literatures." *Purport:* "the highest spiritual realization is knowledge that the Personality of Godhead is the reservoir of all spiritual tastes, or rasas"; the twelve rasas are listed; "the sum total of all these rasas is called affection or love." *Use:* in the talk: rasa is Prabhupada's own first-canto subject, not a secret.

**CC Madhya 8.69–70, 8.83, 8.86, 8.196**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/8/70/
> 8.70: "Pure devotional service in Kṛṣṇa consciousness cannot be had even by pious activity in hundreds and thousands of lives. It can be attained only by paying one price — that is, intense greed to obtain it. If it is available somewhere, one must purchase it without delay."
- **Context:** The Lord asks Ramananda Raya for the goal of life and rejects varnasrama, offering results to Krishna, giving up duties, and even knowledge-mixed devotion as "external" (8.57–66), accepts pure devotion (8.67–68), hears of taste ("As long as there is hunger and thirst within the stomach, varieties of food and drink make one feel very happy", 8.69), then of servitude, friendship, parental love and conjugal love in order (8.72–81). "It is true that whatever relationship a particular devotee has with the Lord is the best for him" (8.83); in conjugal love all the previous qualities are present (8.86). At the end: "This is the limit of the goal of human life. Only by your mercy have I come to understand it conclusively" (8.196).
- **Teaching:** the Lord Himself orders the approach: external means first set aside, then pure devotion, then the mellows from the lowest to the highest, each "best for him" who has it.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**CC Madhya 8.200–201, 8.204–205, 8.228–230**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/8/229/
> 8.229: "After thinking of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa and Their pastimes for a long time and after getting completely free from material contamination, one is transferred to the spiritual world. There the devotee attains an opportunity to serve Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa as one of the gopīs."
- **Teaching:** "The pastimes of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa are very confidential. They cannot be understood through the mellows of servitude, fraternity or parental affection" (8.201); "Without the help of the gopīs, one cannot enter into these pastimes. Only one who worships the Lord in the ecstasy of the gopīs, following in their footsteps, can engage in the service of Śrī Śrī Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa... There is no other procedure" (8.204–205). "Unless one follows in the footsteps of the gopīs, he cannot attain the service of the lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa, the son of Nanda Mahārāja" (8.230).
- **Purport point (8.200):** Sanatana Gosvami's rule from Padma Purana: "one should not hear anything about Kṛṣṇa from an avaiṣṇava, however great a mundane scholar he may be. Milk touched by the lips of a serpent has poisonous effects; similarly, talks about Kṛṣṇa given by an avaiṣṇava are also poisonous."
- **Purport point (8.229):** Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati on *siddha-deha*: "when one is spiritually situated and does not desire a gross or subtle body, he attains his original spiritual body"; the condition in the verse is "completely free from material contamination".
- **Purport point (8.230):** "The process of vidhi-mārga, following the regulative principles, is utilized in the worship of Lakṣmī-Nārāyaṇa, whereas the process of spontaneous service — following in the footsteps of the gopīs... is transcendentally more advanced."
- **Application (preacher's):** the goal is stated without softening, and so is its condition: "for a long time" and "completely free from material contamination". The two belong together on every slide.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**CC Madhya 22.149, 22.153, 22.156–157**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/22/153/
> 22.156–157: "When self-realized, the advanced devotee externally remains like a neophyte and executes all the śāstric injunctions, especially those concerning hearing and chanting. But within his mind, in his original, purified, self-realized position, he serves Kṛṣṇa in Vṛndāvana in his particular way."
- **Purport point (22.153):** "This stage is not achieved in the beginning. In the beginning one has to render service strictly according to the regulative principles set forth by the revealed scriptures and the spiritual master."
- **Teaching:** even the advanced devotee "externally remains like a neophyte"; the inner service is never a licence to drop hearing and chanting. Raganuga itself is TRD-38.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**NOD ch. 15–16**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/nod/16/
> NOD 16: "Sometimes someone is found imitating such devotional love, but factually he is not freed from anarthas, or unwanted habits. It has been seen that a so-called devotee proclaims himself a follower of Nanda, Yaśodā or the gopīs, while at the same time his abominable attraction for mundane sex life is visible. Such a manifestation of divine love is mere imitation and has no value. When one is actually spontaneously attracted to the loving principles of the gopīs, there will be found no trace of any mundane contamination in his character."
- **Teaching (NOD 16):** "in the beginning, everyone should strictly follow the regulative principles of devotional service according to the injunctions of the scriptures and the spiritual master. Only after the stage of liberation from material contamination can one actually aspire to follow in the footsteps of the devotees in Vṛndāvana." "The so-called siddha-praṇālī process is followed by the prākṛta-sahajiyā, a pseudosect of so-called Vaiṣṇavas. In the opinion of Rūpa Gosvāmī, such activities are simply disturbances to the standard way of devotional service." NOD 15 describes spontaneous devotion itself (DPS-30).
- **Use:** in the talk; the test of imitation, given as Prabhupada gives it.

**SB 10.33.30–31**: Verified · *BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples.*
> 10.33.30: "One who is not a great controller should never imitate the behavior of ruling personalities, even mentally. If out of foolishness an ordinary person does imitate such behavior, he will simply destroy himself, just as a person who is not Rudra would destroy himself if he tried to drink an ocean of poison."
- **Purport point:** "Just as we should not imitate this pastime of Śiva's, we should not imitate Lord Kṛṣṇa's activities with the gopīs." 10.33.31: the intelligent "should carry out their instructions" of the Lord's empowered servants.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**CC Antya 5.45–46**: Verified. "When one hears or describes with great faith the pastimes of Lord Kṛṣṇa, such as His rāsa dance with the gopīs, the disease of lusty desires in his heart and the agitation caused by the three modes of material nature are immediately nullified, and he becomes sober and silent." *Purport:* "When a pure Vaiṣṇava speaks on Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam and another pure Vaiṣṇava hears Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam from such a realized soul, both of them live in the transcendental world." *Use:* in the talk: the pastimes are heard "with great faith" from a pure Vaisnava; the fruit is sobriety, not agitation.

**BG 18.54–55; BG 10.9; NOI 7; NOI 8; CC Madhya 8.280**: Verified. Pure devotion is attained by one "transcendentally situated" who "never laments or desires to have anything" (18.54); Krishna is understood "as I am... only by devotional service" (18.55). Devotees "derive great satisfaction and bliss from always enlightening one another and conversing about Me" (10.9). The relish for the name awakens by careful daily chanting (NOI 7). One should "reside in Vraja... and serve Kṛṣṇa under the guidance of devotees... follow in the footsteps of the Lord's beloved devotees" (NOI 8; purport: "under their eternal guidance"). Lord Caitanya "came to taste Your own spiritual bliss, and at the same time You are transforming the whole world by spreading the ecstasy of love of Godhead" (CC Madhya 8.280). *Use:* in the talk (relishing by giving).

***Madhurya-kadambini* (Srila Visvanatha Cakravarti Thakura)**: approved by the head; edition: Gaudiya Vedanta Publications, 2018, with Bhaktivedanta Narayana Gosvami Maharaja's commentary (`research/books/`). Supporting.
- **Fifth Shower, text 1:** "Before the stage of ruci, one is not able to fully relish topics about Śrī Kṛṣṇa, but at the stage of ruci, such topics are extremely delectable, and mādhurya (a delightful sweetness) is realized." Quoting Jiva Gosvami: "at the stage of ruci, the seed of sinful activity is completely destroyed and one begins to experience the sweetness of bhajana"; "to relish the forms, qualities and pastimes of Śrī Kṛṣṇa exclusively is to realize His delightful sweetness."
- **Fifth Shower, text 3:** two kinds of taste: one "dependent on external excellence" (a sweet melody, ornate language, perfect arrangements), which "reveals that one's mind is still enveloped in a layer of contamination"; and one "not dependent on such external excellence", "extremely powerful from the moment one engages in śravaṇa and kīrtana".
- **Application (preacher's):** the acharya places the realisation of sweetness at ruci, after nistha; and he gives a test a resident can apply tonight: does my relish depend on the kirtana being good?

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The sweetness is Krishna's, and it is the goal.** Prabhupada's first canto already says the highest realisation is that the Lord "is the reservoir of all spiritual tastes, or rasas" (SB 1.1.3 purport); Lord Caitanya tells Sanatana that a fraction of Krishna's beauty "can merge all three worlds in the ocean of love" (CC Madhya 21.102). A resident who avoids the subject out of fear has lost the point of his renunciation. The head's aim is relish, not dryness.
2. **Who tastes it, and by what.** "Devotees taste it according to their own respective love" (CC Adi 4.143). The highest tasting is not reached by any method, "regulative devotional service" included (CC Madhya 21.119); it is given to a heart "completely free from material contamination" after "thinking of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa... for a long time" (8.229). Visvanatha Cakravarti places the first realisation of madhurya at ruci (Fifth Shower 1), after the stages DPS-12…15 describe.
3. **The road is the ordinary road.** Hearing from pure Vaisnavas (CC Antya 5.45; CC Madhya 8.200 purport), chanting with care (NOI 7), service "under the guidance of devotees" (NOI 8), and the regulative principles kept "strictly" "in the beginning" (NOD 16; CC Madhya 22.153 purport). Even the self-realised devotee "externally remains like a neophyte" (22.156–157). There is no second road.
4. **What imitation is and why it fails.** NOD 16 gives the sign: the so-called follower of the gopis whose "abominable attraction for mundane sex life is visible". Such love "is mere imitation and has no value", because the fruit is being acted before the anarthas are gone. SB 10.33.30: the imitator "will simply destroy himself", like one who is not Rudra drinking poison. Prabhupada's own words to the 1976 club: "First deserve, then desire!" (IYF note). The imitator's mistake is not that he wants the sweetness; it is that he reaches for the fruit with an unclean hand and thinks the reaching is the taste.
5. **Two tests a resident can apply.** (a) *Character:* "no trace of any mundane contamination" (NOD 16); if lust is visible, the "relish" is not relish. (b) *Dependence:* if my taste needs a good melody, a famous speaker, perfect arrangements, "a layer of contamination" remains (MK Fifth Shower 3). Both tests point back to the road, never to more reading.
6. **Relishing by giving.** The Lord "came to taste Your own spiritual bliss, and at the same time You are transforming the whole world by spreading" it (CC Madhya 8.280). Devotees relish "enlightening one another and conversing about Me" (BG 10.9). The resident's own relish grows in the class he gives, the book he distributes, the newcomer he brings to the Deity: that is the fourth aim fulfilled while the first three are kept.
7. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *"Reading the rasa-lila is itself sahajiya."* No: CC Antya 5.45 says hearing it "with great faith" from a pure Vaisnava nullifies lust. The question is from whom, in what mood, and whether the hearer's own stage is honoured.
   - *"I have no attraction to the gopis, so I am safe."* Imitation has other forms: claiming stages, performing symptoms, treating regulated devotees as inferior. NOD 16's rule ("strictly follow the regulative principles") covers all.
   - *"Since the goal is spontaneous, rules hold me back."* CC Madhya 22.156–157: the advanced devotee "executes all the śāstric injunctions, especially those concerning hearing and chanting."
   - *"Preaching is lower than bhajana."* CC Madhya 8.280 and the 1976 episode: the sahajiya in Vrindavana asked Prabhupada "why are you bothering to preach?" Prabhupada's answer was ISKCON.
8. **Pitch for TRD.** Keep the goal and its condition in the same sentence every time. Residents hear either "sweetness" or "danger"; the Lord's own teaching to Ramananda Raya says both at once.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **The sweetness is Krishna's own, and the goal is to taste it.** CC Madhya 21.101–119; CC Adi 4.143; SB 1.1.3 purport. *Example:* it attracts even Krishna (21.104). *Transition:* how did the Lord teach the approach?
2. **The Lord's own order.** CC Madhya 8.57–90: four answers called "external", then pure devotion, taste, greed, and the mellows in order; "whatever relationship a particular devotee has with the Lord is the best for him" (8.83). *Transition:* and the highest?
3. **The highest stated fully, with its condition.** CC Madhya 8.201–230: "no other procedure" than following the gopis; and "for a long time... completely free from material contamination" (8.229). *Transition:* what happens when the condition is skipped?
4. **Imitation has no value and destroys the imitator.** NOD 16 (the visible sign); SB 10.33.30; CC Madhya 22.153 purport and 22.156–157 ("externally remains like a neophyte"). *Example:* "First deserve, then desire!" (IYF note). *Transition:* how do I know which I am doing?
5. **Two tests.** Character (NOD 16); dependence on external excellence (MK Fifth Shower 3); and the acharya's placing of madhurya at ruci (Fifth Shower 1). *Transition:* then how does a resident relish today?
6. **By hearing from Vaisnavas, and by giving.** CC Antya 5.45; CC Madhya 8.200 purport; BG 10.9; CC Madhya 8.280. *Transition:* P.

## J. Stories and illustrations

**The Lord and Ramananda Raya at Vidyanagara** (CC Madhya 8.57–90, 8.193–196). The Lord asks for a verse on "the ultimate goal of life" (8.57). Ramananda answers with varnasrama; "This is external" (8.59). Offering one's work to Krishna; "This is also external" (8.61). Giving up occupational duties; the Lord "again rejected his statement" (8.64). Devotion mixed with knowledge; "rejected it, considering it to be external" (8.66). Pure devotion free of speculation; "This is all right, but still you can speak more" (8.68). Then taste (8.69), greed (8.70), servitude (8.72–73), friendship (8.74–75), parental love (8.77–78), conjugal love (8.80–81). When Ramananda begins to sing his own song, the Lord "immediately covered Rāmānanda's mouth with His own hand" (8.193), then says, "This is the limit of the goal of human life" (8.196), and asks for "the means by which this goal can be attained" (8.197). *Lesson:* the Lord Himself climbs step by step and names what is external; the highest is reached by the Lord's questioning, not by the student's leaping.

**The 1976 "gopi-bhava club"** (IYF note quoting *Transcendental Diary*; the episode is DPS-43). Prabhupada heard that a study group found the Gita "too bodily conscious" and read only the gopis' dealings. He called the GBC men and the group, rebuked them, and said: "First deserve, then desire! There is no question of desire unless one is actually liberated"; everything is in his books "but they are not all to be immediately studied"; and "this sahajiyā philosophy means the preaching will be finished." *Lesson:* the founder-acharya himself drew the line this page draws. Verify the wording against the published diary before quoting in class.

**Madhavendra Puri and Gopala** (CC Madhya 4; TRD-10) is the positive picture of relish: a paramahamsa whose taste was given, not taken. Refer to it in one sentence.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"If the sweetness is the goal, why not read the tenth canto's intimate chapters now?"**
   - Read the whole Bhagavatam in order, as Prabhupada presented it; the tenth canto is his too. Hear the intimate pastimes "with great faith" from a pure Vaisnava (CC Antya 5.45), honouring your stage; do not form a private circle around them (DPS-43). "Not all to be immediately studied" (Prabhupada, 1976).
2. **"I avoid the subject to be safe. Is that wrong?"**
   - Avoiding Krishna's sweetness is not safety; it is dryness (TRD-09). The Lord told Sanatana, "Just try to understand it" (CC Madhya 21.102). The safe way is the Lord's order: hearing from devotees, service, the stages, and the sweetness received as it is given.
3. **"How do I know whether my relish is real?"**
   - NOD 16: "no trace of any mundane contamination in his character". MK Fifth Shower 3: real taste does not depend on the kirtana being good. And 20.28 of the Siksastaka (TRD-12): the one who has it does not think he has it.
4. **"Is regulated devotion inferior?"**
   - It is the beginning of every devotee's road, including the advanced devotee's outer life (CC Madhya 22.153 purport, 22.156–157). CC Madhya 8.230 says vidhi-marga by itself does not reach Vraja; it does not say to drop it. TRD-38.
5. **"Does preaching take time away from relishing?"**
   - CC Madhya 8.280: the Lord tasted and spread at once. BG 10.9: bliss comes from "enlightening one another". The resident's relish is in the giving; the sahajiya asked Prabhupada why he bothered to preach.
6. **"Someone in the ashram claims advanced feelings and wants followers."**
   - NOD 16 and SB 10.33.30 give the sastra's judgment; CC Antya 20.28 the test. Tell your authority privately; do not join, do not debate publicly. TRD-39 and TRD-50 treat the harder cases.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Krishna's sweetness is real, and tasting it is the goal of our life. Lord Caitanya told Sanatana Gosvami: "the sweet, attractive transcendental form of Kṛṣṇa is so nice. Just try to understand it. Even a fractional understanding of Kṛṣṇa's beauty can merge all three worlds in the ocean of love" (CC Madhya 21.102). His form "attracts even Kṛṣṇa to taste His own association" (21.104). Krishna Himself says, "My sweetness is always newer and newer. Devotees taste it according to their own respective love" (CC Adi 4.143). Srila Prabhupada writes in the first canto that the highest realisation is that the Lord "is the reservoir of all spiritual tastes, or rasas" (SB 1.1.3 purport). So a devotee who avoids the subject out of fear has lost the point.

How does the taste come? The Lord showed the order when He questioned Ramananda Raya (CC Madhya 8.57–90): He called varnasrama, offering one's work, giving up duties and knowledge-mixed devotion "external", accepted pure devotion, then heard of taste, greed, and the mellows of servitude, friendship, parental love and conjugal love in turn; "whatever relationship a particular devotee has with the Lord is the best for him" (8.83). He stated the highest fully: "Without the help of the gopīs, one cannot enter into these pastimes" (8.204–205). And He stated its condition fully: "After thinking of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa and Their pastimes for a long time and after getting completely free from material contamination, one is transferred to the spiritual world" (8.229). The road is the one we walk: hearing from pure Vaisnavas (CC Antya 5.45), careful chanting (NOI 7), service "under the guidance of devotees" (NOI 8), and the regulative principles kept strictly; even the self-realised devotee "externally remains like a neophyte and executes all the śāstric injunctions" (CC Madhya 22.156–157). Visvanatha Cakravarti says Krishna's sweetness "is realized" at the stage of ruci, taste, after steadiness (*Madhurya-kadambini*, Fifth Shower 1).

The shortcut is imitation. Srila Prabhupada writes: "a so-called devotee proclaims himself a follower of Nanda, Yaśodā or the gopīs, while at the same time his abominable attraction for mundane sex life is visible. Such a manifestation of divine love is mere imitation and has no value" (NOD 16). The Bhagavatam says the imitator "will simply destroy himself, just as a person who is not Rudra would destroy himself if he tried to drink an ocean of poison" (SB 10.33.30). To devotees who formed a private study group on the gopis in 1976, Prabhupada said, "First deserve, then desire!" Two tests help us: is there any mundane contamination in my character (NOD 16)? Does my relish depend on a good melody or a famous speaker (*Madhurya-kadambini*, Fifth Shower 3)? And the resident relishes most by giving: the Lord came "to taste Your own spiritual bliss, and at the same time... spreading the ecstasy of love of Godhead" (CC Madhya 8.280); devotees find bliss in "enlightening one another and conversing about Me" (BG 10.9).

## S2. Key ideas

- Krishna's sweetness is His own quality and the goal; it attracts even Him (CC Madhya 21.102–104; CC Adi 4.143).
- The Lord's order of approach: external means set aside, pure devotion, taste, the mellows in order (CC Madhya 8.57–90).
- The highest and its condition belong together: following the gopis; "for a long time... completely free from material contamination" (CC Madhya 8.204–229).
- The road is hearing from Vaisnavas, careful chanting, service under devotees, regulative principles kept (CC Antya 5.45; NOI 7–8; NOD 16; CC Madhya 22.156–157).
- Imitation "has no value" and destroys the imitator (NOD 16; SB 10.33.30); "First deserve, then desire!"
- We relish most by giving (CC Madhya 8.280; BG 10.9).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **CC Madhya 21.102:** "Even a fractional understanding of Kṛṣṇa's beauty can merge all three worlds in the ocean of love." *For me:* the goal is sweetness, not dryness.
- **CC Madhya 8.229:** "After thinking of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa and Their pastimes for a long time and after getting completely free from material contamination, one is transferred to the spiritual world." *For me:* the goal and its condition in one line.
- **NOD 16:** "Such a manifestation of divine love is mere imitation and has no value." *For me:* the test is my character.
- **CC Madhya 8.280:** "You have come to taste Your own spiritual bliss, and at the same time You are transforming the whole world by spreading the ecstasy of love of Godhead." *For me:* I taste by giving.

## S4. Words to know

- **Madhurya:** sweetness; Krishna's own attractive quality (CC Madhya 21.110).
- **Rasa:** the taste of a loving relationship with Krishna; the Lord is "the reservoir of all spiritual tastes" (SB 1.1.3 purport).
- **Sahajiya (prakrta-sahajiya):** one who imitates the fruit of devotion before the heart is clean (NOD 16).
- **Vidhi-marga / raga-marga:** devotion by regulation; devotion by spontaneous attachment (CC Madhya 8.230 purport; TRD-38).
- **Ramananda-samvada:** the conversation between Lord Caitanya and Ramananda Raya, CC Madhya 8.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Should I read the intimate tenth-canto chapters now?** Read the whole Bhagavatam in order as Prabhupada gave it, and hear the intimate pastimes "with great faith" from a pure Vaisnava (CC Antya 5.45), honouring your stage; never in a private circle. "Not all to be immediately studied" (Prabhupada, 1976).
2. **Is it safer to avoid Krishna's sweetness altogether?** No; that is dryness, not safety. The Lord said, "Just try to understand it" (CC Madhya 21.102). Receive it as it is given on the ordinary road.
3. **How do I know my relish is real?** No trace of mundane contamination in character (NOD 16); relish that does not depend on a good melody or speaker (*Madhurya-kadambini*, Fifth Shower 3); and not thinking oneself advanced.
4. **Is regulated devotion inferior?** It is the beginning of every road and the outer life even of the advanced devotee (CC Madhya 22.153 purport, 22.156–157). It is never to be dropped.
5. **Does preaching take me away from relishing?** The Lord tasted and spread at once (CC Madhya 8.280); devotees find bliss in "enlightening one another" (BG 10.9). Your relish grows in the giving.
