# Paramatma-sandarbha: the Lord's energies and the jiva (acintya-bhedabheda)

> **TRD · up to 180 min · review: drafted (30 Oct 2026).** Group F, fourth of eighteen: the third Sandarbha. Every verse
> checked on vedabase (research/verification/trd-26.json). The *Paramatma-sandarbha*, *Prameya-ratnavali* and *Jaiva-dharma*

> content rests on Lord Caitanya's words to Sanatana and Sarvabhauma and on the Gita, with Srila Prabhupada's purports.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on trd-25: there the Paramatma was placed as the Lord's localised plenary portion; here the Paramatma feature is
studied as the Lord in relation to His energies and to the souls who are His energy. Builds on dps-43: there the controversy
about the jiva's fall was handled; here the settled siddhanta on what the jiva *is* is given, and the controversy is not
reopened. Builds on sps-03: God is a person; here, what the soul is in relation to Him.

Every preacher meets Sanatana's first question, "Who am I?", from students. The two wrong answers, "you are God" and "you are
the body", are the two most common views in the world. Lord Caitanya's answer to Sanatana (CC Madhya 20.108–117) and His
argument to Sarvabhauma (CC Madhya 6.162–172) give the exact position: one with the Lord in quality, different in quantity;
His energy, never the energetic. Residents who can state this clearly save students from both errors. No lower batch takes
the energies as a subject.

## C. Central message

"It is the living entity's constitutional position to be an eternal servant of Kṛṣṇa because he is the marginal energy of Kṛṣṇa and a manifestation simultaneously one with and different from the Lord, like a molecular particle of sunshine or fire. Kṛṣṇa has three varieties of energy" (CC Madhya 20.108–109). "The Lord is the master of the potencies, and the living entity is the servant of them. That is the difference between the Lord and the living entity" (CC Madhya 6.162). The world is a transformation of the Lord's energy, never of the Lord (CC Madhya 6.170–171).

## D. Learning outcomes

Residents should be able to:
1. Name Krishna's three energies and their scriptural seat (CC Madhya 20.111–112; BG 7.4–5).
2. Explain "simultaneously one with and different from" using the Lord's own examples (sunshine and sun, sparks and fire)
   and Bhaktivinoda's explanation in the CC Madhya 20.108–109 purport.
3. State the difference between the Lord and the jiva as Lord Caitanya states it to Sarvabhauma: master of the potencies
   versus servant of them (CC Madhya 6.162), and show from BG 15.7 that the jiva is eternally a fragment.
4. Distinguish parinama-vada (transformation of energy) from vivarta-vada (illusion) and say why Sankara's reading is
   rejected (CC Madhya 6.169–172).
5. Answer "I am God" and "the universe is only energy" in three sentences each, from this page's verses.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**CC Madhya 20.108–109**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/20/108-109/
> "It is the living entity's constitutional position to be an eternal servant of Kṛṣṇa because he is the marginal energy of Kṛṣṇa and a manifestation simultaneously one with and different from the Lord, like a molecular particle of sunshine or fire. Kṛṣṇa has three varieties of energy."
- **Context:** Lord Caitanya's first answer to Sanatana's "Who am I?" (trd-23).
- **Purport point:** Srila Prabhupada gives Bhaktivinoda Thakura's explanation in full: "You are a pure living entity. You are neither the gross material body nor the subtle body composed of mind and intelligence. Actually you are a spirit soul, eternally part and parcel of the Supreme Soul, Kṛṣṇa. Therefore you are His eternal servant. You belong to Kṛṣṇa's marginal potency." And: "Because you are spirit soul, you are one in quality with the Supreme Personality of Godhead, but because you are a very minute particle of spirit soul, you are different from the Supreme Soul. Therefore your position is simultaneously one with and different from the Supreme Soul."
- **Use:** in the talk. The central verse; the whole page expands it.

**CC Madhya 20.110–112**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/20/111/
> 20.110: "Just as the illumination of a fire, which is situated in one place, is spread all over, the energies of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Parabrahman, are spread all over this universe."
> 20.111: "Lord Kṛṣṇa naturally has three energetic transformations, and these are known as the spiritual potency, the living entity potency and the illusory potency."
> 20.112: "Originally, Kṛṣṇa's energy is spiritual, and the energy known as the living entity is also spiritual. However, there is another energy, called illusion, which consists of fruitive activity. That is the Lord's third potency."
- **Teaching:** The Visnu Purana verses (1.22.53; 6.7.61) quoted by the Lord: the Lord stays in one place and His energies
  spread everywhere; three energies, two of them spiritual and the third illusory, "which consists of fruitive activity."
- **Use:** in the talk, with N.

**CC Madhya 20.117**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/20/117/
> "Forgetting Kṛṣṇa, the living entity has been attracted by the external feature from time immemorial. Therefore the illusory energy [māyā] gives him all kinds of misery in his material existence."
- **Purport point:** "The living entity is called the marginal energy because by nature he is spiritual but by forgetfulness he is situated in the material energy. Thus he has the power to live either in the material energy or in the spiritual energy, and for this reason he is called marginal energy." The forgetfulness "is described herein as anādi, which indicates that it has existed since time immemorial. One should understand that due to his desire to enjoy himself in competition with Kṛṣṇa, the living entity comes into material existence."
- **Use:** in the talk, as Srila Prabhupada states it; the controversy about it is dps-43, not this page.

**BG 7.4–5**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/7/5/
> 7.4: "Earth, water, fire, air, ether, mind, intelligence and false ego – all together these eight constitute My separated material energies."
> 7.5: "Besides these, O mighty-armed Arjuna, there is another, superior energy of Mine, which comprises the living entities who are exploiting the resources of this material, inferior nature."
- **Purport point (7.5):** "Energies are always controlled by the energetic, and therefore the living entities are always controlled by the Lord – they have no independent existence. They are never equally powerful, as unintelligent men think." SB 10.87.30 is quoted: if the living entities were all-pervading like the Lord "they would not be under Your control", but as minute energies they are; "Therefore real liberation entails surrender by the living entities to Your control".
- **Purport point (7.4):** material nature is the Lord's energy through the three purusa incarnations (Narada Pancaratra quoted); "Generally one who does not know the science of God (Kṛṣṇa) assumes that this material world is for the enjoyment of the living entities and that the living entities are the puruṣas"; "this atheistic conclusion is false."
- **Use:** in the talk. Krishna's own classification of His energies.

**BG 15.7**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/15/7/
> "The living entities in this conditioned world are My eternal fragmental parts. Due to conditioned life, they are struggling very hard with the six senses, which include the mind."
- **Purport point:** "It is not that he assumes individuality in his conditional life and in his liberated state becomes one with the Supreme Lord. He is eternally fragmented. It is clearly said, sanātanaḥ." The visnu-tattva are "personal expansions" and the living entities "separated expansions"; "Every living entity, as an individual soul, has his personal individuality and a minute form of independence. By misuse of that independence one becomes a conditioned soul, and by proper use of independence he is always liberated."
- **Use:** in the talk. The answer to "the soul merges at liberation".

**CC Madhya 6.162**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/6/162/
> "The Lord is the master of the potencies, and the living entity is the servant of them. That is the difference between the Lord and the living entity. However, you declare that the Lord and the living entities are one and the same."
- **Context:** Lord Caitanya to Sarvabhauma Bhattacarya.
- **Purport point:** The Mundaka Upanisad (3.1.1–2) is quoted: two birds on one tree; "The living entity is subjected to the reactions of fruitive activity, whereas the Lord simply witnesses such activity and bestows the results." "That is the difference between māyādhīśa and māyā-vaśa."
- **Use:** in the talk. The one-line statement of the difference.

**CC Madhya 6.169–172**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/6/171/
> 6.170: "The Vedānta-sūtra aims at establishing that the cosmic manifestation has come into being by the transformation of the inconceivable potency of the Supreme Personality of Godhead."
> 6.171: "The touchstone, after touching iron, produces volumes of gold without being changed. Similarly, the Supreme Personality of Godhead manifests Himself as the cosmic manifestation by His inconceivable potency, yet He remains unchanged in His eternal, transcendental form."
> 6.172: "Śaṅkarācārya states that the theory of transformation presented in the Vedānta-sūtra implies that the Absolute Truth itself is transformed. In this way the Māyāvādī philosophers denigrate Śrīla Vyāsadeva by accusing him of error. They thus find fault in the Vedānta-sūtra and misinterpret it to try to establish the theory of illusion."
- **Teaching (6.169):** "if one hears the commentary of Śaṅkarācārya, everything is spoiled."
- **Purport point (6.169):** merging "means denying one's individual existence. In other words, it is a kind of spiritual suicide."
- **Purport point (6.171):** "The Supreme Lord is the master of innumerable eternal energies, which are unlimited." The Mayavadis, "being unable to understand the inconceivable energies of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, imagine an impersonal void".
- **Purport point (6.172):** *janmady asya yatah* "does not suggest that the original person has been transformed. Rather, it clearly indicates that He produces this cosmic manifestation through His inconceivable energy." "All of these śāstric statements indicate the transformation of the Lord's energy, not of the Lord Himself. Tranformation of the Lord's energy is called pariṇāma-vāda." Sankara "put forward his theory of illusion ( vivarta-vāda )".
- **Use:** in the talk (second half). The siddhanta on the world.

**BG 9.4–5; SB 2.9.35**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/9/4/
> 9.4: "By Me, in My unmanifested form, this entire universe is pervaded. All beings are in Me, but I am not in them."
> 9.5: "And yet everything that is created does not rest in Me. Behold My mystic opulence! Although I am the maintainer of all living entities and although I am everywhere, I am not a part of this cosmic manifestation, for My Self is the very source of creation."
- **Purport point (9.4):** "the entire material cosmic manifestation is only a combination of His two different energies – the superior, spiritual energy and the inferior, material energy. Just as the sunshine is spread all over the universe, the energy of the Lord is spread all over the creation, and everything is resting in that energy. Yet one should not conclude that because He is spread all over He has lost His personal existence."
- **Purport point (9.5):** "Although they are situated on My inconceivable energy, as the Supreme Personality of Godhead I am aloof from them." His determination "is itself actual fact."
- **Teaching (SB 2.9.35):** the Lord is within everything and outside everything, as the elements enter the cosmos and
  remain apart (trd-24).
- **Use:** in the talk. The Lord pervades by His energy and remains Himself.

**BG 13.23, BG 18.61** (the Paramatma): Verified. The Supersoul is "the overseer and permitter"; "His name is Paramātmā, not ātmā" (13.23 purport); the Lord in the heart directs the wanderings of all living beings (18.61). *Use:* in the talk, to say who controls the marginal energy from within.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** Krishna is the energetic; everything else is His energy. His energies are three: the spiritual
   (*cit-sakti*), the living entity (*jiva-sakti*) and the illusory (*maya-sakti*) (CC Madhya 20.111–112). The Gita gives
   the same in two: the inferior, material energy and the superior energy, the living entities (BG 7.4–5). The jiva is
   called marginal because he is spiritual by nature yet can live in either energy (CC Madhya 20.117 purport).
2. **One and different at once.** The jiva is one with the Lord in quality (spirit) and different in quantity (minute); a
   particle of sunshine is sunshine, but it is not the sun (CC Madhya 20.108–109 and purport). Srila Prabhupada's books call
   this the Gaudiya conclusion of inconceivable, simultaneous oneness and difference (acintya-bhedabheda); the word
   "inconceivable" is Lord Caitanya's own ("inconceivable potency", CC Madhya 6.170–171): the relation is real and cannot be
   worked out by material logic, only received from scripture.
3. **The exact difference.** "The Lord is the master of the potencies, and the living entity is the servant of them"
   (CC Madhya 6.162). The jiva is controlled; the Lord controls. The jiva is eternally a fragment, before and after liberation
   (BG 15.7 and purport); he never becomes the whole. Energies "are never equally powerful" with the energetic (BG 7.5
   purport).
4. **The world: transformation of energy, not of God.** The Vedanta-sutra teaches that the cosmos comes from the
   transformation of the Lord's inconceivable potency (CC Madhya 6.170); the Lord Himself is unchanged, as the touchstone
   makes gold without changing (6.171). Sankara, to avoid saying that God changes, said the world is an illusion
   (vivarta-vada), and so accused Vyasadeva of error (6.172). The Gaudiya answer: the energy transforms, the energetic does not
   (parinama-vada). The world is therefore real but temporary and dependent, "that reflection which appears to be in darkness"
   (SB 2.9.34), not false: "the rope is not false" (purport).
5. **The Lord within and outside.** He pervades everything by His energy (BG 9.4; CC Madhya 20.110), yet remains aloof as
   the Person (BG 9.5; SB 2.9.35). Within each body He is Paramatma, "the overseer and permitter" (BG 13.23), directing the
   wandering jiva (BG 18.61). This is why the third Sandarbha is called *Paramatma*: the Lord in relation to the energies and
   the souls is the Paramatma feature.
6. **Connections.**
   - trd-23: all this is sambandha-jnana: oneself, the universe, God and their relationship (CC Adi 7.146 purport).
   - trd-25: the Paramatma is His localised portion; here that portion is studied as controller.
   - dps-43: how the jiva came to forget is a controversy; Prabhupada's statement here is that the forgetfulness is *anadi*
     and that the cause is "his desire to enjoy himself in competition with Kṛṣṇa" (CC Madhya 20.117 purport). Teach it as
     given and send detailed questions to dps-43.
   - trd-35 and trd-40 take the argument with Mayavada and the Vedanta-sutra further.
7. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *"Aham brahmasmi means I am God."* I am Brahman in quality, as the spark is fire; I am not the master of the potencies
     (CC Madhya 6.162). Thinking oneself the Supreme is "offensive" and leads to falling again (CC Madhya 22.21 purport, trd-28).
   - *"At liberation the soul merges."* "He is eternally fragmented" (BG 15.7 purport); merging is "a kind of spiritual suicide" (CC Madhya 6.169 purport).
   - *"The world is false."* It is the Lord's energy transformed, real but temporary (CC Madhya 6.170–172; SB 2.9.34).
   - *"If God is everywhere He has no form."* He pervades by His energy and "one should not conclude that because He is spread all over He has lost His personal existence" (BG 9.4 purport).
   - *"Maya is a second power beside God."* Maya is His third energy (CC Madhya 20.112); "the illusory energy is also an energy of the Lord" (SB 2.9.34 purport).
8. **Pitch for TRD.** Keep the Lord's own examples (sunshine, sparks, touchstone, two birds) and the Lord's own sentences.
   Residents should leave able to reproduce the Sarvabhauma argument (6.162–172) in five minutes, since that is the argument
   they will need before an educated impersonalist.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet the residents and say that today's session answers Sanatana's first question, "Who am I?", exactly as Lord Caitanya
   answered it, and then answers the two wrong answers the world gives.
2. Ask them to write privately which wrong answer they hear more often from students: "I am the body" or "I am God".
3. Read CC Madhya 20.108–109. Ask: in this verse, how is the soul one with Krishna, and how is he different? Take two answers.
4. Say that Lord Caitanya gave the same teaching to the greatest logician of His day, and that the Gosvamis made it the third
   of the six Sandarbhas.
5. Ask H.2 question 1 and take three answers.

**H.2 Opening questions**
1. A spark leaps from a fire and lands on your hand. Is it fire? Is it the fire? What does each answer teach about the soul?
2. If someone tells you "you are God", what would you expect to be able to do, and what can you actually do?
3. When a scientist says "everything is energy", what question does he leave unasked?

## I. Core teaching points

1. **The soul is Krishna's marginal energy, one and different.** CC Madhya 20.108–109: eternal servant, marginal energy,
   "simultaneously one with and different from the Lord, like a molecular particle of sunshine or fire." Bhaktivinoda: one in
   quality, different as a minute particle (purport). *Transition:* how many energies does Krishna have?
2. **Three energies.** Spiritual, living entity, illusory (CC Madhya 20.111–112); the Gita's superior and inferior energies
   (BG 7.4–5). The jiva is "marginal" because he can live in either (20.117 purport). *Transition:* does the soul ever stop
   being a soul?
3. **Eternally a fragment.** The living entities "are My eternal fragmental parts" (BG 15.7); "He is eternally fragmented" (purport); merging is "a kind of spiritual suicide" (CC Madhya 6.169 purport). *Transition:* then what exactly is the difference between the soul and God?
4. **Master and servant of the potencies.** "The Lord is the master of the potencies, and the living entity is the servant of them" (CC Madhya 6.162). Two birds on one tree: one eats the fruit, one witnesses (purport). Energies "are never equally powerful" (BG 7.5 purport). *Transition:* and this world, is it God changed into matter?
5. **The world is God's energy transformed, not God.** Transformation of His inconceivable potency (CC Madhya 6.170); the
   touchstone unchanged (6.171); Sankara's illusion theory accuses Vyasa of error (6.172); "From Me, everything emanates" (BG 10.8). The world is real but dependent; "the rope is not false" (SB 2.9.34 purport). *Transition:* if everything rests on Him, is He everywhere and nowhere?
6. **Within everything, outside everything, still a Person.** BG 9.4–5: all beings are in Him, yet He is aloof; SB 2.9.35.
   Within each body He is Paramatma, overseer and permitter (BG 13.23), directing the wanderer (BG 18.61). *Transition:* what
   does this mean for me tonight?
7. **Application (preacher's).** I am Krishna's energy: so I cannot be proud (I am not the master) and I cannot despair (I am
   His, eternally). The remedy for forgetfulness is to act as what I am, His servant, by hearing, chanting and service.

## J. Stories and illustrations

**Lord Caitanya answers Sanatana's "Who am I?"** (CC Madhya 20.108–117). Sanatana Gosvami had asked who he is and why the
threefold miseries trouble him (20.102, trd-23). The Lord answers that the living entity is Krishna's eternal servant, His marginal energy, "simultaneously one with and different from the Lord, like a molecular particle of sunshine or fire" (20.108–109); He quotes the Visnu Purana on the Lord's energies spreading like the light of a fire (20.110) and on the three energies (20.111–112); and He says: "Forgetting Kṛṣṇa, the living entity has been attracted by the external feature from time immemorial. Therefore the illusory energy [māyā] gives him all kinds of misery in his material existence" (20.117). *Devotional lesson:* the answer to "who am I?" is given in terms of relationship to Krishna; the misery Sanatana asked about is the result of forgetting Him, and the cure is remembering Him in service.

**Lord Caitanya and Sarvabhauma on the potencies** (CC Madhya 6.162–172). To Sarvabhauma Bhattacarya, who held the Mayavada
view, the Lord says: "The Lord is the master of the potencies, and the living entity is the servant of them. That is the difference between the Lord and the living entity. However, you declare that the Lord and the living entities are one and the same" (6.162). He says that Vyasadeva gave the Vedanta "for the deliverance of conditioned souls, but if one hears the commentary of Śaṅkarācārya, everything is spoiled" (6.169); that the Vedanta-sutra teaches transformation of the Lord's inconceivable potency (6.170), like the touchstone that makes gold unchanged (6.171); and that Sankara, misreading transformation as a change in the Absolute, "misinterpret[s] it to try to establish the theory of illusion" (6.172). *Devotional lesson:* the Lord corrected the greatest scholar with the simplest image, a touchstone; siddhanta protects the soul's eternal service from being argued away.

**Two birds on one tree** (Mundaka Upanisad 3.1.1–2, quoted in the CC Madhya 6.162 purport). Srila Prabhupada: "The living entity is subjected to the reactions of fruitive activity, whereas the Lord simply witnesses such activity and bestows the results." When the living entity "comes to his senses by the mercy of the Lord, he is awarded devotional service... At such a time he can see his eternal friend". *Devotional lesson:* the friend was on the same branch all along; devotion is turning to look at Him.

**The touchstone** (CC Madhya 6.171). "The touchstone, after touching iron, produces volumes of gold without being changed." *Devotional lesson:* Krishna gives rise to everything and loses nothing; so surrender to Him takes nothing from Him and gives everything to us.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. *"The Upanisads say aham brahmasmi, I am Brahman. So I am God."* You are Brahman in quality, spirit, as a spark is fire
   (CC Madhya 20.108–109). You are not the master of the potencies but their servant (CC Madhya 6.162). Ask: can you create,
   maintain and withdraw a universe? Then give BG 7.5 purport: energies "are never equally powerful, as unintelligent men think."
2. *"Science shows the universe is energy changing form. Where is God in that?"* The Gaudiya siddhanta agrees the world is
   energy transformed, and asks whose energy it is: "the transformation of the inconceivable potency of the Supreme Personality of Godhead" (CC Madhya 6.170); "From Me, everything emanates" (BG 10.8). Energy belongs to an energetic (BG 7.4–5).
3. *"If the soul is eternally a fragment, is liberation real?"* Yes: liberation is freedom from the material condition, not
   loss of identity; "by proper use of independence he is always liberated" (BG 15.7 purport). Merging is "a kind of spiritual suicide" (CC Madhya 6.169 purport).
4. *"Is the world false, as Sankara says?"* No. It is the Lord's energy transformed, real but temporary and dependent;
   "accepting a rope as a snake is illusion, but the rope is not false" (SB 2.9.34 purport). What is false is seeing it as unrelated to Him (SB 2.9.34).
5. *"How did the soul fall under maya, and when?"* Srila Prabhupada's statement here: the forgetfulness is *anadi*, from time
   immemorial, and its cause is "his desire to enjoy himself in competition with Kṛṣṇa" (CC Madhya 20.117 purport). The
   controversy around this is handled in dps-43; this page teaches what Prabhupada states and no more.
6. *"How can the soul be both one and different? That is a contradiction."* It is inconceivable to material logic, as Lord
   Caitanya says of the Lord's potency (CC Madhya 6.170–171), and received from scripture; the Lord's own examples make it
   intelligible: sunshine and sun, spark and fire (CC Madhya 20.108–109).
7. *"Why study *Paramatma-sandarbha* if the purports say this?"* Because Jiva Gosvami treats the jiva, maya and the Lord's
   control systematically there, as his own summary in *Bhakti-sandarbha* anuccheda 1 shows; but until a readable edition is
   checked, teach from the purports on this page.

## L. Case studies

**Case 1: "I am God."** *Invented case for discussion.* A university student who has read some Vedanta tells Haridasa, a
brahmacari: "Your scriptures say aham brahmasmi. I am God. Why should I worship another?"
- *Dilemma:* deny the verse, or accept the conclusion.
- *Principles:* CC Madhya 20.108–109 and purport; CC Madhya 6.162; BG 7.5 purport.
- *Questions:* What is true in the student's claim? What is false? What question shows him the difference?
- *Analysis:* True: he is spirit, Brahman in quality. False: he is the master of the potencies. Haridasa agrees that he is
  Brahman, asks whether he controls his own breathing in sleep, and reads CC Madhya 6.162 and 20.108–109. He ends by
  inviting the student to act as what he is, an eternal servant, by chanting.
- *Takeaway:* agree with the true part; then show the difference from the Lord's own words.

**Case 2: The physics student.** *Invented case for discussion.* At a book table a physics student says: "Everything is
energy transforming. Your God is unnecessary."
- *Dilemma:* argue physics, or ask the unasked question.
- *Principles:* CC Madhya 6.170–171; BG 7.4–5; BG 10.8.
- *Questions:* What does the student already accept that the Gita also says? What does he leave out?
- *Analysis:* He accepts transformation of energy, which is parinama-vada (CC Madhya 6.170). He leaves out the energetic.
  The preacher agrees with the transformation, asks "whose energy?", reads BG 7.4–5 (two energies of "Mine"), and offers the
  touchstone image (6.171). He does not pretend to refute physics; he completes it.
- *Takeaway:* the siddhanta of energies lets a preacher meet a scientist on his own ground and lead him to the energetic.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

When Sanatana Gosvami asked Lord Caitanya "Who am I?", the Lord answered: "It is the living entity's constitutional position to be an eternal servant of Kṛṣṇa because he is the marginal energy of Kṛṣṇa and a manifestation simultaneously one with and different from the Lord, like a molecular particle of sunshine or fire. Kṛṣṇa has three varieties of energy" (CC Madhya 20.108–109). Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura explains, in Srila Prabhupada's purport, that the soul is one with Krishna in quality, being spirit, and different from Him as a very minute particle. Krishna's three energies are the spiritual energy, the living entity energy and the illusory energy (CC Madhya 20.111–112); the Gita calls them the superior energy, the living entities, and the inferior, material energy (BG 7.4–5). The soul is called marginal because he is spiritual by nature but can forget Krishna and live in the material energy, where maya "gives him all kinds of misery" (CC Madhya 20.117).

To Sarvabhauma Bhattacarya the Lord stated the difference exactly: "The Lord is the master of the potencies, and the living entity is the servant of them. That is the difference between the Lord and the living entity" (CC Madhya 6.162). The soul is Krishna's "eternal fragmental" part (BG 15.7); he never becomes the whole, even in liberation, and to seek merging is, in Srila Prabhupada's words, "a kind of spiritual suicide" (CC Madhya 6.169 purport). The material world is not God changed into matter and not an illusion; it is "the transformation of the inconceivable potency of the Supreme Personality of Godhead" (CC Madhya 6.170), as a touchstone makes gold without being changed (6.171). Sankaracarya, to avoid saying God changes, called the world an illusion and so accused Vyasadeva of error (6.172). Krishna pervades everything by His energy and remains a Person, aloof (BG 9.4–5), and within every heart He is the Supersoul, "the overseer and permitter" (BG 13.23), directing the wandering soul (BG 18.61).

Srila Jiva Gosvami's *Paramatma-sandarbha* treats these truths systematically; his own summary says the jivas "are the Lord's marginal potency, who are spiritual in nature" and that their knowledge was covered by illusion because of aversion to the Lord (*Bhakti-sandarbha*, anuccheda 1). For us the lesson is practical: because I am Krishna's energy, I cannot be proud, for I am not the master; and I cannot despair, for I am eternally His. The cure for forgetfulness is to act as what I am, His servant, by chanting, hearing and service.

## S2. Key ideas
- The soul is Krishna's marginal energy: one with Him in quality, different as a minute particle (CC Madhya 20.108–109).
- Krishna has three energies: spiritual, living entity, illusory (CC Madhya 20.111–112; BG 7.4–5).
- The Lord is the master of the potencies; the soul is their servant (CC Madhya 6.162). The soul is eternally a fragment (BG 15.7).
- The world is the transformation of Krishna's energy, not of Krishna; it is real but temporary, not an illusion (CC Madhya 6.170–172).
- Krishna is within everything as the Supersoul and outside everything as the Person (BG 9.4–5; 13.23).

## S3. Verses to remember
- **CC Madhya 20.108–109:** the soul is Krishna's eternal servant, His marginal energy, "simultaneously one with and different from the Lord, like a molecular particle of sunshine or fire." *For me:* I am His, and I am small.
- **CC Madhya 6.162:** "The Lord is the master of the potencies, and the living entity is the servant of them." *For me:* the one-line answer to "I am God".
- **BG 15.7:** the living entities "are My eternal fragmental parts". *For me:* I never stop being myself, and I never stop being His.

## S4. Words to know
- **Sakti:** energy, potency. **Saktiman:** the energetic, the possessor of energy; Krishna.
- **Cit-sakti, jiva-sakti, maya-sakti:** the spiritual, living entity and illusory energies.
- **Tatastha (marginal):** the soul's position, spiritual by nature but able to live in either energy.
- **Acintya-bhedabheda:** inconceivable simultaneous oneness and difference; the Gaudiya conclusion on the soul and God.
- **Parinama-vada:** the teaching that the world is a transformation of the Lord's energy. **Vivarta-vada:** Sankara's theory that the world is an illusion.
- **Paramatma:** the Supersoul, the Lord within every heart.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *The Upanisads say "I am Brahman". Am I God?* You are spirit, Brahman in quality, as a spark is fire; but you are the servant of the potencies, not their master (CC Madhya 20.108–109; 6.162).
2. *Does the soul merge into God at liberation?* No; "He is eternally fragmented" (BG 15.7 purport); seeking to merge is "a kind of spiritual suicide" (CC Madhya 6.169 purport).
3. *Is the world an illusion?* No; it is Krishna's energy transformed, real but temporary. What is illusory is to see it as unrelated to Him (SB 2.9.34 and purport).
4. *If everything is energy, where is God?* Energy belongs to an energetic; the world is the transformation of Krishna's inconceivable potency (CC Madhya 6.170; BG 7.4–5).
5. *How did the soul forget Krishna?* Srila Prabhupada states that the forgetfulness is from time immemorial and comes from the soul's "desire to enjoy himself in competition with Kṛṣṇa" (CC Madhya 20.117 purport). The discussion around this is in dps-43.

## S6. Check your understanding
1. Krishna's three energies are: (a) body, mind, soul (b) spiritual, living entity, illusory (c) past, present, future.
2. In what way is the soul one with Krishna, and in what way different?
3. Complete the sentence from CC Madhya 6.162: The Lord is the master of the potencies, and the living entity is the ________ of them.
4. True or false: at liberation the soul becomes one with God and loses its individuality.
5. What image did Lord Caitanya use for how God produces the world without changing?
6. Short answer: what is the difference between parinama-vada and vivarta-vada?

**Answers:** 1. (b) (CC Madhya 20.111–112). 2. One in quality as spirit; different as a minute particle (CC Madhya 20.108–109).
3. servant. 4. False (BG 15.7 purport). 5. The touchstone that produces gold without being changed (CC Madhya 6.171).
6. Parinama-vada: the world is a transformation of the Lord's energy; vivarta-vada: Sankara's theory that it is an illusion
(CC Madhya 6.170–172).
