# Hearing Srimad-Bhagavatam daily: tasting katha

> **TRD · up to 180 min · review: drafted (30 Oct 2026).** For residents who have sat in the morning class for years.
> How to hear and take notes is VL3-06 and SPS-11; why Srila Prabhupada's books are the basis is SPS-10; why the
> Bhagavatam is the final evidence is TRD-24. This page asks: is the daily class still nourishment, or has it become a
> formality that fills the hour between arati and breakfast?

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on SPS-11 (why hearing is first among the nine processes) and VL3-06 (how to hear and make notes): this page does not teach note-taking or argue that hearing matters; it treats the class as a resident lives it, after the thousandth morning.

A newcomer must be brought to the class. A resident is in the class every day, and that is where the Bhagavatam's own words become urgent: hearing "regularly" is the cause of fixed devotion (SB 1.2.18), and the same text says that hearing from the wrong speaker, or for the wrong subject, gives nothing (SB 10.1.4 purport; 2.1.2). The resident is also, often, the one speaking; Suta Gosvami and Sukadeva Gosvami show what a speaker is.

**Not done here:** note-taking method (VL3-06); the authority of the Bhagavatam as pramana (TRD-24); the content of any one canto (the Bhakti-vaibhava and Bhaktivedanta courses); the ten offences in hearing (DPS-06, TRD-36).

## C. Central message

"Simply by giving aural reception to this Vedic literature, the feeling for loving devotional service to Lord Krsna... sprouts up at once to extinguish the fire of lamentation, illusion and fearfulness" (SB 1.7.7). The daily class is not an item on the timetable; it is the hour in which Krishna Himself, as the Supersoul, "cleanses desire for material enjoyment from the heart of the devotee who has developed the urge to hear" (SB 1.2.17). What we bring is the urge and the question; what we must guard is the mouth we hear from.

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Say from SB 1.2.16–18 and 1.7.7 what regular hearing does and who does it.
2. Name the three things the Bhagavatam asks of a hearer: a real question (SB 1.2.5; 2.1.1), submissive attention (SB 1.1.3 purport), and the right speaker (SB 10.1.4 purport; CC Antya 5.131).
3. Explain why the Bhagavatam does not grow stale after years (SB 1.1.19; 12.13.15) and why "the same points" are not the problem.
4. State what a speaker is, from SB 1.4.1 purport and SB 10.1.4 purport, and what disqualifies him.
5. Choose one change as a hearer and, if they speak, one as a speaker, for the coming month.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**SB 1.2.16–18**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/17/
> 1.2.16: "O twice-born sages, by serving those devotees who are completely freed from all vice, great service is done. By such service, one gains affinity for hearing the messages of Vāsudeva."
> 1.2.17: "Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the Personality of Godhead, who is the Paramātmā [Supersoul] in everyone's heart and the benefactor of the truthful devotee, cleanses desire for material enjoyment from the heart of the devotee who has developed the urge to hear His messages, which are in themselves virtuous when properly heard and chanted."
> 1.2.18: "By regular attendance in classes on the Bhāgavatam and by rendering of service to the pure devotee, all that is troublesome to the heart is almost completely destroyed..."
- **Teaching:** the sequence is service to devotees → affinity for hearing → Krishna cleanses the heart → fixed devotion. The hearer's part is "the urge to hear"; the cleansing is Krishna's.
- **Purport point (1.2.17):** "Messages of the Personality of Godhead, Śrī Kṛṣṇa, are nondifferent from Him. Whenever, therefore, offenseless hearing and glorification of God are undertaken, it is to be understood that Lord Kṛṣṇa is present there in the form of transcendental sound, which is as powerful as the Lord personally."
- **Purport point (1.2.18):** "The messages of the book Bhāgavata, therefore, have to be received from the devotee Bhāgavata, and the combination of these two Bhāgavatas will help the neophyte devotee to make progress on and on."
- **Use:** in the talk; the central block. DPS-15 uses 1.2.18 for steadiness and TRD-09 for taste; here it is the class itself.

**SB 1.7.7; SB 2.2.36; SB 1.8.36; SB 3.9.11**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/7/7/
> 1.7.7: "Simply by giving aural reception to this Vedic literature, the feeling for loving devotional service to Lord Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, sprouts up at once to extinguish the fire of lamentation, illusion and fearfulness."
- **Purport point (1.7.7):** "There are various senses, of which the ear is the most effective. This sense works even when a man is deep asleep... it is definitely suggested herein that simply by hearing the message of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam one gets attachment for the Supreme Personality of Godhead."
- **Teaching:** "it is therefore essential that every human being hear about, glorify and remember the Supreme Lord... always and everywhere" (2.2.36); those who hear and chant His activities "certainly see Your lotus feet" (1.8.36); devotees "see You through the ears by the process of bona fide hearing, and thus their hearts become cleansed, and You take Your seat there" (3.9.11).
- **Use:** in the talk (1.7.7, 3.9.11); background (2.2.36, 1.8.36).

**SB 1.1.2–3; SB 12.13.15, 12.13.18; SB 1.3.43**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/1/3/
> 1.1.3: "O expert and thoughtful men, relish Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, the mature fruit of the desire tree of Vedic literatures... Therefore this fruit has become even more tasteful, although its nectarean juice was already relishable for all, including liberated souls."
- **Purport point (1.1.3):** "patient and submissive hearing is definitely essential. With great respect and attention, one should receive the message and lessons imparted by the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam." The purport then explains that "the highest spiritual realization is knowledge that the Personality of Godhead is the reservoir of all spiritual tastes, or rasas".
- **Teaching:** "As soon as one attentively and submissively hears the message of Bhāgavatam, by this culture of knowledge the Supreme Lord is established within his heart" (1.1.2). "One who has felt satisfaction from its nectarean mellow will never be attracted to any other literature" (12.13.15; BBT translation completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples). Those "who have lost their vision due to the dense darkness of ignorance in the Age of Kali shall get light from this Purāṇa" (1.3.43).
- **Purport point (12.13.18):** "even completely liberated souls are eager to hear and narrate Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam. Those who are trying to be liberated should faithfully serve this literature by hearing and reciting it with faith and devotion."
- **Use:** in the talk (1.1.3, 12.13.15); background.

**SB 1.1.19; SB 1.16.6; SB 10.1.13**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/1/19/
> 1.1.19: "We never tire of hearing the transcendental pastimes of the Personality of Godhead, who is glorified by hymns and prayers. Those who have developed a taste for transcendental relationships with Him relish hearing of His pastimes at every moment."
- **Purport point (1.1.19):** "anyone may read Bhagavad-gītā or the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam repeatedly throughout his whole life and yet find in them new light of information. Mundane news is static whereas transcendental news is dynamic."
- **Teaching:** "The devotees of the Lord are accustomed to licking up the honey available from the lotus feet of the Lord. What is the use of topics which simply waste one's valuable life?" (1.16.6). Pariksit, fasting to death, says that "because I am drinking the nectar of topics about Kṛṣṇa, which is flowing from the lotus mouth of your Lordship, my hunger and thirst... cannot hinder me" (10.1.13).
- **Use:** in the talk (why the class never grows stale).

**SB 1.2.5, 1.2.8; SB 2.1.1–3**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/2/1/1/
> 2.1.1: "My dear King, your question is glorious because it is very beneficial to all kinds of people. The answer to this question is the prime subject matter for hearing, and it is approved by all transcendentalists."
- **Teaching:** The Bhagavatam is spoken in answer to questions: the sages' (1.2.5: "Only questions of this sort are capable of completely satisfying the self") and Pariksit's. The opposite hearer: "Those persons who are materially engrossed... have many subject matters for hearing in human society" (2.1.2), and their life passes "at night either in sleeping or in sex indulgence, and in the daytime either in making money or maintaining family members" (2.1.3). Duties that "do not provoke attraction for the message of the Personality of Godhead" are "only so much useless labor" (1.2.8).
- **Purport point (2.1.1):** "Simply by such questioning and hearing, one can achieve the highest perfectional stage of life... Mahārāja Parīkṣit wanted to absorb his mind completely in Kṛṣṇa, and such absorption can be effected simply by hearing."
- **Purport point (2.1.2):** the *grhamedhi* "lives only for the benefit of the family members" and is "envious of others"; "In the Age of Kali, all the householders are jealous of one another because they are blind to the knowledge of ultimate truth. They have many subject matters for hearing."
- **Application (preacher's):** a resident can be a grhamedhi in hearing: many subjects, no question.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 10.1.4; CC Antya 5.131; SB 1.4.1**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/10/1/4/
> 10.1.4: "Glorification of the Supreme Personality of Godhead is performed in the paramparā system... Such glorification is relished by those no longer interested in the false, temporary glorification of this cosmic manifestation. Descriptions of the Lord are the right medicine for the conditioned soul undergoing repeated birth and death. Therefore, who will cease hearing such glorification of the Lord except a butcher or one who is killing his own self?"
- **Purport point (10.1.4):** "such recitation of Bhagavad-gītā and Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam must be done by persons completely freed from material desires (nivṛtta-tarṣaiḥ)... If we hear the glories of the Supreme Personality of Godhead from liberated persons, this hearing will certainly free us from the bondage of material activities, but hearing Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam spoken by a professional reciter cannot actually help us achieve liberation."
- **CC Antya 5.131** (Svarupa Damodara to the Bengali poet): "If you want to understand Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, you must approach a self-realized Vaiṣṇava and hear from him." *Purport:* "there is now a class of professional Bhāgavatam readers whose means of livelihood is to go from village to village... Nowhere does Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam say that the Bhāgavatam should be heard for one week from professionals. Rather, Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam (1.2.17) says... one should regularly hear Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam from a self-realized Vaiṣṇava."
- **Purport point (1.4.1):** the speaker presents the Bhagavatam "exactly as he heard it... and also realized it personally. Personal realization does not mean that one should, out of vanity, attempt to show one's own learning by trying to surpass the previous ācārya. He must have full confidence in the previous ācārya, and at the same time he must realize the subject matter so nicely that he can present the matter for the particular circumstances in a suitable manner."
- **Use:** in the talk; the speaker's half of the session.

**SB 1.5.10–11; SB 3.25.25**: Verified. Words that do not describe the Lord's glories are "like unto a place of pilgrimage for crows" (1.5.10); literature full of His glories "is a different creation... directed toward bringing about a revolution in the impious lives of this world's misdirected civilization" and "even though imperfectly composed" is "heard, sung and accepted by purified men" (1.5.11). "In the association of pure devotees, discussion of the pastimes and activities of the Supreme Personality of Godhead is very pleasing and satisfying to the ear and the heart" (3.25.25). *Use:* in the talk (1.5.10–11); background (3.25.25).

**BG 10.9–11**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/10/9/
> 10.9: "The thoughts of My pure devotees dwell in Me, their lives are fully devoted to My service, and they derive great satisfaction and bliss from always enlightening one another and conversing about Me."
- **Purport point (10.9):** devotees "take pleasure in discussing Him with other devotees. In the preliminary stage of devotional service they relish the transcendental pleasure from the service itself, and in the mature stage they are actually situated in love of God."
- **Teaching:** to such devotees Krishna gives "the understanding by which they can come to Me" (10.10) and, "dwelling in their hearts", destroys "the darkness born of ignorance" (10.11).
- **Use:** in the talk: the class is *bodhayantaḥ parasparam*, devotees enlightening one another, with Krishna inside.

**CC Madhya 25.278**: Verified. The devotee who "does not taste the transcendental pastimes of Lord Caitanya Mahāprabhu and Kṛṣṇa gradually becomes weak and falls down". *Purport:* devotees "must read all the books that have been translated... otherwise, after some time, they will simply eat, sleep and fall down." *Use:* in the talk (the warning).

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **What the class is.** Not information, not a formality: Krishna "is present there in the form of transcendental sound, which is as powerful as the Lord personally" (SB 1.2.17 purport), and He Himself, as Supersoul, does the cleansing. The ear is "the most effective" sense (1.7.7 purport). The hour is the Lord's work on the heart; our attendance lets Him work.
2. **What the hearer brings.** (a) *A question.* The whole Bhagavatam is spoken to questioners: the sages (1.2.5) and Pariksit (2.1.1). A hearer without a question is a grhamedhi in hearing: "many subject matters for hearing" (2.1.2). (b) *Submissive attention*: "patient and submissive hearing is definitely essential" (1.1.3 purport). (c) *The urge*: "the devotee who has developed the urge to hear" (1.2.17), which itself comes from serving devotees (1.2.16).
3. **Why it never grows stale.** "Mundane news is static whereas transcendental news is dynamic" (1.1.19 purport). The sages had heard it all and said "we never tire". The "same points" complaint mistakes the subject for a lecture topic; the subject is a person, and persons are not exhausted by repetition. The resident who hears the same verse in the tenth year is not the same hearer; the heart has been cleansed in between (1.2.18).
4. **What a speaker is.** One who speaks in parampara, "completely freed from material desires" (10.1.4 and purport), who presents it "exactly as he heard it" and "also realized it personally", never trying "to surpass the previous ācārya" (1.4.1 purport). Prabhupada names the opposite: "a professional reciter" (10.1.4 purport), the Bhagavata business (CC Antya 5.131 purport). A resident who gives class is speaking Prabhupada's purports, not his own thoughts about them.
5. **The warning.** Hearing is the medicine (10.1.4). The one who stops "becomes weak and falls down" (CC Madhya 25.278). The Bhagavatam's own word for the one who ceases hearing is "a butcher or one who is killing his own self" (10.1.4). Given as it is.
6. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *"I know this canto; I can skip."* SB 12.13.18 purport: "even completely liberated souls are eager to hear and narrate Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam."
   - *"Hearing from a recording of Prabhupada is better than our junior speaker."* Prabhupada's recorded classes are hearing from the acharya and are nourishment. But the daily class in the temple, with devotees "enlightening one another" (BG 10.9), is the form Prabhupada established and SB 1.2.18 describes ("regular attendance in classes"); the speaker who repeats the purport faithfully is in parampara. Do both; do not use one to avoid the other.
   - *"The speaker should entertain."* SB 1.5.11: transcendental literature "even though imperfectly composed" is accepted by the honest. The hearer's duty is the question and the attention; the speaker's is faithfulness.
   - *"Questions after class are for newcomers."* Pariksit and the sages were the questioners. A class without a real question has no Bhagavatam in it.
7. **Pitch for TRD.** Half the room gives class. Speak to both roles without separating them: every speaker is a hearer first.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet everyone. Say: "This session is about the hour we all spend every morning. Nobody will be asked to report on anyone's class."
2. Read SB 1.7.7 once, slowly.
3. Ask H.2 question 1 for private writing (two minutes).
4. Ask H.2 question 2; take three answers; thank each by name.
5. Say: "The Bhagavatam was spoken to people who asked. We will start with what they asked, and with what the sound does to the one who hears."

**H.2 Opening questions**
1. (Private) What was this morning's class about? What question did you bring into the room?
2. The sages at Naimisaranya had heard everything and said, "We never tire." What makes a subject tiring, and what makes it fresh?
3. If you give class: what are you trying to do to the hearers in those forty minutes?

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Krishna is in the sound and does the cleansing.** SB 1.2.17 and purport; 1.7.7; 3.9.11. *Example:* the ear works even in sleep (1.7.7 purport). *Transition:* then what is our part?
2. **Our part is the urge and the question.** SB 1.2.16 (service to devotees gives the affinity), 1.2.5, 2.1.1. *Example:* Pariksit's question "approved by all transcendentalists". The grhamedhi has "many subject matters for hearing" (2.1.2). *Transition:* but I have heard it all.
3. **The subject is a person, so it is never finished.** SB 1.1.19 and purport; 12.13.15; Pariksit's fast (10.1.13). *Example:* "mundane news is static". *Transition:* who should speak it?
4. **The speaker: in parampara, free of material desire, faithful to the acharya.** SB 10.1.4 and purport; CC Antya 5.131 and purport; SB 1.4.1 purport. *Example:* the professional reciter and the Bhagavata business, named by Prabhupada. *Transition:* the class as a shared act.
5. **Devotees enlighten one another, and Krishna lights the lamp inside.** BG 10.9–11. *Transition:* and the one who stops?
6. **The one who stops hearing becomes weak and falls.** CC Madhya 25.278 and purport; SB 10.1.4 ("a butcher or one who is killing his own self"). *Transition:* P.

## J. Stories and illustrations

**The sages' questions at Naimisaranya** (SB 1.1.4–23; 1.2.5). The sages, assembled for a thousand-year sacrifice, ask Suta Gosvami what is the ultimate good for people in general, and what Krishna's teachings are now that He has departed. Suta replies: "Your questions are worthy because they relate to Lord Kṛṣṇa and so are of relevance to the world's welfare. Only questions of this sort are capable of completely satisfying the self" (1.2.5). *Lesson:* the Bhagavatam begins with a question; so does every real class.

**Pariksit's seven days** (SB 1.19; 2.1.1; 10.1.13; stories list "pariksit-curse"). Cursed to die in seven days, the king sits on the Ganga bank and asks Sukadeva Gosvami what a man about to die should hear and do. Sukadeva: "your question is glorious because it is very beneficial to all kinds of people" (2.1.1). Later, into the tenth canto, the king says: "Because of my vow on the verge of death, I have given up even drinking water, yet because I am drinking the nectar of topics about Kṛṣṇa... my hunger and thirst, which are extremely difficult to bear, cannot hinder me" (10.1.13). *Lesson:* katha heard with a real question nourishes more than food.

**Vyasa and Narada** (SB 1.5; stories list "vyasa-narada"). Vyasadeva, having compiled the Vedas, is dissatisfied. Narada tells him that words which do not describe the Lord's glories are "a place of pilgrimage for crows" (1.5.10), while literature full of His glories brings "a revolution in the impious lives of this world's misdirected civilization" (1.5.11). Vyasa then compiles the Bhagavatam. *Lesson:* the book we hear each morning exists because its author was told that nothing else satisfies.

**Svarupa Damodara and the poet from Bengal** (CC Antya 5.131 and purport). A poet wrote a drama with wrong conclusions. Svarupa Damodara tells him: "If you want to understand Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, you must approach a self-realized Vaiṣṇava and hear from him." *Lesson:* from whom we hear decides what we understand; Prabhupada's purport names the professional Bhagavata business as the opposite.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"I have heard the same points for ten years. How do I stay interested?"**
   - The sages said "we never tire" after hearing everything (SB 1.1.19); the purport says transcendental news is dynamic. Bring a question (2.1.1); you are not the same hearer you were, since hearing has been cleansing the heart (1.2.18). If still dry, TRD-09.
2. **"Our class speakers are juniors; I learn more from Prabhupada's recorded lectures."**
   - Hear Prabhupada's lectures daily; that is hearing from the acharya. And attend the class: SB 1.2.18 says "regular attendance in classes", BG 10.9 describes devotees "enlightening one another", and a junior who repeats the purport faithfully speaks in parampara (SB 1.4.1 purport). Help him by asking a real question.
3. **"I give class. How much of my own realization should I add?"**
   - SB 1.4.1 purport: present it "exactly as he heard it" and "also realized it personally"; realization "does not mean that one should, out of vanity, attempt to show one's own learning by trying to surpass the previous ācārya." Read the purport; explain it; apply it; do not replace it.
4. **"Is it wrong to leave class early for service?"**
   - Service to devotees is itself one of the two inputs (SB 1.2.18), so an authorised service is not a fault. But CC Madhya 25.278 warns what happens to the one who stops tasting the pastimes. Arrange with your authority to hear the class on most days, or hear the recording the same day.
5. **"What about hearing from speakers outside ISKCON, if they are learned?"**
   - SB 10.1.4 purport: hearing "spoken by a professional reciter cannot actually help us"; CC Madhya 8.200 purport (TRD-13) gives Sanatana Gosvami's rule that talks about Krishna from a non-Vaisnava are like milk touched by a serpent. The question is not learning but parampara and freedom from material desire.
6. **"Does hearing replace reading?"**
   - No. CC Madhya 25.278 purport: devotees "must read all the books that have been translated". Hearing in class and reading alone are the two halves of the book Bhagavata (SPS-10, VL3-05).

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The back row.** Gauranga dasa, twelve years in the ashram, sits in the back of class answering messages for the day's programme. He says he has "heard it all" and that his service is preaching.
- **Dilemma:** What has he stopped doing, and what does the Bhagavatam call it?
- **Principles:** SB 2.1.2 ("many subject matters for hearing"); SB 1.1.19 (never tiring); SB 1.2.18 (regular attendance); CC Madhya 25.278 (the one who stops tasting becomes weak).
- **Questions:** Which hearer in SB 2.1.1–3 does he resemble this morning? What would change if he brought one question?
- **Takeaway:** phone away; one written question before class; one line after class on what the purport answered.

**Case 2: The brilliant speaker.** Nimai dasa gives popular classes full of his own analogies, current affairs and jokes; the purport is read quickly and left. Hearers love him; seniors are uneasy.
- **Principles:** SB 1.4.1 purport (not surpassing the acharya); SB 10.1.4 purport (the professional reciter); SB 1.5.11 (imperfectly composed, but about the Lord).
- **Questions:** What is he giving the hearers, and what is he withholding? How can realization be shown without replacing the purport?
- **Takeaway:** read the purport slowly, explain it, apply it, and let the analogies serve its point, not the speaker's.

3. What did the sages say about hearing Krishna's pastimes repeatedly? *Expected:* "we never tire" (SB 1.1.19).
4. What must the hearer bring, from SB 1.2.5 and 2.1.1? *Expected:* a real question about Krishna.
5. From whom should the Bhagavatam be heard (CC Antya 5.131)? *Expected:* a self-realised Vaisnava; not a professional reciter.
6. What happens to the devotee who stops tasting the pastimes (CC Madhya 25.278)? *Expected:* he becomes weak and falls down.

**M.3 Reflection**

- (Private) Which of the hearers in SB 2.1.1–3 was I this morning?

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Every morning we sit in the Bhagavatam class. After years it can become the hour between arati and breakfast. The Bhagavatam itself says what that hour is. Krishna, "the Paramātmā [Supersoul] in everyone's heart... cleanses desire for material enjoyment from the heart of the devotee who has developed the urge to hear His messages" (SB 1.2.17); Srila Prabhupada's purport says that when the Bhagavatam is heard without offence, "Lord Kṛṣṇa is present there in the form of transcendental sound, which is as powerful as the Lord personally." "Simply by giving aural reception to this Vedic literature, the feeling for loving devotional service to Lord Kṛṣṇa... sprouts up at once to extinguish the fire of lamentation, illusion and fearfulness" (SB 1.7.7). The ear, the purport adds, is "the most effective" sense.

What do we bring? The Bhagavatam was spoken to people who asked: the sages at Naimisaranya, whose questions "relate to Lord Kṛṣṇa and so are of relevance to the world's welfare" (SB 1.2.5), and King Pariksit, whose question Sukadeva called "glorious" (SB 2.1.1). The opposite hearer is the *grhamedhi*, the materially engrossed man who has "many subject matters for hearing" (SB 2.1.2). So we bring a real question and "patient and submissive hearing" (SB 1.1.3 purport). And the class never grows stale: the sages, who had heard everything, said, "We never tire of hearing the transcendental pastimes of the Personality of Godhead" (SB 1.1.19); the purport explains that "mundane news is static whereas transcendental news is dynamic". Pariksit, fasting to death, said that drinking "the nectar of topics about Kṛṣṇa" made his hunger and thirst unable to hinder him (SB 10.1.13).

From whom do we hear? Svarupa Damodara said: "If you want to understand Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, you must approach a self-realized Vaiṣṇava and hear from him" (CC Antya 5.131); the purport names the opposite, the professional Bhagavatam reader. The speaker presents the text "exactly as he heard it" and realised it, never trying "to surpass the previous ācārya" (SB 1.4.1 purport). Devotees "derive great satisfaction and bliss from always enlightening one another and conversing about Me" (BG 10.9), and Krishna, inside their hearts, destroys the darkness of ignorance (BG 10.11). The warning is also given: the devotee who stops tasting the pastimes "gradually becomes weak and falls down" (CC Madhya 25.278), and the Bhagavatam asks, "who will cease hearing such glorification of the Lord except a butcher or one who is killing his own self?" (SB 10.1.4).

## S2. Key ideas

- In the class Krishna is present as sound and Himself cleanses the heart (SB 1.2.17 and purport).
- Devotion sprouts "at once" from hearing; the ear is the most effective sense (SB 1.7.7).
- The hearer brings a real question and submissive attention (SB 1.2.5; 2.1.1; 1.1.3 purport).
- The subject is a person, so it never grows stale: "we never tire" (SB 1.1.19).
- Hear from a self-realised Vaisnava in parampara, not a professional reciter (CC Antya 5.131; SB 10.1.4 purport).
- The one who stops hearing becomes weak and falls (CC Madhya 25.278).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **SB 1.7.7:** "Simply by giving aural reception to this Vedic literature, the feeling for loving devotional service to Lord Kṛṣṇa... sprouts up at once." *For me:* the class is where love for Krishna begins each day.
- **SB 1.2.17:** Krishna "cleanses desire for material enjoyment from the heart of the devotee who has developed the urge to hear His messages." *For me:* my part is the urge; the cleansing is His.
- **SB 1.1.19:** "We never tire of hearing the transcendental pastimes of the Personality of Godhead." *For me:* when I am bored, the problem is in me, not in the subject.
- **CC Antya 5.131:** "If you want to understand Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, you must approach a self-realized Vaiṣṇava and hear from him." *For me:* whom I hear from decides what I understand.

## S4. Words to know

- **Sravanam:** hearing about Krishna, the first of the nine processes of devotional service.
- **Katha (krsna-katha):** topics about Krishna; "the nectar of topics about Kṛṣṇa" (SB 10.1.13).
- **Grhamedhi:** one who lives only for family and sense enjoyment and has "many subject matters for hearing" (SB 2.1.2).
- **Parampara:** the chain of teachers through which the Bhagavatam is spoken (SB 10.1.4).
- **Bhagavata:** both the book and the devotee who lives by it (SB 1.2.18 purport).

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **I have heard the same points for years. How do I stay interested?** The sages who had heard everything said "we never tire" (SB 1.1.19). Bring one real question to class (SB 2.1.1), and remember that you are not the same hearer: hearing has been cleansing your heart all these years (SB 1.2.18).
2. **Is hearing Prabhupada's recorded lectures enough?** Hear them daily, and also attend the class: SB 1.2.18 speaks of "regular attendance in classes", and devotees "enlightening one another" is Krishna's own description (BG 10.9).
3. **If I give class, how much of my own ideas may I add?** Present it "exactly as he heard it" and realised it, without trying "to surpass the previous ācārya" (SB 1.4.1 purport). Explain and apply the purport; do not replace it.
4. **May I leave class early for service?** Service to devotees is one of the two inputs (SB 1.2.18), so authorised service is not a fault; but arrange to hear the class on most days, since the one who stops tasting the pastimes becomes weak (CC Madhya 25.278).
5. **Can I hear the Bhagavatam from any learned speaker?** Hear from a self-realised Vaisnava in parampara (CC Antya 5.131); Prabhupada says hearing from "a professional reciter cannot actually help us" (SB 10.1.4 purport).

## S6. Check your understanding

1. According to SB 1.2.17, who cleanses the heart of the hearer, and what is the hearer's part?
2. Which sense does the SB 1.7.7 purport call the most effective, and why?
3. What did the sages say about hearing Krishna's pastimes again and again (SB 1.1.19)?
4. What kind of hearer is the grhamedhi (SB 2.1.2)?
5. From whom did Svarupa Damodara say the Bhagavatam must be heard (CC Antya 5.131)?
6. True or false: a speaker should try to improve on the previous acharya's explanation.

**Answers:** 1. Krishna as the Supersoul; the hearer's part is "the urge to hear" (SB 1.2.17). 2. The ear; it "works even when a man is deep asleep" (SB 1.7.7 purport). 3. "We never tire" (SB 1.1.19). 4. One who has "many subject matters for hearing", blind to the ultimate truth (SB 2.1.2). 5. A self-realised Vaisnava (CC Antya 5.131). 6. False: he must not "out of vanity, attempt to show one's own learning by trying to surpass the previous ācārya" (SB 1.4.1 purport).
