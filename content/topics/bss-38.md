## B. Why this topic belongs at this level
A brahmacari usually lives with other men in one building, and bathes, changes and sleeps near them. Small habits in these moments either protect the mind or disturb it. BIKC chapter 28 gives plain, practical advice. This page explains the reason for that advice from the Srimad-Bhagavatam, so that modesty is understood as the Bhagavatam and BIKC give it.

## C. Central message
In the Bhagavatam, "to live naked or almost naked is uncivilized" (SB 9.14.23 purport), and the sons of Kuvera show what happens when modesty is lost through intoxication. A brahmacari keeps himself covered at all times, because, as BIKC (ch. 28) says, "To see the private parts of the body is not only vulgar, but tends to stimulate unwanted desires", so that his mind stays free for chanting and service.

## D. Learning outcomes
1. Explain in two sentences why SB 9.14.23 and its purport treat modesty as part of higher culture.
2. Retell the story of Nalakuvara and Manigriva and end it with its devotional lesson (SB 10.10.38); retell Pururava and Urvasi (SB 9.14.30-39) as the verses tell it, including Urvasi's words (SB 9.14.36-38) with their purports.
3. Describe three habits of modesty from BIKC and say how each can be adapted where you live.
4. Explain from SB 1.4.5 why a beginner should not imitate Sukadeva Gosvami's freedom from body-consciousness.
5. Say where to go for help if privacy, pressure or distress are a problem.

## F. Philosophical explanation
**F.1 What BIKC says (ch. 28), in its own words.** "Nakedness is not at all appreciated in Vedic culture. (SB 9.14.22-3) The private parts should remain unexposed at all times. A brahmacārī should as much as possible avoid seeing even his own genitals. To see the private parts of the body is not only vulgar, but tends to stimulate unwanted desires." "Even if alone in a room, one should not for a moment be exposed. That includes while bathing, while changing, and even while sleeping." BIKC also reports a remark of Srila Prabhupada, spoken to Upendra dasa in 1976: "To see oneself naked is the beginning of madness." That remark has not been located in a published source, so it is given as BIKC's report (**Requires source review** for the original), not quoted as Prabhupada's words on its own.

**F.2 What the Bhagavatam shows.** Three scenes teach the point. First, Urvasi asks for a standard of privacy and the king agrees (SB 9.14.22-23); the purport says "To live naked or almost naked is uncivilized". Second, when that standard breaks, the king is thrown into distress, wanders like a madman, and hears from Urvasi herself what the nature of a woman is and why he should not be controlled by his senses (SB 9.14.30-38, with the purports). Third, the sons of Kuvera lose modesty, drunk, proud and attached to women, and Narada, out of mercy, wakes them (SB 10.10.5-19).

**F.3 Why a celibate student keeps to this.** Chapters bss-21 to bss-23 show that the mind is moved by what the eyes see. BIKC (ch. 28) gives the reason in one sentence: seeing the private parts "tends to stimulate unwanted desires". Covering the body is a simple, daily way of not feeding that movement.

**F.4 How BIKC describes the change.** BIKC (ch. 28) says: "Those from a Western background may find this all rather peculiar at first. But to remain covered is not at all difficult—one just has to become aware of the necessity." It compares it to becoming a vegetarian: "so to one who learns to cover himself up, not doing so, even for a few seconds while changing, seems uncouth." If a person is in distress about his body, a trusted senior or counsellor can help.

**F.5 Medical care and ordinary life.** Nothing here applies to a medical examination, an emergency, the care of a baby or a sick person, or a swimming lesson run by a qualified person. In these cases, the purpose is health and safety, and a modest approach (a gown, a screen, a person of the right gender) is a courtesy that devotees can ask for politely.

**F.6 Beginners and Sukadeva.** SB 1.4.5 is easy to misuse. The women's reply is about Sukadeva's vision, which is free from the idea of male and female. Prabhupada's purport says that as long as we still see the distinction, we should not try to imitate him. So modesty is not a lesser stage that we outgrow by deciding to; it is the correct behaviour until the vision of Sukadeva is real (Application (preacher's)).

**F.7 Women in the two stories, as the verses give them.** In SB 10.10.6 the young women of the demigods covered themselves at once when the sage appeared, "afraid of being cursed", and the two sons of Kuvera, "not caring about Nārada", remained naked; SB 10.10.19 names their state as drunk, senses uncontrolled, proud and "attached to women". In SB 9.14.36-38 Urvasi, "who was a woman herself and who knew the nature of women" (9.14.36 purport), tells the king that "the heart of a woman is like that of a fox", that "women as a class are merciless and cunning", and that "women are very easily seduced by men"; the purports say why (E). Read both as they stand; the purport to 9.14.36 also says that "on the spiritual platform ... whether one is a man, woman, śūdra or whatever, everyone is equal."

**F.8 Practical habits from BIKC.** BIKC (ch. 28) says: "While bathing, wear a gāmcha. Simply a kaupīna is insufficient." It describes changing with "a dhoti or gāmcha wrapped around the waist until the cloth you’re changing into is in position", and says "while sleeping wear at least kaupīnas and gāmcha. Gāmchas should be at least knee length, for the sake of decency." It adds that "millions of Indians still bathe like this every day—the majority of them outdoors" and "never expose their private parts, even to the elements." Where a gamchha is not available, a long towel or wrap serves the same purpose. If your housing allows no privacy at all (a shared dormitory with open showers, for example), do not hide your difficulty. Tell a senior so that the arrangement can be changed. It is the responsibility of the ashram, not of one student, to provide a decent arrangement.

**F.9 Safety.** If anyone is pressured, filmed, touched or made to undress against his will, or if a minor is involved, that is a safeguarding and legal matter, not a matter of self-control. Report it to the temple's child-protection or safeguarding officer and, where the law requires, to the police. Pastoral guidance.

## H. Opening and engagement
**H.1 How to start.** (1) Say in a calm voice: "Today we speak about modesty. It is an ordinary topic, and nobody here has to say anything about himself." (2) Say: "If you have a problem with privacy where you live, please tell me or a senior after the session; it is the community's job to fix it." (3) Read SB 10.10.6 slowly. (4) Ask H.2.

**H.2 Opening questions.**
1. In your home or village, how do people bathe or change when many live together? (Easy; the answers vary by country and are all welcome.)
2. Why do you think almost every culture has some rule of modesty?
3. What do you think the sage Narada did when he saw the scene in SB 10.10.6?

## I. Core teaching points
1. **Modesty is part of higher culture.** SB 9.14.22-23 ("To live naked or almost naked is uncivilized"). Then J.2: the king goes out naked, Urvasi leaves, and when he finds her she tells him the nature of a woman and not to be controlled by his senses (SB 9.14.30-38, with the purports read as they are). 2. **The sons of Kuvera.** SB 10.10.5-19: the cause is intoxication, pride and attachment to women, and the cure is mercy. Transition: "What was the mercy for?"
3. **Mercy leads to devotion.** SB 10.10.27, 37, 38, 42. Transition: "But is covering not just a bodily idea?"
4. **Sukadeva and the beginner.** SB 1.4.5 and purport. Transition: "How do I do this where I live?"
5. **Daily habits and help.** F.8, F.9, and the help note.

## J. Stories and illustrations
**J.1 Nalakuvara and Manigriva (SB 10.10; verses used: 5, 6, 7, 19, 27, 37, 38, 42).** Two sons of the demigod Kuvera are proud of their wealth and drunk with a liquor (SB 10.10.19), and young women of the demigods are with them (SB 10.10.6). The sage Narada arrives by chance (SB 10.10.5). The young women are ashamed and cover themselves; the two brothers do not (SB 10.10.6). Narada, out of mercy, decides to give them a curse that will cure their pride (SB 10.10.7, 10.10.19). He cures them by a curse that makes them two arjuna trees. Later the child Krishna, tied to a wooden mortar, drags the mortar and uproots the two trees (SB 10.10.27). Two shining persons come out of the fallen trees and offer prayers (SB 10.10.28). They say they are servants of Krishna's servants, especially of Narada, and they ask that their words, ears, hands, legs, senses and minds be engaged in Krishna's service (SB 10.10.37-38). Krishna says that they will never fall from the platform of devotion (SB 10.10.42).
**Devotional lesson:** the curse was a gift. The lack of modesty came from pride and intoxication; the cure was the mercy of a devotee and the Lord's own touch. This is the model of how a fallen person is raised: not with scorn but with love. A student who feels embarrassed by his own past should remember it. (Use no detail that is not in the verses.)

**J.2 King Pururava and Urvasi (SB 9.14.30-39).** Pururava had promised not to be seen naked (SB 9.14.22). One night, angered by Urvasi's sharp words about the stolen lambs, he went out in anger, "not even dressing himself properly" (SB 9.14.30). The Gandharvas lit the house with lightning, Urvasi saw him naked, and she left (SB 9.14.31). Then, "because of his great attraction for her", he travelled "like a madman" (SB 9.14.32). He found her at Kuruksetra on the bank of the Sarasvati and begged her, "O my dear wife, O most cruel one, kindly stay, kindly stay" (SB 9.14.33-34), saying that if she refused, his body would fall and be eaten by foxes and vultures (9.14.35). Urvasi answered: "Be sober and don’t allow the senses to overcome you like foxes ... you should know that the heart of a woman is like that of a fox. There is no use making friendship with women" (9.14.36); "Women as a class are merciless and cunning ... they do not fear killing even a faithful husband or brother" (9.14.37); "Women are very easily seduced by men ... they seek newer and newer friends, one after another" (9.14.38). Read the purports with them (E): Urvasi "frankly explained the nature of a woman" because the king "was wasting his rarely achieved human form of life" (9.14.37 purport); "If a man cannot control his senses, he becomes a victim of such sly foxes. But if one can control the senses, there is no chance of his being victimized" (9.14.36 purport); "unless women are trained to be chaste and faithful to their husbands, there cannot be peace or prosperity in society" (9.14.37 purport); "A woman must always be protected, either by her father, by her husband, or by her elderly son" (9.14.38 purport). Still attached, he accepted her offer of one night a year (9.14.39). **Lesson:** as the purport to 9.14.36 ends, "One should go to the forest and take shelter of the lotus feet of Hari". SB 11.26 (bss-32, bss-53) tells how he later came to his senses. The devotional answer is the one in the Nalakuvara story: to engage the senses and mind in Krishna's service.

**J.3 Everyday image (Application (preacher's)).** A farmer who carries a small oil lamp across a windy field cups his hand around the flame. He does not hate the wind. He protects the flame so that it can keep giving light. In the same way, our modest habits are a hand cupped around our attention on the holy name. The Krishna conscious point is the prayer of SB 10.10.38: that our minds may always think of the Lord's lotus feet.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections
- *Does this mean the body is dirty?* The Bhagavatam speaks of the body as it is: King Pururava, when he came to his senses, called it "this polluted body ... so filthy and full of bad odors" (SB 11.26.18; bss-32). The body is to be engaged in the Lord's service (SB 10.10.38). If you are in distress about your body, speak to a senior or counsellor.
- *My culture or climate has different habits. Is it wrong?* The purport to SB 9.14.23 says: "To live naked or almost naked is uncivilized, but on this planet earth it has now become fashionable to dress half naked." BIKC (ch. 28) says that those from a Western background "may find this all rather peculiar at first. But to remain covered is not at all difficult." Keep covered with whatever cloth you have.
- *Is it fanatical to cover even when alone?* BIKC (ch. 28) says: "Even if alone in a room, one should not for a moment be exposed." If it feels hard, begin with bathing and changing, and tell your senior how it goes.
- *If we are not the body, why does covering matter?* The Bhagavatam answers in SB 1.4.5's purport: until we see no difference between male and female, we should not try to imitate Sukadeva.
- *Some verses in the Bhagavatam say harsh things about women. Are they part of this lesson?* Yes. SB 9.14.36-38 are Urvasi's words to Pururava, and the page reads them with their purports (E, J.2). The purport to 9.14.37 gives the reason: "considering that the King was wasting his rarely achieved human form of life, Urvaśī frankly explained the nature of a woman." The purport to 9.14.36 adds that on the spiritual platform "whether one is a man, woman, śūdra or whatever, everyone is equal."
- *We have no privacy in our building. What can I do?* Tell a senior; the community should arrange screens, doors or schedules. You are not at fault.
- *I once saw something that disturbed me and I feel upset.* That is common and is not a sin. Chant, and speak to a trusted senior or a qualified counsellor. In India, Tele-MANAS 14416 offers free support; elsewhere use your local crisis service.

## L. Case studies
**L.1 Invented case for discussion.** *Situation:* Daniel lives in a shared house of six devotees. There is one bathroom with a broken lock, and two roommates often walk in and out while others change. *Dilemma:* he is shy and thinks it is petty to complain. *Principles:* SB 10.10.6 (modesty), SB 9.14.23 purport (a standard of culture), F.8 (the ashram's responsibility). *Questions:* (a) What is the real problem? (b) Whom should he tell and what should he say? (c) What can the house do together? *Analysis:* the broken lock is a fault of the house, not of Daniel. He can say to the senior in charge, "The bathroom lock is broken. Could we fix it this week and agree to knock before entering?" The house can agree on a simple rule and a time schedule. *Takeaway:* modesty is a shared habit, and a polite request is the right way to start it.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary
A brahmacari lives close to other men and keeps himself covered at all times. BIKC (ch. 28) says: "To see the private parts of the body is not only vulgar, but tends to stimulate unwanted desires." The Srimad-Bhagavatam shows why. In SB 9.14.22 the heavenly woman Urvasi asks that she not see the king unclothed, and Prabhupada's purport on the next verse says: "To live naked or almost naked is uncivilized." When the king went out naked, she left him, and he wandered like a madman (SB 9.14.30-32). When he found her and begged her to stay, she told him: "Be sober and don’t allow the senses to overcome you like foxes ... the heart of a woman is like that of a fox. There is no use making friendship with women" (SB 9.14.36), and that "women as a class are merciless and cunning" and "very easily seduced by men" (9.14.37-38). The purport says she "frankly explained the nature of a woman" because he "was wasting his rarely achieved human form of life", and that "if one can control the senses, there is no chance of his being victimized" (9.14.36-37 purports). In SB 10.10, the two sons of the demigod Kuvera, drunk and proud, did not cover themselves when the sage Narada came, though the young women there did (SB 10.10.6). Narada, out of mercy, cursed them to become trees. When Krishna freed them, they prayed to engage all their senses in His service, and the Lord told them they would never fall from devotion (SB 10.10.37-42).

Some people say "we are not the body, so covering does not matter". In SB 1.4.5, the sage Sukadeva passed women who were bathing, and they did not cover themselves, because they saw that he made no difference between male and female. Prabhupada's purport says that as long as we still see the distinction, we should not try to behave like Sukadeva. So we keep modesty as a protection for our chanting.

BIKC says: "While bathing, wear a gāmcha", change with a cloth wrapped around the waist, and "while sleeping wear at least kaupīnas and gāmcha"; "Even if alone in a room, one should not for a moment be exposed." A long towel or wrap serves where there is no gamchha. If you have no privacy where you live, tell a senior; the community should fix it. If you feel shame or distress, talk to a senior or a qualified counsellor (Tele-MANAS 14416 in India, or your local crisis service). If anyone is forced, filmed or touched against his will, tell the safeguarding officer of the temple.

## S2. Key ideas
- Modesty protects the mind that wants to chant: seeing the private parts "tends to stimulate unwanted desires" (BIKC ch. 28).
- Urvasi told Pururava not to be controlled by his senses and that there is no use making friendship with women (SB 9.14.36).
- The sons of Kuvera lost modesty through pride, intoxication and attachment to women; Narada's mercy cured them.
- Beginners should not imitate the vision of Sukadeva; they should keep the rules of decency.
- Where privacy is not possible, tell a senior; this is the community's duty.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **SB 9.14.36**: "Be sober and don’t allow the senses to overcome you like foxes ... There is no use making friendship with women." *For me: if I control my senses, the purport says, there is no chance of being victimized.*
- **SB 10.10.6**: the women covered themselves, but the sons of Kuvera did not. *For me: pride and intoxication destroy the natural sense of modesty.*
- **SB 10.10.38**: "may our minds always think of Your lotus feet". *For me: the aim of all control is to engage the senses in Krishna's service.*
- **SB 1.4.5**: Sukadeva's purified vision. *For me: I do not yet have that vision, so I keep modesty.*

## S4. Words to know
- **Modesty:** keeping the private parts of the body covered.
- **Gamchha:** a thin cotton cloth used as a towel and wrap.
- **Brahmacari:** a celibate student in devotional training.
- **Mercy:** kindness that helps a person who does not deserve it.

## S5. Questions and answers
- *Is the body dirty?* King Pururava, when sane, called it "polluted ... so filthy and full of bad odors" (SB 11.26.18); it is to be engaged in the Lord's service (SB 10.10.38).
- *Is this the same everywhere?* The purport to SB 9.14.23 says "To live naked or almost naked is uncivilized", and BIKC says to remain covered "is not at all difficult". Use whatever cloth you have.
- *What if I have no privacy?* Tell a senior. The ashram should arrange a decent setup.
- *Why not copy Sukadeva?* He saw no male and female; we still do (SB 1.4.5 purport).
- *Where can I get help if I feel upset?* A senior, a counsellor, or a crisis line.
