# BG and Art of Work

> **VL1 · 45–60 min · review: sources checked, awaiting senior review.** A relevance talk for students and young professionals. Scripture light
> but exact: six Gita verses carry the talk, the rest are for the preacher's questions.

## A. Topic overview

| | |
|---|---|
| Batch / level | VL1 (Vedic Level 1), a stand-alone talk |
| Suggested duration | 45–60 min |
| Preparation time | About 90 min, the first time |
| Difficulty | Introductory |
| Prerequisites | None |
| Audience | Students facing exams and placements; young professionals under targets, deadlines and appraisal pressure; many with no settled faith |
| Teaching objective | Show that the Bhagavad-gita speaks directly about work: how to work hard without being eaten by anxiety about results, and how ordinary work can become something more than earning. Leave the listener with one practical experiment |

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Work is the one subject every student and young professional is already thinking about. The Gita was spoken
on a battlefield to a man about to do the hardest work of his life, and its advice is practical before it
is theological. At VL1 the aim is **attraction through usefulness**: the listener should go home with a
better way to face Monday morning, and curiosity about where the idea came from.

IYF's own slides for this topic (VOICE, "Bhagavad gita and Art of Work", 2007) spend their first half on
the world-view (who am I, the soul, reincarnation) and their second half on "how to perform work": work
with the knowledge that everything belongs to God; do your best and leave the rest to God; work with
knowledge of the goal of life; give up harmful professions; remember God by chanting; dovetail your talents;
proactive behaviour. This page keeps that spine but **leads with work** and lets the world-view come in
only where the work-advice needs it. For a VL1 room, "Who am I?" is a better *second* talk than a first
ten minutes.

How the subject grows at other levels:

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **VL1** (here) | Work without anxiety about results (BG 2.47–48); work as an offering (BG 9.27, 18.46); the five factors of any result (BG 18.14); one experiment |
| VL2 | Karma as a system: action, forbidden action, inaction (BG 4.17–18); why results bind (BG 3.9) |
| VL3 | Work as a daily practice: one task offered each morning |
| SPS | Work as devotional service: dovetailing one's nature and profession (BG 18.47) with guidance |
| DPS | Leading others at work without being driven by targets |
| BSS | Service as the brahmacari's work; effort without claiming the fruit |

**Not done here:**
- A full theory of karma and rebirth. Say "action and reaction" once and move on.
- Lists of sinful activities and their punishments. IYF's slides include the "four pillars" and lower
  species; at VL1 say only that work which harms others or oneself cannot be offered (point 4 in I).
- Asking the audience to chant. Chanting is *mentioned* as the method many use to remember God at work; it
  is not pressed.

## C. Central message

Krsna tells Arjuna: you have a right to your work, not to its results (BG 2.47). Work done with full effort
and without anxiety about the outcome, offered to something higher than yourself, is "the art of all work"
(BG 2.50). It makes you better at your job and steadier inside it.

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. State the difference between *effort*, which is in my hands, and *result*, which depends on five factors
   (BG 2.47; BG 18.14), and give one example from their own study or job.
2. Explain "yoga is the art of all work" (BG 2.50) in plain words: evenness in success and failure
   (BG 2.48) is a skill, not indifference.
3. Describe how the same task can be done as a burden, as a bargain, or as an offering (BG 9.27; BG 18.46).
4. Pick one task for the coming week and do it as an experiment in this way.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** six references are enough for the talk. The rest prepare you for questions.

**BG 2.47**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/47/
> "You have a right to perform your prescribed duty, but you are not entitled to the fruits of action. Never consider yourself the cause of the results of your activities, and never be attached to not doing your duty."
- **Context:** Arjuna wants to leave the battlefield. Krsna has established that the self is not the body
  (2.13–30) and now turns to how to act.
- **Teaching:** Four instructions in one verse: do your duty; do not claim the result; do not think of
  yourself as the cause of the results; do not use "detachment" as an excuse for not working.
- **Purport point:** Prabhupada distinguishes prescribed duty, "capricious work" and inaction, and says
  "Inaction is sinful." Attachment to the result makes one the "enjoyer or sufferer" of it; attachment to
  *not* acting is "another side of attachment".
- **Application (preacher's):** Both the workaholic who lives for the result and the student who gives up
  because "it won't matter anyway" are breaking this verse. The middle is full effort, open hands.
- **Use:** in the talk; the anchor verse.

**BG 2.48**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/48/
> "Perform your duty equipoised, O Arjuna, abandoning all attachment to success or failure. Such equanimity is called yoga."
- **Teaching:** Yoga is defined here not as a posture but as evenness while working.
- **Purport point:** "Yoga means to concentrate the mind upon the Supreme by controlling the
  ever-disturbing senses." Because Krsna Himself asks Arjuna to fight, "Gain or victory are Kṛṣṇa's
  concern".
- **Application (preacher's):** The person who stays level after a bad result *and* after a good one is
  doing yoga whether or not they use the word.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 2.50**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/50/
> "A man engaged in devotional service rids himself of both good and bad reactions even in this life. Therefore strive for yoga, which is the art of all work."
- **Teaching:** The phrase that gives the topic its name. The Sanskrit is *yogaḥ karmasu kauśalam*:
  skill, or art, in action.
- **Purport point:** Arjuna "is therefore advised to act in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, the purifying process of
  resultant action."
- **Application (preacher's):** "Art" here is not decoration; it is mastery. A master craftsman is absorbed
  in the work, not in the applause.
- **Use:** in the talk (title verse).

**BG 18.14**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/14/
> "The place of action [the body], the performer, the various senses, the many different kinds of endeavor, and ultimately the Supersoul – these are the five factors of action."
- **Teaching:** Any result has five contributors; my effort is one of them.
- **Purport point:** The soul is "the doer", the senses are the instruments, "but all one's activities
  depend on the will of the Supersoul".
- **Application (preacher's):** This is the scriptural basis for IYF's slogan "Do your best and leave the
  rest." Only one of the five factors is fully mine to decide: the effort.
- **Use:** in the talk (with the diagram in N).

**BG 9.27**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/9/27/
> "Whatever you do, whatever you eat, whatever you offer or give away, and whatever austerities you perform – do that, O son of Kuntī, as an offering to Me."
- **Teaching:** Ordinary activity becomes an offering by a change of intention, not a change of job.
- **Purport point:** "Everyone has to work for maintenance of his body and soul together, and Kṛṣṇa
  recommends herein that one should work for Him."
- **Application (preacher's):** Present this as an invitation ("here is what the Gita proposes"), not as a
  rule. At VL1 the word "offering" can be explored with a non-religious analogy first: work done for
  someone you love feels different from the same work done for a pay-slip.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 18.46**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/46/
> "By worship of the Lord, who is the source of all beings and who is all-pervading, a man can attain perfection through performing his own work."
- **Teaching:** Nobody has to abandon their profession to reach the goal of life. One's own work, done as
  worship of the Lord, is the means.
- **Purport point:** "In whatever occupation one may be engaged, if he serves the Supreme Lord he will
  achieve the highest perfection." One should think "that he is engaged in a particular type of occupation
  by Hṛṣīkeśa, the master of the senses."
- **Application (preacher's):** This is IYF's point 6, "dovetail your talents and profession". An
  engineer, a nurse and a designer each have a way to do this; the talk asks each listener what theirs is.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**For questions (K):**
- **BG 3.8** (Verified): "Perform your prescribed duty, for doing so is better than not working. One cannot
  even maintain one's physical body without work." Against "so should I renounce my job?"
- **BG 3.9** (Verified): work done as a sacrifice for Visnu must be performed, "otherwise work causes
  bondage". Purport: "This is the great art of doing work, and in the beginning this process requires very
  expert guidance." Useful for "why does the result bind me?"
- **BG 4.17** (Verified): "The intricacies of action are very hard to understand. Therefore one should
  know properly what action is, what forbidden action is and what inaction is." IYF's slide on *karma,
  vikarma, akarma* rests on this verse; the purport names "vikarmas, or prohibited actions".
- **BG 4.18** (Verified): "One who sees inaction in action and action in inaction is intelligent among
  men". Purport: "Akarma means without reaction to work."
- **BG 5.10** (Verified): one who works "without attachment, surrendering the results unto the Supreme
  Lord, is unaffected by sinful action, as the lotus leaf is untouched by water."
- **BG 18.47** (Verified): "It is better to engage in one's own occupation, even though one may perform it
  imperfectly, than to accept another's occupation and perform it perfectly." For career-comparison
  questions.
- **BG 4.22** (Verified): "He who is satisfied with gain which comes of its own accord… who is steady in
  both success and failure, is never entangled, although performing actions." Purport: such a person
  "labors honestly as far as is in his power, and is satisfied with whatever is obtained by his own honest
  labor." Guards against reading "leave the rest" as laziness.
- **BG 2.40** (Verified): "In this endeavor there is no loss or diminution, and a little advancement on
  this path can protect one from the most dangerous type of fear." Purport: "One percent done in Kṛṣṇa
  consciousness bears permanent results". For "what if I try this and stop?"
- **BG 4.33** (Verified): "the sacrifice performed in knowledge is better than the mere sacrifice of
  material possessions." IYF uses it for "work with knowledge of the goal of life"; the verse itself is
  about kinds of sacrifice, so cite it carefully as background only.
- **BG 2.14** (Verified): "one must learn to tolerate them without being disturbed." For IYF's point 7,
  "proactive behaviour: tolerate joys and sorrows".

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** The Gita separates two things we usually fuse: the *work* and the *result*. The work
   is my right and my responsibility (BG 2.47). The result is produced by five factors, of which my effort
   is one (BG 18.14). Evenness toward the result, while working fully, is what the Gita calls yoga
   (BG 2.48) and "the art of all work" (BG 2.50). The second move is intention: the same work done "as an
   offering" (BG 9.27) is no longer only a transaction, and a person can reach the goal of life "through
   performing his own work" (BG 18.46).
2. **Why it matters.** Anxiety about results is the main tax on young working people: before the exam,
   after the interview, at the appraisal. The Gita's answer is not "care less" but "put your care where
   it works": in the effort. A mind not spent on the result has more for the task.
3. **Connections.** It opens on to karma (why results bind, BG 3.9; the three kinds of action, BG 4.17–18)
   at VL2, and on to devotional service, where offering becomes a relationship, at SPS/DPS. "Positive
   thinking" (VL1-11) and "Art of living" (VL1-13) continue the practical thread.
4. **Consequences.** People who work this way are, in experience, more reliable (they do not slacken when
   the reward is uncertain), more honest (the result is not everything, so they need not cheat for it) and
   calmer. This is a claim about *how it tends to go*, not a promise of success.
5. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - **"Detachment means not caring."** BG 2.47 forbids attachment to *not* working; BG 3.8 says one cannot
     even maintain the body without work. The purport to BG 4.22 describes honest labour "as far as is in
     his power".
   - **"Leave the rest to God" as fatalism.** The five factors include my effort and the senses I bring; the
     verse does not remove responsibility, it right-sizes it.
   - **"Offering" as a ritual tacked on.** BG 9.27 is about the spirit of the whole day's work, not a
     formula said at the start.
   - **Prosperity promise.** Nothing here says devotion brings promotion. BG 4.22 praises satisfaction
     "with gain which comes of its own accord".
6. **Pitch for VL1.** Say "effort and result", "evenness", "offering". The Sanskrit *yogaḥ karmasu
   kauśalam* may be said once, because it is memorable; *karma* once, defined as "action and its
   reaction". Present Krsna as the speaker of the Gita without arguing His divinity; the advice stands on
   its own at this level and the question "who is speaking?" is a VL2 door.

## G. Lecture flow

**Main plan (45 min)**

| Time | Segment | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | Opening: "the week before the result" (H) | Hands up, no sharing required |
| 5–12 | Two ways to fail at work: the workaholic and the quitter | Invented sketches (H); both are forms of attachment (BG 2.47 purport) |
| 12–22 | The anchor: BG 2.47 read slowly, four instructions | Write the four on the board; BG 2.48 "evenness is yoga" |
| 22–30 | Why the result is not mine: the five factors (BG 18.14) | Diagram in N; "do your best, leave the rest" |
| 30–38 | The second move: the same work as an offering (BG 9.27, BG 18.46) | Analogy first (work for someone you love), then the verses; IYF's "dovetail your talents" |
| 38–42 | "Yoga is the art of all work" (BG 2.50): one sentence to take home | Mention that many who practise this remember God through chanting at work; no pressure |
| 42–52 | Activity (M.2) | Pairs, then two shares |
| 52–60 | Summary + one experiment + invitation | P |

**Shorter (45 min):** skip the activity and the second-move analogy; or drop the "two ways to fail" sketches and shorten the five factors to a two-minute
drawing.

**Alternative structure (workshop):** give each table one task card (a group project with a free-rider; a
job interview; a boring data-entry shift; a startup pitch). Ask: what is the effort here, what is the
result, and which parts of the result are outside your hands? Collect answers, then show BG 2.47 and
BG 18.14 as the Gita's version of what they just worked out. End with BG 9.27 as the "second move".

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet, say your name, and say today's talk is for anyone who has ever waited anxiously for a result.
2. Ask H.2 question 1 by show of hands; no one needs to speak.
3. Take two volunteers on question 2 and thank each by name.
4. Say you will come back to their words at minute 12.
5. Give the transition line below.

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the room's own life)
1. Think of the last time you waited for a result. Was the waiting worse than the work? (hands up)
2. What do you do with your mind during that wait?

*Adapt for any room:* the same wait is felt by a farmer before the harvest, a trader before a sale, a manager before a review, a student before exam results anywhere. Use the example nearest your audience.

- **Opening question:** "Think of the last time you waited for a result: an exam, an interview, a
  deadline review. Raise your hand if the waiting was worse than the work." Pause. "The Bhagavad-gita was
  spoken to a man in exactly that state, minutes before the biggest test of his life."
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** Two interns, Neha and Rohan, get the same project. Neha works late
  every night, checks the manager's face for approval, and sleeps badly. Rohan decides the outcome is
  rigged and does the minimum. On presentation day the client cancels; neither result counts. Who used the
  month better? What did each lose?
- **Transition:** "Krsna's first piece of practical advice to Arjuna is about exactly this: where to put
  your attention when the result is not in your hands."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** Placement season, probation period, appraisal cycle, the family's
  expectations. Work is where most of their anxiety already lives.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **You own the effort, not the result.** BG 2.47. *Plain:* give the task everything; hold the outcome
   lightly. *Example:* the student who studies fully and sleeps the night before the exam versus the one
   who revises till 4 a.m. in fear. *Transition:* but is it realistic to "not care" about results?
2. **Evenness is a skill, and it is what "yoga" means here.** BG 2.48. *Plain:* steadiness after a good
   result and a bad one. *Example:* a sportsperson who plays the next ball the same way after a boundary
   and after a dropped catch. *Transition:* why is the result not fully mine?
3. **Five factors make a result; my effort is one.** BG 18.14. *Plain:* body, doer, senses, effort, and a
   factor beyond me. *Example:* a farmer (IYF's analogy) ploughs, sows, waters; the rain is not his.
   *Transition:* so far this is good psychology. The Gita's next move is bigger.
4. **The same work can be an offering.** BG 9.27; BG 18.46. *Plain:* intention changes the inner quality
   of a task without changing the task. *Example:* cooking for a sick parent versus cooking for a canteen
   shift; same chopping, different experience. The Gita proposes doing one's whole profession in that
   spirit toward God, and says perfection is reached "through performing his own work". *Transition:* what
   about work that cannot be offered?
5. **Some work cannot be offered.** IYF's point 4, "give up unholy professions". *Plain:* work that
   harms others or ruins oneself does not fit BG 9.27. Keep this to one sentence at VL1 and no list.
   *Transition:* how do people actually remember this at a desk?
6. **Remembering at work.** Many who follow the Gita keep the day connected by a short practice, for
   example chanting (IYF's point 5). State it as what practitioners do, not as homework. *Transition:* to
   the one-sentence summary, "yoga is the art of all work" (BG 2.50).

## J. Stories and illustrations

- **Arjuna before the battle** (BG 1–2, context of BG 2.47). The frame of the whole talk: a competent man
  who wants to walk away from his duty because the outcome terrifies him. Tell it from the text: his
  compassion and confusion in chapter 1, Krsna's reply beginning at 2.11. Do not add dialogue.
  *Connection:* the Gita's advice on work is given to someone in distress about results.
- **The carpenter and the farmer** (IYF's teaching analogies from the slides, not scripture). The carpenter
  works with wood he did not grow and tools he did not invent; the farmer works and waits for rain.
  *Connection:* BG 18.14's five factors; "everything belongs to God" (IYF's point 1) can be stated gently
  as "none of the raw materials were ours to begin with".
- **Contemporary (invented sketch):** Neha and Rohan, the two interns (H). Label it invented.
- **Not used:** IYF's slides carry quotations attributed to business leaders about chanting. None could
  be verified here; do not read them out as facts. The slides' story "Liquid Beauty" is also not sourced
  on this page.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"If I don't care about results, I'll lose my edge. My company rewards results."**
   - BG 2.47 does not say "don't care about results"; it says do not be *attached* to them and do not think
     you are their cause (BG 18.14 lists five factors). BG 4.22's purport describes honest labour "as far as is in his power". The
     claim is that anxiety about the result is a cost, not a fuel. Invite them to test it for one week.
2. **"Isn't 'leave the rest to God' just an excuse for failure?"**
   - The five factors (BG 18.14) include my body, my senses and my endeavour. Three of five are mine to
     bring fully. The verse right-sizes responsibility; it does not cancel it. BG 3.8 adds that one cannot
     even maintain the body without work.
3. **"Why should I offer my work to God? I don't know if I believe in God."**
   - Fair. Start with what you can test: work done for someone you love feels different from work done for
     a transaction. The Gita proposes extending that to the source of everything (BG 9.27, BG 18.46). You
     do not need to settle the question of God tonight to notice the difference intention makes. VL2 takes
     up the question itself.
   - *Supporting (added 28 Oct 2026, attributed):* the Preaching Encyclopedia's two short pieces "Is work
     worship?" (O) turn the common saying into a question: "Certainly work is worship, but the question is,
     whose worship?" Read the object of worship off the goal of the work: the senses, the ego ("see what a great
     soul I am"), or God. A listener who does not believe in God can still see the first two in the office.
4. **"My job is boring. How can data entry be 'perfection'?"**
   - BG 18.47 says one's own work done imperfectly is better than another's done perfectly, and BG 18.46
     says perfection comes "through performing his own work", whatever it is. The purport: "In whatever
     occupation one may be engaged". The job's status is not the point; the consciousness in it is.
5. **"Should I quit my job and do something spiritual?"**
   - The Gita's answer to Arjuna, who wanted to quit, was *no*: do your duty (BG 2.47, BG 3.8). The
     purport to BG 3.33 (Verified; see also VL1-11) warns against suddenly giving up one's duties to
     become "a so-called yogī". Vocation questions are real but belong in a one-to-one conversation, not
     a decision made during a talk.
6. **"What is karma then? Is everything I do coming back to me?"**
   - Action does carry reaction; BG 4.17 says the subject is "very hard to understand" and distinguishes
     action, forbidden action and inaction. That is a full VL2 session. For tonight: work done as an
     offering is said to be free of reaction (BG 4.18 purport, BG 5.10). Do not go further.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The appraisal.** Priya has done the best work on her team all year. At appraisal, a colleague who
presents better is promoted; she is told to "wait for the next cycle". She is furious and thinking of
resigning in protest.
- **Dilemma:** Is her anger justified? What should she do this week?
- **Principles:** BG 2.47 (effort vs result); BG 18.14 (factors beyond her); BG 2.48 (evenness as a skill,
  not approval of unfairness).
- **Questions:**
  - Which part of this outcome was in Priya's hands, and which was not?
  - Does "evenness" mean she should not raise the unfairness with her manager?
  - What would Priya lose by resigning in anger that she would not lose by resigning after a calm decision?
- **Suggested analysis:** Evenness is not silence; she can ask for feedback and state her case. What the
  Gita removes is the sense that the verdict defines her worth. A decision made from steadiness will be
  better than one made from injury.
- **Takeaway:** Steadiness and standing up for oneself are not opposites.

**Case 2: The exam that was not his.** Arjun's parents want him to sit the civil services exam. He wants
to study design. He is now preparing for the exam half-heartedly and resenting every page.
- **Dilemma:** Is "do your duty" an argument for obeying his parents?
- **Principles:** BG 18.47 (one's own work, even imperfectly); BG 2.47 (never be attached to not doing
  your duty); BG 4.22 (honest labour).
- **Questions:**
  - Is half-hearted preparation "doing his duty"?
  - How might BG 18.47 help him speak to his parents honestly?
  - Whatever he chooses, what does working "as an offering" (BG 9.27) change?
- **Suggested analysis:** The Gita does not decide his career; it forbids the half-hearted middle. Either
  commit to the exam fully for an agreed period, or make the case for design honestly and then commit to
  that. Both are "duty" done with full effort; neither is resentment.
- **Takeaway:** The enemy is not the wrong job; it is the divided mind.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first; praise every try)
1. According to BG 2.47, what is mine: the effort or the result? *The effort.*
2. What does BG 2.48 call evenness in success and failure? *Yoga.*
3. Name three of the five factors in BG 18.14. *Body, doer, senses, endeavour, the Supersoul.*
4. Is "not attached to results" the same as "not caring"? *No: full effort, without making the result the measure of my worth.*
5. What does BG 9.27 add? *Offer the work to something higher; the task is the same, the intention changes.*

**M.2 Activities (choose one; about 10 minutes)**
- **Option A, "Task cards" (10 min).** 1. Pairs get one card (group project with a free-rider; job interview; boring data entry; startup pitch). 2. 4 min: write what is effort and what is outside their hands. 3. 3 min: share. 4. 3 min: link to BG 2.47 and 18.14; thank every pair.
- **Option B, "Two-column week" (8 min).** 1. Each draws two columns, "mine" and "not mine", for one task this week. 2. 3 min: fill them alone. 3. 3 min: pairs compare; praise honest columns. 4. 2 min: one sentence to take home.

**M.3 Reflection**
- **Reflection:** In your current work or study, where does your attention actually go: to the task or to
  the result? Which of the "two interns" are you closer to this month?
- **Application (private, optional):** Choose one task for the coming week. Before it, decide: "I will give
  this everything; the result is not mine to hold." Afterward, notice what changed. *(Nobody needs to
  share.)*

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

The Bhagavad-gita was spoken to Arjuna minutes before the hardest work of his life, when he wanted to walk
away because the result frightened him. Krsna's first practical advice was about work: you have a right to
your effort, not to its result (BG 2.47). A result comes from five factors, and your effort is only one of
them (BG 18.14). So give the task everything and hold the outcome lightly. Staying even after a good result
and a bad one is what the Gita calls yoga (BG 2.48), and it calls this evenness "the art of all work"
(BG 2.50).

The five factors are the body, the doer, the senses, the many kinds of effort, and the Supersoul (the Lord in the heart, BG 18.14); only the effort is fully ours. This is not "do not care". It is full effort without making the result the measure of our worth.

The Gita then proposes a second step: do the same work as an offering (BG 9.27), and your own
profession becomes the path (BG 18.46). The task stays the same; the intention changes. Try it on one task this week.

## S2. Key ideas

- I own the effort. I do not own the result (BG 2.47).
- Five factors make a result; my effort is one of them (BG 18.14). "Do your best, leave the rest."
- Evenness in success and failure is a skill. The Gita calls it yoga (BG 2.48).
- The same task can be a burden, a bargain, or an offering. The task does not change; I do (BG 9.27).
- Nobody has to quit their profession to reach the goal of life (BG 18.46).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **BG 2.47:** "You have a right to perform your prescribed duty, but you are not entitled to the fruits of
  action." *For me:* put my whole attention on the work in front of me, not on the verdict.
- **BG 2.48:** "Perform your duty equipoised… abandoning all attachment to success or failure. Such
  equanimity is called yoga." *For me:* steadiness after a win and after a loss is something I can practise.
- **BG 2.50:** "…strive for yoga, which is the art of all work." *For me:* mastery is being absorbed in the
  work, not in the applause.

## S4. Words to know

- **Yoga (in these verses):** evenness of mind while working; linking one's work to something higher. Not a
  posture.
- **Karma:** action and its reaction. The Gita says the subject is "very hard to understand" (BG 4.17), so
  tonight we only touched it.
- **Offering:** doing a task with the intention that it is for God, without changing the task itself
  (BG 9.27).

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **If I stop caring about results, won't I lose my edge?**
   The Gita never says "stop caring". It says do not be *attached* to the result and do not think you are
   its cause (BG 2.47). Anxiety about the outcome is a cost, not a fuel. Try one week without it and
   see.
2. **Isn't "leave the rest to God" an excuse?**
   Of the five factors in a result (BG 18.14), three are things I bring: my body, my senses and my
   endeavour. The verse right-sizes my responsibility; it does not remove it.
3. **I'm not sure I believe in God. Can I still use this?**
   Yes. Start with what you can test: work done for someone you love feels different from work done for a
   transaction. The Gita proposes extending that to the source of everything (BG 9.27). You can explore the
   question of God at your own pace.
4. **My work is boring. How can it be "perfection"?**
   The Gita says perfection comes "through performing his own work" (BG 18.46), and that one's own work
   done imperfectly is better than someone else's done perfectly (BG 18.47). The job's status is not the
   point; the consciousness in it is.
5. **Should I quit my job for something spiritual?**
   Arjuna wanted to quit, and Krsna said no: do your duty (BG 2.47; BG 3.8). Big vocation questions
   deserve a calm one-to-one conversation, not a decision made during a talk.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. According to BG 2.47, which of these is mine: (a) the result of my work, (b) the effort in my work,
   (c) both equally.
2. BG 18.14 lists five factors of any action. Name three.
3. True or false: BG 2.48 defines yoga as a set of physical postures.
4. In your own words: what is the difference between "not caring about results" and "not being attached to
   results"?
5. Give one example from your own week of the same task done as a burden and as an offering.

**Answers:** 1. (b) (BG 2.47). 2. Any three of: the body (place of action), the doer, the senses, the
endeavour, the Supersoul (BG 18.14). 3. False: "Such equanimity is called yoga" (BG 2.48). 4. Not caring
means withdrawing effort; non-attachment means full effort while not making the result the measure of my
worth (BG 2.47 and purport). 5. Any honest example; the task is the same, the intention differs (BG 9.27).
