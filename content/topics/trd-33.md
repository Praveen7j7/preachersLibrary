# Rasa-tattva as Srila Prabhupada presents it

> **TRD · up to 180 min · review: drafted · `sensitive: true` (rasa; madhurya-rasa is named; the head reviews).** Group F, advanced
> Gaudiya siddhanta. Rasa-tattva is the science of the relationships (rasas, "mellows") in which the soul loves Krishna. This page
> gives the framework exactly as Srila Prabhupada gave it: in The Nectar of Devotion (his summary study of Rupa Gosvami's
> Bhakti-rasamrta-sindhu), in Lord Caitanya's teaching to Rupa Gosvami at Prayaga (CC Madhya 19), and in Adi 4. The twelve rasas,
> how a rasa arises, the gopis' love and the stages of prema are each a page of their own (trd-41 to trd-48); here the preacher learns
> the whole map, Prabhupada's vocabulary, and his rules for speaking of it. **Source note:** the Bhakti-rasamrta-sindhu file in
> `research/books/` is an OCR of an edition whose translator is not named and which carries the translator's personal remarks; it is
> not quoted. BRS is quoted only as Srila Prabhupada quotes it. Ujjvala-nilamani and Visvanatha Cakravarti's Kirana are confidential
> rasa texts: the preacher's background only (research/advanced-books.md §2), and the files are font-scrambled in any case.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on trd-13: that page warned against imitation and showed that taste grows by sadhana; this page gives the positive science the
warning protects. Builds on trd-21 (the happiness of service as rasa) and trd-32 (the Lord came "to taste the sweet essence of the mellows
of love of God", CC Adi 4.15-16): if the Lord came for rasa, His servants must know what rasa is. Builds on dps-18: prema is the stage;
rasa is the form prema takes.

A resident preaches from the Krsna book and the Caitanya-caritamrta, and students ask: "What is the relationship between Krishna and the
gopis?" "Why is Radharani worshipped?" "Is this not just romance?" Without rasa-tattva he either avoids the question or answers from
sentiment. Srila Prabhupada did neither. He translated the Bhakti-rasamrta-sindhu as a summary study, chose the word "mellow", taught the
twelve rasas with their examples, placed conjugal love at the top, and set firm rules about discussing these matters from the material
platform (NOD 34). This page is those rules and that map. Group G (trd-41 to trd-48) then takes each rasa in turn; no lower batch studies
rasa as a science, and SPS-33 (Krishna's sweetness) gives only the taste of it.

## C. Central message

Rasa is the relationship in which the soul loves and serves Krishna, and Krishna is "the reservoir of pleasure for devotees in all
transcendental mellows" (CC Madhya 8.142). There are five direct rasas, neutrality, servitude, friendship, parental love and conjugal
love, permanently situated in the devotee's heart, and seven indirect ones that appear in particular situations (CC Madhya 19.185-188).
Conjugal love is the highest in sweetness (CC Adi 4.44-45). All of this is spiritual, "the diametric opposite of material, lusty
affairs" (SB 10.33.39 purport), and Srila Prabhupada's rule stands: no one on the material platform should discuss these ecstasies by
quoting here and there; one should understand that there are varieties of reciprocal love in the spiritual world and never think them
material (NOD 34).

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Say what rasa means and why Prabhupada translated it "mellow" (NOD 20), and what The Nectar of Devotion is.
2. Name the five direct and seven indirect rasas with the examples Lord Caitanya gave Rupa Gosvami (CC Madhya 19.185-191).
3. Explain from CC Adi 4.42-46 in what sense conjugal love is the highest, and from CC Adi 4.164-165 why it has nothing to do with lust.
4. Describe rasabhasa (NOD 49, 51) in outline and explain why the acharyas analyse incompatible mixtures.
5. State and apply Prabhupada's rules for speaking of rasa (NOD 34; SB 10.33.39 purport): what to say to a new audience, what to leave to the Caitanya-caritamrta class, and what never to imitate.

**NOD 20 ("Transcendental Mellow")** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/nod/20/
- **Context:** the opening of the second division of the Bhakti-rasamrta-sindhu in Prabhupada's summary study.
- **Teaching:** this division has five topics: "(1) vibhāva – special symptoms or causes of ecstasy, (2) anubhāva – subsequent ecstasy, (3) sāttvika-bhāva – constitutional or existential ecstasy, (4) vyabhicārī-bhāva – aggressive ecstasy and (5) sthāyi-bhāva – fervent or continuous ecstasy."
- **Purport point (on the word):** "The word rasa, used in the Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu, is understood by different persons differently because the exact English equivalent is very difficult to find. But as we have seen our spiritual master translate this word rasa into 'mellow,' we shall follow in his footsteps".
- **Application (preacher's):** Prabhupada's choice of "mellow" is itself a teaching: he follows his guru's word, and so do we. The five
  ingredients are trd-43's subject; here they are only named.
- **Use:** in the talk; the vocabulary segment.

**NOD 1; NOD Preface** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/nod/1/
- **Teaching (Preface, https://vedabase.io/en/library/nod/preface/):** "The Nectar of Devotion is a summary study of Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu, which was written in Sanskrit by Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī Prabhupāda."
- **Teaching (NOD 1):** pure devotional service begins where the devotee wants nothing but service; "A devotee, what to speak of rejecting material sense gratification, does not even want any of the five kinds of liberation."
- **Application (preacher's):** rasa-tattva stands on pure bhakti (trd-28, trd-29); a rasa sought for one's own enjoyment is not rasa.
- **Use:** background; one line.

**CC Madhya 8.142 (with purport)** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/8/142/
> "He is the most attractive lover of Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī and is the reservoir of pleasure for devotees in all transcendental mellows."
- **Context:** Ramananda Raya quoting the opening verse of the Bhakti-rasamrta-sindhu to Lord Caitanya.
- **Purport point:** "Everyone has a particular transcendental mellow by which he loves and serves Kṛṣṇa. Kṛṣṇa is the most attractive feature for every kind of devotee. He is therefore called akhila-rasāmṛta-mūrti". The five rasas are named there.
- **Use:** in the talk; the definition of Krishna in terms of rasa.

**CC Madhya 19.183-184 (with purport)** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/19/183-184/
> "According to the devotee, attachment falls within the five categories of śānta-rati, dāsya-rati, sakhya-rati, vātsalya-rati and madhura-rati. These five categories arise from devotees' different attachments to the Supreme Personality of Godhead. The transcendental mellows derived from devotional service are also of five varieties."
- **Context:** Lord Caitanya teaching Rupa Gosvami at Prayaga for ten days; this is the heart of the rasa teaching.
- **Purport point:** Prabhupada quotes BRS 2.5.16-18 and 2.5.27 and explains santa-rati: "An appreciation of the greatness of the Lord is called śānta-rati. This is attachment not to the personal feature but to the impersonal feature. Generally, one in this stage is attached to the Paramātmā feature". He cites BG 18.61.
- **Use:** in the talk; rati (attachment) becomes rasa.

**CC Madhya 19.185 (with purport)** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/19/185/
> "The chief transcendental mellows experienced with the Supreme Personality of Godhead are five — śānta, dāsya, sakhya, vātsalya and madhura."
- **Purport point:** quoting BRS 3.1.4-6 on santa-bhakti-rasa: "Śānta-bhakti-rasa devotees generally relish the impersonal feature of the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Since their taste of transcendental bliss is incomplete, it is called aghana, or not concentrated. A comparison is made between ordinary milk and concentrated milk."
- **Use:** in the talk; the five direct rasas.

**CC Madhya 19.186-188** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/19/187/
> 19.187: "In addition to the five direct mellows, there are seven indirect mellows, known as laughter, wonder, chivalry, compassion, anger, disaster and fear."
> 19.188: "The five direct transcendental mellows of devotional service are permanently situated in the heart of the devotee, whereas the seven indirect emotions appear suddenly under certain conditions and appear more powerful."
- **Purport point (19.186):** the verse "is found in the Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu (2.5.116)". **(19.187):** Prabhupada quotes BRS 4.1.6, 4.2.1 and 4.3.1 for laughter, wonder and chivalry, e.g. "When through devotional service a laughing attachment to Kṛṣṇa is developed, it is called hāsya-bhakti-rasa by learned scholars."
- **Use:** in the talk; the seven indirect rasas, and the key distinction permanent / occasional.

**CC Madhya 19.189-191** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/19/190/
> 19.190: "In Vṛndāvana, examples of devotees in fraternity are Śrīdāmā and Sudāmā; in Dvārakā the Lord's friends are Bhīma and Arjuna; in Vṛndāvana the devotees in parental love are mother Yaśodā and father Nanda Mahārāja, and in Dvārakā the Lord's parents are Vasudeva and Devakī."
> 19.191: "The chief devotees in conjugal love are the gopīs in Vṛndāvana, the queens in Dvārakā and the goddesses of fortune in Vaikuṇṭha. These devotees are innumerable."
- **Teaching (19.189):** santa-bhaktas are the nine Yogendras and the four Kumaras; dasya-bhaktas are innumerable. The purport names Raktaka, Citraka and Patraka in Gokula, Daruka in Dvaraka, and Hanuman.
- **Use:** in the talk and the handout; the examples make the map concrete.

**CC Adi 4.42-46** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/adi/4/44/
> 4.42: "Four kinds of devotees are the receptacles of the four kinds of mellows in love of God, namely servitude, friendship, parental affection and conjugal love."
> 4.44: "But if we compare the sentiments in an impartial mood, we find that the conjugal sentiment is superior to all others in sweetness."
- **Teaching (4.43):** each devotee feels his own sentiment to be the best and tastes great happiness in it. **(4.45, quoting BRS 2.5.38):** "Increasing love is experienced in various tastes, one above another. But that love which has the highest taste in the gradual succession of desire manifests itself in the form of conjugal love." **(4.46):** "Therefore I call it madhura-rasa. It has two further divisions, namely wedded and unwedded love."
- **Purport point (4.44):** "No one is higher or lower than anyone else in transcendental relationships with the Lord, for in the absolute realm everything is equal. But although these relationships are absolute, there are also transcendental differences between them. Thus the transcendental relationship of conjugal love is considered the highest perfection."
- **Use:** in the talk; the gradation and its exact sense.

**CC Adi 4.15-16 (purport)** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/adi/4/15-16/
- **Teaching:** the reciprocal exchanges between the living entity and the Lord in Vrajabhumi are "rāga-bhakti, or devotional service to the Lord in transcendental rapture"; the Lord "is more attracted by rāga-bhakti than vidhi-bhakti"; the Vedas say raso vai sah, "the Absolute Truth is the reservoir for all kinds of reciprocal exchanges of loving" sentiments.
- **Also in:** trd-32 uses this verse for the reasons of the Lord's appearance; here the purport's statement that the Absolute is rasa.
- **Use:** in the talk; the Vedic root of the word.

**CC Adi 4.164-165** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/adi/4/164/
> 4.164: "Lust and love have different characteristics, just as iron and gold have different natures."
> 4.165: "The desire to gratify one's own senses is kāma [lust], but the desire to please the senses of Lord Kṛṣṇa is prema [love]."
- **Purport point (4.164):** "One should try to discriminate between sexual love and pure love, for they belong to different categories, with a gulf of difference between them."
- **Purport point (4.165):** of the gopis: "There was not the slightest tinge of sexual love between the gopīs and Kṛṣṇa. The author of Śrī Caitanya-caritāmṛta asserts with authority that sexual love is a matter of personal sense enjoyment."
- **Also in:** trd-29 uses 4.165 for the definition of prema against the desire for liberation; here for madhurya-rasa against lust.

**NOD 34 ("The Nectar of Devotion")** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/nod/34/
- **Teaching:** "No one, while remaining on the material platform, should discuss these different descriptions of bhāva and anubhāva by quoting different statements of transcendental literatures. Such manifestations are displays of the transcendental pleasure potency of the Lord. One should simply try to understand that on the spiritual platform there are many varieties of reciprocal love. Such loving exchanges should never be considered to be material."
- **Application (preacher's):** this is Prabhupada's rule for every speaker on rasa. It does not forbid teaching the framework (he published it);
  it forbids speculative discussion of ecstasies from the material platform. The rule is read aloud in the talk.
- **Use:** in the talk; the rule.

**NOD 49 ("Mixing of Rasas"); NOD 51 ("Perverted Expression of Mellows")** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/nod/49/
- **Teaching (49):** "there are twelve different kinds of rasas, or ecstatic relationships that are shared with Kṛṣṇa. Five of these rasas are direct… Seven of the rasas are indirect". "The five direct rasas are eternally manifested in the Vaikuṇṭha world, the spiritual kingdom, whereas the seven indirect rasas are eternally manifesting and unmanifesting in Gokula Vṛndāvana". Mixtures are compatible or incompatible; for example, neutral love with ghastliness or astonishment is compatible, with conjugal love, chivalry, anger or dread incompatible.
- **Teaching (51):** "Rasābhāsa, or incompatible mixtures of mellows, may be classified as uparasa (false expression), anurasa (imitation) and aparasa (perverted or misrepresented mellows)." Prabhupada's first example is an impersonalist's statement on seeing Krishna, called "śānta-uparasa, or a perverted reflection of mixed impersonalism and personalism."
- **Application (preacher's):** rasabhasa is why the acharyas are strict about who speaks of rasa and how: a wrong mixture in a kirtana, a
  drama or a lecture is not harmless. The detail is trd-42.
- **Use:** in the talk; one segment.

**SB 10.33.39** — Verified · *BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples* · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/10/33/39/
> "Anyone who faithfully hears or describes the Lord's playful affairs with the young gopīs of Vṛndāvana will attain the Lord's pure devotional service. Thus he will quickly become sober and conquer lust, the disease of the heart."
- **Purport point:** "Qualitatively, the Lord's spiritual, loving pastimes are the diametric opposite of material, lusty affairs, so much so that simply by hearing about the Lord's pastimes a devotee conquers sex desire. By reading pornographic literature or hearing about material romance, we certainly do not conquer sex desire but rather increase our lust."
- **Also in:** trd-13 R.1 holds SB 10.33.30-31 (the warning against imitation); read the two together.
- **Use:** in the talk; the effect of faithful hearing, and the word "faithfully".

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** The Absolute Truth is rasa (raso vai sah; CC Adi 4.15-16 purport). The soul's eternal nature is to love and serve
   Krishna in a particular relationship, and Krishna is the "reservoir of pleasure for devotees in all transcendental mellows" (CC Madhya
   8.142). Rasa-tattva is the systematic study of these relationships.
2. **Prabhupada's vocabulary.** He gave "mellow" for rasa, following his guru (NOD 20); he gave The Nectar of Devotion as a "summary study"
   (Preface), not a verse-by-verse translation; he kept the Sanskrit terms (vibhava, anubhava, sattvika, vyabhicari, sthayi) and defined
   each in one phrase (NOD 20). A preacher uses his words and his definitions.
3. **From rati to rasa.** Attachment (rati) to Krishna comes in five kinds; when it is relished with its ingredients it becomes rasa of five
   kinds (CC Madhya 19.183-184; the ingredients are trd-43). Santa-rati is appreciation of the Lord's greatness, leaning to the Paramatma
   feature; its taste is "not concentrated", like ordinary milk beside condensed milk (19.183-185 purports). Each further rasa adds what the
   previous lacks.
4. **Five and seven.** The five direct rasas are permanent in the heart; the seven indirect (laughter, wonder, chivalry, compassion, anger,
   disaster, fear) "appear suddenly under certain conditions and appear more powerful" (19.188). Prabhupada's summary: the five "are
   eternally manifested in the Vaikuṇṭha world", the seven "eternally manifesting and unmanifesting in Gokula Vṛndāvana" (NOD 49). The
   examples (19.189-191) anchor each: the Kumaras, Hanuman, Sridama and Arjuna, Yasoda and Devaki, the gopis and the queens.
5. **The gradation.** Each devotee feels his own rasa to be best (CC Adi 4.43), and in the absolute realm "No one is higher or lower"
   (4.44 purport); yet compared impartially, conjugal love "is superior to all others in sweetness" (4.44) because it contains the tastes
   of all the others (4.45; BRS 2.5.38). The gradation is of completeness of taste, not of rank among devotees.
6. **The reflection.** Madhurya-rasa is named only after the line is drawn: lust and love differ as iron and gold (4.164); lust seeks my
   pleasure, love seeks Krishna's (4.165); "There was not the slightest tinge of sexual love between the gopīs and Kṛṣṇa" (4.165 purport).
   The Bhagavatam adds that faithful hearing of the rasa-lila conquers lust, while material romance increases it, because the two are
   "the diametric opposite" (SB 10.33.39 and purport).
7. **Rasabhasa.** Because rasa is exact, wrong mixtures exist: compatible and incompatible (NOD 49), and the three kinds of incompatible
   reflection, uparasa, anurasa, aparasa (NOD 51). This is why an acharya corrects a song, a drama or a sentence. The detail is trd-42.
8. **The rules.** (a) The framework is taught openly: Prabhupada published it and taught it. (b) Ecstasies are not discussed speculatively
   from the material platform, nor pieced together from quotations (NOD 34). (c) The intimate pastimes are heard "faithfully" from the
   Bhagavatam in the devotees' company, not described in public talks or dramatised for effect (SB 10.33.39; trd-13; trd-44). (d) Nothing
   is imitated (trd-39). (e) The confidential rasa texts stay the preacher's background (research/advanced-books.md §2).
9. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *Rasa means a devotee's "flavour of personality".* It is the soul's eternal relationship with Krishna, with definite ingredients (NOD 20).
   - *Conjugal love being highest makes other devotees lower.* "No one is higher or lower" (CC Adi 4.44 purport); the comparison is of taste.
   - *Rasa talk is for everyone, or for no one.* The framework is for all who hear the Caitanya-caritamrta; its ecstasies are not for speculation (NOD 34).
   - *Hearing the rasa-lila is dangerous for a brahmacari.* Faithful hearing conquers lust (SB 10.33.39); unfaithful hearing, treating it as romance, is the danger (purport). The qualification is faith and the devotees' company.
10. **Pitch for TRD.** Teach the whole map in Prabhupada's words; name madhurya-rasa plainly and draw the iron-and-gold line before it; end
    with the rules, read aloud. Say which texts we hold and which stay background.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet the devotees and say that today's subject is rasa, and that we will take it exactly as Srila Prabhupada gave it, with his words and his limits.
2. Say: "Everyone here has heard the word a thousand times. Today we learn what it means in Rupa Gosvami's science, and what Prabhupada said about speaking of it."
3. Ask H.2 question 1 for private writing (no sharing).
4. Ask H.2 question 2; take three answers and write the relationships named on the board.
5. Say that Lord Caitanya taught this to Rupa Gosvami in ten days at Prayaga, and begin with CC Madhya 8.142.

**H.2 Opening questions**
1. (Private) When you think of Krishna in japa, is He a master, a friend, a child, or something else? Write one word; keep it to yourself.
2. Name one devotee from the Bhagavatam and the relationship he or she has with Krishna.
3. What is the difference between "Krishna is attractive" and "Krishna is all-attractive"?

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Krishna is the reservoir of all rasas.** akhila-rasamrta-murti (CC Madhya 8.142 and purport); the Absolute is rasa (CC Adi 4.15-16 purport).
   *Transition:* how does Prabhupada name this?
2. **"Mellow", following his guru.** NOD 20; The Nectar of Devotion as a summary study. *Transition:* what are the mellows?
3. **Five direct, from attachment.** santa, dasya, sakhya, vatsalya, madhura (CC Madhya 19.183-185); santa's taste is unconcentrated, like ordinary
   milk (purport). *Transition:* and the seven?
4. **Seven indirect, occasional and strong.** laughter, wonder, chivalry, compassion, anger, disaster, fear (19.186-188). *Transition:* who tastes which?
5. **The examples.** The Yogendras and Kumaras; Hanuman; Sridama and Arjuna; Yasoda and Devaki; the gopis, the queens, the goddesses of fortune
   (19.189-191). *Transition:* is there an order?
6. **Conjugal love is highest in sweetness, with no one lower.** CC Adi 4.42-46 and the 4.44 purport. *Transition:* then the line.
7. **Iron and gold.** Lust and love (4.164-165); faithful hearing conquers lust (SB 10.33.39). *Transition:* and the rule.
8. **Prabhupada's rule.** NOD 34, read aloud. *Transition:* each rasa, one by one, from trd-41.

## J. Stories and illustrations

**1. Lord Caitanya instructs Rupa Gosvami at Prayaga (CC Madhya 19.183-191).** Not a narrative of events but the scene: at the Dasasvamedha
ghat, for ten days, the Lord empowers Rupa Gosvami and teaches him the science of devotional service, including the mellows. Read the verses
in order and name the examples the Lord Himself gives: fraternity in Sridama and Sudama, in Bhima and Arjuna; parental love in Yasoda and Nanda,
in Vasudeva and Devaki (19.190); conjugal love in the gopis, the queens of Dvaraka and the goddesses of fortune of Vaikuntha (19.191). *Lesson:*
the science came from the Lord's own mouth to the acharya who wrote it; we receive it in that line.

**2. The Bhagavatam on hearing the rasa dance (SB 10.33.39 and purport; BBT translation completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples).** Sukadeva
Gosvami ends the rasa-lila chapters with the promise that one who "faithfully hears or describes" these pastimes attains pure devotional service
and "will quickly become sober and conquer lust, the disease of the heart." The purport: the Lord's pastimes are "the diametric opposite of
material, lusty affairs", and material romance increases lust while these pastimes remove it. *Lesson:* the same words heard in two ways give
opposite results; the difference is faith and company. (The warning verses SB 10.33.30-31 are in trd-13.)

**3. Prabhupada's one word (NOD 20).** Asked, in effect, how to render rasa, Prabhupada writes that the exact English equivalent is very hard to
find, "But as we have seen our spiritual master translate this word rasa into 'mellow,' we shall follow in his footsteps". *Lesson:* even in
vocabulary, rasa-tattva is received, not invented. A preacher who coins his own terms for rasa has already left the line.

**Illustration (writer's, not scriptural):** white light passed through a prism shows its colours; the colours were in the light. The twelve rasas
are not twelve Krishnas but the one all-attractive Krishna tasted through twelve relationships (CC Madhya 8.142 purport).

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"Isn't the whole subject just religious romance?"** Lust and love differ as iron and gold (CC Adi 4.164); love seeks Krishna's pleasure (4.165).
   The proof is in the effect: faithful hearing removes lust, romance increases it (SB 10.33.39 purport).
2. **"If conjugal love is highest, are servants and friends of Krishna inferior?"** "No one is higher or lower than anyone else in transcendental
   relationships with the Lord" (CC Adi 4.44 purport). The comparison is of sweetness of taste; each devotee relishes his own as best (4.43).
3. **"Then may I speak of the gopis in a college talk?"** Teach what Prabhupada taught publicly: the five relationships, the examples, the
   iron-and-gold line. The intimate pastimes are heard from the Bhagavatam in the devotees' company (SB 10.33.39), not described for effect; and
   nothing is imitated (NOD 34; trd-13, trd-39).
4. **"Is santa-rasa a real rasa if it leans to the impersonal?"** Yes; it is the first of the five, with the Yogendras and Kumaras as its devotees
   (CC Madhya 19.185, 19.189). Its taste is "not concentrated" (19.185 purport), and the further rasas add to it.
5. **"Why do the acharyas object to a wrongly mixed song or drama? Isn't any devotion good?"** Because incompatible mixtures (rasabhasa) exist and
   are analysed precisely (NOD 49, 51). The sincerity of the singer is not the question; the fitness of the expression is (trd-42).
6. **"Why can we not read Ujjvala-nilamani here?"** It is a confidential rasa text; the head has ruled it the preacher's background, never lecture
   content (research/advanced-books.md §2). Our copy is also unreadable. BRS, the open text, is read through Prabhupada.

## L. Case studies

**Case 1 (Invented case for discussion).** A brahmacari prepares a youth drama in which Krishna and the gopis are played with romantic gestures
"to make it relatable". *Dilemma:* where is the line? *Principles:* CC Adi 4.164-165 and purport; SB 10.33.39 purport; NOD 34; NOD 51 (rasabhasa).
*Questions:* What will the audience take home? Which rasa does the drama actually present? *Analysis:* the drama presents the reflection, not the
rasa; it is rasabhasa and it increases lust in the audience. Replace it with a pastime from the Bhagavatam told as written, or with a drama of
Vrindavana's friendship or parental love. *Takeaway:* "relatable" is not the standard; the Bhagavatam's own words are.

**Case 2 (Invented case for discussion).** In a Caitanya-caritamrta class a junior asks the speaker, "Which rasa am I in?" *Dilemma:* answer,
deflect, or teach? *Principles:* CC Madhya 19.188 (the direct rasas are permanent in the heart); CC Adi 4.43 (each feels his own as best); NOD 34.
*Questions:* Who reveals a devotee's rasa, and when? What can be said now? *Analysis:* the speaker teaches that the rasa is eternal and is revealed
by Krishna and guru in due course as bhava awakens (dps-18), and that the present business is pure sadhana (trd-13). He does not assign a rasa.
*Takeaway:* teach the science; never play the role of the one who reveals.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Rasa means the relationship in which a soul loves and serves Krishna. Srila Prabhupada translated it "mellow", because his own spiritual master
used that word (NOD 20), and he gave Rupa Gosvami's Bhakti-rasamrta-sindhu to the world as a summary study, The Nectar of Devotion. The Vedas say
that the Absolute Truth is rasa (CC Adi 4.15-16 purport), and the first verse of the Bhakti-rasamrta-sindhu calls Krishna "the reservoir of pleasure
for devotees in all transcendental mellows" (CC Madhya 8.142). Every devotee has his own mellow by which he loves Krishna (8.142 purport).

Lord Caitanya taught Rupa Gosvami that a devotee's attachment to Krishna is of five kinds, and so the mellows are of five kinds: santa (neutrality),
dasya (servitude), sakhya (friendship), vatsalya (parental love) and madhura (conjugal love) (CC Madhya 19.183-185). These five are permanently
situated in the devotee's heart. Seven more are indirect: laughter, wonder, chivalry, compassion, anger, disaster and fear; they "appear suddenly
under certain conditions and appear more powerful" (19.187-188). The Lord gave examples: the four Kumaras in neutrality; servants everywhere; Sridama
and Sudama, Bhima and Arjuna in friendship; Yasoda and Nanda, Vasudeva and Devaki in parental love; the gopis, the queens of Dvaraka and the
goddesses of fortune in conjugal love (19.189-191).

Each devotee feels his own mellow to be the best (CC Adi 4.43), and in the spiritual world no one is higher or lower (4.44 purport). Yet compared
impartially, conjugal love "is superior to all others in sweetness" (4.44), because it contains the tastes of all the others (4.45). Before this is
said, a line is drawn: "Lust and love have different characteristics, just as iron and gold have different natures" (4.164); lust wants my pleasure,
love wants Krishna's (4.165), and there was not the slightest tinge of sexual love between the gopis and Krishna (4.165 purport). The Bhagavatam
promises that one who faithfully hears Krishna's pastimes with the gopis conquers lust, while material romance increases it (SB 10.33.39 and purport).

Because rasa is exact, wrong mixtures exist and are analysed (NOD 49, 51). And Prabhupada gives a rule: no one on the material platform should
discuss these ecstasies by quoting here and there; one should understand that there are many varieties of reciprocal love in the spiritual world and
never think them material (NOD 34). The framework is taught openly; the ecstasies are not speculated about; nothing is imitated.

## S2. Key ideas
- Rasa is the soul's relationship of love with Krishna; Prabhupada's word is "mellow" (NOD 20).
- Five direct rasas, permanent in the heart; seven indirect, occasional and strong (CC Madhya 19.185-188).
- Conjugal love is the sweetest; no devotee is lower than another (CC Adi 4.44 and purport).
- Lust and love differ as iron and gold (CC Adi 4.164); faithful hearing conquers lust (SB 10.33.39).
- The rule: do not discuss these ecstasies from the material platform; never think them material (NOD 34).

## S3. Verses to remember
- **CC Madhya 19.185:** "The chief transcendental mellows experienced with the Supreme Personality of Godhead are five — śānta, dāsya, sakhya, vātsalya and madhura." For me: the map in one line.
- **CC Adi 4.164:** "Lust and love have different characteristics, just as iron and gold have different natures." For me: the line I draw before I speak.
- **SB 10.33.39:** one who faithfully hears these pastimes "will quickly become sober and conquer lust, the disease of the heart." For me: hear faithfully, in good company.

## S4. Words to know
- **Rasa / mellow:** the relationship in which a soul loves and serves Krishna.
- **Rati:** attachment to Krishna; it comes in five kinds and matures into rasa.
- **Santa, dasya, sakhya, vatsalya, madhura:** neutrality, servitude, friendship, parental love, conjugal love.
- **Direct and indirect rasas:** the five permanent mellows and the seven that appear in particular situations.
- **Rasabhasa:** an incompatible mixture of mellows (NOD 51).
- **Akhila-rasamrta-murti:** Krishna, the form of the nectar of all mellows (CC Madhya 8.142).

## S5. Questions and answers
1. **Is this about romance?** No. Lust and love are as different as iron and gold (CC Adi 4.164-165); faithful hearing of Krishna's pastimes removes lust (SB 10.33.39).
2. **Are Krishna's servants and friends lower than the gopis?** No one is higher or lower in the spiritual world; conjugal love is called highest in sweetness of taste (CC Adi 4.44 and purport).
3. **Which rasa am I in?** It is eternal and is revealed by Krishna and guru as love awakens; now the business is pure sadhana (CC Madhya 19.188; dps-18).
4. **May I speak about the gopis to new people?** Teach what Prabhupada taught openly: the relationships, the examples, the iron-and-gold line; hear the intimate pastimes from the Bhagavatam in the devotees' company (NOD 34; SB 10.33.39).
5. **Why did Prabhupada say "mellow"?** Because his spiritual master used that word for rasa (NOD 20).
