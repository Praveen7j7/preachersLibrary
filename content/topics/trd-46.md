# The stages of prema: sneha, mana, pranaya, raga, anuraga, bhava, mahabhava

> **TRD · up to 180 min · review: drafted · `sensitive: true` (the higher stages of love; Radharani's mahabhava; the head reviews).** Group G,
> page 6. dps-18 taught the ladder from sraddha to prema; trd-43 taught how a permanent love (sthayi-bhava) is tasted as rasa. This page
> follows the sthayi-bhava's own growth beyond prema: "affection, abhorrence, love, attachment, further attachment, ecstasy and great
> ecstasy" (CC Madhya 19.178), the seven stages Lord Caitanya named to Rupa Gosvami and to Sanatana Gosvami with the analogy of sugar refined
> from seed to rock candy. Srila Prabhupada defines each stage in his purports, mostly from the Bhakti-rasamrta-sindhu and Ujjvala-nilamani,
> and he also says where the summit is found: "The symptoms of mahā-bhāva are visible only in the bodies of eternal associates like the gopīs"
> (CC Madhya 6.13 purport), and "these symptoms are not to be found anywhere but in the body of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu" (CC Madhya 2.11
> purport). The resident learns the stages as a goal to aspire to and revere, never to claim. **Source note:** the Ujjvala-nilamani
> (Sthayibhava-prakarana) is quoted only as Prabhupada quotes it; the OCR in `research/books/` is the preacher's background
> (research/advanced-books.md §2). Madhurya-kadambini is quoted from the approved Gaudiya Vedanta Publications edition.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on dps-18, which climbed from sraddha through nistha, ruci and asakti to bhava and prema and stopped there, as a DPS page should: prema is
the goal of sadhana. Builds on trd-43, which showed that prema, as sthayi-bhava, is tasted as rasa with four ingredients; here the same sthayi-bhava
is seen growing: "Attachment for Kṛṣṇa never wanes; it increases more and more as one attains different stages" (CC Madhya 19.180 purport). Builds
on trd-45, where mahabhava was named as Radharani's own nature (CC Adi 4.68–69).

A resident needs this page for a precise reason. The songs he sings and the Caitanya-caritamrta he reads daily use these seven words constantly;
the Antya-lila is a description of mahabhava in the Lord's body. Without the definitions he either reads past the words or, worse, applies them to
himself or to others ("he has reached raga"). Prabhupada's purports give each stage a definition and then place the summit where it belongs: in the
gopis, in Radharani and in Lord Caitanya. Knowing this, the resident reveres what he reads and keeps to his sadhana. Lower batches learn the ladder
to prema (DPS); no lower batch studies the stages beyond it.

## C. Central message

"By regularly rendering devotional service, one gradually becomes attached to the Supreme Personality of Godhead. When that attachment is
intensified, it becomes love of Godhead" (CC Madhya 19.177), and prema itself keeps growing: "The basic aspects of prema, when gradually increasing
to different states, are affection, abhorrence, love, attachment, further attachment, ecstasy and great ecstasy" (19.178), as sugarcane seed becomes
cane, juice, molasses, sugar, candy, rock candy and lozenges (19.179). "All these stages combined are called sthāyibhāva, or continuous love of
Godhead" (19.180). The summit, mahabhava, is embodied in Radharani (CC Adi 4.69) and its symptoms "are visible only in the bodies of eternal
associates like the gopīs" (CC Madhya 6.13 purport). The perfect devotee at that summit says, "I have not the slightest tinge of love of Godhead
within My heart" (CC Madhya 2.45). The resident learns the stages to revere them and to chant; he grades no one, least of all himself.

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Recite the seven stages in order and give Prabhupada's definition of each (sneha and raga from CC Madhya 19.178 purport; mana and pranaya from 2.66 purport; anuraga, bhava and mahabhava from 6.13 purport).
2. Explain the sugar analogy (19.179; 23.43–44) and what it teaches: one substance, increasing taste, no new ingredient.
3. State that every stage is sthayi-bhava and how it relates to rasa (19.180–182; trd-43).
4. Say where mahabhava is found and why no sadhaka claims any stage (CC Adi 4.69; CC Madhya 8.160; 6.13 and 2.11 purports; 23.45 purport on raty-abhasa).
5. Describe the perfect devotee's own view of his love (CC Madhya 2.45, 2.48) and apply it to the resident's speech about his own and others' advancement.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**CC Madhya 19.177 (with purport)** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/19/177/
> "By regularly rendering devotional service, one gradually becomes attached to the Supreme Personality of Godhead. When that attachment is intensified, it becomes love of Godhead."
- **Purport point (BRS 1.2.2; 1.3.41):** sadhana-bhakti awakens the dormant love; "when this attachment is intensified, it results in ecstatic love for Kṛṣṇa." Rati: "When a tenderness of the heart is manifested, there is rati, or attachment."
- **Also in:** dps-18 (the ladder). Here as the starting line of the seven stages.
- **Use:** in the talk; one line.

**CC Madhya 19.178 (with purport)** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/19/178/
> "The basic aspects of prema, when gradually increasing to different states, are affection, abhorrence, love, attachment, further attachment, ecstasy and great ecstasy."
- **Purport point (sneha, BRS 3.2.84):** "That aspect of prema in which the melting of the heart for the lover is concentrated is called sneha, or affection. The symptom of such affection is that the lover cannot for a moment remain without the association of the beloved." **(raga, BRS 3.2.87):** "That stage at which affection for the beloved converts unhappiness into happiness is called rāga, or attachment. When one has such attachment for Kṛṣṇa, he can give up his own life to satisfy his beloved Kṛṣṇa." Mana and pranaya are referred to Madhya 2.66; anuraga, bhava and mahabhava to Madhya 6.13.
- **Application (preacher's):** the English words Srila Prabhupada uses for the seven, "affection, abhorrence, love, attachment, further attachment, ecstasy and great ecstasy", is the vocabulary the resident uses; "abhorrence" for mana will need explaining (see 2.66 purport).
- **Use:** in the talk; the list and two definitions.

**CC Madhya 19.179–182 (with purport)** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/19/180/
> 19.179: "The gradual development of prema may be compared to different states of sugar. First there is the seed of the sugarcane, then sugarcane and then the juice extracted from the cane. When this juice is boiled, it forms liquid molasses, then solid molasses, then sugar, candy, rock candy and finally lozenges."
> 19.180: "All these stages combined are called sthāyibhāva, or continuous love of Godhead in devotional service. In addition to these stages, there are vibhāva and anubhāva."
- **Purport point (19.180):** "Attachment for Kṛṣṇa never wanes; it increases more and more as one attains different stages. All the stages together are called sthāyibhāva, or continuous existence of ecstasy."
- **Teaching (19.181–182):** mixed with sattvika and vyabhicari symptoms, the devotee relishes "a variety of nectarean tastes", like yogurt with sugar candy, ghee, black pepper and camphor.
- **Use:** in the talk; the analogy and the link to trd-43.

**CC Madhya 23.42–44** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/23/44/
> 23.42: "Love of Godhead increases and is manifested as affection, counterlove, love, attachment, subattachment, ecstasy and sublime ecstasy."
> 23.44: "One should understand that just as the taste of sugar increases as it is gradually purified, so when love of Godhead increases from rati, which is compared to the beginning seed, its taste increases."
- **Context:** the same teaching given to Sanatana Gosvami at Varanasi; the English differs slightly ("counterlove" for mana, "subattachment" for anuraga).
- **Use:** in the talk; the second telling, and the word "purified".

**CC Madhya 23.7; CC Madhya 23.45 (purport)** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/23/7/
> 23.7 (BRS 1.4.1): "When that bhāva softens the heart completely, becomes endowed with a great feeling of possessiveness in relation to the Lord and becomes very much condensed and intensified, it is called prema [love of Godhead] by learned scholars."
- **Purport point (23.45, BRS 1.3.41–44):** symptoms of attachment "found among speculators and fruitive actors" are "raty-ābhāsa, a mere glimpse of attachment."
- **Also in:** trd-43 (the glimpse). Here: the definition of prema from which the stages rise, and the warning before anyone names a stage in himself.
- **Use:** in the talk; the base and the warning.

**CC Madhya 2.66 (with purport)** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/2/66/
- **Purport point (mana, Ujjvala-nilamani):** "When the lover feels novel sweetness by exchanging hearty loving words but wishes to hide his feelings by crooked means, māna is experienced." **(pranaya):** "When there is a possibility of receiving direct honor but it is avoided, that love is called praṇaya." **(unmada):** "extreme joy, misfortune and bewilderment in the heart due to separation".
- **Application (preacher's):** this is the only place Prabhupada defines mana and pranaya, and he does it inside the description of the Lord's madness in separation. The definitions are read; nothing is added.
- **Use:** in the talk; the second and third stages.

**CC Madhya 6.13 (with purport)** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/6/13/
> "Sārvabhauma Bhaṭṭācārya considered, 'The uncommon ecstatic symptoms of adhirūḍha-bhāva are appearing in the body of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu. This is very wonderful! How are they possible in the body of a human being?'"
- **Purport point (Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati quoting Rupa Gosvami):** "The loving propensity of the āśraya (devotee) toward the viṣaya (Lord) becomes so ecstatic that even after enjoying the company of the beloved, the devotee feels that his enjoyment is insufficient. At such a time, the lover sees the beloved in different ways. Such a development of ecstasy is called anurāga. When anurāga reaches its highest limit and becomes perceivable in the body, it is called bhāva. When the bodily symptoms are not very distinct, however, the emotional state is still called anurāga, not bhāva. When bhāva ecstasy is intensified, it is called mahā-bhāva. The symptoms of mahā-bhāva are visible only in the bodies of eternal associates like the gopīs."
- **Context:** Sarvabhauma Bhattacarya seeing the Lord fallen unconscious in the Jagannatha temple.
- **Use:** in the talk; the last three stages, and where the summit is.

**CC Adi 4.68–69; CC Madhya 8.160** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/adi/4/69/ · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/8/160/
> CC Adi 4.69: "Śrī Rādhā Ṭhākurāṇī is the embodiment of mahābhāva."
> CC Madhya 8.160: "The essential part of love of Godhead is called mahābhāva, transcendental ecstasy, and that ecstasy is represented by Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī."
- **Also in:** trd-45 (Radha-tattva). Here: the summit of this ladder has a name and a person.
- **Use:** in the talk; one line each.

**CC Madhya 2.11 (with purport)** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/2/11/
> "The extraordinary transformations of the body due to transcendental feelings would never have been possible for anyone but the Lord, in whose body all transformations were manifest."
- **Purport point:** "These symptoms are indicative of mahābhāva, or the highest ecstasy. Sometimes sahajiyās artificially imitate these symptoms, but experienced devotees reject such imitations immediately. The author admits herein that these symptoms are not to be found anywhere but in the body of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu."
- **Use:** in the talk; the fence around the summit.

**CC Madhya 2.45; CC Madhya 2.48 (with purport)** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/2/45/
> 2.45: "'My dear friends, I have not the slightest tinge of love of Godhead within My heart. When you see Me crying in separation, I am just falsely exhibiting a demonstration of My great fortune. Indeed, not seeing the beautiful face of Kṛṣṇa playing His flute, I continue to live My life like an insect, without purpose.'"
> 2.48: "Love for Lord Kṛṣṇa is very pure, just like the waters of the Ganges. That love is an ocean of nectar. That pure attachment to Kṛṣṇa does not conceal any spot, which would appear just like a spot of ink on a white cloth."
- **Purport point (2.48):** "Just as the black spot is prominent, so the absence of love of Godhead is prominent on the platform of pure love of Godhead."
- **Application (preacher's):** the Lord, at mahabhava, says He has no love; the higher the love, the more its smallest absence shows. This is the attitude the stages produce in the one who has them, and therefore the test of anyone who claims them.
- **Use:** in the talk; the perfect devotee's humility.

**Madhurya-kadambini, Seventh Shower, text 2c (Visvanatha Cakravarti; Gaudiya Vedanta Publications edition)** — Verified in `research/books/Madhurya-kadambini (Visvanatha Cakravarti), English 1st ed.txt`
> "In the gradual maturation of this rati, it acquires different names, such as prema, sneha, māna, praṇaya, rāga, anurāga and mahābhāva. All of these are the sthāyi-bhāva of kṛṣṇa-bhakti-rasa."
- **Also in:** trd-43 (the ingredients). Here: the acharya's confirmation that the stages are one sthayi-bhava maturing.
- **Use:** in the talk; one line.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **One substance, rising taste.** Rati becomes prema when it "softens the heart completely" and is "very much condensed and intensified" (CC Madhya
   23.7); prema then rises through seven states (19.178; 23.42). The analogy is deliberate: seed, cane, juice, molasses, sugar, candy, rock candy,
   lozenges are one substance refined (19.179); "just as the taste of sugar increases as it is gradually purified", so prema's taste increases (23.44).
   Nothing is added; what was there is purified and concentrated.
2. **All of it is sthayi-bhava.** "All these stages combined are called sthāyibhāva" (19.180); Visvanatha Cakravarti says the same (Madhurya-kadambini 7.2c).
   So the stages are not new rasas but the deepening of the one permanent love that trd-43 described, and at every stage the four ingredients make it
   rasa (19.180–182).
3. **The seven, in Prabhupada's words.** *Sneha:* the melting of the heart "is concentrated"; the lover "cannot for a moment remain without the
   association of the beloved" (19.178 purport). *Mana:* the lover "feels novel sweetness by exchanging hearty loving words but wishes to hide his
   feelings by crooked means" (2.66 purport); the English word Srila Prabhupada uses, "abhorrence" or "counterlove", names this apparent contrariness. *Pranaya:* "When
   there is a possibility of receiving direct honor but it is avoided" (2.66 purport); intimacy that refuses formality. *Raga:* affection "converts
   unhappiness into happiness"; one "can give up his own life to satisfy his beloved Kṛṣṇa" (19.178 purport). *Anuraga:* "even after enjoying the company
   of the beloved, the devotee feels that his enjoyment is insufficient", and "sees the beloved in different ways" (6.13 purport). *Bhava:* anuraga
   "reaches its highest limit and becomes perceivable in the body" (6.13 purport). *Mahabhava:* bhava "intensified" (6.13 purport); "the essential part of
   love of Godhead", "represented by Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī" (CC Madhya 8.160; CC Adi 4.69).
4. **Where the summit is.** "The symptoms of mahā-bhāva are visible only in the bodies of eternal associates like the gopīs" (6.13 purport); the bodily
   transformations of the Antya-lila "are not to be found anywhere but in the body of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu" (2.11 purport). The summit has a person,
   Radharani, and a display, the Lord in Puri (trd-47). It has no sadhaka.
5. **Why nobody grades himself.** Three fences stand together. Symptoms without a melted heart are raty-abhasa, a glimpse (23.45 purport; trd-43). Sahajiyas
   "artificially imitate these symptoms, but experienced devotees reject such imitations immediately" (2.11 purport). And the one who truly has the stages
   says, "I have not the slightest tinge of love of Godhead within My heart" (2.45), because on the white cloth of pure love the smallest absence shows like
   ink (2.48 and purport). A devotee who announces his stage has, by that announcement, shown which stage he is not at.
6. **Why study them at all.** Because the Lord taught them twice (to Rupa and to Sanatana Gosvami), because the Antya-lila cannot be read without them, and
   because "Attachment for Kṛṣṇa never wanes; it increases more and more" (19.180 purport) is the most hopeful sentence a tired resident can hear. The
   stages tell him that prema is not an end point but a beginning without end.
7. **Aspire, do not claim.** Aspiration means: chant and serve so that rati awakens (19.177); hear of the gopis' and the Lord's love with reverence (6.13; the
   Antya-lila); pray, as the acharyas pray, for the dust of those who have it (trd-41 J.4); and expect, if ever a drop comes, to feel like 2.45, not like a
   man who has arrived.
8. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *The stages are a syllabus I progress through by effort.* Effort is sadhana up to rati (19.177); the stages beyond are prema's own growth by Krishna's grace in the devotee's heart (CC Adi 4.68 purport: hladini "bestowed").
   - *Mana (abhorrence) means the devotee dislikes Krishna.* It is love hiding itself "by crooked means" while feeling "novel sweetness" (2.66 purport); a feature of intimacy, not of distaste.
   - *Senior devotees can be placed on this ladder by their behaviour.* The stages are internal and known to Krishna; symptoms can be imitated (2.11 purport) and glimpses mistaken (23.45 purport). The acharyas' rule: judge no one (trd-43's CC Madhya 23.39).
   - *Mahabhava is attainable in this body by meditation.* Its symptoms appear only in the eternal associates' bodies (6.13 purport); the sadhaka's business is to follow in their footsteps in the servant's mood (trd-44).
9. **Pitch for TRD.** Teach the seven with Prabhupada's definitions read aloud, the sugar analogy on the board, the summit located and fenced, and end with
   2.45 and 2.48 as the only correct speech about one's own love.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet the devotees and recall dps-18: the ladder ended at prema. Say that today we learn that prema itself has a ladder, seven stages Lord Caitanya named, and where that ladder ends.
2. Say: "Lord Caitanya compared it to sugar: seed, cane, juice, molasses, sugar, candy, rock candy. One substance, refined. We will learn the seven names, Prabhupada's definition of each, and then why no sadhaka ever says 'I am at this stage'."
3. Ask H.2 question 1; take three answers.
4. Ask H.2 question 2 for private writing.
5. Read CC Madhya 19.178–179 aloud and begin.

**H.2 Opening questions**
1. Which of these words have you heard in a bhajana or a class this month: sneha, mana, pranaya, raga, anuraga, bhava, mahabhava? Could you have defined it?
2. (Private) Have I ever placed myself, or a godbrother, on a ladder of advancement? What did it do to my service? One line; keep it.
3. Why would the devotee with the most love be the one who says he has none?

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Rati, then prema.** Tenderness of heart (19.177 purport); prema softens the heart completely and is condensed (23.7); glimpses exist (23.45 purport). *Transition:* and prema grows.
2. **Seven stages, one sugar.** 19.178–179; 23.42–44: refined, not added to. *Transition:* the first two.
3. **Sneha and raga.** Melting concentrated; not a moment apart; unhappiness turned to happiness; life given (19.178 purport). *Transition:* the two Prabhupada defines in the Lord's madness.
4. **Mana and pranaya.** Love hiding by crooked means; honour avoided (2.66 purport). *Transition:* the last three.
5. **Anuraga, bhava, mahabhava.** Enjoyment insufficient; perceivable in the body; intensified; only in the eternal associates (6.13 purport). *Transition:* what are all these?
6. **One sthayi-bhava.** 19.180; Madhurya-kadambini 7.2c; with the ingredients, rasa (19.181–182). *Transition:* who has the summit?
7. **Radharani, and the Lord in Puri.** CC Adi 4.69; CC Madhya 8.160; 2.11 and purport; the sahajiya rejected. *Transition:* then how do the perfect speak of their love?
8. **"Not the slightest tinge."** 2.45; the ink spot on white cloth (2.48 and purport). *Transition:* so, for us.
9. **Aspire, revere, chant, grade no one.** F.7.

## J. Stories and illustrations

**1. Yasoda never apart (SB 10.8.46 purport, as used in trd-41; the definition in CC Madhya 19.178 purport).** Prabhupada writes that mother Yasoda "was
not separated from Kṛṣṇa at any moment" and that "Even when Kṛṣṇa was grown up, He would go to Vṛndāvana and sit on the lap of mother Yaśodā." Beside it
the definition of sneha: the lover "cannot for a moment remain without the association of the beloved." *Lesson:* the Bhagavatam shows what the
Bhakti-rasamrta-sindhu defines; a stage of prema is seen in a person, not in a list.

**2. Sarvabhauma Bhattacarya in the temple (CC Madhya 6.13 and purport).** When Lord Caitanya fell unconscious before Lord Jagannatha and was carried to
Sarvabhauma's house, the great scholar "considered, 'The uncommon ecstatic symptoms of adhirūḍha-bhāva are appearing in the body of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu.
This is very wonderful! How are they possible in the body of a human being?'" The purport gives Rupa Gosvami's definitions of anuraga, bhava and mahabhava
and ends: "The symptoms of mahā-bhāva are visible only in the bodies of eternal associates like the gopīs." *Lesson:* a learned man recognised the summit
from the texts, and the texts told him it could not belong to an ordinary human; this is how the stages are to be known: in the Lord, with wonder.

**3. The Lord at the Gambhira (CC Madhya 2.7–20).** In the small room called the Gambhira the Lord "did not sleep for a moment. All night He used to grind
His mouth and head on the wall, and His face sustained injuries all over" (2.7). Though the doors were closed He would be found at the Simha-dvara, or fallen
into the sea (2.8); He ran across the sand dunes taking them for Govardhana, crying loudly (2.9). "The extraordinary transformations of the body due to
transcendental feelings would never have been possible for anyone but the Lord" (2.11). He would say: "Where is the Lord of My life, who is playing His
flute? What shall I do now? Where should I go to find the son of Mahārāja Nanda?" (2.15). And yet: "My dear friends, I have not the slightest tinge of love
of Godhead within My heart" (2.45). *Lesson:* mahabhava looks like this from outside and speaks like this from inside; the one who has it denies it, and
Krishnadasa Kaviraja says it is found in no other body. Told as the verses tell it; the detail is trd-47's.

**Illustration (writer's, not scriptural):** sugar refined is sweeter at each step and is still sugar; nobody adds honey to make rock candy. Lord Caitanya's
own analogy (CC Madhya 19.179) is the point: prema's stages are purifications of one love, not acquisitions. The illustration adds only this: the sugar
does not grade itself.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"Can I know which stage I am at?"** The stages begin after prema, and the one who has prema says he has none (CC Madhya 2.45); symptoms can be glimpses
   (23.45 purport) or imitations (2.11 purport). The honest sadhaka knows he is in sadhana-bhakti (19.177) and leaves the rest to Krishna.
2. **"Then what use is the list to me?"** To read the Caitanya-caritamrta and the acharyas' songs with understanding, to revere what Radharani and the Lord
   display, and to know that love "increases more and more" (19.180 purport). Knowledge of the summit keeps the climber climbing and keeps him humble.
3. **"Is mana really a stage of love? It sounds like a quarrel."** It is love "exchanging hearty loving words" while hiding itself "by crooked means"
   (2.66 purport), the apparent contrariness of great intimacy. Prabhupada's "abhorrence" and "counterlove" are his attempts at an English word for this.
4. **"If mahabhava is only in the gopis, why does Prabhupada teach it to us?"** Because it is the nature of Radharani, whom we worship (CC Adi 4.69; trd-45),
   and because the Lord displayed it for us in Puri (2.11 purport; trd-47). We study the summit to worship it, as we study the sun without flying to it.
5. **"A senior devotee weeps and trembles; is he at bhava?"** Bhava is anuraga "perceivable in the body" (6.13 purport), but bodily symptoms alone prove nothing
   (trd-43). Serve him; do not grade him (CC Madhya 23.39, in trd-43).
6. **"Does the ladder differ by rasa?"** Prabhupada places mahabhava in the gopis (6.13 purport) and in Radharani (8.160); for the other rasas he gives no
   separate ladder in these purports, and this page does not supply one. Requires source review before any further statement is made from the Bhakti-rasamrta-sindhu's
   Western Division, which our OCR does not hold.

## L. Case studies

**Case 1 (Invented case for discussion).** In a bhajana evening a resident says of a visiting senior, "He is surely at raga; see how he cries." Others nod; a
new devotee asks what raga is and whether he can reach it. *Dilemma:* has the resident taught or misled? *Principles:* 19.178 purport (raga defined by
unhappiness turned to happiness and life given, not by tears); 2.11 purport (imitation exists); 23.45 purport (glimpse); 2.45 (the one who has it denies it).
*Questions:* What defines raga, according to Prabhupada? Who can see it? *Analysis:* the resident graded a devotee by a bodily sign, taught the junior to do
the same, and turned the ladder into a spectator sport. The correction is to give the definition, point to where the stage is displayed (the Lord, the gopis),
and say that we chant. *Takeaway:* definitions are for reverence, not for grading.

**Case 2 (Invented case for discussion).** After reading this page a brahmacari feels hopeless: "Prema is already beyond me, and now seven more stages; what is
the use?" *Dilemma:* has the page discouraged or encouraged? *Principles:* 19.177 (sadhana leads to rati, rati to prema: the path is open); 19.180 purport
("Attachment for Kṛṣṇa never wanes; it increases more and more"); 23.44 ("its taste increases"); CC Adi 4.68 purport (hladini is bestowed). *Questions:*
Is the ladder a demand or a promise? What is his part? *Analysis:* the ladder describes what Krishna's grace does with a love that has begun; the brahmacari's
part is the beginning, sadhana with faith, and the seven stages are the promise that there is no ceiling. *Takeaway:* the stages are the most hopeful teaching in
the book, when read as grace and not as examination.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

In dps-18 we learned that devotional service, practised with faith, awakens attachment (rati) to Krishna, and that "When that attachment is intensified,
it becomes love of Godhead" (CC Madhya 19.177). Lord Caitanya told Rupa Gosvami that prema itself keeps growing: "The basic aspects of prema, when gradually
increasing to different states, are affection, abhorrence, love, attachment, further attachment, ecstasy and great ecstasy" (19.178), in Sanskrit sneha,
mana, pranaya, raga, anuraga, bhava and mahabhava. He compared this to sugar: "First there is the seed of the sugarcane, then sugarcane and then the juice
extracted from the cane. When this juice is boiled, it forms liquid molasses, then solid molasses, then sugar, candy, rock candy and finally lozenges"
(19.179); "just as the taste of sugar increases as it is gradually purified", so prema's taste increases (23.44). "All these stages combined are called
sthāyibhāva, or continuous love of Godhead" (19.180).

Srila Prabhupada defines each stage. Sneha: the melting of the heart is concentrated and the lover "cannot for a moment remain without the association of the
beloved" (19.178 purport). Mana: the lover "feels novel sweetness by exchanging hearty loving words but wishes to hide his feelings by crooked means" (2.66
purport). Pranaya: "When there is a possibility of receiving direct honor but it is avoided" (2.66 purport). Raga: affection "converts unhappiness into
happiness" and one "can give up his own life to satisfy his beloved Kṛṣṇa" (19.178 purport). Anuraga: "even after enjoying the company of the beloved, the
devotee feels that his enjoyment is insufficient." Bhava: anuraga "becomes perceivable in the body." Mahabhava: bhava "intensified" (6.13 purport). "Śrī Rādhā
Ṭhākurāṇī is the embodiment of mahābhāva" (CC Adi 4.69).

Where is the summit found? "The symptoms of mahā-bhāva are visible only in the bodies of eternal associates like the gopīs" (6.13 purport), and the bodily
transformations of Lord Caitanya in Puri "are not to be found anywhere but in the body of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu"; sahajiyas imitate them, "but experienced
devotees reject such imitations immediately" (2.11 purport). And the Lord, at that summit, said: "My dear friends, I have not the slightest tinge of love of
Godhead within My heart" (2.45), because pure love is like a white cloth on which the smallest absence of love shows like a spot of ink (2.48 and purport). So we
learn the stages to revere them, to read the Caitanya-caritamrta with understanding, and to know that "Attachment for Kṛṣṇa never wanes; it increases more and
more" (19.180 purport). We do not place ourselves or anyone else on this ladder. We chant.

## S2. Key ideas
- Prema grows through seven stages of one love, refined like sugar; nothing is added (CC Madhya 19.178–179; 23.44).
- All the stages are sthayi-bhava, permanent love (19.180).
- Prabhupada defines each stage in his purports (19.178, 2.66, 6.13).
- The summit is Radharani's and is displayed by Lord Caitanya; imitations are rejected (CC Adi 4.69; 6.13 and 2.11 purports).
- The perfect devotee says he has no love (2.45); we grade no one, and love has no ceiling (19.180 purport).

## S3. Verses to remember
- **CC Madhya 19.178:** "affection, abhorrence, love, attachment, further attachment, ecstasy and great ecstasy." For me: the seven, to recognise in the acharyas' words.
- **CC Madhya 19.180 purport:** "Attachment for Kṛṣṇa never wanes; it increases more and more as one attains different stages." For me: there is no ceiling.
- **CC Madhya 2.45:** "I have not the slightest tinge of love of Godhead within My heart." For me: how the one who has love speaks of it.

## S4. Words to know
- **Sneha, mana, pranaya, raga, anuraga, bhava, mahabhava:** the seven stages of prema (CC Madhya 19.178).
- **Sthayi-bhava:** continuous love of Godhead; all the stages together (19.180).
- **Adhirudha-bhava:** the highest form of mahabhava, seen in Lord Caitanya (6.13 and purport).
- **Raty-abhasa:** a glimpse of attachment, not the thing itself (23.45 purport).

## S5. Questions and answers
1. **Can I find out which stage I am at?** No. The stages begin after prema; the one who has prema says he has none (CC Madhya 2.45); symptoms can be glimpses or imitations (23.45, 2.11 purports). We are in sadhana (19.177), and we chant.
2. **Is "abhorrence" (mana) a stage of love?** Yes: love that feels "novel sweetness" yet hides itself "by crooked means" (2.66 purport); the contrariness of great intimacy, not dislike.
3. **If mahabhava is only in the gopis, why learn it?** To worship Radharani, whose nature it is (CC Adi 4.69), to understand the Lord's pastimes in Puri (2.11), and to know that love has no end (19.180 purport).
4. **A devotee weeps in kirtana; is he at bhava?** Bodily signs alone prove nothing (trd-43); bhava is defined by anuraga reaching its limit (6.13 purport). Serve him; do not grade him.
5. **Does this page discourage a beginner?** It should encourage him: sadhana leads to rati and prema (19.177), and from there love "increases more and more" (19.180 purport). The ladder is a promise, not an examination.
