# SB Canto 7, Ch 12, Verse 1-16

> Status line: BSS (brahmacari training) · 120 min · drafted · **sensitive** (celibacy, association with women; the purports are given in their own words under E). Study session: verse by verse. Builds on bss-16.

## A. Topic overview

|---|---|

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level
**Builds on bss-16.** bss-16 defined brahmacarya and used SB 7.12.1 (submission to the guru is part of the definition), 7.12.7 (the senses are powerful; the two kinds of brahmacari) and 7.12.11 (regulated householder life) for those points. This page does not repeat them. It is the **verse-by-verse key to the whole chapter**: the other thirteen verses, and a different point from each of those three (7.12.1: the motive "for the benefit of the guru"; 7.12.7: what the rule means for speech and reading; 7.12.11: the guru's direction over every ashram).

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| VL1 | Not taught; at most one idea, "a student serves his teacher" |
| VL2 | The aim of student life as service and learning (SB 7.12.1, 7.12.3) |
| VL3 | Daily practice: sandhya-style morning and evening worship, moderate eating (SB 7.12.2, 7.12.6) |
| SPS | The four ashrams and their duties as a frame for one's own stage |
| DPS | Association and attraction in depth (SB 7.12.9-10 with bss-21 to bss-24) |
| BSS | This page: the full chapter as a rule of life, and its Krishna conscious goal |

Not done here: the ashram duties of householders, forest-dwellers and sannyasis (SB 7.12.17 onwards); the long argument about celibacy and its benefits (bss-16, bss-17); sex desire and the fall (bss-21 to bss-24).

## C. Central message
Narada's rules for the brahmacari are not only a list of things forbidden. They describe a day built around the guru, the Lord and service, in which the senses are protected from disturbance. The rules lead to one understanding: Visnu is both inside everything and beyond everything, and a person who follows these rules in any stage of life can realise Him (SB 7.12.15-16).

## D. Learning outcomes
By the end, participants can:
1. Recount, in order, what SB 7.12.1-6 asks of a brahmacari from dawn to evening, and say how each item serves the guru and the Lord.
2. State what SB 7.12.7-10 and their purports say about speech and association, in their own words (the senses are so powerful that they may agitate even the mind of a sannyasi; woman is compared to fire and man to a butter pot).
3. Say which three things SB 7.12.12 asks a vow-keeper to give up besides ornaments and scents, and what kind of life they protect.
4. Explain how a brahmacari leaves the gurukula (SB 7.12.13-14) and why the same rules and the same guru stay with every ashram (SB 7.12.11, 7.12.16).
5. Explain the vision in SB 7.12.15 in plain words and name one habit that keeps it in mind.
6. Choose one concrete change for the coming week and know whom to talk to if they find the rules hard.

## E. Scriptural foundation
Context for all verses: Narada Muni is instructing Maharaja Yudhisthira in the duties of the four ashrams (SB 7.11-7.12). Use every verse in the talk in order; the long ones (7.12.8-10) get most time. Where I say a verse has "no purport on Vedabase", I opened the page and found none, so no purport claim is made for it.

**On 7.12.6-10 and 7.12.12.** Read the translations and the purports aloud as they stand, in Srila Prabhupada's words; the passages are given below. BIKC (ch. 9) says of these strictures: "Such strong strictures may seem odd in the context of modern social relationships, but the fact is that unless this discrimination between the sexes is reestablished, there is no hope for human society."

**SB 7.12.1** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/12/1/
> "A student should practice completely controlling his senses. He should be submissive and should have an attitude of firm friendship for the spiritual master."
- **Context:** Opening of Narada's description of the brahmacari ashram.
- **Teaching:** The student lives at the gurukula "with a great vow", "only for the benefit of the guru". The word-for-word meanings give *dasa-vat* (very humbly, like a slave) and *su-drdha-sauhrdah* (firmly, in friendship or good will).
- **Purport point:** No purport on Vedabase.
- **Application (preacher's):** bss-16 used this verse for "submission is part of the definition". The new point here is that the verse joins two attitudes: humble service and firm friendship. The student serves the guru, and the guru is also his friend. The verse also says the aim is the *guru's* benefit, not the student's own. So the training begins when the student changes the answer to the question "Whom is my work for?"
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 7.12.2** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/12/2/
> "At both junctions of day and night, namely, in the early morning and in the evening, he should be fully absorbed in thoughts of the spiritual master, fire, the sun-god and Lord Viṣṇu"
- **Teaching:** The two junctions are marked by absorption in thoughts of the guru, fire, the sun-god and Visnu, and worship by chanting the Gayatri mantra.
- **Purport point:** No purport on Vedabase.
- **Application (preacher's):** For ISKCON brahmacaris the dawn and dusk worship is lived through mangala-arati, japa and sandhya time; the lesson is the practice, not the exact ritual: the day begins and ends with remembering the Lord.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 7.12.3** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/12/3/
> "Being called by the spiritual master, the student should study the Vedic mantras regularly. Every day, before beginning his studies and at the end of his studies, the disciple should respectfully offer obeisances unto the spiritual master."
- **Teaching:** Study is taken up when called by the guru; obeisances begin and end each study.
- **Purport point:** No purport on Vedabase.
- **Application (preacher's):** The student receives knowledge from his guru; he does not take it for himself. The words "being called" remind a modern student who loves reading to wait for the guru's guidance, so that learning does not make him proud.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 7.12.4** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/12/4/
> "…dress regularly with a belt of straw and with deerskin garments. He should wear matted hair, carry a rod and waterpot and be decorated with a sacred thread, as recommended in the śāstras."
- **Teaching:** The dress of the brahmacari in Narada's description.
- **Purport point:** No purport on Vedabase.
- **Application (preacher's):** BIKC (Bhakti Vikasa Swami, *Brahmacarya in Krishna consciousness*, ch. 27) says: "Śrīla Prabhupāda, the ācārya for the modern age in the line of Nārada Muni, dressed his brahmacārīs in saffron and had them shave their heads. ISKCON brahmacārīs should have a shaved head with śikhā, and tilaka markings on twelve places of the body; wear saffron cloth; wear a kaupīna; be simple, neat, and clean; and look blissful."
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 7.12.5** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/12/5/
> "The brahmacārī should go out morning and evening to collect alms, and he should offer all that he collects to the spiritual master. He should eat only if ordered to take food by the spiritual master"
- **Teaching:** Morning and evening alms; all of it offered to the guru; eating on his order; otherwise fasting may be necessary.
- **Purport point:** No purport on Vedabase.
- **Application (preacher's):** BIKC (ch. 22) says: "By begging alms, a brahmacārī learns humility. By offering all he collects to the guru, he fortifies the mood of slavery to the spiritual master. He practices sacrifice and detachment, and practically applies the maxim that 'nothing belongs to me.'" And: "the ISKCON brahmacārī's alms collecting has been dovetailed with preaching through the most ingenious transcendental plan ever devised: book distribution." The BIKC line attributing a training-by-collecting-alms remark to a lecture of 5 April 1974 was not opened on Vedabase: Requires source review before quoting it as Srila Prabhupada's.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 7.12.6** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/12/6/
> "A brahmacārī should be quite well-behaved and gentle and should not eat or collect more than necessary. He must always be active and expert, fully believing in the instructions of the spiritual master and the śāstra."
- **Teaching:** Gentle, moderate in eating and collecting, active and expert, faithful to guru and scripture, in control of the senses; "he should associate only as much as necessary with women or those controlled by women."
- **Purport (Srila Prabhupada's words, in full):** "A brahmacārī should be very careful not to mix with women or with men addicted to women. Although when he goes out to beg alms it is necessary to talk with women and with men very much attached to women, this association should be very short, and he should talk with them only about begging alms, and not more. A brahmacārī should be very careful in associating with men who are attached to women."
- **Application (preacher's):** On "expert", BIKC (ch. 29) says: "As stated by Nārada Muni, a brahmacārī should be dakṣa, expert. (SB 7.12.6) Śrīla Prabhupāda elaborated on this by stating that a brahmacārī should be expert in everything. (Told by Prabhaviṣṇu Swami)"; it lists giving class, receiving guests, preaching, quoting slokas, cooking, Deity worship, kirtana, bhajanas and mrdanga. The "told by" remark is BIKC's report, not a Vedabase text.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 7.12.7** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/12/7/
> "A brahmacārī, or one who has not accepted the gṛhastha-āśrama [family life], must rigidly avoid talking with women or about women, for the senses are so powerful that they may agitate even the mind of a sannyāsī"
- **Teaching:** The rule covers talking with women and about women; its reason is the power of the senses.
- **Purport (Srila Prabhupada's words):** "A brahmacārī or sannyāsī should avoid talking with women or reading literature concerning talks between man and woman. The injunction restricting association with women is the basic principle of spiritual life. Associating or talking with women is never advised in any of the Vedic literatures. The entire Vedic system teaches one to avoid sex life so that one may gradually progress from brahmacarya to gṛhastha, from gṛhastha to vānaprastha, and from vānaprastha to sannyāsa and thus give up material enjoyment, which is the original cause of bondage to this material world."
- **Application (preacher's):** bss-16 used this verse for the senses' strength and the two kinds of brahmacari. The new point is practical: the rule extends to *reading* and, by extension, to what one views or scrolls (preacher's application; the purport names literature).
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 7.12.8** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/12/8/
> "If the wife of the spiritual master is young, a young brahmacārī should not allow her to care for his hair, massage his body with oil, or bathe him with affection like a mother."
- **Teaching:** The guru's wife is like a mother, but a young brahmacari should not receive these bodily services from a young guru-patni.
- **Purport (Srila Prabhupada's words):** "The relationship between the student or disciple and the wife of the spiritual master or teacher is like that between son and mother. … If the wife of the teacher is a young woman, however, a young brahmacārī should not allow such a mother to touch him. This is strictly prohibited." After the list of seven mothers (the original mother, the wife of the teacher or spiritual master, the wife of a brāhmaṇa, the king's wife, the cow, the nurse and the earth): "Unnecessary association with women, even with one's mother, sister or daughter, is strictly prohibited. This is human civilization. A civilization that allows men to mix unrestrictedly with women is an animal civilization. In Kali-yuga, people are extremely liberal, but mixing with women and talking with them as equals actually constitutes an uncivilized way of life."
- **Application (preacher's):** Read the purport as it stands. BIKC (ch. 9) quotes these last two sentences and adds: "The scriptures enjoin that we see all women as our mother."
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 7.12.9** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/12/9/
> "Woman is compared to fire, and man is compared to a butter pot. Therefore a man should avoid associating even with his own daughter in a secluded place."
- **Teaching:** The comparison is of fire and a butter pot; the advice is not to be in seclusion even with a daughter, and to associate with women only for important business.
- **Purport (Srila Prabhupada's words, in full):** "If a butter pot and fire are kept together, the butter within the pot will certainly melt. Woman is compared to fire, and man is compared to a butter pot. However advanced one may be in restraining the senses, it is almost impossible for a man to keep himself controlled in the presence of a woman, even if she is his own daughter, mother or sister. Indeed, his mind is agitated even if one is in the renounced order of life. Therefore, Vedic civilization carefully restricts mingling between men and women. If one cannot understand the basic principle of restraining association between man and woman, he is to be considered an animal. That is the purport of this verse."
- **Application (preacher's):** Read the purport as it stands. BIKC (ch. 36) applies the same image to preaching: "if a brahmacārī repeatedly preaches to the same woman, the fire and butter principle is sure to act."
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 7.12.10** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/12/10/
> "…he cannot be relieved of the conception of duality, which is epitomized by the duality between man and woman. Thus there is every chance that he will fall down because his intelligence is bewildered."
- **Teaching:** Until one is self-realised and free of identification with the body, the duality of man and woman remains, and there is a chance of falling.
- **Purport (Srila Prabhupada's words):** "Here is another important warning that a man must save himself from attraction to woman. Until one is self-realized, fully independent of the illusory conception of the material body, the duality of man and woman must undoubtedly continue, but when one is actually self-realized this distinction ceases." It quotes Bg. 5.18 (the sage's equal vision on a brahmana, cow, elephant, dog and dog-eater) and continues: "This is the test of self-realization. … Until that time, the duality continues, and the conception of man and woman also continues. In this stage, one should be extremely careful about mixing with women. No one should think himself perfect and forget the śāstric instruction that one should be very careful about associating even with his daughter, mother or sister, not to speak of other women."
- **Application (preacher's):** Give both parts as the purport gives them: the test of self-realisation is equal vision, and until then "one should be extremely careful about mixing with women." BIKC (ch. 9) quotes this purport's words on the pandita, "one who knows".
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 7.12.11** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/12/11/
> "All the rules and regulations apply equally to the householder and the sannyāsī, the member of the renounced order of life. The gṛhastha, however, is given permission by the spiritual master to indulge in sex during the period favorable for procreation."
- **Teaching:** Regulation applies in every ashram; the householder's allowance is by the guru's permission and for procreation.
- **Purport point:** It is a misunderstanding that a householder may act at any time; in every ashram one is under the spiritual master's control; the gist is *guru-vrttir vikalpena*, following the guru's order. The purport ends: only one who executes spiritual life under the spiritual master can achieve Krishna's mercy.
- **Application (preacher's):** bss-16 used this verse to answer "what about marriage?". Here the different point is the **guru's direction over every ashram**: the brahmacari is not training in a rule he will later be free of; he is training in being guided. Keep this point short. The duties of householders are not the topic of this session.
- **Use:** in the talk, one minute.

**SB 7.12.12** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/12/12/
> "…seeing a woman or painting a woman’s picture, eating meat, drinking wine, decorating the body with flower garlands, smearing scented ointment on the body, or decorating the body with ornaments. These they should give up."
- **Teaching:** The list also begins with applying powder or ointment to the eyes, massaging the head with oil and massaging the body by hand. It is addressed to those who have taken the vow of celibacy.
- **Purport point:** No purport on Vedabase.
- **Application (preacher's):** Read the list as it stands: applying powder or ointment to the eyes, massaging the head with oil, massaging the body by hand, seeing a woman or painting a woman's picture, eating meat, drinking wine, garlands, scented ointment, ornaments: "These they should give up." Follow your guru's instruction on how your temple keeps it. Photos and videos on a screen are the modern form of a painted picture (preacher's application).
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 7.12.13-14** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/12/13-14/
> "…one who is twice-born, namely a brāhmaṇa, kṣatriya or vaiśya, should reside in the gurukula under the care of the spiritual master."
- **Teaching:** Study all the Vedic literature with its supplements and the Upanisads, according to ability; if possible, reward the guru as he requests; then, on his order, leave for another ashram "as he desires".
- **Purport (Srila Prabhupada's words):** "Gurukula, or the reformatory educational institution, should be used only to understand Vedic knowledge. At the present time there are many educational institutions for training and technology, but such knowledge has nothing to do with understanding of the Absolute Truth. Technology, therefore, is meant for the śūdras, whereas the Vedas are meant for the dvijas. … At the present time, in the Age of Kali, practically everyone is a śūdra, and no one is a dvija. Therefore the condition of society has very much deteriorated." And: "It is not compulsory for a brahmacārī to become a gṛhastha. … Thus one may proceed to the sannyāsa-āśrama directly from the brahmacārī-āśrama. Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura accepted the sannyāsa-āśrama directly from the brahmacārī-āśrama."
- **Application (preacher's):** The brahmacari's training has an end. He finishes it by the guru's order and then chooses his next stage. So joining the ashram does not trap him, and leaving it by the guru's order is not a betrayal.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 7.12.15** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/12/15/
> "…in the fire, in the spiritual master, in one’s self and in all living entities — in all circumstances and conditions — the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Viṣṇu, has simultaneously entered and not entered."
- **Teaching:** The Lord is externally and internally situated, the full controller.
- **Purport point:** Realising the Lord's omnipresence is the perfect realisation to be attained by studying the Vedic literature. Wherever He is present He is present with His name, form, associates and servants. This realisation is possible if one strictly follows the ashram regulations. The purport quotes Madhvacarya on the Lord as both entered and not entered, and Bg. 9.4 (all beings are in Him, He is not in them).
- **Application (preacher's):** Read this verse together with 7.12.2. In the morning worship the student thinks of the fire, the guru and Visnu. This verse says the Lord is present in those same places. So the rules lead the student to a new way of seeing the world.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 7.12.16** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/12/16/
> "By practicing in this way, whether one be in the brahmacārī-āśrama, gṛhastha-āśrama, vānaprastha-āśrama or sannyāsa-āśrama, one must always realize the all-pervading presence of the Supreme Lord, for in this way it is possible to understand the Absolute Truth."
- **Purport point:** This is the beginning of self-realisation; one must first understand how Brahman is present everywhere and how He is acting (*brahma-jijnasa*), the real concern of human life.
- **Application (preacher's):** The closing verse answers the question "What is all this for?" The whole day, the rules and the vow exist so that the student can understand the Lord who is present everywhere. Remind the audience, as in bss-16, that brahmacarya serves devotional service and is not the final goal.
- **Use:** in the talk, as the conclusion.

**SB 1.5.23-26** (Narada's own boyhood; used in J.1) — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/5/23/
> "I was engaged in their personal service." (SB 1.5.23)
- **Teaching:** Narada, as a boy son of a maidservant, served the Vedanta-following brahmanas in the four months of rains (1.5.23); was self-controlled, did not play, was not naughty and did not speak more than required (1.5.24); once took the remnants of their food and was purified (1.5.25); heard them describe Krishna's activities and his taste for hearing increased (1.5.26).
- **Purport point (SB 1.5.23, read):** the purport says the service of the Lord begins with the service of the Lord's bona fide servants.
- **Use:** in the talk (J.1). The speaker of SB 7.12 had himself lived these rules.

## F. Philosophical explanation
**Principle.** The chapter does not give only a rule-book. It describes a student life built around three things: the guru (7.12.1-3 and 7.12.5), the Lord (7.12.2), and the vision of the Lord everywhere (7.12.15-16). The restrictions on speech, sight and company (7.12.6-10 and 7.12.12) protect that life, as a fence protects a garden.
**Why it matters.** A brahmacari who learns only the restrictions, without the main purpose, will feel dry and lonely. A brahmacari who knows the purpose will see the restrictions as a shelter. The purport of 7.12.15 says this realisation is possible if one strictly follows the rules of the ashram, so the rules and the purpose go together.
**Connections.** The duality in 7.12.10 is the same bodily conception that SB 3.31 treats in bss-52; the senses' power connects to bss-21 to bss-24; surrender to the guru to bss-27.
**Consequences.** If the guru is not at the centre (7.12.1), the rules become hard exercises with no devotion in them. If the student does not aim at the vision of 7.12.15-16, the rules become only good manners.
**Common misunderstandings.**
1. "These verses are only old custom." The purports say otherwise, in their own words: "The injunction restricting association with women is the basic principle of spiritual life" (7.12.7); "Woman is compared to fire, and man is compared to a butter pot. … If one cannot understand the basic principle of restraining association between man and woman, he is to be considered an animal" (7.12.9); "a man must save himself from attraction to woman" (7.12.10). The purport of 7.12.10 sets the test of self-realisation: equal vision.
2. "This is only for the ancient gurukula." The verses are about training the senses and the heart; the forms (7.12.4) adapt, the principle does not. How a temple adapts is for its leaders.
3. "A householder is free." 7.12.11: the guru directs every ashram.
4. "Rules without purpose." 7.12.2, 7.12.15 and 7.12.16 name the purpose.
**How to pitch it.** BSS audience: serious, introspective, preparing to join the ashram. Put the Lord at the centre; read the safeguards and their purports as they stand.

## H. Opening and engagement
**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes).**
1. Offer a short prayer or one round of kirtana; say the title once.
2. Say: "Today we read Narada Muni's description of a student's day. We will go verse by verse. Notice who he puts at the centre of the day."
3. Read SB 7.12.1 slowly, English only; pause three seconds.
4. Say: "By the end we will see why the chapter ends with the Lord everywhere, not with a rule."
**H.2 Opening questions.**
1. "If you wrote a perfect daily timetable for a student, what would be in it?" (Take three answers; write them up; compare with Narada's at the end.)
2. "Which part of a hard routine becomes joyful after some weeks, and why?"
3. "Who in your life do you serve without being paid? What does it do to you?"

## I. Core teaching points
**I.1 A day built around the guru and the Lord (SB 7.12.1-3).** The student lives "for the benefit of the guru"; dawn and dusk belong to the guru, fire, sun-god and Visnu; study is taken up when called and begins and ends with obeisances. Illustration (preacher's): before every flight a pilot does safety checks and contacts the control tower. The checks are not the flight, but the flight is not safe without them. In the same way, morning and evening worship keep the whole day connected to the Lord. End: ask the audience to think about whom their work is for. Transition: what does this person wear and eat?
**I.2 Simple dress, alms and moderate food (SB 7.12.4-6).** Dress that marks the vow; alms collected morning and evening and given to the guru; eating on his order; gentleness; moderation; expertise; faith in guru and scripture. Point: the verses train humility (begging), detachment (giving all), and fitness for service (expertise). End: ask the audience, "Which parts of my day are for my comfort, and which parts are for service?" Transition: the biggest test, speech and company.
**I.3 Guarding the senses through speech and association (SB 7.12.6-10).** Short, business-only contact when alms-seeking (6); avoid talking with women or about women, and avoid literature of man-woman talk (7); boundaries kept even with a young guru-patni who is as a mother (8); do not be alone with even a daughter (9); until the bodily conception goes, the duality stays (10). Read the purports in Srila Prabhupada's words (E): "Unnecessary association with women, even with one's mother, sister or daughter, is strictly prohibited. This is human civilization" (7.12.8); "it is almost impossible for a man to keep himself controlled in the presence of a woman, even if she is his own daughter, mother or sister" (7.12.9); "one should be extremely careful about mixing with women. No one should think himself perfect" (7.12.10). End: the test of self-realisation is equal vision (Bg. 5.18, quoted in the purport of 7.12.10); until then, the purport says, extreme care. The day is filled with Krishna's names and with service. Transition: who decides how to keep all this?
**I.4 The guru's direction in every ashram (SB 7.12.11-12).** Rules bind every ashram; permission comes from the guru. The list of 7.12.12: pampering the body with oils and scents, adornment, gazing at women or pictures of them, meat and wine. Point: the verse describes a life that is simple, active and protected from disturbance. End: ask each person to choose one item from the list and honestly think about it this week. Transition: how does it end?
**I.5 Graduation and vision (SB 7.12.13-16).** Study and reward the guru; leave on his order for the ashram of one's choice (which may be sannyasa directly: the purport gives Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura as an example); realise the Lord entered and not entered, in the fire, the guru, oneself and all beings; do it in any ashram. End: the brahmacari practises seeing the Lord in his guru, in his service and in every heart. In this way the whole day becomes devotional service.

## J. Stories and illustrations
**J.1 Narada the boy servant (SB 1.5.23-26).** The story is told in full on bss-44; here say only this: in his previous life Narada was a maidservant's son who served Vedanta-following brahmanas through the rainy season, was self-controlled and spoke only as needed, and heard them speak of Krishna until his taste for hearing grew (SB 1.5.23-26). Devotional lesson: the speaker of SB 7.12 learned the life he describes by serving devotees and hearing about Krishna; service first, taste next. Link to 7.12.1, 7.12.3, 7.12.6.
**J.2 Invented illustration (labelled):** a young man volunteers at a community kitchen. In the first week he is unhappy that he is asked only to wash pots and is not allowed to cook. The cook is kind to him and trusts him. By the sixth week, washing pots is his favourite time of day, because the cook teaches him while they work. The point is SB 7.12.1 (humble service together with friendship) and SB 7.12.5 (offering what one gathers). Trust grows through service. The service becomes devotion when the cook is a devotee and the meal is offered to Krishna.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections
1. **"Doesn't 7.12.7 say women are the problem?"** Answer in the verse's and purport's words: "the senses are so powerful that they may agitate even the mind of a sannyāsī"; "The injunction restricting association with women is the basic principle of spiritual life." The purport of 7.12.10: "a man must save himself from attraction to woman"; the test of self-realisation is equal vision (Bg. 5.18). BIKC (ch. 9): "The scriptures enjoin that we see all women as our mother."
2. **"Why is the guru's wife handled with such distance if she is like a mother (7.12.8)?"** The purport: she is a mother (guru-patni) and may care for the disciple in a motherly way; "If the wife of the teacher is a young woman, however, a young brahmacārī should not allow such a mother to touch him. This is strictly prohibited."
3. **"What is a modern temple supposed to do about begging and deerskin (7.12.4-5)?"** BIKC says the traditional dress and alms are adapted today: saffron, shaved head, book distribution. Ask your temple's leaders and your guru for the local standard.
4. **"What if I struggle with attraction, loneliness or shame?"** That is common and not a mark of failure. Seek the help your path offers: a trusted senior devotee or mentor, a qualified counsellor, and, if you feel unsafe or overwhelmed, a crisis service (in India, Tele-MANAS 14416; elsewhere, your local crisis line). The verses give a direction (service, chanting, hearing, guidance), not a verdict on you.
5. **"Do these rules apply if I intend to marry?"** The brahmacari stage is training and the exit is by the guru's order (7.12.13-14). Seek his guidance.
6. **"The purports use strong words."** Read them as they stand and give the purports' own reasons: "This is human civilization. A civilization that allows men to mix unrestrictedly with women is an animal civilization" (7.12.8); "If one cannot understand the basic principle of restraining association between man and woman, he is to be considered an animal. That is the purport of this verse" (7.12.9); "Here is another important warning that a man must save himself from attraction to woman" (7.12.10). BIKC (ch. 9): "unless this discrimination between the sexes is reestablished, there is no hope for human society."
7. **"Is the verse about the Lord 'entering and not entering' just philosophy?"** The purport says it is the realisation reached through the Vedic study and through following the ashram rules (7.12.15), and that the beginning of self-realisation is to see the Lord everywhere (7.12.16).

## L. Case studies
**L.1 Invented case for discussion.** A brahmacari is asked to bring prasadam to a family's home. The mother of the family greets him warmly and offers to talk about his studies and to help him with his torn clothes. *Dilemma:* he wants to be warm and not offend. *Principles:* SB 7.12.6 (association only as much as necessary), 7.12.7 (speech), 7.12.8 (respect without bodily care). *Questions:* How can he be gracious and brief? What topic keeps the visit about service? Who can he ask for the right etiquette afterwards? *Analysis:* greet with respect, say what he came for, refer the sewing to the temple, thank them. *Takeaway:* the purport of 7.12.6: "this association should be very short, and he should talk with them only about begging alms, and not more."
**L.2 Invented case for discussion (extension).** A student of the chapter finds the rules dry and feels the temple is "just discipline". *Principles:* SB 7.12.1 (friendship), 7.12.15-16 (vision), SB 1.5.26 (taste through hearing). *Questions:* what is missing from his day? *Analysis:* hearing about Krishna and service with devotees are the source of taste in Narada's own story. *Takeaway:* add hearing and service before adding rules.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary
Narada Muni tells how a student of the Vedic school, a brahmacari, lives. He lives at his teacher's place with a great vow, controls his senses, is humble and a firm friend of his guru, and acts only for the guru's benefit (SB 7.12.1). At dawn and dusk he worships, thinking of the guru, fire, the sun and Visnu (7.12.2). He studies when called and bows before and after study (7.12.3). He dresses simply (7.12.4), collects alms and gives it all to the guru, eating when the guru orders (7.12.5). He is gentle, eats little, is active and skilled, and has faith in the guru and scripture (7.12.6).
Narada also gives safeguards: avoid talking with women or about women, since the senses are strong (7.12.7); keep a respectful distance even from a young guru's wife who is like a mother (7.12.8); do not stay alone in a secluded place even with a daughter (7.12.9); until a person is self-realised, the sense of man and woman remains, so there is a risk of falling (7.12.10). Srila Prabhupada's purports say: "Unnecessary association with women, even with one's mother, sister or daughter, is strictly prohibited. This is human civilization" (7.12.8); "it is almost impossible for a man to keep himself controlled in the presence of a woman, even if she is his own daughter, mother or sister" (7.12.9); "a man must save himself from attraction to woman", and the test of self-realisation is to see every living being with equal vision (7.12.10). All ashrams follow the guru's direction (7.12.11); the student gives up body-pampering, gazing at women, meat and wine (7.12.12). After studying, he leaves by his guru's order for the ashram he chooses (7.12.13-14). Narada ends with the purpose: the Lord is present in the fire, the guru, oneself and all beings, and in any ashram one should realise this (7.12.15-16).

## S2. Key ideas
- A brahmacari's day is built around the guru and the Lord.
- Simple dress, alms given to the guru and moderate food train humility.
- The safeguards on speech and company are, in the purport's words, "the basic principle of spiritual life" (7.12.7).
- The guru guides every ashram.
- The goal is to realise the Lord everywhere.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **SB 7.12.1**: Be submissive and a firm friend to the guru, living for his benefit. *For me:* my training is a friendship.
- **SB 7.12.7**: Avoid talking with women or about women, because the senses are powerful. *For me:* I guard my heart with simple habits and good company.
- **SB 7.12.16**: In every ashram, realise the all-pervading presence of the Lord. *For me:* the day is for seeing and serving Him.

## S4. Words to know
- **Brahmacari:** a celibate student who lives simply and serves his guru.
- **Gurukula:** the home of the guru where the student lives and studies.
- **Alms:** food or gifts that a student gathers and offers to his guru.
- **Ashram:** a stage of life: student, householder, retired, renounced.
- **Sannyasi:** a person in the renounced order of life.
- **Gayatri:** a sacred mantra chanted at dawn and dusk.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *Why does the student give all his alms to the guru?* So that he learns humility and that nothing is his; the verse says he offers all to the guru (SB 7.12.5).
2. *Why are the rules so strict?* The verse says the senses are so powerful that they may agitate even a sannyasi (SB 7.12.7); the purport says "a man must save himself from attraction to woman", and that the test of self-realisation is equal vision of all souls (SB 7.12.10).
3. *Can a brahmacari go on to sannyasa?* Yes; he may take any other ashram when his guru orders (SB 7.12.13-14).
4. *What if I find the rules hard?* Speak with a trusted senior devotee or mentor; a qualified counsellor can help; and if you feel overwhelmed, call a crisis line (Tele-MANAS 14416 in India, or your local service). Having a struggle is not shameful.

## S6. Check your understanding
1. When does the student worship, according to SB 7.12.2?
2. What does he do with the alms (SB 7.12.5)?
3. Why does the verse say to avoid talking about women (SB 7.12.7)?
4. Who gives the order to leave the gurukula (SB 7.12.13-14)?
5. What should be realised in every ashram (SB 7.12.16)?

**Answers:** 1. At both junctions of the day, early morning and evening (SB 7.12.2). 2. Offers all to the spiritual master (SB 7.12.5). 3. The senses are so powerful that they may agitate even a sannyasi's mind (SB 7.12.7). 4. The guru (SB 7.12.13-14). 5. The all-pervading presence of the Supreme Lord (SB 7.12.16).
