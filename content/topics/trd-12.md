# Siksastaka: the mood of the perfect devotee

> **TRD · up to 180 min · review: drafted (30 Oct 2026).** Lord Caitanya's eight verses of instruction, read in the order
> and with the explanation He Himself gives in Caitanya-caritamrta Antya 20, with Srila Prabhupada's purports. Verses 2–3
> on chanting are used in TRD-08; love in separation in depth is TRD-47 (`sensitive: true`); Srimati Radharani is TRD-45.
> This page reads the eight as one prayer: the mood in which a resident is meant to live.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on TRD-08 (the holy name's stages) and DPS-11 (humility): this page does not re-teach namabhasa or the Vaisnava qualities; it reads the Lord's own prayer as a whole and lets Him explain it.

The Siksastaka is sung in every temple and quoted in every class. Residents know the first three verses by heart. Few have read how Lord Caitanya Himself explains each verse to Svarupa Damodara and Ramananda Raya in CC Antya 20, or noticed that He "had formerly composed these eight verses to teach people in general" and "now... personally tasted the meaning" (20.64). A resident is meant to live inside these verses: that is why they are here.

## C. Central message

"If anyone recites or hears these eight verses of instruction by Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, his ecstatic love and devotion for Kṛṣṇa increase day by day" (CC Antya 20.65). The eight verses are one movement: the holy name cleanses the heart (1); I lack taste for it because of my offences (2); the humble can chant always (3); I want nothing but unmotivated service (4); I am Your eternal servant fallen into this ocean (5); when will I chant with tears? (6); a moment without You is an age (7); whatever You do, You alone are the Lord of My heart (8). The perfect devotee's mood is humility, eagerness and surrender that does not bargain.

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Recite the eight verses in order and say which CC Antya 20 text each is.
2. Give for each verse the meaning Lord Caitanya attaches to it in His own words (20.13–14, 17–19, 22–26, 30, 33–34, 37, 40–42, 48–60).
3. Explain the progression from verse 1 to verse 8 and why the Lord "tasted" verses He had composed "to teach people in general" (20.64).
4. State the mark of real love given in 20.28 and why claiming a stage contradicts the verses.
5. Take verses 3 and 4 as a daily examination and say exactly how (P).

## E. Scriptural foundation

All eight verses and the Lord's explanations are in CC Antya 20 (Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/20/12/). The purport to 20.12 says: "This is the first verse of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu's Śikṣāṣṭaka. The other seven verses are found in texts 16, 21, 29, 32, 36, 39 and 47." The Lord speaks them, in Puri, to Svarupa Damodara and Ramananda Raya (20.8), after saying that "chanting the holy names is the most feasible means of salvation in this Age of Kali" (20.8) and quoting SB 11.5.32 (20.10).

**Verse 1: CC Antya 20.12** (ceto-darpana-marjanam)
> "Let there be all victory for the chanting of the holy name of Lord Kṛṣṇa, which can cleanse the mirror of the heart and stop the miseries of the blazing fire of material existence. That chanting is the waxing moon that spreads the white lotus of good fortune for all living entities. It is the life and soul of all education. The chanting of the holy name of Kṛṣṇa expands the blissful ocean of transcendental life. It gives a cooling effect to everyone and enables one to taste full nectar at every step."
- **The Lord's explanation (20.11, 20.13–14):** chanting frees one "from all undesirable habits", "is the means of awakening all good fortune and initiating the flow of waves of love for Kṛṣṇa" (20.11); it destroys "the sinful condition of material existence", purifies "the unclean heart" and awakens "all varieties of devotional service" (20.13); "one awakens his love for Kṛṣṇa and tastes transcendental bliss. Ultimately, one attains the association of Kṛṣṇa" (20.14).
- **Also in:** SB 1.2.17 purport: "Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, in His Śikṣāṣṭaka, declares clearly that the holy name of the Lord has all the potencies of the Lord."
- **Use:** in the talk.

**Verse 2: CC Antya 20.16** (namnam akari)
> "My Lord, O Supreme Personality of Godhead, in Your holy name there is all good fortune for the living entity, and therefore You have many names, such as 'Kṛṣṇa' and 'Govinda,' by which You expand Yourself. You have invested all Your potencies in those names, and there are no hard and fast rules for remembering them. My dear Lord, although You bestow such mercy upon the fallen, conditioned souls by liberally teaching Your holy names, I am so unfortunate that I commit offenses while chanting the holy name, and therefore I do not achieve attachment for chanting."
- **Context (20.15):** "Lamentation and humility awoke within Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu... By hearing the meaning of that verse, one can forget all unhappiness and lamentation."
- **The Lord's explanation (20.17–19):** "Because people vary in their desires, You have distributed various holy names" (20.17); "Regardless of time or place, one who chants the holy name, even while eating or sleeping, attains all perfection" (20.18); "You have invested Your full potencies in each individual holy name, but I am so unfortunate that I have no attachment for chanting Your holy names" (20.19).
- **Use:** in the talk (the offences themselves: DPS-06; the stages: TRD-08).

**Verse 3: CC Antya 20.21** (trnad api sunicena); also CC Adi 17.31
> "One who thinks himself lower than the grass, who is more tolerant than a tree, and who does not expect personal honor but is always prepared to give all respect to others can very easily always chant the holy name of the Lord."
- **Context (20.20):** "hear from Me the symptoms of how one should chant the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra to awaken very easily one's dormant love for Kṛṣṇa."
- **The Lord's explanation (20.22–26):** the chanter, "although he is very exalted... thinks himself lower than the grass on the ground, and like a tree, he tolerates everything in two ways" (20.22): "When a tree is cut down, it does not protest, and even when drying up, it does not ask anyone for water" (20.23); it gives "its fruits, flowers and whatever else it possesses to anyone and everyone" and "tolerates scorching heat and torrents of rain, yet it still gives shelter to others" (20.24). "Although a Vaiṣṇava is the most exalted person, he is prideless and gives all respect to everyone, knowing everyone to be the resting place of Kṛṣṇa" (20.25). "If one chants the holy name of Lord Kṛṣṇa in this manner, he will certainly awaken his dormant love" (20.26).
- **Purport point (CC Adi 17.31):** "The grass is specifically mentioned in this verse because everyone tramples upon it yet the grass never protests. This example indicates that a spiritual master or leader should not be proud of his position; being always humbler than an ordinary common man, he should go on preaching."
- **Use:** in the talk; the resident's daily examination (P).

**Verse 4: CC Antya 20.29** (na dhanam na janam)
> "O Lord of the universe, I do not desire material wealth, materialistic followers, a beautiful wife or fruitive activities described in flowery language. All I want, life after life, is unmotivated devotional service to You."
- **Context (20.27–28):** "His humility increased, and He began praying to Kṛṣṇa that He could discharge pure devotional service" (20.27). "Wherever there is a relationship of love of Godhead, its natural symptom is that the devotee does not think himself a devotee. Instead, he always thinks that he has not even a drop of love for Kṛṣṇa" (20.28).
- **The Lord's explanation (20.30):** "I only pray that by Your causeless mercy You give Me pure devotional service to You, life after life."
- **Use:** in the talk; 20.28 is the test against claiming a stage.

**Verse 5: CC Antya 20.32** (ayi nanda-tanuja)
> "O My Lord, O Kṛṣṇa, son of Mahārāja Nanda, I am Your eternal servant, but because of My own fruitive acts I have fallen into this horrible ocean of nescience. Now please be causelessly merciful to Me. Consider Me a particle of dust at Your lotus feet."
- **Context (20.31):** "In great humility, considering Himself a conditioned soul of the material world."
- **The Lord's explanation (20.33–34):** "I am Your eternal servant, but I forgot Your Lordship. Now I have fallen into the ocean of nescience and have been conditioned by the external energy" (20.33); "giving Me a place with the particles of dust at Your lotus feet so that I may engage in the service of Your Lordship as Your eternal servant" (20.34).
- **Use:** in the talk.

**Verse 6: CC Antya 20.36** (nayanam galad-asru-dharaya)
> "My dear Lord, when will My eyes be beautified by filling with tears that constantly glide down as I chant Your holy name? When will My voice falter and all the hairs on My body stand erect in transcendental happiness as I chant Your holy name?"
- **Context (20.35):** "Natural humility and eagerness then awoke."
- **The Lord's explanation (20.37):** "Without love of Godhead, My life is useless. Therefore I pray that You accept Me as Your servant and give Me the salary of ecstatic love of God."
- **Use:** in the talk; with the caution of K.3 (prayed for, never produced).

**Verse 7: CC Antya 20.39** (yugayitam nimesena)
> "My Lord Govinda, because of separation from You, I consider even a moment a great millennium. Tears flow from My eyes like torrents of rain, and I see the entire world as void."
- **Context (20.38):** "Separation from Kṛṣṇa awoke various mellows of distress, lamentation and humility. Thus Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu spoke like a crazy man."
- **The Lord's explanation (20.40–42):** "a day never ends, for every moment seems like a millennium... My eyes are like clouds in the rainy season" (20.40); "The three worlds have become void because of separation from Govinda. I feel as if I were burning alive in a slow fire" (20.41); "Lord Kṛṣṇa has become indifferent to Me just to test My love, and My friends say, 'Better to disregard Him'" (20.42).
- **Use:** in the talk, read as the text gives it; depth in TRD-47.

**Verse 8: CC Antya 20.47** (aslisya va pada-ratam)
> "Let Kṛṣṇa tightly embrace this maidservant who has fallen at His lotus feet, or let Him trample Me or break My heart by never being visible to Me. He is a debauchee, after all, and can do whatever He likes, but still He alone, and no one else, is the worshipable Lord of My heart."
- **Context (20.43–46):** the verse is spoken in the mood of Srimati Radharani; "as soon as He did so, He felt like Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī" (20.46).
- **The Lord's explanation (20.48, 20.52, 20.60):** "Kṛṣṇa is the Lord of My life in all conditions, whether He shows Me affection or kills Me by giving Me unhappiness" (20.49, in 20.48–49); "I do not mind My personal distress. I only wish for the happiness of Kṛṣṇa... if He feels great happiness in giving Me distress, that distress is the best of My happiness" (20.52); "Service to My lover is the home of happiness and is more sweet than direct union with Him" (20.60).
- **Then (20.62, 20.64–65):** "In Vṛndāvana there is not a trace of personal sense gratification. It is to advertise such pure love in this material world that Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu has written the previous verse" (20.62). "The Lord had formerly composed these eight verses to teach people in general. Now He personally tasted the meaning of the verses" (20.64). "If anyone recites or hears these eight verses... his ecstatic love and devotion for Kṛṣṇa increase day by day" (20.65).
- **Use:** in the talk, read as given; the gopis' mood and its non-imitation are TRD-13 and TRD-44.

**Bhaktivinoda Thakura's *Sanmodana-bhasya*** (commentary on the Siksastaka): **Requires source review.** The two Desire Tree files in `research/books/` (`Bhaktivinoda_Thakura_Sri_Sanmodana_Bhasyam.txt`, `Bhaktivinoda_Thakura_Sri_Siksastaka.txt`) have a scrambled font and could not be read (30 Oct 2026). Nothing from them is quoted here. Until a readable edition is checked, teach from CC Antya 20 alone, which is complete in itself.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **One prayer, eight steps.** The Lord does not give eight separate teachings; the text shows one mood rising. Verse 1 states what the name does; verse 2 confesses that the speaker lacks taste for it; verse 3 gives the mood that removes the block; verse 4 removes every other desire; verse 5 states who I am (eternal servant, fallen); verse 6 asks for the signs of love; verses 7–8 are love itself, in separation and in surrender without conditions. Krsnadasa Kaviraja marks each turn: "lamentation and humility awoke" (20.15), "His humility increased" (20.27), "natural humility and eagerness" (20.35), "separation from Kṛṣṇa awoke various mellows" (20.38).
2. **Where the resident lives.** Verses 1–4 are the sadhaka's daily life: chant, admit the lack of taste, be lower than the grass, want nothing else. Verses 5–6 are his prayer. Verses 7–8 are what he hears about with faith; they describe the goal, "to teach people in general" (20.64), and are not a mood to adopt by effort (TRD-13).
3. **The mark of love is not thinking oneself a devotee.** 20.28: "the devotee does not think himself a devotee. Instead, he always thinks that he has not even a drop of love for Kṛṣṇa." This is why the Lord Himself says "I have no attachment" (20.19). Anyone who announces his stage has, by this verse, described his absence from it.
4. **Humility is the technique.** The Lord introduces verse 3 as "the symptoms of how one should chant... to awaken very easily one's dormant love" (20.20). Humility is not a virtue added to chanting; it is the way of chanting. The purport to CC Adi 17.31 applies it to leaders: "a spiritual master or leader should not be proud of his position."
5. **Pure desire is the absence of three things and the presence of one.** Verse 4 names wealth, followers, a beautiful wife and flowery fruitive activities; it asks for "unmotivated devotional service... life after life". A resident has renounced the first three outwardly; the verse examines whether he still wants them, and whether his service has a motive.
6. **Verses 7–8 are given as they are.** The text says "debauchee" and "trample Me". These are the words of the Lord in the mood of Radharani, and Prabhupada's translation keeps them. We read them aloud as written and do not explain them away; the Lord's own gloss follows (20.48–60), and their depth is TRD-47 and TRD-44.
7. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *"The Siksastaka is a song for kirtana."* It is that; it is also the Lord's own explanation of His teaching (20.64).
   - *"Verse 3 means never correcting anyone."* 20.25: the Vaisnava "gives all respect to everyone, knowing everyone to be the resting place of Kṛṣṇa"; the purport to CC Adi 17.31 says the humble leader "should go on preaching". Humility and responsibility are not opposites.
   - *"Verse 6 asks us to cry."* It asks "when will" the tears come. It is a prayer for love's signs, not an instruction to produce them (K.3; TRD-13).
   - *"Verse 8 is only for the gopis."* It is "to advertise such pure love in this material world" (20.62). We hear it to know what pure love is, not to claim it.
8. **Pitch for TRD.** Read every verse aloud in Sanskrit and English, then the Lord's own explanation, before any comment of your own. The Lord has already given the class.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet everyone. Say: "Today we read Lord Caitanya's eight verses in the order He spoke them, and we let Him explain each one. Nothing of ours comes first."
2. Recite or sing the eight verses together, in Sanskrit, without commentary.
3. Ask H.2 question 1 for private writing (two minutes).
4. Ask H.2 question 2; take three answers; thank each by name.
5. Say: "Krsnadasa Kaviraja tells us the Lord composed these verses to teach people in general, and then tasted them Himself. We begin in Puri, with two friends."

**H.2 Opening questions**
1. (Private) Which of the eight verses do you know best? Which have you never thought about?
2. The Lord says in verse 2, "I have no attachment for chanting Your holy names." Why would the Lord say this?
3. In one sentence: what is verse 3 asking us to do while we chant?

## I. Core teaching points

1. **The name cleanses and gives everything (verse 1).** 20.12–14. *Example:* the mirror of the heart; "waves of love" (20.11). *Transition:* then why do I not taste it?
2. **I lack taste because of offence, and the Lord confesses it first (verse 2).** 20.16–19. *Example:* the Lord in "lamentation and humility" (20.15). *Transition:* what removes the block?
3. **Humility is how one chants (verse 3).** 20.20–26; CC Adi 17.31 purport. *Example:* the tree cut down does not protest; the leader humbler than a common man. *Transition:* and what should the chanter want?
4. **Nothing but unmotivated service, life after life (verse 4).** 20.29–30; 20.28 on not thinking oneself a devotee. *Transition:* who is praying?
5. **The eternal servant fallen into the ocean (verse 5); the servant who asks for the salary of love (verse 6).** 20.32–34; 20.36–37. *Transition:* and love itself?
6. **Separation and surrender without conditions (verses 7–8).** 20.39–42; 20.47–60, read as given; "to advertise such pure love" (20.62). *Transition:* 20.64–65 and P.

## J. Stories and illustrations

**The Lord's last twelve years, as Krsnadasa Kaviraja frames them** (CC Antya 20.3–8, 20.61–70). The Lord stayed at the Gambhira with Svarupa Damodara and Ramananda Raya; "He would recite His own verses, expressing their meanings and emotions, and thus enjoy tasting them with these two friends" (20.6); "sometimes the Lord would be absorbed in a particular emotion and would stay awake all night reciting related verses" (20.7). After the eighth verse, "His mind was unsteady. Transformations of transcendental love spread throughout His entire body, and He could not sustain His body and mind" (20.61). "For twelve years, Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu remained in that state day and night" (20.69). *Lesson:* the verses we sing were lived by their author; we recite what He tasted.

**The tree and the grass** (CC Antya 20.22–25). Not a story but the Lord's own picture: the tree "when it is cut down, does not protest, and even when drying up, it does not ask anyone for water"; it gives everything "to anyone and everyone" and "tolerates scorching heat and torrents of rain, yet it still gives shelter to others". *Lesson:* this is the chanter's posture toward everyone in the ashram.

**The Lord confesses no attachment** (CC Antya 20.15–19). See TRD-08 J; here read as the second step of the prayer.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"Why does the Lord say He commits offences and has no attachment? He is the Lord."**
   - 20.64: He "composed these eight verses to teach people in general" and then "personally tasted" them. He speaks as the devotee so that the devotee has words. 20.28 gives the principle: where there is love, the devotee "always thinks that he has not even a drop of love for Kṛṣṇa."
2. **"Verse 3 says to tolerate everything. Must a resident tolerate wrongdoing in the ashram?"**
   - The tree tolerates what is done to itself and still gives shelter to others (20.23–24). Verse 3 is about one's own honour and comfort. Protecting others, correcting under authority and preaching remain duties: the purport to CC Adi 17.31 says the humble leader "should go on preaching". DPS-07 and TRD-19 treat correction among devotees.
3. **"Verse 6 prays for tears. Should I try to feel them?"**
   - The Lord asks "when will" they come; He does not produce them. TRD-13 gives Prabhupada's warning (NOD 16) that imitated symptoms are "mere imitation" with "no value". Pray the verse; leave the signs to Krishna.
4. **"Verse 8 calls Krishna a debauchee. Should we read that aloud?"**
   - Yes, as written: the words are the Lord's in the mood of Radharani, kept in Prabhupada's translation, and the Lord's own explanation follows (20.48–60). We neither skip nor soften the text; we also do not expound its inner meaning here. That is TRD-47 and TRD-44, reviewed by the head.
5. **"Can a resident pray verses 7 and 8 honestly?"**
   - He can recite and hear them; 20.65 promises that whoever "recites or hears these eight verses" grows in love. He does not claim their mood. The sadhaka lives in verses 1–4 and prays 5–6.
6. **"Is there an acharya's commentary?"**
   - Bhaktivinoda Thakura's *Sanmodana-bhasya*. Its text in our research folder is unreadable (E, "Requires source review"); until a checked edition is available, teach from CC Antya 20, which carries the Lord's own commentary.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The temple commander.** Balarama dasa manages the ashram well and is respected. He sings trnad api every morning. A junior devotee corrected him publicly about a mistake in the Deity schedule; he did not speak to that devotee for a week.
- **Dilemma:** Which verse did he sing and which did he live? What does the Lord's picture of the tree ask of him?
- **Principles:** CC Antya 20.21–25; CC Adi 17.31 purport ("a spiritual master or leader should not be proud of his position").
- **Questions:** What was cut down? What did the tree do? How can he keep his responsibility and lose the pride?
- **Takeaway:** thank the junior devotee the same day; correct the schedule; keep managing.

**Case 2: The resident who has arrived.** Syama dasa tells newer devotees that he has reached ruci and sometimes "feels separation like verse 7". Juniors are impressed and a little afraid of him.
- **Principles:** CC Antya 20.28 (the devotee does not think himself a devotee); 20.19 (the Lord's own "no attachment"); 20.64 (verses composed "to teach people in general").
- **Questions:** What does 20.28 say about anyone who announces his love? Which verses is a sadhaka meant to live in, and which to hear?
- **Takeaway:** the claim is itself the symptom; a senior devotee speaks to him privately; he returns to verses 2–4.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first; thank every answer)
1. How many verses, and where are they in Caitanya-caritamrta? *Expected:* eight; CC Antya 20.12, 16, 21, 29, 32, 36, 39, 47.
2. In verse 1, what does the name cleanse? *Expected:* the mirror of the heart.
3. Why, in verse 2, does the speaker lack attachment for the name? *Expected:* because of offences while chanting.
4. What two ways does the tree tolerate (20.23)? *Expected:* cut down, it does not protest; drying, it does not ask for water.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Lord Caitanya composed eight verses of instruction, the Siksastaka, "to teach people in general", and in His last years in Puri He "personally tasted the meaning of the verses" with His two friends Svarupa Damodara and Ramananda Raya (CC Antya 20.64). Caitanya-caritamrta Antya 20 gives the eight verses and the Lord's own explanation of each. Verse 1 (20.12): the chanting of Krishna's name "can cleanse the mirror of the heart", stops "the blazing fire of material existence" and lets one "taste full nectar at every step". Verse 2 (20.16): Krishna has put all His power into His names and made no hard rules for chanting them, "but I am so unfortunate that I commit offenses while chanting the holy name, and therefore I do not achieve attachment for chanting". Verse 3 (20.21): "One who thinks himself lower than the grass, who is more tolerant than a tree, and who does not expect personal honor but is always prepared to give all respect to others can very easily always chant the holy name." The Lord explains: a tree "when it is cut down, does not protest, and even when drying up, it does not ask anyone for water", yet "still gives shelter to others" (20.23–24). Verse 4 (20.29): "I do not desire material wealth, materialistic followers, a beautiful wife or fruitive activities... All I want, life after life, is unmotivated devotional service to You." Just before it the text gives the mark of real love: "the devotee does not think himself a devotee. Instead, he always thinks that he has not even a drop of love for Kṛṣṇa" (20.28).

Verse 5 (20.32): "I am Your eternal servant, but because of My own fruitive acts I have fallen into this horrible ocean of nescience... Consider Me a particle of dust at Your lotus feet." Verse 6 (20.36): "when will My eyes be beautified by filling with tears... as I chant Your holy name?" The Lord explains, "Without love of Godhead, My life is useless" (20.37). Verse 7 (20.39): "because of separation from You, I consider even a moment a great millennium... I see the entire world as void." Verse 8 (20.47), spoken in the mood of Srimati Radharani: "Let Kṛṣṇa tightly embrace this maidservant... or let Him trample Me or break My heart by never being visible to Me. He is a debauchee, after all, and can do whatever He likes, but still He alone, and no one else, is the worshipable Lord of My heart." The Lord explains: "I do not mind My personal distress. I only wish for the happiness of Kṛṣṇa" (20.52). We, as practising devotees, live in verses 1–4, pray verses 5–6, and hear verses 7–8 with faith; their depth is for a later study. "If anyone recites or hears these eight verses... his ecstatic love and devotion for Kṛṣṇa increase day by day" (20.65).

## S2. Key ideas

- The eight verses are one prayer that rises: the name, no taste, humility, pure desire, identity, eagerness, separation, surrender (CC Antya 20.12–47).
- The Lord composed them "to teach people in general" and then tasted them Himself (20.64).
- Humility is how one chants: lower than the grass, tolerant as a tree, expecting no honour (20.20–26).
- Real love does not think itself love: "he has not even a drop of love for Kṛṣṇa" (20.28).
- The sadhaka lives in verses 1–4, prays 5–6, hears 7–8 with faith.
- Reciting or hearing the eight increases love "day by day" (20.65).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **CC Antya 20.21 (verse 3):** "One who thinks himself lower than the grass, who is more tolerant than a tree, and who does not expect personal honor but is always prepared to give all respect to others can very easily always chant the holy name of the Lord." *For me:* this is the way to chant, not an extra virtue.
- **CC Antya 20.29 (verse 4):** "I do not desire material wealth, materialistic followers, a beautiful wife or fruitive activities... All I want, life after life, is unmotivated devotional service to You." *For me:* my daily examination of what I still want.
- **CC Antya 20.28:** "the devotee does not think himself a devotee. Instead, he always thinks that he has not even a drop of love for Kṛṣṇa." *For me:* never announce a stage.
- **CC Antya 20.16 (verse 2):** "I am so unfortunate that I commit offenses while chanting the holy name, and therefore I do not achieve attachment for chanting." *For me:* the Lord gives me words for my own lack of taste.

## S4. Words to know

- **Siksastaka:** "eight verses of instruction", Lord Caitanya's own prayer (CC Antya 20).
- **Ceto-darpana-marjanam:** "cleansing the mirror of the heart", the opening words of verse 1.
- **Trnad api sunicena:** "lower than the grass", the opening words of verse 3.
- **Svarupa Damodara and Ramananda Raya:** the two intimate associates with whom the Lord tasted these verses in Puri.
- **Vipralambha:** love in separation, the mood of verses 7–8 (studied in TRD-47).

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Why does the Lord say He has no attachment for chanting?** He composed the verses "to teach people in general" and then tasted them (20.64); He speaks as the devotee so that we have words. And real love never thinks itself love (20.28).
2. **Must I tolerate everything, even wrongdoing?** The tree tolerates what is done to itself and still shelters others (20.23–24). Verse 3 is about my own honour; protecting others and correcting under authority remain duties.
3. **Verse 6 prays for tears. Should I try to cry?** No. The Lord asks "when will" they come. We pray the verse and leave the signs to Krishna; imitated signs have no value.
4. **May we read verse 8 aloud with the word "debauchee"?** Yes, exactly as Srila Prabhupada translated it; the Lord's own explanation follows (20.48–60). We do not skip or soften it, and we do not expound its inner meaning until TRD-47.
5. **Can I pray verses 7 and 8?** You can recite and hear them, and 20.65 promises love grows by that. You do not claim their mood; a practising devotee lives in verses 1–4.
