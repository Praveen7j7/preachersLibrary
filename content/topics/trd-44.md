# Madhurya rasa and the gopis' love: why it is the highest, and why it is never imitated

> **TRD · up to 180 min · review: drafted · `sensitive: true` (madhurya-rasa; the rasa dance; the head reviews).** Group G, page 4.
> **Builds on bss-19 (Adi-rasa):** that page, from BIKC chapter 4 and SB 5.25.5, taught where attraction comes from, drew the iron-and-gold
> line (CC Adi 4.164–165), gave the Bhagavatam's warning and promise (SB 10.33.30–31, 39) and set the brahmacari's orientation. This page
> takes the next step the head allowed for TRD: the positive content of madhurya-rasa in Srila Prabhupada's words. Why is the gopis' love
> the highest (CC Adi 4.47–50; the gopis' love as the very definition of prema, CC Adi 4.166–190)? What did Krishna Himself say of it (SB
> 10.32.22)? And why is it never imitated but only followed, "in the footsteps of the gopīs" (CC Madhya 8.204–205, 228–230; KB 29, 33)?

> chapters of the Bhagavatam (SB 10.29–33) are heard in the devotees' company, not narrated here. Ujjvala-nilamani is the preacher's

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on bss-19. That page stayed at the level of SB 5.25.5's purport, as a brahmacari training page must: adi-rasa is the origin of all
attraction, its material reflection differs from it as iron from gold, the pastimes are not imitated, faithful hearing conquers lust. The head
has said (30 Oct) that this subject "can be taken, even can be in TRD". So this page goes where bss-19 stopped. It also builds on trd-41,
which ended Ramananda Raya's climb at conjugal love and gave the sky-to-earth analogy, and on trd-33, which drew the gradation (CC Adi 4.42–46).

A resident needs the positive content for two reasons. First, he preaches from books whose climax is the gopis, and a student's question,
"Why are the gopis the highest devotees?", deserves Prabhupada's answer, not evasion. Second, he lives in a tradition whose acharyas aspire to
the gopis' mood, and he must know, with precision, the difference between following their footsteps (anugatya) and imitating their acts
(anukarana), because the second has produced the sahajiya (trd-39) and the first has produced the Gosvamis. Lower batches receive the
iron-and-gold line and the Krsna book's stories; no lower batch studies why madhurya is highest or how it is followed.

## C. Central message

"There is a great increase of mellow in the unwedded conjugal mood. Such love is found nowhere but in Vraja" (CC Adi 4.47); in the gopis it is
unbounded and in Sri Radha perfect (4.48–49). The gopis' love is the definition of prema itself: they "have forsaken all these, as well as their
own relatives and their punishment and scolding, for the sake of serving Lord Kṛṣṇa. They render loving service to Him for the sake of His
enjoyment" (4.167–169), and Krishna admits He cannot repay them (SB 10.32.22). For that very reason it is never imitated: "we should not imitate
Lord Kṛṣṇa's activities with the gopīs" (SB 10.33.30 purport), "The rāsa-līlā dance cannot be imitated by anyone" (KB 33); it is followed,
"following in their footsteps" (CC Madhya 8.204–205), in the servant's body one has now, under the acharyas.

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Explain from CC Adi 4.47–50 and purport why the unwedded (parakiya) love of Vraja is the summit, where alone it exists, and why "risk" belongs only to the transcendental realm.
2. Show from CC Adi 4.166–190 that the gopis' love defines prema: what they gave up, whom their bodies belong to, where their joy lies.
3. Quote Krishna's own words to the gopis (SB 10.32.22; CC Adi 4.180) and Prabhupada's conclusion that on the gopis' recommendation Krishna accepts a devotee (4.189 purport).
4. Distinguish following the gopis' footsteps (CC Madhya 8.204–205, 228–230) from imitating their acts (SB 10.33.30 and purport; KB 29, 33), with Ramananda Raya's case (CC Antya 5.40–42) as the limit.
5. Say what a resident does today with madhurya-rasa: hears it faithfully in the devotees' company, honours it as Uddhava did (trd-41), serves as a servant, speaks of it only within Prabhupada's rules (NOD 34, 44).

The validity of such risk, however, is possible only in the transcendental realm. Svakīya and parakīya conjugal love of Godhead have no existence in the material world, and parakīya is not exhibited anywhere in Vaikuṇṭha, but only in the portion of Goloka Vṛndāvana known as Vraja." ## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **Where bss-19 ended.** Attraction originates in the Lord (SB 5.25.5 purport); lust and love are iron and gold (CC Adi 4.164–165); the pastimes are
   not imitated and faithful hearing conquers lust (SB 10.33.30–31, 39). This page assumes all of it.
2. **Why highest: completeness.** Conjugal love holds the qualities of all the other rasas, as earth holds the qualities of sky, air, fire and water
   (trd-41's CC Madhya 8.85–88); "Love of Godhead lacks nothing in the conjugal stage" (8.79 purport). This is the first reason, and it ranks taste, not
   devotees (8.83).
3. **Why highest: the unwedded love of Vraja.** Within conjugal love there is "a great increase of mellow in the unwedded conjugal mood. Such love is found
   nowhere but in Vraja" (CC Adi 4.47). The purport: parakiya "is better because it is more enthusiastic", because of "the risks involved"; but "The
   validity of such risk, however, is possible only in the transcendental realm"; it "has no existence in the material world" and is found only in Vraja,
   "a confidential part of Goloka Vṛndāvana" (4.50 purport). Prabhupada states the superiority and its fence in one paragraph.
4. **Why highest: the gopis define prema.** Krishnadasa Kaviraja does not argue; he describes. The gopis forsook social custom, scripture, body, shyness,
   relatives and the scolding that followed, "for the sake of serving Lord Kṛṣṇa" (4.167–169); their attachment is "spotlessly pure" (4.170); their bodies
   are His (4.182–184); their joy is His joy, so that their pleasure exceeds His ten million times (4.187–189). Prabhupada: "The selfless love of Godhead
   exhibited by the gopīs cannot have any parallel" (4.181 purport). When prema is defined as the desire to please Krishna's senses (4.165), the gopis are
   its complete instance.
5. **Krishna's own verdict.** He who promised to reciprocate in proportion (BG 4.11) says to them: "I am not able to repay My debt for your spotless service,
   even within a lifetime of Brahmā" (SB 10.32.22; CC Adi 4.180). Therefore "on the recommendation of the gopīs Kṛṣṇa at once accepts the service of a
   devotee" (4.189 purport), and Lord Caitanya "wanted to please the gopīs instead of Kṛṣṇa" (same purport).
6. **Why never imitated: it is yogamaya, not mahamaya.** The rasa dance is "on the platform of yogamāyā", the world's dances on mahamaya, gold and iron
   (KB 29). "Unusual and uncommon activities by the controller can never be imitated" (KB 33); "One who is not a great controller should never imitate the
   behavior of ruling personalities, even mentally" (SB 10.33.30). He descends "to demonstrate that He is God and we are not" (purport). Bhaktivinoda
   Thakura "punished a so-called incarnation of Viṣṇu who was imitating the rāsa-līlā with young girls" (KB 33).
7. **Why never imitated: the love itself forbids it.** The gopis' love is the desire for Krishna's pleasure with no trace of one's own (4.172, 4.174). An
   imitation is, by definition, for the imitator's pleasure; it is lust wearing the dress of the summit. This is why the sahajiya's end is not an excess of
   love but its absence (trd-39).
8. **Followed, not imitated.** The one door is "following in their footsteps" (CC Madhya 8.204–205), "in the ecstasy of sakhī-bhāva" (purport): accepting
   "the mood of the gopīs in their service" (8.228). The service itself is attained in the siddha-deha, after one is "completely free from material
   contamination" (8.229); it is not done with this body. Following means: serve as a servant now (trd-41), hear the Bhagavatam faithfully in the devotees'
   company (bss-19's SB 10.33.39), honour the gopis and pray for their dust as Uddhava did (trd-41 J.4), and leave the mood's awakening to Krishna and
   guru. Imitating means acting the part with this body or this mind.
9. **The limit case.** Ramananda Raya trained young dancing girls with his own hands and "his mind never changes" (CC Antya 5.41); the Lord says "The authority
   for such acts is the prerogative of Rāmānanda Rāya alone" (5.42). One man, named, with a spiritualised body. The verse closes the door it seems to open.
10. **Misunderstandings.**
    - *Since parakiya is highest, married devotees are lower than unmarried.* The purport places parakiya "only in the transcendental realm"; it says nothing about human marriage (4.50 purport). The topic is the gopis' love, not our ashrama.
    - *"Following in the footsteps of the gopis" means dressing or feeling like one.* It means accepting their mood of service (8.228) and reaching their service in the siddha-deha (8.229); never imitating, "even mentally" (SB 10.33.30).
    - *A resident who feels attraction to these topics is advanced.* Attraction to the subject is not the gopis' mood; the test remains the one bss-19 gave: does hearing reduce lust or increase it (SB 10.33.39 purport)?
    - *Speaking often of the gopis shows realisation.* Rupa Gosvami "describes conjugal love very briefly" (NOD 44); Lord Caitanya kept it for Ramananda Raya and Svarupa Damodara.
11. **Pitch for TRD.** Read Prabhupada's sentences whole, the superiority with its fence every time; let the gopis' own words (4.173) and Krishna's (4.180) do
    the work; close with following-not-imitating and the resident's exact steps.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Where we start.** bss-19: origin, line, warning, promise. *Transition:* now the summit itself.
2. **Highest because complete.** Conjugal love lacks nothing (trd-41). *Transition:* and within it?
3. **Highest because unwedded, in Vraja alone.** CC Adi 4.47–50: "more enthusiastic" because of risk; risk valid "only in the transcendental realm"; no existence in the material world. *Transition:* what does this love look like?
4. **The gopis define prema.** They gave up everything for His pleasure (4.167–172); their bodies are His (4.182–184); their joy is His joy (4.187–189). *Transition:* what did He say?
5. **Krishna cannot repay.** SB 10.32.22 (4.180); His promise broken by their worship (4.177–179); His happiness limited by theirs; we approach Him through them (4.189 purport). *Transition:* then why not copy them?
6. **Yogamaya, not mahamaya.** KB 29; the controller's acts are never imitated (KB 33; SB 10.33.30 "even mentally"); Bhaktivinoda Thakura's punishment of an imitator (KB 33). *Transition:* and the imitation contradicts the love itself (F.7).
7. **Following.** The one door (CC Madhya 8.201, 8.204–205); the mood of service (8.228); the siddha-deha (8.229); only then Goloka (8.230). *Transition:* the exception.
8. **Ramananda Raya alone.** CC Antya 5.40–42. *Transition:* so, today.
9. **The resident's steps.** Serve as a servant; hear faithfully in company; honour and pray for the gopis' dust; speak briefly within the rules (NOD 34, 44).

## J. Stories and illustrations

**1. Krishna returns to the gopis (SB 10.32.21–22, as given in CC Adi 4.176–180 with purports).** When Krishna returned to the arena of the rasa dance
after hearing the gopis' songs of separation, He told them: "O My beloved gopīs, you have renounced social customs, scriptural injunctions and your
relatives for My sake. I disappeared behind you only to increase your concentration upon Me" (4.176). Then: "O gopīs, I am not able to repay My debt for
your spotless service, even within a lifetime of Brahmā. Your connection with Me is beyond reproach. You have worshiped Me, cutting off all domestic
ties, which are difficult to break. Therefore please let your own glorious deeds be your compensation" (4.180). *Lesson:* the Lord who reciprocates with
all (BG 4.11) declares Himself unable to reciprocate here; this, and not any argument, is why the gopis' love is called highest.

**2. Bhaktivinoda Thakura and the imitator (KB 33).** Prabhupada records: "In Orissa, Ṭhākura Bhaktivinoda punished a so-called incarnation of Viṣṇu who
was imitating the rāsa-līlā with young girls." He frames it with the Bhagavatam's warning that "Unusual and uncommon activities by the controller can
never be imitated" and his own conclusion, "The rāsa-līlā dance cannot be imitated by anyone." *Lesson:* the acharya who gave us Jaiva-dharma and
Harinama-cintamani also used a magistrate's authority against imitation; reverence for the summit and severity toward its counterfeit are one attitude.

**3. Ramananda Raya and the dancing girls (CC Antya 5.11–47).** Pradyumna Misra, sent by the Lord to hear about Krishna from Ramananda Raya, found him in
his garden teaching two young temple dancers the songs of his drama, massaging them with oil, bathing and dressing them with his own hands (5.13–17).
Pradyumna Misra returned disturbed. Lord Caitanya told him: "The two professional dancing girls are beautiful and youthful, yet Śrī Rāmānanda Rāya
personally massages their entire bodies with oil" (5.38); "Nevertheless, the mind of Śrī Rāmānanda Rāya never changes, although he teaches the girls how
to physically express all the transformations of ecstasy" (5.40); "The authority for such acts is the prerogative of Rāmānanda Rāya alone, for I can
understand that his body is not material but has been completely transformed into a spiritual entity" (5.42). Pradyumna Misra then went back and heard
from him. *Lesson:* the Lord Himself defines the exception and names one man; everyone else is under SB 10.33.30.

**Illustration (writer's, not scriptural):** a student may study a great surgeon's hands for years, follow his method, and one day, qualified, operate;
the same student who picks up the knife on the first day because he "feels" like a surgeon harms the patient. Following and imitating use the same word,
footsteps, and are opposite acts. The verses that give the point are CC Madhya 8.204–205 and 8.229: the service is reached in the perfected body, after the method.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"If the gopis broke social and scriptural rules for Krishna, may a devotee break rules out of love?"** Prabhupada's purport says love of God is prepared
   to do "anything and everything to please the Lord, even at the risk of violating the Vedic principles" (CC Adi 4.170 purport), and the same tradition says
   such risk "is possible only in the transcendental realm" (4.50 purport). A conditioned soul who breaks rules "out of love" is pleasing himself (4.165); the
   gopis are the standard of love, not a licence.
2. **"Is parakiya not simply adultery given a holy name?"** "Svakīya and parakīya conjugal love of Godhead have no existence in the material world" (4.50 purport);
   "Actually, Kṛṣṇa is the husband of everyone because He is the supreme enjoyer" (KB 29). The word is borrowed; the thing is on the platform of yogamaya (KB 29).
3. **"May I meditate on the rasa-lila to develop the gopis' mood?"** SB 10.33.30 forbids imitation "even mentally". The authorised path is to hear it "faithfully"
   in the devotees' company (bss-19's 10.33.39) and to accept the gopis' mood of service (CC Madhya 8.228) under guru; the mood's awakening is Krishna's gift,
   not a meditation technique (trd-13, trd-38).
4. **"Why may Ramananda Raya do what we may not?"** Because the Lord says so and says why: his body "has been completely transformed into a spiritual entity"
   and the authority is his "alone" (CC Antya 5.42). The verse is a boundary, not a precedent.
5. **"Should I tell new students that the gopis are the highest devotees?"** Tell them what Prabhupada taught publicly: the five relationships, the iron-and-gold
   line, Krishna's words that He cannot repay the gopis (SB 10.32.22). Leave 4.47–50 and the Madhya 8 verses for those who have bss-19 and trd-33 (NOD 34).
6. **"Does this page make me want something I should not want?"** Uddhava's response is the model (trd-41 J.3–4): reverence and the prayer for the gopis' dust. If
   hearing increases agitation rather than reverence, stop, take the devotees' association, and return to bss-19's test (SB 10.33.39 purport).

## L. Case studies

**Case 1 (Invented case for discussion).** A resident who has read Ujjvala-nilamani in translation begins to give evening classes on the gopis' moods to new
students, quoting freely, and says he is "following Rupa Gosvami". *Dilemma:* following or imitating? *Principles:* NOD 44 (Rupa Gosvami "describes conjugal love
very briefly"); NOD 34; CC Antya 5.95–98 (Svarupa Damodara heard first; trd-42); research/advanced-books.md §2. *Questions:* Who was Rupa Gosvami's audience?
What does "following" him look like for a resident? *Analysis:* Rupa Gosvami wrote for the qualified and was brief in the open text; the resident imitates his
subject and ignores his method. Following him means studying under seniors, teaching the five rasas and the line to new students, and keeping the confidential
texts as background. *Takeaway:* the acharyas' footsteps include their silences.

**Case 2 (Invented case for discussion).** A brahmacari finds that the rasa-lila chapters agitate him and concludes he must avoid the Tenth Canto altogether and
discourage others from it. *Dilemma:* is avoidance the Bhagavatam's instruction? *Principles:* SB 10.33.39 and purport (faithful hearing conquers lust; the
pastimes are the diametric opposite of lusty affairs, bss-19); SB 10.33.30 (no imitation, even mentally); CC Adi 4.171 ("Lust is like dense darkness, but love
is like the bright sun"). *Questions:* What kind of hearing produced the agitation? What does "faithfully" require? *Analysis:* the chapters are not the
problem; solitary, curious reading is. He should hear them in class from a senior devotee, in the mood the acharyas model, and spend his private study on
Cantos 1–9 for now, without condemning the text or others. *Takeaway:* the medicine is the right hearing, not no hearing.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

In bss-19 we learned where attraction comes from, that lust and love differ as iron from gold (CC Adi 4.164–165), and that Krishna's pastimes with the
gopis are never imitated but heard faithfully (SB 10.33.30–31, 39). This page takes the next step. Krishnadasa Kaviraja writes: "There is a great
increase of mellow in the unwedded conjugal mood. Such love is found nowhere but in Vraja" (CC Adi 4.47); it is unbounded in the gopis and perfect in
Sri Radha (4.48–49). Srila Prabhupada explains that this love is superior "because it is more enthusiastic", on account of the risks involved, but
"The validity of such risk, however, is possible only in the transcendental realm"; such love "has no existence in the material world" and is found only
in Vraja, "a confidential part of Goloka Vṛndāvana" (4.50 purport).

The gopis show what love is. They gave up "Social customs, scriptural injunctions, bodily demands, fruitive action, shyness, patience, bodily pleasures,
self-gratification and the path of varṇāśrama-dharma", their relatives and the scolding that followed, "for the sake of serving Lord Kṛṣṇa" (4.167–169).
"Thus there is not the slightest taint of lust in the gopīs' love. Their relationship with Kṛṣṇa is only for the sake of His enjoyment" (4.172). Even their
bodies they regard as His (4.182–184), and their joy lies in His joy (4.189). Krishna Himself told them: "O gopīs, I am not able to repay My debt for your
spotless service, even within a lifetime of Brahmā" (SB 10.32.22; CC Adi 4.180). Prabhupada concludes that "on the recommendation of the gopīs Kṛṣṇa at
once accepts the service of a devotee" (4.189 purport).

For the same reason it is never imitated. The rasa dance is on the platform of yogamaya, as different from the world's dances as gold from iron (KB 29).
"One who is not a great controller should never imitate the behavior of ruling personalities, even mentally" (SB 10.33.30); "The rāsa-līlā dance cannot be
imitated by anyone" (KB 33), and Bhaktivinoda Thakura punished a man who tried (KB 33). The gopis are followed, not imitated: "Only one who worships the
Lord in the ecstasy of the gopīs, following in their footsteps, can engage in the service of Śrī Śrī Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa" (CC Madhya 8.204–205); one accepts "the
mood of the gopīs in their service" (8.228), and that service is reached in the perfected spiritual body after one is free from material contamination
(8.229). Lord Caitanya named one exception, Ramananda Raya, whose body was spiritual, and said the authority was his "alone" (CC Antya 5.42). For us
today: serve as servants, hear the Bhagavatam faithfully in the devotees' company, honour the gopis and pray for their dust, and speak of these things
briefly and only to those ready to hear (NOD 34, 44).

## S2. Key ideas
- The unwedded love of Vraja is the summit of conjugal love; it exists only in the transcendental realm (CC Adi 4.47–50 and purport).
- The gopis define prema: everything given up for Krishna's pleasure, nothing for their own (4.167–174).
- Krishna cannot repay them; devotees approach Him through them (SB 10.32.22; CC Adi 4.189 purport).
- Never imitated, even mentally (SB 10.33.30; KB 33); followed in the mood of service, reached in the siddha-deha (CC Madhya 8.204–205, 8.228–229).
- The one named exception, Ramananda Raya, closes the door for everyone else (CC Antya 5.42).

- **CC Adi 4.180 (SB 10.32.22):** "I am not able to repay My debt for your spotless service, even within a lifetime of Brahmā." For me: this is what love of Krishna is.
- **SB 10.33.30:** "never imitate the behavior of ruling personalities, even mentally." For me: I follow; I do not copy.

## S4. Words to know
- **Madhurya-rasa:** the relationship of conjugal love with Krishna; the gopis' rasa (trd-41).
- **Svakiya and parakiya:** wedded and unwedded conjugal love; parakiya exists only in Vraja (CC Adi 4.50 purport).
- **Yogamaya and mahamaya:** the Lord's internal and external energies; the rasa dance is on the platform of yogamaya (KB 29).
- **Following in the footsteps (anugatya):** accepting the gopis' mood of service under the acharyas (CC Madhya 8.228).
- **Siddha-deha:** the perfected spiritual body in which the gopis' service is attained (CC Madhya 8.229 purport).

## S5. Questions and answers
1. **Why are the gopis the highest devotees?** Because their love is entirely for Krishna's pleasure, with nothing for their own (CC Adi 4.172, 4.174), so complete that Krishna says He cannot repay it (SB 10.32.22).
2. **Is parakiya not just a holy name for adultery?** No. Such love "has no existence in the material world" (4.50 purport); it is on the platform of yogamaya, gold not iron (KB 29); and Krishna is the husband of everyone (KB 29).
3. **If the gopis broke rules for Krishna, may I?** The gopis are the standard of love, not a licence; the risk is valid "only in the transcendental realm" (4.50 purport). A conditioned soul who breaks rules "for love" is pleasing himself (4.165).
4. **How do I follow the gopis without imitating them?** Accept their mood of service (CC Madhya 8.228), hear faithfully in the devotees' company, serve as a servant, pray for their dust; never copy their acts, even in the mind (SB 10.33.30). Their service comes in the perfected body (8.229).
5. **Why could Ramananda Raya train young dancing girls?** Lord Caitanya said his body was spiritual and the authority was his "alone" (CC Antya 5.42). The exception is for one man, named by the Lord.
