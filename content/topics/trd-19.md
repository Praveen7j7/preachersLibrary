# Resolving conflicts between devotees the Vaisnava way

> **TRD · up to 180 min · review: drafted.** Group D (living and serving together), second page. Builds on DPS-22
> (constructive criticism), DPS-57 (tolerance and forgiveness) and trd-18. Vaisnava-aparadha itself is trd-50.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on trd-18: a leader who is the servant of the servants will still find his servants quarrelling, and will be one
party in some quarrels himself. Living together makes conflict certain; what the sastra forbids is not disagreement but
the loss of Vaisnava vision in it. The Bhagavatam shows Krishna Himself settling a dispute among His dearest devotees,
Draupadi, Bhima, Yudhisthira and Arjuna, over what to do with Asvatthama, with a solution that fulfilled every side.

## C. Central message

A conflict between devotees is settled the Vaisnava way when each side keeps seeing the other as Krishna's servant,
speaks the truth without agitation, takes the matter to the right authority, and accepts a solution that honours every
legitimate claim, as Krishna's judgment on Asvatthama honoured Draupadi's mercy, Bhima's justice and Arjuna's vow at once.

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. State NOI 6's rule (do not see a devotee's material faults) and NOI 5's rule (deal with each by level, and seek the one "devoid of the propensity to criticize").
2. Tell the Asvatthama judgment as SB 1.7.42–57 tells it and name the four principles it models (I.3).
3. Tell the Lord's settlement between Jagadananda and Sanatana (CC Antya 4.156–170) and state the Lord's two reasons.
4. Apply BG 17.15 and *apaisunam* (BG 16.1–3) to a disagreement in their own department.
5. Know when and how to take a dispute to authority, and what never to do (gossip, taking sides in public).

## E. Scriptural foundation

**NOI 6** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/6/
> "Being situated in his original Kṛṣṇa conscious position, a pure devotee does not identify with the body. Such a devotee should not be seen from a materialistic point of view. Indeed, one should overlook a devotee's having a body born in a low family, a body with a bad complexion, a deformed body, or a diseased or infirm body…"
- **Purport point:** "Kṛṣṇa's devotee is not subjected to material conditions, even though his bodily features may appear materially conditioned. One should therefore not see a pure devotee from a materialistic point of view. Unless one is actually a devotee, he cannot see another devotee perfectly."
- **Use:** in the talk: the vision that must survive the quarrel.

**NOI 5** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/5/
> "…one should associate with and faithfully serve that pure devotee who is advanced in undeviated devotional service and whose heart is completely devoid of the propensity to criticize others."
- **Purport point:** "one must select proper persons with careful discrimination… meet with the Vaiṣṇavas in an appropriate way, according to their particular status." The uttama-adhikari "is not interested in blaspheming others."
- **Use:** in the talk: whom to go to with a dispute (the one who does not criticise), and dealing by level.

**BG 12.13–14, 12.15** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/12/13-14/
> 12.13–14: "One who is not envious but is a kind friend to all living entities… who is tolerant, always satisfied, self-controlled…" 12.15: "He by whom no one is put into difficulty and who is not disturbed by anyone, who is equipoised in happiness and distress, fear and anxiety, is very dear to Me."
- **Purport point (12.15):** "Since a devotee is kind to everyone, he does not act in such a way as to put others into anxiety. At the same time, if others try to put a devotee into anxiety, he is not disturbed."
- **Use:** in the talk: the two sides of a devotee in conflict: he agitates no one, and is not agitated.

**BG 16.1–3** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/16/1-3/
> Divine qualities include "aversion to faultfinding… forgiveness… freedom from envy."
- **Purport point:** "Apaiśunam means that one should not find fault with others or correct them unnecessarily. Of course to call a thief a thief is not faultfinding, but to call an honest person a thief is very much offensive."
- **Use:** in the talk: the line between correction and fault-finding.

**BG 17.15** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/17/15/
> "Austerity of speech consists in speaking words that are truthful, pleasing, beneficial, and not agitating to others…"
- **Purport point:** "One should not speak in such a way as to agitate the minds of others… The process of speaking in spiritual circles is to say something upheld by the scriptures."
- **Use:** in the talk: how to speak in a dispute.

**SB 1.7.43, 1.7.47, 1.7.49, 1.7.51, 1.7.53–54, 1.7.55, 1.7.56** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/7/53-54/

> 1.7.47: "My lord, do not make the wife of Droṇācārya cry like me."

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** Disagreement is not the fault; the Pandavas disagreed in Krishna's presence (SB 1.7.43–51). The
   fault is to lose sight of the other as Krishna's servant (NOI 6), to agitate him (BG 12.15), to find fault
   unnecessarily (BG 16.1–3) or to speak untruthfully or harshly (BG 17.15). A devotee in conflict keeps all four.
2. **Krishna's model: honour every legitimate claim.** Draupadi's mercy ("do not make the wife of Droṇācārya cry like me"),
   Yudhisthira's judgment that her words were "without duplicity," Bhima's justice (the murderer of sleeping children) and
   Arjuna's vow were all real and all contradictory. Krishna's "equivocal orders" (1.7.55) pointed to a solution that
   satisfied each: Asvatthama was "simultaneously killed and not killed" (1.7.56 purport). The purport's words are the
   method: "a compromise was selected… by his sharp intelligence" within the sastra's rulings (1.7.57). *Application
   (preacher's):* the Vaisnava asks "what in each side's claim is legitimate?" before asking "who is right?"
3. **The Lord's model: etiquette and affection together.** With Jagadananda and Sanatana, the Lord corrected the junior
   publicly, reassured the senior that affection was not the issue ("do not think that Jagadānanda is more dear to Me than
   you"), and named the exact principle at stake: "I cannot tolerate transgressions of the standard etiquette" (CC Antya
   4.166). The rebuked one was declared the fortunate one (4.161–163). Correction, affection and the principle were kept
   together, and nothing was left to rumour.
4. **Whom to go to.** NOI 5 describes the devotee to serve: "whose heart is completely devoid of the propensity to
   criticize others." A dispute is taken to such a person, or to the authority responsible, and to no one else. Taking it
   to peers is *prajalpa* (NOI 2) and spreads the fault (*apaisunam*).
5. **Truth without agitation.** BG 17.15 does not ask for silence about wrong ("to call a thief a thief is not
   faultfinding," BG 16.1–3 purport). It asks that the truth be beneficial and not agitating, and "upheld by the
   scriptures." Draupadi's plea is the model: full of mercy, "without duplicity," spoken from "full knowledge" (1.7.49
   purport).
6. **Misunderstandings.** *"A good devotee never disagrees":* Bhima disagreed with the king and the queen, in Krishna's
   presence, and was satisfied (1.7.51–54). *"Resolving means someone wins":* the Lord's solution gave each side what was
   legitimate in its claim. *"NOI 6 means I may never raise a devotee's wrong conduct":* NOI 6 concerns bodily and
   external features; conduct that harms the mission goes to the authority, truthfully (BG 17.15), and to no one else.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet the residents. Say: "Everyone who lives with other people has conflicts. Krishna's dearest devotees, the
   Pandavas, disagreed in front of Him. Today we see how He settled it, and how Lord Caitanya settled a quarrel between
   two of His own associates."
2. Say: "No present dispute will be discussed by name. We will work with invented cases."
3. Write on the board: "truthful, pleasing, beneficial, not agitating" (BG 17.15).
4. Ask H.2 question 1 for private writing.
5. Ask question 2 aloud; take two or three answers; thank each; begin with NOI 6.

**H.2 Opening questions**
1. (Private.) Think of your last disagreement with a devotee. Did you, at any moment, stop seeing him as Krishna's
   servant? Write yes or no.
2. When two people both have a fair point, what usually goes wrong in the argument? (Expected: each attacks the other's
   weak point instead of hearing the fair one.)
3. Whom do you tell first when a devotee has wronged you? (Let them answer honestly; NOI 5 and NOI 2 come later.)

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Keep the vision.** NOI 6: do not see a devotee's material features; BG 12.13–15: agitate no one, be agitated by no
   one. *Transition:* but the disagreement is real; what then?
2. **Disagreement in Krishna's presence is allowed.** Draupadi, Yudhisthira, Bhima and Arjuna each held a position
   (SB 1.7.43–51). *Transition:* how did Krishna settle it?
3. **The four principles of Krishna's judgment (Application, preacher's, drawn from SB 1.7.53–57 and purports).**
   (a) *Name what is legitimate in each claim* (mercy, justice, the vow, the sastra). (b) *Stay within sastra* ("all these
   rulings are in the scriptures… act accordingly," 1.7.54; the prescribed punishment, 1.7.57). (c) *Find the act that
   honours all of them* (hair and jewel: "simultaneously killed and not killed," 1.7.56 purport). (d) *Let the one
   responsible act* (Arjuna, by "sharp intelligence," 1.7.55 purport). *Transition:* and when the quarrel is about etiquette
   between devotees themselves?
4. **The Lord's settlement: correct, reassure, name the principle.** CC Antya 4.161–169 (J.2). *Transition:* what do I say,
   and to whom?
5. **Speak truth without agitation; take it to the right person.** BG 17.15; *apaisunam* (BG 16.1–3 purport); NOI 5: the
   devotee "devoid of the propensity to criticize." Never to peers, never in public.

## J. Stories and illustrations

**J.1 The judgment on Asvatthama (SB 1.7.42–57).** Told as the text tells it. Arjuna brought Asvatthama, "bound with
ropes like an animal," who had murdered Draupadi's sleeping sons (1.7.42). Draupadi "showed him due respects as a
brāhmaṇa" and said, "Release him, release him, for he is a brāhmaṇa, our spiritual master" (1.7.42–43). She pleaded:
"My lord, do not make the wife of Droṇācārya cry like me. I am aggrieved for the death of my sons. She need not cry
constantly like me" (1.7.47). Yudhisthira "fully supported the statements of the Queen, which were in accordance with the
principles of religion and were justified, glorious, full of mercy and equity, and without duplicity" (1.7.49); Nakula,
Sahadeva, Satyaki, Arjuna, Krishna and the ladies agreed (1.7.50). "Bhīma, however, angrily disagreed with them and
recommended killing this culprit, who had murdered sleeping children for no purpose" (1.7.51). Krishna then said: "A
friend of a brāhmaṇa is not to be killed, but if he is an aggressor he must be killed. All these rulings are in the
scriptures, and you should act accordingly. You have to fulfill your promise to your wife, and you must also act to the
satisfaction of Bhīmasena and Me" (1.7.53–54). "Just then Arjuna could understand the motive of the Lord by His
equivocal orders, and thus with his sword he severed both hair and jewel from the head of Aśvatthāmā" (1.7.55). He was
"unbound and driven out of the camp" (1.7.56); "cutting the hair from his head, depriving him of his wealth and driving
him from his residence are the prescribed punishments for the relative of a brāhmaṇa" (1.7.57).
- *Prabhupada's reading (1.7.55–56 purports):* "Contradictory orders of different persons are impossible to carry out.
  Therefore a compromise was selected by Arjuna by his sharp intelligence… This was as good as cutting off his head, and
  yet his life was saved"; "simultaneously killed and not killed by the intelligence of Lord Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna."
- *Use:* thirty minutes; every detail as in the verses; the four principles (I.3) afterwards, labelled as the preacher's.

**J.2 The Lord settles between Jagadananda and Sanatana (CC Antya 4.156–170).** Full narration: trd-15, section J. The
settlement: the Lord chastised the junior for advising the senior (4.157–160); Sanatana called the rebuked Jagadananda
the fortunate one (4.161–163); the Lord told Sanatana, "please do not think that Jagadānanda is more dear to Me than you.
However, I cannot tolerate transgressions of the standard etiquette" (4.166), and "Jagā's advising you is intolerable
for Me. Therefore I am chastising him" (4.169). *Connection:* correction, affection and the principle, all stated openly.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"A devotee wronged me. NOI 6 says not to see his faults. Do I just keep quiet?"**
   - NOI 6 is about material features; it does not forbid truth about conduct ("to call a thief a thief is not
     faultfinding," BG 16.1–3 purport). Speak to him, or to the authority, in the manner of BG 17.15, and to no one else.
2. **"What if the authority is one of the parties?"**
   - Go to the next authority, or to the devotee NOI 5 describes, "devoid of the propensity to criticize." Sanatana went
     to the Lord Himself with the whole matter (trd-16 J.1).
3. **"Krishna's solution looks like a clever trick. Is compromise really Vaisnava?"**
   - Prabhupada's own word is "compromise… by his sharp intelligence" (1.7.55 purport), inside the sastra's rulings
     (1.7.54, 1.7.57). Honouring every legitimate claim is not weakness; it is what the Lord Himself arranged.
4. **"Two leaders in my temple have not spoken for a year. What can a resident do?"**
   - Nothing in public, nothing to peers. Give both all respect (CC Antya 20.21, trd-15), speak to the authority
     responsible if the service suffers, and pray for both. Vaisnava-aparadha and its remedy: trd-50.

## L. Case studies

*Invented case for discussion. Not real people.*

**Case 1: The kitchen and the programme.** The kitchen head, Damodara, refuses to cook extra for a Sunday programme
announced late by the preaching head, Gopala. Gopala tells three bhaktas that Damodara "doesn't care about preaching";
Damodara tells the pujari that Gopala "treats the kitchen like servants." The programme is in four days.
- **Dilemma:** Both have a fair point. What has each already done wrong, and how is it settled the Vaisnava way?
- **Principles:** BG 17.15 and *apaisunam* (what each said to third parties); NOI 5 (whom to go to); SB 1.7.53–56 (honour
  both legitimate claims within the rule); NOI 6 (keep the vision).
- **Questions:** What is legitimate in Damodara's claim? In Gopala's? Who should bring it to whom, and in what words?
  What solution gives each what is legitimate (as hair and jewel did)?
- **Analysis (one possible):** Both broke BG 17.15 by speaking to third parties. Gopala goes to Damodara first, admits the
  late notice, asks what is possible; if unresolved, both go together to the temple authority. A solution that honours
  both: a simpler menu this Sunday, and a written rule that programmes are announced a week ahead.
- **Takeaway:** Name what is legitimate on each side, stop the third-party talk, and let the responsible person decide.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first; thank each answer)
1. What does NOI 6 tell us not to see in a devotee? *Expected:* his material, bodily features; not to view him from a materialistic point of view.
2. What are the four marks of truthful speech in BG 17.15? *Expected:* truthful, pleasing, beneficial, not agitating.
3. Who disagreed with Draupadi and the king, and what did he want? *Expected:* Bhima; to kill Asvatthama (SB 1.7.51).
4. How did Arjuna satisfy everyone (SB 1.7.55)? *Expected:* he cut off Asvatthama's hair and jewel: "simultaneously killed and not killed."
5. What did the Lord say He could not tolerate (CC Antya 4.166)? *Expected:* transgressions of standard etiquette.
6. According to NOI 5, which devotee should we serve and go to? *Expected:* the one advanced in devotion, "devoid of the propensity to criticize others."

**M.2 Activities**
- **Option 1: Four principles on a case (20 min).** Groups apply I.3's four principles to Case L.1 and write the solution
  in four lines; each reads; the speaker checks each against the Asvatthama model.
- **Option 2: Role-play (25 min).** Two volunteers play Gopala and Damodara meeting privately; a third plays the authority.
  Rule: every sentence must pass BG 17.15. The room notes any sentence that fails and why. Debrief: what made the
  conversation possible?

**M.3 Reflection**
- Draupadi had lost five sons that night and pleaded for the murderer's life "without duplicity." What vision made that
  possible? Which of my grievances looks small beside it?

## P. Practical takeaway

- **Main lesson:** Disagreement is allowed in Krishna's presence; what is not allowed is losing the vision of the other as
  His servant, agitating speech, and third-party talk. The Vaisnava solution honours every legitimate claim within sastra.
- **One action (TRD):** This week, in one live disagreement, write down before speaking: what is legitimate in his claim,
  what is legitimate in mine, and the sentence I will say, checked against BG 17.15. Say it to him or to the authority,
  and to no one else.
- **Reflection question:** In my last quarrel, did I look for what was right in his claim, or for what was weak in it?

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Living with devotees means disagreeing with devotees. The scriptures do not forbid disagreement; they tell us how to
hold it. A devotee does not see another devotee's material features (NOI 6); he is "a kind friend to all," "tolerant,"
one "by whom no one is put into difficulty and who is not disturbed by anyone" (BG 12.13–15). He avoids fault-finding,
though "to call a thief a thief is not faultfinding" (BG 16.1–3 purport), and his speech is "truthful, pleasing,
beneficial, and not agitating" (BG 17.15).

The Bhagavatam shows Krishna settling a dispute among His dearest devotees. Arjuna brought Asvatthama, who had murdered
Draupadi's sleeping sons. Draupadi said, "Release him, release him, for he is a brāhmaṇa, our spiritual master," and
pleaded, "do not make the wife of Droṇācārya cry like me" (SB 1.7.43, 47). Yudhisthira supported her words as "full of
mercy and equity, and without duplicity" (1.7.49). Bhima "angrily disagreed" and wanted him killed (1.7.51). Krishna said
that the scriptures both forbid killing a brahmana's friend and require killing an aggressor, and told Arjuna, "You have
to fulfill your promise to your wife, and you must also act to the satisfaction of Bhīmasena and Me" (1.7.53–54). Arjuna
understood "the motive of the Lord by His equivocal orders" and cut off Asvatthama's hair and jewel (1.7.55). Prabhupada
writes that Asvatthama was "simultaneously killed and not killed" (1.7.56 purport): every legitimate claim was honoured
within the rule of scripture.

Lord Caitanya settled a quarrel between His own associates the same way: He corrected the junior, Jagadananda, for
advising the senior, Sanatana, reassured Sanatana that affection was not the question, and named the principle: "I cannot
tolerate transgressions of the standard etiquette" (CC Antya 4.166). A dispute is taken to the authority responsible, or
to the devotee NOI 5 describes, "devoid of the propensity to criticize others," and to no one else.

## S2. Key ideas

- Keep seeing the other as Krishna's servant (NOI 6); agitate no one, be disturbed by no one (BG 12.15).
- Disagreement in Krishna's presence is allowed (SB 1.7.43–51); losing the vision is not.
- Krishna's method: honour what is legitimate in every claim, within sastra (SB 1.7.53–57 and purports).
- Speak truthfully, pleasingly, beneficially, without agitation (BG 17.15); no third-party talk (BG 16.1–3; NOI 5).
- The Lord's settlement: correct, reassure, name the principle (CC Antya 4.161–169).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **BG 17.15:** speech "truthful, pleasing, beneficial, and not agitating." *For me:* the test for every sentence in a dispute.
- **SB 1.7.54:** "fulfill your promise to your wife, and… act to the satisfaction of Bhīmasena and Me." *For me:* look for the act that honours every fair claim.
- **NOI 6:** a devotee "should not be seen from a materialistic point of view." *For me:* the quarrel must not change how I see him.

## S4. Words to know

- **Apaisunam:** aversion to fault-finding; not correcting others unnecessarily (BG 16.1–3).
- **Prajalpa:** idle talk about mundane matters, including others' faults (NOI 2).
- **Standard etiquette (Vaisnava):** the respect due from junior to senior, which the Lord would not see transgressed (CC Antya 4.166).
- **Equivocal orders:** Krishna's instructions that pointed to a solution satisfying all sides (SB 1.7.55).

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **A devotee wronged me. Must I stay silent?** No. NOI 6 is about material features; conduct may be raised truthfully (BG 17.15) with him or the authority, and with no one else.
2. **What if the authority is a party?** Go to the next authority or to the devotee NOI 5 describes, free of the tendency to criticise.
3. **Isn't Krishna's solution just a trick?** Prabhupada calls it "a compromise… by his sharp intelligence" within scripture's rulings (SB 1.7.55 purport, 1.7.57). Honouring every fair claim is the Lord's own way.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. What do BG 12.15's two halves say about a devotee in conflict?
2. Who disagreed with Draupadi and the king, and what did he want (SB 1.7.51)?
3. What did Arjuna do to satisfy everyone (SB 1.7.55)?
4. What does "to call a thief a thief is not faultfinding" teach (BG 16.1–3 purport)?
5. What principle did the Lord name when He settled between Jagadananda and Sanatana (CC Antya 4.166)?

**Answers:** 1. He puts no one into difficulty and is not disturbed by anyone (BG 12.15). 2. Bhima; killing Asvatthama
(SB 1.7.51). 3. Cut off his hair and jewel (SB 1.7.55). 4. Truthful correction is not fault-finding; accusing the innocent
is (BG 16.1–3 purport). 5. Standard etiquette between junior and senior must not be transgressed (CC Antya 4.166).
