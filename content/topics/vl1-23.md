# PARTHA: The Formula of Success

> **VL1 · 45–60 min · review: sources checked, awaiting senior review.** A relevance talk for students and young professionals. "P.A.R.T.H.A." is an
> acronym from IYF's own material (VOICE, Radheshyam Das: Positive thinking, Aim, Restraint, Training, Hard work,
> Abiding interest); the Gita verses carry each letter. Scripture light but exact: six verses in the talk, the rest
> for the preacher.

## A. Topic overview

| | |
|---|---|
| Batch / level | VL1 (Vedic Level 1), a stand-alone talk |
| Suggested duration | 45–60 min (G gives the 60-minute flow and a 30-minute cut) |
| Preparation time | About 90 min, the first time |
| Difficulty | Introductory |
| Prerequisites | None (VL1-10 "BG and art of work" and VL1-22 help) |
| Audience | Students preparing for exams and placements; young professionals chasing targets; many with no settled faith and a shelf of self-help books |
| Teaching objective | Give a memorable six-letter formula for success that is actually a summary of the Gita's advice on mind, aim, restraint, work and results, so that "success" is redefined from outcome to the quality of the person who works |

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Success formulas sell. A VL1 room has heard a dozen. This one is attractive because it is honest about the one thing
the others skip: the result is not in my hands (BG 2.47; BG 18.14). The aim is **attraction through a formula that
holds up**: six letters a student can remember, each anchored in a verse they can check.

IYF's document (by Radheshyam Das, Director of VOICE, Pune) expands each letter with examples and verses. This page
keeps the **acronym, the order and the Gita verses** and drops the material that cannot be presented as fact:
"thought vibrations", "psychometry", "law of thought induction", the prana–disease claim, and several anecdotes
without a source (see J and O). The popular-book material (Covey's "map" story, Kaizen) is kept as illustration and
labelled.

*Builds on vl1-10 (BG and art of work: the right to work, not to results, BG 2.47) and vl1-03 / vl1-09 (training the mind, BG 6.5–6, 6.35): here those are only two letters (T and P) of a six-letter formula, taught in a few minutes with different examples. This talk's own material is A, R, H and the second A.*

How the subject grows at other levels:

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **VL1** (here) | The six letters as practical advice: train the mind (BG 6.5–6), one aim (BG 2.41), restraint builds will (BG 6.26), work without doership (BG 2.47; BG 3.27), steady enthusiasm (BG 18.26), no loss (BG 2.40) |
| VL2 | Why the mind can be trained: the self is not the mind; the five factors of action (BG 18.14) as a theory of agency |
| VL3 | Determination (*vyavasayatmika buddhi*, resolute intelligence) and a regulated daily routine as a practice |
| SPS | The same letters as *sadhana* (a daily spiritual practice): steady enthusiasm, patience, confidence |
| DPS | Service without taking credit; the formula for those who guide teams |
| BSS | Restraint and routine in a student ashram as the training ground for will |

**Not done here:**
- Promising outcomes. Nothing in the Gita says this formula brings marks or money; it describes the person who works
  well whatever the result.
- Teaching the full doctrine of the soul. "You are not your mind" is said once and left for VL2.
- Pseudo-science. IYF's sections on thought vibrations and psychometry are not reused.

## C. Central message

Success is not first a result; it is a way of working. Six habits make it: a trained mind (P), one aim (A), restraint
that builds will (R), training in attention and non-doership (T), hard work that is prioritised and regulated (H), and
abiding interest, which the Gita guarantees cannot be lost (A). "In this endeavor there is no loss or diminution"
(BG 2.40).

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. Recite the six letters of PARTHA and give one Gita verse for at least four of them.
2. Explain why the Gita treats the mind as both friend and enemy (BG 6.5–6) and give one practice for making it a
   friend (BG 6.26; BG 6.35).
3. State the difference between effort and result (BG 2.47) and between "I did it" and "it was done through me"
   (BG 3.27; BG 18.14), in their own words.
4. Choose one letter to work on this week and define what "done" looks like.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** six references are enough for the talk. The rest prepare you for questions.

**BG 6.5** and **BG 6.6** (P, positive thinking): Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/5/ · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/6/
> 6.5: "One must deliver himself with the help of his mind, and not degrade himself. The mind is the friend of the conditioned soul, and his enemy as well."
> 6.6: "For him who has conquered the mind, the mind is the best of friends; but for one who has failed to do so, his mind will remain the greatest enemy."
- **Teaching:** The mind is powerful and neutral; trained, it lifts; untrained, it drags.
- **Purport point (6.5):** "the mind should be trained so that it will not be attracted by the glitter of material
  nature". **(6.6):** "Unless the mind is controlled, the practice of yoga (for show) is simply a waste of time."
- **Application (preacher's):** "Positive thinking" in the Gita's sense is not cheerfulness; it is a trained mind that
  does not run after every glitter. IYF's "give up negative and wasteful thoughts" rests here.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 2.41** (A, aim): Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/41/
> "Those who are on this path are resolute in purpose, and their aim is one. O beloved child of the Kurus, the intelligence of those who are irresolute is many-branched."
- **Purport point:** Resolute intelligence is based on knowledge and "strong faith"; by watering the root of a tree
  "one automatically distributes water to the leaves and branches". The purport's "one aim" is Krsna consciousness.
- **Application (preacher's):** IYF applies the "many-branched" half to indecisive students who take up one thing after
  another. That is a fair application of the verse's contrast, and we say so. The verse's positive half, one aim in
  the Supreme, is stated as the Gita's own proposal, not pressed.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 6.26** (R, restraint): Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/26/
> "From wherever the mind wanders due to its flickering and unsteady nature, one must certainly withdraw it and bring it back under the control of the Self."
- **Purport point:** "One who controls the mind (and therefore the senses as well) is called gosvāmī, or svāmī, and one
  who is controlled by the mind is called go-dāsa, or the servant of the senses."
- **Application (preacher's):** Restraint is not a wall; it is the repeated act of bringing the mind back. Every time
  it is done, will grows. IYF's examples (the dam, the kite on its string) illustrate this.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 2.47** (T, training: give the credit to God): Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/47/
> "You have a right to perform your prescribed duty, but you are not entitled to the fruits of action. Never consider yourself the cause of the results of your activities, and never be attached to not doing your duty."
- **Purport point:** Attachment to results makes one "the enjoyer or sufferer"; attachment to not working is
  "another side of attachment". "Inaction is sinful."
- **Application (preacher's):** IYF's "all anxieties spring from doership" is drawn from this verse's third clause,
  "never consider yourself the cause of the results". Full treatment is VL1-10; here it is one letter of six.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 18.26** (H, hard work): Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/26/
> "One who performs his duty without association with the modes of material nature, without false ego, with great determination and enthusiasm, and without wavering in success or failure is said to be a worker in the mode of goodness."
- **Purport point:** Such a worker "has no expectations for the result… Still, he is always enthusiastic till the
  completion of such work."
- **Application (preacher's):** The Gita's portrait of the good worker: determination and enthusiasm *with* evenness
  about results. "Hard work" means this, not hours.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 2.40** (A, abiding interest): Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/40/
> "In this endeavor there is no loss or diminution, and a little advancement on this path can protect one from the most dangerous type of fear."
- **Purport point:** "One percent done in Kṛṣṇa consciousness bears permanent results, so that the next beginning is
  from the point of two percent, whereas in material activity without a hundred percent success there is no profit."
- **Application (preacher's):** IYF's "start from today; small continuous improvements" fits the purport's percent
  image. The verse is about spiritual endeavour; we apply it to the spiritual dimension of the formula and say so.
- **Use:** in the talk, at the end.

**For questions (K):**
- **BG 17.16** (Verified): "satisfaction, simplicity, gravity, self-control and purification of one's existence are the
  austerities of the mind." Purport: "The more we think of sense enjoyment, the more the mind becomes dissatisfied."
  The Gita's own list of mental training, for "what does training the mind involve?"
- **BG 6.35** (Verified): the restless mind is "very difficult to curb… but it is possible by suitable practice and by
  detachment." Purport: hearing about Krsna is "expert treatment for the mad mind". For "I've tried; my mind won't
  obey."
- **BG 15.7** (Verified): conditioned living beings "are struggling very hard with the six senses, which include the
  mind." IYF cites it for the "struggle for survival" that keeps people aimless. Background.
- **BG 2.67** (Verified): "even one of the roaming senses on which the mind focuses can carry away a man's
  intelligence", as wind carries a boat. For "does one small indulgence matter?"
- **BG 3.27** (Verified): the soul "bewildered by the influence of false ego thinks himself the doer of activities
  that are in actuality carried out by the three modes of material nature." Purport: the person in false ego "takes
  all credit for doing everything independently". For "give the credit to God".
- **BG 18.14** (Verified): five factors of action: "The place of action [the body], the performer, the various senses,
  the many different kinds of endeavor, and ultimately the Supersoul". The basis of "do your best, leave the rest".
- **BG 3.8** (Verified): "Perform your prescribed duty, for doing so is better than not working." Against reading
  "non-doership" as laziness.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** The Gita's advice on work can be arranged as six habits. **P**: train the mind, which is friend
   or enemy (BG 6.5–6), by the austerities of the mind (BG 17.16). **A**: resolute intelligence has one aim; irresolute
   intelligence branches (BG 2.41). **R**: restraint is bringing the mind back whenever it wanders (BG 6.26), possible
   by practice and detachment (BG 6.35). **T**: train attention, and train out doership: I have a right to the work,
   not the result (BG 2.47); five factors produce any result (BG 18.14); false ego makes me think I alone did it
   (BG 3.27). **H**: the good worker is determined, enthusiastic and even in success and failure (BG 18.26); not
   working is not an option (BG 3.8). **A**: begun sincerely, this endeavour cannot be lost (BG 2.40).
2. **Why it matters.** The audience is drowning in success advice that promises outcomes. The Gita's formula is the
   only one that is honest about outcomes and still demands full effort. That honesty is its attraction.
3. **Connections.** VL1-10 (effort and result) is the long version of T; VL1-22 (IQ/EQ/SQ) is the map behind P and R;
   VL1-24 (time) is the method of H; VL1-25 (smart work) classifies the worker of BG 18.26.
4. **Consequences.** A person working this way is steadier under pressure, less crushed by failure, less inflated by
   success, and able to begin again after a lapse (BG 2.40 purport). This is a claim about how it tends to go, not a
   promise of marks.
5. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - **"Positive thinking fixes everything."** IYF's own document says that without the right "map" positive
     attitude "would be foolish". The Gita's version is a trained mind, not forced cheer.
   - **"Non-doership means passivity."** BG 2.47 forbids attachment to not working; BG 3.8 says work is better than
     not working; BG 18.26 praises "great determination and enthusiasm".
   - **"One aim" as fanaticism.** BG 2.41's purport grounds resolute intelligence in knowledge and in watering the
     root, which nourishes everything else. At VL1 the one aim is offered as the Gita's proposal.
   - **Thought as magic.** IYF's "thoughts materialise" and "vibrations" are not taught; the Gita's claim is that the
     mind shapes action, which the room can verify.
6. **Pitch for VL1.** Say "train the mind", "one aim", "bring it back", "effort not result", "steady enthusiasm",
   "no loss". *Vyavasāyātmikā buddhiḥ* may be said once because IYF's slides use it. Present Krsna as the Gita's
   speaker without arguing His divinity.

**30-minute cut:** merge R into P, and give T and H four minutes each.

**Alternative structure (self-audit workshop):** hand out the six letters with one question each ("Does my mind
run me or do I run it?" … "Will I still be at this in five years?"). Each person scores themselves privately 1–5,
picks their lowest letter, and the preacher teaches the verses for the three letters the room most often chose.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet the room, say your name, and say the talk is about a way of working, not a promise of results.
2. Write "SUCCESS" on the board.
3. Ask H.2 question 1; collect single words from three or four people and write them up.
4. Thank each speaker; point out how many words are results.
5. Give the transition line below and go to "P".

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the room's own life)
1. Define success in one word.
2. Think of someone you consider successful. What do they do that others do not?
3. Think of a time you worked hard and the result was not what you hoped. What stayed with you?

- **Opening question:** "Define success in one word." Collect: money, marks, freedom, respect, peace. "Notice that
  most of your words are results. Tonight's formula is about the person who gets results, and about the one thing
  no formula controls."
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** Tanvi and Harsh prepare for the same exam. Tanvi has a study plan, sleeps, brings
  her mind back whenever it drifts, and treats each paper as done when it is done. Harsh studies in bursts, checks
  rankings obsessively, switches targets twice, and rehearses failure every night. They score similarly. Who is
  "successful"? Who will be in five years?
- **Transition:** "Arjuna was in Harsh's state before the battle: brilliant, trained, and paralysed by the result.
  The Gita's answer to him is this formula."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** Exam seasons, job-hunting (placement cycles on Indian campuses), the first appraisal, and the growing sense
  that "success" is a treadmill. The same holds for a farmer waiting on a harvest or a small trader waiting on a season: the effort is theirs, the result is not.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **P: a trained mind is the first success.** BG 6.5–6; BG 17.16. *Plain:* the mind is a friend or an enemy; train
   it. *Example:* carrying a hot coal to throw at an enemy burns your own hand first (IYF's illustration).
   *Transition:* a trained mind needs somewhere to point.
2. **A: one aim.** BG 2.41. *Plain:* resolute intelligence is one-pointed; the irresolute life branches. *Example:*
   the student on their fourth "passion" in two years. *Transition:* an aim is kept by restraint.
3. **R: restraint builds will.** BG 6.26; BG 6.35. *Plain:* bring the mind back every time it wanders; each return
   is a repetition that strengthens. *Example:* a dam makes water useful; a kite flies because of the string (IYF).
   *Transition:* will power applied to work is training.
4. **T: attention, and the end of doership.** BG 2.47; BG 18.14; BG 3.27. *Plain:* be fully present in the task; then
   remember that five factors make the result and I am one. *Example:* the farmer sows and waters; he does not make
   the rain. *Transition:* this frees energy for hard work.
5. **H: hard work, done smart.** BG 18.26; BG 3.8. *Plain:* determination and enthusiasm with evenness; one task at a
   time, then close the file. *Example:* the stress of trying to think about all tasks at once (IYF). *Transition:*
   how do we sustain this for years?
6. **A: abiding interest; nothing is lost.** BG 2.40. *Plain:* start today, improve a little continuously; the
   purport's "one percent… permanent". *Example:* the student who begins a practice, lapses, and resumes from two
   percent, not zero. *Transition:* to the takeaway.

## J. Stories and illustrations

- **Arjuna before the battle** (BG 1–2). The frame: a capable person paralysed by the result. Tell it from the text;
  no added dialogue. *Connection:* the formula is Krsna's reply to that paralysis.
- **IYF's teaching analogies** (document): the hot coal; the camera without focus; the dam and the flood; the kite and
  its string; the farmer and the rain; the ship sunk only by water that gets inside; the half-full glass; the two
  shoe salesmen in a barefoot country. Illustrations, not scripture; say so. The "right map" story (a map of
  Bombay labelled Pune) is adapted from S. R. Covey's *The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People* (1989), a popular
  book, where it is a map of Detroit printed as Chicago.
- **Contemporary (invented sketch):** Tanvi and Harsh (H). Label it invented.
- **Not used:** IYF's document mentions Srila Prabhupada speaking of a worldwide mission to a subway conductor in New
  York, and a remark about "5,000 children"; both are from his biography and **require source review** (locate the
  passage in *Srila Prabhupada-lilamrta* before telling). *Checked 28 Oct 2026 against the new sources (acharyas'
  books, Preaching Encyclopedia, Desire Tree transcripts, IYF decks, the head's book folder): none has either passage;
  the item stays open.* The Haridhan Patel bus-stand anecdote, the divorcing husband who sold everything cheaply, and

  only as "a story that is told", or omit. The saying "Watch your thoughts, they become words…" is popular and of

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"Positive thinking is a cliché. Half-full glass, so what?"**
   - Agreed, and IYF's own document says so: without the right map, positive attitude "would be foolish". The Gita's
     version is not cheerfulness but a *trained* mind that does not run after every glitter (BG 6.5 purport) and
     practises satisfaction, gravity and self-control (BG 17.16). That is harder and more useful.
2. **"Why should 'one aim' be God? I have career aims."**
   - The verse (BG 2.41) contrasts one aim with a many-branched intelligence; its purport names the one aim as Krsna
     consciousness and explains why with the root-and-tree image: watering the root reaches every branch. At VL1 we
     offer this as the Gita's proposal. Career aims are branches; the question is whether they have a root.
3. **"If I'm not the doer, why work hard at all?"**
   - BG 2.47 forbids attachment to *not* working; BG 3.8 says work is better than not working; BG 18.26 asks for
     "great determination and enthusiasm". Non-doership (BG 3.27; BG 18.14) removes the anxiety and the arrogance,
     not the effort. The farmer still ploughs.
4. **"I've tried controlling my mind. It doesn't work."**
   - Krsna accepts Arjuna's complaint that the mind is harder to control than the wind, and answers: "by suitable
     practice and by detachment" (BG 6.35). BG 6.26 describes the practice: bring it back, every time. The purport
     adds that hearing about Krsna is the most effective practice; many here find chanting the same. Offered, not
     pressed.
5. **"Does one small indulgence really matter?"**
   - BG 2.67: as a strong wind sweeps away a boat, one roaming sense "can carry away a man's intelligence". Not a
     threat; an observation the room can test against their last lost evening.
6. **"What if I follow all six and still fail?"**
   - Then you will have failed as the kind of person who can begin again. The Gita's promise is about the endeavour,
     not the result: "no loss or diminution" (BG 2.40), and steadiness "in success or failure" (BG 18.26). It never
     promises the outcome, and a formula that did would be lying.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: Four passions in two years.** Vikram has started coding, then a startup, then civil-services prep, then
design. Each began with excitement and ended within months. He now calls himself "a generalist".
- **Dilemma:** Is variety a virtue here, or avoidance?
- **Principles:** BG 2.41 (one aim versus many-branched intelligence); BG 6.26 (bringing the mind back); BG 2.40 (no
  loss, so a lapse is not a reason to switch).
- **Questions:**
  - What does Vikram do at the moment a pursuit gets hard?
  - What would "one aim for one year" require of him?
  - Which letter of PARTHA is his weakest?
- **Suggested analysis:** The pattern is switching at the first plateau, which is the mind's flicker (BG 6.26)
  dressed as curiosity. One aim, held for an agreed period, is the experiment; BG 2.40 means the earlier attempts
  were not wasted if the lesson is learned.
- **Takeaway:** Breadth chosen is strength; breadth from flinching is drift.

**Case 2: The credit.** Priyanka led a project that succeeded. In the review she lists everything she did; a
colleague notes the team, the timing and a lucky market. She feels robbed. A month later the next project fails
and she is devastated.
- **Principles:** BG 3.27 (false ego takes all credit); BG 18.14 (five factors); BG 18.26 (unwavering in success or
  failure).
- **Questions:**
  - Were the success and the failure both "hers" in the same sense?
  - How does taking full credit for the first set up the devastation of the second?
  - What would "giving the credit to God" change in how she speaks about both?
- **Suggested analysis:** The same false ego that owns success must own failure. Seeing the five factors right-sizes
  both. She can still state her contribution honestly; she stops staking her worth on the verdict.
- **Takeaway:** Who owns your successes owns your failures.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first; praise every try)
1. What do the six letters of PARTHA stand for? *Positive thinking, Aim, Restraint, Training, Hard work, Abiding interest.*
2. Which verse says the mind is friend and enemy? *BG 6.5.*
3. What does BG 6.26 say to do when the mind wanders? *Bring it back, again and again.*
4. What do we have a right to, according to BG 2.47? *The work, not its results.*
5. What does BG 2.40 say about effort in this path? *There is no loss or diminution.*
6. Does the formula promise marks or money? *No; it describes the person who works well whatever the result.*

**M.2 Activity (about 10 minutes): "Six-letter self-audit"**
1. 2 min: each person scores themselves privately from 1 to 5 on each letter and circles the lowest.
2. 4 min, in pairs: each says what "one step this week" would be for the lowest letter (the score stays private).
3. 3 min: volunteers share one step. Thank each volunteer.
4. 1 min: link to BG 2.40: small, steady steps are not lost.

**M.3 Reflection**
- **Reflection:** Score yourself privately 1–5 on each letter. Which is lowest? Which was highest two years ago?
- **Application (private, optional):** For your lowest letter, define what "done" would look like by next week.
  *(Nobody needs to share.)*

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

PARTHA is a six-letter formula for success drawn from the Bhagavad-gita (an ancient Sanskrit scripture, a conversation between Krsna and the warrior Arjuna). "Partha" is also a name of Arjuna. The formula does not promise marks or money. It describes the person who works well whatever happens.

**P**ositive thinking: the mind is "the friend of the conditioned soul, and his enemy as well" (BG 6.5), so train it. **A**im: resolute intelligence has one aim; the irresolute mind is "many-branched" (BG 2.41). **R**estraint: wherever the mind wanders, "bring it back" (BG 6.26); each return builds will.

**T**raining: give the task full attention, and remember that you have a right to the work, not to the result (BG 2.47); five factors make any result (BG 18.14). **H**ard work: the good worker has "great determination and enthusiasm" and does not waver "in success or failure" (BG 18.26). **A**biding interest: start today and improve a little at a time, because in this endeavour "there is no loss or diminution" (BG 2.40). This week, score yourself on the six letters and pick the lowest.

## S2. Key ideas

- Success is first a way of working, not a result.
- The mind is friend or enemy depending on training (BG 6.5–6).
- One aim; many branches scatter (BG 2.41).
- Restraint is bringing the mind back, again and again (BG 6.26).
- Effort is mine; the result has five factors (BG 2.47; BG 18.14).
- Steady enthusiasm, even in failure (BG 18.26); nothing sincere is lost (BG 2.40).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **BG 6.6:** "For him who has conquered the mind, the mind is the best of friends; but for one who has failed to do
  so, his mind will remain the greatest enemy." *For me:* my mind is a tool to train, not a boss to obey.
- **BG 2.41:** "Those who are on this path are resolute in purpose, and their aim is one… the intelligence of those who
  are irresolute is many-branched." *For me:* when I keep switching, I can ask what I am avoiding.
- **BG 2.40:** "In this endeavor there is no loss or diminution…" *For me:* a lapse is not zero; I resume from where I
  reached.

## S4. Words to know

- **PARTHA:** Positive thinking, Aim, Restraint, Training, Hard work, Abiding interest. Also Arjuna's name (son of
  Prtha), to whom the Gita was spoken.
- **Doership:** the feeling "I alone did this". The Gita says five factors make a result (BG 18.14) and that false ego
  makes us forget it (BG 3.27).
- **Resolute intelligence:** one-pointed determination (BG 2.41).

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Isn't positive thinking a cliché?**
   Yes, if it means forced cheer. The Gita's version is a trained mind that does not run after every glitter
   (BG 6.5 purport) and practises satisfaction, gravity and self-control (BG 17.16).
2. **Why should my one aim be God? I have career goals.**
   The Gita contrasts one aim with a scattered mind (BG 2.41) and proposes the Supreme as the root that nourishes
   every branch. Career goals are branches. Whether they have a root is yours to explore.
3. **If I'm not the doer, why work hard?**
   The Gita forbids attachment to not working (BG 2.47) and praises "great determination and enthusiasm"
   (BG 18.26). Non-doership removes anxiety and arrogance, not effort.
4. **My mind won't obey. What then?**
   Krsna agrees it is hard and says it is possible "by suitable practice and by detachment" (BG 6.35). The practice
   is simple and repeated: bring it back (BG 6.26).
5. **What if I follow all six and still fail?**
   The formula never promised the outcome. It promises that the endeavour is not lost (BG 2.40) and that you can
   stay steady and begin again (BG 18.26).

## S6. Check your understanding

1. What do the six letters of PARTHA stand for?
2. According to BG 6.5–6, when is the mind a friend and when an enemy?
3. BG 2.41 says the intelligence of the irresolute is: (a) sharp, (b) many-branched, (c) empty.
4. Name the five factors of action in BG 18.14.
5. In your own words: why does BG 2.40 matter for someone who has lapsed in a good habit?

**Answers:** 1. Positive thinking, Aim, Restraint, Training, Hard work, Abiding interest. 2. A friend when conquered
(trained), an enemy when not (BG 6.6). 3. (b) (BG 2.41). 4. The place of action (body), the performer, the senses, the
endeavour, the Supersoul (BG 18.14). 5. Because sincere endeavour has "no loss or diminution": one resumes from what
was gained, not from zero (BG 2.40 and purport).
