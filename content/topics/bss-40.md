# 44. Falldown

> BSS · 120 min · drafted · SENSITIVE (sexuality, failure and shame; adult audience; no private disclosure). Follows BIKC chapter 44. Builds on bss-05 (the ladder from contemplation to falldown, and Ajamila) and bss-39 (the Krishna conscious way to deal with desire). This page begins where those end: after a fall has happened, or when we are asked to help someone who has fallen.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level
BIKC chapter 44 says honestly that falls happen, that senior devotees also fall, and that this disturbs everyone. A BSS student needs two things: a clear picture of how serious a fall is, and a clear picture of how mercy works. If he has only the first, he may despair after a mistake. If he has only the second, he may become careless. The Bhagavatam gives both, and this page gives them together.

## C. Central message
A fall is serious, but it is not the end. The Lord and the scriptures promise that a sincere devotee who returns to chanting and service is not lost, and the right response from the devotee himself and from his friends and seniors is honesty, care and a quick return, never contempt and never despair.

## D. Learning outcomes
1. Explain from BG 9.30-31 and SB 11.5.42 why a sincere devotee who falls is still a devotee, and what the same texts warn against.
2. Retell Bharata Maharaja's fall and recovery (SB 5.8.26, SB 5.9.1-2) and end it with its devotional lesson.
3. List five practical steps for returning after a fall.
4. Explain how a friend or senior should respond to someone who confides a fall.
5. Say where to get help, including in a crisis.

**BG 9.30** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/9/30/
> "Even if one commits the most abominable action, if he is engaged in devotional service he is to be considered saintly because he is properly situated in his determination."
- Context: Krishna to Arjuna, in the chapter on the most confidential knowledge.
- Purport point: the purport speaks of "a temporary falldown" that "does not disqualify" a person who is wholeheartedly engaged in Krishna's service, because the Lord "being situated within his heart, purifies him and excuses him from that abomination". It says "No one should deride a devotee for some accidental falldown from the ideal path". It also warns: "one should not misunderstand that a devotee in transcendental devotional service can act in all kinds of abominable ways; this verse only refers to an accident due to the strong power of material connections." And: "No one should take advantage of this verse and commit nonsense and think that he is still a devotee. If he does not improve in his character by devotional service, then it is to be understood that he is not a high devotee."
- Application (preacher's): read the verse together with its warning. The verse is for an accident of a sincere person. It is not permission to fall on purpose.
- Use: in the talk.

## F. Philosophical explanation
**F.1 The honest picture first.** BIKC says that a fall causes "great disturbance", especially when the person is senior; that it usually begins with contemplation, from "the seed of sinful desire in the mind"; and that it can sometimes happen accidentally, "without previous contemplation", when a devotee finds himself in a situation for which he has "insufficient spiritual strength". It says that falls, "even mental" ones, are damaging, and that if there is no attempt at rectification, "we can expect Kṛṣṇa to reciprocate such insincerity by withdrawing His mercy. Thus the privilege of devotional service is lost." It quotes CC Madhya 25.279, "If one falls into such positions, he is finished", and says: "Therefore a brahmacārī is cautious at every step of his life, so as to avoid danger." Bharata's story (SB 5.8.26 and purport) shows the consequences were real. The Bhagavatam is never casual about falls.

**F.2 Then the honest hope.** The same scriptures give a very clear promise. A sincere devotee who falls is not rejected (BG 9.30-31, SB 11.5.42). The Lord who is in his heart removes the reaction (SB 11.5.42). He forgives an accidental fall (SB 3.12.32 purport). Nothing done in Krishna consciousness is lost (BG 2.40, 6.40, SB 1.5.17). BIKC puts it in one line: an inadvertent, temporary fall "is not a disqualification for devotional service", and the devotee "must pick himself up quickly and carry on". The two halves belong together. The sincerity that makes a fall painful is the same sincerity that makes return possible.

**F.3 What "sincere" means.** The purport of BG 9.30 explains that the verse is about "an accident due to the strong power of material connections", and that a person who takes advantage of it and does not improve in character "is not a high devotee". So the question to ask is not "How bad was it?" but "Am I turning back to Krishna, and am I willing to change what led here?" (Application (preacher's).)

**F.4 Guilt that protects and guilt that paralyses.** Chaitanya Charan Das says there are two extremes: being so consumed by guilt that one cannot do anything in devotional life, and being so numb that one does wrong without feeling it. A healthy sense of regret protects us, but guilt that keeps us away from chanting is itself a temptation. He advises that, once the first emotion has calmed, a person should look calmly at what led to the fall and make practical changes, "not to punish oneself" but so as not to be trapped again; and that the failure should be "an impetus … to strengthen our devotional service". He compares it to a patient who must take medicine enthusiastically and also avoid spreading the disease (https://www.thespiritualscientist.com/as-guilt-due-to-falldown-can-also-be-a-temptation-should-one-just-neglect-guilt-and-keep-practicing-devotional-service/). This is a supporting realization, not a verse.

**F.5 A way back in five steps (Application (preacher's), drawn from the texts above).**
1. **Chant today.** BG 9.31's purport says to continue chanting "without stoppage". SB 11.5.42's purport says to "immediately resume" service. Do not wait until you feel clean. Do your rounds, go to the next programme, and eat prasada.
2. **Tell a senior you trust.** BIKC's masturbation chapter and chapter 43 both say a devotee should speak to a trusted senior and not hide. This is difficult, and it is the step that removes the most weight. Ask for a private place and say only what you can.
3. **Look calmly at what led up to it.** Use the steps in bss-05 (the ladder from a first look or thought to the act): where did it start, and which one change would have helped? Make that change with your senior's help (a different routine, a different company, a time limit on the phone).
4. **Make things right where others are affected.** If another person was hurt, or a promise was broken, ask your senior how to be honest and fair. If force, a minor or any pressure was involved, this is a safeguarding and legal matter, and the temple's safeguarding officer and the law must be told. It cannot be handled as a private falldown.
5. **Rebuild with company and service.** Return to kirtana, the Bhagavatam class and a regular service. BIKC says to stay "tight in the association of devotees" and to keep hearing and chanting with full attention.

Whether a person should remain as a brahmacari or choose family life is a question to decide with a senior. BIKC (ch. 44) says: "if a brahmacārī persistently cannot control his mind, he had better get married before he falls down into illicit sex", and it quotes a letter of Srila Prabhupada to the same effect (Requires source review: the letter was not found on vedabase.io during preparation, so it is not quoted here).

**F.6 The senior's care.** When a friend confides in you, do these things. Listen to the end without interrupting. Thank him for trusting you. Say that BG 9.30-31 and SB 11.5.42 give hope. Do not tell anyone else unless safety requires it. Do not joke, gossip or lower your respect for him ("No one should deride a devotee for some accidental falldown", BG 9.30 purport). Help him find a senior and, if he needs it, a counsellor. Stay his friend. Invite him to kirtana the next day.

**F.7 When a senior or leader falls.** BIKC says the fall of a senior devotee causes great disturbance. Chaitanya Charan Das says a leader who cannot follow the standard may need to step back from a responsible position so as not to teach one thing and do another. For ordinary members, the fall of any person is a reason to look to the Lord, who is not changed, and to pray for the person. Do not spread rumours.

**F.8 Health and crisis.** Shame can sometimes lead to dark thoughts. If you, or a friend, feel hopeless, think of harming yourself, or cannot sleep or eat for days, speak to someone today. Tell a person near you. In India, Tele-MANAS (14416) is a free helpline. Elsewhere, call your local crisis service. A qualified counsellor or doctor is fully compatible with devotional life. If there is a risk of a sexually transmitted infection, a doctor can advise; this page makes no medical claim. (Pastoral guidance.)

## J. Stories and illustrations
**J.1 Bharata Maharaja (SB 5.8.26 and SB 5.9.1-2).** Bharata was a king who left his kingdom, his son and his family for the forest to worship the Lord (SB 5.8.26 mentions his giving up his family). He became attached to an orphaned deer and cared for it so much that he fell from his worship (SB 5.8.26). At the time of death his mind was on the deer, and so he took the body of a deer (Prabhupada's purport to SB 1.5.17 explains this). Prabhupada's purport says that this happened because he "purposefully became over-addicted" to the deer and neglected his spiritual advancement, and that the deer body was given "for a short time" to rectify him. In the deer body he did not forget his past; he was repentant, lived in a solitary forest and "always thought of Kṛṣṇa" (SB 5.9.1-2 purport). Then he was born in a very pure brahmana family (SB 5.9.1-2). **Devotional lesson:** Bharata's fall had real consequences, and the Lord's purpose in them was mercy. The deer body was given for a short time to rectify him and bring him to pure devotional service (SB 5.8.26 purport). The same Lord who allowed the consequence brought him back. (bss-05 covers Ajamila, who also fell and returned; do not retell it here. Do not add details about Bharata's later births that are not in these verses.)

**J.2 Everyday image (Application (preacher's)).** A mountain climber who slips on a rock stops, checks his hands and feet, looks at the rock to see what went wrong, and then climbs on, usually with a rope and a companion. He does not sit down and call himself a failure. In the same way, the Krishna conscious point is that after a fall we look to our Lord and to our companions, the devotees, and we climb again. BIKC makes the same comparison: "We may stumble while climbing a mountain, but we have to re-gather our strength and determination and resume the climb."

## K. Anticipated questions and objections
- *If the Lord forgives, why should I try hard?* The purport of BG 9.30 answers: a person who uses the verse as a licence and does not improve is "not a high devotee". Forgiveness is for the sincere person who is trying.
- *How can I be sure I am sincere?* Ask: Am I chanting and going to programmes? Am I willing to tell a senior? Am I willing to change what led to the fall? If yes, you are on the path BG 9.31 describes.
- *Does one fall cancel all my service?* No. BG 2.40 says a little advancement protects from the greatest fear, and "one percent done in Kṛṣṇa consciousness bears permanent results".
- *I feel too ashamed to chant.* This is the guilt that Chaitanya Charan Das calls a temptation. The purport of SB 11.5.42 says to resume service immediately. Chant softly if you must, but chant.
- *Should I leave the ashram?* That is a decision to take calmly with a senior (F.5). It is not a punishment, and a person who leaves is still a devotee.
- *A senior devotee fell. Does that prove the philosophy is false?* No. The philosophy rests on Krishna and the scriptures, not on a person. BIKC says the fall of a senior is very disturbing. Pray for him; do not gossip.
- *My friend told me something serious about himself. What do I do?* Follow F.6. If he speaks of harming himself, stay with him and call a helpline or a senior at once.

## L. Case studies
**L.1 Invented case for discussion.** *Situation:* Anil, a brahmacari student, fell on a trip. For two weeks he has not chanted properly, avoids his mentor, and has stopped attending the Bhagavatam class. A friend, Pavan, notices. *Dilemma:* Pavan does not know whether to speak to him. *Principles:* BG 9.31 (continue chanting without stoppage), SB 11.5.42 (resume at once), BG 9.30 purport (do not deride). *Questions:* (a) What would Pavan say first? (b) What should he avoid? (c) What small step can Anil take today? *Analysis:* Pavan can say, "I have missed you in the class. I am not here to judge. Can I walk with you to the next kirtana?" He should avoid asking for details, gossiping, or saying "how could you?". Anil's step today is to chant a few rounds and ask his mentor for a private meeting. *Takeaway:* the first help a friend gives is company, not questions.

## M. Discussion and reflection
**M.1 Closing check questions.**
1. What does BG 9.31 say about Krishna's devotee? *"My devotee never perishes."*
2. What warning does the purport of BG 9.30 add? *That nobody should take advantage of the verse; a person who does not improve is not a high devotee.*
3. What happened to Bharata Maharaja, and what did he do in the deer body? *He took a deer's body because of his attachment; he remembered, repented and always thought of Krishna; then he was born in a good brahmana family.*
4. Name three of the five steps back. *For example: chant today, tell a senior, look calmly at what led up to it.*
5. How should a friend respond to a person who confides? *Listen, thank him, keep it private, do not mock, help him find a senior or counsellor, invite him to kirtana.*
6. What can you do if you or a friend feel hopeless? *Tell someone nearby now; call a helpline (Tele-MANAS 14416 in India) or local crisis service.*

**M.2 Activities.**
(a) *The friend's reply (15 min):* in groups of three, one person plays Anil's friend Pavan from L.1, one plays Anil, and one watches. Pavan speaks for one minute using F.6. The watcher says what was kind and what could be changed. Rotate roles. The scene is invented; nobody plays his own life.
(b) *Find the promise (10 min):* groups read BG 9.30, BG 9.31 and SB 11.5.42 and write one sentence each that a devotee could say to himself after a fall, with the warning included.
(c) *Anonymous question box (10 min):* the preacher answers written questions from E and F only.

**M.3 Reflection.** "If I fell, whom would I trust to tell, and what would I want him to say first?" Private.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary
A falldown means breaking the basic rules of devotional life, especially in the area of illicit sex. It is serious. The story of Bharata Maharaja shows that. He became attached to a deer, lost his devotion for a time and took a deer's body. But the story is also a story of mercy. In the deer body he remembered, repented and thought of Krishna, and then he was born in a good family (SB 5.8.26, SB 5.9.1-2).

Krishna gives a clear promise. If a person engaged in devotional service falls, he is still to be considered saintly because of his determination (BG 9.30), and Krishna says, "My devotee never perishes" (BG 9.31). The Lord in the heart takes away the reaction of an accidental sin and the devotee should immediately resume his service (SB 11.5.42). Nothing done in Krishna consciousness is lost (BG 2.40, BG 6.40, SB 1.5.17). But Prabhupada's purport also warns that nobody should use these verses as a licence. The promise is for the sincere person who turns back and improves.

After a fall, five steps help. Chant today. Tell a trusted senior. Look calmly at what led to the fall and change one thing. Make things right with anybody who was affected. Rebuild with kirtana, hearing and service. If a friend tells you about his fall, listen, keep it private, do not mock him and invite him to kirtana. If you feel hopeless, tell someone near you now and call a helpline (Tele-MANAS 14416 in India, or your local crisis service). If anyone was forced or harmed, tell the temple's safeguarding officer.

## S2. Key ideas
- A fall is serious, but a sincere devotee who falls is not rejected.
- The Lord in the heart forgives an accidental fall, and the devotee should resume service at once.
- The promise is not a licence. The purport of BG 9.30 warns against taking advantage of it.
- The way back: chant, tell a senior, learn from the causes, make things right, rebuild.
- A friend who confides in you needs your company, not your questions.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **BG 9.31**: "declare it boldly that My devotee never perishes." *For me: I can always return.*
- **SB 11.5.42**: the Lord takes away the reaction of an accidental sin. *For me: I resume my service at once.*
- **BG 2.40**: no loss in this endeavour. *For me: what I have done for Krishna is not wasted.*

## S4. Words to know
- **Falldown:** breaking the regulative principles, especially by illicit sex.
- **Regulative principles:** no meat, intoxication, gambling or illicit sex.
- **Rectification:** setting right.
- **Safeguarding officer:** the person at a temple who handles reports of harm to others.

## S5. Questions and answers
- *Is a fall the end?* No; BG 9.31 says the devotee never perishes.
- *Can I rely on forgiveness and fall again?* No; the purport of BG 9.30 warns against that.
- *Why should I tell a senior?* It removes the weight and he can help you make a plan.
- *What if I feel too ashamed to chant?* Resume at once, even softly (SB 11.5.42 purport).
- *What if someone else was harmed?* Tell a senior and the safeguarding officer; this is not a private matter.
