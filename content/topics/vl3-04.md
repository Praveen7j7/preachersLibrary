## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

By VL3 everyone chants; few have been *taught* to chant. VL2 gave do's and don'ts and the reason to start (batch-criteria §8). VL3 is
where the practice is formed, and the Gita's "suitable practice" of VL3-01 and the "approach" point of VL3-03 both land here. The page
therefore spends a third of its time on why (so that the practice has roots) and two thirds on how (so that it has a shape). The
offences get one minute: the Caitanya-caritamrta says there are offences to be considered (Adi 8.24), so a VL3 chanter should know the
list exists and one safeguard; the ten are treated fully at DPS-06.

Builds on VL3-01 and VL3-03: BG 6.26 (bring the mind back) and the "approach" point of the peace formula are only referred to; this page teaches the japa method itself and does not re-teach routine-building or the formula.

## C. Central message

In this age there is no other way than chanting the holy name (CC Adi 17.21); the name is Krsna Himself present as sound (CC Adi 17.22;
SB 1.1.14 purport), and it cleanses the mirror of the heart (CC Antya 20.12). The art is simple and learnable: a fixed time and place,
clear pronunciation, attentive hearing, the mind brought back each time it leaves (BG 6.26), and the mood of a child calling its mother,
humbler than the grass (CC Antya 20.21). Chant a number you will keep every day, agreed with your facilitator, and increase from there.

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. Give the scriptural case for chanting in Kali-yuga in three sentences (CC Adi 17.21–22; SB 12.3.51–52; SB 11.5.32).
2. State from Siksastaka 1 (CC Antya 20.12) what chanting does to the heart, and from SB 6.2.19 why it works even before understanding.
3. Demonstrate the method for one round: posture, beads, pronunciation, hearing, pace, returning the mind (BG 6.26), prayerful mood.
4. Say what the Siksastaka's third verse asks of the chanter (CC Antya 20.21) and why humility makes chanting possible.
5. Know that there are ten offences to the Name, name the first (criticising devotees, CC Adi 8.24 purport) and one safeguard; and fix a
   daily number with their facilitator for the coming month.

> "‘In this Age of Kali there is no other means, no other means, no other means for self-realization than chanting the holy name, chanting the holy name, chanting the holy name of Lord Hari.’"

- **Teaching:** The triple repetition is the text's own emphasis: in this age, this is the means.

**CC Adi 17.22**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/adi/17/22/

- **Teaching:** The name *is* the Lord's incarnation for this age; chanting is direct association.

> 12.3.52: "Whatever result was obtained in Satya-yuga by meditating on Viṣṇu, in Tretā-yuga by performing sacrifices, and in Dvāpara-yuga by serving the Lord’s lotus feet can be obtained in Kali-yuga simply by chanting the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra."

- **Teaching:** The mood that makes constant chanting possible: humility, tolerance, respect for others.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **Why this practice in this age.** The texts are unanimous and specific: no other means (CC Adi 17.21); the name is the Lord's
   incarnation for Kali-yuga and chanting is direct association (17.22); the age's one virtue (SB 12.3.51); the results of all earlier
   ages' methods by this one (12.3.52); the Lord Himself appears to inaugurate it (11.5.32); the wise even praise the age for it (11.5.36).
   The Gita names japa the best of sacrifices (10.25). A VL3 participant should be able to say this in three sentences.
2. **What the name is.** Not a sound *about* Krsna but Krsna as sound (SB 1.1.14 purport: "as powerful as Lord Kṛṣṇa Himself"). Hence
   it works like medicine, before understanding (SB 6.2.19), and even hearing it purifies (9.5.16). Prabhupada's explanation of the
   words (Hare as the Lord's energy; Krsna, the all-attractive; Rama, the reservoir of pleasure; the mantra as a prayer: "O energy of the
   Lord, O Lord, please engage me in Your service") is in his essay on the maha-mantra, reprinted in *The Science of Self-Realization*
   (**Partially verified**: read it before quoting; the IYF deck's version adds "Radharani" and "Radha-ramana", which is a Gaudiya
   gloss, not Prabhupada's wording there).
3. **What it does.** Siksastaka 1 (CC Antya 20.12): it cleanses the heart's mirror, extinguishes the fire, spreads good fortune, is the
   life of all education, expands the ocean of bliss, cools, and gives nectar at every step. The first effect explains the VL3
   experience: the mirror shows dust first. A chanter who sees more of their own restlessness in the first months is seeing the mirror
   being cleaned, not failing.
4. **Why a method.** Siksastaka 2 (20.16) says "no hard and fast rules", and 20.18 says any time, any place. So why teach time, place,
   beads and pace? SB 2.1.11: chant "after the ways of the great authorities", and the purport: "hearing from the authorities is the
   first essential". NOI 8: chant *nicely*. The method is not a rule the name needs; it is help the chanter needs (CC Madhya 22.113: the
   rules serve remembrance). Say both sentences together, always.
5. **The method (teach this slowly; it is the heart of the page).** *The method is the preacher's arrangement*, drawn from the texts
   and from how we have been taught. Only the step "before beginning" is an explicit instruction in Srila Prabhupada's purports
   (CC Adi 7.4, 8.24); the text named beside each other step is what it draws on, not a text that prescribes it.
   - *Time:* early morning before the day's noise, after bathing if possible; fixed. (Why: the mind is least crowded; a fixed time
     makes a habit. *Application (preacher's)*, consistent with the 12.9 purport's "rise early", VL3-01.)
   - *Place:* the same spot; sitting upright, or walking slowly if sleepy. Phone out of reach or silent.
   - *Beads:* 108 beads and a head bead; the tradition holds the beads in the right hand between thumb and middle finger, not touching
     the index finger, and turns back at the head bead rather than crossing it (the tradition's practice, received from teachers, not
     a verse; teach it as "how we have been taught").
   - *Before beginning:* the Pancha-tattva mantra (*śrī-kṛṣṇa-caitanya prabhu-nityānanda śrī-advaita gadādhara śrīvāsādi-gaura-bhakta-vṛnda*):
     Srila Prabhupada's instruction: the CC Adi 7.4 purport ("we first offer our obeisances… by chanting this Pañca-tattva mantra; then
     we say Hare Kṛṣṇa") and the CC Adi 8.24 purport (chanting *śrī-kṛṣṇa-caitanya prabhu-nityānanda* first is "very beneficial"; one
     then "becomes free of offenses").
   - *Pronunciation:* every word, every syllable: Ha-re Kṛṣ-ṇa; no swallowing of "Hare", no "Krishnaaa" slide. Loud enough to hear
     yourself (japa is soft, not silent).
   - *Hearing:* the ear is the main sense in japa (SB 9.5.16; 2.1.11 purport). Listen to each mantra as if someone else were saying
     it to you.
   - *Pace:* steady, neither racing nor dragging. A round commonly takes about six to eight minutes for a clear chanter; this is a
     *typical* pace reported by practitioners, not a rule. Time one round once to know your own.
   - *The mind:* it will leave. BG 6.26: bring it back, without a quarrel. Return to the sound of the current mantra. The IYF handout's
     advice to replace a thought with its opposite (Patanjali's *pratipakṣa-bhāvanam*) is reasonable; at VL3 the simpler instruction is
     "back to the sound". Count returns, not failures (VL3-01).
   - *The mood:* a child calling its mother (Srila Prabhupada's recurring image, summarised in the Encyclopedia's *Setting the Right
     Mood*; **Partially verified** as to any exact sentence: quote the image, not a sentence). Humility and respect for others
     (Siksastaka 3, 20.21). A short prayer before the first bead ("please let me hear You") is the "approach" of VL3-03.
   - *The number:* a daily number you will keep *every* day, agreed with your facilitator; the VL3 target is four, the KPI is increase.
     Finish the number; record it honestly on the sadhana card.
   - *Afterwards:* a moment of quiet; no immediate phone.
6. **Offences, briefly.** CC Adi 8.24: there are offences; simply chanting does not make one ecstatic. The purport: ten of them, the first
   being to criticise devotees who spread the name. 20.16: the Lord's own voice confessing that offences block attachment. One safeguard
   for VL3: do not criticise devotees, and begin with the Pancha-tattva mantra. 8.26: offenceless chanting brings pure devotion and love
   of God. The list is DPS-06's work; a VL3 chanter should know it exists and want to learn it.
7. **Mood and the rest of the day.** 20.21 ties chanting to humility and tolerance; the Encyclopedia's *Whole day's activities affects
   next day japa* draws the practical consequence. The four principles (VL3-02) and the six urges (VL3-01) are japa's day-long support.
8. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - "If my mind wanders, the round is wasted." SB 6.2.19: the medicine works regardless; attention makes it work faster.
   - "I should feel ecstasy by now." SB 2.3.24 and its purport: a *gradual* change of heart shown by detachment. Tears are not the
     weekly target.
   - "More rounds, faster, is better." NOI 8: *nicely*. Increase from a number you keep, not from a number you race.
   - "The rules make the name work." 20.16: all potency is in the name already; the method helps *me*.
   - "Chanting is a technique for calm." The texts claim more: association with the Lord (17.22). The deck's brain-wave claims are out (O).
   - "I’m not initiated, so it doesn’t count." CC Madhya 15.108: initiation is not a precondition for the name to act.
9. **Pitch for VL3.** Practical, encouraging. Terms: *japa*, *round*, *mahā-mantra*, *Śikṣāṣṭaka*, *nāmāparādha* (defined once each).
   Half the session should be about *how*, and the workshop form includes an actual round.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **No other way in this age.** CC Adi 17.21–22; SB 12.3.51–52; 11.5.32, 36; BG 10.25. *Example:* the king who kills all thieves at once
   (12.3.51 purport). *Transition:* what is this name?
2. **The name is Krsna as sound.** SB 1.1.14 purport; 6.2.19 (medicine). *Example:* the medicine works before the patient understands.
   *Transition:* what does it do?
3. **It cleans the mirror.** Siksastaka 1 (Antya 20.12). *Example:* the first wipe shows the dust. *Transition:* then how do I chant?
4. **No hard and fast rules, and after the authorities.** 20.16; 20.18; SB 2.1.11; NOI 8 ("nicely"). *Transition:* the method.
5. **The method.** Time, place, beads, Pancha-tattva, pronunciation, hearing, pace, the mind (BG 6.26), the mood (20.21), the number.
   *Example:* one mantra demonstrated slowly. *Transition:* what can spoil it?
6. **Offences exist.** Adi 8.24 purport (ten; the first); 8.26. *Transition:* so, your number.
7. **A number you keep.** BG 9.14 (determination); the facilitator; the card. *Transition:* to P and VL3-05.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"I’m not initiated. Does my chanting count?"**
   - CC Madhya 15.108: "One does not have to undergo initiation… One simply has to vibrate the holy name with his lips." It counts.
     Initiation (DPS) formalises a relationship; it does not switch the name on.
2. **"My mind wanders through every round. Is it wasted?"**
   - SB 6.2.19: the medicine works even without understanding. Not wasted. But attention makes it work faster, and the method exists
     for that. Bring the mind back (BG 6.26) and count the returns.
3. **"Why beads, a fixed time and a count, if there are 'no hard and fast rules'?"**
   - Both are scripture: 20.16 and 20.18 (any time, any place) and SB 2.1.11 ("after the ways of the great authorities"). The rules do
     not make the name work; they make *you* work (CC Madhya 22.113: the rules serve remembrance).
4. **"I can only chant on the metro. Is that acceptable?"**
   - 20.18: "Regardless of time or place… attains all perfection." Yes. And it will be better if one of your rounds is at a fixed quiet
     time at home; try to add that one.
5. **"I feel nothing when I chant."**
   - CC Madhya 17.136: material senses cannot appreciate the name; by the service of chanting and prasadam the tongue is purified and
     understanding comes gradually. Taste follows practice; it does not precede it (BG 2.59 purport, VL3-01).
6. **"The handout says if I don’t feel ecstasy my chanting is offensive. Should I be worried?"**
   - SB 2.3.24 says a heart that does not change under concentrated chanting is steel-framed; the purport stresses *gradual* change,
     shown by detachment. Take it as a reason to learn the offences (DPS-06) and chant attentively, not as a verdict. No one is asked
     to produce tears.
7. **"How many rounds should I chant?"**
   - The VL3 standard is four daily, and the goal is to increase (batch-criteria §3). Fix the number with your facilitator: one you will
     keep every day for a month. Then increase. NOI 8 says "nicely"; a kept four beats a raced eight.
8. **"Why does it matter if I skip a day?"**
   - BG 8.5–7: what we remember at the end is what we practised; "always think of Me" (8.7). Daily practice builds the habit of
     remembrance. One missed day is restarted the next morning, not mourned (VL3-01, BG 12.10).
9. **"Can I use a chanting app or a counter instead of beads?"**
   - The name works regardless (20.16). Beads keep the hand, ear and mind together and keep the phone away; that is the only
     argument, and it is a practical one. If beads are impossible in a situation, a counter is better than no round.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The metro chanter.** Sumit chants four rounds daily on the metro with earphones in, finishing in about twenty minutes, and
has done so for eight months. He says it has become "just something I do".
- **Principles:** 20.18 (place is fine); NOI 8 ("nicely"); SB 2.1.11 (after the authorities: pace, hearing); BG 6.26.
- **Questions:** Is anything wrong with the metro? What has gone missing: place, pace or hearing? What one change (one round at home at
  a fixed time, slower, aloud) would he try for two weeks?
- **Takeaway:** Keep the metro rounds, add one attentive round; let the attentive one teach the others.

**Case 2: The discouraged chanter.** Meera read that chanting without ecstasy is offensive, examined herself, found no tears, and has
started to dread her rounds.
- **Principles:** SB 2.3.24 purport (gradual change, detachment as the sign); CC Antya 20.16 (even the Lord's own voice confesses
  offences and asks for mercy); 8.24 purport (learn the ten; begin with Pancha-tattva); 6.2.19 (the medicine works).
- **Questions:** What does the purport say the change of heart looks like? What has changed in Meera's life in a year of chanting
  (habits, company, desires)? What would she tell her facilitator?
- **Takeaway:** Measure detachment over months, not tears over minutes; learn the offences as a help, not a fear.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first; praise every try)
1. In this age, what does CC Adi 17.21 give as the way? *Chanting the holy name.*
2. What is the first effect of chanting in Siksastaka 1? *It cleanses the mirror of the heart (CC Antya 20.12).*
3. Why does the name work even if we do not understand it? *Like medicine, its potency does not depend on the patient's understanding (SB 6.2.19).*
4. What does BG 6.26 say to do when the mind wanders? *Bring it back.*
5. Name two steps of one attentive round. *E.g. fixed time and place; the Pancha-tattva mantra first; clear pronunciation; hearing each mantra; the mood of a child calling its mother.*
6. How many rounds should I keep daily? *The number fixed with my facilitator, kept every day for a month, then raised.*

**M.2 Activities** (engagement block; pick two or three)

*Option A: Live japa (30 min)*
1. 5 min: stand or sit, set a timer for one round; read the N checklist aloud.
2. 15 min: everyone chants one to two rounds softly, following the checklist, no phones.
3. 5 min: in pairs, each says what helped them hear and what pulled the mind away.
4. 5 min: volunteers share one thing they will keep. Thank each.

*Option B: My round card (25 min)*
1. 5 min: each takes the N checklist and circles three steps they already do and one they will add.
2. 10 min: pairs agree a time and place for next week's first round and say it aloud.
3. 10 min: pairs write the number they will keep every day for a month and tell their facilitator or a friend.

*Option C: The metro chanter (20 min)*
1. 2 min: read the Sumit scenario (H, invented).
2. 10 min: in threes, one plays Sumit, one a friendly coach, one an observer; the coach suggests three small changes that fit his day (the 20.18 point: any time and place is fine; add one fixed round).
3. 8 min: observers report what was kind and practical; praise it.

**M.3 Reflection**
- **Reflection:** Which step of the method do I already do well, and which have I never been shown?
- **Application (private, optional):** The number I will keep every day this month, and the one step of the method I will add.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

The scriptures say that in this age there is no other way to self-realisation than chanting the holy name (CC Adi 17.21), that the name is Krsna Himself present as sound, so chanting is direct association with Him (CC Adi 17.22; SB 1.1.14), and that this is the one good quality of Kali-yuga, the present age of quarrel (SB 12.3.51). Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu's first Siksastaka verse says chanting cleanses the mirror of the heart (CC Antya 20.12), so the first thing you may notice is the dust. The name works even before you understand it, like medicine (SB 6.2.19). You need no initiation to begin: "One does not have to undergo initiation… One simply has to vibrate the holy name with his lips" (CC Madhya 15.108), and any time or place is allowed (CC Antya 20.18).

There are "no hard and fast rules" (CC Antya 20.16), yet we chant "after the ways of the great authorities" (SB 2.1.11), that is, by a method that helps us hear: a fixed time and place, the Pancha-tattva mantra first (the mantra honouring Sri Caitanya and His associates; CC Adi 7.4 purport), clear pronunciation, attentive hearing, a steady pace, the mind brought back each time it leaves (BG 6.26), and the mood of a child calling its mother, humbler than the grass (CC Antya 20.21). If you feel nothing at first, the material senses cannot yet appreciate the name; by chanting and taking prasadam understanding comes gradually (CC Madhya 17.136). A missed day is not mourned; restart the next morning (BG 8.5–7).

There are ten offences to the name, to be learnt later; the first is to blaspheme devotees who spread the name (CC Adi 8.24 purport), so for now never criticise devotees. Fix with your facilitator a number of rounds you will keep every day, "nicely" (NOI 8) rather than fast, and increase from there.

## S2. Key ideas

- No other way in this age (CC Adi 17.21); the name is Krsna as sound (CC Adi 17.22; SB 1.1.14).
- Chanting cleans the mirror of the heart; the first wipe shows the dust (CC Antya 20.12).
- It works before you understand it, like medicine (SB 6.2.19); attention makes it work faster.
- No hard and fast rules (CC Antya 20.16) *and* after the way we have been taught (SB 2.1.11): the method helps you, not the name.
- The mind leaves; bring it back to the sound and count the returns (BG 6.26).
- Mood: a child calling its mother; lower than the grass (CC Antya 20.21).
- There are ten offences (CC Adi 8.24 purport); the first is criticising devotees. Learn the list at DPS.
- Chant a number you keep every day; increase from stability (BG 9.14).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **CC Adi 17.21:** "In this Age of Kali there is no other means, no other means, no other means for self-realization than chanting the holy name…" *For me:* this is the practice for my age.
- **CC Antya 20.12:** chanting "can cleanse the mirror of the heart". *For me:* seeing my restlessness is the cleaning, not the failure.
- **SB 6.2.19:** the medicine acts "even without his knowledge because its potency does not depend on the patient’s understanding". *For me:* a wandering round is not a wasted round.
- **CC Antya 20.21:** "One who thinks himself lower than the grass, who is more tolerant than a tree… can very easily always chant the holy name of the Lord." *For me:* humility is part of the method.

## S4. Words to know

- **Japa:** chanting the mantra softly on beads; **a round** is 108 mantras.
- **Pancha-tattva mantra:** *śrī-kṛṣṇa-caitanya prabhu-nityānanda śrī-advaita gadādhara śrīvāsādi-gaura-bhakta-vṛnda*, chanted before japa (CC Adi 7.4 purport; CC Adi 8.24 purport recommends its first names).
- **Śikṣāṣṭaka:** Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu's eight verses on the holy name (CC Antya 20).
- **Nāmāparādha:** an offence against the holy name; there are ten (CC Adi 8.24 purport).
- **Sadhana card:** your weekly record of rounds, shared with your facilitator.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **I'm not initiated. Does my chanting count?** Yes. "One does not have to undergo initiation… One simply has to vibrate the holy name with his lips" (CC Madhya 15.108).
2. **My mind wanders the whole round. Is it wasted?** No. The name works like medicine, even without understanding (SB 6.2.19). Bring the mind back (BG 6.26); attention makes it work faster.
3. **If there are no hard and fast rules, why a method?** Both are scripture: "no hard and fast rules" (CC Antya 20.16) and chant "after the ways of the great authorities" (SB 2.1.11). The method helps you hear; it does not switch the name on.
4. **I can only chant on the metro.** "Regardless of time or place… attains all perfection" (CC Antya 20.18). Fine. Try to add one quiet round at home at a fixed time.
5. **I feel nothing.** Material senses cannot yet appreciate the name; by chanting and taking prasadam the tongue is purified and understanding comes gradually (CC Madhya 17.136). Taste follows practice.
6. **How many rounds?** The VL3 standard is four a day, and the aim is to increase. Fix with your facilitator a number you will keep *every* day for a month, then raise it. "Nicely" (NOI 8) matters more than fast.
7. **Does a missed day matter?** Daily practice builds remembrance (BG 8.5–7). Restart the next morning; don't mourn.

3. Why does chanting work even when you don't understand it (SB 6.2.19)?

6. What is the first of the ten offences (CC Adi 8.24 purport), and which names does the purport recommend chanting before japa?

**Answers:** 1. (c) (CC Adi 17.21). 2. It cleanses the mirror of the heart (CC Antya 20.12). 3. Its potency "does not depend on the patient’s understanding",
like medicine (SB 6.2.19). 4. Chant "after the ways of the great authorities": a received method (SB 2.1.11). 5. Withdraw it and bring it back (BG 6.26). 6. To
blaspheme devotees who spread the holy name; *śrī-kṛṣṇa-caitanya prabhu-nityānanda*, the opening of the Pancha-tattva mantra (CC Adi 8.24
purport; the full mantra: CC Adi 7.4 purport). 7. False: "One does not have to undergo initiation" (CC Madhya 15.108).
