# Art of living

> **VL1 · 45–60 min · review: sources checked, awaiting senior review.** A relevance talk that sums up the VL1 series: "making a living" versus
> "making a life". Scripture light but exact: five Gita verses in the talk, the rest for the preacher.

## A. Topic overview

| | |
|---|---|
| Batch / level | VL1 (Vedic Level 1), a stand-alone talk; works well as the closing talk of a VL1 cycle |
| Suggested duration | 45–60 min |
| Preparation time | About 90 min, the first time |
| Difficulty | Introductory |
| Prerequisites | None; richer if VL1-10 to VL1-12 have been heard |
| Audience | Students and young professionals who have been trained to make a living and never asked what a life is for; many with no settled faith |
| Teaching objective | Show that "living well" is an art with learnable principles, name three common misconceptions about life that spoil it, and offer the Gita's view and a simple daily practice (IYF's "ABCD": association, books, chanting, diet) as something to try, not to swallow |

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Everyone in the room is already an apprentice in this art; nobody has been taught it. IYF's slides for the
topic ("Art of living" and a second deck, "Art of Re-living") take a two-part approach: first, quotations
and three **misconceptions about life** (I am only matter; life ends with death; life is a bed of roses),
each with the behaviour it produces and the correction the scriptures give; second, the positive
programme, an acronym **RESPECT** (Reasoning, Education, Service, Patience, Example, Consultation, Trust)
and the daily **ABCD** (Association, Books, Chanting, Diet/prasadam). The second deck, "Art of Re-living",
is almost entirely management-style self-help (responsibility, comfort zone, attitude, values, protective
shields, humility) and is used here only for its one scriptural touch, "response-ability" versus the four
animal activities.

**Builds on vl1-09 to vl1-12 (closing talk of the cycle):** recap in a sentence each (vl1-09 self-management, vl1-10 work as offering, vl1-11 habits of thought, vl1-12 attention); do not repeat their arguments, examples or stories. BG 10.25 (chanting) appears in vl1-12 for attention; here it is only one letter of ABCD. This page's own angle is the three misconceptions and a daily practice.

At VL1 the aim is **attraction through a wider horizon**: the listener should leave feeling that the
question "what is a life for?" is answerable, and that this tradition answers it with care rather than
slogans.

How the subject grows at other levels:

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **VL1** (here) | Three misconceptions and their corrections (BG 2.13, 2.20; BG 8.15–16); living by principles (RESPECT); a daily practice to try (ABCD) |
| VL2 | The arguments: the self is not matter (VL2-01); why this world is "temporary" and "full of miseries" (BG 8.15) and what that does and does not mean |
| VL3 | ABCD as *sadhana* (regular spiritual practice): association (BG 10.9), hearing and reading, attentive chanting, prasadam (food offered to the Lord, BG 3.13) with guidance |
| SPS | Keeping the daily practice steady through the first years |
| DPS | Living by principles while leading others |
| BSS | ABCD as the frame of a brahmacari's day |

**Not done here:**
- Arguing for the soul. One verse (BG 2.13) is offered as "a way of seeing"; the argument is VL2's.
- Hell, heaven and planetary systems. BG 8.16 is used only for its first clause, that every material
  position is temporary.
- Treating the Gita's "full of miseries" (BG 8.15) as pessimism. The point is realism about what the world
  can and cannot give, not gloom (F.5).
- The "Paradox of our time" piece from IYF's slides (see J).

## C. Central message

We are taught to make a living and never taught to make a life. A human life can do what an animal's cannot:
ask what I am, where I am, and what lasts. The Gita calls understanding the body and its knower "knowledge"
(BG 13.3; BG 13.8–12). Three
misconceptions spoil the art: that I am only this body, that life ends at death, and that this world will
finally satisfy me. Correct those and a daily practice follows.

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. Name the three misconceptions about life in IYF's list, the behaviour each produces, and the Gita's
   correction for each (BG 2.13, 2.20; BG 8.15–16).
2. Explain in plain words why the Gita calls knowing "this body and its knower" knowledge (BG 13.3), and
   name three of the qualities BG 13.8–12 lists as knowledge (humility first).
3. Describe the seven principles of RESPECT and connect three of them to a verse (reasoning: BG 13.8–12;
   consultation: BG 4.34; example: BG 3.21).
4. Try one element of ABCD for a week and say what they noticed.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** five references carry the talk (BG 2.13, BG 8.15, BG 13.3, BG 13.8–12, BG 4.34). The rest prepare
you for questions and for ABCD.

**BG 2.13**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/13/
> "As the embodied soul continuously passes, in this body, from boyhood to youth to old age, the soul similarly passes into another body at death. A sober person is not bewildered by such a change."
- **Context:** Krsna's first teaching to the grieving Arjuna.
- **Teaching:** The "I" continues through every change of the body.
- **Purport point:** "the same spirit soul is there and does not undergo any change"; one with this
  knowledge "is called a dhīra, or a most sober man."
- **Application (preacher's):** Misconception 1 ("I am only matter") is answered first by an observation
  everyone can make: the person in their childhood photos is "me" though every cell has changed.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 2.20**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/20/
> "For the soul there is neither birth nor death at any time… He is unborn, eternal, ever-existing and primeval. He is not slain when the body is slain."
- **Purport point:** The body "is subject to six kinds of transformations": birth, remaining, growth,
  producing effects, dwindling, vanishing. "The soul, however, does not go through such changes." IYF's
  slide on the six changes of every living body comes from here.
- **Application (preacher's):** Misconception 2 ("life ends with death"). At VL1 present it as the Gita's
  claim and invite reflection, not assent.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 8.15**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/8/15/
> "After attaining Me, the great souls, who are yogīs in devotion, never return to this temporary world, which is full of miseries, because they have attained the highest perfection."
- **Teaching:** The Gita's two adjectives for this world: temporary, full of miseries.
- **Purport point:** "this temporary material world is full of the miseries of birth, old age, disease
  and death".
- **Application (preacher's):** Misconception 3 ("life is a bed of roses"). The correction is not gloom; it
  is to stop demanding from the world what it cannot give, and to look for what can. IYF's own phrase:
  "make the best use of a bad bargain".
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 8.16**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/8/16/
> "From the highest planet in the material world down to the lowest, all are places of misery wherein repeated birth and death take place…"
- **Application (preacher's):** Only the first clause at VL1: no material position, however high, is
  permanent. Do not open the subject of planets.
- **Use:** background.

**BG 13.3**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/13/3/
> "O scion of Bharata, you should understand that I am also the knower in all bodies, and to understand this body and its knower is called knowledge. That is My opinion."
- **Teaching:** Knowledge, in the Gita's sense, is knowing the body, the knower in it, and the supreme
  knower.
- **Application (preacher's):** IYF's "Education" principle: "knowledge about matter, spirit and the
  controller of both". Degrees teach the first; the art of living needs all three.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 13.8-12**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/13/8-12/
> "Humility; pridelessness; nonviolence; tolerance; simplicity; approaching a bona fide spiritual master; cleanliness; steadiness; self-control… the perception of the evil of birth, death, old age and disease… even-mindedness amid pleasant and unpleasant events… and philosophical search for the Absolute Truth – all these I declare to be knowledge…"
- **Teaching:** Knowledge is a way of living, listed as qualities, and it begins with humility.
- **Purport point:** "the process of knowledge terminates in unalloyed devotional service to the Lord"
  (the purport's reading of 13.11).
- **Application (preacher's):** The Gita's own "art of living" list. It covers IYF's Reasoning
  ("perception of the evil of birth, death, old age and disease"; "philosophical search"), Consultation
  ("approaching a bona fide spiritual master") and the humility that closes the "Art of Re-living" deck.
  Read four or five items aloud; do not lecture on all twenty.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 4.34**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/4/34/
> "Just try to learn the truth by approaching a spiritual master. Inquire from him submissively and render service unto him. The self-realized souls can impart knowledge unto you because they have seen the truth."
- **Purport point:** "Inquiries and submission constitute the proper combination for spiritual
  understanding."
- **Application (preacher's):** IYF's "Consultation": no art is learnt alone. At VL1, say "a teacher who
  has walked the road", not "guru".
- **Use:** in the talk (RESPECT).

**For RESPECT and ABCD (background):**
- **BG 3.21** (Verified): "Whatever action a great man performs, common men follow. And whatever standards
  he sets by exemplary acts, all the world pursues." Example: a role model is needed to excel in any art.
- **BG 4.39** (Verified): "A faithful man who is dedicated to transcendental knowledge and who subdues his
  senses is eligible to achieve such knowledge, and having achieved it he quickly attains the supreme
  spiritual peace." Trust: IYF's point that every science begins with a working trust in the teacher and
  the method. Pitch it as "reasonable trust", not blind faith.
- **BG 5.25** (Verified): "…who are always busy working for the welfare of all living beings… achieve
  liberation in the Supreme." Service: to God, to saintly persons, to all living beings (IYF's three).
- **BG 6.45** (Verified): "…achieving perfection after many, many births of practice, he attains the
  supreme goal." Patience: "no art is learnt overnight" (IYF).
- **BG 10.9** (Verified): "The thoughts of My pure devotees dwell in Me… they derive great satisfaction and
  bliss from always enlightening one another and conversing about Me." A: association.
- **BG 10.25** (Verified): "Of sacrifices I am the chanting of the holy names [japa]". C: chanting.
- **BG 3.13** (Verified): "The devotees of the Lord are released from all kinds of sins because they eat
  food which is offered first for sacrifice. Others, who prepare food for personal sense enjoyment, verily
  eat only sin." D: diet/prasadam. At VL1, present offered food as a practice to taste, not as a judgement
  on anyone's dinner.
- **BG 17.8** (Verified): "Foods dear to those in the mode of goodness increase the duration of life,
  purify one's existence and give strength, health, happiness and satisfaction. Such foods are juicy,
  fatty, wholesome, and pleasing to the heart." D: what kind of diet.
- **BG 2.40** (Verified): "In this endeavor there is no loss or diminution, and a little advancement on
  this path can protect one from the most dangerous type of fear." For "what if I start and stop?"
  Purport: "One percent done in Kṛṣṇa consciousness bears permanent results".
- **SB 1.3.24** (Verified): the Lord "will appear as Lord Buddha, the son of Añjanā, in the province of
  Gayā". IYF's slides use "the life of Gautam Buddha" as the example of someone who saw the facts of life
  and did not despair. The Bhagavatam names Lord Buddha as an incarnation; the biographical details
  (the palace, the sights of old age, disease and death) are from Buddhist tradition, not from BBT books.
  Tell them as "the traditional account" if you use them.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** Living is an art, and arts have principles. The Gita's first principle is to know
   what I am: not the body alone but the knower in it (BG 13.3; BG 2.13). The second is to see the world as
   it is: temporary and unable to give lasting satisfaction (BG 8.15–16). The third is that knowledge is a
   way of living, beginning with humility and including a teacher, steadiness, and the search for the
   Absolute Truth (BG 13.8–12). IYF's RESPECT and ABCD are practical arrangements of these principles.
2. **Why it matters.** Three misconceptions quietly run most lives (IYF's analysis):
   - *I am only matter* → people become things to use; ethics loses its ground.
   - *Life ends at death* → "eat, drink, be merry"; indifference to good and bad.
   - *Life is a bed of roses* → disillusionment when reality arrives, sometimes bitterness.

   Each misconception has a behaviour attached. Correct the belief and the behaviour has a chance.
3. **Connections.** VL1-10 (work), VL1-11 (the mind) and VL1-12 (attention) are three rooms of this house.
   The argument for the self is VL2-01; ABCD becomes sadhana at VL3.
4. **Consequences.** A person who holds the three corrections treats others as persons, takes
   responsibility beyond this lifetime without fear, and stops expecting the world to be what it is not.
   The practical mark is steadiness: "even-mindedness amid pleasant and unpleasant events" (BG 13.8–12).
5. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - **"Full of miseries" as pessimism.** The Gita does not say life is worthless; it says this *world*
     cannot be the final home (BG 8.15). The right response is to use it well: "make the best use of a bad
     bargain" (IYF).
   - **Misconception 1 as contempt for the body.** BG 13.3 says knowledge includes knowing the body; the
     body is the field, to be cared for, not despised.
   - **RESPECT as a moral checklist.** It is a description of how any art is learnt (reasoning, education,
     a teacher, patience, an example, consultation, trust), applied to life.
   - **ABCD as conversion.** At VL1 it is an experiment: good company, good reading, a few minutes of
     chanting, a meal offered. Each is testable in a week.
6. **Pitch for VL1.** Use "the knower", "temporary", "an art with principles", "a life, not only a
   living". *Prasadam* can be said once and translated as "food offered first". No appeal to authority:
   "Here is how the Gita diagnoses the three mistakes. See if you recognise any of them."

**Shorter (45 min):** skip the activity; or teach the three misconceptions and ABCD; name RESPECT on a slide without expounding it.

**Alternative structure (gallery walk):** three stations, one per misconception, each with the behaviour it

three corrections. Close with ABCD as the practice that keeps the corrections alive. Works well for a group
that has heard the earlier VL1 talks and wants to think rather than listen.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet, say your name, and say this is the closing talk of the series: putting the pieces together.
2. Ask the two hands-up questions below (living / life); no one speaks.
3. Take two answers to H.2 question 1 and thank each by name.
4. Write "Living" and "Life" on the board; you return to them at minute 5.
5. Give the transition line below.

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the room's own life)
1. What is one thing someone taught you that has shaped your life, not only your income?
2. If you had a free year, what would you want to understand?

- **Opening questions:** "Hands up if anyone has ever taught you how to make a living." Most hands.
  "Hands up if anyone has ever taught you how to make a life." Few. "That gap is tonight's subject."
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** Vikram, 27, has everything on the checklist: the job, the flat, the
  bike, the Sunday brunch. *(Adapt: a farmer's son with a good harvest and a new tractor, or a village teacher with a steady post; the question "is this it?" is universal.)* On a Tuesday night he catches himself asking, "Is this it?" He feels guilty for
  asking. Should he?
- **Transition:** "In this tradition, his question is the first sign of a human being waking up. Animals
  eat, sleep, mate and defend, and so do we. Only a human asks what it is for."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** They are at the age when the checklist is being written. Whether a
  life or only a living comes out of it depends on principles they have not yet been shown.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **A human life can ask what it is for.** BG 13.3. *Plain:* eating, sleeping, mating and defending are
   shared with every animal (IYF); the capacity to ask "who is the knower in this body?" is not. The Gita
   calls understanding the body and its knower knowledge. *Example:* Vikram's Tuesday question. *Transition:* three wrong answers to it run
   most lives.
2. **Misconception 1: I am only matter.** BG 2.13. *Plain:* if I am a bag of chemicals, so are you, and
   persons become things. The correction: the "I" that passes from childhood to youth to age is one
   through all of it. *Example:* the childhood photograph. *Transition:* if the self continues through the
   body's changes, what about the last change?
3. **Misconception 2: life ends at death.** BG 2.20. *Plain:* "this is the last one" produces "eat, drink,
   be merry" and indifference to good and bad (IYF). The Gita's claim: the body passes through six changes;
   the self through none. *Offer it; do not demand it.* *Transition:* even those who accept this often hold
   the third misconception.
4. **Misconception 3: life is a bed of roses.** BG 8.15. *Plain:* the world is "temporary" and "full of
   miseries": birth, old age, disease, death. Those who never face this are disillusioned when it arrives;
   those who face it with knowledge "make the best use of a bad bargain" (IYF). *Example (traditional
   account, not from BBT books):* Gautama Buddha leaving the palace after seeing old age, disease and
   death; the Bhagavatam names Lord Buddha as an incarnation (SB 1.3.24). *Transition:* corrections need
   principles to live by.
5. **Living by principles: RESPECT.** BG 13.8–12; BG 4.34; BG 3.21. *Plain:* any art is learnt by reasoning
   about it, education in it, serving it, patience, a model to follow, consultation with those ahead, and
   reasonable trust in the method. The Gita's own list of knowledge begins with humility and includes
   approaching a teacher and the search for truth. *Example:* how anyone learnt music or a sport.
   *Transition:* principles need a daily practice.
6. **A practice to try: ABCD.** BG 10.9; BG 10.25; BG 3.13; and books (BG 4.34 for learning from those who
   have seen). *Plain:* Association (spend time with people who ask the question), Books (read something
   that answers it), Chanting (a few minutes of a mantra daily; many use Hare Krsna), Diet (food offered
   first, *prasadam*; try it at a programme). "In this endeavor there is no loss" (BG 2.40): a small start
   is not wasted. *Transition:* to the takeaway.

## J. Stories and illustrations

- **Gautama Buddha and the four sights** (traditional Buddhist account; IYF's example for misconception 3).
  The Bhagavatam names Lord Buddha as an incarnation who appeared in Gaya (SB 1.3.24). The palace and the
  four sights are not in BBT books; present them as "the traditional account" and keep to the one point:
  seeing old age, disease and death did not make him despair; it made him search.
- **The six changes of the body** (BG 2.20 purport): birth, remaining, growth, by-products, dwindling,
  vanishing. The soul goes through none. *Connection:* misconceptions 1 and 2.
- **The red balloon** (IYF's illustration, "Art of Re-living"): it is not the colour of the balloon that
  makes it rise but what is inside. *Connection:* "container versus content" (IYF); character over
  appearance.
- **Learning any art** (contemporary, generic): the cricketer's coach, the musician's riyaz, the first
  thousand bad sketches. *Connection:* RESPECT describes what they already did for a skill.
- **Contemporary (invented):** Vikram's Tuesday (H).
- **Not used:** IYF's slides include the widely circulated piece "The Paradox of Our Time" ("taller
  buildings, shorter tempers…"). It has been misattributed to George Carlin; the confirmed author is a
  1995 American pastor whose later record makes the attribution unsuitable for an IYF talk. Make the same
  point in your own words. The many quotations in both decks (Volkswagen, Shaw, Wiesel and unattributed
  one-liners) were not verified for this page; use at most one or two, as sayings, not as sources.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"Isn't 'the world is full of miseries' just pessimism? I'm happy."**
   - Good. The Gita is not against happiness; it is against expecting *this* world to supply the permanent
     kind. BG 8.15's adjectives are "temporary" and "full of miseries"; the purport names the miseries as
     birth, old age, disease and death.
     Happiness that knows its own season (VL1-06) is steadier than happiness that expects to last.
2. **"Why should I believe I'm not my body?"**
   - At VL1, no one asks you to believe it. BG 2.13 offers an observation to reflect on: the "I" in your
     childhood photos is still you though every cell has changed. The full discussion is VL2-01,
     "Reincarnation: fact or fiction".
3. **"If the body is temporary, why care for it?"**
   - BG 13.3 says knowledge is to understand "this body and its knower": both. The body is the field in
     which the art is practised; a musician looks after the instrument. Diet (BG 17.8) and regulation are
     part of the art, not opposed to it.
4. **"I'm not religious. Can I do ABCD without chanting?"**
   - Try the three you can and leave the fourth for later; "in this endeavor there is no loss" (BG 2.40).
     Many begin with association and books. Be honest that the tradition considers chanting the centre
     (in BG 10.25 Krsna says, "Of sacrifices I am the chanting of the holy names"), and leave the invitation open.
5. **"RESPECT says 'trust'. Isn't that blind faith?"**
   - BG 4.39 pairs faith with dedication to knowledge and sense control; BG 4.34 pairs approaching a
     teacher with *inquiry*. The trust asked for is the working trust any student gives a teacher and a
     method until results appear, not a ban on questions.
6. **"Buddha isn't in your scriptures, is he?"**
   - He is: SB 1.3.24 names Lord Buddha among the incarnations. The life story told tonight is the
     traditional Buddhist account, used for one point. Vaisnava and Buddhist readings of his teaching
     differ; that is a conversation for another evening.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The checklist.** Vikram (H) has decided his Tuesday question was weakness. He has signed up for
a second job to "stay busy" and tells friends that thinking too much is the problem.
- **Dilemma:** Is his diagnosis right?
- **Principles:** BG 13.3 (to know the body and its knower is knowledge); BG 8.15 (the world cannot supply
  what he is asking for); BG 13.8–12 ("philosophical search for the Absolute Truth" is knowledge, not
  weakness).
- **Questions:**
  - Which misconception is Vikram acting on?
  - What does "staying busy" do to the question?
  - Which one element of RESPECT would help him most this month?
- **Suggested analysis:** Misconception 3, with a dose of 1. Busyness postpones the question; the Gita
  treats the question as the beginning of knowledge. Consultation (BG 4.34), someone ahead on the road, is
  the element he lacks.
- **Takeaway:** The question "is this it?" is not a malfunction; it is the human faculty working.

**Case 2: The pact.** Three friends make a pact at 22: "We'll enjoy our twenties completely, then get
serious at 30." One is now 29, exhausted, and does not recognise himself.
- **Dilemma:** What was wrong with the pact?
- **Principles:** BG 2.20 (the self is not the body; "eat, drink, be merry" rests on misconception 2);
  BG 6.45 (the art takes patient practice; it cannot be scheduled for later); BG 2.40 (a small start now is
  never lost).
- **Questions:**
  - Which misconception does "enjoy now, get serious later" assume?
  - What does "no loss in this endeavour" (BG 2.40) mean for someone who feels he wasted seven years?
  - What is the smallest ABCD step he could take this week?
- **Suggested analysis:** Misconception 2 in disguise: it treats the self as a body with a timetable. The
  Gita's answer to the "wasted years" fear is BG 2.40: start now, nothing is lost. One book and one
  evening of good association is a real beginning.
- **Takeaway:** The art of living is not a phase; it starts whenever it starts.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first; praise every try)
1. What does the talk say we are taught and not taught? *To make a living, not a life.*
2. What does BG 13.3 call knowledge? *Understanding this body and its knower.*
3. Which quality comes first in BG 13.8–12? *Humility.*
4. Name the three misconceptions and a verse for each. *Only matter (BG 2.13); life ends at death (BG 2.20); the world will satisfy (BG 8.15).*
5. What do the letters of ABCD stand for? *Association, Books, Chanting, Diet (prasadam).*

**M.2 Activities (choose one; about 10 minutes)**
- **Option A, "Gallery walk" (10 min).** 1. Three posters, one per misconception, each with a blank "what would correct this?". 2. 5 min: small groups rotate and write. 3. 3 min: read the answers aloud and link to the three verses. 4. 2 min: thank every group.
- **Option B, "My ABCD week" (8 min).** 1. Each writes one small action for A, B, C, D. 2. 3 min: pairs swap and say which is easiest. 3. 3 min: two volunteers share (optional). 4. Close with BG 2.40: no loss in a small start.

**M.3 Reflection**
- **Reflection:** Which of the three misconceptions has quietly shaped a decision of yours? Which letter of
  RESPECT did you rely on most when you learnt a skill you are proud of?
- **Application (private, optional):** Choose one letter of ABCD for a week: an evening in good company, a

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Most of us were taught how to make a living and never how to make a life. A human life can do something an
animal's cannot: ask who the knower in this body is. The Gita calls understanding the body and its knower
knowledge (BG 13.3).
Three misconceptions quietly spoil the art of living: that I am only matter, that life ends at death, and
that this world will finally satisfy me. The Gita corrects each: the "I" passes through every change of the
body (BG 2.13) and has no birth or death (BG 2.20); this world is "temporary" and "full of miseries"
(BG 8.15), so it cannot be the final home, and it should be used well. Living by principles (IYF's RESPECT)
and a simple daily practice (ABCD: association, books, chanting, diet) keep the corrections alive.

Which quality comes first in the Gita's list of knowledge? Humility (BG 13.8–12). We then looked at the daily practice ABCD: good association, good books, chanting and prasadam (food offered to the Lord). The Gita says that "in this endeavor there is no loss" (BG 2.40), so a small start counts. Choose one letter this week and try it.

## S2. Key ideas

- A human life can ask what it is for; the Gita calls understanding the body and its knower knowledge (BG 13.3).
- Three misconceptions: I am only matter; life ends at death; life is a bed of roses. Each produces a way
  of behaving.
- The corrections: the knower continues through the body's changes (BG 2.13; BG 2.20); the world is
  temporary and cannot satisfy (BG 8.15).
- Knowledge is a way of living, and it begins with humility (BG 13.8–12).
- Any art is learnt by principles and daily practice: RESPECT and ABCD.

## S3. Verses to remember

- **BG 13.3:** "to understand this body and its knower is called knowledge." *For me:* my degree taught me
  the body's world; the art of living asks about the knower.
- **BG 2.13:** the self passes "from boyhood to youth to old age" and on. *For me:* the "I" in my childhood
  photos is still me.
- **BG 8.15:** this world is "temporary" and "full of miseries". *For me:* use it well; do not ask it for
  what it cannot give.

## S4. Words to know

- **The knower:** the self within the body, which experiences the body's changes without being them
  (BG 13.3; BG 2.13).
- **RESPECT:** IYF's seven principles for learning the art of living: Reasoning, Education, Service,
  Patience, Example, Consultation, Trust.
- **ABCD:** a daily practice: Association, Books, Chanting, Diet (food offered first, *prasadam*).
- **Prasadam:** food offered before eating (BG 3.13).

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Isn't "the world is full of miseries" pessimism?**
   No. The Gita is not against happiness; it is against expecting *this* world to supply the lasting kind.
   BG 8.15 calls the world "temporary" and "full of miseries" (birth, old age, disease and death), so it
   cannot be the final home; it can still be used well.
2. **Why should I believe I'm not my body?**
   Nobody asks you to believe it tonight. BG 2.13 offers something to notice: the "I" has stayed the same
   through every change of your body. VL2 discusses the arguments.
3. **If the body is temporary, why look after it?**
   BG 13.3 says knowledge is to understand "this body and its knower": both. A musician looks after the
   instrument.
4. **I'm not religious. Can I try ABCD without the chanting?**
   Start with the parts you can; "in this endeavor there is no loss" (BG 2.40). The tradition does consider
   chanting the centre ("Of sacrifices I am the chanting of the holy names", BG 10.25), and the invitation
   stays open.
5. **Is "trust" in RESPECT blind faith?**
   The Gita pairs approaching a teacher with inquiry (BG 4.34). The trust asked for is the working trust a
   student gives any teacher and method until results appear.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. BG 13.3 says knowledge is: (a) knowing many facts, (b) understanding this body and its knower, (c) a
   degree.
2. Match each misconception to its correction: "I am only matter"; "life ends at death"; "life is a bed of
   roses" — BG 2.20; BG 8.15; BG 2.13.
3. True or false: the first quality in the Gita's list of knowledge (BG 13.8–12) is intelligence.
4. What do the four letters of ABCD stand for?
5. In your own words: what is the difference between "making a living" and "making a life"?

**Answers:** 1. (b) (BG 13.3). 2. Matter → BG 2.13; death → BG 2.20; bed of roses → BG 8.15. 3. False: it is
humility (BG 13.8–12). 4. Association, Books, Chanting, Diet (prasadam). 5. Any honest answer; the talk's
version: a living meets the four needs shared with animals, a life asks who the knower is and what lasts
(BG 13.3).
