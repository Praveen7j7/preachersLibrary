# Haridas Thakur and the prostitute

> Status line: BSS · 120 min · drafted · SENSITIVE (temptation, celibacy; a woman in a vulnerable profession). Told as CC Antya 3 tells it. Reviewed by the head after the build. Builds on bss-11.

|---|---|

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level
After bss-11 shows that enjoyment cannot satisfy, this page shows what does: unbroken absorption in the holy name.
The page is not a lesson in testing oneself; it is a lesson in what the name does.

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| VL1 | Haridasa's tolerance and the power of the name (vl1-44) |
| VL2 | The story as an example of steadiness |
| VL3 | The offences to avoid and the strength of chanting (vl3-04) |
| SPS | Haridasa as namacarya (sps-13) |
| DPS | The Antya-lila setting (dps-06, dps-15) |
| BSS | This page: the story in detail, what protected him, what a brahmacari learns, and what he does not copy |

Not repeated here: the verse of SB 9.19.17 and the arguments of bss-11. Falldown and its remedies: bss-39, bss-40.

## C. Central message
Haridasa Thakura stayed absorbed in the holy name; by hearing it the woman sent to disturb him was
transformed. A brahmacari learns that the name is the protection; Srila Prabhupada's purport (3.122): "The prostitute came to pollute Haridāsa Ṭhākura, but he took it as his duty to deliver the prostitute."

## D. Learning outcomes
Participants can:
1. Retell the story in order, naming each night.
2. Say what Haridasa was doing at each stage and what it did for the woman.
3. Explain why this story is not a test to copy.
4. Describe what Haridasa told her to do (CC Antya 3.136-137) and why that is mercy.
5. Choose one daily practice that keeps the name at the centre.

## E. Scriptural foundation
No purport text was found by the checker for most of these verses; the exceptions are marked. Where there is no purport, the interpretation is labelled as the preacher's.

**CC Antya 3.100**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/3/100/
> "There he planted a tulasī plant, and in front of the tulasī he would chant the holy name of the Lord 300,000 times daily."
- **Context:** Haridasa has left home and stays in the forest of Benapola.
- **Teaching:** He lived in a cottage with a tulasi plant and chanted day and night.
- **Purport point:** The purport locates Benapola near Jessore (now in Bangladesh) and calls Haridasa the acarya of chanting.
- **Application (preacher's):** Preacher's arithmetic: if each name of the mahamantra is counted separately (16 names to a mantra), 300,000 names equal 18,750 mantras, about 174 rounds of 108. Requires source review for how the tradition counts. Say only "an extraordinary amount".
- **Use:** in the talk.

**CC Antya 3.101**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/3/101/
> "For his bodily maintenance he would go to a brāhmaṇa’s house and beg some food."
- **Use:** background: simple means and respect from neighbours.

**CC Antya 3.102**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/3/102/

## F. Philosophical explanation
**Principle.** The holy name is not different from the Lord and purifies whoever hears it. **Why it matters.** The brahmacari's real protection is not only avoidance, it is absorption in the name; what he cannot manage by strength he receives through chanting. **Connections.** bss-11 explained why desire cannot be fed to rest; here the name satisfies the soul. bss-07: association with a pure devotee is the door of liberation (SB 5.5.2); this woman is the example. **Consequences.** A brahmacari who understands this chants carefully and attentively; one who misreads the story as a test may endanger himself and others. **Misunderstandings.** (1) That the story invites a brahmacari to place himself in such a situation: the text portrays a rare, great devotee acting for her deliverance (CC Antya 3.134). (2) That she is the villain: the planner is Ramacandra Khan, and she becomes a devotee. (3) That Haridasa did something by willpower: the text shows him chanting. **Pitch.** Reverent and hopeful; avoid any detail beyond the verses.

## H. Opening and engagement
**H.1 How to start.** Bow to the picture or name of Haridasa Thakura (or sing his name). Say: "Today's story is told in the Caitanya-caritamrta by Krishnadasa Kaviraja. It has a hard setting and a happy end. We will keep to what the text says."
**H.2 Opening questions.** (1) "What is the most you have ever focused on one task for a long time?" (2) "What would make a person able to stay steady when others try to distract him?" (3) "If someone is sent to trouble you, how could you respond so that she or he is helped?"

## I. Core teaching points
**I.1 The three nights (CC Antya 3.109-3.130).** The first night she comes "after dressing herself most attractively" (3.109), sits on the threshold "exposing part of her body to his view" (3.111) and asks for union (3.113); Haridasa asks her to sit and hear the name while he completes his chanting (3.114-115). Each night she comes, and each night the night ends while he is still chanting. She hears, and begins to say "Hari, Hari" herself (3.122, 3.128). End: it is the sound that is working.
**I.2 The change (3.130-3.131).** The text says that by his association her mind changed; she fell at his feet, and told the truth. Explain: the name purifies the hearer; honesty followed. End: she is "now purified", in the text's words.
**I.3 A new life (3.135-3.140).** She asked for a guru; she was told to give her possessions to the brahmanas, stay in the room, chant, and serve tulasi. She did. Explain: this is mercy, a way of life offered to someone who asked. It is also the answer to the question "what can a person do after a hard past": start chanting.
**I.4 What a brahmacari takes, and what he does not copy.** Takes: the name is the protection; hearing is powerful; the purport's reading (3.122): "he took it as his duty to deliver the prostitute"; sincerity of rounds (the vow, not the number). Does not copy: placing himself in a similar situation. The BIKC and common sense advise guarding the senses and association (summary; see O). Haridasa's conduct belongs to a maha-bhagavata acting for her sake (3.134); the student keeps his rounds, his seniors' counsel and his community.

## J. Stories and illustrations
**J.1 Haridasa Thakura and the prostitute (CC Antya 3; key verse 3.104).** In the forest of Benapola Haridasa chanted 300,000 names daily by his tulasi (3.100). The landowner Ramacandra Khan, envious, could find no fault in him (3.104) and sent a young prostitute, who promised to attract his mind within three days (3.106). She came at night dressed most attractively (3.109), sat on the threshold exposing part of her body to his view, spoke sweet words asking for union (3.111-113), was asked to wait and listen, and did so; the next night too; and by the third night her mind had changed. She told the truth, took Haridasa as guru, gave away her possessions and stayed to chant. Devotional lesson: the name is stronger than any plan, and the Lord's devotee helps everyone to Him.
**J.2 Invented illustration (labelled):** a lamp placed in a storm room stays lit in a closed lantern but goes out in the open; the lantern is association and rules, the lamp is the heart's attention. The lesson: do not take the lantern off to see if the flame is strong. Keep the name central and the lantern near.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections
1. **What does the text say about the woman?** She was a prostitute sent by Ramacandra Khan (3.104-106) and "came to pollute Haridāsa Ṭhākura" (3.122 purport); by hearing the holy name she became "purified" (3.131) and a devotee (3.140).
2. **Why did Haridasa say "I shall accept you"?** The lines are 3.114-115; there is no purport. The preacher's reading, supported by 3.121-3.137, is that he is bringing her into chanting. Requires source review before any other reading is used.
3. **Should I test myself in this way?** No; Haridasa is an acarya of the holy name acting for her good (3.134). The brahmacari follows the guidance of his seniors.
4. **What if I feel attraction when I chant?** Common; continue the name, ask a senior, and rely on community (see bss-39, bss-40).
5. **Why 300,000 names?** Haridasa's vow and practice (3.100). Srila Prabhupada's purport there says no one should imitate it; we follow his example by chanting sixteen rounds daily before tulasi. The student sets any further number with his guru.

## L. Case studies
**L.1 Invented case for discussion.** A brahmacari on a book-distribution trip is approached by a person who is kind and persistent. He feels unsteady. *Principles:* name as protection; community; compassion. *Questions:* what could he do at once? *Analysis:* move on to a public place or his team, chant, report to his leader. BIKC (chapter "Dealings with Nondevotee Women"): "Saṅkīrtana brahmacārīs who feel agitation from contact with women on saṅkīrtana are advised to concentrate on preaching and distributing books to men." *Takeaway:* the team and the name are the protection.
**L.2 Invented case.** A visitor to the temple tells a brahmacari she wants to start a new life. *Principles:* CC Antya 3.135-3.137. *Questions:* what can he offer? *Analysis:* introduce her to the temple's women devotees and counsellors; share the name and prasadam. *Takeaway:* compassion is practical and appropriate.

## M. Discussion and reflection
**M.1 Closing check questions.**
1. *Where was Haridasa staying?* In a forest cottage at Benapola, with a tulasi plant (CC Antya 3.99-3.100).
2. *How much did he chant?* 300,000 names daily (3.100).
3. *Why did Ramacandra Khan send the woman?* He could not find a fault in Haridasa (3.104).
4. *What did Haridasa ask her to do?* Sit and hear the chanting (3.121).
5. *What changed?* By his association her mind changed; she became purified (3.130-3.131).
6. *What did he tell her to do next?* Give away her property, chant continuously and serve tulasi (3.136-3.137).
**M.2 Activities.** (a) *Retell as the text tells it (10 min):* pairs retell the story; the listener raises a hand at any detail that is not in CC Antya 3. (b) *The lantern (10 min):* each writes on a card three things that "keep the lamp lit" in their day (rounds, prasadam, association). (c) *Offer help (10 min, extension):* groups prepare three sentences a brahmacari can say to a visitor in need, ending with directing her to the temple's women devotees.
**M.3 Reflection.** "What keeps my attention on the name?"

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary
Haridasa Thakura lived in a forest cottage at Benapola. He chanted 300,000 names of the Lord every day in front of a tulasi plant (CC Antya 3.100). A landowner named Ramacandra Khan was envious and could find no fault in him (3.104), so he sent a young prostitute to disturb him (3.104-106).
She came at night, dressed most attractively (3.109), sat on the threshold exposing part of her body to his view and spoke sweet words to him (3.111-113). Haridasa asked her to sit and hear the chanting until he finished (3.121). She sat; she also began to say "Hari, Hari" (3.122). By the third night, by his association, her mind had changed (3.130). She fell at his feet and told the truth (3.131). She asked him to be her spiritual master. He told her to give her property to the brahmanas, chant the Hare Krsna mantra continuously and serve the tulasi plant (3.136-3.137). She did, and chanted 300,000 names a day (3.140). The lesson for a brahmacari: the holy name purifies, every person is a soul to help, and we keep close to our chanting and our community.

## S2. Key ideas
- The holy name is the protection and the cure.
- The woman was changed by hearing the name.
- Haridasa acted for her deliverance.
- Haridasa "took it as his duty to deliver the prostitute" (3.122 purport).
- A brahmacari keeps his rounds and his community.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **CC Antya 3.130**: by his association her mind had changed. *For me:* hearing the name changes the heart.
- **CC Antya 3.137**: chant continuously and serve the tulasi. *For me:* a way of life for anyone.
- **CC Antya 3.100**: Haridasa chants 300,000 names daily. *For me:* regular chanting.

## S4. Words to know
- **Tulasi:** a plant dear to Krsna.
- **Holy name:** the Hare Krsna mantra.
- **Spiritual master:** a teacher who guides toward Krsna.
- **Association:** time with someone.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *Should I test myself like this?* No; Haridasa was acting for her good. Keep your rounds and your seniors' guidance.
2. *Who was the woman?* A prostitute sent by Ramacandra Khan to pollute Haridasa (3.104-106; 3.122 purport); by hearing the name she was purified and became a devotee (3.131, 3.140).
3. *What if I feel attraction?* Common; chant, speak to a senior; see Tele-MANAS 14416 or your local crisis service if distressed.
4. *What protects me?* The holy name, association and service.
