## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on trd-49, where profit, adoration and position are weeds, and on trd-15, where pratistha is the preacher's
subtle desire; dps-10 asks the first question, "why do I serve?" This page meets the devotee for whom those weeds have
stopped feeling like weeds: the position is simply his life now. It is quieter than burnout (trd-52) and the opposite
response to the same loss of taste: not despair, but comfort.

## C. Central message

"The occupational activities a man performs according to his own position are only so much useless labor if they do not
provoke attraction for the message of the Personality of Godhead" (SB 1.2.8). A resident's duties can continue for
decades after they have stopped provoking that attraction; then they are a career. Dharma is "never [to] be performed for
material gain" (SB 1.2.9), and a secure place, a respected title and a comfortable routine are material gain. The
remedy is the Bhagavatam's own: endeavour only for "that purposeful end which is not obtainable even by wandering from
the topmost planet… down to the lowest" (SB 1.5.18).

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Explain SB 1.2.8-10 as Prabhupada's purports do: dharma that does not attract one to Krishna is useless labour;
   dharma for material gain is a cheat; life's desires are not for sense gratification but for inquiry into the
   Absolute Truth.
2. Identify, from the CC Madhya 19.160 purport, the forms in which a devotee turns bhakti into "a professional business".
3. Distinguish the devotee's security from the demon's security (BG 16.13-15) by what each counts as "mine".
4. State the one aim worth endeavour (SB 1.5.18) and why happiness from sense enjoyment "can be obtained automatically in
   course of time".
5. Apply NOI 2's *niyamagraha* and *laulya* to the settled resident, and name one action that breaks the settlement.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**SB 1.2.8**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/8/
> "The occupational activities a man performs according to his own position are only so much useless labor if they do not provoke attraction for the message of the Personality of Godhead."
- **Purport point:** "Without knowing the need of the dormant soul, one cannot be happy simply with emolument of the
  body and mind" (the purport continues; read it whole). Also: "Because foolish people have no information of the soul…
  they are not satisfied in the performance of their occupational duties."
- **Application (preacher's):** the verse does not say "non-devotional duties"; it says any occupational activity,
  including a temple service, is useless labour when it has stopped provoking attraction to Krishna's message.
- **Use:** in the talk, the central verse.

**SB 1.2.9**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/9/
> "All occupational engagements are certainly meant for ultimate liberation. They should never be performed for material gain. Furthermore, according to sages, one who is engaged in the ultimate occupational service should never use material gain to cultivate sense gratification."
- **Purport point:** "The general tendency of any ordinary man in any part of the world is to gain some material
  profit in exchange for religious or any other occupational service… Why are such so-called men of religion allured by
  material gain? Because material gain can enable one to fulfill desires, which in turn satisfy sense gratification…
  One should not engage himself in any sort of occupational service for material gain only. Nor should material gain be
  utilized for sense gratification."
- **Use:** in the talk: a position, a room, security and respect are material gain "in exchange for religious…
  service".

**SB 1.2.10**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/10/
> "Life's desires should never be directed toward sense gratification. One should desire only a healthy life, or self-preservation, since a human being is meant for inquiry about the Absolute Truth. Nothing else should be the goal of one's works."
- **Purport point:** "in all spheres of life, the ultimate end is sense gratification. In politics, social service,
  altruism, philanthropy and ultimately in religion or even in salvation, the very same tint of sense gratification is
  ever-increasingly predominant… One should satisfy the senses only insomuch as required for self-preservation, and not
  for sense gratification."
- **Use:** in the talk: the measure of what a resident may want from the temple: a healthy life for inquiry, nothing
  else.

**SB 1.5.17**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/5/17/
> "One who has forsaken his material occupations to engage in the devotional service of the Lord may sometimes fall down while in an immature stage, yet there is no danger of his being unsuccessful. On the other hand, a nondevotee, though fully engaged in occupational duties, does not gain anything."
- **Purport point:** "it so happens sometimes that one surrenders himself unto the service of the Lord by some
  temporary sentiment, and in the long run, due to so many other reasons, he falls down from the path of service by
  undesirable association… But in spite of all this, the stress is given here to surrendering unto the lotus feet of the
  Lord… Once engaged in the devotional service of the Lord, one will continue the service in all circumstances."
- **Use:** in the talk: the resident "forsook his material occupations"; the verse's second half describes what he must
  not quietly become: one "fully engaged in occupational duties" who "does not gain anything". dps-15 used this verse
  against despair after a fall; here the point is the nondevotee's busy emptiness.

**SB 1.5.18**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/5/18/
> "Persons who are actually intelligent and philosophically inclined should endeavor only for that purposeful end which is not obtainable even by wandering from the topmost planet [Brahmaloka] down to the lowest planet [Pātāla]. As far as happiness derived from sense enjoyment is concerned, it can be obtained automatically in course of time, just as in course of time we obtain miseries even though we do not desire them."
- **Purport point:** "all such material positions are impermanent. The positions in the topmost Brahmaloka and in the
  lowest Pātāla are also changeable according to our own work" (the purport continues on the one thing worth
  endeavour; read it whole).
- **Use:** in the talk: the single aim, and the assurance that comfort need not be pursued because it comes and goes by
  itself.

**NOI 2**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/2/
> "…(4) practicing the scriptural rules and regulations only for the sake of following them and not for the sake of spiritual advancement, or rejecting the rules and regulations of the scriptures and working independently or whimsically; … (6) being greedy for mundane achievements."
- **Purport point:** on *prajalpa*: "In the Western countries old men, retired from active life, play cards, fish,
  watch television and debate about useless socio-political schemes. All these and other frivolous activities are
  included in the prajalpa category." On endeavour: "If our endeavor (prayāsa) is not to inquire about the Absolute
  Truth, we will simply increase our endeavor to satisfy our artificial needs."
- **Use:** in the talk: *niyamagraha* (rules kept for their own sake) is the settled devotee's religion; *laulya*
  (greed for mundane achievements) is his ambition; *prajalpa* is his evenings. trd-52 used the same verse for
  over-endeavour.

**BG 16.13-15**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/16/13-15/
> "The demoniac person thinks: 'So much wealth do I have today, and I will gain more according to my schemes. So much is mine now, and it will increase in the future, more and more… I am the lord of everything. I am the enjoyer. I am perfect, powerful and happy. I am the richest man, surrounded by aristocratic relatives. There is none so powerful and happy as I am. I shall perform sacrifices, I shall give some charity, and thus I shall rejoice.' In this way, such persons are deluded by ignorance."
- **Use:** in the talk, as the Gita gives it: the demon's security speech, including "I shall perform sacrifices, I
  shall give some charity." Religious activity is inside the demon's plan. The application is the preacher's: read the
  speech with "my department", "my disciples", "my seniority" in mind.

**CC Madhya 19.160**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/19/160/
> "If one does not distinguish between the bhakti creeper and the other creepers, the sprinkling of water is misused because the other creepers are nourished while the bhakti creeper is curtailed."
- **Purport point (Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura's description, as Prabhupada gives it):** one "may also try to
  carry out a professional business by means of chanting the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra or reading Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, or one may
  try to increase his monetary strength by illegal means. Also, one may become a cheap Vaiṣṇava by trying to chant in a
  secluded place for material adoration, or one may desire mundane reputation by making compromises with nondevotees…
  Just to cheat some innocent people, one makes a show of advanced spiritual life and becomes known as a sādhu,
  mahātmā or religious person."
- **Use:** in the talk: trd-49 used this purport for the weeds in general; here the point is the single phrase
  "professional business", which is what a career in the temple is.
- **Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati's own essays** (named as a core source in research/trd-topics.md): **Requires
  source review.** The files `Bhaktisiddhanta_Sarasvati_Thakura_Vaishnavism_Real_and_Apparent.txt` and
  `_Being_Guru.txt` in `research/books/` are stored in a font-encoded OCR that cannot be read; no passage could be
  checked. His teaching on this subject is therefore cited only as Prabhupada quotes it above, the most reliable source
  available.

**BG 3.21**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/21/
> "Whatever action a great man performs, common men follow. And whatever standards he sets by exemplary acts, all the world pursues."
- **Purport point:** "A leader cannot teach the public to stop smoking if he himself smokes. Lord Caitanya said that a
  teacher should behave properly before he begins teaching."
- **Use:** in the talk: the settled senior sets the standard the juniors will follow; complacency is taught by example.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** The Bhagavatam judges an occupation by one test: does it "provoke attraction for the message of
   the Personality of Godhead" (SB 1.2.8)? If not, it is "useless labor", however religious its form. It forbids one
   motive: material gain (1.2.9). It allows one desire: a healthy life for inquiry into the Absolute Truth (1.2.10). It
   names one aim worth endeavour: the end not obtainable anywhere in the universe (1.5.18). A temple service that has
   become a career fails the test, carries the forbidden motive, exceeds the allowed desire and has lost the aim.
2. **How a service becomes a career.** Not by a decision. The weeds of trd-49 (labha, puja, pratistha) grow; the
   devotee grows used to them; they become his normal. He keeps the rules "only for the sake of following them"
   (NOI 2, *niyamagraha*); his ambitions are "mundane achievements" in a spiritual dress (*laulya*); his conversation
   is *prajalpa* about the institution. The CC Madhya 19.160 purport calls the end point "a professional business by
   means of chanting the Hare Krsna mantra or reading Srimad-Bhagavatam."
3. **Why complacency is material gain.** The 1.2.9 purport says ordinary men want "material profit in exchange for
   religious… service", because profit lets them "fulfill desires, which in turn satisfy sense gratification". The
   resident's profit is not money. It is a secure room, meals, respect, a title, a routine that asks nothing new. These
   fulfil desires; they are material gain. The 1.2.10 purport allows only what is "required for self-preservation".
4. **The demon's speech in a devotee's mouth.** BG 16.13-15 is the clearest mirror because it includes religion: "I
   shall perform sacrifices, I shall give some charity, and thus I shall rejoice." The demon is not irreligious; he
   counts religion among his possessions. The settled resident who counts his seniority, his department and his
   following as "mine… and it will increase" has the demon's grammar with Vaisnava vocabulary. The Gita calls this
   "deluded by ignorance".
5. **The nondevotee's busy emptiness.** SB 1.5.17's second half: "a nondevotee, though fully engaged in occupational
   duties, does not gain anything." The resident forsook his material occupation to serve Krishna; if his service has
   become an occupation like any other, the verse's second half describes him, though he lives in the temple.
6. **The remedy: one aim.** SB 1.5.18 does not ask the devotee to give up comfort by effort; it says comfort "can be
   obtained automatically in course of time", like misery, and so is not worth endeavour. Endeavour belongs to the one
   thing not obtainable anywhere else. The practical form is dps-10's question asked again, daily: "why do I serve?",
   answered with Krishna's pleasure, and tested by whether today's duties provoked attraction to His message (1.2.8).
7. **The leader's responsibility.** BG 3.21: juniors follow what the senior does. A settled senior teaches settling.
8. **Connections.** trd-49 (the weeds this grows from), trd-15 (pratistha), trd-17 (money), trd-52 (the other response
   to lost taste), trd-18 (leadership as service), dps-10 and dps-26 (service attitude and talents for the cause).
9. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *Stability is complacency.* No. Steadiness (nistha, dps-15) is stability in attraction to Krishna; complacency is
     stability without it. SB 1.2.8 is the test.
   - *A senior deserves his comfort.* The 1.2.10 purport allows self-preservation, not reward; the 1.5.18 purport
     says every material position "is impermanent".
   - *If I keep all the rules I am safe.* NOI 2 names rules kept for their own sake as a spoiler.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes).** Say: "Last time we looked at lost enthusiasm. Tonight is its quiet twin: the
devotee who has not lost anything, because he has settled. The service is secure, the title respected, the routine
fixed, and the question 'why am I here?' has stopped, because the answer is now 'this is my life'. The Bhagavatam has a
test for every occupation, including ours." Read SB 1.2.8. Then: "Useless labour: not because the duty is wrong, but
because it no longer provokes attraction to Krishna's message. We will read what the Bhagavatam forbids as the motive of
dharma, what it allows us to want, and what alone is worth our effort."

**H.2 Opening questions:**
1. "When did you last do a service that frightened you a little? What was it?"
2. "If you were moved tomorrow to another temple with no title, which loss would you feel first: the service, or the
   position?"
3. "The Gita's demon says, 'I shall perform sacrifices, I shall give some charity, and thus I shall rejoice.' Which of
   your religious activities would you miss as a possession?"

## I. Core teaching points

1. **The test of every occupation: does it provoke attraction to Krishna's message?** SB 1.2.8. Example (preacher's
   application): two residents cooking the same offering; one tastes nothing of Krishna in it for a year. Transition:
   the forbidden motive.
2. **Dharma is never for material gain, and comfort is gain.** SB 1.2.9-10 and purports. Example: the room, the
   respect, the routine, listed honestly. Transition: how it happens.
3. **Rules for their own sake, mundane ambition in spiritual dress, institutional gossip.** NOI 2. Example: a resident
   whose fifteen years are perfect in form and whose evenings are temple politics. Transition: the end point.
4. **"A professional business by means of chanting… or reading Srimad-Bhagavatam."** CC Madhya 19.160 purport.
   Example: the class given for the fee of respect. Transition: the mirror.
5. **The demon counts religion among his possessions.** BG 16.13-15. Example: "my department… and it will increase".
   Transition: what we became.
6. **The nondevotee's busy emptiness; the devotee's one aim.** SB 1.5.17-18. Example: the resident who left a career to
   serve and built a career in the temple. Transition: the leader.
7. **The settled senior teaches settling.** BG 3.21. Transition to M.

## J. Stories and illustrations

- **Vyasadeva's dissatisfaction after the greatest work (SB 1.4.26 to 1.5.9, taught in trd-52).** Not retold here;
  point the residents to it: the one who had done everything was "not satisfied", and the remedy was Krishna's direct
  glorification. Devotional lesson for this page: Vyasadeva did not settle; he reflected, and his spiritual master came.
- **Rupa Gosvami's own list through Prabhupada (CC Madhya 19.160 purport).** Not a narrative but the acharya's
  catalogue of how bhakti becomes a show; read in Prabhupada's words (E). Devotional lesson: the acharyas foresaw the
  career-devotee and named him so that we can recognise him in the mirror.
- **Contemporary illustration (preacher's own; label it):** describe, without any identifying detail, the common pattern
  of a devotee who joined to preach and now manages, and ask the audience whether the Bhagavatam's test still passes.
  Use the invented cases in L if no honest general example is available.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. *"The temple needs stable managers. Is stability wrong?"* Stability is needed and is not the problem; attraction to
   Krishna's message is the test (SB 1.2.8). A stable manager who hears, preaches and is still moved by Krishna's
   pastimes is steady (dps-15). One who manages well and is moved by nothing is settled.
2. *"I am not seeking money or fame. How can I be in SB 1.2.9?"* The purport says material gain is wanted because it
   fulfils desires. A secure room, respect and an unquestioned routine fulfil desires; they are gain in exchange for
   religious service, though no money changes hands.
3. *"Should I give up my position to break the complacency?"* Not necessarily; ask your authority (dps-02). The
   Bhagavatam asks for a change of aim, not of post (SB 1.5.18). Often the test is passed again by adding what was lost:
   direct preaching to new people, hearing without preparing a class, a service that frightens a little.
4. *"Is wanting the mission to grow laulya?"* Wanting Krishna's mission to grow for His pleasure is enthusiasm (NOI 3);
   wanting my part of it to grow for my standing is laulya (NOI 2). Ask who gains.
5. *"The demon's speech is about wealth; I own nothing."* Read it again: "I shall perform sacrifices, I shall give some
   charity." The demon's possessions include religion. "Mine" can be a department or a following.

## L. Case studies

**Case 1 (Invented case for discussion).** A resident of sixteen years is the temple's most reliable administrator. He
rises on time, chants his rounds, attends every programme, and has not spoken to a new person about Krishna in three
years. He is content, respected and plans to remain exactly as he is until old age. *Principles:* SB 1.2.8 (useless
labour if no attraction); NOI 2 (niyamagraha); BG 3.21. *Questions:* Does his life pass the Bhagavatam's test? What do
the juniors learn from him? *Analysis:* everything is in place except the one thing the verse tests. *Takeaway:* one
hour a week of direct preaching to strangers and one hour of hearing without a purpose; the administration stays.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions (expected answers in brackets):**
1. What is the Bhagavatam's test for any occupational activity (SB 1.2.8)? [Whether it provokes attraction for the
   message of the Personality of Godhead; otherwise it is useless labour.]
2. For what should dharma never be performed (SB 1.2.9)? [Material gain.]
3. What alone may one desire, according to SB 1.2.10? [A healthy life, self-preservation, for inquiry into the
   Absolute Truth.]
4. What phrase in the CC Madhya 19.160 purport describes bhakti turned into a livelihood? ["A professional business by
   means of chanting the Hare Krsna mantra or reading Srimad-Bhagavatam."]
5. What religious activities does the demon of BG 16.13-15 include among his plans? [Sacrifices and charity.]
6. Why does SB 1.5.18 say sense happiness is not worth endeavour? [It comes automatically in course of time, like
   misery; only the end not obtainable anywhere in the universe deserves effort.]

**M.2 Activity (25 min): the two columns.** Each resident writes privately: column A, what he receives from the temple
(room, meals, respect, title, routine, security); column B, what in the last month provoked his attraction to
Krishna's message (a verse, a class, a conversation, a service). Then he marks which column he would defend harder if
both were threatened. Pairs discuss only one question: "what service that frightens me a little will I take this month?"

**M.3 Reflection.** "If I read the demon's speech with my own nouns, which line is mine?"

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

The Bhagavatam gives one test for every duty a person performs: "The occupational activities a man performs according
to his own position are only so much useless labor if they do not provoke attraction for the message of the Personality
of Godhead" (SB 1.2.8). This includes service in a temple. A devotee can do his duties perfectly for years after they
have stopped drawing him to Krishna; then his service has become a career. The next verse names the motive that is
forbidden: duties "should never be performed for material gain" (1.2.9). Srila Prabhupada writes that ordinary men want
"material profit in exchange for religious… service" because profit fulfils desires. A resident's profit is not money
but a secure room, meals, respect, a title and a routine that asks nothing new. These also fulfil desires; they are
material gain. What may one want? "One should desire only a healthy life, or self-preservation, since a human being is
meant for inquiry about the Absolute Truth. Nothing else should be the goal of one's works" (1.2.10).

Rupa Gosvami names the habits of the settled devotee: keeping rules "only for the sake of following them", "being greedy
for mundane achievements", and idle talk (NOI 2). Srila Prabhupada, quoting Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, describes
the end point: one "may also try to carry out a professional business by means of chanting the Hare Krsna mantra or
reading Srimad-Bhagavatam" (CC Madhya 19.160 purport). The Gita holds up a mirror. The demoniac person thinks, "So much
is mine now, and it will increase in the future… I shall perform sacrifices, I shall give some charity, and thus I shall
rejoice" (BG 16.13-15). He is not irreligious; he counts religion among his possessions. A devotee who thinks "my
department, my students, my seniority… and it will increase" has the same grammar in Vaisnava words.

Narada tells Vyasadeva that "a nondevotee, though fully engaged in occupational duties, does not gain anything"
(SB 1.5.17). A resident who left a worldly career to serve, and then built a career in the temple, must not quietly
become that busy, empty person. The remedy is to recover the one aim: "Persons who are actually intelligent… should
endeavor only for that purposeful end which is not obtainable even by wandering from the topmost planet… down to the
lowest planet. As far as happiness derived from sense enjoyment is concerned, it can be obtained automatically in course
of time" (SB 1.5.18). Comfort need not be pursued; it comes and goes by itself. And because "whatever action a great
man performs, common men follow" (BG 3.21), a settled senior teaches the juniors to settle.

## S2. Key ideas
- The test of any duty: does it provoke attraction to Krishna's message? Otherwise it is useless labour (SB 1.2.8).
- Dharma for material gain is forbidden (SB 1.2.9), and comfort, respect and position are material gain.
- Only self-preservation for inquiry may be desired (SB 1.2.10).
- The demon counts even his sacrifices and charity as "mine… and it will increase" (BG 16.13-15).
- One aim alone is worth endeavour; sense happiness comes and goes by itself (SB 1.5.18).

## S3. Verses to remember
- **SB 1.2.8**: duties are "useless labor if they do not provoke attraction for the message of the Personality of
  Godhead". For me: the test of my day.
- **SB 1.2.9**: duties "should never be performed for material gain". For me: my room and my title are gain too.
- **SB 1.5.18**: endeavour "only for that purposeful end which is not obtainable" anywhere in the universe. For me:
  comfort is not worth my effort; it comes by itself.

## S4. Words to know
- **Niyamagraha:** following rules only for their own sake (or rejecting them whimsically) (NOI 2).
- **Laulya:** greed for mundane achievements (NOI 2).
- **Prajalpa:** idle talk about mundane subjects (NOI 2).
- **Nistha:** steadiness in attraction to Krishna, the real thing that complacency imitates (dps-15).
- **Tattva-jijnasa:** inquiry into the Absolute Truth (SB 1.2.10).

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *The temple needs stable managers. Is stability wrong?* No; the test is attraction to Krishna's message (SB 1.2.8).
   A stable manager who still hears and preaches and is moved by Krishna is steady; one moved by nothing is settled.
2. *I am not after money or fame. How does SB 1.2.9 apply to me?* Material gain is anything that fulfils desires in
   exchange for religious service: a secure room, respect, an unquestioned routine.
3. *Should I give up my position?* Ask your authority. The Bhagavatam asks for a change of aim (SB 1.5.18), not
   necessarily of post. Add what was lost: preaching to new people, hearing without a purpose, a service that frightens
   you a little.
4. *Is wanting the mission to grow greed?* Wanting it to grow for Krishna is enthusiasm; wanting my part to grow for my
   standing is laulya. Ask who gains.

## S6. Check your understanding
1. What is the Bhagavatam's test of any occupational activity (SB 1.2.8)?
2. For what must dharma never be performed (SB 1.2.9)?
3. True or false: the demon of BG 16.13-15 has no religious activities.
4. What phrase does Prabhupada use, quoting Bhaktisiddhanta, for bhakti turned into a livelihood?
5. Why is sense happiness not worth endeavour, according to SB 1.5.18?

**Answers:** 1. Whether it provokes attraction for the message of the Personality of Godhead (SB 1.2.8). 2. Material
gain (SB 1.2.9). 3. False: he plans sacrifices and charity (BG 16.13-15). 4. "A professional business by means of
chanting the Hare Krsna mantra or reading Srimad-Bhagavatam" (CC Madhya 19.160 purport). 5. It comes automatically in
course of time, like misery (SB 1.5.18).
