# Why we do? what we do?

> **VL2 · 180 min · review: sources-checked · sensitive.** IYF's angle (site subtitle): *"Can spirituality be scientific,
> and [are the] so-called scientific biased?"* The page argues the first half from the texts' own description of a
> method with observable stages, and handles the second half fairly: scientists have human biases, the method
> exists to catch them, and "science is biased" is never said (F.4). IYF notes: `research/iyf-materials/vl2-09/`
> (slide text: two kinds of science, jada and cit jagat, sambandha–abhidheya–prayojana, Maharaja Ambarisa).

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on [VL2-08](vl2-08.md) (science versus scientism, and the question of bias, already settled there) and [DYS-5](dys-02.md) (the soul is part of the whole and is satisfied by serving it; hearing and chanting as a first taste). Neither is repeated; this page's own angle is **the reason and the predicted stages behind each practice**.

VL2-08 separated science from scientism and ended with the texts' claim that knowledge of the self comes by a
different route: practice that gives "direct perception… by realization" (BG 9.2). This topic takes up that
claim. If bhakti is a method, each of its practices should have a reason, and the method should predict results
that a practitioner can observe. A reasoned-faith talk presents that structure, states candidly what "scientific"
can and cannot mean for it, and sends the listener to VL3 to begin.

**IYF's VL2 frame** (IYF's own documents, `research/batch-criteria.md` §2 and §8 point 2): course name "Technical Science
for Spiritual Value", mood *questioning, reasoned*; motive "developing strong faith in practicing spirituality" and "removing all
misconceptions about ISKCON and spirituality"; KRAs "Start doing chanting", "Coming to regular classes", "Meeting with
facilitators". The 2015 VL2 lesson plan runs DYS 1–6 first and then the EBG study-sessions; this page closes the EBG sequence and hands over to VL3, where the practices are taught. Practice is
expected to *begin* during VL2 (by its end: 2–4 rounds, 15 min of Prabhupada's books and 15–30 min of kirtan hearing a day), so
this page may carry a first practical step without breaking IYF's level (P).

| Level | Treatment |

**Not done here:**
- Teaching the techniques (that is VL3).
- Claiming laboratory evidence for bhakti's effects. The texts describe inner results; peer-reviewed studies of meditation or chanting are not cited here because none was checked for this page.
- Any sentence that calls science or scientists biased against spirituality.

## C. Central message

We do what we do because of what we are for. The Vedic literature gives the structure in three words: *sambandha*,
our eternal relationship with Krsna; *abhidheya*, acting in that relationship; *prayojana*, the goal, love of
God (CC Madhya 20.124). Each practice is a way of engaging the senses in that relationship (SB 9.4.18–20), and the
texts describe what a practitioner will observe as it works (SB 1.2.17–21). That is as far as "scientific"
honestly goes: a method, with stated stages, that you can test on yourself.

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. Define sambandha, abhidheya and prayojana (CC Madhya 20.124–125) and place each practice under abhidheya.
2. Describe the sequence of inner results the Bhagavatam predicts for hearing and practice (SB 1.2.17–21) in plain words.
3. Give the reason behind at least four practices (chanting, hearing/reading, prasadam, the four principles, association, engaging the senses) with a verse for each.
4. Say in what sense bhakti is "scientific" and in what sense it is not, without hostility to science.
5. Choose one practice to begin in VL3 and know what result to look for.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** seven references carry the talk (CC Madhya 20.124, SB 1.2.17–21, BG 9.2, SB 9.4.18–20, SB 12.3.51, BG 9.26, BG 10.9). The rest give the reason for each practice and prepare K.

**CC Madhya 20.108, 20.124, 20.125**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/20/124/
> 20.108: "It is the living entity's constitutional position to be an eternal servant of Kṛṣṇa because he is the marginal energy of Kṛṣṇa and a manifestation simultaneously one with and different from the Lord…"
> 20.124: "The Vedic literatures give information about the living entity's eternal relationship with Kṛṣṇa, which is called sambandha. The living entity's understanding of this relationship and his acting accordingly is called abhidheya. Returning home, back to Godhead, is the ultimate goal of life and is called prayojana."
> 20.125: "Devotional service, or sense activity for the satisfaction of the Lord, is called abhidheya because it can develop one's original love of Godhead, which is the goal of life."
- **Context:** Caitanya Mahaprabhu teaching Sanatana Gosvami.
- **Teaching:** The living entity is eternally Krsna's servant (20.108); the literature teaches relationship, practice and goal (20.124); practice is "sense activity for the satisfaction of the Lord" that develops love of God (20.125).
- **Application (preacher's):** This is IYF's slide "What we do: abhidheya. Why we do: prayojana." Every later verse in this talk sits under one of the three words.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 1.2.6, 1.2.7**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/7/
> 1.2.7: "By rendering devotional service unto the Personality of Godhead, Śrī Kṛṣṇa, one immediately acquires causeless knowledge and detachment from the world."
- **Teaching:** The supreme dharma leads to unmotivated, uninterrupted devotion (1.2.6); devotional service brings knowledge and detachment (1.2.7).
- **Application (preacher's):** 1.2.7 is a prediction: do this, and these two things follow. A method that predicts is the first sense of "scientific".
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 1.2.17, 1.2.18, 1.2.19, 1.2.20, 1.2.21**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/17/ to /21/
> 1.2.17: "Śrī Kṛṣṇa… cleanses desire for material enjoyment from the heart of the devotee who has developed the urge to hear His messages…"
> 1.2.18: "By regular attendance in classes on the Bhāgavatam and by rendering of service to the pure devotee, all that is troublesome to the heart is almost completely destroyed, and loving service unto the Personality of Godhead… is established as an irrevocable fact."
> 1.2.19: "As soon as irrevocable loving service is established in the heart, the effects of nature's modes of passion and ignorance, such as lust, desire and hankering, disappear from the heart. Then the devotee is established in goodness, and he becomes completely happy."
> 1.2.20: "Thus established in the mode of unalloyed goodness, the man whose mind has been enlivened by contact with devotional service to the Lord gains positive scientific knowledge of the Personality of Godhead in the stage of liberation from all material association."
> 1.2.21: "Thus the knot in the heart is pierced, and all misgivings are cut to pieces…"
- **Teaching:** A sequence: hearing → cleansing of material desire → regular hearing and service → the heart's troubles almost destroyed and loving service fixed → lust and hankering leave, goodness, happiness → "positive scientific knowledge" of the Lord → the knot pierced, misgivings cut.
- **Application (preacher's):** Five verses, one sequence, each stage observable in oneself. The translation of 1.2.20 itself uses the word "scientific". This is the heart of the talk: the texts describe what to expect and in what order.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 9.2**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/9/2/
> "This knowledge is the king of education, the most secret of all secrets. It is the purest knowledge, and because it gives direct perception of the self by realization, it is the perfection of religion. It is everlasting, and it is joyfully performed."
- **Teaching:** This knowledge gives direct perception of the self by realisation and is joyfully performed.
- **Purport point:** "there is, unfortunately, no university or educational institution where the science of the spirit soul is instructed"; the soul "is always active", and its activities in the spiritual kingdom are "indicated here as constituting the king of all knowledge, the most confidential part of all knowledge".
- **Application (preacher's):** *Pratyaksavagamam*, direct perception, is the second sense of "scientific": the result is experienced, not only believed.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 9.4.18–20**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/9/4/18-20/
> "Mahārāja Ambarīṣa always engaged his mind in meditating upon the lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa, his words in describing the glories of the Lord, his hands in cleansing the Lord's temple, and his ears in hearing the words spoken by Kṛṣṇa or about Kṛṣṇa. He engaged his eyes in seeing the Deity of Kṛṣṇa…"
- **Teaching:** Ambarisa engaged every sense in the Lord's service, desiring nothing for himself.
- **Purport point:** "*hṛṣīkeṇa hṛṣīkeśa-sevanaṁ bhaktir ucyate*: bhakti means to engage the senses in the service of the master of the senses"; "the first business of a devotee is to accept a bona fide spiritual master who can train him to engage his various senses".
- **Application (preacher's):** IYF's slide uses this passage. It is the map of *what we do*: each practice engages one sense. Draw it (N).
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 7.5.23–24**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/5/23-24/
> "Hearing and chanting about the transcendental holy name, form, qualities, paraphernalia and pastimes of Lord Viṣṇu, remembering them, serving the lotus feet of the Lord, offering the Lord respectful worship… offering prayers to the Lord, becoming His servant, considering the Lord one's best friend, and surrendering everything unto Him…"
- **Teaching:** Prahlada lists nine processes of devotional service.
- **Purport point:** "There are no distinctions permitting only a man or only a brāhmaṇa to offer devotional service to the Lord. Everyone can do so"; sravana means hearing the holy name and descriptions of the Lord from "authorized scriptures", after which "one should memorize these vibrations and repeat them (kīrtanam)".
- **Use:** in the talk: the classical list of what we do.

**CC Adi 17.21, SB 12.3.51, 12.3.52**: Verified. (12.3.51–52: *BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples.*)
> SB 12.3.52: "Whatever result was obtained in Satya-yuga by meditating on Viṣṇu, in Tretā-yuga by performing sacrifices, and in Dvāpara-yuga by serving the Lord's lotus feet can be obtained in Kali-yuga simply by chanting the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra."
- **Teaching:** In this age there is no other means than the holy name (CC Adi 17.21); chanting is the age's one good quality and yields what earlier ages' methods yielded (SB 12.3.51–52).
- **Use:** in the talk: *why we chant*.

**BG 9.26, 9.27, 3.13, 17.8, 17.9, 17.10**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/9/26/
> 9.27: "Whatever you do, whatever you eat, whatever you offer or give away, and whatever austerities you perform – do that, O son of Kuntī, as an offering to Me."
- **Teaching:** The Lord accepts a leaf, flower, fruit or water offered with love (9.26); everything done and eaten is to be an offering (9.27); eating offered food frees from sin (3.13); foods in goodness give life, strength, health and satisfaction, foods in passion cause disease, foods in darkness are stale and impure (17.8–10).
- **Purport point (9.26):** "If one wishes to engage in devotional service… one should find out what the Lord desires of him"; He asks for "a leaf, fruit, flowers and water", so "meat, fish and eggs should not be offered to Kṛṣṇa".
- **Purport point (17.10):** "The purpose of food is to increase the duration of life, purify the mind and aid bodily strength"; "The best food is the remnants of what is offered to the Supreme Personality of Godhead."
- **Use:** in the talk: *why we eat prasadam and are vegetarian*.

**BG 6.17, 2.59, SB 1.17.38**: Verified. 6.17: regulated eating, sleeping, recreation and work mitigate material pains through yoga. 2.59: restriction leaves the taste, but "experiencing a higher taste" fixes one. SB 1.17.38: Kali resides where there is gambling, drinking, prostitution and slaughter. **Use:** *why we regulate* and *why the four principles*.

**BG 10.9, 9.14, 18.65**: Verified. 10.9: the devotees "derive great satisfaction and bliss from always enlightening one another and conversing about Me". 9.14: great souls perpetually worship by chanting, endeavouring, bowing down. 18.65: "Always think of Me, become My devotee, worship Me and offer your homage unto Me. Thus you will come to Me without fail." **Use:** *why we associate* and *prayojana*.

**CC Adi 9.41**: Verified. One born in India should make his life successful and benefit others. **Use:** IYF's closing slide "Mera Bharat Mahan": the practices are meant to be shared, not hoarded.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle: three words.** *Sambandha*: I am eternally Krsna's servant, His marginal energy (CC Madhya 20.108). *Abhidheya*: acting in that relationship, "sense activity for the satisfaction of the Lord" (20.125). *Prayojana*: love of God, returning to Him (20.124; BG 18.65). "Why we do what we do" is answered by prayojana; "what we do" is abhidheya; both rest on sambandha. IYF's slides frame the session exactly this way.
2. **The method, with stages.** The Bhagavatam does not say "believe and be saved". It describes a sequence a practitioner can check against experience: hearing with urge → desire for enjoyment cleansed (SB 1.2.17) → regular hearing and service → the heart's troubles almost destroyed, loving service fixed (1.2.18) → lust and hankering leave, goodness, happiness (1.2.19) → "positive scientific knowledge" of the Lord (1.2.20) → the knot pierced, misgivings cut (1.2.21). SB 1.2.7 predicts knowledge and detachment as by-products. BG 9.2 says the knowledge is perceived directly, by realisation, and "joyfully performed".
3. **Why each practice.** Every practice engages a sense in the relationship (SB 9.4.18–20 purport: "engage the senses in the service of the master of the senses"):
   - *Chanting*: the age's prescribed means (CC Adi 17.21; SB 12.3.51–52); engages tongue and ear.
   - *Hearing and reading*: the first of the nine processes (SB 7.5.23–24); starts the SB 1.2.17 sequence.
   - *Prasadam and vegetarian food*: everything eaten becomes an offering (BG 9.27); the Lord specifies what He accepts (9.26 and purport); offered food frees from sin (3.13); foods in goodness sustain life and mind (17.8–10).
   - *Regulation and the four principles*: regulated habits mitigate pain (6.17); Kali lives in gambling, drinking, prostitution and slaughter (SB 1.17.38); restriction alone fails, higher taste succeeds (2.59).
   - *Association*: devotees enlighten one another and converse about Him (BG 10.9).
   - *Service with the body*: Ambarisa's hands cleaned the temple, his legs walked to holy places (SB 9.4.18–20).
   - *Sharing*: one who has received this should benefit others (CC Adi 9.41).
4. **"Can spirituality be scientific?" Said honestly.** Yes, in two senses the texts themselves supply: it is a *method* with predicted, ordered results (SB 1.2.17–21, 1.2.7), and the results are *directly perceived* (BG 9.2). No, in the sense a physicist means: the results are inner, first-person, and not measured by instruments or settled by controlled trials. Say both. IYF's slide puts it as two kinds of science: of what is within the senses' range and of what is beyond it; Chaitanya Charan's "scopes" talk (https://www.thespiritualscientist.com/understanding-the-scopes-of-science-and-spirituality/, 21 Oct 2015, linked from chaitanyacharan.com) argues the same complementarity. Never claim more.
   **"Are scientists biased?"** Settled in [VL2-08](vl2-08.md) (K.4): scientists are people and the method is built to catch bias. Say no more here.
5. **Connections.** VL2-04 (whom we worship) gives sambandha its object; VL2-05 and -06 the culture that carries the practices; VL2-07 the guide who trains the senses (SB 9.4.18–20 purport); VL2-08 the ground for "direct perception"; VL3 begins the practices.
6. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - **"These are Hindu customs."** Each has a stated reason and a predicted result; present the reason, not the custom. The purport to SB 7.5.23–24 says "Everyone can do so".
   - **"Scientific means proven in a lab."** Not claimed. The texts' sense is method plus direct perception.
   - **"Vegetarianism is the point."** It is a consequence of offering food to the Lord (9.26–27), not a goal in itself; the health note (17.8–10) is secondary.
   - **"Rules first."** BG 2.59: restriction without higher taste does not hold. The practices are built to produce the taste.
7. **Pitch for VL2.** Define *sambandha*, *abhidheya*, *prayojana*, *prasadam*, *anartha* (the heart's troubles, SB 1.2.18) once each. Argue from structure and predicted results; end with one practice to test in VL3.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Welcome by name; say today we ask "why" of every practice, not only "what".
2. Ask H.2 question 1 and write the answers on the board; thank each.
3. Say: "By the end you will have a reason for each, and a way to test whether the reason holds."
4. Draw the three headings: relationship, practice, goal.
5. Ask the room to hold one question: "If this works, what result would I expect?"

**H.2 Opening questions**
- What is something you have seen devotees do that you do not understand?
- Think of any habit you keep (exercise, prayer, study, a craft). What result do you expect from it?
- How long would you give a new method before deciding whether it works?

**Adapting for your audience:** the exercise or craft question works anywhere; for a village audience use a farmer's practice (sowing, watering) and the harvest as the predicted result; for professionals use a training programme with a measurable outcome.

**More material for the opening**

- **Opening question:** "Name one thing you have seen devotees do that you do not understand: chanting on beads, not eating onion, bowing to a picture, waking at four." Write them up. "By the end you will have a reason for each, and a way to test whether the reason holds."
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** A new visitor watches a kirtan and says, "It looks like people enjoying themselves and calling it religion." A devotee replies, "What result would you expect if it works?" The visitor has never thought of spiritual practice as something with an expected result.
- **Transition:** "The Bhagavatam gives the expected results, in order (SB 1.2.17–21). That is what we will examine."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** they are deciding whether to start practising. A method with reasons and predicted results can be tried; a bundle of customs can only be joined.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Three words organise everything.** Relationship, practice, goal (CC Madhya 20.124–125). *Example:* medicine has diagnosis, treatment, health. *Transition:* a treatment should predict its effects.
2. **The method predicts its effects, in order.** SB 1.2.7 and 1.2.17–21; direct perception (BG 9.2). *Example:* read the five verses as a patient-information leaflet. *Transition:* now each practice.
3. **Each practice engages a sense in the relationship.** SB 9.4.18–20 and purport. *Transition:* down the list.
4. **The reasons.** Chanting: the age's means (SB 12.3.51–52). Hearing: the first process (SB 7.5.23–24). Prasadam: everything an offering, and the Lord says what He accepts (BG 9.26–27, 3.13). The four principles: Kali's places (SB 1.17.38); higher taste over restriction (2.59). Association: mutual enlightenment (10.9). *Transition:* is this "scientific"?
5. **Honest yes, honest no.** Method with ordered, perceived results: yes. Lab-measured: no. Scientists biased: people are; the method catches it; say no more. *Transition:* what the result is for.
6. **The result is for sharing.** CC Adi 9.41. *Transition:* to P.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

> **Before you teach:** "scientific" is a word this audience guards. Claim only what F.4 allows. If asked for a study you have not read, say so.

1. **"Why Hare Krsna and not Om or another mantra?"**
   - The texts prescribe the holy name of Hari for this age (CC Adi 17.21; SB 12.3.51–52). Chaitanya Charan's answer (https://thespiritualscientist.com/why-do-we-chant-hare-krishna-not-om-krishna, 23 Sep 2016) adds that Om is often linked to the Lord's impersonal aspect and that the maha-mantra is the form recommended for this age; supporting source only. Honest question.
2. **"Why vegetarian? Plants are alive too."**
   - The reason is not "plants feel less" but offering: everything eaten is to be offered (BG 9.27), and the Lord says what He accepts (9.26 and purport). Health follows (17.8–10), but is secondary. Answer the real question; do not argue biology.
3. **"Why rules about sex, intoxication, gambling, meat? Isn't that control?"**
   - SB 1.17.38 names them as where Kali lives; BG 6.17 says regulation mitigates pain; BG 2.59 says restriction alone fails and higher taste is the method. Present the map and the taste, not the rule.
4. **"Can you prove any of this works?"**
   - Not in a laboratory, and the texts do not claim it. They describe a sequence you can check in yourself (SB 1.2.17–21). The honest offer is: try hearing and chanting for a month and compare your experience with 1.2.17. Objection accepted on its own terms.
5. **"Are scientists biased against spirituality?"** (answered in [VL2-08](vl2-08.md) K.4)
   - Give the VL2-08 answer in one sentence and return to the method of this page.
6. **"Isn't this just culture dressed as method?"**
   - Each practice has a stated reason and a predicted result (F.3). The purport to SB 7.5.23–24 says "Everyone can do so", which is the opposite of a cultural boundary. Misconception; correct gently.
7. **"What if I do it and feel nothing?"**
   - *Pastoral guidance, not a scriptural claim:* 1.2.17 speaks of one "who has developed the urge to hear"; the method is gradual and needs guidance (SB 9.4.18–20 purport). Nobody is judged by a month. VL3-08 covers getting help.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The sceptical flatmate.** Aditya has begun chanting. His flatmate says, "You're just conditioning yourself. Show me one study." Aditya has no study and feels foolish.
- **Principles:** F.4 (what "scientific" can mean here); SB 1.2.17–21 (the predicted sequence); BG 9.2 (direct perception).
- **Questions:** What can Aditya honestly claim? What should he not claim? What could he invite the flatmate to observe?
- **Takeaway:** "Here is the predicted sequence; watch me for three months" is honest and more persuasive than a borrowed study.

**Case 2: The rule-keeper.** Sneha follows the four principles strictly, resents them, and has stopped hearing or reading. She asks why she feels worse than before.
- **Principles:** BG 2.59 (restriction without higher taste); SB 1.2.17–18 (hearing comes first); BG 6.17 (regulation as a whole).
- **Questions:** Which part of the method did Sneha keep and which did she drop? What does 2.59 predict for her state? What would she add first?
- **Takeaway:** The practices are a method; one part without the rest does not work as the texts say.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first)
1. What do the three words sambandha, abhidheya and prayojana mean? *Expected:* relationship with Krsna, acting in it, and the goal of love of God.
2. Name two practices and the sense each engages. *Expected:* for example chanting (tongue and ear), prasadam (tongue).
3. What does SB 1.2.17 say hearing does first? *Expected:* it cleanses the desire for enjoyment in the heart.
4. In what sense is bhakti "scientific", and in what sense not? *Expected:* a method with ordered, directly perceived results; not measured by instruments in a laboratory.
5. Why is vegetarian food not the main point? *Expected:* it follows from offering food to the Lord (BG 9.26–27); the health note is secondary.

**M.2 Activities (engagement block, ~90 min; choose two)**
- **A. Reverse engineering (35 min).** Pairs take two practices from the board, write the result they would predict if it works (10 min), then match their predictions to the stages of SB 1.2.17–21 (10 min), and report (15 min). Praise every honest prediction, including "I would expect nothing".

Design your own test (35 min).** Each person chooses one small practice (ten minutes of reading, one round of chanting, or an ordinary habit such as regular sleep) and writes: what I expect, how I will observe it (a one-line daily note), and when I will review. Pairs read each other's plans for clarity. This teaches the method; no one needs to share results.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Why do devotees chant, read, offer their food, avoid certain things and spend time together? Because of what we are for. The Vedic literature gives the structure in three Sanskrit words: *sambandha*, our eternal relationship with Krsna as His servants (CC Madhya 20.108); *abhidheya*, acting in that relationship by engaging our senses in His service (20.125); and *prayojana*, the goal, love of God (20.124; BG 18.65). A practice is not a random custom: it is a way of engaging one sense in this relationship, as Maharaja Ambarisa engaged his mind, words, hands, ears, eyes and legs (SB 9.4.18–20).

The scriptures also say what to expect, in order. Hearing about the Lord cleanses the desire for enjoyment in the heart (SB 1.2.17). Regular hearing and service then almost destroy what troubles the heart, called *anarthas*, and loving service becomes fixed (SB 1.2.18). Lust and hankering leave, the mind turns to goodness and happiness comes (1.2.19), and knowledge of the Lord follows (1.2.20–21). The Gita calls this knowledge "direct perception of the self by realization" (BG 9.2).

Each practice has its stated reason. Chanting is the means prescribed for this age (CC Adi 17.21; SB 12.3.51). Hearing is the first of nine processes (SB 7.5.23–24). Food is offered as *prasadam* (food offered to the Lord and then eaten): vegetarian because the Lord names what He accepts (BG 9.26–27). Regulation, the four principles, reduces pain (BG 6.17) and avoids the places where Kali, the spirit of this age, lives (SB 1.17.38), while a higher taste makes restriction easy (BG 2.59). That is the honest sense in which bhakti is scientific: a method with predicted stages you can check in yourself. It is not laboratory science, and we do not say it is. What you receive is meant to be shared (CC Adi 9.41).

## S2. Key ideas

- Three words: relationship (sambandha), practice (abhidheya), goal (prayojana) (CC Madhya 20.124).
- Bhakti means engaging the senses in the service of the master of the senses (SB 9.4.18–20 purport).
- The method predicts its results in order (SB 1.2.17–21) and they are perceived directly (BG 9.2).
- Each practice has a reason: chanting (SB 12.3.51), hearing (SB 7.5.23–24), prasadam (BG 9.26–27), regulation (BG 6.17, 2.59), association (BG 10.9).
- "Scientific" here means a method with checkable inner results, not a laboratory proof.

## S3. Verses to remember

- **CC Madhya 20.124:** sambandha, abhidheya, prayojana: the relationship, acting in it, and "returning home, back to Godhead". *For me:* I know why I do what I do.
- **SB 1.2.17:** Krsna "cleanses desire for material enjoyment from the heart of the devotee who has developed the urge to hear His messages." *For me:* the first result to watch for.
- **BG 9.27:** "Whatever you do, whatever you eat, whatever you offer or give away… do that… as an offering to Me." *For me:* this is why we offer our food.

## S4. Words to know

- **Sambandha:** our eternal relationship with Krsna (CC Madhya 20.124).
- **Abhidheya:** devotional practice, "sense activity for the satisfaction of the Lord" (CC Madhya 20.125).
- **Prayojana:** the goal, love of God; going back to Him (CC Madhya 20.124).
- **Prasadam:** food first offered to the Lord (BG 3.13, 9.26).
- **Anarthas:** "all that is troublesome to the heart", which hearing and service remove (SB 1.2.18).

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Why Hare Krsna and not another mantra?** The texts prescribe the holy name of Hari for this age (CC Adi 17.21; SB 12.3.51–52).
2. **Why vegetarian?** Because everything we eat is to be offered (BG 9.27), and the Lord says what He accepts (BG 9.26). Health is a bonus (BG 17.8–10), not the reason.
3. **Why the four principles?** They are where Kali lives (SB 1.17.38); regulation reduces pain (BG 6.17); and the method gives a higher taste so that restriction is not the whole story (BG 2.59).
4. **Can you prove it works?** Not in a lab. The texts describe what you will observe in yourself (SB 1.2.17–21). Try and compare.
5. **Are scientists biased against spirituality?** Scientists are people, and the method exists to catch bias. Some hold a materialist philosophy beyond their data; we discuss the philosophy, we do not accuse the people.
6. **What if I feel nothing?** The method is gradual and needs guidance (SB 9.4.18–20 purport). Nobody is judged by a month; VL3 helps you begin properly.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. Which of the three words names what we *do*: sambandha, abhidheya or prayojana (CC Madhya 20.124–125)?
2. In SB 1.2.17, what does Krsna cleanse from the heart, and of whom?
3. Name three senses Maharaja Ambarisa engaged and how (SB 9.4.18–20).
4. According to BG 9.26, what does the Lord say He will accept?
5. True or false: BG 2.59 says that restricting the senses is enough by itself.

**Answers:** 1. Abhidheya (CC Madhya 20.125). 2. "Desire for material enjoyment", from "the devotee who has developed the urge to hear His messages" (SB 1.2.17). 3. Any three: mind in meditating on Krsna's feet, words in describing His glories, hands in cleaning the temple, ears in hearing about Him, eyes in seeing the Deity (SB 9.4.18–20). 4. "A leaf, a flower, a fruit or water" offered "with love and devotion" (BG 9.26). 5. False: the taste remains until one experiences "a higher taste" (BG 2.59).
