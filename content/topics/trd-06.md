> **TRD · up to 180 min · review: drafted (30 Oct 2026).** Group A. Builds on trd-02 and dps-36 (the six loving
> exchanges among devotees); here NOI 4 is applied to the care of newcomers, as Srila Prabhupada's own purport applies it.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Residents are good at the first meeting (trd-02) and the first book (trd-05). The loss happens afterwards: the student who
came three times and was never called; the guest who was fed and forgotten. Srila Prabhupada's NOI 4 purport says the
Society's life "is nourished by these six types of loving exchange", and that "people must be given the chance to
associate with the devotees of ISKCON". That is this page.

(dps-36, dps-09); leadership (trd-18).

## C. Central message

Srila Prabhupada writes that the Society "was started single-handedly, but because people are coming forward and dealing
with the give-and-take policy, the Society is now expanding", and that "simply by reciprocating in the six ways
mentioned above an ordinary man can fully revive his dormant Krsna consciousness" (NOI 4 purport). A resident gives and
receives, feeds and is fed, opens his mind and listens, with each person he brings, patiently, until that person is
chanting and following; "that is our great victory" (letter, 9 January 1973).

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Name the six exchanges of NOI 4 and show how each applies to a newcomer, from the purport's own examples.
2. Explain from BG 2.62 and CC Madhya 22.107 why association, not argument, awakens Krishna consciousness.
3. Quote Srila Prabhupada's 1973 instructions on staying, recruiting "two or three good men", and the measure of victory.
4. Apply NOI 5's three levels to the people in their care without pretension or pressure.
5. Plan the next three months for one real newcomer (named only to the authority).

## E. Scriptural foundation

**NOI 4** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/4/
> "Offering gifts in charity, accepting charitable gifts, revealing one's mind in confidence, inquiring confidentially, accepting prasāda and offering prasāda are the six symptoms of love shared by one devotee and another."
- **Context:** Rupa Gosvami's fourth instruction, after the advice to give up worldly association and keep devotees' company (NOI 3).
- **Teaching:** Six activities: giving, accepting, opening one's mind, inquiring, honouring prasadam, feeding.
- **Purport point:** "An experienced devotee explains, and an inexperienced devotee learns from him. This is guhyam akhyati prcchati." The Society "has been established to facilitate these six kinds of loving exchanges"; it "was started single-handedly, but because people are coming forward and dealing with the give-and-take policy, the Society is now expanding all over the world." Srila Prabhupada describes life members and friends being invited to festivals and "whatever little prasada we are able to offer them", and members who "inquire very confidentially about the methods of performing devotional service, and we try to explain this." Then: "people must be given the chance to associate with the devotees of ISKCON, because simply by reciprocating in the six ways mentioned above an ordinary man can fully revive his dormant Krsna consciousness."
- **Application (preacher's):** The purport itself applies the six exchanges to outsiders becoming devotees; this page follows it.
- **Use:** in the talk; the central text.

**BG 2.62** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/62/
- **Teaching:** Quoted in the NOI 4 purport (*sangat sanjayate kamah*): "one's desires and ambitions develop according to the company one keeps." "If an ordinary man associates with devotees, he will certainly develop his dormant Krsna consciousness."
- **Use:** in the talk; read the purport on vedabase before quoting beyond this line.

**CC Madhya 22.107** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/22/107/
> "Pure love for Kṛṣṇa is eternally established in the hearts of the living entities. It is not something to be gained from another source. When the heart is purified by hearing and chanting, this love naturally awakens."
- **Teaching:** The NOI 4 purport quotes this verse (in a slightly different rendering: "the living entity naturally awakens"): the newcomer's Krishna consciousness is dormant, not absent; our care provides hearing and chanting, Krishna does the awakening.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**NOI 5** — Verified (trd-02). Deal with each person according to his status; the madhyama is "merciful to the ignorant" (SB 11.2.46, quoted there).
- **Use:** in the talk, for *how* to care: mercy, not management.

**SB 11.2.46** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/2/46/ · *BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples*; the translation used is Srila Prabhupada's in the NOI 5 purport.
- **Use:** background; R.1.

**CC Madhya 22.54** — Verified (trd-02). A moment's association with a pure devotee gives all success; the reason to arrange association rather than lectures only.

**BG 3.21** — Verified (trd-02). What the carer does, the newcomer follows.

**SB 7.9.44** — Verified (trd-01). "I wish to bring them back to shelter at Your lotus feet": the aim of care.

**CC Madhya 7.130** — Verified (trd-02). The purport: one need not change location or status; "one may remain a householder, a medical practitioner, an engineer or whatever." The newcomer is not asked to join the ashram to be cared for.

**Srila Prabhupada's letter to Satsvarupa and Hrdayananda, Bombay, 9 January 1973** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/letters/letter-to-satsvarupa-hrdayananda/ (the Preaching Encyclopedia prints it as "16 Instructions on College Preaching", research/encyclopedia/Basis of College Preaching/0004):
> "try to recruit some of the intelligent class of student class to take up this Krsna Consciousness philosophy and study it carefully. Try to recruit some of such men to become our devotees. There is need for intelligent young men to train them up as future leaders and preachers to go all over the world for spreading the message of Lord Caitanya Mahaprabhu. If we simply go to the college on one night and leave immediately for another place, it will be difficult to make very much impression upon these students. I think it is better to stay at one place for some time, at least one week, or as long as the students are interested, and try to sell as many books as possible. After your lecture in the college you may request if there are any interested students, that they may make further programs for you in their homes, or in some club or hall, like that, so that you may remain some time longer and concentrate on recruiting two or three good men, whoever appears to be the most interested, and by your diligent preaching to them you may be able to persuade some of them to become our devotees."
> The letter continues (Encyclopedia points 9–16; verified against the vedabase page's wording where read): "we shall not expect them immediately to come into the temple… but if at least the seed is planted and they begin chanting rounds and following the principles while continuing their studies at school, that is our great victory"; "if they like, they may go and live in one of our centers"; present Krishna consciousness not as "dry or like set of rules and regulations only" but as "the topmost philosophy"; "present everything very logically and always quoting our Sanskrit authorities"; "write something daily"; "dress neatly and conduct yourselves properly".
- **Use:** in the talk; the founder's own follow-up method.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **Care is the Society's life.** The NOI 4 purport does not treat the six exchanges as etiquette among initiated devotees only; it describes life members, friends and "an ordinary man" reviving Krishna consciousness through them. Missionary work is the exchanges continued over months.
2. **Why association works.** Desire follows company (BG 2.62). Krishna consciousness is dormant in the newcomer (CC Madhya 22.107) and awakens by hearing and chanting in devotees' company (CC Madhya 22.54). Argument may open a door; association walks the person through it.
3. **The six exchanges with a newcomer** (from the purport's examples): we give (a book, time, an invitation) and accept what he offers (a question, a small donation, his help); he opens his mind ("inquire very confidentially about the methods") and we explain ("we try to explain this"); we feed him prasadam and, in time, accept his offering of it. The purport's business-friends example shows that even ordinary friendship uses the six; we use them for Krishna.
4. **The founder's method (9 Jan 1973).** Stay, do not pass through; sell books; let interested students arrange further programmes; concentrate on "two or three good men"; do not expect them to move into the temple; the victory is chanting and following "while continuing their studies"; present the philosophy as the highest knowledge, logically, with Sanskrit authority; write daily; dress and behave well. Every point is a care instruction.
5. **Levels, not labels (NOI 5).** The newcomer is the "innocent" to whom the madhyama shows mercy. Mercy means patience with his pace, honesty about what we ask, and no pressure. He is not a number on a chart.
6. **No change of status required (CC Madhya 7.130 purport).** "One may remain a householder, a medical practitioner, an engineer or whatever." Care that aims only at recruitment to the ashram misreads the purport and the 1973 letter alike.
7. **The carer's behaviour (BG 3.21).** The newcomer will copy what the resident does, not what he says: punctuality, how he treats the cook, whether he keeps his word about the call he promised.
8. **Misunderstandings.** (a) "Follow-up is marketing": the purport calls it love shared in six ways. (b) "Care means never challenging": the exchanges include explaining confidentially what the person asks, including the regulative principles, plainly. (c) "If he does not join, we failed": the letter's measure of victory is chanting and following, wherever he lives.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes).** Ask: "Think of a person you brought to a programme in the last year who no
longer comes. Hold his face in mind; do not say his name." Pause. "Tonight is about him. Srila Prabhupada wrote that our
Society's life is nourished by six loving exchanges, and he applied them to exactly such people. We will read what he
wrote and plan three months for one person." Read NOI 4.

**H.2 Opening questions**
1. "Who looked after you in your first three months? What did he actually do: calls, prasadam, time, answers?"
2. "Why do people stop coming after two or three visits? Give one honest reason you have seen."
3. "If someone asks you a confidential question about his own life, what do you do with it?"

## I. Core teaching points

1. **The six exchanges (NOI 4).** Give, accept, open the mind, inquire, honour prasadam, feed. Example: the purport's own picture: friends invited to a festival, fed, asking confidential questions and being answered. Transition: "He wrote that for people exactly like our guests."
2. **The Society lives by them (NOI 4 purport).** Started single-handedly; expanded by give-and-take; "people must be given the chance to associate". Example: a programme with a lecture and no meal, no conversation and no follow-up gives one of the six and withholds five. Transition: "Why does association do what lectures cannot?"
3. **Association awakens (BG 2.62; CC Madhya 22.107; 22.54).** Desire follows company; love of Krishna is dormant and awakens by hearing and chanting. Example: the student who came for the food and stayed for the people is following BG 2.62 exactly. Transition: "The founder gave the method."
4. **Stay, concentrate, measure rightly (9 Jan 1973).** Stay at least a week; two or three good men; victory is chanting and following while they continue their studies. Example: a resident who counts attendance but cannot name two students he is personally carrying has reversed the letter's priority. Transition: "How do we deal with them?"
5. **Mercy by level (NOI 5); no change of status (7.130).** The newcomer is the innocent; mercy means patience and honesty; he need not join the ashram. Example: a professional who chants eight rounds and attends weekly is the letter's "great victory", not a half-success. Transition: "And they watch us."
6. **Behaviour (BG 3.21).** Keep the promised call; treat the cook as you treat the guest. Transition to J.

## J. Stories and illustrations

**How the Society grew (NOI 4 purport, in Srila Prabhupada's words).** "This Society was started single-handedly, but
because people are coming forward and dealing with the give-and-take policy, the Society is now expanding all over the
world. We are glad that people are donating very liberally… and people are also eagerly accepting whatever humble
contribution we are giving them in the shape of books and magazines… We sometimes hold Hare Krsna festivals and invite
life members and friends to participate in the feasting by accepting prasada… Sometimes the members and supporters
inquire very confidentially about the methods of performing devotional service, and we try to explain this." Lesson: the
founder describes his own care of newcomers as the six exchanges.

**The letter of 9 January 1973 (Verified).** Written from Bombay to two leaders running college programmes in America.
Tell it as the letter stands: praise for their "Bhagavata-dharma program", the instruction to stay a week or more, to
concentrate on two or three, and the definition of victory. Lesson: Srila Prabhupada's follow-up method was personal,
patient and realistic.

**Prahlada's aim (SB 7.9.44, trd-01).** "I wish to bring them back to shelter at Your lotus feet." Lesson: care has a
destination; it is not hospitality for its own sake.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"Isn't 'follow-up' manipulative?"** Not if it is the six exchanges: real gifts, real listening, real prasadam, honest answers. Manipulation is pressure and concealment; NOI 5 asks for mercy, and the letter says "we shall not expect them immediately to come into the temple".
2. **"I have forty contacts. How can I care for all of them?"** Srila Prabhupada said "two or three good men". Carry two or three personally; let the programme carry the rest; pass others to fellow residents.
3. **"What if a newcomer confides something serious (family trouble, addiction, mental distress)?"** Listen, keep the confidence, and bring the matter to your authority; for mental distress encourage professional help (in India, Tele-MANAS 14416) alongside chanting and association. Do not promise what you cannot give. (trd-20 and trd-54 treat strain in more depth.)
4. **"He chants but will not give up one habit. Do I keep caring?"** Yes. The letter's victory is "chanting rounds and following the principles"; the exchanges continue while he grows. Be honest about the principle; do not withdraw friendship to force it.
5. **"Should I keep contact with women newcomers?"** A brahmacari follows BIKC's dealings (bss-31, bss-32): warmth with boundaries; hand such care to women devotees or householders. Care never overrides the ashram's standards.

## L. Case studies

**Case 1: Three visits (Invented case for discussion).** Aman, a second-year student, came to three Sunday programmes
after a book table conversation, asked many questions, then stopped. The resident who met him has his number and has
not called in two months.
- **Dilemma:** Has the resident done his part?
- **Principles:** NOI 4 (inquiring confidentially must be answered; giving and accepting); 9 Jan 1973 (concentrate on the interested); BG 2.62.
- **Questions:** Which of the six exchanges happened? Which did not? What one call could Aman receive this week, and what would it offer (prasadam at the temple, a book, a question answered)?
- **Analysis:** Aman offered the exchange of inquiry; the resident did not complete it after the programme. The letter's "diligent preaching" to the two or three most interested is exactly what lapsed.
- **Takeaway:** The first contact created a debt of care; pay it with one honest call and one invitation.

**Case 2: The target (Invented case for discussion).** A resident is told to "move" five students into the ashram by
year's end and begins pressing a final-year student to leave his studies.
- **Principles:** 9 Jan 1973 ("we shall not expect them immediately to come into the temple… that is our great victory"); CC Madhya 7.130 purport ("one may remain… an engineer or whatever"); NOI 5 (mercy).
- **Takeaway:** Care measures victory as the founder did; a devotee finishing his degree and chanting is a success, not a loss.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions**
1. Name the six exchanges of NOI 4. (Giving, accepting, revealing the mind, inquiring confidentially, accepting prasadam, offering prasadam.)
2. What does the purport say the Society's life is nourished by? (These six types of loving exchange.)
3. Why does association awaken Krishna consciousness? (Desire follows company, BG 2.62; love of Krishna is dormant and awakens by hearing and chanting, CC Madhya 22.107.)
4. How long did Srila Prabhupada say to stay at a college, and on how many should one concentrate? (At least a week or as long as interest lasts; two or three good men.)
5. What did he call "our great victory"? (That they begin chanting rounds and following the principles while continuing their studies.)
6. How does the madhyama-adhikari treat the innocent? (With mercy: NOI 5 purport.)

**M.2 Activity (35 min): "Three months for one person."** Each resident chooses one real newcomer (named only to his
authority later) and writes a three-month plan with one line per exchange of NOI 4: what he will give, what he will
accept, how he will invite the person to open his mind, what he will ask, when he will feed him prasadam, when he will
accept the person's own offering. Then he writes the founder's measure of victory for that person in one sentence. Pairs
check each plan for pressure or concealment and remove it.

**M.3 Reflection (private).** "Who carried me through my first three months, and which of the six did he do most? Which do I do least?"

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Bringing someone to Krishna consciousness does not end at the first meeting or the first book. Rupa Gosvami lists six
exchanges of love between devotees: giving gifts, accepting gifts, revealing one's mind in confidence, inquiring
confidentially, accepting prasadam and offering prasadam (NOI 4). Srila Prabhupada's purport applies these to people
coming to the Society: it "was started single-handedly, but because people are coming forward and dealing with the
give-and-take policy, the Society is now expanding all over the world." He describes friends invited to festivals, fed
prasadam, asking confidential questions and being answered, and concludes: "people must be given the chance to associate
with the devotees of ISKCON, because simply by reciprocating in the six ways mentioned above an ordinary man can fully
revive his dormant Krsna consciousness."

Why association? Because "one's desires and ambitions develop according to the company one keeps" (BG 2.62, quoted in
the purport), and because love for Krishna is already in the heart and "when the heart is purified by hearing and
chanting, this love naturally awakens" (CC Madhya 22.107). Even a moment's association with a pure devotee can
give all success (CC Madhya 22.54).

Srila Prabhupada's letter of 9 January 1973 gives the method for colleges: stay at one place at least a week, sell
books, let interested students arrange more programmes, concentrate on "two or three good men", and do not expect them
to move into the temple; "if at least the seed is planted and they begin chanting rounds and following the principles
while continuing their studies at school, that is our great victory." Present the philosophy as the highest knowledge,
logically and with scriptural authority; write daily; dress neatly and behave properly.

The carer deals with the newcomer as the mature devotee deals with the innocent: with mercy (NOI 5), never with
pressure or concealment, and remembers that the newcomer copies what he does (BG 3.21). The person need not change his
occupation: "one may remain a householder, a medical practitioner, an engineer or whatever" (CC Madhya 7.130 purport).

## S2. Key ideas

- Six exchanges: give, accept, open the mind, inquire, honour prasadam, feed (NOI 4).
- The Society's life is nourished by these exchanges, with newcomers too (NOI 4 purport).
- Association awakens dormant Krishna consciousness (BG 2.62; CC Madhya 22.107; 22.54).
- Stay, concentrate on two or three, and measure victory as chanting and following (letter, 9 Jan 1973).
- Mercy, not pressure (NOI 5); no change of occupation required (CC Madhya 7.130).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **NOI 4:** "Offering gifts in charity, accepting charitable gifts, revealing one's mind in confidence, inquiring confidentially, accepting prasada and offering prasada are the six symptoms of love." *For me:* a checklist for each person I bring.
- **CC Madhya 22.107:** "Pure love for Krsna is eternally established in the hearts of the living entities… When the heart is purified by hearing and chanting, this love naturally awakens." *For me:* I provide the hearing; Krishna does the awakening.
- **BG 2.62 (as the NOI 4 purport uses it):** "one's desires and ambitions develop according to the company one keeps." *For me:* give the person my company, not only my lecture.

## S4. Words to know

- **Guhyam akhyati prcchati:** "reveals confidentially, inquires confidentially" (NOI 4).
- **Prasadam:** food offered to Krishna; here the fifth and sixth exchanges.
- **Madhyama-adhikari:** the intermediate devotee, merciful to the innocent (NOI 5).
- **Life member:** a supporter of the Society who has made a one-time contribution (NOI 4 purport).
- **Bhagavata-dharma programme:** the name of the college programme praised in the 1973 letter.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Isn't follow-up a sales technique?** Not when it is the six exchanges: real gifts, listening, prasadam and honest answers, with no pressure.
2. **I know too many people to care for all.** Srila Prabhupada said "two or three good men". Carry those personally; let the programme and other devotees carry the rest.
3. **Someone told me a serious personal problem. What do I do?** Keep the confidence, bring it to your authority, and encourage professional help where needed, together with chanting and association.
4. **He chants but keeps one bad habit. Have I failed?** No. The letter's victory is chanting and following; growth takes time. Be honest about the principle and keep the friendship.
5. **Must he join the ashram?** No. "One may remain a householder, a medical practitioner, an engineer or whatever" (CC Madhya 7.130 purport).

## S6. Check your understanding

1. List the six exchanges of NOI 4.
2. According to the purport, how did the Society expand from a single-handed start?
3. Why does company change a person (BG 2.62)?
4. How long did Srila Prabhupada say to stay at a college?
5. What did he call "our great victory"?
6. True or false: a newcomer must leave his job to be considered a devotee.

**Answers:** 1. Giving gifts, accepting gifts, revealing the mind in confidence, inquiring confidentially, accepting
prasadam, offering prasadam. 2. By the give-and-take policy, people coming forward and dealing in the six exchanges.
3. Desires and ambitions develop according to the company one keeps. 4. At least a week, or as long as the students are
interested. 5. That they begin chanting rounds and following the principles while continuing their studies. 6. False
(CC Madhya 7.130 purport).
