# Modern Kurukshetra: winning the game of life

> **VL1 · 45–60 min · review: sources checked, awaiting senior review.** Scripture light but exact. IYF holds no deck for this title; the sources
> map lists only HG Sundar Gopal Prabhu's DU lecture "Life is a Game" (29 Jan 2020, audio) as "hear next". The
> page is therefore built from the Gita's own frame: Arjuna on the field as every young person at a decision,
> and the Gita's redefinition of "winning". Not sensitive. Mood: light, curious; the game metaphor is kept
> playful and is never allowed to trivialise the text.

## A. Topic overview

| | |

| Teaching objective | Show that everyone stands on a Kurukshetra of daily choices, that Arjuna's first move was to ask, and that the Gita's definition of winning (right action, equanimity, clarity, guided choice) is one a person can achieve in every match, win or lose on the scoreboard |

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

The audience already lives on a battlefield: competing duties (family vs career), competing values (honesty vs
the shortcut), competing voices (parents, peers, feeds). The Gita opens on exactly such a field with a capable
man who cannot decide. At VL1 the text is offered as a mirror and a method, not as a doctrine: here is a person
like you; here is what he did first; here is what his teacher called winning. The game metaphor gives the room
permission to enjoy it.

Builds on vl1-10 and vl1-23 (BG 2.47, work and results, taught in depth there): here 2.47 is only one of four rules, so give it two
minutes and link those pages. vl1-42 uses a "tournament" image for society; here the field is one person's daily choices.

## C. Central message

Everyone stands on a Kurukshetra: a field of choices where duties and voices collide and the clock is running.
Arjuna's first winning move was to stop and ask. The Gita then redefines winning: do your part fully, without
owning the result; stay even in success and failure; act for a reason bigger than yourself; choose freely after
deliberating. By that definition you can win every match, whatever the scoreboard says.

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. Describe their own "Kurukshetra" (one current decision with competing duties and voices) and see Arjuna's
   situation in it.
2. State what Arjuna did first, in his own words (BG 2.7), and why asking is a move and not a defeat.
3. Explain the Gita's four rules of the game: right to work, not to fruits (BG 2.47); equanimity is yoga
   (BG 2.48); fight for the sake of what is right (BG 2.38); deliberate, then choose (BG 18.63).
4. Say what "winning" looks like in BG 18.73 (clarity restored) and BG 18.78 (where Krsna and Arjuna are).
5. Apply one rule to one decision this week.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** BG 2.7, 2.47, 2.48, 18.63 and 18.73 carry the talk. BG 2.38 and 2.14 give the "every match" point;
BG 2.41 and 18.78 are for the preacher and for questions.

**BG 2.7**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/7/
> "Now I am confused about my duty and have lost all composure because of miserly weakness. In this condition I am asking You to tell me for certain what is best for me. Now I am Your disciple, and a soul surrendered unto You. Please instruct me."
- **Context:** After collapsing in chapter 1, Arjuna turns to Krsna.
- **Teaching:** Confused about duty and without composure, Arjuna asks to be told what is best, and accepts the
  position of a student.
- **Purport point:** "In every step there is perplexity, and therefore it behooves one to approach a bona fide
  spiritual master". The perplexities of life "are like a forest fire that somehow blazes without being set by
  anyone". The purport defines the *krpana* (miser) as one "who does not solve the problems of life as a human".
- **Application (preacher's):** The first winning move on any field is to admit confusion and ask someone who
  sees further. At VL1 "spiritual master" becomes "someone who knows the game": a mentor, a text, a guide. The
  surrender language waits for later levels.
- **Use:** in the talk (the first move).

**BG 2.47**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/47/
> "You have a right to perform your prescribed duty, but you are not entitled to the fruits of action. Never consider yourself the cause of the results of your activities, and never be attached to not doing your duty."
- **Teaching:** The right is to the work, not to its fruits; neither own the result nor refuse the work.
- **Purport point:** Three options: prescribed duty, capricious work, inaction. "Any attachment, positive or
  negative, is cause for bondage. Inaction is sinful."
- **Application (preacher's):** Rule 1 of the game. Note the second half: the verse forbids quitting as much as
  grasping; "not playing" is also an attachment.
- **Use:** in the talk (rule 1).

**BG 2.48**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/48/
> "Perform your duty equipoised, O Arjuna, abandoning all attachment to success or failure. Such equanimity is called yoga."
- **Teaching:** Duty done with evenness toward success and failure is yoga.
- **Purport point:** "Gain or victory are Krsna's concern; Arjuna is simply advised to act according to the
  dictation of Krsna."
- **Application (preacher's):** Rule 2. Evenness is not indifference; it is the player who plays the same shot
  at 0–0 and at match point.
- **Use:** in the talk (rule 2).

**BG 2.38**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/38/
> "Do thou fight for the sake of fighting, without considering happiness or distress, loss or gain, victory or defeat – and by so doing you shall never incur sin."
- **Teaching:** Fight for the sake of fighting, without weighing happiness or distress, loss or gain, victory or
  defeat; acting so, one incurs no sin.
- **Purport point:** "There is no consideration of happiness or distress, profit or loss, victory or defeat in
  the activities of Krsna consciousness."
- **Application (preacher's):** Rule 3: the reason you play. At VL1 phrase it "for the sake of what is right",
  and let "for Krsna" be heard by those ready for it.
- **Use:** in the talk (rule 3).

**BG 18.63**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/63/
> "Thus I have explained to you knowledge still more confidential. Deliberate on this fully, and then do what you wish to do."
- **Teaching:** After the whole teaching, Krsna leaves the choice to Arjuna.
- **Purport point:** "God does not interfere with the little independence of the living entity." "Before
  surrendering, one is free to deliberate on this subject as far as the intelligence goes".
- **Application (preacher's):** Rule 4, and the no-pressure verse of the talk: the game is played by a free
  player who deliberates. Nothing is forced, not even by the coach.
- **Use:** in the talk (rule 4).

**BG 18.73**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/73/
> "Arjuna said: My dear Kṛṣṇa, O infallible one, my illusion is now gone. I have regained my memory by Your mercy. I am now firm and free from doubt and am prepared to act according to Your instructions."
- **Teaching:** Illusion gone, memory regained, firm and free from doubt: Arjuna's state at the end.
- **Purport point:** "Moha refers to that which is opposed to knowledge." Arjuna "became free from all illusion."
- **Application (preacher's):** This is what "winning" looks like before a single arrow is fired: clarity. The
  battle's outcome is told later in the Mahabharata, not in the Gita.
- **Use:** in the talk (the win).

**BG 18.78**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/78/
> "Wherever there is Kṛṣṇa, the master of all mystics, and wherever there is Arjuna, the supreme archer, there will also certainly be opulence, victory, extraordinary power, and morality. That is my opinion."
- **Context:** Sanjaya's closing words to Dhrtarastra.
- **Purport point:** Dhrtarastra "was hopeful of the victory of his sons", but "where Krsna and Arjuna are
  present, there will be all good fortune." The purport adds that Yudhisthira "never spoke a lie during his life".
- **Application (preacher's):** The Gita's final word on victory: where the player and the guide are together.
  At VL1 one line; the devotional meaning is for later levels.
- **Use:** in the talk, closing line.

**BG 2.41**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/41/
> "Those who are on this path are resolute in purpose, and their aim is one. O beloved child of the Kurus, the intelligence of those who are irresolute is many-branched."
- **Purport point:** "One who is not firmly fixed in mind is diverted by various types of fruitive acts."
- **Application (preacher's):** For the question "why am I always torn?": many aims, many branches.
- **Use:** background; in the talk if asked.

**BG 2.14**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/14/
> "…the nonpermanent appearance of happiness and distress, and their disappearance in due course, are like the appearance and disappearance of winter and summer seasons … one must learn to tolerate them without being disturbed."
- **Purport point:** "In the proper discharge of duty, one has to learn to tolerate nonpermanent appearances and
  disappearances of happiness and distress."
- **Application (preacher's):** Seasons: a losing streak is weather, not identity.
- **Use:** in the talk, one line.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** The Gita is set on a battlefield because life is one: duties and voices collide and a
   choice must be made under time. Arjuna's first move is to ask (BG 2.7). The teaching then changes the
   definition of winning from the scoreboard to the player: work fully without owning the fruit (2.47); stay
   even (2.48); act for what is right, not for gain (2.38); deliberate and choose freely (18.63). The result is
   clarity (18.73), and the promise is that where the player and the guide are together, good fortune follows
   (18.78).
2. **Why it matters.** A generation measured by rankings is losing even when it wins: the next match is always
   coming. A definition of winning that depends on the player's action and state, not on the outcome, is the
   only one that can be met every day.
3. **Connections.** VL1-23/10 (work and its fruits in depth); VL1-42 (society's scoreboard); VL1-16 (the mind
   as the player's own opponent); VL2-03/09 (why a scripture begins with a war); VL3-08 (asking a guide, in
   practice).
4. **Consequences.** Someone who accepts this asks before deciding; does the work and lets go of the fruit;
   notices when a result has gripped their mood and returns to evenness; checks the reason they are playing;
   and stops confusing a losing season with a lost life (2.14).
5. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - **"Not attached to results = don't try."** The verse forbids inaction too (2.47 purport: "Inaction is
     sinful"). Play fully; own the effort, not the trophy.
   - **"Equanimity = not caring who wins."** Arjuna fought to win. Evenness is about the player's steadiness,
     not the quality of play.
   - **"The Gita glorifies war."** It uses a war that was already there to teach the inner one; 18.78's purport
     calls it "the supreme instruction in morality". The historical question is VL2.
   - **"Asking for help is losing."** It is the first move of the text's hero (2.7).
6. **Pitch for VL1.** Keep the game metaphor light and consistent (field, player, coach, rules, scoreboard). No
   Sanskrit except *yoga* as defined by 2.48 ("such equanimity is called yoga"), which surprises people
   pleasantly. Never make a listener's real exam or match the example.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet, give your name, and say the Gita begins on a battlefield, and the talk is about our own.
2. Ask the opening question silently; nobody shares.
3. Ask H.2 question 2 and take two or three answers; thank each.
4. Say: "Kurukshetra" is the field where the Gita is spoken; it is a place name, not a word you need to know.
5. Use the transition line below and begin "Kurukshetra, then and now".

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the room's own life)
1. What is one decision this month where two things you care about pull in opposite directions?
2. Whose voices do you hear when you choose: family, friends, a feed, your own?
3. Think of a time you asked for help before deciding. How did it feel?

- **Opening question:** "Think of one decision in front of you right now where two things you care about pull
  in opposite directions. Don't share it. Notice how many voices are in your head about it." Pause. "That is a
  Kurukshetra. The Gita begins on one."
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** Arnav has a start-up offer and a safe job; his father wants the job, his
  friends want the start-up, his feed wants him to be a founder by 25. He has two days. He has stopped sleeping.
  What should his first move be?
- **Transition:** "The greatest archer of his age stood on a field with two armies and put down his bow.
  What he did next is the whole talk."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** They are being scored every day. A definition of winning they can meet
  every day is worth more than any single result.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Everyone's on a field.** Duties collide, voices compete, the clock runs (BG 1–2 setting). *Example:*
   Arnav's two days. *Transition:* what did Arjuna do first?
2. **The first move is to ask.** "I am confused about my duty … I am asking You … Please instruct me" (BG 2.7).
   *Example:* the student who asks the senior before the exam, not after. *Transition:* then the rules.
3. **Rule 1: play; don't own the fruit.** "You have a right to perform your prescribed duty, but you are not
   entitled to the fruits" (BG 2.47). And no quitting: "never be attached to not doing your duty." *Example:*
   full preparation, result released. *Transition:* rule 2.
4. **Rule 2: even at match point.** "Such equanimity is called yoga" (BG 2.48). *Example:* the same shot at 0–0
   and at 10–9. *Transition:* rule 3.
5. **Rule 3: know why you play.** "Fight for the sake of fighting, without considering … victory or defeat"
   (BG 2.38): for the right reason, not the prize. *Example:* the honest answer in an interview that may cost
   the job. *Transition:* rule 4.
6. **Rule 4: deliberate, then choose, freely.** "Deliberate on this fully, and then do what you wish to do"
   (BG 18.63). *Example:* the coach who gives the plan and leaves the shot to you. *Transition:* what does
   winning look like?
7. **Winning is clarity, and company.** "My illusion is now gone … I am now firm and free from doubt" (BG 18.73);
   where Krsna and Arjuna are together, victory follows (18.78). Losing streaks are seasons (2.14). *Transition:*
   one decision this week.

## J. Stories and illustrations

- **Arjuna's two states** (BG 1.28–2.7 and 18.73; verified 2.7 and 18.73). The same man: in chapter 1 the bow
  slips from his hand; in 18.73 he is "firm and free from doubt". Nothing on the field has changed; the player
  has. *Connection:* winning before the first arrow. Tell from the text; no invented dialogue.
- **Sanjaya's verdict** (BG 18.78; verified). The blind king hopes for his sons' victory; his narrator tells him
  that where Krsna and Arjuna are, victory is certain. *Connection:* the guide and the player together.
- **Arnav's two days** (invented, above). *Connection:* the first move is to ask.
- **A game metaphor, labelled:** "the ball never wins" (Chaitanya Charan's piece *The Cricket of
  Life*, VOICE; supporting, cite as his): the body, team and company win and lose; the player is someone else.
  Use lightly; it is an essay, not scripture.
- **Not used:** any real recent match or exam result as an illustration of a listener's life.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"If I'm not attached to results, why would I work hard?"**
   - BG 2.47 forbids quitting as firmly as grasping ("never be attached to not doing your duty"; the purport:
     "Inaction is sinful"). Attachment to the fruit is what makes you choke at match point; releasing it is how
     you play your best.
2. **"Equanimity sounds like not caring whether I win."**
   - Arjuna cared enough to fight a war. 2.48 is about the player's steadiness, which is exactly what wins.
     Play the same shot at 0–0 and at match point.
3. **"Why does a book of wisdom begin with a war? Isn't that violent?"**
   - It uses a war that had already come to teach the inner one, and the purport to 18.78 calls the Gita "the
     supreme instruction in morality". The historical question is a VL2 topic; here we take the field as a mirror.
4. **"Asking someone else what to do: isn't that weakness?"**
   - It is the first move of the text's hero (2.7). The purport says perplexity comes "like a forest fire";
     nobody is expected to put it out alone. Choose your advisers; then, by 18.63, the choice is still yours.
5. **"Who is the 'coach' for me? I don't have a Krsna."**
   - Start with what is in front of you: a text that has coached people for millennia, and someone who knows it
     and knows you. VL3-08 is about exactly that relationship. Nobody here is asked to accept anyone today.
6. **"I keep losing. Does that mean I'm doing life wrong?"**
   - 2.14: happiness and distress come and go "like … winter and summer seasons". A season is not a verdict.
     Check the four rules, not the scoreboard; if the rules are kept, the match was won where it counts.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: Two days.** Arnav must choose between a start-up and a safe job; father, friends and feed each pull
one way. He has not slept.
- **Dilemma:** What is his first move, and what would "winning" this decision mean?
- **Principles:** BG 2.7 (ask); BG 18.63 (deliberate, then choose freely); BG 2.47 (own the work, not the
  outcome); BG 2.41 (many aims, many branches).
- **Questions:** Whom should Arnav ask, and what should he ask for (a decision, or a way of seeing)? After he
  chooses, what belongs to him and what does not? How would he know in a year whether he "won", by the rules
  rather than the scoreboard?
- **Takeaway:** Ask first; choose freely; own the effort.

**Case 2: Match point.** Simran has topped every test this year and is terrified of the final. The fear itself
is now costing her marks.
- **Principles:** BG 2.48 (equanimity is yoga); BG 2.38 (why she plays); BG 2.14 (seasons).
- **Questions:** What has she attached to the result that makes the last test different from the first? What
  would "the same shot at match point" look like in her preparation? What is her reason for studying, and does
  it survive a bad result?
- **Takeaway:** Evenness is not caring less; it is the condition for playing well.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first; praise every try)
1. What did Arjuna do first, before any teaching? *He admitted confusion and asked (BG 2.7).*
2. What does BG 2.47 say we have a right to? *Our work, not its fruits.*
3. How does BG 2.48 define yoga? *Equanimity in success and failure.*
4. What is the order in BG 18.63? *Deliberate fully, then choose freely.*
5. What had changed by the end (BG 18.73)? *Arjuna: his illusion was gone and he was firm.*

**M.2 Activity (about 10 minutes): "Name your field"**
1. 2 min: each person writes (privately) one live decision and the voices around it.
2. 3 min: in pairs, each picks one of the four rules and says, in one sentence, what it would change for that decision (the decision itself need not be shared).
3. 3 min: volunteers share the rule they chose and why. Thank each.
4. 2 min: each writes the first move: one person to ask or one fact to find out.

**M.3 Reflection**
- **Comprehension:** What did Arjuna do in BG 2.7 before any teaching was given? What are the two attachments
  BG 2.47 warns against? How does BG 2.48 define yoga?
- **Reflection:** On my current field, which rule do I break most: owning fruits, losing evenness, playing for
  the wrong reason, or not deliberating before I choose?
- **Application (private):** One decision this week, and which rule I will apply to it. *(Nobody needs to share.)*

## P. Practical takeaway

- **Main lesson:** I stand on a Kurukshetra every day. First move: ask. Then play fully without owning the
  fruit, stay even, play for the right reason, and choose freely after deliberating. That is a win I can have
  every day.
- **One action (VL1):** This week, choose one real decision you have to make. Before you decide, ask a senior
  devotee or your mentor for advice, and read Bhagavad-gita chapter 2 for ten minutes. Then write one sentence: "My
  duty is to do this work sincerely as service to Krishna; the result is in Krishna's hands" (BG 2.47). Make your
  decision, and keep the sentence where you can see it.
- **Reflection question:** When I fail at something, do I think "this result was bad", or "I am a failure"? How
  would I feel if I saw a run of failures as a passing phase that Krishna allows for my growth?

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

The Bhagavad-gita begins on a battlefield because life is one: duties collide, voices compete and the clock runs.
Its hero, the greatest archer of his age, put down his bow. His first winning move was to admit confusion
and ask: "I am confused about my duty … please instruct me" (BG 2.7). Then his teacher changed the meaning of
winning. Rule 1: you have a right to your work, not to its fruits, and you may not quit either (BG 2.47). Rule 2:
stay even in success and failure; "such equanimity is called yoga" (BG 2.48). Rule 3: play for what is right,
not for the prize (BG 2.38). Rule 4: deliberate fully, then choose freely (BG 18.63). At the end nothing on the
field had changed, but Arjuna had: "my illusion is now gone … I am now firm and free from doubt" (BG 18.73).
That is a win available in every match, whatever the scoreboard says.

"Kurukshetra" is the name of the field where Arjuna and Krsna spoke. "Yoga" here means steadiness of mind, not exercise. If you play a team sport, farm a season or run a shop, you
know that the result is partly out of your hands, but your preparation, your honesty and your calm are yours. The talk invites you to try this on one real decision this week:
ask one person for guidance, think it through, then choose. This is a way to test, not a rule you have to believe first.

## S2. Key ideas

- Everyone stands on a Kurukshetra: a field of colliding duties and competing voices.
- The first move is to ask (BG 2.7). Asking is not losing.
- Rule 1: play; don't own the fruit, and don't quit (BG 2.47).
- Rule 2: even at match point; "such equanimity is called yoga" (BG 2.48).
- Rule 3: know why you play (BG 2.38).
- Rule 4: deliberate, then choose freely (BG 18.63).
- The win is clarity (BG 18.73) and good company (BG 18.78). A losing streak is a season (BG 2.14).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **BG 2.47:** "You have a right to perform your prescribed duty, but you are not entitled to the fruits of
  action…" *For me:* the effort is mine; the trophy is not.
- **BG 2.48:** "Perform your duty equipoised, O Arjuna, abandoning all attachment to success or failure. Such
  equanimity is called yoga." *For me:* the same shot at 0–0 and at match point.
- **BG 18.73:** "…my illusion is now gone. I have regained my memory by Your mercy. I am now firm and free from
  doubt…" *For me:* winning can happen before the result.

## S4. Words to know

- **Kurukshetra:** the field where the Gita was spoken; here, any field of hard choices.
- **Yoga (as BG 2.48 defines it):** evenness in success and failure while doing one's duty.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **If I'm not attached to results, why work hard?**
   Because BG 2.47 also forbids quitting ("never be attached to not doing your duty"). Attachment to the fruit
   is what makes you choke; releasing it is how you play your best.
2. **Isn't equanimity just not caring who wins?**
   Arjuna cared enough to fight a war. BG 2.48 is about the player's steadiness, which is what wins.
3. **Isn't asking for help weakness?**
   It is the first move of the Gita's hero (BG 2.7). And after asking, the choice is still yours (BG 18.63).
4. **Who is my "coach"?**
   Start with the text itself and someone who knows it and knows you. Nobody is asking you to accept anyone today.
5. **I keep losing. Am I doing life wrong?**
   Happiness and distress come and go like seasons (BG 2.14). Check the four rules, not the scoreboard.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. In BG 2.7, what does Arjuna ask Krsna to do?
2. BG 2.47 warns against two attachments. Name both.
3. How does BG 2.48 define yoga?
4. True or false: BG 18.63 says Krsna forces Arjuna to fight.
5. In your own words: what does "winning" mean in BG 18.73, and why can it happen before the result?

**Answers:** 1. To tell him for certain what is best for him and to instruct him (BG 2.7). 2. Attachment to the
fruits of action, and attachment to not doing one's duty (BG 2.47). 3. "Such equanimity is called yoga": doing
one's duty even in success and failure (BG 2.48). 4. False: "Deliberate on this fully, and then do what you wish
to do" (BG 18.63). 5. Clarity: illusion gone, memory regained, firm and free from doubt (BG 18.73); it depends on
the player's state, not on the scoreboard.
