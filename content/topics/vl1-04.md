## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on vl1-03 (Art of mind control): that talk gave BG 6.34-35 (the mind is hard to control but trainable) and the higher taste (BG 2.59). Here they are a one-minute reminder; this talk's own angle is the habit loop, the three levers and a written plan.

Habits are something every young person recognises, whatever they believe. IYF's notes (a seminar called
*Power of Habits* and a related *Self Management* deck) go from habit as a force, to how habits form (a cycle),
to the effects, to the body-and-self question, to what changes habits: the "scripts" we feed the mind (books,
movies, music, friends) and good habits such as mantra meditation and reading wisdom literature. This page keeps that flow.

## C. Central message

Habits start too weak to be felt and end too strong to be left, but they can be rebuilt. The Gita says everyone
is driven by acquired tendencies (BG 3.5), that the mind can be trained by practice and detachment (BG 6.35), and
that a higher taste is what loosens an old one (BG 2.59).

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. Describe in plain words how a habit forms (trigger, action, reward, repetition) and why it is hard to break.
2. Explain from BG 3.5 that we act under acquired tendencies, without making that an excuse.
3. Name three levers for changing a habit: environment (what we feed the mind), replacement (a higher taste), and regular practice.
4. Choose one habit to begin changing and one small first step.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** five references are enough for the talk (BG 3.5, 3.34, 6.35, 2.59, 6.17; NOI 3 is in R.1). The rest are for questions.

**BG 3.5**: Verified (exists; translation read) · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/5/
> "Everyone is forced to act helplessly according to the qualities he has acquired from the modes of material nature; therefore no one can refrain from doing something, not even for a moment."
- **Context:** Krsna explains why no one can simply stop acting.
- **Teaching:** Everyone acts under the qualities acquired from the modes of nature, "helplessly"; no one can
  stop acting even for a moment.
- **Application (preacher's):** Acknowledge the force of habit without blame: acting from deep tendency is part of
  the human condition. *(The verse speaks of the modes of nature; presenting it as a general account of habit is our application.)*
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 3.34**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/34/
> "There are principles to regulate attachment and aversion pertaining to the senses and their objects. One should not come under the control of such attachment and aversion, because they are stumbling blocks on the path of self-realization."
- **Teaching:** Likes and dislikes can be regulated by principles; we need not be governed by them.
- **Purport point:** Those not yet absorbed in Krsna consciousness should follow the scriptures' rules and
  regulations, yet not rely on them alone; attachment to Krsna's service is what finally detaches one.
- **Application (preacher's):** Regulation is the tool; a good schedule is an example of a regulating principle.
  *(Using this verse for the "environment" lever is our application; the verse speaks of principles that
  regulate likes and dislikes.)*
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 6.35**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/35/
> "…it is undoubtedly very difficult to curb the restless mind, but it is possible by suitable practice and by detachment."
- **Teaching:** The restless mind is very difficult to curb, but it can be curbed by suitable practice and by
  detachment.
- **Application (preacher's):** Habits are the mind's grooves; practice builds new ones. "Detachment" in the
  habit sense: not leaving the trigger on the table.
- **Use:** in the talk. The central verse. See also VL1-03.

**BG 2.59**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/59/
> "…ceasing such engagements by experiencing a higher taste, he is fixed in consciousness."
- **Teaching:** A taste is replaced by a higher taste, not only by an effort of will.
- **Application (preacher's):** Replacement is a lever: pair the old trigger with a better action. This lines up with IYF's slide "replace bad habits by good ones".
- **Use:** in the talk.

**NOI 3**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/3/
> "There are six principles favorable to the execution of pure devotional service: (1) being enthusiastic, (2) endeavoring with confidence, (3) being patient, (4) acting according to regulative principles…"
- **Teaching:** The full verse lists enthusiasm, confidence, patience and regulative principles among the six favourable principles for devotion. (Read the full translation before quoting; only the first four items are used here.)
- **Application (preacher's):** These are also good habit-building qualities: enthusiasm to start, confidence, patience through lapses, regular routine. At VL1, mention it in passing as the tradition's own recipe for a lasting practice.
- **Use:** in the talk, lightly.

**For questions (K):**
- **BG 6.17** (Verified): "He who is regulated in his habits of eating, sleeping, recreation and work can
  mitigate all material pains by practicing the yoga system." Helps with "where do I start?" Regulate the basics.
- **BG 2.62** (Verified): "While contemplating the objects of the senses, a person develops attachment for them,
  and from such attachment lust develops, and from lust anger arises." Links habit to what we dwell on (see VL1-03).

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** We are shaped by repeated action. The Gita sees the mind as strong, restless and moved by
   acquired qualities (BG 3.5, 6.34), yet trainable by practice and detachment (BG 6.35).
2. **Why it matters.** A single decision is easy; a repeated one is what a life is made of. A person who
   understands how a habit forms can design a better one.
3. **Connections.** To the mind (VL1-03), to happiness (VL1-01: likes vs good), and to regular sadhana at VL3.
4. **Consequences.** Someone who sees habit as a tool can start small and be patient with lapses.
5. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - **"It's all my karma / tendency, so I can't change."** BG 3.5 describes a force; BG 6.35 and 3.34 describe a way out. Both are in the Gita.
   - **"Willpower alone should do it."** BG 2.59 points to a higher taste, and the research in O shows habits depend on context.
   - **"One slip ruins it."** The research in O found that missing a single occasion did not materially affect habit formation.
   - **Shame and spiritual bypassing.** Addictions and compulsions need compassion, and sometimes treatment.
6. **Pitch for VL1.** "Habit", "trigger", "replace", "practice". No Sanskrit needed. No claim of
   authority: "Here is how an ancient text and modern research both treat this."

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet, give your name, and say today is about small things done daily, with no judgment.
2. Ask everyone to think of one habit they wish they had and one they wish they didn't (silently).
3. Ask H.2 question 1; take two or three answers and thank each.
4. Draw a loop on the board: trigger, action, reward; you return to it at minute 6.
5. Give the transition below and begin.

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the room's own life)
1. What is a small habit that has changed your day for the better?
2. What is the first thing you reach for when you wake up?
3. What habit did you drop, and what helped?

*Adapting:* "phone before sleep" can be swapped for betel, tea, television or a game, whichever your listeners know; for working adults, the first hour of the day; for those without screens, any daily routine.

- **Opening question:** "Think of one habit you wish you had and one you wish you didn't. Don't say them aloud.
  Now, which one is stronger?" Pause. "Today we'll see why, and what can be done."
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** Every night you tell yourself, "Just ten minutes on my phone." *(Invented
  case for discussion.)*
- **Transition:** "A saying in IYF's slides goes: *Habits start too weak to be felt and end too strong to be
  left.* Let's see how true that is." *(Popular saying; origin not verified.)*
- **Why it matters to this audience:** Exams, sleep, screens, fitness, friendships and substances are all habit questions.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **A habit is a repeated action that has become automatic.** IYF's dictionary-style definition: a settled
   or regular tendency, hard to give up. *Example:* tying your shoes. *Transition:* so how do habits form?
2. **They form through a loop.** Trigger → action → reward → repeat. *(IYF slide: "cycle of habit
   formation"; here described in plain terms.)* *Example:* boredom → phone → small pleasure → repeat. *Transition:* how strong is it?
3. **The force is real.** BG 3.5. *Example:* the hand that reaches for the phone before you decide. *Transition:* is there a way out?
4. **Three levers.** Environment (what we feed the mind: BG 3.34 on regulating attachment; IYF's "scripts"),
   replacement (BG 2.59: a higher taste), practice (BG 6.35). *Example:* put the phone out of reach; schedule a walk at the same time. *Transition:* how long will it take?
5. **Patience and routine.** BG 6.17 (see R.1 for NOI 3). *Example:* sleep and meals at fixed times. *Transition:* to the takeaway.

## J. Stories and illustrations

- **The "scripts" illustration (from IYF's slides):** the books we read, the films we watch, the music we
  hear and the friends we keep shape what we think and do. It is IYF's framing, not a verse. If used, ask the
  audience to identify their own "scripts".
- **"Garbage in, garbage out"** (IYF slide): a common computing saying used as a mnemonic.
- **From the Preaching Encyclopedia, IYF Books ch. 4 "Power of habits"** (HG Radheshyam Das, VOICE; added
  28 Oct 2026). The chapter is the fuller written form of IYF's slides: it has the same "scripts" list (books,
  films, music, friends) and the same GIGO line, and it also prints "Sow a thought… reap a destiny" without an
  author, which confirms treating it as a saying. Usable pieces:
  - the **word game**: take H from HABIT and "a bit" remains; take A and "bit" remains; take B and "it" is
    still there;
  - the sayings "habits are first cobwebs, then cables" and "too weak to be felt, too strong to be left"
    (traditional sayings, not scripture);
  - the **boy at the road crossing**: the mind says "just cross", the intelligence says "wait a few seconds";
    whichever is stronger decides. A clean picture of the chain in BG 2.62 and of the Gita's order of mind under
    intelligence (taught fully at VL1-09);
  - the **three S** for saying no: *starve* the habit (its objects and company), *supplement* it with good
    habits and good company, *stay away* from the tempting environment. These are the chapter's names for the
    three levers already in I.
  **Not taken:** its two long cautionary "stories" (a millionaire jogger, an engineering student) carry no source
  and name no one; a dog's-tail and a "king who stole" anecdote; a heart-disease percentage attributed to a

- **Contemporary:** the "ten minutes on my phone" scenario (invented).

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

> **Care note:** Someone in the room may be dealing with addiction (to substances, screens or pornography),
> self-harm or an eating disorder. This talk is about everyday habits. Do not ask people to disclose. If a
> participant describes something serious, speak privately, encourage them to see a doctor or counsellor,
> and say that professional help is compatible with spiritual life *(pastoral guidance, not a scriptural
> claim)*. Do not suggest that chanting replaces treatment.

1. **"If it's in my nature (BG 3.5), why try?"**
   - BG 3.5 describes a force; it is not a verdict. The same Gita gives practice and detachment (BG 6.35)
     and regulation (BG 3.34). Both are true.
2. **"How long does it take to build a habit?"**
   - Research says it varies a lot (O). Avoid promising a number of days.
3. **"Is it enough to just use willpower?"**
   - Willpower helps, but a higher taste and a changed environment help more (BG 2.59; BG 3.34).
4. **"Is the Gita's idea of habit the same as the psychologist's?"**
   - Not the same. The Gita speaks of tendencies from the modes of nature and the pull of the senses; psychology
     studies behaviour and context. They meet at the practical point: repetition shapes us, and change is possible.
5. **"Will chanting break my habits?"**
   - IYF's slides recommend mantra meditation as a good habit. At VL1, offer it as something to try, not as a
     promise. Do not quote testimonials from the slides (unchecked).
6. **"I've tried and failed many times."**
   - *Pastoral guidance:* one lapse is not the end; start again smaller; speak to a trusted person; get help if needed.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The morning alarm.** Neha wants to wake early to study, but each day she snoozes the alarm and then
feels guilty. She says, "I'm just a lazy person."
- **Principles:** BG 3.5 (tendencies exert force); BG 6.17 (regulate sleep); BG 6.35 (practice).
- **Questions:** What is the trigger? What could change the evening before? How should she think about a slip?
- **Suggested analysis:** She changes the environment (phone across the room, earlier bedtime), starts with
  small steps and treats a missed day as information.
- **Takeaway:** A habit is built by design and patience; "lazy" is a label, not a plan.

**Case 2: The friend group.** Arjun wants to cut down on late-night gaming but his friends play every night.
- **Principles:** BG 3.34 (regulating attachment); BG 2.59 (a higher taste); BG 6.17 (regulated habits).
- **Questions:** What can he change alone? What can he do about the group? What might replace the evening?
- **Takeaway:** Environment includes people; changing a habit may mean negotiating or adding a new circle.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first; praise every try)
1. What is a habit? *Something done so often it becomes automatic.*
2. Name the three levers. *Environment, replacement (higher taste), practice.*
3. What does BG 3.5 say about acquired tendencies? *Everyone is pushed to act by them; it is a real force.*
4. What does BG 6.35 add? *The restless mind can still be trained by practice and detachment.*
5. A phone beside the bed: which lever first? *Environment: move the phone away.*
6. Does one missed day ruin a habit? *No; start again smaller (see O).*

**M.2 Activity (about 8 minutes): "One habit, one trigger, one step"**
1. 2 min: each writes one habit to build, its trigger (time, place, cue) and its reward.
2. 2 min: in pairs, each adjusts the environment (move, hide, prepare) to make the first step easy.
3. 3 min: volunteers share their first step; thank and praise each; sharing the habit itself is optional.
4. 1 min: fix when to check in (day 3 and day 7).

**M.3 Reflection**
- **Reflection:** Which "scripts" (books, films, music, friends) are shaping you most now?
- **Application (private, optional):** Pick one habit. What is its trigger? What would a better action in the same place be? *(Nobody needs to share.)*

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

A habit is something we do so often that it becomes automatic. It runs in a loop: a trigger (a time, place or feeling), an action, and a reward. Habits can start too small to notice and become hard to leave, but they can be rebuilt. This is true for a student with a phone, a farmer with a morning routine and a manager with an inbox.

The Bhagavad-gita says we are often pushed to act by tendencies we have acquired (BG 3.5), so the pull is real and nobody needs to feel ashamed of it. It also says the restless mind can be brought under control by practice and detachment (BG 6.35). Three things help. Change your surroundings, so the old trigger is harder to meet (BG 3.34 speaks of regulating likes and dislikes). Replace the old habit with something better, because a higher taste lets the lower fall away (BG 2.59). And practise patiently and regularly (BG 6.17).

Research finds that how long a habit takes varies a lot between people, and that missing one day does not undo your effort. Start again, smaller. Write down one habit, one trigger and one first step. If a habit is an addiction, talk to a doctor or counsellor.

## S2. Key ideas

- A habit is an action repeated until it is automatic.
- Habits are cued by triggers and kept by small rewards.
- The Gita is honest about the force of acquired tendencies (BG 3.5).
- Change comes from environment, a higher taste and regular practice (BG 3.34, 2.59, 6.35).
- Patience matters; one slip is not the end.

## S3. Verses to remember

- **BG 3.5:** Everyone acts under acquired tendencies and "no one can refrain from doing something". *For me:* habits are strong; I need not blame myself.
- **BG 6.35:** The restless mind can be curbed "by suitable practice and by detachment". *For me:* change is possible.
- **BG 2.59:** A higher taste helps one let go. *For me:* replace, don't only remove.

## S4. Words to know

- **Habit:** an action repeated until it is automatic.
- **Trigger:** what sets a habit off (a time, a place, a feeling).
- **Detachment:** not feeding what pulls you.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **If it's my nature, why try?** BG 3.5 describes a force; the Gita also gives a way to change (BG 6.35).
2. **How long does a habit take?** It varies a lot between people and habits. Don't trust a fixed number of days.
3. **Is willpower enough?** It helps. A changed environment and a better replacement help more.
4. **Will chanting break my habits?** You can try it as a good habit. It is not a promise, and it does not replace medical help for addictions.
5. **I've failed many times.** Start again smaller. Talk to someone you trust, and seek professional help if needed.
