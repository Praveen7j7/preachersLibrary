# Chanting beyond the count: from namabhasa towards suddha-nama

> **TRD · up to 180 min · review: drafted (30 Oct 2026).** For temple residents who have chanted sixteen rounds every day
> for years. The ten offences themselves are DPS-06; Haridasa Thakura and the prostitute is BSS-12; *Harinama-cintamani*
> in depth is TRD-36. This page asks one question only: after the count is settled, how does the chanting itself grow?

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on DPS-06 (what the ten offences are) and DPS-15 (how steadiness grows): this page does not re-list the offences or re-draw the stages; it takes a resident who already keeps his vow and asks what the vow is for.

A newcomer's problem with japa is finishing the rounds. A resident's problem is different: the rounds are finished every day, often early, and still the mind wanders, the taste is thin, and a quiet voice says "this is as far as it goes". Srila Prabhupada's books answer that voice precisely. The holy name has stages. The count is the floor of the first stage, not the ceiling of the last.

## C. Central message

Sixteen rounds are the vow that keeps us within reach of the holy name; they are not the goal. The goal is the pure name, *suddha-nama*, which "awakens one's ecstatic love for the lotus feet of Krsna" (CC Antya 3.178). Between offensive chanting and the pure name lies *namabhasa*, the "faint light" of the name, which already gives liberation (CC Antya 3.63, 3.186) but not love. Our chanting moves along that road by one thing only: hearing the name without offence, attentively and humbly, day after day.

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Define nama-aparadha, namabhasa and suddha-nama from Haridasa Thakura's words in CC Antya 3, and say what each gives.
2. Explain why Srila Prabhupada fixed sixteen rounds "as the minimum" (CC Antya 11.23 purport), and why the count is a vow and not a measure of advancement.
3. State the two tests of chanting that CC Adi 8.29 gives (love develops; tears come) without turning them into a show.
4. Name the four kinds of unintentional namabhasa and the one kind with faith, as *Harinama-cintamani* gives them (edition named).
5. Choose one concrete change to their own japa for the next month, drawn from NOI 7, CC Antya 20.21 and the purport to CC Antya 11.23.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**CC Antya 3.53, 3.55–56**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/3/55/
> 3.55: "The chanting of the Lord's holy name to indicate something other than the Lord is an instance of nāmābhāsa. Even when the holy name is chanted in this way, its transcendental power is not destroyed."
- **Context:** Lord Caitanya asks Haridasa Thakura how the *yavanas* (people outside Vedic culture) will be delivered. Haridasa answers that because they say "hā rāma, hā rāma", they "will very easily be delivered by this nāmābhāsa" (3.53), and quotes the verse about the *mleccha* killed by a boar who cries "hā rāma" and attains liberation (3.56).
- **Teaching:** The holy name acts even when the chanter means something else. This is namabhasa.
- **Use:** in the talk, as the first definition.

**CC Antya 3.59–61, 3.63**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/3/59/
> 3.59: "The letters of the holy name have so much spiritual potency that they act even when uttered improperly."
> 3.63: "Even a faint light from the holy name of the Lord can eradicate all the reactions of sinful life."
- **Purport point (3.59):** Srila Prabhupada cites Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura: "improperly uttered" does not mean "negligent utterance for the sense gratification of materialistic persons", which "is not a vibration of transcendental sound"; "utterance of the holy name while one engages in sense gratification is an impediment on the path toward achieving ecstatic love for Kṛṣṇa". But "if one who is eager for devotional service utters the holy name even partially or improperly", the name "exhibits its spiritual potency because of that person's offenseless utterance".
- **Purport point (3.60):** the Padma Purana verse, quoted in *Hari-bhakti-vilasa* 11.289, with Sanatana Gosvami's gloss: if one "hears, utters or remembers the holy name, or if it catches his mind while coming near his ears", the name "will act". The verse itself adds the limit: chanting "for the benefit of the material body, for material wealth and followers, or under the influence of greed or atheism — in other words, if one utters the name with offenses — such chanting will not produce the desired result very soon."
- **Use:** in the talk; the purport to 3.59 is the hinge of the whole session (eagerness versus negligence).

**CC Antya 3.178, 3.180–181, 3.183–186**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/3/178/
> 3.178: "These two benedictions are not the true result of chanting the holy name. Actually, by chanting the holy name without offenses, one awakens his ecstatic love for the lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa."
> 3.186: "Liberation is the insignificant result derived from a glimpse of the awakening of offenseless chanting of the holy name."
- **Context:** Scholars at Hiranya and Govardhana Majumadara's house say that chanting frees one from sin and gives liberation. Haridasa corrects them: these are "concomitant by-products" (3.180), "gleams of morning sunlight". He gives the sunrise example: before the sun is seen, darkness goes and fear of thieves and ghosts ends (3.183–184); when it rises, everyone does his duty. "Similarly, the first hint that offenseless chanting... has awakened dissipates the reactions of sinful life immediately. And when one chants the holy name offenselessly, one awakens to service in ecstatic love at the lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa" (3.185).
- **Purport point (3.181):** the sun verse is Padyavali 16, compiled by Rupa Gosvami.
- **Use:** in the talk, as the map of the three stages.

**CC Antya 3.100**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/3/100/
> "...in front of the tulasī he would chant the holy name of the Lord 300,000 times daily. He chanted throughout the entire day and night."
- **Purport point:** "One should not, however, imitate Haridāsa Ṭhākura, for no one else can chant the holy name 300,000 times a day. Such chanting is for the mukta-puruṣa, or liberated soul. We can follow his example, however, by chanting sixteen rounds of the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra on beads every day and offering respect to the tulasī plant... chanting the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra with a vow before the tulasī plant has such great spiritual potency that simply by doing this one can become spiritually strong. Therefore we request the members of the Hare Kṛṣṇa movement to follow Haridāsa Ṭhākura's example rigidly."
- **Use:** in the talk; the vow with tulasi is the standard, and "rigidly" is Prabhupada's own word.

**CC Antya 11.17, 11.19, 11.23–24**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/11/23/
> 11.23 (Haridasa, in the purport's rendering of the Bengali): "My disease is that I cannot complete my rounds."
- **Context:** Govinda brings maha-prasadam; Haridasa is "lying on his back and chanting his rounds very slowly" (11.17). He says, "I have not finished chanting my regular number of rounds. How, then, can I eat?" (11.19). The next day Lord Caitanya asks about his disease; the Lord then says that being old and already liberated he "may reduce the number of rounds" (11.24).
- **Purport point (11.23):** "If one cannot complete the fixed number of rounds he is assigned, he should be considered to be in a diseased condition of spiritual life... Of course, we cannot imitate Haridāsa Ṭhākura, but everyone must chant a prescribed number of rounds. In our Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement we have fixed sixteen rounds as the minimum so that the Westerners will not feel burdened. These sixteen rounds must be chanted, and chanted loudly, so that one can hear himself and others."
- **Purport point (11.24):** "Unless one has come to the platform of spontaneous love of God, he must follow the regulative principles... The first regulative principle is that one must chant the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra loudly enough so that he can hear himself, and one must vow to chant a fixed number of rounds."
- **Application (preacher's):** the count is a vow, not a measure of advancement: Haridasa at the end of his life still calls an unfinished count a disease; the Lord alone may lift the count, for a liberated soul.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**CC Adi 8.26, 8.29, 8.31**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/adi/8/29-30/
> 8.29: "If one chants the exalted holy name of the Lord again and again and yet his love for the Supreme Lord does not develop and tears do not appear in his eyes, it is evident that because of his offenses in chanting, the seed of the holy name of Kṛṣṇa does not sprout."
- **Teaching:** The test of chanting is love awakening (8.26: offenceless chanting "vanquishes all sinful activities. Thus pure devotional service... becomes manifest"). Where it does not come after long chanting, the cause named is offence.
- **Purport point (8.31):** Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura: one who takes shelter of Lord Caitanya and Nityananda, "follows Their instructions to become more tolerant than the tree and humbler than the grass, and in this way chants the holy name of the Lord, very soon he achieves the platform of transcendental loving service to the Lord, and tears appear in his eyes." "There are offenses to be considered in chanting the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra, but there are no such considerations in chanting the names of Gaura-Nityānanda."
- **Application (preacher's):** this verse is a mirror for oneself, never a measuring stick for another's eyes. Tears are not to be produced; love is to be sought.
- **Use:** in the talk, with the care in K.3.

**CC Madhya 15.106–107, 15.109**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/15/106/
> 15.109: "By chanting the holy name of the Lord, one dissolves his entanglement in material activities. After this, one becomes very much attracted to Kṛṣṇa, and thus dormant love for Kṛṣṇa is awakened."
- **Purport point (15.106):** the Padma Purana statement that the name "is identical with Kṛṣṇa and is like a cintāmaṇi gem"; "Having such faith, one must continue to chant the holy name."
- **Purport point (15.107):** "As for being freed from all sinful reactions by chanting the holy name of Kṛṣṇa only once, one must chant the holy name without committing any offenses. Then chanting one name is sufficient."
- **Use:** in the talk (why the name's power is never the problem; the offence is).

**CC Antya 4.71**: Verified. "Of the nine processes of devotional service, the most important is to always chant the holy name of the Lord. If one does so, avoiding the ten kinds of offenses, one very easily obtains the most valuable love of Godhead." *Purport:* "By humility and meekness one attracts the attention of Kṛṣṇa." *Use:* in the talk.

**CC Antya 20.16, 20.19, 20.21**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/20/16/
> 20.16: "...I am so unfortunate that I commit offenses while chanting the holy name, and therefore I do not achieve attachment for chanting."
> 20.21: "One who thinks himself lower than the grass, who is more tolerant than a tree, and who does not expect personal honor but is always prepared to give all respect to others can very easily always chant the holy name of the Lord."
- **Teaching:** Lord Caitanya Himself speaks the resident's exact complaint, "I have no attachment for chanting Your holy names" (20.19), and names its cause (offence), then gives the mood that makes constant chanting easy (20.21). The whole prayer is TRD-12; here only verses 2 and 3.
- **Use:** in the talk and as the closing meditation.

**CC Adi 17.22; SB 2.1.11; SB 6.2.14; SB 1.2.17**: Verified. The name is Krishna's incarnation in Kali-yuga; chanting it "one associates with the Lord directly" (CC Adi 17.22). Constant chanting "after the ways of the great authorities is the doubtless and fearless way of success for all" (SB 2.1.11). Even chanting "indirectly, jokingly, for musical entertainment, or even neglectfully" frees one from sins (SB 6.2.14: the scriptural root of namabhasa). Krishna "cleanses desire for material enjoyment from the heart of the devotee who has developed the urge to hear His messages" (SB 1.2.17; its purport says the Siksastaka "declares clearly that the holy name of the Lord has all the potencies of the Lord"). *Use:* background and one line each in the talk.

**NOI 7; NOI 8; BG 9.14**: Verified. The tongue with "the jaundice of avidyā" cannot taste the name's sweetness, yet "by carefully chanting these sweet names every day, a natural relish awakens" (NOI 7). "The essence of all advice is that one should utilize one's full time – twenty-four hours a day – in nicely chanting and remembering the Lord's divine name" (NOI 8). The great souls are "always chanting My glories, endeavoring with great determination" (BG 9.14). *Use:* in the talk (the remedy for no taste is more careful chanting, not less).

***Harinama-cintamani* (Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura)**: acharya's book; principles above rest on Prabhupada's books. Edition cited: *Hari-nama-cintamani & Nama-bhajana*, tr. Bhanu Swami, World Sankirtana Party (ISKCON Desire Tree compilation; text in `research/books/Hari_Nama_Cintamani_Nama_Bhajana.txt`). Chapter numbers are this edition's.
- **Ch. 2 "Accepting the Holy Name":** "That person is called a Vaishnava who takes the holy name in faith and pure intention; and that person is called a semi-Vaishnava who takes the name, but with the impurity of namabhasa. However, by the mercy of Krishna, the semi-Vaishnava gradually becomes a pure Vaishnava." "If the name is chanted in namabhasa (impurely) the benefit will come slowly... By the process of namabhasa chanting, sins are gradually destroyed, until the name is chanted and heard purely."
- **Ch. 3 "Namabhasa":** "Namabhasa as unintentional pious activity may be classed into four types: sanketa, parihasa, stobha, and hela": indirectly (Ajamila calling his son Narayana; the yavana's "haram"), in joke (Jarasandha), in derision (Sisupala), in disregard "without attention". "These types of namabhasa... being without real faith in Krishna, are situated on the lower border of namabhasa. On the other hand, if one has genuine faith in Krishna and takes to the holy name while anarthas are still present, that stage is called sraddha-namabhasa." "By chanting in namabhasa one can get everything except prema." "If one gets to the point of sraddha-namabhasa, quickly the anarthas will go and the pure name will be revealed."
- **Ch. 12 "Inattention While Chanting":** "It is from this offense that all other offenses spring. The wise men recognize three types of inattention: indifference or no attention, laziness, and distraction." "He may chant 64 rounds counting strictly on his japa beads, but in his heart he has not received one drop of the taste of the name." Remedies given: chant in the company of Vaisnavas who chant properly, near tulasi, "gradually increase the period of chanting"; or chant "in a room by oneself with the door locked".
- **Application (preacher's):** the resident who keeps his vow with faith is, in this book's own word, at *sraddha-namabhasa*: not a failure, not yet the goal, and "quickly" moving if attention and humility grow.
- **Note for the head:** the two other *Harinama-cintamani* files in `research/books/` (Desire Tree PDFs) have a scrambled font and are unreadable; only this compilation was used.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The name has stages because the chanter has coverings, not because the name varies.** The name is Krishna Himself (CC Madhya 15.106 purport; CC Adi 17.22). Nama-aparadha, namabhasa and suddha-nama describe how much of that name reaches a covered heart. Haridasa's sunrise (CC Antya 3.183–185) is the whole doctrine: pre-dawn light already ends fear and darkness (sins gone, liberation possible); the risen sun lets everyone act (love and service awaken).
2. **Why the count comes first and stays.** Without a fixed number chanted aloud, there is no regulated hearing at all (CC Antya 11.23–24 purports). Prabhupada fixed sixteen "as the minimum" so that nobody "will feel burdened", and asked members to follow Haridasa "rigidly" (CC Antya 3.100 purport). Haridasa himself calls an unfinished count a disease. The count is therefore never belittled on this page. It is the floor.
3. **Why the count alone is not enough.** *Harinama-cintamani* ch. 12 says a man may chant sixty-four rounds "but in his heart he has not received one drop of the taste of the name", and names the cause: inattention, "from which all other offenses spring". CC Adi 8.29 says the same from the other side: long chanting without love growing shows offence.
4. **What moves chanting forward.** Srila Prabhupada's books name four things, and all four are in the resident's hands: (a) *offenceless hearing*: "chanted loudly, so that one can hear himself" (CC Antya 11.23 purport); (b) *attention and care*: "carefully chanting these sweet names every day" (NOI 7); (c) *humility*: "lower than the grass" (CC Antya 20.21), "by humility and meekness one attracts the attention of Kṛṣṇa" (CC Antya 4.71 purport); (d) *the vow kept with tulasi* (CC Antya 3.100 purport). None of them is a new technique; all four are the old instructions taken seriously.
5. **Eagerness is the dividing line.** The purport to CC Antya 3.59 distinguishes the person "eager for devotional service" whose imperfect utterance still acts, from "negligent utterance for the sense gratification of materialistic persons", which "is not a vibration of transcendental sound". A resident's japa therefore depends less on his skill than on what he wants while he chants.
6. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *"My sixteen are done; I have done my chanting."* Done the vow, yes. NOI 8: the essence of all advice is to use twenty-four hours in chanting and remembering.
   - *"Namabhasa means my chanting is useless."* No: namabhasa gives "everything except prema" (*Harinama-cintamani* ch. 3); liberation is its "insignificant" by-product (CC Antya 3.186). It is a stage on the road.
   - *"Tears and trembling prove pure chanting."* CC Adi 8.29 gives tears as a sign of love that has come; producing them is not the sign. NOD 16 (TRD-13) warns against imitation.
   - *"More rounds will fix it."* Haridasa's 300,000 is "for the mukta-puruṣa" and not to be imitated (CC Antya 3.100 purport). Bhaktivinoda recommends increasing gradually, and first fixing attention.
7. **Pitch for TRD.** Speak as one chanter to others. Nobody in the room is graded; everyone is located. The tone of CC Antya 20.19, "I have no attachment for chanting Your holy names", spoken by the Lord Himself, is the tone of the session.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet everyone and say: "This session is about our own japa. Nobody will be asked to say anything about his heart; everything personal is written privately."
2. Define the three words once, on the board: *nama-aparadha* (chanting with offence), *namabhasa* (the shadow or faint light of the name), *suddha-nama* (the pure name).
3. Ask H.2 question 1 for private writing (two minutes).
4. Ask H.2 question 2 and take three or four general answers; thank each.
5. Say: "Haridasa Thakura, at the end of his life, called an unfinished count a disease. We start there."

**H.2 Opening questions**
1. (Private) When you finished your rounds this morning, what was your mind doing during the last four?
2. What do you think the sixteen rounds are for? One sentence each.
3. If a new devotee asked you, "Is chanting without taste still chanting?", what would you answer?

## I. Core teaching points

1. **The count is a vow, and the vow stands.** CC Antya 11.19, 11.23–24 and the purport to 3.100. *Example:* Haridasa refuses to eat before his rounds; the Lord alone lifts the count, and only for a liberated soul. *Transition:* then what is the vow for?
2. **The name has three stages; the vow serves the third.** CC Antya 3.178–186: sin removed and liberation are by-products, "gleams of morning sunlight"; the real fruit is love. *Example:* the sunrise, in Haridasa's own words. *Transition:* where is the ordinary resident on this road?
3. **Namabhasa is a real stage with real fruit, and faith changes it.** CC Antya 3.53–63; SB 6.2.14; *Harinama-cintamani* ch. 3: four unintentional kinds, and *sraddha-namabhasa* for the faithful chanter still carrying anarthas, from which "quickly the anarthas will go". *Transition:* so what holds us there?
4. **Inattention is the root offence.** *Harinama-cintamani* ch. 12: indifference, laziness, distraction; sixty-four rounds with "not one drop of the taste". CC Adi 8.29: no love after long chanting shows offence. *Transition:* the remedy.
5. **Four things move chanting forward.** Hear yourself (CC Antya 11.23 purport); chant carefully every day (NOI 7); be lower than the grass (CC Antya 20.21; CC Antya 4.71 purport); keep the vow before tulasi (CC Antya 3.100 purport). *Example:* Bhaktivinoda's two settings, with Vaisnavas or alone behind a locked door. *Transition:* the Lord's own prayer.
6. **The Lord prays our prayer.** CC Antya 20.16, 20.19: "I have no attachment for chanting Your holy names." Then 20.21. *Transition:* P.

## J. Stories and illustrations

**Haridasa Thakura and the yavanas** (CC Antya 3.49–65). The Lord says that most people in Kali-yuga, bereft of Vedic culture, are "concerned only with killing cows and brahminical culture", and asks how they will be delivered (3.50–51). Haridasa says they are "accustomed to saying 'hā rāma, hā rāma'" and "will very easily be delivered by this nāmābhāsa" (3.53), quotes the mleccha killed by a boar (3.56), recalls Ajamila calling his son Narayana (3.57), explains that "rā" and "ma" are unseparated and decorated with the loving word "hā" (3.58), and concludes that even "a faint light from the holy name" eradicates all sinful reactions (3.63). *Lesson:* the name's power does not depend on the chanter; what depends on the chanter is how much of the fruit he receives.

**Haridasa and the scholars** (CC Antya 3.177–186). At the Majumadaras' assembly, scholars give liberation and freedom from sin as the fruit of chanting. Haridasa protests: these are by-products; the fruit is "ecstatic love for the lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa" (3.178). He recites the sunrise verse (3.181) and asks the scholars to explain it; they ask him; he gives the pre-dawn/sunrise explanation (3.183–186). *Lesson:* even learned men stop at liberation; the namacarya does not.

**Haridasa's last days** (CC Antya 11.16–25). Lying on his back, chanting slowly, he will not eat before his rounds; asked about his disease, "My disease is that I cannot complete my rounds." The Lord allows him to reduce the count because he is old and liberated and "has sufficiently preached the glories of the holy name". *Lesson:* the vow is held to the end; the Lord, not the devotee, relaxes it.

**Haridasa and the prostitute** is BSS-12 (and used in DPS-15 for steadiness); refer to it in one sentence, do not retell.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"I have chanted sixteen rounds for eight years. Honestly, where am I?"**
   - Nobody can place another. *Harinama-cintamani* ch. 3 gives the name for a faithful chanter with anarthas still present: *sraddha-namabhasa*. From there the book says anarthas go "quickly" if attention and humility grow. Use CC Adi 8.29 as a private mirror, not as a verdict.
2. **"If namabhasa already gives liberation, why worry about offences?"**
   - Because liberation is the "insignificant result" (CC Antya 3.186); the aim is love, and offence blocks love, not liberation (CC Antya 3.60: with offence "such chanting will not produce the desired result very soon"). DPS-06 covers the ten.
3. **"Some devotees cry during japa. Should I?"**
   - CC Adi 8.29 names tears as a sign that love has come; it does not ask anyone to produce them. NOD 16 (see TRD-13) calls imitated symptoms "mere imitation" with "no value". Chant carefully (NOI 7) and let Krishna decide the signs.
4. **"Would 32 or 64 rounds help?"**
   - Prabhupada: Haridasa's 300,000 is "for the mukta-puruṣa", not to be imitated (CC Antya 3.100 purport). Bhaktivinoda: sixty-four rounds with inattention yield "not one drop". Fix attention first; then, with your authority, increase gradually (*Harinama-cintamani* ch. 12).
5. **"I chant faster to finish before service. Is that wrong?"**
   - The purport to CC Antya 11.23 asks that the rounds be "chanted loudly, so that one can hear himself and others". If speed removes hearing, the first regulative principle is lost (11.24 purport). Arrange the time with your authority rather than the hearing.
6. **"Chanting the names of Gaura-Nityananda has no offences, so why not chant only that?"**
   - The purport to CC Adi 8.31 says one first takes shelter of Gaura-Nityananda and then comes to Radha-Krishna; it is the order of approach, not a replacement for the maha-mantra, which the same purport says one goes on chanting.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The early finisher.** Govinda dasa, eleven years in the ashram, finishes sixteen rounds by 6:15 every day and is proud that he has never missed. He admits, privately, that he could not say one thing his mind did during the rounds.
- **Dilemma:** Is his chanting healthy? What would Haridasa Thakura and Bhaktivinoda Thakura each say to him?
- **Principles:** CC Antya 11.23 purport (the vow is kept: good); *Harinama-cintamani* ch. 12 (indifference is inattention); NOI 7 (care).
- **Questions:** What has Govinda kept? What is missing? Which one change would he make for one month?
- **Takeaway:** keep the vow exactly as it is; add hearing. Bhaktivinoda's locked room, or japa beside a devotee who chants attentively, for two of the sixteen rounds daily.

**Case 2: The discouraged preacher.** Nitai dasa travels and preaches well. His rounds are chanted on buses and in queues, often late at night. He says: "After so long I feel nothing; perhaps the name is not for me."
- **Principles:** CC Antya 3.59 purport (eagerness, not perfection, is what the name answers); CC Antya 20.19 (the Lord Himself says "I have no attachment"); NOI 7 (no taste is the disease, not the medicine).
- **Questions:** Which half of his sentence is honest and which half is a conclusion the scriptures do not draw? What does the purport to CC Antya 3.59 say about imperfect chanting by an eager person?
- **Takeaway:** he has reached the exact point at which the Siksastaka prays. Chant the second verse with him (TRD-12).

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first; thank every answer)
1. What is namabhasa? *Expected:* chanting the name indirectly, imperfectly or without full faith; "a faint light" of the name.
2. What does namabhasa give, and what does it not give? *Expected:* freedom from sin and liberation; not prema (CC Antya 3.186; HNC ch. 3).
3. What did Haridasa call an unfinished count? *Expected:* a disease (CC Antya 11.23).
4. Why did Prabhupada fix sixteen rounds? *Expected:* as the minimum, so that no one feels burdened; to be chanted loudly enough to hear oneself (11.23 purport).
5. Which offence does Bhaktivinoda call the root of the others? *Expected:* inattention (pramada), in three forms.
6. What mood makes constant chanting easy? *Expected:* lower than the grass, more tolerant than a tree, no personal honour, respect to all (CC Antya 20.21).

**M.2 Activities**
- **Option 1: Two rounds, heard (20 min).** Everyone chants two rounds aloud together at a pace at which each syllable is heard, as the purport to CC Antya 11.23 asks. Then pairs answer only: "What was different from this morning?" Praise honest observation.
- **Option 2: The three inattentions (20 min).** Three groups each take one of Bhaktivinoda's inattentions (indifference, laziness, distraction) and write two everyday ashram situations in which it appears and one remedy from the page (HNC ch. 12; NOI 7; CC Antya 20.21). Groups report; the preacher adds the verse where a group gave only a technique.

**M.3 Reflection**
- Why does Haridasa call liberation "insignificant"? What would change in my japa if I believed that?
- (Private) Which of the four things in F.4 is weakest in my japa this month?

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Most of us settled our sixteen rounds long ago. Then a quieter question comes: the rounds are finished every day, but the mind still wanders and the taste is thin. Haridasa Thakura, the namacarya (the teacher of the holy name), answers it in Caitanya-caritamrta. The holy name has stages. When the name is chanted with offence (*nama-aparadha*), its fruit is delayed (CC Antya 3.60). When it is chanted indirectly, imperfectly or without full faith, that is *namabhasa*, "a faint light from the holy name", and even that "can eradicate all the reactions of sinful life" (CC Antya 3.63) and gives liberation. But liberation, Haridasa says, is only "the insignificant result" (CC Antya 3.186). The pure name, *suddha-nama*, "awakens one's ecstatic love for the lotus feet of Krsna" (CC Antya 3.178). He compares it to sunrise: before the sun is seen, darkness and fear already go; when it rises, everyone begins his work (CC Antya 3.183–185).

The count stays. At the end of his life Haridasa, chanting slowly on his back, would not eat before his rounds and called an unfinished count his "disease" (CC Antya 11.19, 11.23). Srila Prabhupada writes that we cannot imitate Haridasa's 300,000 names, but "everyone must chant a prescribed number of rounds"; sixteen were fixed "as the minimum", to be "chanted loudly, so that one can hear himself" (CC Antya 11.23 purport; 3.100 purport). The count is the floor, not the goal.

What moves chanting forward is in our own hands: hearing ourselves, chanting "carefully... every day" (NOI 7), thinking ourselves "lower than the grass" (CC Antya 20.21), and keeping the vow before tulasi. Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura, in *Harinama-cintamani*, calls inattention the offence "from which all other offenses spring", and says a faithful chanter who still has bad habits is at *sraddha-namabhasa*, from which "quickly the anarthas will go and the pure name will be revealed". Lord Caitanya Himself prays our prayer: "I am so unfortunate that I have no attachment for chanting Your holy names" (CC Antya 20.19).

## S2. Key ideas

- The holy name has three stages: with offence, as a faint light (namabhasa), and pure (suddha-nama) (CC Antya 3.59–63, 3.178–186).
- Namabhasa removes sin and gives liberation; only the pure name gives love (CC Antya 3.186).
- Sixteen rounds are the minimum Prabhupada fixed, to be heard; an unfinished count is a "disease" (CC Antya 11.23 and purport).
- Inattention is the root offence (*Harinama-cintamani* ch. 12); no love after long chanting shows offence (CC Adi 8.29).
- Four things move chanting forward: hearing, daily care (NOI 7), humility (CC Antya 20.21), the vow kept (CC Antya 3.100 purport).
- Signs such as tears are given by Krishna, never produced by us (CC Adi 8.29; see TRD-13).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **CC Antya 3.186:** "Liberation is the insignificant result derived from a glimpse of the awakening of offenseless chanting of the holy name." *For me:* I am chanting for love, not for release.
- **CC Antya 3.63:** "Even a faint light from the holy name of the Lord can eradicate all the reactions of sinful life." *For me:* my imperfect chanting is not wasted.
- **CC Antya 20.21:** "One who thinks himself lower than the grass, who is more tolerant than a tree, and who does not expect personal honor but is always prepared to give all respect to others can very easily always chant the holy name of the Lord." *For me:* humility is the japa technique.
- **NOI 7:** "simply by carefully chanting these sweet names every day, a natural relish awakens within his tongue." *For me:* no taste means keep taking the medicine carefully.

## S4. Words to know

- **Nama-aparadha:** chanting with one of the ten offences (DPS-06); the fruit is delayed.
- **Namabhasa:** the shadow or "faint light" of the name: chanting indirectly, imperfectly, or with faith but with bad habits still present (*sraddha-namabhasa*).
- **Suddha-nama:** the pure name, which awakens love for Krishna.
- **Namacarya:** "teacher of the holy name", the title of Haridasa Thakura.
- **Pramada:** inattention while chanting; indifference, laziness or distraction (*Harinama-cintamani* ch. 12).

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Is my chanting useless if I have no taste?** No. Even a faint light of the name removes sin (CC Antya 3.63). The lack of taste is the disease of the tongue, and the cure is to go on chanting carefully (NOI 7).
2. **Should I chant more rounds?** First chant the sixteen so that you hear every name (CC Antya 11.23 purport). Bhaktivinoda Thakura says one can chant sixty-four rounds and get "not one drop of the taste" if inattentive. Increase only gradually and with your authority's advice.
3. **Some devotees weep while chanting. Must I?** No. Tears are a sign that love has come (CC Adi 8.29), not something to make. Imitated signs have "no value" (NOD 16).
4. **Why did Haridasa call liberation insignificant?** Because the real fruit of the name is love for Krishna and service to Him (CC Antya 3.178, 3.185); liberation comes by the way, like the light before sunrise.
5. **What one thing changes chanting most?** Attention with humility. Bhaktivinoda calls inattention the root of all offences; Lord Caitanya says the humble can chant always (CC Antya 20.21).

2. True or false: namabhasa gives love of God.
3. What did Haridasa Thakura say his disease was (CC Antya 11.23)?
4. Why did Srila Prabhupada fix sixteen rounds, and how are they to be chanted?
5. Which offence does Bhaktivinoda Thakura call the root of the others?
6. Complete: "One who thinks himself lower than the ___, who is more tolerant than a ___..."

**Answers:** 1. Nama-aparadha, namabhasa, suddha-nama. 2. False: it gives freedom from sin and liberation; love comes from the pure name (CC Antya 3.186). 3. "My disease is that I cannot complete my rounds." 4. As the minimum, so that no one feels burdened; chanted loudly enough to hear oneself (CC Antya 11.23 purport). 5. Inattention (pramada), in three forms: indifference, laziness, distraction (*Harinama-cintamani* ch. 12). 6. grass; tree (CC Antya 20.21).
