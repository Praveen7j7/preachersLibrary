# 21. Regulation

> Status line: BSS (brahmacari training) · 120 min · drafted · not sensitive. Based on chapter 21 of BIKC. Builds on bss-44 (what the daily practice contains) and bss-27 (discipline in general): this page is only about *doing the same thing at the same time*, why it is the basis of sadhana, and how rules stay servants of remembering Krishna. SB 11.18.36 is BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level
BIKC calls regulation "the basis of steady sadhana": without it, proper sadhana is impossible. A brahmacari who cannot rely on a fixed time to rise has to win a new argument with his mind every morning. IYF's own description of the BSS batch is "maintaining strictly the initiation vows" and "following the principles of brahmachari life" (research/batch-criteria.md); regulation is how strictness becomes ordinary.

## C. Central message
Regulation is how the mind is trained to serve: the same acts, in the same order, at the same time, until they are natural, as a typist learns the keys (CC Madhya 22.109). But every rule is the servant of one principle, "always remember Krishna, never forget Him" (CC Madhya 22.113), so we keep the rules with love and not as a master.

## D. Learning outcomes
1. Define regulation as BIKC does and say what it saves the mind from.
2. Explain the typing-book illustration of CC Madhya 22.109 and its conclusion.
3. Show from BG 6.17 which four areas of life are regulated and what Prabhupada's purport adds.
4. State the principle that makes rules servants (CC Madhya 22.113) and the check against whimsical abuse (SB 11.18.36 purport).
5. Draft a fixed sequence for one day.

## E. Scriptural foundation
Use six verses in the talk (CC Madhya 22.109, 22.113, BG 6.17, BG 6.16, NOI 3, SB 11.18.36). "Verified" means the verse exists (research/verification/bss-45.json) and I read the translation and the purport used.

**CC Madhya 22.109**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/22/109/
> "Those who have not attained the platform of spontaneous attachment in devotional service render devotional service under the guidance of a bona fide spiritual master according to the regulative principles mentioned in the revealed scriptures."
- **Context:** Sri Caitanya teaches Sanatana Gosvami that sadhana-bhakti is of two kinds, regulative (*vaidhi*) and spontaneous (*raganuga*) (22.108).
- **Purport point (read in full):** A child is sent to school by force, but later, tasting education, he participates automatically; "one cannot force a person to become a scholar, but sometimes force is used in the beginning." Likewise one learns to type by "following the regulative principles of the typing book", placing the fingers on the keys in a certain way, "but when one becomes adept, he can type swiftly and correctly without even looking at the keys." Love is already in the heart; it "simply has to be awakened by the regulative process". One should practise hearing and chanting and follow the other principles "by washing the temple, cleansing oneself, rising early in the morning, attending maṅgala-ārati and so on."
- **Use:** in the talk (the centre of the page).

**CC Madhya 22.113**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/22/113/
> "He should always be remembered and never forgotten at any time. All the rules and prohibitions mentioned in the śāstras should be the servants of these two principles."
- **Purport point:** The regulative principles "should act as servants of the basic principle", which is to remember Krishna and never forget Him. One must refrain from things that make one forget Him. (Sixteen rounds as the most essential order: bss-44.)
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 6.17**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/17/
> "He who is regulated in his habits of eating, sleeping, recreation and work can mitigate all material pains by practicing the yoga system."
- **Purport point (read in full):** Extravagance in eating, sleeping, defending and mating "can block advancement". Eating is regulated by taking *prasadam* (food offered to Krishna); sleep is kept to a minimum because a Krishna conscious person "cannot bear to pass a minute of his life without being engaged in the service of the Lord", the ideal being Rupa Gosvami, who "could not sleep more than two hours a day, and sometimes not even that", and Thakura Haridasa, who would not take prasadam or sleep until he had finished chanting three hundred thousand names. Work is "untainted by sense gratification"; because he is "regulated in all his work, speech, sleep, wakefulness and all other bodily activities, there is no material misery for him."
- **Application (preacher's):** these two are the heights, not the minimum: say so, so nobody copies the hours without the engagement.
- **Use:** in the talk. (Other BSS pages use 6.17 for sense control and austerity; here it gives the four areas of a timetable.)

**BG 6.16**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/16/
> "There is no possibility of one's becoming a yogī, O Arjuna, if one eats too much or eats too little, sleeps too much or does not sleep enough."
- **Purport point:** "One should not sleep more than six hours daily"; one who sleeps more is influenced by ignorance. Fasting should be as recommended in the scriptures, not by "manufacturing his own personal process of fasting".
- **Use:** in the talk (sleep and meals in the timetable).

**BG 8.14**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/8/14/
> "For one who always remembers Me without deviation, I am easy to obtain, O son of Pṛthā, because of his constant engagement in devotional service."
- **Purport point:** The words *satatam* and *nityasah* mean "always", "regularly", or "every day"; the pure devotee "constantly remembers Kṛṣṇa". The qualification is thinking of Krishna "without considering the time or place".
- **Application (preacher's):** the daily regularity is the practical form of "nityasah".
- **Use:** in the talk.

**NOI 3**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/3/
> "(4) acting according to regulative principles"
- **Purport point:** "Neglect of the regulative principles will destroy devotional service"; four prohibitions (*yama*) and positive principles (*niyama*) such as sixteen rounds "must be faithfully performed with enthusiasm"; the purport requires "everyone to rise early in the morning, by four A.M." for the centres' programme. (See bss-44.)
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 11.18.36**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/18/36/ (BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples)
> "execute other regulative duties not by force but by his own free will"
- **Purport point:** A self-realized soul "is a servant of Lord Kṛṣṇa and not of rules and regulations. Nevertheless, a transcendentalist strictly complies with regular duties for the pleasure of the Supreme Lord." The purport adds that the verse "should not be ignorantly misinterpreted to justify immoral, whimsical behavior" and that it speaks of the paramahamsa stage.
- **Use:** in the talk, for the check against mechanical rules and against abuse.

**CC Antya 20.18**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/20/18/
> "Regardless of time or place, one who chants the holy name, even while eating or sleeping, attains all perfection."
- **Teaching:** The Name itself is not limited by time or place. (No purport on vedabase for this verse.)
- **Application (preacher's):** this does not cancel the fixed times; the fixed times are how a *conditioned* mind gets reliably to the Name. See bss-48.
- **Use:** extension.

**SB 7.12.2-3**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/12/2/ and /3/ (translations only; no purports)
> "At both junctions of day and night, namely, in the early morning and in the evening, he should be fully absorbed in thoughts of the spiritual master, fire, the sun-god and Lord Viṣṇu" (7.12.2)
> "the student should study the Vedic mantras regularly. Every day, before beginning his studies and at the end of his studies, the disciple should respectfully offer obeisances unto the spiritual master." (7.12.3)
- **Application (preacher's):** the traditional brahmacari's life had fixed junctions and a daily act of respect; ours has the same shape.
- **Use:** extension.

**CC Madhya 22.108, 22.110**: Verified, for R.1 only (not for this level's talk or handout): https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/22/108/ and /110/.

## F. Philosophical explanation
**F.1 What regulation is.** BIKC: do the same thing at the same time every day, or at least in a fixed sequence around an approximate schedule. A devotee with a fixed rising time does not ask "am I too tired?" or "is it too cold?"; he rises. Because he does not have to decide what is next, the mind is spared constant small decisions. BIKC adds that temple timings (programmes, prasadam) regulate us automatically if we simply follow them with the devotees.

**F.2 Why regulation, if love is spontaneous.** CC Madhya 22.109 answers: love is in the heart already, but our minds are not trained to it. Regulative bhakti is the training; spontaneous bhakti is the result. Prabhupada's two pictures: a child forced to school who later loves learning, and a typist who looks at the book until the fingers know the keys. In both, the rule is not the destination. It is the way to the point where the rule is no longer needed as a prop.

**F.3 Rules are servants.** CC Madhya 22.113 says all rules serve two principles: remember Krishna, never forget Him. Thus a rule that makes me forget Krishna (for example, a ritual done so rapidly that I am not present) has lost its purpose. And an activity not on the list that makes me remember Him is good. The check is always: does this make me remember?

**F.4 The two ditches.** (a) Laxity: NOI 3's purport says neglect of the regulative principles destroys devotional service. (b) Rigidity (the traditional word is *niyamagraha*, clinging to rules for their own sake). SB 11.18.36's purport shows the balance: the advanced soul performs regulative duties "by his own free will" and for the Lord's pleasure, and it warns against misusing that statement to excuse whimsy. A secondary source, a Desire Tree text quoting Bhaktivinoda Thakura's *Bhaktyaloka*, puts it that all the rules of every level are subordinate to "Always remember Krishna, and never forget Him" and that those who cling to the rules of an earlier level are "bound by those rules like chains". That text is supporting only; I have not checked the original (**Requires source review**).

**F.5 Four areas (BG 6.17).** Eating, sleeping, recreation, work. In a brahmacari's timetable they become: prasadam at fixed times; bed and rising times (BG 6.16 purport: not over six hours of sleep); recreation that does not scatter the mind (see bss-24); work as service.

**F.6 Rupa Gosvami and Haridasa as direction, not as target.** The BG 6.17 purport quotes their sleep and chanting to show how a person absorbed in service regulates. They did this out of absorption. BIKC and bss-26 warn against imitating extremes; do not copy the numbers without the absorption.

**F.7 Misunderstandings.**
- "Regulation makes me a robot." It does if there is no purpose; SB 11.18.36 and CC Madhya 22.113 show the purpose.
- "I will regulate myself when I feel like it." The point of regulation is that it does not wait for the mood (BIKC).
- "If I travel, regulation is impossible." BIKC says it is harder but possible; see bss-48.

## H. Opening and engagement
**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes).**
1. Short prayer or kirtana verse. Say the title and explain: regulation means "doing the same things at the same time".
2. Ask for a show of hands: "Who set an alarm this morning?" Then: "Who woke because they decided at that moment?" Say: "Today we find out why the alarm is a friend."
3. Read the translation of CC Madhya 22.109 slowly.
4. Say: "In two hours we will learn why Prabhupada compared devotional training to a typing book."

**H.2 Opening questions.**
1. "Think of something you do at a fixed time every day, without thinking (brushing teeth, a prayer, a train). What would happen if you had to decide each day whether to do it?"
2. "Which part of your day changes most from day to day?"
3. "Have you ever learned to type, play an instrument or drive? Did you look at the keys or the road at first? When did you stop?"

## I. Core teaching points
**I.1 What regulation saves us from (BIKC).** Define regulation (F.1). Stress BIKC's two benefits: it fixes determination and keeps the mind peaceful; it also makes best use of time. Example (preacher's, labelled): a village market opens when the bell rings; the vendors do not discuss whether to open. The Krishna conscious point is that a brahmacari's bell is the programme, and the energy he saves by not deciding every morning can be given to Krishna's service. Next question for the group: how can a fixed routine ever lead to love?

**I.2 The typing book (CC Madhya 22.109).** Read the translation, then the purport: the school child and the typist. Explain that the verse separates those who have "attained the platform of spontaneous attachment" from those who have not. For the second group, the instruction is clear: follow the regulative principles under the guru. Ask: what is the equivalent of looking at the keys for us? (The written programme; the senior who reminds us.) Application (preacher's): reliance on the book at first is not a weakness. Next question for the group: which parts of life belong in the timetable?

**I.3 Four areas (BG 6.17, 6.16).** Eating, sleeping, recreation, work. Use the purport: prasadam regulates eating; sleep is kept short out of eagerness for service; work is for Krishna. Add BG 6.16's purport on six hours. Invite the group to draw a sequence (not a clock) for one day: rise, bathe, japa, mangala-arati, class, prasadam, service, evening programme, reading, rest. Tell them BIKC's point: a fixed sequence is enough if exact times vary. Next question for the group: what keeps the rules from becoming a heavy burden?

**I.4 Servants, not masters (CC Madhya 22.113; SB 11.18.36).** Read both. Ask one check question for any rule: "Does this help me remember Krishna?" Explain the free-will line of SB 11.18.36 and the warning in its purport. Application (preacher's): a beginner should not read the free-will line as permission to skip; the purport applies it to the paramahamsa stage.

**I.5 When the schedule breaks (BIKC).** Illness, an urgent service, a late-night program. BIKC's guidance on the programme: if some of it must be missed, set aside time elsewhere to make up. Restart the next day at the usual time; a broken day is not a broken sequence. Application (preacher's): tell the guide when it happens; do not hide it.

## J. Stories and illustrations
**J.1 Haridasa Thakura's chanting and Rupa Gosvami's service (from the BG 6.17 purport).** The purport itself says that Thakura Haridasa would not take prasadam or sleep before he finished his daily chanting of three hundred thousand names, and that Rupa Gosvami slept no more than two hours a day. (Haridasa's pastime with the prostitute is told in bss-12; do not retell it here.) **Devotional lesson:** their regulation was a consequence of their absorption in Krishna, and they set a direction for us, not a number.

**J.2 The mountain road (Chaitanya Charan Das, supporting, not authority; Gita Daily, 2 Sept 2024; his words from an AI transcript, here summarised).** A road to a beautiful peak has barriers on both sides. In fog, one thinks the barriers are obstacles; once one sees the peak, one sees they protect from the valley. The point: first give the direction and destination; the rules then make sense. **Devotional lesson:** the goal is Krishna's service and the rules protect the road to it.

**J.3 Invented illustration (labelled).** A farmer gets up at the same hour every day and waters his field in the same order. He does not argue with himself about it, and the crop depends on it. The Krishna conscious point is that our field is the heart, and the crop is love for Krishna, which grows through regular practice (CC Madhya 22.109).

## K. Anticipated questions and objections
1. *Is regulation against freedom?* Prabhupada's typing illustration (CC Madhya 22.109): freedom of the fingers comes through the book.
2. *What if I have a late-night service?* Keep the sequence; BIKC's guidance is to make up the missed portion elsewhere, and talk to the guide; do not make a medical claim about sleep.
3. *What if I fall asleep in class even though I rise on time?* Sleep too little is as much a fault as too much (BG 6.16); discuss with the guide about timing.
4. *Doesn't SB 11.18.36 say we need not follow rules?* Only for the self-realized who keep them freely; the purport warns against whimsical misuse.
5. *CC Antya 20.18 says no time rules for chanting. Why do we fix times?* The verse describes the Name's power; fixed times are how our unsteady mind reaches it daily (preacher's application).
6. *Is Rupa Gosvami's two hours of sleep the standard?* No; it describes absorption; BG 6.16's purport sets six hours as the upper limit.

## L. Case studies
**L.1 Invented case for discussion.** Neel keeps the schedule perfectly but says it feels empty: "I am just doing steps." *Principles:* CC Madhya 22.113, SB 11.18.36. *Questions:* What is missing? What can he do inside the schedule (listen to the sound, ask for a purpose)? *Analysis:* the rule is a servant of remembrance; bring attention to the Name inside the sequence. *Takeaway:* when a routine feels mechanical, Neel should pay closer attention to the holy name during it, and he should not drop the routine.

**L.2 Invented case for discussion (extension).** Kiran skips the morning programme twice a week and chants later "to be flexible". *Principles:* NOI 3 (neglect destroys), BIKC (regulation is the basis of sadhana). *Questions:* What would a gentle step be? *Takeaway:* Kiran should first fix one point, his rising time, and keep it for a week.

## M. Discussion and reflection
**M.1 Closing check questions (with expected answers).**
1. *What is regulation, in BIKC's words?* Doing the same thing at the same time every day, or at least in a fixed sequence.
2. *What is the typing illustration in CC Madhya 22.109?* A typist follows the book at first, then types without looking.
3. *Which four areas does BG 6.17 regulate?* Eating, sleeping, recreation, work.
4. *What do all rules serve (CC Madhya 22.113)?* Always remembering Krishna and never forgetting Him.
5. *What does the SB 11.18.36 purport warn about?* Misusing the free-will statement to justify whimsical behaviour.
6. *If a programme is missed, what does BIKC advise?* Make up the time at another hour.

**M.2 Activities.**
(a) *Typing drill (5 min, whole group).* Everyone types "Hare Krishna" on an imaginary keyboard with eyes closed after reading it once. Discuss how repetition frees attention; link to CC Madhya 22.109.
(b) *Build a sequence (15 min, pairs).* Draw ordered boxes for one day with approximate times. Exchange with a pair and ask "which box could be skipped without anyone noticing?" Fix it.
(c) *Rule or servant? (10 min, groups; extension).* Give three rules (rising time, fixed class, silence in the dining hall). Each group says how it helps remembering Krishna; if it cannot, it must propose how to make it do so.

**M.3 Reflection (private).** "What is the one point in my day I would like to stop deciding about?"

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary
Regulation means doing the same things at the same time every day, or at least in a fixed order. A devotee with a fixed rising time does not argue with himself about whether it is too cold; he gets up. This keeps the mind peaceful and uses time well, and it is the basis of steady sadhana (the daily practice of devotional service).

Srila Prabhupada explains why this works with a picture: a person learning to type follows the typing book and puts his fingers in the right place, and after some time can type without looking. In the same way a devotee follows the rules at first, such as rising early, cleansing, and attending the morning worship, and after some time the love for Krishna already in the heart comes out naturally (CC Madhya 22.109). Krishna's own teaching of a regulated life covers eating, sleeping, recreation and work (BG 6.17); too much or too little sleep or food is a hindrance (BG 6.16).

The rules are not the goal. Every rule is a servant of one principle: always remember Krishna and never forget Him (CC Madhya 22.113). So we ask of each rule: does it help me remember? We also remember that a self-realized soul keeps the rules by free will (SB 11.18.36), and that this is not an excuse for a beginner to be careless. If the schedule breaks, make up the missed time and begin again at the usual time.

## S2. Key ideas
- Regulation means doing the same things in the same order at the same time every day.
- Regulation is the training, and love for Krishna is the result, as in the typing example.
- The four areas to regulate are eating, sleeping, recreation and work.
- Every rule exists to help us remember Krishna.
- If we miss a programme, we make up the time and do not give up the routine.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **CC Madhya 22.113** · "All the rules and prohibitions mentioned in the śāstras should be the servants of these two principles." · Rules serve remembering Krishna.
- **BG 6.17** · The one who is regulated in eating, sleeping, recreation and work can mitigate all material pains. · A regulated day protects the mind.
- **CC Madhya 22.109** · Follow the regulative principles under the guidance of a guru until spontaneous devotion appears. · Rules are the training book.

## S4. Words to know
- **Regulation:** doing the same things in a fixed order and time.
- **Prasadam:** food offered to Krishna.
- **Mangala-arati:** the dawn worship of the Deity.
- **Vaidhi bhakti:** devotional service by rules, for beginners.
- **Spontaneous bhakti:** service out of natural love.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *Does regulation take away my freedom?* Like a typist's book, it trains you until the work becomes natural (CC Madhya 22.109).
2. *Why have rules if love should be natural?* The love is in the heart, but our mind is not yet trained to it.
3. *What if I feel like a robot?* Ask whether the rule helps you remember Krishna (CC Madhya 22.113), and give attention to the Name inside the routine.
4. *What if I miss part of the program?* Make up the time at another hour and begin again on time the next day.
5. *Must I sleep only two hours like Rupa Gosvami?* No. That shows his absorption. BG 6.16 says not to sleep more than six hours, and not too little.

## S6. Check your understanding
1. What picture does Prabhupada use for regulative devotional service (CC Madhya 22.109)?
2. Which four areas does BG 6.17 mention?
3. What are the rules servants of (CC Madhya 22.113)?
4. What should you do if a programme is missed?
5. True or false: SB 11.18.36 allows a beginner to skip the rules.

**Answers:** (1) Learning to type from a typing book. (2) Eating, sleeping, recreation, work. (3) Always remembering Krishna and never forgetting Him. (4) Make it up at another time and restart the next day. (5) False; it describes the advanced soul, and the purport warns against whimsical misuse.
