# How to be positive

> **VL1 · 45–60 min · review: sources checked, awaiting senior review · sensitive: true.** IYF's slides for this topic end with the same eight tips that VL1-11 "Eight
> practical tips for positive thinking" teaches in full. This page does **not** repeat the eight tips; it gives the
> frame the slides open with (why positivity matters; character ethic versus personality ethic; "the right map";
> what negativity costs; the ABCD formula) and the Gita's definition of a positive person. Marked sensitive (as VL1-11)
> because low mood and depression will come up; read the note in K before teaching.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

"Be positive" is advice this audience hears from parents, teachers, coaches and posters. Many have concluded it is
hollow: you cannot think your way past a failed exam. The Gita agrees with the objection and then does something
better. It never asks anyone to pretend. It asks them to see correctly (the body and its results are temporary,
BG 2.14), to put their effort where it belongs (BG 2.47), and to train the mind so that it works for them (BG 6.5–6).
A positive person, in the Gita's terms, is not someone who feels good but someone who is "not disturbed… or
elated" (BG 2.56) and therefore free to act.

IYF's slides (VOICE material) open with a contrast the audience will recognise from management books:
**character ethic** (success built on integrity, patience, humility) versus **personality ethic** (techniques and
attitudes without the character underneath), and the illustration of a traveller with the wrong map: more effort
and a better attitude only get you to the wrong place faster. Both come from Stephen Covey's *The 7 Habits of
Highly Effective People* (1989) and are credited to it here. The slides then list what modern life costs
(insecurity, poor sleep, low trust, stress) and the eight tips. This page keeps the opening and the close.

Builds on vl1-10 (BG 2.47, work without owning the result) and vl1-03 / vl1-16 (the mind as friend or enemy): here those verses appear only as parts of the "positive person" definition; do not repeat their examples.

## C. Central message

Real positivity is not pretending things are fine. It is seeing clearly (results come and go), doing your part
fully (the effort is yours, the result is not), and feeding the mind daily with what steadies it. The Gita calls
such a person steady, not cheerful; and steady people are the ones who can actually help.

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. Tell the difference between the "ostrich" kind of positive thinking and the kind built on character, with the
   wrong-map illustration (credited).
2. Sort their own thoughts into the Preaching Encyclopedia's four kinds (positive, necessary, negative, waste).
3. State the Gita's definition of a steady person (BG 2.56) and the two verses that make it possible (BG 2.14, 2.47).
4. Explain, from BG 18.37–38, why a positive person reads time correctly.
5. Describe the ABCD formula and choose one letter to try this week.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** BG 2.14, 2.47, 2.56, 17.16 and SB 1.7.7 carry the talk. The rest prepare you for questions.

**SB 1.7.7**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/7/7/
> "Simply by giving aural reception to this Vedic literature, the feeling for loving devotional service to Lord Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, sprouts up at once to extinguish the fire of lamentation, illusion and fearfulness."
- **Context:** Suta Gosvami describes the effect of hearing the Bhagavatam that Vyasadeva compiled.
- **Teaching:** Hearing this literature awakens devotion, which extinguishes lamentation, illusion and fear.
- **Purport point:** "Everyone is full of lamentation at every moment, he is after the mirage of illusory things,
  and he is always afraid of his supposed enemy. These are the primary symptoms of the material disease."
- **Application (preacher's):** The three fires map onto the three directions negative thought runs: lamentation
  looks back, illusion looks around for a quick fix, fear looks ahead. (The mapping is the Preaching
  Encyclopedia's gloss, not the verse's.) The verse names the remedy: hearing. This is the "B" of ABCD.
- **Use:** in the talk (the diagnosis and the remedy).

**BG 2.47**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/47/
> "You have a right to perform your prescribed duty, but you are not entitled to the fruits of action. Never consider yourself the cause of the results of your activities, and never be attached to not doing your duty."
- **Teaching:** Effort is ours; results are not; and not acting is not an option.
- **Purport point:** "Any attachment, positive or negative, is cause for bondage. Inaction is sinful."
- **Application (preacher's):** The most practical positive-thinking verse in the Gita. Anxiety lives in the
  result; effort is the one thing we control. The last clause rules out the "ostrich": detachment is not
  withdrawal.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 2.14**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/14/
> "…the nonpermanent appearance of happiness and distress, and their disappearance in due course, are like the appearance and disappearance of winter and summer seasons… one must learn to tolerate them without being disturbed."
- **Teaching:** Good and bad times come and go like seasons; tolerance is learned.
- **Purport point:** The examples are duties done despite the weather, such as bathing early in a cold month.
- **Application (preacher's):** Positivity is knowing that the season will change, and doing your duty in the
  meantime. Not denial of winter.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 2.56**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/56/
> "One who is not disturbed in mind even amidst the threefold miseries or elated when there is happiness, and who is free from attachment, fear and anger, is called a sage of steady mind."
- **Teaching:** The goal is steadiness, not elation.
- **Purport point:** Such a person "is always daring and active"; "Success or no success, a Kṛṣṇa conscious person
  is always steady in his determination."
- **Application (preacher's):** The Gita's positive person is *active* and *undisturbed*, which is rarer and more
  useful than cheerful. Note that the verse rules out being "elated" too: the high and the crash are one system.
- **Use:** in the talk (the definition).

**BG 17.16**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/17/16/
> "And satisfaction, simplicity, gravity, self-control and purification of one's existence are the austerities of the mind."
- **Teaching:** Satisfaction is listed as an austerity, something practised.
- **Purport point:** "Satisfaction of the mind can be obtained only by taking the mind away from thoughts of sense
  enjoyment. The more we think of sense enjoyment, the more the mind becomes dissatisfied." The purport recommends
  diverting the mind to the Vedic literature, "which is full of satisfying stories".
- **Application (preacher's):** Satisfaction is not a mood that arrives; it is a practice, like fitness. The
  purport also supplies the "scripts" idea: what the mind is fed, it becomes.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 18.37**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/37/
> "That which in the beginning may be just like poison but at the end is just like nectar and which awakens one to self-realization is said to be happiness in the mode of goodness."
- **Use:** in the talk, with 18.38.

**BG 18.38**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/38/
> "That happiness which is derived from contact of the senses with their objects and which appears like nectar at first but poison at the end is said to be of the nature of passion."
- **Teaching:** Two kinds of happiness run opposite curves over time.
- **Application (preacher's):** A positive person is one who reads time correctly: who knows that the hard start
  of a good practice is not a bad sign, and the easy start of a bad one is not a good sign. This is the character
  ethic in the Gita's words.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 6.5**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/5/
> "One must deliver himself with the help of his mind, and not degrade himself. The mind is the friend of the conditioned soul, and his enemy as well."
- **Purport point:** The mind "must be so trained that it can deliver the conditioned soul"; the quoted Upanisad
  line: mind is "the cause of bondage" and "the cause of liberation".
- **Use:** in the talk, one line, with 6.6.

**BG 6.6**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/6/
> "For him who has conquered the mind, the mind is the best of friends; but for one who has failed to do so, his mind will remain the greatest enemy."
- **Application (preacher's):** Negativity is the untrained mind's default commentary. Training it is possible and
  is the subject of VL1-16.
- **Use:** in the talk, one line.

**BG 16.1–3**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/16/1-3/
> "Fearlessness; purification of one's existence…; truthfulness; freedom from anger; renunciation; tranquillity; aversion to faultfinding; compassion for all living entities; freedom from covetousness; gentleness; modesty; steady determination…"
- **Teaching:** The qualities of the godly nature include tranquillity, aversion to fault-finding and freedom from
  envy: a scriptural portrait of a positive person.
- **Use:** background; for K ("isn't this just a personality type?") and for the "blame game" theme.

**SB 12.3.51**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/12/3/51/ · *BBT translation, completed by Srila
Prabhupada's disciples*
> "My dear King, although Kali-yuga is an ocean of faults, there is still one good quality about this age: Simply by chanting the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra, one can become free from material bondage and be promoted to the transcendental kingdom."
- **Purport point:** "Just as one powerful king can kill innumerable thieves, one brilliant spiritual quality can
  destroy all the contamination of this age."
- **Application (preacher's):** The scripture models positivity: a whole chapter on the faults of the age, then
  the one good quality that outweighs them. This is the "C" of ABCD, offered as what practitioners do.
- **Use:** in the talk, near the end.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** Negativity has three directions: lamentation (back), illusion (around), fear (ahead)
   (SB 1.7.7 and purport). All three live in a wrong relationship with results. The Gita corrects the relationship:
   results are seasonal (BG 2.14) and not ours to own (BG 2.47); the self who sees this is steady (BG 2.56). Steadiness
   is maintained by what the mind is fed (BG 17.16 purport) and by a trained mind (BG 6.5–6).
2. **Why it matters.** The popular version of positive thinking fails this audience in two ways: it asks them to
   deny real problems (the ostrich), and it offers technique without character (the personality ethic). The Gita's
   version faces reality harder than the pessimist does (BG 2.14 does not pretend winter is warm) and roots
   positivity in character: satisfaction, simplicity, gravity, self-control (BG 17.16).
3. **Connections.** To the mind as friend or enemy (VL1-16), to the eight tips (VL1-11), to suffering (VL1-06),
   and later to hearing and chanting as daily practice (VL3).
4. **Consequences.** A person who stops owning results becomes braver, not lazier: "daring and active"
   (BG 2.56 purport). Comparison loses its grip because the measure is the race run, not the trophy. And fault-finding
   drops away as a habit of mind (BG 16.1–3).
5. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - **"Positive means I should feel happy."** BG 2.56 rules out elation as much as despair. Steady, not high.
   - **"Positive means problems aren't real."** BG 2.14 names distress as real and tolerable, not imaginary.
   - **"Detachment means not trying."** BG 2.47's last clause: never be attached to not doing your duty.
   - **"If I can't be positive, something is wrong with me."** Low mood is human; persistent low mood is medical, not
     moral (K).
6. **Pitch for VL1.** Words: "steady", "clear sight", "effort versus result", "what you feed the mind". No
   Sanskrit required; *tapas* (austerity) may be mentioned once with BG 17.16. Offer the Gita as a text that
   understood the problem with "be positive" long before the posters.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet, give your name, and say today is about positivity that survives a bad day.
2. Ask H.2 question 1 with a show of hands.
3. Take two or three answers; thank each speaker and laugh with, not at, the room.
4. Promise to "defend your irritation" first, then give a better answer.
5. Give the transition line below and start with the wrong map.

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the room's own life)
1. When did someone last tell you to "think positive"? Did it help?
2. Think of a recent disappointment. Which came first: the feeling, or the story you told yourself?
3. Who in your life stays steady when things go wrong? What do they do?

- **Opening question:** "Hands up if someone told you this week to 'be positive'. Did it help?" Pause. "Today I
  want to defend your irritation, and then show you what the advice should have said."
- **Relatable scenario (invented; use any exam, interview or business setback):** Priya fails an entrance exam. Her friend says "think positive, it's fine".
  Her uncle says "you didn't work hard enough". Her mother says nothing and worries aloud about the neighbours.
  Which of the three helped? What would have?
- **Transition:** "A management writer tells of a traveller with the map of a different city (the original uses Pune and Mumbai; use two cities your audience knows). He tries harder and
  thinks positively and gets to the wrong place faster. The problem was never his attitude. It was his map. The
  Gita is a map."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** Results, comparison, parents, placements: the room is full of people who
  have been told to feel better about things that have not changed.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Positivity without the right map is dangerous.** The traveller with the wrong map (Covey's illustration,
   credited). Character ethic versus personality ethic. *Example:* a cheerful person who never studies.
   *Transition:* so what does the right map say about bad news?
2. **Negativity runs in three directions.** Lamentation (back), illusion (around), fear (ahead): the three fires of
   SB 1.7.7, as the Encyclopedia reads them. *Example:* Priya's week. *Transition:* the Gita answers each.
3. **Seasons change; tolerance is learned.** BG 2.14. *Example:* nobody panics in December because summer is gone.
   *Transition:* and what about the things we can control?
4. **Effort is yours; results are not; quitting is not allowed.** BG 2.47. *Example:* the student who studies fully
   and sleeps well before the exam, versus the one who studies and worries all night. *Transition:* what does such a
   person look like?
5. **Steady, not elated.** BG 2.56; "daring and active" (purport). *Example:* the calm senior everyone turns to in a
   crisis. *Transition:* and how do they read time?
6. **Read the curve.** Poison-then-nectar versus nectar-then-poison (BG 18.37–38). *Example:* the first week of early
   rising versus the first week of a new gaming habit. *Transition:* so what do we feed the mind?
7. **Satisfaction is a practice; the mind is trainable.** BG 17.16 and purport; BG 6.5–6. The four kinds of thought
   (positive, necessary, negative, waste: Preaching Encyclopedia). *Example:* an hour of scrolling versus an hour of
   a satisfying story. *Transition:* here is a daily formula practitioners use.
8. **ABCD.** Association, books, chanting, diet. The scripture's own positivity: an age full of faults with one good
   quality (SB 12.3.51). *Transition:* to the takeaway.

- **Bathing early in a cold month** (BG 2.14 purport). Srila Prabhupada's example of doing one's duty despite the

- **The king and the thieves** (SB 12.3.51 purport). "Just as one powerful king can kill innumerable thieves, one

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

> **Before you teach:** persistent low mood, loss of interest, sleep and appetite changes, or thoughts of self-harm
> are not "negativity" and are not solved by a talk. If a question or a face suggests this, acknowledge the person,
> don't analyse them in public, and offer to talk afterwards. Say plainly that professional help is compatible with
> spiritual life. **Tele-MANAS, 14416, is a free 24-hour national mental-health helpline.** VL1-19 carries the fuller
> guidance.

1. **"Isn't positive thinking just denial?"**
   - The ostrich kind is, and the Gita rejects it: BG 2.14 calls distress real; BG 2.47 forbids inaction. The Gita's
     positivity is clear sight plus full effort. It is harder than pessimism, not softer.
2. **"If I'm not attached to results, why would I work?"**
   - Because effort is the one thing that is yours (BG 2.47), and because the steady person is "daring and active"
     (BG 2.56 purport). Detachment from results frees energy that anxiety was consuming.
3. **"I try to be positive and I just can't. Is my faith weak?"**
   - No. Moods are seasonal (BG 2.14), and satisfaction is an *austerity*, something practised over time
     (BG 17.16), not a switch. And if low mood persists for weeks, that is a medical question, not a spiritual
     failure (see the note above).
4. **"Isn't this just a personality type? Some people are born cheerful."**
   - Temperament varies, but the Gita lists tranquillity, freedom from envy and aversion to fault-finding among
     qualities that are *cultivated* (BG 16.1–3), and satisfaction among austerities (BG 17.16). Trainable, not fixed.
5. **"What do association, books, chanting and diet have to do with my mood?"**
   - BG 17.16 purport: the mind becomes what it is fed; "divert the mind to the Vedic literature". SB 1.7.7: hearing
     extinguishes lamentation, illusion and fear. Offer ABCD as a one-week experiment, not a creed.
6. **"Why should I count blessings when things are genuinely bad?"**
   - Not instead of facing them, but alongside: the one checked study in O found that people who kept a weekly
     gratitude list reported better well-being than controls. The Gita's version is deeper: the age is "an ocean of
     faults" *and* has one quality that outweighs them (SB 12.3.51). Both are true at once.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The placement season.** Arjun's batchmates are getting offers; he has three rejections. He tells himself
"stay positive" every morning and feels worse every evening. He has stopped preparing.
- **Dilemma:** Is Arjun's problem his attitude?
- **Principles:** BG 2.47 (effort versus result; never be attached to not acting); BG 2.14 (seasons); the
  wrong-map illustration.
- **Questions:**
  - Which of Arjun's thoughts look back, around, ahead?
  - What is the one thing in the situation that is his?
  - What would "positive" mean if it meant action rather than mood?
- **Suggested analysis:** Arjun has the ostrich kind of positivity: feeling without effort. BG 2.47 turns him
  round: prepare fully today; the offer is a season. A friend who studies with him (association) does more than a
  slogan.
- **Takeaway:** Positivity is a verb.

**Case 2: The fault-finder.** Sneha's group chat is a daily list of what is wrong with teachers, hostel, friends.
She is funny and popular and increasingly unhappy.
- **Principles:** BG 16.1–3 (aversion to fault-finding as a godly quality); BG 17.16 purport (the mind becomes what
  it is fed); the four kinds of thought.
- **Questions:**
  - Which column do most of Sneha's thoughts fall in?
  - What is the cost of the habit to her, apart from the targets?
  - What one change in what she reads, watches or hears would alter the column?
- **Takeaway:** The blame game is a diet; change the diet.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first; praise every try)
1. Does the Gita ask us to pretend things are fine? *No: it asks for clear sight (BG 2.14).*
2. What does BG 2.47 say about quitting? *The effort is ours; never be attached to not doing duty.*
3. What are the three "fires" in SB 1.7.7? *Lamentation, illusion, fearfulness.*
4. How does BG 2.56 describe the steady person? *Not disturbed, not elated.*
5. What does ABCD stand for? *Association, Books, Chanting, Diet.*

**M.2 Activity (about 8 minutes): "Back, around, ahead"**
1. Read three invented lines aloud (G alternative): "I always fail", "everyone is ahead", "my parents will be upset".
2. In pairs, 3 min: sort each line into back, around or ahead, and say which Gita idea answers it.
3. 3 min: volunteers share a sorted line (never their own private story). Thank each.
4. 2 min: link to ABCD as the daily source.

**M.3 Reflection**
- **Reflection:** Which direction does your negativity usually run: back, around or ahead?
- **Application (private, optional):** Sort yesterday's thoughts into the four kinds. Which column was fullest? *(Nobody
  needs to share.)*

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

"Be positive" annoys us because it usually means "pretend". The Gita never asks that. It asks for clear sight: good and bad times come and go like seasons, so a setback is real but passing (BG 2.14). Our effort is ours, but results are not, and quitting is not an option (BG 2.47). The person who lives this way is not always cheerful but *steady*, "not disturbed… or elated", and therefore "daring and active" (BG 2.56 and purport).

Negative thinking runs in three directions: back (lamentation, replaying the past), around (illusion, comparing and misjudging), and ahead (fear of the future). The Bhagavatam says that hearing spiritual knowledge puts out all three fires (SB 1.7.7). Think of a traveller with the wrong map: a better attitude only takes him to the wrong place faster, so see correctly first, then effort and attitude help.

Time also matters: happiness in the mode of goodness is like poison at first and nectar in the end (BG 18.37), so a hard start is not a sign of failure. Satisfaction is a practice, not a mood (BG 17.16), and the mind becomes what it is fed. A daily formula practitioners use is ABCD: Association (good company), Books (uplifting reading), Chanting (the holy name, offered as an invitation), Diet (what we eat and consume). Try it for a week and see.

## S2. Key ideas

- Positive thinking with the wrong map gets you to the wrong place faster. First fix the map.
- Negativity looks back, around or ahead. Each has an answer: BG 2.14, 2.47, 2.56.
- Steady beats cheerful. The steady person is the one who can act.
- Good practices feel like poison first and nectar later; bad ones the reverse (BG 18.37–38). Read the curve.
- Satisfaction is an austerity of the mind, something practised (BG 17.16). Feed the mind well.
- Even in an "ocean of faults" the scripture points to one good quality (SB 12.3.51). That is how it thinks.

## S3. Verses to remember

- **BG 2.47:** "You have a right to perform your prescribed duty, but you are not entitled to the fruits of
  action…" *For me:* I control the effort, not the result; so I give the effort everything.
- **BG 2.56:** "One who is not disturbed in mind even amidst the threefold miseries or elated when there is
  happiness… is called a sage of steady mind." *For me:* steady is the goal, not high.
- **SB 1.7.7:** hearing this literature extinguishes "the fire of lamentation, illusion and fearfulness." *For me:*
  what I hear daily shapes what I feel.

## S4. Words to know

- **Character ethic / personality ethic:** success built on who you are versus on techniques and attitudes (from
  Stephen Covey's *7 Habits*). The Gita builds character.
- **Austerity of the mind (tapas):** practices such as satisfaction, simplicity, gravity and self-control (BG 17.16).
- **ABCD:** Association, Books, Chanting, Diet: a daily source of steadiness that practitioners use.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Isn't positive thinking just denial?**
   The pretend kind is. The Gita's kind faces reality (BG 2.14 calls distress real) and forbids giving up (BG 2.47).
2. **If I don't care about results, why work?**
   You do care; you just don't *own* them. The effort is yours (BG 2.47). The steady person is "daring and active"
   (BG 2.56 purport).
3. **I can't make myself positive. Is something wrong with me?**
   Moods are seasonal (BG 2.14), and satisfaction is a practice built over time (BG 17.16). If low mood lasts for
   weeks, talk to someone qualified (in India, Tele-MANAS 14416 is free and open all hours; elsewhere, your local crisis line or doctor). Spiritual life and professional
   help go together.
4. **What do books and chanting have to do with my mood?**
   The mind becomes what it is fed (BG 17.16 purport). Hearing spiritual knowledge extinguishes lamentation,
   illusion and fear (SB 1.7.7). Try it for a week and see.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. According to BG 2.56, the steady person is: (a) always cheerful, (b) neither disturbed nor elated, (c) detached
   from all action.
2. True or false: BG 2.47 says that if results are not ours we may stop working.
3. Name the three "fires" in SB 1.7.7.
4. Which happiness is "poison in the beginning, nectar at the end" (BG 18.37)?
5. In your own words: what is wrong with a positive attitude and the wrong map?

**Answers:** 1. (b) (BG 2.56). 2. False: "never be attached to not doing your duty" (BG 2.47). 3. Lamentation,
illusion, fearfulness (SB 1.7.7). 4. Happiness in the mode of goodness, which awakens self-realisation (BG 18.37).
5. Attitude cannot correct direction; you reach the wrong place faster. First see correctly (BG 2.14, 2.47), then
effort and attitude help.
