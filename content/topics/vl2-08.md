# God and Science

> **VL2 · 180 min · review: sources-checked · controversy topic, sensitive.** IYF's angle (site subtitle): *"How science is
> so unscientifically establishing our material identity."* The page argues this as a claim about **philosophical
> overreach** ("you are nothing but your brain"), not as a complaint against science or scientists, and states the
> mainstream view honestly (F.3). IYF's text notes for this row are on faith and doubt
> (`research/iyf-materials/vl2-08/IMPORTANCE OF FAITH AND DANGER OF DOUBTS.txt`); they are built into F.6 and K.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on [DYS-1](dys-01-s1.md) (the design argument, "who created God?", and scientists who believed), [DYS-2](dys-01-s2.md) (the limits of the senses; axioms are not blind faith) and [DYS-4](dys-01-s4.md) (consciousness as the symptom of the soul, BG 13.34). Those arguments are not repeated here; this page's own angle is **where science ends and scientism begins**, and what reasonable faith means for a trained scientific mind.

VL2 asks "Why should I accept this?" For an educated Indian audience the strongest reason for not accepting
anything spiritual is "science has shown otherwise". A reasoned-faith talk neither mocks science nor pretends
scripture is a physics textbook. It asks what science actually shows, what is philosophy wearing a lab coat,
and what the Gita claims and on what grounds. IYF's notes add the companion theme: faith is necessary in every
field and must be reasonable, "sensible and verifiable".

**IYF's VL2 frame** (IYF's own documents, `research/batch-criteria.md` §2 and §8 point 2): course name "Technical Science
for Spiritual Value", mood *questioning, reasoned*; motive "developing strong faith in practicing spirituality" and "removing all
misconceptions about ISKCON and spirituality"; KRAs "Start doing chanting", "Coming to regular classes", "Meeting with
facilitators". The 2015 VL2 lesson plan runs DYS 1–6 first and then the EBG study-sessions; this page mirrors DYS session 1 "Can a Scientist Believe in God?" argued at VL2 depth (Encyclopedia transcript, O). Practice is
expected to *begin* during VL2 (by its end: 2–4 rounds, 15 min of Prabhupada's books and 15–30 min of kirtan hearing a day), so
this page may carry a first practical step without breaking IYF's level (P).

**Not done here:**
- Claiming scripture "already contained" modern discoveries.
- Any sentence beginning "scientists are biased". Scientists are people, with the biases people have; science is a method built to catch them.
- Pretending there is a scientific proof of the soul or of God. There is an argument, not a measurement.

## C. Central message

Science measures what can be measured and has given us medicine, machines and a trustworthy method. It has not
shown, and cannot show by measurement, that you are nothing but matter; that is a philosophical claim added on
top. The Gita makes the opposite claim with reasons: consciousness pervading the body is the symptom of the
self (BG 2.17), matter and the living being are two different energies of the Lord (7.4–5), and nature works
"under My direction" (9.10). Reasonable faith examines these claims; blind faith, in either direction, is condemned.

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. Distinguish science (a method of observation and testing) from scientism (the claim that only what science measures is real), and give an example of each.
2. State the Gita's argument that consciousness is the symptom of the soul (BG 2.17 and purport) and say fairly what the mainstream scientific view is.
3. Explain the Gita's two energies (BG 7.4–5) and what it says about nature's working (9.10) and the first cause (SB 1.1.1), as claims with reasons.
4. Say what "reasonable faith" means in IYF's terms (sensible and verifiable) and in Prabhupada's words ("apply your logic").
5. Respond to "science has disproved God" without hostility and without overclaiming.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** seven references carry the talk (BG 2.17, 2.13, 7.4, 7.5, 9.10, SB 1.1.1, BG 4.39–40). The rest prepare you for K.

**BG 2.17**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/17/
> "That which pervades the entire body you should know to be indestructible. No one is able to destroy that imperishable soul."
- **Context:** Krsna's teaching to Arjuna on the self, after 2.13.
- **Teaching:** What pervades the body is indestructible.
- **Purport point:** "Anyone can understand what is spread all over the body: it is consciousness"; "the material body minus consciousness is a dead body, and this consciousness cannot be revived in the body by any means of material administration. Therefore, consciousness is not due to any amount of material combination, but to the spirit soul." The purport also gives the Upanisads' statement of the soul's minute size.
- **Application (preacher's):** This is the Gita's argument, stated as an argument: consciousness is the datum; its source is the question. Present it beside the mainstream view (F.3) and let the listener weigh it. Do not call it a proof.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 2.13, 2.20, 2.22, 2.25, 2.29, 13.34**: Verified.
> 2.20: "For the soul there is neither birth nor death at any time… He is not slain when the body is slain."
> 2.25: "It is said that the soul is invisible, inconceivable and immutable."
> 13.34: "as the sun alone illuminates all this universe, so does the living entity, one within the body, illuminate the entire body by consciousness."
- **Teaching:** The self passes through bodily changes (2.13) and bodies (2.22); it is unborn and undying (2.20); it is invisible and inconceivable (2.25); some find it amazing and some cannot understand it even after hearing (2.29); it illuminates the body by consciousness (13.34).
- **Application (preacher's):** 2.25 is important for honesty: the Gita itself says the soul is not seen. The texts do not claim it can be put under a microscope, so the preacher must not either.
- **Use:** in the talk (2.13, 2.25, 13.34) and K.

**BG 7.4, 7.5**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/7/4/
> 7.4: "Earth, water, fire, air, ether, mind, intelligence and false ego – all together these eight constitute My separated material energies."
> 7.5: "Besides these, O mighty-armed Arjuna, there is another, superior energy of Mine, which comprises the living entities who are exploiting the resources of this material, inferior nature."
- **Teaching:** Eight material elements are the Lord's separated energy; the living entities are a superior energy.
- **Purport point (7.4):** "Material science comprises these ten items [the five gross elements and five sense objects] and nothing more. But the other three items, namely mind, intelligence and false ego, are neglected by the materialists." Also: "The science of God analyzes the constitutional position of God and His diverse energies."
- **Purport point (7.5):** the living entities "are never equally powerful, as unintelligent men think"; "Energies are always controlled by the energetic."
- **Application (preacher's):** The purport's "material science comprises… nothing more" describes physical science's scope in Prabhupada's time; psychology and neuroscience study mind too, by their own methods. The fair point is that the Gita places mind within matter and the self beyond it, which no measurement of either confirms or refutes.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 9.10, 18.61**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/9/10/
> 9.10: "This material nature, which is one of My energies, is working under My direction, O son of Kuntī, producing all moving and nonmoving beings. Under its rule this manifestation is created and annihilated again and again."
- **Teaching:** Nature works under the Lord's direction and produces all beings; the Lord in the heart directs the wanderings of all living entities (18.61).
- **Purport point (9.10):** "The Supreme Lord is the supreme will and the background of this material manifestation, but the management is being conducted by material nature"; "material nature, without the superintendence of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, cannot do anything."
- **Application (preacher's):** The Gita does not deny that nature has laws; it says whose laws they are. This is the frame for "does science leave room for God": the texts put God behind the mechanism, not in its gaps.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 1.1.1**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/1/1/
> "…I meditate upon Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa because He is the Absolute Truth and the primeval cause of all causes of the creation, sustenance and destruction of the manifested universes. He is directly and indirectly conscious of all manifestations, and He is independent because there is no other cause beyond Him…"
- **Teaching:** Krsna is the cause of all causes, conscious of all, and independent.
- **Purport point:** "It is natural that a philosophical mind wants to know about the origin of the creation. At night he sees the stars in the sky, and he naturally speculates about their inhabitants. Such inquiries are natural for man because man has a developed consciousness which is higher than that of the animals."
- **Application (preacher's):** The Bhagavatam opens with the question cosmology asks, and honours it. The scientist's curiosity is not the enemy here.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 15.15, 7.26, 10.10, 13.5, SB 1.5.22**: Verified.
- 15.15: from the Lord come "remembrance, knowledge and forgetfulness". 7.26: He knows past, present and future, "but Me no one knows". 10.10: He gives understanding to those who serve with love. 13.5: knowledge of the field and its knower is "especially presented in Vedānta-sūtra with all reasoning as to cause and effect". SB 1.5.22: learned circles conclude that all advancement of knowledge culminates in describing the Lord. **Use:** F.4 and K (the texts' own account of how knowledge of God is reached: reasoning plus revelation).

**BG 4.39, 4.40, 9.3**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/4/40/
> 4.39: "A faithful man who is dedicated to transcendental knowledge and who subdues his senses is eligible to achieve such knowledge…"
> 4.40: "But ignorant and faithless persons who doubt the revealed scriptures do not attain God consciousness; they fall down. For the doubting soul there is happiness neither in this world nor in the next."
- **Teaching:** Faith plus dedication plus sense control make one eligible for knowledge; the faithless doubter does not attain it and finds no happiness; without faith one returns to birth and death (9.3).
- **Purport point (4.40):** "those who have no faith and are always doubtful make no progress at all"; "One should therefore follow the principles of revealed scriptures with faith and thereby be raised to the platform of knowledge."
- **Application (preacher's):** IYF's notes pair these verses with "the importance of doubting". The verse condemns the *settled* doubter who never tests; it does not condemn questions. Prabhupada's 1966 lecture (O) says it in his own words.
- **Use:** in the talk and K.

**BG 9.2**: Verified. This knowledge "gives direct perception of the self by realization" (*pratyaksavagamam*). Purport: "there is, unfortunately, no university or educational institution where the science of the spirit soul is instructed." **Use:** F.4.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle: two different questions.** Science asks *how* nature behaves and answers by observation, hypothesis, test and revision. Scripture asks *who* I am, *who* is behind nature, and *what* I am for (SB 1.1.1 opens with the cause of all causes). The Gita's own list of material elements (7.4) overlaps with what science studies; the Gita then says there is a second energy, the living being (7.5), and a director behind nature (9.10). Those are claims science neither makes nor refutes.
2. **What IYF's subtitle gets right.** "Science is unscientifically establishing our material identity" is true of a specific move: taking the success of neuroscience in correlating brain states with experiences and concluding "therefore you are nothing but your brain". The correlation is science; the "nothing but" is philosophy. The BG 2.17 purport says the opposite with its own reason: a body minus consciousness is dead, and no material administration revives it; so consciousness is not a product of matter. Present both as positions with reasons.
3. **The mainstream view, honestly.** Most neuroscientists and philosophers of mind hold that consciousness arises from brain activity, and the evidence of correlation is strong (injury, anaesthesia, drugs change experience). How subjective experience arises from physical processes is unresolved; philosopher David Chalmers called it the "hard problem" (*Journal of Consciousness Studies*, 1995; developed in *The Conscious Mind*, Oxford University Press, 1996), and the field has several competing theories and no consensus. Chaitanya Charan's "Understanding the scopes of science and spirituality" (https://www.thespiritualscientist.com/understanding-the-scopes-of-science-and-spirituality/, 21 Oct 2015, linked from chaitanyacharan.com) argues that the experiencer is not found among the parts, and that denying consciousness presupposes it. State the Gita's position as a reasoned alternative in a live debate, not as the winner by default.
4. **How the texts say knowledge of God is reached.** Not by measurement: the soul "is invisible" (BG 2.25), and "Me no one knows" by their own power (7.26). By reasoning as far as it goes (BG 13.5: Vedanta-sutra "with all reasoning as to cause and effect"), by hearing (VL2-07), by practice that yields "direct perception of the self by realization" (BG 9.2), and by the Lord giving understanding to those who serve (10.10). This is why VL2-09 asks whether bhakti is itself a kind of science: a method with results.
5. **Connections.** VL2-01 (the soul) supplies the ground; VL2-07 the means of knowledge; VL2-09 the method of bhakti; VL1-08 the status of the texts.
6. **Reasonable faith (IYF's notes).** Faith operates in every field: an airline ticket bought on trust in an authorised company (Prabhupada's examples in IYF's notes; **Requires source review** for the lecture dates). IYF defines reasonable faith as *sensible* (credentials, reputation, coherence) and *verifiable* (see the result). Prabhupada, lecturing on BG 4.11–12 in New York on 28 July 1966: "Apply your arguments. Apply your logic. Don't take it as a sentiment or as a blind faith. You have got reason; you have got arguments; you have got sense. Apply it and try to understand it" (Vaniquotes, "Blind faith (Lectures)"). BG 4.40 condemns the settled doubter, not the questioner; 4.34 purport condemns blind following.
7. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - **"Science has disproved the soul."** No experiment can measure what is defined as "invisible, inconceivable" (2.25). Science has shown correlations; the inference to "nothing but" is philosophical.
   - **"Science is biased against God."** Scientists hold varied views on God; the method is designed to catch bias. Say nothing of the kind.
   - **"The Vedas already had quantum physics / Einstein said so."** The Einstein quotation in IYF's notes has no source and circulates in several forms: **Requires source review; do not use.** Overclaiming here destroys credibility with exactly this audience.
   - **"Faith means not asking."** 4.40 and the 1966 lecture say the opposite.
8. **Pitch for VL2.** Define *atma*, *prakrti*, *scientism*, *pratyaksa* once each. Use the listeners' own training: ask them what counts as evidence in their field, and what question their field does not ask.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Welcome by name; say today respects both science and faith and asks them to meet honestly.
2. Write on the board: "Science has proved that I am…" and ask for three endings; thank each.
3. Ask: "Which of these came from an instrument, and which from an interpretation?" Take two answers.
4. Say: "Today we learn to tell the difference, in science and in scripture."
5. Draw two columns: Measured, Inferred.

**H.2 Opening questions**
- What is something you believe because a trusted expert told you, not because you tested it yourself?
- Have you ever heard "science says…" used to end a discussion? What did you feel?
- In your own work or study, what is a question your method cannot answer?

**Adapting for your audience:** the three questions need no adaptation for engineers, doctors or students. For a village or trades audience, use a farmer's reading of the weather or a mechanic's ear: knowledge from long experience, and its limits. For professionals use the point where a model or report stops and judgment begins.

**More material for the opening**

- **Opening question:** H.2, with the first board exercise "Science has proved that I am…" then "which came from an instrument, and which from an interpretation?"
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** An engineering student tells his mother that her prayers are "chemical reactions in the brain". She asks, "And is your love for me a chemical reaction?" He says yes. She asks, "Then why does it matter whether you keep your promises?" He has no answer, and realises the question is not chemistry.
- **Transition:** "The Gita begins with exactly this: what pervades the body (BG 2.17)? It gives an argument, not a measurement. Let us weigh it fairly, beside the best science."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** they will spend their lives inside the scientific method. They deserve to know where its authority ends and philosophy begins, from someone who respects it.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Science is a method; scientism is a philosophy.** Observation and test vs "only the measurable is real". *Example:* a thermometer measures temperature; it does not prove that only temperature exists. *Transition:* the key case is you.
2. **Consciousness is the datum.** A body minus consciousness is dead and no material administration revives it; the Gita infers a soul (BG 2.17 purport; 13.34). Mainstream science infers the brain and admits the "how" is unresolved. *Transition:* the Gita's wider picture.
3. **Two energies, one director.** Matter including mind (7.4), the living being (7.5), nature working under direction (9.10). *Example:* a claim about a source behind nature is the Gita's claim, stated as a claim ([DYS-1](dys-01-s1.md) argued the design question). *Transition:* the first question.
4. **The texts honour the question of origins.** SB 1.1.1 and purport: a philosophical mind naturally asks about the origin of the creation. *Transition:* but how could anyone know?
5. **Knowledge of the self and God comes by a different route.** Reasoning (13.5), hearing (VL2-07), practice giving direct perception (9.2), the Lord's gift (10.10); not by instruments (2.25, 7.26). *Transition:* which requires a kind of faith.
6. **Reasonable faith.** Needed in every field (IYF's examples); sensible and verifiable; "apply your logic" (Prabhupada, 1966); the settled doubter alone is condemned (4.40). *Transition:* to P.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

> **Before you teach:** this audience will know more science than you do about their own field. Never bluff. "I do not know; I will check" earns more than a wrong confident answer.

1. **"Science has explained consciousness as brain activity. Why add a soul?"**
   - Science has shown strong correlations and has several theories of how experience arises; it has no consensus (F.3). The Gita's inference from the same datum is different (2.17 purport). Honest question; present both and the reasons for each.
2. **"Isn't 'God did it' just filling gaps science will close?"**
   - The Gita does not place God in gaps; 9.10 places Him behind the whole mechanism, laws included. Whether that is true is a philosophical question, not a race with the next discovery.
3. **"Prove the soul exists."**
   - The texts say it is "invisible, inconceivable" (2.25) and "Me no one knows" (7.26). No one can offer a laboratory proof, and the preacher should say so. The texts offer an argument (2.17), a method (9.2) and an invitation (10.10). That is a different kind of claim.
4. **"Scientists are biased against religion."**
   - Do not say this. Scientists hold every view about God. If a particular writer argues "nothing but matter", that is a philosophical position to engage, not evidence of bias. Misconception; correct it gently.
5. **"Einstein / Schrödinger believed in the Vedas."**
   - Schrödinger's interest in Vedanta is documented in his own books; the Einstein lines in circulation are unsourced. Use only what you have read in a primary source, and never as proof of anything (F.7).
6. **"Faith is the opposite of science."**
   - IYF's notes: every act of trust in an authorised expert is faith that is sensible and verifiable. Science itself runs on trust in peers and instruments. Prabhupada: "Apply your logic. Don't take it as a sentiment or as a blind faith" (lecture on BG 4.11–12, New York, 28 July 1966). BG 4.40 condemns the settled doubter, not the questioner.
7. **"Why does BG 4.40 say doubters 'fall down'? That sounds like a threat."**
   - Read the verse: it describes an outcome for one who never resolves doubt, in this world and the next. The purport's remedy is to follow revealed scripture with faith "and thereby be raised to the platform of knowledge". Pair it with 4.34 purport (blind following condemned). It is a diagnosis of a stuck state, not a threat.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The lab partner.** Divya's lab partner says, "Love, prayer, free will: all neurons. Get over it." Divya feels he is wrong but can only say "I believe differently".
- **Principles:** science vs scientism (F.1–2); BG 2.17 purport as an argument; F.3 (state the mainstream view).
- **Questions:** What did the partner measure, and what did he infer? What argument could Divya give rather than a belief? What should she concede?
- **Takeaway:** "Your inference is philosophical, and here is mine" is a stronger reply than "I believe".

**Case 2: The over-eager preacher.** Karan tells a physics class that "quantum mechanics proves the Vedas" and quotes Einstein. A student asks for the source. Karan has none.
- **Principles:** F.7 (overclaiming); VL1-08's rule on dating and science claims; BG 4.34 purport (absurd inquiry condemned applies to absurd assertion too).
- **Questions:** What did Karan lose, and for whom? What could he have said about physics and the Gita honestly? How does one recover credibility after an unsourced claim?
- **Takeaway:** One unsourced quotation costs the whole talk.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first)
1. Is science a method or a philosophy? *Expected:* a method; "only the measurable is real" is a philosophy added on.
2. What does the BG 2.17 purport say is spread all over the body? *Expected:* consciousness.
3. Does science say how experience arises from matter? *Expected:* No consensus; it shows correlations, and the "how" is an open question.
4. What does BG 9.10 say nature works under? *Expected:* the Lord's direction.
5. What is "sensible and verifiable" faith? *Expected:* trust that has credentials and coherence, and whose results can be checked.

**M.2 Activities (engagement block, ~90 min; choose two)**
- **A. Measured or inferred (30 min).** Pairs sort twelve claim cards (for example "the brain correlates with experience", "only matter exists", "the universe has a modelled history", "there is a soul") into three piles: measured, inferred, philosophy. They explain two cards to the room (10 min). Praise every honest "I'm not sure".
- **B. Fair hearing (35 min).** Groups of four: two state the mainstream view of consciousness as the others would accept it, then swap to state the Gita's view (BG 2.17). Rule: restate the other side to its holder's satisfaction before replying. Debrief: what did you learn about each view?
- **C. Evidence in my field (25 min).** Each person, from their own work or study (engineering, medicine, farming, trade, teaching), writes one thing their method measures and one question it does not ask. Share in threes; the preacher notes that every field has such a line.

**M.3 Reflection (private, optional).** In my own field, where does measurement end and interpretation begin? Which one claim about myself have I accepted as "scientific" without asking whether it was measured or inferred? Nobody needs to share.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Science is a method: observe, test, revise. It has given us medicine and machines and deserves respect. But "you are nothing but your brain" is not a measurement; it is a philosophy added on top of measurements. Scientists and philosophers agree that brain activity and experience are closely linked, and also that how experience arises from matter is not yet understood. Other talks in this course asked whether there is a designer ([DYS-1](dys-01-s1.md)) and what the soul is ([DYS-4](dys-01-s4.md)); this talk asks only what science can and cannot say.

The Bhagavad-gita (an ancient Sanskrit scripture, here in Srila Prabhupada's translation) offers a reasoned alternative. What is spread all over the body is consciousness, and a body without it is dead and cannot be revived by any material means; so, the Gita argues, consciousness comes from the soul, not from matter (BG 2.17 and purport). Matter is one energy of the Lord, and living beings are another (BG 7.4–5). The eight elements of matter are earth, water, fire, air, ether, mind, intelligence and false ego, so mind and intelligence are counted as subtle matter. Nature works "under My direction" (BG 9.10). The soul is "invisible" (BG 2.25), so no instrument will settle the matter; the texts offer reasoning, hearing, practice and grace instead (BG 13.5, 4.34, 9.2, 10.10).

Faith here is not blind. We act on trust in every field, but the faith the Gita recommends is sensible (it has credentials and coherence) and verifiable (its result can be seen). It condemns not the questioner but the person who never resolves doubt and finds no happiness (BG 4.40). Prabhupada said in 1966, "Apply your logic. Don't take it as a sentiment or as a blind faith."

## S2. Key ideas

- Science measures; scientism says only the measured is real. Keep them apart.
- Consciousness is the one thing everyone has; the Gita infers a soul from it (BG 2.17); mainstream science infers the brain and admits the "how" is open.
- Matter (including mind) and the living being are two energies (BG 7.4–5); nature works under direction (BG 9.10).
- The soul is not seen by instruments (BG 2.25); it is known by another route (BG 9.2, 10.10).
- Faith must be reasonable: sensible and verifiable. The settled doubter, not the questioner, is warned (BG 4.40).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **BG 2.17:** "That which pervades the entire body you should know to be indestructible." *For me:* my consciousness is the clue to what I am.
- **BG 9.10:** "This material nature… is working under My direction." *For me:* laws of nature and a director behind them are not rivals.
- **BG 4.40:** "For the doubting soul there is happiness neither in this world nor in the next." *For me:* questions are good; staying stuck in doubt is not.

## S4. Words to know

- **Atma:** the self or soul, which pervades the body as consciousness (BG 2.17).
- **Prakrti:** material nature, the Lord's separated energy, including mind, intelligence and false ego (BG 7.4).
- **Scientism:** the belief that only what science measures is real; a philosophy, not a scientific result.
- **Pratyaksa:** direct perception; BG 9.2 says this knowledge gives "direct perception of the self by realization".

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Has science disproved the soul?** No. It has shown that brain and experience are linked and has not settled how experience arises. The soul is defined as "invisible" (BG 2.25); the Gita's case is an argument, not a measurement.
2. **Isn't God just a gap-filler?** The Gita places God behind the whole of nature and its laws (BG 9.10), not in gaps.
3. **Are scientists biased against religion?** No. Scientists hold every view. If a writer says "nothing but matter", answer the argument.
4. **Did Einstein believe in the Vedas?** The quotations in circulation are unsourced. We do not use them.
5. **Is faith the opposite of science?** Every expert we trust is an act of reasonable faith: sensible and verifiable. Prabhupada: "Apply your logic. Don't take it as a sentiment or as a blind faith."

## S6. Check your understanding

1. In the purport to BG 2.17, what is "spread all over the body"?
2. Which three of the eight elements in BG 7.4 are not gross matter?
3. True or false: BG 2.25 says the soul can be seen with the right instrument.
4. What does BG 9.10 say material nature works under?
5. Which kind of person does BG 4.40 say finds no happiness?

**Answers:** 1. Consciousness (BG 2.17 purport). 2. Mind, intelligence and false ego (BG 7.4). 3. False: "the soul is invisible, inconceivable and immutable" (BG 2.25). 4. The Lord's direction (BG 9.10). 5. The faithless, doubting soul who doubts the revealed scriptures (BG 4.40).
