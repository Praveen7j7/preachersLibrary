# 16. Determination

> BSS (brahmacari training) · 120 min · drafted · SENSITIVE (celibacy; no private disclosure). Follows BIKC chapter 16. Builds on bss-02 (Dhruva and the three kinds of determination, BG 18.33-35) and bss-27 (discipline).

## A. Topic overview
| | |
|---|---|
| Batch / level | BSS, brahmacari training (Bhakti Siddhanta Sabha). Brahmacari = a celibate student who lives simply, serves the guru and studies scripture |
| Suggested duration | 120 minutes (lecture, with an "if time allows" extension) |
| Preparation time | About 2 hours: read BIKC ch. 16 (two pages), CC Madhya 17.14 and its purport, SB 7.15.22 and its purport, BG 6.24 and its purport, then this page |
| Difficulty | Intermediate |

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level
BSS members are "preparing the plan to join K.C as full time brahmachari" and "maintaining strictly the initiation vows"
(IYF KRA workbook, quoted in research/batch-criteria.md, section 6). A vow is kept by a prior decision, not by the strength
of the moment. This page gives that decision its scriptural basis and its practice; the pages before it gave the aim
(bss-16, bss-17) and the dangers (bss-21 to bss-23); the next ones give the daily protections.

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| VL1 | Not taught as a topic; at most, "decide your morning chanting in advance" |
| VL2 | Determination as a quality of the intelligence (BG 18.33-35, see bss-02) |
| VL3 | Fixing a chanting and hearing routine and keeping it (NOI 3) |
| SPS | Determination in sadhana and the first vows |
| DPS | Determination for service and leadership under pressure |
| BSS | This page: the decision not to fall, how it works, what feeds it (the BIKC chapter) |

Not done here: the three kinds of determination and the story of Dhruva (bss-02), the full routine of discipline (bss-27),
the stages of the fall (bss-05), methods of sense control (bss-24).

## C. Central message
Mercy and protection are always on offer, but they work through a person who has decided. A brahmacari decides in
advance, keeps the decision alive by a higher taste in chanting and by the company of devotees, and when he slips he
stands up again with the same decision.

## D. Learning outcomes
By the end, participants can:
1. Explain, from CC Madhya 17.14 and its purport, why even the Lord's company did not protect a man who was not determined.
2. Explain SB 7.15.22's point that a decision taken in advance conquers lusty desire "automatically", with the fasting example.
3. Retell the sparrow and Garuda story from the purport of BG 6.24 and say what it teaches about perseverance and the Lord's help.
4. Name what keeps determination alive (taste, association, an instruction to follow) and say why philosophy alone is not enough (BIKC).
5. Write one concrete decision for the coming week that a senior can check.

## E. Scriptural foundation
Use CC Madhya 17.14, SB 7.15.22, BG 6.24 and BG 9.14 in the talk. Others are for questions or the extension.
All verses and purports were read on Vedabase (Srila Prabhupada's translation and purport).

**CC Madhya 17.14**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/17/14/
> "Such a person must be a new man, and he must have a peaceful mind."
- **Context:** Lord Caitanya is preparing to leave Puri for Vrindavan. His devotees ask Him to take one brahmana with Him for cooking and carrying (CC Madhya 17.11-12). He declines to choose any of His close associates, because the others would be unhappy (17.13), and says He would take a new man with a peaceful mind.
- **Teaching:** The Lord wants a companion who is new and whose mind is peaceful.
- **Purport point:** The purport recalls Kala Krsnadasa, who accompanied the Lord in South India, fell victim to a woman and had to be rescued. It says: "One whose mind is not peaceful is agitated by certain drives, especially sex desire, even though he be in the company of Caitanya Mahāprabhu. Such a man will become a victim of women and will fall down even in the company of the Supreme Personality of Godhead." It adds that Maya is so strong that unless one is determined not to fall victim, even the Supreme Lord cannot give protection; the Lord and His representative always want to protect, but the person must take advantage of the personal contact, and one who thinks the Lord or His representative ordinary will fall. BIKC ch. 16 quotes this purport; the verse itself speaks only of the new man and the peaceful mind, so cite the purport for the "determination" sentence.
- **Application (preacher's):** Proximity to great persons is a gift to use, not a guarantee. A brahmacari living in a temple among senior devotees is in the same position as the Lord's companion: the protection is real, and it needs his own decision.
- **Use:** in the talk (I.1).

**CC Madhya 17.15**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/17/15/
> "He is an honest, learned scholar, and he is advanced in spiritual consciousness."
- **Context and purport point:** Svarupa Damodara recommends Balabhadra Bhattacarya in reply. The purport says the Lord wanted a new man, that He did not want a person like Kala Krsnadasa who would fall for women, and that Svarupa Damodara therefore pointed to Balabhadra (read the full purport before quoting).
- **Use:** background; shows the Lord's choice was by character, not by seniority.

**CC Madhya 9.226-228 and 9.233**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/9/226/ (and /227/, /228/, /233/)
> "He was a brāhmaṇa, but he met with the Bhaṭṭathāris there." (9.226)
- **Context:** On the Lord's South India tour (CC Madhya 9), He was accompanied by His servant Krsnadasa. In the region of Malara-desa a community called the Bhattathari lived (9.224).
- **Teaching:** 9.227: "With women the Bhaṭṭathāris allured the brāhmaṇa Kṛṣṇadāsa, who was simple and gentle. By virtue of their bad association, they polluted his intelligence." 9.228: he went to them early in the morning and the Lord went quickly to find him. 9.229: the Lord asked, "Why are you keeping My brāhmaṇa assistant?" 9.231-232: the Bhattatharis came running with weapons to hurt the Lord; their weapons fell from their hands and struck their own bodies, some were cut to pieces and the others ran away. 9.233: "Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu grabbed Kṛṣṇadāsa by the hair and took him away."
- **Use:** in the talk, told as the verses tell it (J.1).

**SB 7.15.22**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/15/22/
> "By making plans with determination, one should give up lusty desires for sense gratification."
- **Context:** Narada instructs Yudhisthira on conquering the enemies of the mind. BIKC ch. 16 quotes this verse as spoken by "the greatest brahmacari", Narada.
- **Teaching:** The verse pairs each enemy with its remedy: lust is conquered by determination, anger by giving up envy, greed by considering the disadvantages of accumulating wealth, fear by discussing the truth.
- **Purport point:** Citing Visvanatha Cakravarti Thakura, the purport says one cannot stop thinking of women altogether, since it is natural and one sees women even on the street; but if one is determined not to live with a woman, one does not become lusty on seeing her. If one is determined not to have sex, one can automatically conquer lusty desire. The example: on a day when one has decided to fast, one conquers hunger and thirst naturally.
- **Application (preacher's):** The decision is made before the situation, calmly, and then needs no new debate. Treat the "automatically" as the purport's own word: it describes what a settled decision does, not what an unsettled wish does.
- **Use:** in the talk (I.2).

**BG 6.24**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/24/
> "One should engage oneself in the practice of yoga with determination and faith and not be deviated from the path."
- **Context:** Krsna describes the yogi who controls the mind; Arjuna will object in 6.33-34 that the mind is restless (bss-27 covers that exchange).
- **Purport point:** The purport asks the practitioner to be determined, patient and sure of success, not becoming discouraged by delay. It quotes Rupa Gosvami (NOI 3): bhakti succeeds by enthusiasm, perseverance and determination, by following the prescribed duties, in the association of devotees. It then tells the story of the sparrow whose eggs the ocean carried away: she began to empty the ocean with her beak and was laughed at; Garuda heard, was pleased by her determination and made the ocean return the eggs. It concludes that if anyone follows the principles of bhakti-yoga with great determination, the Lord will surely help.
- **Use:** in the talk (I.3, with J.2).

**NOI 3**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/3/
- Quoted inside the purport of BG 6.24 as Srila Rupa Gosvami's six helps to bhakti. Builds on bss-02, which uses NOI 3 as a whole; here it is used only as the purport's authority for perseverance and determination. **Use:** background.

**BG 9.14**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/9/14/
> "Always chanting My glories, endeavoring with great determination, bowing down before Me, these great souls perpetually worship Me with devotion."
- **Purport point:** The purport says a mahatma has firm determination to reach the Lord's association and engages mind, body and words in His service. It notes there are "determined" activities, such as fasting on Ekadasi and on the Lord's appearance days, that the great souls observe strictly. It adds that this service can be done in a happy mood, guided by an expert spiritual master, as a householder, sannyasi or brahmacari.
- **Application (preacher's):** Determination in bhakti is positive: chanting and endeavouring together. The verse puts "chanting My glories" first.
- **Use:** in the talk (I.4).

**BG 7.28**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/7/28/
> "…they engage themselves in My service with determination."
- **Purport point:** Those who have conquered sinful reactions can accept devotional service and rise to pure knowledge; this elevation is possible in the association of pure devotees and is rooted in SB 5.5.2 (service of devotees as the door of liberation).
- **Use:** background; links determination to association (builds on bss-07).

**BG 18.73**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/73/
> "I am now firm and free from doubt and am prepared to act according to Your instructions."
- **Purport point:** The purport says Krsna consciousness is acting according to Krsna's order, and that illusion is overcome by the mercy of the Lord or of a pure devotee; the living entity is by nature a servant.
- **Application (preacher's):** Arjuna's firmness is firmness in an instruction received. For the brahmacari that instruction comes through the guru, the vows and the scripture.
- **Use:** in the talk, closing (I.4); the final line of Arjuna is the model of a decision.

## F. Philosophical explanation
**Principle.** In Srila Prabhupada's purports, determination stands on three supports. First, a settled decision taken in advance (SB 7.15.22, with the fasting example). Second, perseverance with faith, with the Lord's help for the one who helps himself (BG 6.24 and NOI 3). Third, a goal that is the Lord's service itself (BG 9.14, 18.73), because determination aimed only at "not doing" something is dry and tires.
**BIKC's emphasis.** BIKC ch. 16 says without full determination no one can get free from sexual bondage, and that although the eight aspects of celibacy seem almost impossible, Narada's verse shows it is possible. Then, in the next section, it asks how determination is maintained: philosophical understanding of the miseries of material life helps but is no guarantee, since even philosophically competent devotees have slipped; unless one develops a sense of bhakti, satisfaction within celibacy is almost impossible in Kali-yuga. We are parts of Krsna, the Supreme Enjoyer, with a pleasure-seeking nature (Vedanta-sutra 1.1.12, as BIKC cites it), so without some real taste from devotional service we are drawn to material enjoyment. BIKC adds that early bliss comes as a strong dose of nectar, and later that one has to work for it. If we serve with our own pleasure in mind we do not get it; we are satisfied by satisfying Krsna, as the limbs are satisfied by feeding the stomach. (Summary; see O. The Vedanta-sutra citation was not checked here: *Requires source review*.)
**Connections.** Higher taste (bss-17, bss-24) feeds the decision; association (bss-07, BG 7.28) protects it; discipline (bss-27) is its daily form; the stages of the fall (bss-05) show what a lapse of attention does.
**Consequences.** Without a decision, every moment becomes a negotiation. With one, most negotiations never begin. And if there is a slip, the decision is the thing one renews, not the thing one has lost.
**Common misunderstandings.**
1. "Determination means gritted teeth." The purport of BG 6.24 describes patience and cheerful perseverance, and BG 9.14 puts chanting first.
2. "If I decide firmly, I do not need the guru or devotees." CC Madhya 17.14's purport says the opposite: the person must take advantage of the personal contact of the Lord's representative.
3. "A slip proves I was never determined." The sparrow's story is of repeated effort, and the Lord helps one who keeps at it; see bss-05 for the way back after a fall.
4. "Protection from falling is someone else's job." The purport of CC Madhya 17.14 says Kala Krsnadasa "fell victim to a woman" even in the Lord's company, and that "unless one is determined not to fall victim, even the Supreme Personality of Godhead cannot give protection"; SB 7.15.22's purport locates the decision in the man's own resolve.
**How to pitch it.** Warm and plain, to young men who want to succeed. Put the Lord's help first and the decision second.

## H. Opening and engagement
**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes).**
1. One round of kirtana or a short prayer; say the title.
2. Say: "Today we look at the Lord choosing a travelling companion. He has many devotees to choose from, and He says He wants one thing in the person He takes. We will find out what."
3. Read CC Madhya 17.14 slowly. Say: "Notice what is not in the list: not learning, not seniority, not even a promise to try."
4. Say: "In two hours we will understand why, and what we can do about it."
**H.2 Opening questions.**
1. "Think of one promise you kept for a long time (a daily routine, a fast, a training schedule, anything). What made it possible?" (Take three answers; praise effort.)
2. "What is the difference between 'I will try' and 'I have decided'?"
3. "Which is easier to keep: a decision made calmly in the morning, or one made in the middle of a difficulty? Why?"

## I. Core teaching points
**I.1 The Lord's protection works through the person (CC Madhya 17.14; J.1).** The Lord wants a companion whose mind is peaceful and new. The purport shows why: a man agitated by desire falls even in the company of the Lord; Maya is strong, and the protection needs the person's own determination and respect for the contact. Transition: "So what is the decision, and how is it taken?"
**I.2 A decision made in advance (SB 7.15.22).** Narada, the eternal brahmacari, says lust is conquered by determination. The purport (Visvanatha Cakravarti) is realistic: one cannot stop noticing, but a settled decision not to engage means that seeing does not make one lusty; it compares this with a fasting day, when hunger is naturally conquered. Application (preacher's): decide the pattern of the day in advance, such as who goes where with whom, what is looked at and read, so that no moment needs a new argument. Transition: "But what if it is a long road?"
**I.3 Perseverance, and the Lord's help (BG 6.24, NOI 3).** Tell the sparrow story as the purport tells it (J.2). The purport's conclusion is that the Lord helps those who persevere. NOI 3 lists determination with enthusiasm and patience, the prescribed duties, and association of devotees: determination is one member of a team. Transition: "What keeps the team alive?"
**I.4 What feeds the decision (BG 9.14, 7.28, 18.73; BIKC).** (a) Taste: BIKC says philosophy is not enough; devotional service must give real taste, and that comes by hearing and chanting and by serving to please Krsna. (b) Devotees: BG 7.28's purport. (c) An instruction to follow: Arjuna's closing words in BG 18.73; for a brahmacari, the vows and the guru's order. (d) The positive object: BG 9.14, "chanting My glories" with determination. Close by saying: the decision is renewed daily, as one renews a vow of fasting, and if one stumbles one stands and renews it.

## J. Stories and illustrations
**J.1 Kala Krsnadasa (CC Madhya 9.226-233 and the purport of 17.14).** In South India, Lord Caitanya was accompanied by a servant, a brahmana named Krsnadasa (9.226). In Malara-desa he met a community called the Bhattathari: "With women the Bhaṭṭathāris allured the brāhmaṇa Kṛṣṇadāsa, who was simple and gentle. By virtue of their bad association, they polluted his intelligence" (9.227). Early one morning he went to them and the Lord went quickly to find him (9.228). The Lord asked the Bhattatharis, "Why are you keeping My brāhmaṇa assistant?" (9.229), and said that He and they were both in the renounced order, yet they were purposefully giving Him pain (9.230). They came running from all sides with weapons to hurt the Lord (9.231); their weapons fell from their hands and struck their own bodies, some were cut to pieces and the others ran away (9.232). Amid the roaring and crying, "Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu grabbed Kṛṣṇadāsa by the hair and took him away" (9.233). Years later, in CC Madhya 17, the Lord's words about a new man with a peaceful mind recall this; the purport says Kala Krsnadasa "fell victim to a woman" and that such a man "will fall down even in the company of the Supreme Personality of Godhead". Tell it as the verses tell it, without added dialogue. **Devotional lesson:** the Lord personally follows and retrieves a devotee who has gone astray; and the Lord's mercy is not an excuse for carelessness, because He says even He cannot protect the one who will not be determined. Everything in the story is also an invitation to ask for the Lord's protection.
**J.2 The sparrow and Garuda (BG 6.24 purport).** A sparrow laid her eggs on the shore and the ocean carried them off with its waves. She asked the ocean to return them; it ignored her. She began to empty the ocean with her beak, and everyone laughed. The news spread to Garuda, Lord Visnu's carrier. He pitied his small sister, came and was pleased by her determination, and told the ocean to return the eggs or he would take up the task. The ocean, frightened, returned them. **Devotional lesson:** the Lord's own servant helps the one who keeps trying. Note that the sparrow never knew whether she would succeed; her determination was the whole of her offering.
**J.3 Arjuna's closing words (BG 18.73).** After hearing Krsna he says he is firm and ready to act on His instruction. **Devotional lesson:** determination ends in surrender to an instruction.
**Illustration (preacher's, labelled).** A traveller who has decided before dawn which road he will take does not stand at every fork. A traveller who has not decided is at the mercy of every signpost. Explain the point plainly: the brahmacari chooses his road in advance, the destination is the Lord's service, and his guru's instructions tell him which way to go at each fork.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections
1. **"I have decided many times and still slipped. Is it useless?"** The sparrow's story is of repeated effort (BG 6.24 purport). Renew the decision, tell a trusted senior, keep chanting. If you feel low or ashamed, please speak to a senior or a qualified counsellor (Tele-MANAS 14416 in India, or your local crisis service); shame is not part of the instruction.
2. **"Why does the purport say the Lord cannot protect?"** It is about the person's cooperation; the Lord and His representative always want to give protection, the purport says, but the person must take advantage of the contact.
3. **"Is determination just willpower?"** Willpower is one part. The purport of BG 6.24 gives the whole recipe: enthusiasm, patience, duties, association (NOI 3), and the Lord's help. BIKC adds taste in service.
4. **"Does SB 7.15.22 mean I will never feel anything?"** The purport says one cannot stop seeing, and that a settled decision means that seeing does not make one lusty. It does not promise a mind free of noticing; it promises that the mind will not be run by it.
5. **"What if my determination is for an ashram I am not sure of?"** Speak to your mentor; the decision of ashram is taken with guidance (the batch's KRAs speak of "moving sensitively for his decision").

## L. Case studies
**L.1 Invented case for discussion.** A young brahmacari has kept his routine well for two years. He starts accompanying a senior devotee's family to events "to help". After some weeks he finds himself looking forward to the family's company more than to the morning programme. *Dilemma:* he does not want to offend the family, and nothing has "happened". *Principles:* CC Madhya 17.14 (do not rely on proximity alone), SB 7.15.22 (decide in advance), BG 7.28 (association). *Questions:* What does he decide in advance? Whom does he tell? What does he continue to do for the family? *Analysis:* he tells his mentor early, keeps service to the family within clear limits (see bss-30), and renews the morning programme and the company of other brahmacaris. *Takeaway:* a determined person acts early, before anything wrong has happened, by speaking to his mentor and adjusting his routine.
**L.2 Invented case for discussion (extension).** A brahmacari who has had a slip feels his vow has ended. *Principles:* BG 6.24 (the sparrow), BG 9.14 (chanting). *Questions:* What does the sparrow do after each wave? Who does he go to? *Takeaway:* stand up, tell a senior, renew the decision, continue chanting (see bss-05 for the way back).

## M. Discussion and reflection
**M.1 Closing check questions (with expected answers).**
1. *What did Lord Caitanya say He wanted in a companion?* A new man with a peaceful mind (CC Madhya 17.14).
2. *What does the purport say about Maya and determination?* Maya is so strong that unless one is determined, even the Lord cannot protect; the person must take advantage of the contact.
3. *How does SB 7.15.22's purport compare determination to a fast?* On a day when one has decided to fast, one naturally conquers hunger; likewise a settled decision conquers lusty desire.
4. *What did the sparrow do, and who helped her?* She began to empty the ocean with her beak; Garuda helped, pleased by her determination (BG 6.24 purport).
5. *What does BIKC say is needed besides understanding?* Real taste in devotional service, got by hearing, chanting and serving to please Krsna.
6. *What is the first thing a brahmacari does after a slip?* Stands up, tells a trusted senior, renews the decision and continues chanting.
**M.2 Activities.**
a) *One decision (10 min, pairs, private).* Each writes one decision for the week in the form "I have decided that ...", about the day's pattern (not about private struggles). Partner asks only: "Is it clear? Is it kind to yourself? Who will check it?" No one reads aloud unless he chooses.
b) *Sparrow retelling (10 min, groups of 4).* One person tells the sparrow story in 90 seconds; the group answers: "What did she believe about the ocean? About Garuda?" Debrief: she believed help comes to the persevering.
c) *Short drama (15 min, extension).* Roles: Lord Caitanya, Balabhadra Bhattacarya, Svarupa Damodara, narrator. Script of five lines, using only the content of CC Madhya 17.13-15 (label as dramatisation). Debrief: Why did the Lord not choose by seniority?
**M.3 Reflection.** "What decision, made in advance, would make most of my difficult moments unnecessary?" Private.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary
Lord Caitanya once said He would take on a journey only "a new man" with a peaceful mind. Srila Prabhupada explains in his purport that a man who was with the Lord earlier, Kala Krsnadasa, had been led astray by bad company and had to be rescued. A mind that is not peaceful is agitated by desires, especially sex desire, even in the company of the Lord. Maya is so strong that unless a person is determined not to fall, even the Lord cannot protect him; the Lord and His representatives always want to protect, but the person must take advantage of their company.
So what is determination? Srila Prabhupada's purport to SB 7.15.22 says: decide in advance. We cannot stop seeing; but if we are determined not to engage, seeing does not make us lusty, just as on a day we have decided to fast, we are not troubled by hunger. Determination also needs patience. In the purport to BG 6.24 there is a story of a small sparrow whose eggs the ocean washed away. She began to empty the ocean with her beak. Garuda, Lord Visnu's carrier, was pleased and made the ocean return the eggs. The Lord helps those who keep trying.
What keeps determination alive? The book *Brahmacarya in Krishna consciousness* says understanding alone is not enough; we need real taste in chanting and service, and we get it by hearing, chanting and serving to please Krsna. We also need devotees (BG 7.28), and an instruction to follow, like Arjuna's "prepared to act according to Your instructions" (BG 18.73). If we slip, we stand up, speak to a senior and renew the decision.

## S2. Key ideas
- A decision made in advance is stronger than a feeling in the moment.
- The Lord and the guru want to protect us; we must take advantage of their company.
- Perseverance is rewarded by the Lord's help (the sparrow and Garuda).
- Taste in chanting and the company of devotees keep the decision alive.
- After a slip, stand up, speak to a senior and begin again.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **SB 7.15.22**: "By making plans with determination, one should give up lusty desires for sense gratification." *For me:* I decide in advance and I do not argue at every moment.
- **BG 6.24**: "One should engage oneself in the practice of yoga with determination and faith and not be deviated from the path." *For me:* I keep going, with faith, even if I am laughed at or delayed.
- **BG 9.14**: "Always chanting My glories, endeavoring with great determination…" *For me:* my determination is for chanting and service, not only for avoiding things.

## S4. Words to know
- **Brahmacari:** a celibate student living simply and serving his guru.
- **Determination (niscaya):** a settled decision that is not reopened at every moment.
- **Maya:** the Lord's material energy, which pulls us away from Krsna.
- **Purport:** Srila Prabhupada's explanation of a verse.
- **Garuda:** the large bird who carries Lord Visnu.
- **Sannyasi / grhastha:** a renounced monk; a married householder.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *Is determination just willpower?* No. It is a decision made in advance, perseverance, the company of devotees and taste in chanting (BG 6.24 purport, NOI 3, BG 7.28).
2. *What if I fall?* Stand up. Tell a trusted senior, keep chanting, renew the decision. The sparrow kept trying. If you feel low, speak to a senior or a counsellor (Tele-MANAS 14416 in India).
3. *Why could Kala Krsnadasa not be protected even by the Lord's company?* The purport says protection works when the person is determined and takes advantage of the contact.
4. *Is it wrong to notice others?* The purport to SB 7.15.22 says noticing is natural; the decision is not to engage.

**Answers:** 1. A new man with a peaceful mind. 2. Fasting on a day one has decided to fast: hunger is conquered naturally. 3. Garuda, pleased by her determination (BG 6.24 purport). 4. Real taste in devotional service. 5. "I am now firm and free from doubt and am prepared to act according to Your instructions."
