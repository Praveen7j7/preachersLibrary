# Absolute necessity of spirituality

> **VL1 · 45–60 min · review: sources checked, awaiting senior review.** Stress, anxiety and depression are named.
> Some listeners may be living with them. Read the note in K before teaching.

## A. Topic overview

| | |
|---|---|
| Batch / level | VL1 (Vedic Level 1), a stand-alone talk |
| Suggested duration | 45 min (G gives a 30-minute cut) |
| Preparation time | About 90 min, the first time |
| Difficulty | Introductory |
| Prerequisites | None |
| Audience | Students and young professionals, many with no settled faith, many doubtful about "religion" |
| Teaching objective | Show, from experience first and scripture second, that comfort and achievement do not by themselves give lasting satisfaction, and that the Gita points to an inner dimension worth exploring |

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

At VL1 the question is "Why should I even explore this?" This talk answers it without asking for belief: look
honestly at what we are chasing, and at whether it delivers. It is the natural partner of VL1-06 (suffering) and
VL1-07 (changing the heart).

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **VL1** (here) | Experience-first. Name the gap between having more and feeling fulfilled; offer the Gita's diagnosis (desire and the senses) and its direction (connect with something higher). No proof of God |
| VL2 | Reasoned faith: the soul, reincarnation and evidence for the Absolute Truth |
| VL3 | Practice: chanting, sadhana, regulation |
| SPS | Spiritual life as a settled direction: purpose, then the daily standards that protect it |
| DPS | The same need seen from service: steadiness for those who carry responsibility |
| BSS | Brahmacari life: why sense control satisfies more than sense indulgence |

**Not done here:**
- Claiming that science, technology or modern education are bad.
- Quoting alarming statistics without a checked source (see O).
- Telling anyone that distress means they lack faith.
- Pressure to commit to anything.

## C. Central message

Better outer conditions are valuable, but they do not, by themselves, answer the need of the person living in them.
The Gita says that peace comes from steadiness within, and that steadiness grows when we connect with something
beyond our own desires. Spirituality is not an optional decoration. It is the missing part of the answer.

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. Describe from their own life the gap between "having more" and "being satisfied".
2. Explain, in plain words, the Gita's picture of why chasing desires does not bring peace (BG 2.70).
3. Identify lust, anger and greed as the three "gates" the Gita warns about (BG 16.21), and say how they show up in daily life.
4. Say why "technology plus spirituality" is a fairer proposal than "technology instead of spirituality".
5. Choose one small step to explore the inner side of life.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** six references are enough for the talk (BG 2.66, 2.70, 3.37, 16.21, SB 5.5.1, SB 1.2.8). The rest are for questions.

**BG 2.66**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/66/
> "…there is no possibility of peace. And how can there be any happiness without peace?"
- **Context:** Krsna describes the person of steady intelligence; the verse says what is lacking when there is no connection with the Supreme.
- **Teaching:** Without connection with the Supreme there is neither transcendental intelligence nor a steady
  mind; without them there is no peace, and without peace no happiness.
- **Purport point:** Disturbance comes from "want of an ultimate goal"; Prabhupada says real peace comes only in
  relationship with Krsna (citing BG 5.29).
- **Application (preacher's):** Many of us chase happiness directly. The verse suggests happiness is a result of peace, and peace of steadiness.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 2.70**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/70/
> "A person who is not disturbed by the incessant flow of desires – that enter like rivers into the ocean… – can alone achieve peace, and not the man who strives to satisfy such desires."
- **Purport point:** The ocean is always being filled, yet stays still and within its limits; so is one who is fixed in Krsna consciousness. The one who strives to satisfy every desire does not find peace.
- **Application (preacher's):** The image fits a consumer culture: new desires arrive faster than any can be fulfilled.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 3.37**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/37/
> "It is lust only, Arjuna, which is born of contact with the material mode of passion and later transformed into wrath, and which is the all-devouring sinful enemy of this world."
- **Purport point:** Prabhupada explains that the living being's original love for Krsna becomes distorted into lust, as milk turns to yogurt on contact with a sour substance.
- **Application (preacher's):** At VL1, take "lust" in the wide sense of insatiable craving (for things, status, approval), then note that it turns into anger when blocked. Do not make the talk about sex.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 16.21**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/16/21/
> "There are three gates leading to this hell – lust, anger and greed. Every sane man should give these up…"
- **Purport point:** One tries to satisfy lust; when that fails, anger and greed arise.
- **Application (preacher's):** Present "hell" as the Gita's strong word for a state of agony, and invite listeners to test the claim against what they see: where do rage, envy and compulsive chasing lead?
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 5.5.1**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/5/1/
> "…one who has been awarded this human form should not work hard day and night simply for sense gratification, which is available even for dogs and hogs that eat stool."
- **Context:** Rsabhadeva advising His sons.
- **Teaching:** A human being should not labour day and night only for sense pleasure, which even animals get;
  one should take up austerity for devotional service, which purifies the heart and leads to lasting happiness.
- **Caution:** The wording is blunt. Quote it only if the audience can hear it as a statement of human potential. A gentle paraphrase is better at VL1: "Human life is for more than eating, sleeping and chasing pleasure."
- **Use:** in the talk, paraphrased.

**SB 1.2.8**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/8/
> "The occupational activities a man performs according to his own position are only so much useless labor if they do not provoke attraction for the message of the Personality of Godhead."
- **Application (preacher's):** For a young professional, the sharp question: "What is all my effort for?" Present this as the text's claim, not as proof.
- **Use:** in the talk (brief).

**For questions (K):**
- **BG 5.22**: Verified. Pleasures born of contact with the senses "have a beginning and an end", so the wise do not delight in them.
- **BG 18.38**: Verified. Happiness from sense contact "appears like nectar at first but poison at the end" (the mode of passion).
- **BG 14.7**: Verified. Passion is "born of unlimited desires and longings".
- **BG 6.5**: Verified. The mind is friend and enemy of the conditioned soul; we must deliver ourselves by it, not degrade ourselves.
- **BG 7.16**: Verified. The distressed, the seeker of wealth, the inquisitive and the seeker of knowledge begin to turn to the Lord. Shows why such a talk is a natural start.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** The Gita's diagnosis is that the living being, whose nature is to love and serve the Lord, tries to find satisfaction in
   matter and sense contact. Desire, unfed by that deeper connection, becomes lust, and when blocked turns to anger and greed (BG 3.37, 16.21).
   Peace, the precondition of happiness, comes from steadiness within (BG 2.66, 2.70).
2. **Why it matters.** Many people are materially better off than their grandparents, yet report stress, loneliness and
   restlessness. (This is an observation to invite from the room. It is not backed here by statistics: see O.)
3. **Connections.** Leads to VL1-06 (how to carry suffering), VL1-07 (transforming the heart) and VL2 (who is the self).
4. **Consequences.** If satisfaction lives on the inside, outer success is necessary but not sufficient; spiritual practice is not a luxury for the old or the failed.
5. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - **"Spirituality means rejecting the world."** The talk says outer progress is useful. The claim is that it is incomplete.
   - **"Unhappy people lack faith."** Never. Distress is a human condition (see K).
   - **"Therefore therapy is unnecessary."** Spiritual ideas never replace medical or psychological care.
6. **Pitch for VL1.** Start with the room's own experience. Use "inner peace", "satisfaction", "purpose". Use
   *lust, anger, greed* in their everyday sense. No pressure, no Sanskrit.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet, say your name, and say the talk is an exploration, not a demand to believe.
2. Put the title on the board and ask the room to say it in their own words.
3. Ask H.2 question 1; take two or three answers and thank each by name.
4. Write the answers on the board; you return to them at minute 14.
5. Give the transition line below, then begin the paradox segment.

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the room's own life)
1. What is one thing you hoped for this year that you already got? How did it feel after a week?
2. When you reach a goal, how soon does the next one appear?
3. If you could have one thing that made you truly settled, what would it be?

- **Opening question:** "Think of something you wanted badly a year ago and got. How long did the satisfaction last?" Take two or three answers. "Today's talk is about that gap."
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** A student works hard for a campus placement and gets it. A week later, he is already anxious about the next goal. What happened?
- **Transition:** "A text spoken on a battlefield addresses exactly this. Its answer is not 'want less' but 'find what you really want'."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** exams, careers, comparison on social media, family expectation: everyone is chasing something.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Achievement is good but does not close the gap.** *Example:* the placement scenario. *Reference:* none needed; ask the room. *Transition:* why?
2. **Desire is an ocean that is never full.** Peace comes to the one who is not disturbed by the endless flow of desires (BG 2.70). *Example:* a phone upgrade cycle (contemporary example, not a claim about any person). *Transition:* where does the restlessness come from?
3. **Craving becomes anger and greed.** Lust blocked becomes wrath (BG 3.37); lust, anger, greed are the three gates (BG 16.21). *Example:* the road-rage or exam-result mood. *Transition:* is there a different source of satisfaction?
4. **Happiness needs peace, and peace needs steadiness.** BG 2.66. *Example:* a day with no major event, yet a restless mind. *Transition:* so what is the missing piece?
5. **Human life has a higher possibility.** SB 5.5.1 (paraphrased), SB 1.2.8. *Example:* the questions people ask on a sleepless night: "What is it all for?" *Transition:* to the takeaway.

## J. Stories and illustrations

- **Rsabhadeva's advice to his sons** (SB 5.5.1). Introduce the speaker and the verse; do not add details the text does not give.
- **Arjuna on the battlefield** as the setting of the Gita: a capable person overwhelmed. Keep to what the text says (BG 1.28–2.9 is the background; not cited here).
- **Contemporary:** the "ocean and rivers" image (BG 2.70) is in the text; the wedding-season or sale-season illustration is the preacher's own, and labelled as such.
- No scriptural story is invented. IYF's own slides mention a "mountain of tyres" and other pictures; use them only if you can source them.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

> **Before you teach:** someone in the room may be anxious, depressed or burnt out. If a question becomes personal,
> thank the person, do not analyse their situation in public, and offer to talk afterwards. If you see signs of
> serious distress, encourage professional help; it is fully compatible with spiritual life. Never imply that
> distress is a sign of weak faith.

1. **"Isn't science enough? It has given us everything."**
   - Science and technology have given real goods, and the talk should say so. The talk's claim is that they address outer needs, while the question "what is satisfying?" is about the person using them. The proposal is "science with spirituality", not "instead of". *(This is the preacher's framing; do not attribute it to a verse.)*
2. **"Plenty of non-religious people are happy and kind."**
   - Agreed; say so. The talk makes no claim that only religious people are happy. It asks what lasts and what answers the question of purpose.
3. **"Isn't this telling me to give up my ambitions?"**
   - No. BG 6.5 asks us to lift ourselves with our own mind. The question is what the ambitions serve (SB 1.2.8).
4. **"Is happiness from the senses wrong?"**
   - The Gita is realistic: such pleasure has a beginning and an end, so the wise do not delight in it (BG 5.22), and the sweet beginning can turn bitter (BG 18.38). The talk's point is these limits and their cost, not guilt; the Gita's direction is a higher taste (BG 2.59, see VL1-03).
5. **"I'm stressed and anxious. Will spirituality fix it?"**
   - Do not promise. Say that practices like reflection and good association can help many people, that medical and psychological help is also valid, and that both can go together.
6. **"Why is spirituality 'absolutely necessary'? That's a strong claim."**
   - Be honest: this is the title's claim, and the talk offers reasons, not a proof. Let the listener decide after seeing the argument.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The topper.** Meera topped her college and got her dream offer. Three months in, she feels empty and tells a friend, "I got everything I wanted."
- **Dilemma:** What can the friend say that is neither "you're ungrateful" nor "just chant"?
- **Principles:** BG 2.70 (desires never fill); BG 2.66 (peace before happiness).
- **Questions:** What was Meera expecting? What would "enough" look like?
- **Suggested analysis:** Listening first. Then suggest asking not "what more?" but "what for?" (SB 1.2.8). Mention that, if the emptiness is heavy or lasting, talking to a professional is wise.
- **Takeaway:** Fulfilment is a question of direction, not only of arrival.

**Case 2: The sceptic.** Dev says, "Religion is for people who couldn't handle reality."
- **Principles:** BG 7.16 (the inquisitive and the knowledge-seeker also begin the path); BG 2.66.
- **Questions:** What does Dev's objection get right? Can a person be rational and still ask what life is for?
- **Takeaway:** Welcome the objection; it is the start of inquiry.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first; praise every try)
1. Does the talk say comfort and achievement are bad? *No: they are useful but incomplete.*
2. In BG 2.70, what are the "rivers" and the "ocean"? *Desires keep flowing in; the ocean stays steady.*
3. What does BG 2.66 say comes before happiness? *Peace.*
4. What are the three gates in BG 16.21? *Lust, anger, greed.*
5. What is the difference between "technology instead of spirituality" and "technology plus spirituality"? *The second keeps the real goods of science and adds the answer to "what for?"*

**M.2 Activity (about 8 minutes): "Five wants"**
1. In pairs, 2 min: each writes "Five things I want in the next 5 years".
2. 2 min: each privately asks, "If I got all five, what would I feel a month later?"
3. 3 min: volunteers share what they noticed (sharing the answer is optional, never the list). Thank each volunteer.
4. 1 min: link to BG 2.70 and 2.66: steadiness first, then peace, then happiness.

**M.3 Reflection**
- **Reflection:** What did you want a year ago that you got? How long did the satisfaction last?
- **Application (private, optional):** Which of lust, anger or greed is loudest in my week? *(Nobody needs to share.)*

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Most of us are chasing something: marks, a job, money, approval. Getting these is good, and technology and science have given real benefits. But many people find the satisfaction does not last. Think of a goal you reached: how soon did the next one appear? Many non-religious people are happy and kind; the talk only asks what lasts and what gives purpose. It does not ask you to give up your ambitions, only to ask what they are for (SB 1.2.8).

The Bhagavad-gita says desires are like rivers flowing into an ocean that is never full, and that peace comes to the person who stays steady and is not carried away by them (BG 2.70). Without peace there is no happiness (BG 2.66), so peace comes first. Craving that is blocked turns into anger, and craving that wants more turns into greed; lust, anger and greed are the three "gates" to trouble (BG 16.21). Sense pleasures are real but have a beginning and an end (BG 5.22), so the wise do not build their lives on them. The point is their limits, not guilt.

The Gita suggests that we need something within, a connection with something higher, to be at peace. Science answers "how"; "what for?" needs more, so the fair proposal is science with spirituality. If you feel anxious, spiritual practice can help many people, medical or psychological help is also good, and the two can go together. This is an invitation to explore, not a demand to believe.

## S2. Key ideas

- Having more is not the same as feeling satisfied.
- Desires keep coming, like rivers into the ocean; peace comes from steadiness (BG 2.70).
- Craving, anger and greed are three "gates" to trouble (BG 16.21).
- Science and spirituality are not enemies; the question is what is missing.
- Human life allows us to ask what it is all for.

## S3. Verses to remember

- **BG 2.66:** "…there is no possibility of peace. And how can there be any happiness without peace?" *For me:* look for peace first.
- **BG 2.70:** Peace comes to one who is not disturbed by the endless flow of desires. *For me:* I do not have to fulfil every desire to be at peace.
- **BG 16.21:** Lust, anger and greed are the three gates leading to "hell"; they "lead to the degradation of the soul". *For me:* I can notice which one is strongest in my week.

## S4. Words to know

- **Lust (in the Gita's sense):** strong craving for pleasure or gain.
- **Peace:** a steady mind that desires do not push around.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Isn't science enough?**
   Science gives real benefits. The talk suggests it answers "how", while "what for?" needs more.
2. **Are non-religious people unhappy?**
   No. Many are happy and kind. The question is what lasts and what gives purpose.
3. **Do I have to give up my ambitions?**
   No. The question is what they are for (SB 1.2.8).
4. **Is pleasure bad?**
   The Gita says sense pleasures have a beginning and an end, so the wise do not build their lives on them (BG 5.22). The point is their limits, not guilt.
5. **I feel anxious. Will this fix it?**
   Not on its own. Spiritual practice can help many people, and medical or psychological help is also good. They can go together.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. In BG 2.70, desires are compared to: (a) rivers entering an ocean, (b) clouds, (c) fire.
2. Name the three "gates" in BG 16.21.
3. True or false: the talk says technology is bad.
4. In your own words, what does BG 2.66 say comes before happiness?
5. Give one example from your own life of a goal that gave a short-lived satisfaction.

**Answers:** 1. (a) (BG 2.70). 2. Lust, anger and greed (BG 16.21). 3. False; the talk says outer progress is useful but incomplete.
4. Peace (BG 2.66). 5. Your own answer.

## S7. Try this week

- **Practice:** When you get something you wanted this week, note how long the satisfaction lasts. Write one line.
