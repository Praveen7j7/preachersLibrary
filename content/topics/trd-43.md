# How rasa arises: sthayi-bhava, vibhava, anubhava, sattvika and vyabhicari bhavas

> **TRD · up to 180 min · review: drafted · `sensitive: true` (rasa; ecstatic symptoms; the head reviews).** Group G, page 3. trd-33 named
> the five topics of the second division of the Bhakti-rasamrta-sindhu; trd-41 and trd-42 gave the twelve rasas. This page opens the
> "anatomy": how a permanent attachment to Krishna (sthayi-bhava) becomes a relished mellow (rasa) when four ingredients join it: the causes
> (vibhava), the outward expressions (anubhava), the eight bodily transformations (sattvika-bhava) and the thirty-three passing emotions
> (vyabhicari-bhava). Lord Caitanya gave the whole scheme to Sanatana Gosvami in eight verses (CC Madhya 23.42–50); Srila Prabhupada spelled
> it out in NOD 20–34. The resident learns it for one reason above others: to recognise real symptoms from their imitation, in himself first,
> so that he neither manufactures tears nor despises them. **Source note:** the BRS OCR in `research/books/` holds the Eastern Division only;
> the Southern Division (2.1–2.5) is read through Prabhupada's summary and his quotations. Madhurya-kadambini is quoted from the approved
> Gaudiya Vedanta Publications edition. Nothing here is an invitation to display symptoms; NOD 34 governs the whole page.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on trd-33, trd-41 and trd-42, which gave the rasas as relationships; this page gives the mechanism by which a relationship is tasted.
It also builds on dps-18, which taught bhava and prema as stages of the devotee's progress; here the same words appear as the "permanent
ecstasy" that the ingredients turn into rasa (CC Madhya 23.46–47).

A resident meets ecstatic symptoms constantly. In kirtana a godbrother weeps; in class the speaker's voice falters; a new devotee dances with
abandon; a senior sits unmoved. The resident is asked, and asks himself, "Which of these is real?" Two wrong answers are common: to imitate what
looks advanced, and to dismiss everything as sentiment. Rupa Gosvami's analysis, as Prabhupada gives it, allows a third: to know what rasa is
made of, to know that symptoms can appear even in nondevotees "in contact with some pure devotee" (NOD 28), to know the Bhagavatam's test of a
heart that has actually changed (SB 2.3.24), and then to mind one's own chanting. Lower batches hear only that love of God has visible signs
(SB 11.2.40 at VL3); no lower batch studies the ingredients.

## C. Central message

"When the permanent ecstasies [neutrality, servitorship and so on] are mixed with other ingredients, devotional service in love of Godhead is
transformed and becomes composed of transcendental mellows" (CC Madhya 23.47). The ingredients are four: vibhava, the causes; anubhava, the
expressions; sattvika-bhava, the eight bodily changes; vyabhicari-bhava, the thirty-three passing emotions. "Yogurt mixed with sugar candy, black
pepper and camphor is very palatable and tasty. Similarly, when permanent ecstasy mixes with other ecstatic symptoms, it becomes unprecedentedly
tasty" (23.49). The symptoms by themselves prove nothing: nondevotees near a pure devotee may show them (NOD 28), and symptoms without a
melted heart are "raty-ābhāsa, a mere glimpse of attachment" (23.45 purport). What proves love is a changed heart, detached from the world and
attached to service (SB 2.3.24 and purport).

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Define sthayi-bhava, vibhava, anubhava, sattvika-bhava and vyabhicari-bhava in Prabhupada's words (NOD 20) and show their relation in the yogurt analogy (CC Madhya 23.47–49).
2. Name the two kinds of vibhava with Lord Caitanya's examples (CC Madhya 23.50), and list the main stimuli (NOD 26).
3. List the eight sattvika symptoms and give three anubhavas with a Nectar of Devotion instance each (NOD 27–28).
4. Explain "dried-up symptoms" (NOD 28), "raty-abhasa" (CC Madhya 23.45 purport) and the "steel-framed heart" (SB 2.3.24), and apply them to a kirtana scene without judging a person.
5. State NOD 34's rule and say how it shapes a resident's speech about symptoms.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**NOD 20 ("Transcendental Mellow")** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/nod/20/
- **Teaching:** the five topics: "(1) vibhāva – special symptoms or causes of ecstasy, (2) anubhāva – subsequent ecstasy, (3) sāttvika-bhāva – constitutional or existential ecstasy, (4) vyabhicārī-bhāva – aggressive ecstasy and (5) sthāyi-bhāva – fervent or continuous ecstasy." "Without relishing some sort of mellow, or loving mood, in one's activities, no one can continue to perform such activities." The taste "is developed only from one's previous life's activities or by the association of unalloyed devotees." "There are eight transcendental symptoms found in the body during ecstasy, and all of them are possible only by a mixture of the above-mentioned five ecstatic divisions." Baladeva astonished at the brahma-vimohana-lila is Prabhupada's example of Krishna Himself as impelling cause.
- **Application (preacher's):** the sentence "no one can continue" explains the resident who leaves after years: service without any taste does not last. The remedy the same paragraph gives is association of unalloyed devotees.
- **Use:** in the talk; the vocabulary and the source of taste.

**CC Madhya 23.42–44** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/23/44/
> 23.44: "One should understand that just as the taste of sugar increases as it is gradually purified, so when love of Godhead increases from rati, which is compared to the beginning seed, its taste increases."
- **Teaching (23.42–43):** prema grows as sneha, mana, pranaya, raga, anuraga, bhava, mahabhava, compared to sugarcane seed up to rock candy.
- **Also in:** trd-46 takes these stages. Here: the sthayi-bhava is itself a growing thing.
- **Use:** in the talk; one line.

- **Purport point (23.45, BRS 1.3.41–44):** "The real symptoms of the fructification of the seed of love (rati) are manifested because the heart is melted. When such symptoms are found among speculators and fruitive actors, they cannot be accepted as real symptoms of attachment." Such symptoms are called "raty-ābhāsa, a mere glimpse of attachment."
- **Purport point (23.46, BRS 2.5.1):** "These moods (bhāvas) bring under control the favorable ecstasies (such as laughing) and unfavorable ecstasies (such as anger). When these moods continue to remain as kings, they are called sthāyi-bhāva, or permanent ecstasies."

> 23.49: "Yogurt mixed with sugar candy, black pepper and camphor is very palatable and tasty. Similarly, when permanent ecstasy mixes with other ecstatic symptoms, it becomes unprecedentedly tasty."
- **Purport point (23.47, BRS 2.1.4–5):** "Love for Kṛṣṇa, Keśava, as previously described, reaches the supreme state of being composed of mellows when its ingredients are fulfilled. By means of vibhāva, anubhāva, sāttvika and vyabhicārī, hearing and chanting are activated, and the devotee is able to taste love for Kṛṣṇa."
- **Use:** in the talk; the definition of rasa (N).

**CC Madhya 23.50 (with purport)** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/23/50/
> "There are two kinds of particular ecstasies [vibhāva]. One is called the support, and the other is called the awakening. The vibration of Kṛṣṇa's flute is an example of the awakening, and Lord Kṛṣṇa Himself is an example of the support."
- **Purport point (BRS 2.1.16):** "The object of love is Kṛṣṇa, and the container of that love is the devotee of Kṛṣṇa. Learned scholars call them ālambana — the foundations." Uddipana (BRS 2.1.301–302): Krishna's qualities and activities, His decoration, "Kṛṣṇa's smile, the fragrance of His transcendental body, His flute, bugle, ankle bells and conchshell, the marks on His feet, His place of residence, His favorite plant [tulasī], His devotees, and the observance of fasts and vows connected to His devotion".
- **Use:** in the talk; vibhava's two kinds.

**NOD 26 ("Stimulation for Ecstatic Love")** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/nod/26/
- **Teaching:** "Some things that give impetus or stimulation to ecstatic love of Kṛṣṇa are His transcendental qualities, His uncommon activities, His smiling features, His apparel and garlands, His flute, His buffalo horn, His leg bells, His conch shell, His footprints, His places of pastimes (such as Vṛndāvana), His favorite plant (tulasī), His devotee and the periodical occasions for remembering Him." **NOD 27 ("Symptoms of Ecstatic Love"); SB 3.1.32** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/nod/27/ · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/1/32/
- **Teaching:** "The bodily symptoms manifested by a devotee in expressing ecstatic love for Kṛṣṇa are called anubhāva. Practical examples of anubhāva are as follows: dancing, rolling on the ground, singing very loudly, stretching the body, crying loudly, yawning, breathing very heavily, neglecting the presence of others, drooling, laughing like a madman, wheeling the head and belching." Vidura on Akrura (SB 3.1.32): "He has such ecstatic love for Kṛṣṇa that I have seen him rolling upon Kṛṣṇa's footprints in the dust as if bereft of all sense." - **Use:** in the talk; anubhava with instances.

**NOD 28 ("Existential Ecstatic Love")** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/nod/28/
- **Teaching:** "There are eight symptoms of existential ecstatic love: becoming stunned, perspiring, standing of the hairs on the body, faltering of the voice, trembling of the body, changing of bodily colors, shedding of tears and devastation." Three kinds: moist, burnt, dried-up. "When there are manifestations of ecstatic symptoms in the body of a nondevotee, these are called dried-up symptoms of ecstatic love. The nondevotees are actually materialistic, but in contact with some pure devotee, they may sometimes manifest some symptoms of ecstasy." Degrees: "they are sometimes called smoky, sometimes called blazing, and sometimes called shining. These three degrees are experienced for many, many years".
- **Application (preacher's):** two sentences settle most ashram arguments about tears: symptoms can appear in a nondevotee near a pure devotee, and the real thing grows over "many, many years". Neither condemn nor crown anyone on the strength of one kirtana.
- **Use:** in the talk; sattvika-bhava, real and dried-up.

- **Teaching (29):** "They are counted at thirty-three as follows: disappointment, lamentation, humility, guilt, fatigue, intoxication, pride, doubt, apprehension, intense emotion, madness, forgetfulness, disease, confusion, death, laziness, inertness, bashfulness, concealment, remembrance, argumentativeness, anxiety, thoughtfulness, endurance, happiness, eagerness, violence, haughtiness, envy, impudence, dizziness, sleepiness and alertness." **NOD 32 ("Symptoms of Continuous Ecstasy")** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/nod/32/

**NOD 34 ("The Nectar of Devotion")** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/nod/34/

> 23.39: "Even the most learned man cannot understand the words, activities and symptoms of a person situated in love of Godhead."
> 23.41 (SB 11.2.40): "When a person is actually advanced and takes pleasure in chanting the holy name of the Lord, who is very dear to him, he is agitated and loudly chants the holy name. He also laughs, cries, becomes agitated and chants just like a madman, not caring for outsiders."

**SB 2.3.24 (with purport)** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/2/3/24/
> "Certainly that heart is steel-framed which, in spite of one's chanting the holy name of the Lord with concentration, does not change when ecstasy takes place, tears fill the eyes and the hairs stand on end."
- **Purport point:** "The whole process of spiritual culture is aimed at changing the heart of the living being in the matter of his eternal relation with the Supreme Lord as subordinate servant, which is his eternal constitutional position." The change shows as "gradual detachment from the sense of material enjoyment" and "an increase in the attitude of rendering loving service to the Lord."
- **Application (preacher's):** the verse names the symptoms and the purport names the test: not the tears but the change of heart they accompany. A resident checks himself by the purport, not by the mirror.
- **Use:** in the talk; the test.

**Madhurya-kadambini, Seventh Shower, text 2c (Visvanatha Cakravarti; Gaudiya Vedanta Publications edition)** — Verified in `research/books/Madhurya-kadambini (Visvanatha Cakravarti), English 1st ed.txt`
> "When the devotee situated at the stage of kṛṣṇa-rati practises the limbs of devotional service such as hearing, the sthāyi-bhāva in his heart mixes with (1) vibhāvas, (2) anubhāvas, (3) sāttvika-bhāvas and (4) sañcārī-bhāvas, or vyabhicārī-bhāvas. The resulting relish he experiences in his heart is known as bhakti-rasa."
- **Teaching:** the commentary adds that the sancari-bhavas "manifest like waves in the nectarous ocean of sthāyi-bhāva. They make that ocean of rasa swell and finally merge back into it."
- **Use:** in the talk; the acharya's one-paragraph summary.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The king.** Rati, attachment to Krishna, in one of five kinds (CC Madhya 19.183–184 purport), when it rules the other emotions "like a
   powerful king" (NOD 32; 23.46 purport), is sthayi-bhava. It is possible "only when one is fully situated in a transcendental position" (NOD 32).
   It grows: from rati to prema and on through sneha to mahabhava (23.42–44; trd-46).
2. **The yogurt.** Sthayi-bhava alone is yogurt; with sugar candy, pepper and camphor it becomes a drink "unprecedentedly tasty" (23.49). The four
   ingredients are vibhava, anubhava, sattvika and vyabhicari (23.48); joined, "the devotee is able to taste love for Kṛṣṇa" (23.47 purport). This
   tasting is rasa (Madhurya-kadambini 7.2c).
3. **Vibhava: the causes.** Two kinds (23.50). Alambana, the support: Krishna as the object of love and the devotee as its container (23.50 purport).
   Uddipana, the awakening: His flute, His qualities, His dress, His footprints, Vrindavana, tulasi, His devotee, Ekadasi (NOD 26; 23.50 purport).
   "To be attracted by the qualities of Kṛṣṇa means to be attracted by Kṛṣṇa Himself" (NOD 26).
4. **Anubhava: the expressions.** What love does outwardly: dancing, rolling on the ground, loud singing, stretching, crying, "neglecting the presence
   of others" (NOD 27). Akrura rolling in the dust of Krishna's footprints (SB 3.1.32); Narada's sacred thread giving way.
5. **Sattvika-bhava: the eight.** Stunned, perspiring, hair standing, faltering voice, trembling, change of colour, tears, devastation (NOD 28). They arise
   from the vital force touching earth, water, fire, sky and air (NOD 28). They have degrees, smoky, blazing, shining, "experienced for many, many years".
6. **Vyabhicari-bhava: the waves.** Thirty-three passing emotions from disappointment to alertness (NOD 29), "apparently disturbed conditions" with
   "acute ecstatic love" inside them (NOD 31). They rise from the sthayi-bhava, swell it, and sink back (Madhurya-kadambini 7.2c commentary).
7. **Real and imitation.** Three texts, read together. (a) NOD 28: symptoms in a nondevotee near a pure devotee are "dried-up", real enough as
   symptoms, not rooted in rati. (b) CC Madhya 23.45 purport: symptoms without a melted heart, "among speculators and fruitive actors", are
   raty-abhasa, a glimpse. (c) SB 2.3.24 and purport: the heart that does not change under the holy name is steel-framed; the change is detachment and
   service. So: a symptom proves nothing by itself, for or against; the heart's direction over years is the evidence (NOD 28: "many, many years").
8. **Neither imitation nor contempt.** Imitation is forbidden by NOD 34 and trd-13; the sahajiya is its end (trd-39). Contempt is forbidden by CC Madhya
   23.39: "Even the most learned man cannot understand the words, activities and symptoms of a person situated in love of Godhead." The resident's
   business is his own chanting; others' tears he leaves to Krishna.
9. **Why the Lord taught it.** Because taste is what keeps a devotee going: "Without relishing some sort of mellow, or loving mood, in one's activities,
   no one can continue to perform such activities" (NOD 20); and because the taste comes "by the association of unalloyed devotees" (NOD 20), which the
   ashram exists to provide.
10. **Misunderstandings.**
    - *Rasa is a feeling I produce by effort or music.* Rasa is sthayi-bhava plus ingredients, tasted when the heart is melted (23.45, 23.47); effort and music are at most uddipana.
    - *Tears in kirtana prove advancement.* Dried-up symptoms exist (NOD 28); glimpses exist (23.45 purport). Watch the heart's change (SB 2.3.24 purport).
    - *Tears in kirtana prove nothing, so ignore them.* The Bhagavatam calls the heart that never changes "steel-framed" (SB 2.3.24); symptoms are expected eventually. Ignore the judging, not the goal.
    - *I can analyse my own bhava by this list.* NOD 34: no one on the material platform should discuss these by quoting here and there; Prabhupada gives the map so we know it exists and revere it.
11. **Pitch for TRD.** Teach the five terms plainly with Lord Caitanya's eight verses as the spine, give one Nectar of Devotion instance for each
    ingredient, and spend the last hour on real and imitation, ending in SB 2.3.24's purport and the resident's own japa.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet the devotees and say that we have now learned the twelve rasas; today we learn what a rasa is made of, as Lord Caitanya told Sanatana Gosvami in eight verses.
2. Say: "Lord Caitanya compared it to a drink: yogurt alone, and yogurt with sugar candy, pepper and camphor. We will learn the four spices."
3. Ask H.2 question 1 for private writing (no sharing).
4. Ask H.2 question 2; take two or three answers and hold them for the segment on real and imitation.
5. Read CC Madhya 23.47 and 23.49 aloud and begin.

**H.2 Opening questions**
1. (Private) When did I last feel real taste in chanting or service? What was present then that is often absent? Write one line; keep it.
2. In kirtana a devotee weeps. What are the possible explanations, and which of them can an onlooker actually know?
3. Prabhupada writes that without some taste "no one can continue" (NOD 20). What does that say about devotees who leave?

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Taste keeps the devotee going, and comes from association.** NOD 20. *Transition:* what is tasted?
2. **The king: sthayi-bhava.** Rati of one of five kinds, ruling the emotions (CC Madhya 23.46 purport; NOD 32); it grows like sugar (23.42–44). *Transition:* the king needs a court.
3. **Rasa: the king with four ingredients.** CC Madhya 23.47–49; Madhurya-kadambini 7.2c. *Transition:* the first ingredient.
4. **Vibhava: support and awakening.** Krishna and His devotee; flute, qualities, dress, footprints, Vrindavana, tulasi, devotees, Ekadasi (23.50; NOD 26). *Transition:* what love does.
5. **Anubhava.** Dancing, rolling, singing, crying, neglecting others (NOD 27); Akrura (SB 3.1.32). *Transition:* what love does to the body by itself.
6. **Sattvika-bhava: the eight.** NOD 28; moist, burnt, dried-up; smoky, blazing, shining, over many years. *Transition:* and the passing weather.
7. **Vyabhicari-bhava: thirty-three waves.** NOD 29, 31. *Transition:* so, is it real?
8. **Real and imitation.** Dried-up (NOD 28); glimpse (23.45 purport); steel-framed heart and its cure (SB 2.3.24 and purport); the learned cannot judge (23.39). *Transition:* and how we speak of all this.
9. **NOD 34.** Read aloud; the cloud and the ocean.

## J. Stories and illustrations

**1. Baladeva's astonishment (NOD 20; the brahma-vimohana-lila).** When Brahma had stolen the calves and boys and Krishna expanded Himself into all of
them, Baladeva, Krishna's own first expansion, "felt astonishment and said, 'How wonderful it is that My ecstatic love for Kṛṣṇa is again being attracted
to so many cowherd boys, calves and cows!'" Prabhupada gives this as the example "in which Kṛṣṇa Himself becomes the object and reservoir of ecstatic love
in the impelling aspect." *Lesson:* Krishna is both alambana and uddipana; even Baladeva's love is stirred by Krishna's forms.

**2. Akrura in the dust (SB 3.1.32, as given in NOD 27).** Vidura asks Uddhava about Akrura: "Not only is he a learned scholar and sinless, but he is also
a devotee of Lord Kṛṣṇa. He has such ecstatic love for Kṛṣṇa that I have seen him rolling upon Kṛṣṇa's footprints in the dust as if bereft of all sense."
*Lesson:* anubhava in a sober, learned man; the footprints were the uddipana; the rolling was love's own expression, not a display.

**3. Nanda Maharaja at the Yamuna (NOD 29).** When Krishna, punishing Kaliya, seemed to have drowned, Nanda Maharaja told Yasoda: "My dear wife, Kṛṣṇa
has gone deep into the water, and so there is no longer any need to maintain our bodies, which are so full of sinful activities! Let us also enter into the
poisonous water of the Yamunā and compensate for the sinful activities of our lives!" Prabhupada: "This is an instance of severe shock, wherein the devotee
becomes greatly disappointed." *Lesson:* a vyabhicari-bhava, disappointment, rising as a wave on the ocean of parental love and sinking back when Krishna
emerges; the passing emotion serves the permanent one.

**4. Radharani's garland (NOD 28).** "Rādhārāṇī was weaving a garland of kunda flowers, and upon hearing the vibration of Kṛṣṇa's flute, She immediately
stopped Her work. This is an example of direct moistened existential ecstatic love." *Lesson:* a sattvika-bhava (stunned) from an uddipana (the flute) in
the highest sthayi-bhava; told in one sentence, as Rupa Gosvami tells it, and left there (NOD 34).

**Illustration (writer's, not scriptural):** a lamp that is lit shines without trying; a painted lamp looks the same in a photograph and warms no one.
The Bhagavatam's own test is not the look but the heat: a heart that changes toward detachment and service (SB 2.3.24 purport).

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"A godbrother weeps in every kirtana. Is he advanced?"** We cannot know from the weeping. Symptoms appear even in nondevotees near pure devotees
   (NOD 28) and as glimpses without a melted heart (CC Madhya 23.45 purport); and "Even the most learned man cannot understand" a real devotee's symptoms
   (23.39). Watch no one; serve him; mind your own heart.
2. **"I feel nothing in kirtana. Is my heart steel-framed?"** The verse (SB 2.3.24) is a diagnosis to act on: the purport says the whole
   process aims at changing the heart toward detachment and service, gradually. If your service and detachment are growing, the heart is changing; taste comes from
   association and previous practice (NOD 20). Chant, hear, serve; do not manufacture.
3. **"Should I try to produce the eight symptoms, since the scripture praises them?"** No. Produced symptoms are not sattvika; they are rasabhasa in the
   heart and the sahajiya's road (NOD 34; trd-13; trd-39). The symptoms follow a melted heart (23.45 purport); the heart melts by hearing and chanting
   (23.47 purport).
4. **"Why does Prabhupada list thirty-three emotions and twelve anubhavas if we are not to analyse ourselves?"** So that we know love of God is a precise
   science with a literature, revere it, recognise its outline in the acharyas' lives, and never mistake our moods for it. The map is for reverence and for
   avoiding imitation; the territory is entered by sadhana (NOD 34).
5. **"Are the senior devotees who sit calmly in kirtana less advanced than the ones who dance?"** Symptoms may be "manifested internally and sometimes
   externally" (NOD 28) and have degrees over many years; a calm devotee may be stunned within. The Bhagavatam's picture includes both the madman (SB
   11.2.40) and the sober servant (SB 2.3.24 purport).
6. **"Can the devotees' association really give taste, or only time?"** Prabhupada names two sources: previous life's practice and "the association of
   unalloyed devotees" (NOD 20). Time alone gives neither. This is the resident's reason to keep the ashram's association pure and to stay in it.

## L. Case studies

**Case 1 (Invented case for discussion).** A brahmacari of two years notices that when he cries in kirtana, seniors look at him kindly, and he begins to
work himself up to tears each evening. He half knows it. *Dilemma:* harmless enthusiasm or the beginning of imitation? *Principles:* CC Madhya 23.45
purport (symptoms without a melted heart are a glimpse); NOD 34; SB 2.3.24 purport (the test is the heart's change). *Questions:* Who is the audience of
these tears? What has changed in his detachment and service? *Analysis:* the tears have an audience, so they are anubhava of a desire for recognition, not
of rati; continued, they become the sahajiya's habit (trd-39). The remedy is private: chant attentively, serve without being seen for a month, and let
whatever comes come. *Takeaway:* produced symptoms are a glimpse at best; the heart's change is the only evidence worth wanting.

**Case 2 (Invented case for discussion).** A senior resident, tired of displays, says in class that "all this crying is sentiment" and that real devotees
show nothing. A new devotee who wept sincerely that morning is crushed. *Dilemma:* has the senior taught sobriety or contempt? *Principles:* SB 2.3.24
(the heart that never changes is steel-framed); SB 11.2.40 (the advanced devotee laughs, cries, chants like a madman); CC Madhya 23.39 (the learned
cannot judge). *Questions:* Which verse did the senior leave out? What should he have said? *Analysis:* he taught contempt and risked an offence; the
teaching is to neither imitate nor despise, and to keep one's eyes on one's own heart. He should correct himself in the next class with SB 2.3.24 and
11.2.40 read together. *Takeaway:* sobriety is not the denial of symptoms but the refusal to judge them.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Lord Caitanya told Sanatana Gosvami how love of God becomes a tasted mellow. First there is rati, attachment to Krishna in one of five kinds; when it
rules all the other emotions like a king, it is called sthayi-bhava, permanent ecstasy (CC Madhya 23.46 and purport). "When the permanent ecstasies
[neutrality, servitorship and so on] are mixed with other ingredients, devotional service in love of Godhead is transformed and becomes composed of
transcendental mellows" (23.47). He compared it to a drink: "Yogurt mixed with sugar candy, black pepper and camphor is very palatable and tasty.
Similarly, when permanent ecstasy mixes with other ecstatic symptoms, it becomes unprecedentedly tasty" (23.49).

Srila Prabhupada names the four ingredients in The Nectar of Devotion: vibhava, the causes of ecstasy; anubhava, its outward expressions; sattvika-bhava,
the bodily changes; and vyabhicari-bhava, the passing emotions (NOD 20). Vibhava has two kinds: the support, which is Krishna Himself and His devotee,
and the awakening, such as His flute (23.50), His qualities, His dress, His footprints, Vrindavana, tulasi, His devotees and Ekadasi (NOD 26). Anubhava
includes "dancing, rolling on the ground, singing very loudly, stretching the body, crying loudly" and more (NOD 27); Akrura rolled in the dust of
Krishna's footprints (SB 3.1.32). The eight sattvika symptoms are "becoming stunned, perspiring, standing of the hairs on the body, faltering of the
voice, trembling of the body, changing of bodily colors, shedding of tears and devastation" (NOD 28). The thirty-three vyabhicari-bhavas run from
disappointment to alertness (NOD 29) and are "apparently disturbed conditions" with love inside them (NOD 31).

The symptoms alone prove nothing. Prabhupada writes that ecstatic symptoms may appear even "in the body of a nondevotee" who is "in contact with some pure
devotee"; these are "dried-up symptoms" (NOD 28). Symptoms without a melted heart are "raty-ābhāsa, a mere glimpse of attachment" (CC Madhya 23.45 purport).
Real symptoms develop "for many, many years" (NOD 28), and "Even the most learned man cannot understand the words, activities and symptoms of a person
situated in love of Godhead" (23.39). The Bhagavatam gives the test: the heart that does not change under the holy name is "steel-framed" (SB 2.3.24), and
the change is detachment from material enjoyment and increased loving service (purport). So we neither imitate symptoms nor judge them; we chant, hear
and serve, and keep the devotees' association, from which taste comes (NOD 20).

## S2. Key ideas
- Rasa is permanent love (sthayi-bhava) tasted with four ingredients: causes, expressions, bodily changes, passing emotions (CC Madhya 23.47–49; NOD 20).
- Vibhava has two kinds: support (Krishna, His devotee) and awakening (His flute and all that reminds of Him) (23.50; NOD 26).
- Symptoms can appear in nondevotees and as glimpses; they prove nothing by themselves (NOD 28; CC Madhya 23.45 purport).
- The test is the heart's change: detachment and service (SB 2.3.24 and purport).
- Neither imitate nor judge (NOD 34; CC Madhya 23.39).

## S3. Verses to remember
- **CC Madhya 23.49:** "Yogurt mixed with sugar candy, black pepper and camphor is very palatable and tasty." For me: love is tasted, not only felt.
- **SB 2.3.24:** "Certainly that heart is steel-framed which, in spite of one's chanting the holy name of the Lord with concentration, does not change". For me: the goal is a changed heart.
- **CC Madhya 23.39:** "Even the most learned man cannot understand the words, activities and symptoms of a person situated in love of Godhead." For me: I do not judge others' tears.

## S4. Words to know
- **Sthayi-bhava:** permanent love for Krishna, ruling the other emotions like a king (CC Madhya 23.46 purport).
- **Vibhava:** the causes of ecstasy; alambana (support) and uddipana (awakening) (23.50).
- **Anubhava:** the outward expressions of love, such as dancing and crying (NOD 27).
- **Sattvika-bhava:** the eight bodily changes, such as tears and trembling (NOD 28).
- **Vyabhicari-bhava:** the thirty-three passing emotions (NOD 29).
- **Raty-abhasa:** a glimpse of attachment; symptoms without a melted heart (CC Madhya 23.45 purport).

## S5. Questions and answers
1. **If I cry in kirtana, am I advanced?** Not necessarily. Symptoms appear even in nondevotees near pure devotees (NOD 28) and as glimpses (CC Madhya 23.45 purport). Look at your detachment and service instead (SB 2.3.24 purport).
2. **If I feel nothing, is something wrong?** The Bhagavatam calls the unchanging heart steel-framed, but the purport gives the cure: the whole process changes the heart gradually. Keep chanting and serving; taste comes from the devotees' association (NOD 20).
3. **Should I try to produce the symptoms?** No. Produced symptoms are not love; the heart melts by hearing and chanting (CC Madhya 23.47 purport), and Prabhupada forbids discussing or displaying these from the material platform (NOD 34).
4. **Why learn these lists at all?** To know that love of God is a precise science, to revere it, and never to mistake our moods for it (NOD 34).
5. **Is a calm senior less advanced than a dancing junior?** We cannot say; symptoms may be internal or external (NOD 28), and the learned cannot judge them (CC Madhya 23.39).

2. Give the two kinds of vibhava with Lord Caitanya's examples (CC Madhya 23.50).
3. Name four of the eight sattvika symptoms.
4. What does Prabhupada call ecstatic symptoms that appear in a nondevotee in contact with a pure devotee?
5. True or false: SB 2.3.24 says that tears prove a changed heart.
6. Complete: "Even the most learned man cannot understand the words, activities and symptoms of ______."

**Answers:** 1 (b) (CC Madhya 23.47–49). 2 Support, Krishna Himself; awakening, the vibration of His flute (23.50). 3 Any four of: stunned, perspiring, hair standing,
faltering voice, trembling, change of colour, tears, devastation (NOD 28). 4 Dried-up symptoms (NOD 28). 5 False; it says the heart that does not change when such
symptoms occur is steel-framed, and the purport says the change is detachment and service (SB 2.3.24 and purport). 6 "a person situated in love of Godhead" (CC Madhya 23.39).
