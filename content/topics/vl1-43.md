# Ambitions vs spiritual life

> **VL1 · 45–60 min · review: sources checked, awaiting senior review.** Scripture light but exact. IYF holds a Q&A-style outline and a deck
> ("Common fear among new devotees: if I take to spiritual life too seriously, will I not lose my ambitions?")
> and a "Spiritual Quotient" PDF. This page follows the outline's question-and-answer flow and keeps its best
> examples (Dhruva, Arjuna, the Gosvamis), gives the half-remembered verses their references, and drops the
> unsourced anecdotes. **At VL1 nobody is asked to give up a career.** The talk removes a fear; it does not
> replace it with a demand. Not sensitive. Mood: light, honest, no pressure.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

IYF's own documents list "off track my career" and "we will lose our ambitions" among the reasons young people
stay away. The fear is real and deserves a straight answer at the first level, before it hardens into a reason
to leave. The Gita gives that answer in its plainest verses: work (BG 3.8), your own work (18.47), as an offering
(9.27, 18.46), without clutching the fruit (2.47). The talk is relevance and reassurance; the deeper question
of ashrama and career direction belongs to DPS.

Builds on vl1-38 (competence needs a direction), vl1-10 and vl1-25 (BG 2.47 and working as offering, taught in depth there) and vl1-20
(greed): this page keeps only its own angle, the fear "I will lose my ambitions", answered by size (SB 1.2.10) and recipient (BG 9.27);
give BG 2.47 one line and link those pages.

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **VL1** (here) | The fear named and answered; ambition vs over-ambition; the examples; one experiment |
| VL2 | Why a changing world cannot give a permanent self lasting satisfaction, argued with reason |
| VL3 | Living the practice while studying and working; dealing with parents (VL3-13) |
| SPS | Keeping standards while holding a job; service as the use of one's field |
| DPS | "Seeing himself in K.C. on long terms (ashram wise, career wise)" (IYF's DPS motive): the real career question, with guidance |
| BSS | Those who do redirect their whole life: brahmacari orientation; never presented at VL1 as the expected path |

**Not done here:**
- Any suggestion that serious spirituality means leaving one's field, or that the brahmacari path is expected.
  One line acknowledges that some do (the Gosvamis), and the outline's own answer: "Everyone is not expected to
  give up material world."
- Teaching about destiny and karma ("a khalasi cannot become a Tata by labour", on IYF's outline): a VL2 topic,
  and easily misheard as fatalism.
- The outline's unsourced anecdotes: Bhaktivinoda Thakura's "special railway line", "Rs 40 to Rs 40 crore",
  the Robert Bruce legend. Left out.

## C. Central message

Spiritual life does not take your ambition; it takes the fever out of it. The Gita tells you to work, in your
own field, fully, as an offering, without clutching the fruit; the Bhagavatam tells you to earn what a healthy
life needs and to spend the rest of your energy on the question only a human can ask. Ambition becomes
over-ambition when it has no end and no "for whom"; the tradition's heroes kept their drive and changed its
direction.

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. State the Gita's position on work in two verses: do your prescribed work (BG 3.8) and your *own* work
   (BG 18.47), and say why "quit and meditate" is not what it recommends.
2. Explain SB 1.2.10's rule for earning (for a healthy life, while the human project is inquiry) and apply it to
   a salary expectation.
3. Describe the Gita's portrait of over-ambition (BG 16.13–15) and name its two missing pieces: an end, and a
   "for whom".
4. Retell Dhruva's story accurately as a case of ambition redirected, not removed, and say what he regretted
   (SB 4.9.31).
5. Answer the fear "Krsna takes away wealth" (SB 10.88.8) honestly, and choose one experiment with their own
   ambition this week.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** BG 3.8, 18.47, SB 1.2.10 and BG 16.13–15 carry the argument; BG 9.27, 18.46, 2.47 give the method;
the Dhruva verses (SB 4.8.12, 4.8.20, 4.9.31) carry the story; BG 18.73, CC Adi 10.84 and SB 10.88.8 answer
questions.

**BG 3.8**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/8/
> "Perform your prescribed duty, for doing so is better than not working. One cannot even maintain one's physical body without work."
- **Context:** Arjuna has asked (3.1) why Krsna urges him to work if knowledge is better.
- **Teaching:** Work is better than not working; the body itself needs it.
- **Purport point:** "So-called renunciation for the purpose of maintenance is never approved by the Lord";
  "Work should not be given up capriciously, without purification of materialistic propensities." Arjuna "was a
  householder and a military general, and therefore it was better for him to remain as such".
- **Application (preacher's):** The whole fear, answered in one verse: the Gita does not tell the ambitious to
  quit. Say it first and plainly.
- **Use:** in the talk (first answer).

**BG 18.47**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/47/
> "It is better to engage in one's own occupation, even though one may perform it imperfectly, than to accept another's occupation and perform it perfectly. Duties prescribed according to one's nature are never affected by sinful reactions."
- **Teaching:** Your own occupation, even imperfectly, over another's done perfectly.
- **Purport point:** "no work is abominable, if performed in the service of the Supreme Lord." "Whether one is a
  ksatriya, a vaisya, or a sudra doesn't matter, if he serves, by his work, the Supreme Personality of Godhead."
- **Application (preacher's):** Not only "work", but *your* work: the engineer is not asked to become a priest.
  This is the verse for the student who thinks spirituality means a different career.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 1.2.10**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/10/
> "Life's desires should never be directed toward sense gratification. One should desire only a healthy life, or self-preservation, since a human being is meant for inquiry about the Absolute Truth. Nothing else should be the goal of one's works."
- **Context:** Suta Gosvami defines the real purpose of human work.
- **Teaching:** Desire a healthy life; the human being is for inquiry about the Absolute Truth; that, not sense
  gratification, is the goal of work.
- **Purport point:** "One should satisfy the senses only insomuch as required for self-preservation, and not
  for sense gratification." "In every sphere of life, therefore, the ultimate end must be seeking after the
  Absolute Truth".
- **Application (preacher's):** IYF's outline quotes this half-remembered as "labho jiveta yavata": here is its
  reference. It gives ambition a *size*: enough for a healthy life, and the surplus energy for the bigger
  question. At VL1, "how much is enough?" is the live question.
- **Use:** in the talk (the rule of size).

**BG 16.13-15**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/16/13-15/
> "The demoniac person thinks: “So much wealth do I have today, and I will gain more according to my schemes. So much is mine now, and it will increase in the future, more and more…"
- **Teaching:** A portrait of the mind that counts its wealth, schemes for more, and sees others as enemies.
- **Purport point:** (The verses have no separate purport; the purport to 16.16 continues the description.)
- **Application (preacher's):** IYF's outline cites this ("idam adya maya labdham"). Read it as a portrait, not
  an insult: this is what ambition sounds like when it has no end and no "for whom". At VL1 say "the Gita's
  portrait of the over-ambitious mind"; avoid "demoniac" of anyone in the room.
- **Use:** in the talk (over-ambition).

**BG 9.27**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/9/27/
> "Whatever you do, whatever you eat, whatever you offer or give away, and whatever austerities you perform – do that, O son of Kuntī, as an offering to Me."
- **Purport point:** "Everyone has to work for maintenance of his body and soul together, and Krsna recommends
  herein that one should work for Him."
- **Application (preacher's):** The "for whom" that over-ambition lacks. Same work, new recipient.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 18.46**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/46/
> "By worship of the Lord, who is the source of all beings and who is all-pervading, a man can attain perfection through performing his own work."
- **Purport point:** "In whatever occupation one may be engaged, if he serves the Supreme Lord he will achieve
  the highest perfection."
- **Application (preacher's):** Perfection *through* one's own work, not away from it. The talk's thesis verse.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 2.47**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/47/
> "You have a right to perform your prescribed duty, but you are not entitled to the fruits of action…"
- **Purport point:** "Inaction is sinful."
- **Application (preacher's):** The fever of ambition is the clutching of fruits; the verse keeps the work and
  releases the clutch. And it forbids quitting.
- **Use:** in the talk, one line (VL1-41 and VL1-10 take it fully).

**BG 2.70**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/70/
> "A person who is not disturbed by the incessant flow of desires – that enter like rivers into the ocean, which is ever being filled but is always still – can alone achieve peace, and not the man who strives to satisfy such desires."
- **Purport point:** "A Krsna conscious man is not in need of anything, because the Lord fulfills all his
  material necessities. Therefore he is like the ocean – always full in himself."
- **Application (preacher's):** For the outline's "cycle → scooter → car → aeroplane": the river never fills the
  one who is not already full.
- **Use:** in the talk, one line.

**SB 4.8.12**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/8/12/
> "My dear child, you are unaware that you were born not of my womb but of another woman. Therefore you should know that your attempt is doomed to failure. You are trying to fulfill a desire which is impossible to fulfill."
- **Context:** Queen Suruci to the five-year-old Dhruva, who tried to climb onto his father's lap.
- **Teaching:** Dhruva is told his ambition (his father's lap, and by implication the throne) is impossible.
- **Use:** in the talk (story: the insult that lit the ambition).

**SB 4.8.20**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/8/20/
> "Sunīti continued: The Supreme Personality of Godhead is so great that simply by worshiping His lotus feet, your great-grandfather, Lord Brahmā, acquired the necessary qualifications to create this universe…"
- **Teaching:** Dhruva's mother points him to the Lord as the source from which even Brahma got his position.
- **Purport point:** "To become successful in any attempt, one not only has to undergo severe penances and
  austerities, but also must be dependent on the mercy of the Supreme Personality of Godhead."
- **Use:** in the talk (story: the ambition is pointed at the right source).

**SB 4.9.31**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/9/31/
> "Alas, just look at me! I am so unfortunate. I approached the lotus feet of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, who can immediately cut the chain of the repetition of birth and death, but still, out of my foolishness, I prayed for things which are perishable."
- **Context:** The Lord has appeared to Dhruva and granted him the kingdom and a planet; Dhruva laments.
- **Teaching:** Having reached the Lord, Dhruva regrets having asked for perishable things.
- **Purport point:** "Dhruva Maharaja regrets his unfortunate position, for although he approached the Supreme
  Personality of Godhead … he foolishly wanted something perishable."
- **Application (preacher's):** The story's turn: ambition was not punished; it was outgrown. He got what he
  asked for and found he had asked too small. IYF's outline: "Dhruva also suffered from the problem of
  overambition. But because he approached the right source, he achieved ultimate success."
- **Use:** in the talk (story climax).

**SB 10.88.8** (deeper verse; the talk uses only its plain meaning): Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/10/88/8/ · *BBT translation, completed by Srila
Prabhupada's disciples*
> "The Personality of Godhead said: If I especially favor someone, I gradually deprive him of his wealth. Then the relatives and friends of such a poverty-stricken man abandon him. In this way he suffers one distress after another."
- **Context:** The Lord explains to Yudhisthira why Siva's worshippers often prosper while His own devotees
  may not.
- **Teaching:** Special favour may take the form of losing wealth and false friends.
- **Purport point (disciples'):** Devotees' happiness and distress are "incidental effects of their loving
  reciprocation with the Lord"; the purport uses the analogy of "a very affectionate father" who restricts his
  children's play and sends them to school, and notes that "even perfect saints like Prahlada, Dhruva and
  Yudhisthira were subjected to great tribulations".
- **Application (preacher's):** IYF's outline raises this verse as a fear ("someone frightened me"). Answer it
  honestly: the verse exists; it is about the Lord's *special* favour to His devotees, which the purport compares
  to a loving father who sends his children to school; the saints it names (Prahlada, Dhruva, Yudhisthira) passed
  through tribulations, and Dhruva and Yudhisthira also ruled kingdoms. Do not hide it; do not wave it at a VL1 room.
- **Use:** background; in the talk only if asked.

**BG 18.73**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/73/
> "…my illusion is now gone. I have regained my memory by Your mercy. I am now firm and free from doubt and am prepared to act according to Your instructions."
- **Application (preacher's):** IYF's outline: "Arjuna did not go to forest after hearing Bhagavad gita." After
  eighteen chapters the warrior stays a warrior and fights. The talk's one-line proof that spiritual life does
  not remove the work.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**CC Adi 10.84** (deeper verse; the talk uses only its plain meaning): Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/adi/10/84/
> "On the western side were the forty-third, forty-fourth and forty-fifth branches — Śrī Sanātana, Śrī Rūpa and Anupama. They were the best of all."
- **Purport point:** The three brothers were "given the title Mullik" "because of working in the Muslim
  government"; Mullik "means 'lord'" and was given "to rich, respectable families that have intimate connections
  with the government."
- **Application (preacher's):** IYF's outline: Rupa and Sanatana "were ministers of Nawab Hussain Shah". The
  purport confirms only that they worked in the Muslim government and held a title given for wealth and
  respectability; the "minister" detail is the outline's, not this purport's. They are the example of people who *did* redirect
  their whole lives, to be stated once, as the outline says: "Everyone is not expected to give up material
  world … not every student of a college returns to college as a teacher."
- **Use:** in the talk, one line, with the caveat.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** The Gita's instruction to the ambitious is to work (BG 3.8), in their own field (18.47),
   and to let that work become perfect by its direction (18.46, 9.27) rather than by its fruits (2.47). The
   Bhagavatam gives the work a size: enough for a healthy life, with the human surplus spent on inquiry
   (SB 1.2.10). Ambition without a size and without a recipient is over-ambition, and the Gita paints its
   portrait (16.13–15): counting, scheming, enemies. The river never fills the one who is not already full
   (2.70). The tradition's heroes kept their drive: Dhruva outgrew his ambition by taking it to the right source
   (SB 4.8–9), Arjuna fought (BG 18.73); a few, like the Gosvamis, redirected everything (CC Adi 10.84), and
   that is not asked of everyone.
2. **Why it matters.** The fear "I will lose my ambition" keeps capable young people away from the one thing
   that would give their ambition a direction and a size. Removing the fear honestly, without the opposite
   pressure, is the service of this talk.
3. **Connections.** VL1-38 (direction for competence), VL1-10/25 (work as offering, in method), VL1-20 (greed:
   the engine of over-ambition), VL1-41 (fruits), VL3-13 (parents), DPS (career and ashrama with guidance).
4. **Consequences.** Someone who accepts this keeps working hard; asks "how much is enough?" (1.2.10) and
   "for whom?" (9.27); notices when the inner monologue turns into 16.13–15; measures a day by the work and its
   direction, not the fruit; and stops fearing that God will confiscate what He is asked to bless.
5. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - **"Spiritual = give up career."** BG 3.8, 18.47, 18.73. No.
   - **"Spiritual = be lazy / content with little."** "Inaction is sinful" (2.47 purport); 3.8 purport condemns
     renunciation "for the purpose of maintenance". Satisfaction is not complacency (IYF's outline says this well).
   - **"Ambition is bad."** Over-ambition is (16.13–15). Dhruva's ambition took him to God.
   - **"Krsna takes away wealth from devotees."** SB 10.88.8 speaks of special favour to His devotees, a loving
     father's strictness (purport); Dhruva and Yudhisthira passed through tribulations and also ruled. Not a threat to a beginner.
   - **"The great devotees all renounced, so that is the standard."** Some did (the Gosvamis); most of the Gita's
     own cast (Arjuna, Yudhisthira) ruled and fought. "Not every student returns as a teacher."
6. **Pitch for VL1.** Use IYF's question-and-answer rhythm: ask the fear aloud, answer it with a verse. No
   Sanskrit except *tattva-jijnasa* (inquiry about the truth, SB 1.2.10) if the room likes a term. Never use
   "demoniac" of a listener's ambition; say "the Gita's portrait of over-ambition".

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet, give your name, and say plainly: nobody is being asked to give up a career today.
2. Ask the opening question (hands up, no names).
3. Tell the pilot joke (label it a joke) and ask H.2 question 1.
4. Take two or three answers and thank each.
5. Use the transition line below and begin "Answer 1: the Gita says work".

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the room's own life)
1. What is one thing you hope to achieve in the next five years?
2. Who in your life has worried that spiritual practice would cost you something? What did they say?
3. If you reached that goal, who would benefit besides you?

- **Opening question:** "Hands up if someone has ever warned you that 'this spiritual stuff' will cost you your
  ambition. Keep them up if a part of you believes it." Pause. "Good. Today we answer that part."
- **A joke (from IYF's deck; it is a joke, not a source):** a pilot announces good news and bad news: "we are
  travelling at the fastest possible speed, and we do not know in which direction." That is ambition without a
  "for whom".
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** Tanvi, 24, has a plan: a product role by 26, a start-up by 30, abroad by 32.
  She has started chanting with a friend and now lies awake wondering whether she has to choose. Does she?
- **Transition:** "The Gita was spoken to a man about to abandon his career. Listen to what it told him."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** IYF's own records list "off-track my career" as a top reason young people
  walk away. If the fear is false, it deserves a clear answer now.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **The Gita says work.** "Perform your prescribed duty, for doing so is better than not working" (BG 3.8); and
   your *own* work (18.47). Arjuna, after eighteen chapters, fought (18.73). *Example:* Tanvi keeps her plan.
   *Transition:* then what changes?
2. **Ambition gets a size.** Earn for "a healthy life"; the human project is "inquiry about the Absolute Truth"
   (SB 1.2.10). *Example:* IYF's question: at ten times the salary, will you eat fifty chapatis? *Transition:*
   what happens to ambition with no size?
3. **Over-ambition: the portrait.** "So much wealth do I have today, and I will gain more … He is my enemy…"
   (BG 16.13–15). Missing: an end, a "for whom". The river never fills the empty ocean (2.70). *Example:* cycle,
   scooter, car, aeroplane (IYF). *Transition:* can ambition be redirected rather than removed?
4. **Dhruva: ambition taken to the right source.** Insulted (SB 4.8.12), pointed to the Lord by his mother
   (4.8.20), he wanted a position higher than his father's and grandfather's; he got it, and regretted asking so small
   (4.9.31). *Transition:* so what do I do with mine?
5. **Ambition gets a recipient.** "Whatever you do … do that … as an offering" (BG 9.27); perfection "through
   performing his own work" (18.46). *Example:* the same product role, built for something larger than a title.
   *Transition:* a caveat and an experiment.
6. **Some redirect everything; it is not asked of you.** Rupa and Sanatana worked in the Muslim government,
   titled for wealth and respectability (CC Adi 10.84 purport), and left it. "Not every student returns to college as a teacher" (IYF's
   outline). *Transition:* P.

## J. Stories and illustrations

- **Dhruva Maharaja** (SB 4.8–9; verified 4.8.12, 4.8.20, 4.9.31; read both chapters before telling it). A
  five-year-old prince climbs onto his father's lap and is pushed off by his stepmother Suruci, who tells him his
  wish is "impossible to fulfill" (4.8.12). Burning, he goes to his mother Suniti, who points him to the Lord,
  from whom even Brahma got his post (4.8.20). He leaves for the forest, meets Narada, receives the mantra
  (4.8.54), and worships in Madhuvana. The Lord appears and grants him his father's kingdom and a planet of his
  own; and Dhruva laments that, having reached the one who ends birth and death, he "prayed for things which are
  perishable" (4.9.31). *Connection:* ambition was the fuel, the right source was the turn, and the regret was at
  having asked too small, not at having been ambitious. Tell from the text; no invented dialogue.
- **Arjuna stays a warrior** (BG 18.73; and the whole frame of the Gita). *Connection:* spiritual life did not
  remove his work.
- **Rupa and Sanatana** (CC Adi 10.84 and purport). Wealthy, titled officials of the Muslim government in
  Bengal who left to serve Lord Caitanya. *Connection:* a few redirect everything; state it once, with IYF's
  caveat.
- **Tolstoy, "How Much Land Does a Man Need?"** (IYF's deck: "a man who ran and ran"): a real 1886 short story,
  literature, not scripture; label it. *Connection:* ambition without a size.
- **Left out:** the Bhaktivinoda Thakura "special railway line" anecdote, "Rs 40 to Rs 40 crore", George
  Harrison and Alfred Ford (contemporary public figures; fine as one line if you know the facts, but the outline
  gives none), the Robert Bruce legend, the "fruit vendor" and "Sabari" references (true stories but not for this
  talk), and the destiny/khalasi argument.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"If I get serious about this, will I lose my drive?"**
   - The Gita's answer to its first student was "work" (BG 3.8), in your own field (18.47), and he did (18.73).
     What you lose is the fever, not the drive: the clutch on fruits (2.47) and the endless "more" (16.13–15).
2. **"So I should just be satisfied with little? That sounds like laziness."**
   - No. "Inaction is sinful" (2.47 purport); giving up work to be maintained by others is condemned (3.8
     purport). SB 1.2.10 says earn for a healthy life, not "earn nothing". Satisfaction is a state of the person
     (2.70), not a ceiling on effort.
3. **"How much is enough?"**
   - The Bhagavatam's measure is "a healthy life, or self-preservation" (1.2.10), with the human surplus for
     inquiry. The number differs by family and city; the question is whether you have *any* number. Over-ambition
     is ambition with no number (16.13–15).
4. **"My parents will panic if I start chanting. They think it means I'll become a monk."**
   - Show them, not tell them: keep working hard, keep your plan, and let them see 18.47 in your life. VL3-13
     takes parents fully. And the tradition's own view: not everyone is expected to leave; most of the Gita's
     cast ruled and fought.
5. **"Someone told me Krsna takes away the wealth of His devotees (SB 10.88.8)."**
   - The verse exists; read it honestly. It is about the Lord's *special* favour to His devotees, which the purport
     compares to a loving father's strictness; the saints it names, Dhruva and Yudhisthira among them, passed
     through tribulations and were also kings. It is not a threat held over a VL1 room. IYF's outline adds a fair point: if He can take with a million hands, He can also give with them.
6. **"Is wanting to go abroad over-ambition?"**
   - IYF's outline: "We didn't say that." The test is not the destination but whether the ambition has a size
     (1.2.10) and a "for whom" (9.27). "If you earn more, give more and purify yourself" (outline).
7. **"Why did people like Rupa and Sanatana leave everything, then?"**
   - Because they were called to a different service, and a few are. "Not every student returns to college as a
     teacher" (IYF). The talk's standard is BG 18.46: perfection through *your own* work.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Many people fear that taking spirituality seriously will cost them their ambition. The Bhagavad-gita was spoken
to a man about to abandon his career, and it told him to work: "Perform your prescribed duty, for doing so is
better than not working" (BG 3.8), and to do his *own* work rather than someone else's (BG 18.47). After
eighteen chapters Arjuna stayed a warrior (BG 18.73). What spiritual life changes is not the drive but its
fever. The Bhagavatam gives ambition a size: earn what "a healthy life" needs, and spend the human surplus on
the bigger question (SB 1.2.10). The Gita gives it a recipient: "Whatever you do … do that … as an offering"
(BG 9.27), and promises perfection "through performing his own work" (BG 18.46). Ambition with no size and no
recipient becomes over-ambition, which the Gita paints as a monologue of counting and scheming (BG 16.13–15).
Dhruva, insulted as a child, took his ambition to the right source, got more than he asked, and regretted only
that he had asked for perishable things (SB 4.9.31). Nobody here is asked to give up a career; a few, like Rupa
and Sanatana, redirect everything, and that is their call, not a standard.

Whether you are a farmer planning the next season, a shop worker saving for a course, or a manager aiming for a promotion, the question is the same: how much is enough, and who else benefits? Try this
once this week: take one real ambition, keep it exactly as it is, and add a size and a recipient. It is an invitation to test, and no one needs to share it.

## S2. Key ideas

- The Gita says work (BG 3.8), your own work (BG 18.47); Arjuna did not go to the forest (BG 18.73).
- A size for ambition: enough for a healthy life; the surplus for inquiry (SB 1.2.10).
- A recipient for ambition: "as an offering" (BG 9.27); perfection through your own work (BG 18.46).
- Over-ambition is ambition with no end and no "for whom" (BG 16.13–15); the river never fills the empty ocean
  (BG 2.70).
- Dhruva: ambition taken to the right source, and regret only at asking small (SB 4.8.20, 4.9.31).
- Laziness is not the alternative: "Inaction is sinful" (BG 2.47 purport).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **BG 3.8:** "Perform your prescribed duty, for doing so is better than not working. One cannot even maintain
  one's physical body without work." *For me:* spirituality is not a reason to quit.
- **SB 1.2.10:** "One should desire only a healthy life, or self-preservation, since a human being is meant for
  inquiry about the Absolute Truth." *For me:* my ambition needs a size.
- **BG 18.46:** "…a man can attain perfection through performing his own work." *For me:* my field is the place,
  not the obstacle.

## S4. Words to know

- **Tattva-jijnasa:** inquiry about the truth (SB 1.2.10); what the human surplus is for.
- **Prescribed duty (sva-dharma):** the work that fits one's nature (BG 18.47); the Gita says keep it.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Will I lose my drive?**
   The Gita told its first student to work (BG 3.8), in his own field (BG 18.47), and he did (BG 18.73). You lose
   the fever, not the drive.
2. **Should I just be satisfied with little? Isn't that laziness?**
   No. "Inaction is sinful" (BG 2.47 purport). Earn for a healthy life (SB 1.2.10); satisfaction is a state of the
   person, not a ceiling on effort.
3. **How much is enough?**
   The Bhagavatam's measure is "a healthy life" (SB 1.2.10). The number differs; the question is whether you have
   one at all. Over-ambition has none (BG 16.13–15).
4. **My parents fear I'll become a monk.**
   Keep working hard and let them see BG 18.47 in your life. Most of the Gita's own cast ruled and fought.
5. **Does Krsna take away His devotees' wealth?**
   A verse (SB 10.88.8) speaks of the Lord's *special* favour to His devotees, like a loving father's strictness;
   devotees it names, Dhruva and Yudhisthira, passed through difficulties and were also kings. It is not a threat to anyone here.
6. **Is wanting to go abroad over-ambition?**
   Not by itself. The test is whether the ambition has a size (SB 1.2.10) and a "for whom" (BG 9.27).

## S6. Check your understanding

1. According to BG 3.8, is working or not working better?
2. What does SB 1.2.10 say a human being is meant for?
3. True or false: BG 18.47 says it is better to do someone else's work perfectly than your own imperfectly.
4. What did Dhruva regret in SB 4.9.31?
5. In your own words: what two things turn ambition into over-ambition when they are missing?

**Answers:** 1. Working (BG 3.8). 2. "Inquiry about the Absolute Truth" (SB 1.2.10). 3. False: one's own
occupation, even imperfectly, is better (BG 18.47). 4. That, having reached the Lord who ends birth and death, he
"prayed for things which are perishable" (SB 4.9.31). 5. A size ("a healthy life", SB 1.2.10) and a recipient
("as an offering", BG 9.27); without them ambition becomes the endless monologue of BG 16.13–15.
