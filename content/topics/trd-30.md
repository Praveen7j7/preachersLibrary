# Brhad-bhagavatamrta: Gopa-kumara's search for the highest abode

> **TRD · up to 180 min · review: drafted.** Group F, advanced Gaudiya siddhanta. Sanatana Gosvami's Brhad-bhagavatamrta
> (the great nectar of the Bhagavata) tells the siddhanta of trd-29 as a story: a cowherd boy, Gopa-kumara, chants the
> mantra his guru gave him and is carried from the earth through every higher realm, finding at each one a limit, until
> he reaches Goloka. **Source note:** the only copy in `research/books/Sanatana_Goswami_Sri_Brhad_Bhagavatamrta.txt` is a
> font-scrambled PDF extraction and could not be read in this session, and Srila Prabhupada's books do not narrate the
> journey. The book's content is therefore taught here from Prabhupada's own description of it (CC Madhya 1.35 purport;
> CC Antya 4.219), and the ladder of realms is taught from his verses (Bs 5.43; BG 8.16, 8.21; SB 2.9, 3.15, 10.28). The
> journey's outline is given in J as "Requires source review" and is not to be told as verified narration until a readable
> edition is checked.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on trd-29: that page proved that prema, not liberation, is the goal; this page shows the same truth as a map of
places. Builds on trd-23: the sambandha section of Lord Caitanya's teaching to Sanatana (CC Madhya 20-21) describes the
spiritual sky, the Vaikunthas and Goloka; Sanatana then wrote the Brhad-bhagavatamrta.

A resident speaks every week of "the spiritual world". If the picture in his mind is vague, his preaching is vague and his
own hankering has nowhere definite to go. Srila Prabhupada's books give the exact gradation: the material realms up to
Brahmaloka, all places of misery and return (BG 8.16); the spiritual sky with the Vaikuntha planets (SB 2.9.9-10; CC Madhya
21.5); and above all Goloka Vrindavana, Krishna's own abode (Bs 5.43; CC Adi 5.17; BG 15.6). The Brhad-bhagavatamrta, as
Prabhupada describes it, walks this whole ladder in the company of one devotee, so that a reader feels at each level what is
gained and what is still missing. No lower batch studies the gradation as a subject.

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| VL1 | Not covered; "Krishna has His own abode" in plain words (BG 15.6) |
| VL2 | Heaven is not the goal: BG 9.21 and 8.16, the return from the higher planets |
| VL3 | The spiritual sky in one picture: BG 8.21, SB 2.9.9-10 |
| SPS | Vaikuntha and Goloka distinguished (CC Adi 5.14-22); the devotee's destination is a place of service, not rest |

| BSS | The brahmacari's restlessness read in the light of the search: each stopping place below Krishna leaves him unsatisfied |

**Not done here:** Goloka's own features and the eternal associates in detail (trd-48, trd-41 to trd-47); the avataras
and their source (trd-31); the Sandarbhas' proofs (trd-24 to trd-29).

## C. Central message

Every realm below Krishna's personal service, from this earth to the highest material planet to the Vaikunthas themselves,
leaves the soul's hankering unfinished; only Goloka, where Krishna is served as the cowherd boy of Vrindavana, is the end of
the search (Bs 5.43; CC Adi 5.17). The Brhad-bhagavatamrta shows this by taking one devotee, mantra in heart, up every step
of the ladder, and Srila Prabhupada's books give the ladder itself.

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Name, in order, the realms Srila Prabhupada's books describe from the earth to Goloka (BG 8.16; Bs 5.43; CC Adi 5.14-17), and
   say which are places of return and which are not (BG 8.21; BG 15.6).
2. Describe Vaikuntha from SB 2.9.9-10, SB 3.15.14 and CC Madhya 21.5, and Goloka from CC Adi 5.22 and SB 10.28.14-17.
3. State what the Brhad-bhagavatamrta is, who wrote it, its two parts and seven-chapter structure, using only CC Madhya 1.35
   and CC Antya 4.219.
4. Explain why Uddhava prays to become a bush in Vrindavana (SB 10.47.58-61), and connect it to the search's end.
5. Apply the shape of the search to their own life: identify the "stopping place" they are tempted to settle at, and name the
   practice that moves them on.

- **Purport point:** Prabhupada describes the book: "Regarding the Bṛhad-bhāgavatāmṛta, there are two parts, both of which deal with the discharge of devotional service. The first part is an analytical study of devotional service, in which there is also a description of different planets, including the earth, the heavenly planets, Brahma-loka and Vaikuṇṭha-loka. There are also descriptions of the devotees, including intimate devotees, most intimate devotees and complete devotees. The second part describes the glories of the spiritual world, known as Goloka-māhātmya-nirūpaṇa, as well as the process of renunciation of the material world. It also describes real knowledge, devotional service, the spiritual world, love of Godhead, attainment of life's destination, and the bliss of the world. In this way there are seven chapters in each part, fourteen chapters in all."
- **Application (preacher's):** this paragraph is our verified description of the book. Everything said about its contents in the
  talk rests on it.
- **Use:** in the talk; read aloud.

**CC Antya 4.219** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/4/219/
> "Śrīla Sanātana Gosvāmī compiled the Bṛhad-bhāgavatāmṛta. From this book one can understand who is a devotee, what is the process of devotional service, and who is Kṛṣṇa, the Absolute Truth."
- **Context:** the author's glorification of Sanatana Gosvami at the end of the chapter on his meeting the Lord at Puri.
- **Teaching:** three things are learned from the book: the devotee, the process, and Krishna. These are the three questions of

- **Purport point:** "The realm of Goloka stands highest above all others." Hari-dhama is "ever resplendent with transcendental majesty and the great splendor of all-sweetness predominates over all other majesties in Goloka."

- **Purport point (8.16):** even on Brahmaloka, "if, on Brahmaloka, one does not cultivate Kṛṣṇa consciousness, then he must return to earth."

- **Purport point (15.6):** "The spiritual world, the abode of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Kṛṣṇa – which is known as Kṛṣṇaloka, Goloka Vṛndāvana – is described here." - **Context (2.9.9):** Brahma's penance pleases the Lord, who shows him Vaikuntha, "the supreme planet above all others".
- **Teaching (3.15.14):** in Vaikuntha "all the residents are similar in form to the Supreme Personality of Godhead" and serve

- **Purport point (2.9.9):** "The personal abodes of the Lord, known as Vaikuṇṭhas, are neither mythical nor material, as conceived by the impersonalists." They are seen only by devotional service.

- **Teaching (5.14):** beyond material nature is the paravyoma, the spiritual sky, with all transcendental attributes.

- **Purport point (5.22):** "Kṛṣṇaloka is the topmost planet in the spiritual sky, and below it are innumerable spheres." Brahma was first shown Vaikuntha by Narayana's grace and "Later, by the grace of Kṛṣṇa, he was shown a transcendental vision of Kṛṣṇaloka."

- **Application (preacher's):** this is the only Bhagavatam passage where Vrindavana's residents are shown the ladder's top directly,

- **Use:** in the talk (J story 1).

- **Teaching (10.47.58):** the gopis "alone have actually perfected their embodied lives"; their love is hankered after by sages and

- **Purport point (10.47.61):** the translation "is taken from Śrīla Prabhupāda's English rendering of Caitanya-caritāmṛta ( Antya 7.47)"; Uddhava "does not pray to be equal to the gopīs in their exalted stage of love, but rather to take birth as a bush or creeper in Vṛndāvana".

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** Reality is graded. Srila Prabhupada's books name the grades: the fourteen material worlds up to
   Brahmaloka, all places of misery and return (BG 8.16; 9.21); above them the Siva realm (Bs 5.43 purport); then the spiritual
   sky with innumerable Vaikunthas, untouched by the modes and by time (SB 2.9.10); and at the top Goloka, Krishna's own abode,
   from which the brahmajyoti itself shines (BG 15.6 purport; Bs 5.43; CC Adi 5.17). Each higher realm includes the good of the
   lower and adds what the lower lacks.
2. **Why the gradation matters to the goal.** trd-29 showed that prema stands above liberation. The gradation shows what that
   means in terms of places: Brahman realisation (the effulgence) is below the Vaikunthas, where the Lord is served in opulence;
   and the Vaikunthas are below Goloka, where "the great splendor of all-sweetness predominates" (Bs 5.43 purport). A devotee's
   hankering is not finished by light, nor by opulence, but by Krishna as the cowherd boy.
3. **What the Brhad-bhagavatamrta does.** Prabhupada (CC Madhya 1.35 purport) says the first part is "an analytical study of
   devotional service" with the planets and the grades of devotees, and the second part is "Goloka-māhātmya-nirūpaṇa", the
   ascertainment of Goloka's glory, with renunciation, knowledge, bhakti, prema and the attainment of life's destination. CC Antya
   4.219 says the book teaches who a devotee is, what the process is, and who Krishna is. So the book is the whole map of trd-23
   told as the experience of devotees rather than as theses.
4. **The shape of a search.** The book's method, as Prabhupada's summary shows, is comparison: each realm and each devotee is
   seen beside a higher one. For a resident, this is the method of honest self-examination. Where have I stopped? At being a good
   manager (earth)? At being respected (heaven)? At peace of mind (Brahman)? At the opulence of a big temple (Vaikuntha mood)?
   The Bhagavatam's answer is Uddhava's: the gopis' love, and the dust of their feet (SB 10.47.58-61).
5. **Goloka is not escape.** The cowherd men shown Krishnaloka saw a place exactly like their Vrindavana, with Krishna present
   (SB 10.28.17 purport). The goal is not a different life but the same service without the covering. This answers the fear that
   "the spiritual world" is an abstraction: it is Vrindavana as it really is.
6. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *Vaikuntha and Goloka are the same thing.* Prabhupada distinguishes them: Brahma was shown Vaikuntha first and Krishnaloka later (CC Adi 5.22 purport).
   - *The higher planets are a step toward Krishna.* They are places of return (BG 8.16); Krishna consciousness is the step, wherever one is.
   - *The Brhad-bhagavatamrta is a travel story.* Its subject, by Prabhupada's account, is devotional service and its grades; the travel is the teaching device.
   - *One can skip the gradation and "just love Krishna".* The gradation protects love from settling early; a devotee who does not know that Vaikuntha is below Goloka may stop at awe.
7. **Pitch for TRD.** Give the ladder exactly as the verses give it and say where our knowledge of the book comes from. The room
   should leave able to draw N from memory and to name the place where their own search is tempted to stop.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet the devotees and say that today's book is a story about a search, and that we will follow the search through Srila
   Prabhupada's verses.
2. Say: "Everyone in this room is searching. The question is where each of us would be content to stop."
3. Ask H.2 question 1 for private writing.
4. Ask H.2 question 2; take a few answers and list the "places" on the board without comment.
5. Say that Sanatana Gosvami wrote a whole book about a boy who could not stop, and begin with CC Madhya 1.35.

**H.2 Opening questions**
1. (Private) If Krishna gave you, permanently, the best thing you have experienced in devotional life so far, would your search be over?
2. People stop at different places: a good position, respect, peace of mind, a beautiful temple. Where do devotees you know tend to stop?
3. What is the difference between seeing the Lord and serving the Lord?

## I. Core teaching points

1. **The book and its purpose.** Two parts, seven chapters each: the grades of devotees and planets; then Goloka's glory and the
   attainment of life's destination (CC Madhya 1.35 purport). It teaches the devotee, the process and Krishna (CC Antya 4.219).
   *Transition:* let us climb the ladder it describes, using Prabhupada's verses.
2. **The material rungs end in return.** From Brahmaloka down, all are places of misery and rebirth (BG 8.16); heaven's pleasure
   runs out (BG 9.21). *Example:* promotion in an office; the post is enjoyed, then lost. *Transition:* what is above matter?
3. **Vaikuntha: service in opulence, no return.** The modes and time do not enter (SB 2.9.10); residents have the Lord's form and serve
   without self-interest (SB 3.15.14); each planet is made of bliss (CC Madhya 21.5). *Transition:* is there anything above this?
4. **Goloka: the top.** Bs 5.43 places it above Hari-dhama; CC Adi 5.17 names it; the effulgence itself comes from it (BG 15.6 purport).
   Brahma saw Vaikuntha first and Krishnaloka later (CC Adi 5.22 purport). *Transition:* what does Goloka look like to a Vrajavasi?
5. **Goloka looks like Vrindavana.** The cowherd men saw Krishnaloka and found Krishna there as in their own village (SB 10.28.17).
   *Transition:* who, among devotees, has reached the top?
6. **The highest devotees.** Uddhava's verdict: the gopis alone perfected embodied life; he prays to be a bush under their feet
   (SB 10.47.58, 61). *Transition:* so where does my search stop?

## J. Stories and illustrations

**1. Krishna shows the cowherd men His abode (SB 10.28.14-17; BBT translation completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples).** Tell
it as the verses tell it. After Krishna brings Nanda back from Varuna's abode, the cowherd men wonder about Him. Considering their
hearts, the all-merciful Lord "revealed to the cowherd men His abode, which is beyond material darkness" (10.28.14). He revealed the
indestructible spiritual effulgence, "which is unlimited, conscious and eternal" (10.28.15). He brought them to the Brahma-hrada,
made them submerge in the water and lifted them up, and "the cowherd men saw the planet of the Absolute Truth" (10.28.16). Nanda
and the others "felt the greatest happiness when they saw that transcendental abode", amazed to see Krishna there, surrounded by the
personified Vedas offering prayers (10.28.17). *Lesson:* the top of the ladder is not light but a place with Krishna in it, and it is
Vrindavana itself seen without covering (10.28.17 purport, citing Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati).

**2. Uddhava in Vrindavana (SB 10.47.58-61; BBT translation completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples).** Uddhava, Krishna's
intimate friend and messenger, having heard the gopis, sings that among all persons on earth these cowherd women alone have
perfected their lives, that their love is hankered after by sages and by Uddhava himself, and that for one who has tasted the
Lord's narrations even birth as Brahma is of no use (10.47.58). He prays to be born as a bush or creeper in Vrindavana so that the
gopis' footdust may fall on him (10.47.61). *Lesson:* the grades of devotees that the Brhad-bhagavatamrta's first part studies
(CC Madhya 1.35 purport) end where Uddhava ends: at the gopis.

**3. The search of Gopa-kumara: outline. Requires source review.** The following is the writer's recollection of the book's second
part and is not verified against a readable edition in this library; teach it only as "the book is said to tell", or leave it to the
handout note. A cowherd boy of Govardhana receives the gopala-mantra from a brahmana who becomes his guru. Chanting it, he is carried
step by step through higher and higher stations: kingship on earth, the heavenly planets, the planets of the sages and of Brahma,
Siva's realm, the Brahman effulgence, the Vaikuntha planets, the abodes of the Lord's other forms, and at last Goloka Vrindavana,
where Krishna receives him as a cowherd friend and his guru is found again. At each station he is honoured and shown the Lord's
presence in some form, yet a hankering remains that drives him on. When a readable edition is checked, replace this paragraph with
the book's own words, cited by part, chapter and verse.

**Illustration (writer's, not scriptural):** a man searching for his father in a great house passes through rooms of increasing
beauty; in each he finds his father's portrait, then his voice, then his clothes. He is not satisfied until he finds the person.
Prabhupada's ladder is that house; the Brhad-bhagavatamrta is the man walking through it.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"Why teach a book we have not read?"** Because Srila Prabhupada himself describes it (CC Madhya 1.35 purport) and praises it
   (CC Antya 4.219), and because the ladder it walks is fully given in his verses. Say clearly which parts are verified.
2. **"Is Vaikuntha a lesser place? That sounds offensive to Lord Narayana."** No one is lesser; Prabhupada's words are that in Goloka
   "all-sweetness predominates over all other majesties" (Bs 5.43 purport). The difference is in the mood of service, not in the Lord.
3. **"If Brahmaloka is a place of return, why do the Vedas recommend sacrifices to reach it?"** Because people want it; the Gita
   describes the result honestly (BG 9.21) and then gives the alternative (BG 8.16, 15.6).
4. **"Can a devotee stop at Vaikuntha and be happy?"** Devotees in Vaikuntha are fully satisfied in their service (SB 3.15.14). The
   point of the gradation is not that they are unhappy but that the follower of Lord Caitanya is given a higher aim (CC Adi 5.17).
5. **"Is wanting Goloka a material desire for a nice place?"** Only if the service is forgotten (CC Madhya 20.142; see trd-29 K.6).
   The cowherd men's joy was at seeing Krishna there (SB 10.28.17).
6. **"How does this help me on Tuesday morning?"** By naming where my search is tempted to stop. A resident who knows that even
   Vaikuntha's opulence is below Vrindavana's sweetness will not let a comfortable service become his destination.

## L. Case studies

**Case 1 (Invented case for discussion).** A senior brahmacari has built a large, well-run department. Juniors admire him; he is at
peace. In private he admits that his japa is dry and that he has not hankered for Krishna in years. *Dilemma:* has he arrived or
stopped? *Principles:* Bs 5.43 (grades); SB 5.6.18 (the Lord gives the lower things easily); SB 10.47.58. *Questions:* Which rung has
he settled on? What did Uddhava, who had everything, pray for? *Analysis:* he has stopped at a Vaikuntha-like opulence of service.
The remedy is to hear about Vrindavana daily (SB 10.28; 10.47) until the hankering returns. *Takeaway:* good management is a rung,
not the roof.

**Case 2 (Invented case for discussion).** A new preacher tells students that the goal is "to merge into the divine light and end
suffering". *Dilemma:* is this wrong or merely incomplete? *Principles:* SB 10.28.15-16 (the effulgence, then lifted above it);
BG 8.21 with 15.6 purport. *Questions:* Where on the ladder has he placed the goal? How would he correct it without contradicting
himself in front of the students? *Analysis:* he has stopped at the Brahma-hrada. He can keep the light and add: the light comes
from a planet, and on that planet is a person (BG 15.6 purport). *Takeaway:* never end a talk at the effulgence.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Sanatana Gosvami, the senior of the six Gosvamis, wrote the Brhad-bhagavatamrta, the great nectar of the Bhagavata. Srila
Prabhupada describes it: two parts of seven chapters each. The first part studies devotional service and describes the planets
from the earth through the heavenly planets and Brahmaloka to Vaikuntha, and the different grades of devotees. The second part
describes the glory of Goloka, renunciation, knowledge, devotion, love of God and the attainment of life's destination (CC Madhya
1.35 purport). From this book, says Caitanya-caritamrta, one understands who a devotee is, what devotional service is, and who
Krishna is (CC Antya 4.219). The copy in our library is unreadable, so the book is taught through Srila Prabhupada's description
and verses.

Those verses give a ladder of realms. At the bottom is the material world: from the highest planet, Brahmaloka, down to the lowest,
all are places of misery where birth and death repeat (BG 8.16); even those who reach heaven return when their merit is used up
(BG 9.21). Above matter is the spiritual sky with countless Vaikuntha planets, where the modes of nature and time do not enter
(SB 2.9.10), where every resident has a form like the Lord's and serves without self-interest (SB 3.15.14). Above all is Goloka
Vrindavana, Krishna's own abode (Bs 5.43; CC Adi 5.17), from which the spiritual light itself shines (BG 15.6 purport). One who
reaches it never returns (BG 15.6).

Two Bhagavatam stories show the top of the ladder. When Krishna showed the cowherd men His abode, He first submerged them in the
spiritual effulgence and then lifted them up to see His planet, where they were amazed to find Krishna Himself (SB 10.28.14-17).
And Uddhava, Krishna's own friend, sang that the gopis alone have perfected their lives, and prayed to be born as a bush in
Vrindavana to receive the dust of their feet (SB 10.47.58, 61). The book's hero, the cowherd boy Gopa-kumara, is said to climb this
whole ladder by chanting the mantra his guru gave him, unsatisfied until he reaches Goloka; this part of the story awaits checking
against a readable edition. The lesson is for each of us: wherever our search is tempted to stop, Krishna's personal service lies higher.

## S2. Key ideas
- The Brhad-bhagavatamrta teaches who a devotee is, what devotional service is, and who Krishna is (CC Antya 4.219).
- All material planets, even Brahmaloka, are places of return (BG 8.16).
- Vaikuntha is beyond the modes and time, full of servants with the Lord's own form (SB 2.9.10; 3.15.14).
- Goloka Vrindavana stands above all, and whoever reaches it never returns (Bs 5.43; BG 15.6).
- The highest devotees are the gopis; Uddhava prays for the dust of their feet (SB 10.47.61).

## S3. Verses to remember
- **BG 8.16:** "From the highest planet in the material world down to the lowest, all are places of misery…" For me: no material promotion is my goal.
- **Bs 5.43:** Devi-dhama, Mahesa-dhama, Hari-dhama, and "above them all is located Kṛṣṇa's own realm named Goloka." For me: I know where the ladder ends.
- **SB 10.47.61:** Uddhava prays to be "one of the bushes, creepers or herbs in Vṛndāvana". For me: the greatest devotee asked for the smallest place near the gopis.

## S4. Words to know
- **Brhad-bhagavatamrta:** Sanatana Gosvami's book in two parts, teaching devotion through the journeys of Narada and of the cowherd boy Gopa-kumara.
- **Devi-dhama, Mahesa-dhama, Hari-dhama:** the material world, Siva's realm, and the Vaikuntha planets (Bs 5.43).
- **Vaikuntha:** the spiritual planets where the Lord is served in opulence; no modes, no time (SB 2.9.10).
- **Goloka Vrindavana / Krishnaloka:** Krishna's own abode, the highest of all (CC Adi 5.17).
- **Brahmajyoti:** the spiritual light that comes from Goloka (BG 15.6 purport).

## S5. Questions and answers
1. **Is heaven the goal?** No; one returns from it (BG 9.21).
2. **What is the difference between Vaikuntha and Goloka?** Both are spiritual and eternal; Goloka is Krishna's own abode above the Vaikunthas, where sweetness predominates (Bs 5.43 and purport).
3. **Did anyone in this world see the spiritual world?** Brahma saw Vaikuntha (SB 2.9.9); the cowherd men saw Krishnaloka (SB 10.28.14-17).
4. **Who are the greatest devotees?** The gopis of Vrindavana, by Uddhava's own words (SB 10.47.58, 61).
5. **Can I read Gopa-kumara's story?** Not from our library's copy yet; it is unreadable. Ask for a readable edition and check it before teaching the details.

## S6. Check your understanding
1. How many parts and chapters does the Brhad-bhagavatamrta have? (a) one part, ten chapters (b) two parts, seven chapters each (c) three parts
2. Put in order from lowest to highest: Goloka, Devi-dhama, Hari-dhama, Mahesa-dhama.
3. True or false: one who reaches Brahmaloka never returns.
4. What two things did Krishna show the cowherd men, in order?
5. Why did Uddhava want to be a bush in Vrindavana?
6. Which three things does one learn from the Brhad-bhagavatamrta, according to Caitanya-caritamrta?

**Answers:** 1 (b) (CC Madhya 1.35 purport). 2 Devi-dhama, Mahesa-dhama, Hari-dhama, Goloka (Bs 5.43). 3 False (BG 8.16). 4 The spiritual
effulgence, then His own planet with Himself there (SB 10.28.15-17). 5 To receive the dust of the gopis' feet (SB 10.47.61). 6 Who a devotee is,
what devotional service is, who Krishna is (CC Antya 4.219).
