## B. Why this topic belongs at this level
A brahmacari's conduct is watched by children, and the safety of children is a duty that no one may treat lightly. BIKC gives
short advice: deal with children on the basis of Krishna consciousness, be responsible, and do not play or fondle frivolously.
The IYF motive for BSS includes "following the principles of brahmachari life" (research/batch-criteria.md, section 6).
This page gives the scriptural reasons and the practical safeguards.

## C. Central message
A child is a soul in a small body. A brahmacari serves children best by being a clean and cheerful example, by giving them a simple impression of Krishna through prasadam, stories and kirtana, and by keeping every contact open, responsible and in the presence of their parents or teachers.

## D. Learning outcomes
By the end, participants can:
1. Explain BG 3.21 and its purport: children copy the conduct of adults, so the example comes first.
2. Explain BG 2.13: the child, the youth and the old person are the same soul in changing bodies, which is why a child deserves respect.
3. Retell how Srila Prabhupada was given Radha-Krishna Deities as a child and why this early impression mattered (SB 1.12.30 purport).
4. Describe how the sages treated the child Narada (SB 1.5.23-26) and what a brahmacari can learn from it.
5. List the child-protection habits in section I.4 and say whom to tell if a concern arises.

## E. Scriptural foundation
Use BG 3.21, BG 2.13, SB 1.12.30 and SB 1.5.24-26 in the talk. SB 7.6.1 is for questions (it builds on bss-06). All verses and purports were read on Vedabase.

**BG 3.21**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/21/
> "Whatever action a great man performs, common men follow. And whatever standards he sets by exemplary acts, all the world pursues."
- **Context:** Krishna tells Arjuna why a leader must set an example.
- **Purport point:** The purport says people need a leader who teaches by practical behaviour; a leader cannot teach the public to stop smoking if he himself smokes. Lord Caitanya said a teacher should behave properly before he teaches. It says the father and the schoolteacher are natural leaders of innocent people and have a great responsibility to their dependents.
- **Application (preacher's):** Children are the quickest imitators. A brahmacari does not need to be a teacher by title: any older person in a child's sight is already a model.
- **Use:** in the talk (I.1).

**BG 2.13**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/13/
> "As the embodied soul continuously passes, in this body, from boyhood to youth to old age"
- **Purport point:** The purport says every living entity is an individual soul who appears sometimes as a child, sometimes as a youth, sometimes as an old man, while the same spirit is there and does not change.
- **Application (preacher's):** A child is not a toy or a pet. He is a soul with his own journey. This is the basis of respect and the reason no one may use a child for his own pleasure.
- **Use:** in the talk (I.2).

**SB 1.12.30**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/12/30/
> "…he would come to constantly contemplate Him."
- **Context:** Brahmanas predict the future of the newborn Pariksit. He saw the Lord in the womb of his mother and would later examine everyone to find that same person.
- **Purport point:** The purport says Pariksit got the impression of the Lord even in the womb. It quotes Srila Jiva Gosvami: every child given an impression of the Lord from childhood becomes a great devotee, as Pariksit did; even a child who was not so fortunate can be made so if the parents desire it. Srila Prabhupada then tells of his own life. When he was four or five years old his father gave him a couple of forms of Radha and Krishna; he and his sister worshipped them playfully and imitated the ceremonies of a neighbouring Radha-Govinda temple. The practice lapsed in school and college, and revived when he met his spiritual master. The purport adds that Prahlada also advised giving such impressions from the beginning of childhood.
- **Application (preacher's):** The one who gave the first impression was the father. A brahmacari who meets children can support the parents' efforts with small, joyful offerings: prasadam, a story, a song. He does not replace the parents.
- **Use:** in the talk (I.3, J.1).

**SB 1.5.23**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/5/23/
> "…I was born as the son of a certain maidservant engaged in the service of brāhmaṇas who were following the principles of Vedānta."
- **Context:** Narada tells Vyasa about his previous life as a boy. His mother served sages in the four months of the rainy season, and he was engaged in their personal service.
- **Purport point:** The purport says Narada was the son of the humblest parentage and was not properly educated, but because he was engaged in service of the Lord's servants he became an immortal sage. It says the service of the Lord begins with the service of the Lord's bona fide servants.

**SB 1.5.24**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/5/24/
> "In addition, I was not naughty, and I did not speak more than required."
- **Purport point:** The purport says that in the varnasrama system small boys after five years of age were sent to the guru's ashram, whatever their family, to be taught self-control, discipline and obedience; it says parents were responsible for the boy's success.
- **Use:** in the talk (J.2).

**SB 1.5.25**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/5/25/
> "Once only, by their permission, I took the remnants of their food, and by so doing all my sins were at once eradicated."
- **Purport point:** The purport says the boy became infected with the purity of the devotees by their association and by taking their remnants once. It warns that one should be cautious about pseudo-devotees.
- **Use:** in the talk (J.2).

**SB 1.5.26**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/5/26/
> "I could hear them describe the attractive activities of Lord Kṛṣṇa."
- **Purport point:** The purport says that simply by attentive hearing of the Lord's pastimes from the right sources one can attain the highest perfection, as Narada did from the pure devotees.
- **Application (preacher's):** The sages did not entertain the boy or fuss over him. They allowed him to serve, gave him prasadam and spoke of Krishna in his hearing. That is a model for adults near children.
- **Use:** in the talk (J.2).

**SB 7.6.1**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/6/1/
> "…from the tender age of childhood"
- **Purport point:** The purport says the brahmacarya system begins from childhood, from about age five, so that a child can practise devotional service; and that children and youths should be taught in schools and at home to hear about the Lord, to hear Bhagavad-gita and put it into practice.
- **Use:** background; builds on bss-06, where Prahlada's talk to his schoolmates is told.

## F. Philosophical explanation
**Principle.** Srila Prabhupada teaches that childhood is the best time to give an impression of the Lord (SB 1.12.30, 7.6.1 purports), that the example of elders teaches more than words (BG 3.21), and that each child is a soul (BG 2.13). The child's own parents and teachers carry the main responsibility (BG 3.21 purport; SB 1.5.24 purport).
**BIKC's guidance (BIKC ch. 37), in summary (see O).** Most brahmacaris, apart from those connected with a gurukula, have little contact with children. Brahmacaris have no business playing frivolously with children or fondling them, because fondling children or animals can mean trying to enjoy their bodies. Any dealing with children should be on the basis of Krishna consciousness. Children look up to the example of adults, so a brahmacari should act in an ideal Krishna conscious way, especially when children are present. Joking and lightness are not forbidden, but the tendency of adults to become childish around children is good neither for the adults nor for the children. If a child needs correction, refer to the parents or teachers, because children are very sensitive.
**Connections.** bss-06 (a child's early training, SB 7.6.1); bss-31 and bss-32 (habits of conduct); bss-34 (dealing with family, including young relatives).
**Consequences.** If adults are careless, children are harmed, and the community's trust is lost. If adults are clear and kind, children grow up trusting devotees, and parents feel safe.
**Common misunderstandings.**
1. "Warmth means physical affection." Warmth means attention, kindness and service. Physical contact is for parents and caregivers, and it follows the rules of the programme.
2. "Safeguards show distrust." They protect children, protect the brahmacari from false suspicion, and protect the mission's good name.
3. "A brahmacari should correct any child who misbehaves." BIKC says to refer to parents or teachers.
4. "Children cannot understand Krishna consciousness." SB 1.12.30 and 1.5.24-26 show how much a child takes in.
**How to pitch it.** Speak positively about what a brahmacari can give. State the safeguards plainly, as a normal part of caring for children.

## H. Opening and engagement
**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes).**
1. One round of kirtana or a short prayer; say the title.
2. Say: "We will talk about children: what we can give them, and how to care for them. Children are the most precious and the most vulnerable people in any community. Everyone here has once been a child, and someone shaped us."
3. Read BG 3.21 slowly. Say: "This verse is about children as much as about leaders."
**H.2 Opening questions.**
1. "Who was one adult in your childhood who left a good impression on you, and what did he or she do?" (Take three answers; thank each speaker. No one needs to talk about painful experiences.)
2. "Why do you think children watch adults so closely?"
3. "What can a visiting brahmacari give a child in half an hour that is good and safe?"

## I. Core teaching points
**I.1 The example comes first (BG 3.21).** Whatever a respected person does, others follow. The purport gives a plain example: a leader cannot teach the public to stop smoking if he himself smokes. A brahmacari who visits a family or helps at a children's programme is a model for the children. Therefore he chants on time, speaks gently, wears clean simple clothes, eats prasadam with respect and does not use bad language or crude jokes. Transition: "Why does this matter so much? Because of who the child is."
**I.2 The child is a soul (BG 2.13).** The soul passes through boyhood, youth and old age. A child is a soul on his own journey. Therefore respect and care are owed to him. BIKC (ch. 37) says: "Brahmacārīs have no business frivolously playing with children or fondling them (fondling children or animals can mean attempting to enjoy their bodies). Any dealings with children should be on the basis of Kṛṣṇa consciousness." Transition: "What, then, can we give?"
**I.3 Giving an impression of the Lord (SB 1.12.30; SB 1.5.23-26).** Srila Prabhupada tells that as a child of four or five he received Radha-Krishna Deities from his father and worshipped them with his sister, and that this early impression stayed with him. The sages who stayed in Narada's village gave the boy a service, allowed him to eat their remnants, and spoke of Krishna's pastimes in his hearing; this changed his life. A brahmacari can do the same in small ways, with the parents' knowledge: offer prasadam, tell a short story from the Bhagavatam, lead a simple kirtana, show how to offer a flower. Always do this in a group or with the parents present. Transition: "Now we need to speak about safety."
**I.4 Child-protection habits (Application, preacher's, based on BIKC and common child-protection practice; confirm your temple's current written policy: *Requires source review*).**
1. Never be alone with a child in a closed room, a vehicle or any hidden place. Choose open places where other adults can see you.
2. Do not give private gifts, keep secrets with a child, or send private messages. All communication goes through the parents or the programme.
3. Physical affection follows the parents' wishes and the programme rules. A brahmacari does not hug, tickle, carry or wrestle with children. A friendly word or a high-five in public is enough.
4. Do not discipline a child. Tell the parent or teacher (BIKC).
5. Be present in a programme as part of a team, with at least two adults.
6. If a child tells you something troubling, listen calmly, thank the child for telling you, do not promise to keep it secret, and tell the temple's child-protection contact or a senior the same day. If a child may be in danger, the matter must also be reported to the proper authorities as the local law requires. In India, Childline 1098 is a free helpline for children in distress; use your local equivalent elsewhere.
7. If you ever feel an unsafe pull toward a child, or an urge you cannot control, tell your mentor or a qualified counsellor at once, before there is any contact, and step back from any service with children until a senior has arranged what is safe. Tele-MANAS 14416 (India) or your local crisis service can help you find a counsellor. Asking for help early protects the child and you, and there is no shame in asking.

## J. Stories and illustrations
**J.1 A child with Deities (SB 1.12.30 purport).** When Srila Prabhupada was four or five years old, his father gave him a couple of forms of Radha and Krishna. In a playful manner he and his sister worshipped them and imitated the ceremonies of a neighbouring temple of Radha-Govinda. This was suspended during school and college, and when he met his spiritual master, Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Gosvami Maharaja, he revived it. **Devotional lesson:** a simple, joyful offering to the Lord in childhood leaves a lasting impression, and the Lord brings the person back to it. Parents and elders carry this service.
**J.2 The sages and the boy Narada (SB 1.5.23-26).** The story is told in full on bss-44; here use only how the sages treated the boy. They let the maidservant's son serve them (1.5.23), allowed him to take the remnants of their food (1.5.25), and let him hear them describe Krishna's activities, so that his taste for hearing grew (1.5.26). **Devotional lesson:** a child changes through service, prasadam and hearing about Krishna from pure devotees. The adults did not fuss over the boy; they were examples.
**Illustration (preacher's, labelled).** A candle can light many candles without becoming less. A brahmacari who is himself lit with chanting can light a child's interest simply by being near, but he lights it only by being a steady flame, not by touching or holding. The point is that the child's interest in Krishna comes from the brahmacari's own devotion and not from familiarity.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections
1. **"Can I joke with children?"** BIKC says joking and lightness are not forbidden. The warning is against becoming childish. Joke gently, in public, and keep the joke clean.
2. **"A child is misbehaving. May I correct him?"** BIKC says to refer to the parents or teacher. Children are very sensitive.
3. **"Does a rule like 'never alone' mean you distrust me?"** No. It protects the child, and it protects you against any false suspicion. It is also the standard in any good community.
4. **"A child hugs me. What do I do?"** Be kind and brief. Give a smile, step back, and say, for example, "Let's go and find your mother." Tell the parent later, without making the child feel at fault.
5. **"What if I am worried about a child's safety or about my own feelings?"** Tell your mentor or the temple's child-protection contact right away. Use Childline 1098 (India) or your local service for a child in danger. For your own feelings, a qualified counsellor or Tele-MANAS 14416 (India) can help. Asking is a sign of responsibility.

## L. Case studies
**L.1 Invented case for discussion.** A brahmacari visits a devotee family for lunch. The eight-year-old son wants to show him his room and his toy Deities. *Dilemma:* the boy's enthusiasm is lovely, and the room is down the hall. *Principles:* BG 3.21 (example), SB 1.12.30 (an impression of the Lord), BIKC (responsible dealing). *Questions:* How can the brahmacari honour the boy's interest and still keep to the habits in I.4? *Analysis:* he asks the boy to bring the Deities to the living room, or asks a parent to come along, then listens, praises, and offers a flower. *Takeaway:* kindness and safety are not in conflict.
**L.2 Invented case for discussion (extension).** A parent at a festival asks a brahmacari to look after her child for ten minutes in a side room. *Principles:* I.4 (habits 1 and 5). *Questions:* What does he say kindly? Who else can help? *Analysis:* he says, "I'd be happy to help here in the hall," or finds a second adult. *Takeaway:* a polite, standard answer protects everyone.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary
A child is a soul in a small body. Bhagavad-gita 2.13 says the soul passes from boyhood to youth to old age. Children therefore deserve respect, and nobody may use a child for his own pleasure. Bhagavad-gita 3.21 and Srila Prabhupada's purport say that people follow the conduct of those they respect. Children are the quickest to copy. A brahmacari should therefore be an ideal Krishna conscious example, especially when children are present.
Giving a child an impression of the Lord is a great service. Srila Prabhupada tells, in his purport to SB 1.12.30, that as a child of four or five he was given Radha-Krishna Deities by his father and worshipped them with his sister. The early impression stayed with him. The sages in Narada's earlier life gave the boy a service, allowed him to eat their remnants, and spoke of Krishna in his hearing (SB 1.5.23-26). In the same way, a brahmacari can offer prasadam, tell a short Bhagavatam story or lead simple kirtana, always with the parents' knowledge and in a group.
The book *Brahmacarya in Krishna consciousness* says that brahmacaris should not play frivolously with children or fondle them, and that any dealing with children should be Krishna conscious. If a child needs correction, tell the parent or teacher. Child safety needs clear habits: never be alone with a child in a closed place; no private gifts, messages or secrets; physical affection only as parents and programme rules allow; and report any concern to the temple's child-protection contact the same day. In India, Childline 1098 helps children in distress. If you ever feel an unsafe pull, tell your mentor or a counsellor before there is any contact, and step back from service with children until a senior has arranged what is safe. There is no shame in asking for help.

## S2. Key ideas
- A child is a soul and deserves respect.
- Children copy the conduct of adults, so the example comes first.
- A simple impression of Krishna (prasadam, stories, kirtana) is the best gift.
- Parents and teachers correct children; a brahmacari does not.
- Open places, never alone, and prompt reporting keep children safe.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **BG 3.21**: "Whatever action a great man performs, common men follow." *For me:* children copy me, so I act well.
- **BG 2.13**: the soul passes "from boyhood to youth to old age". *For me:* a child is a soul and deserves respect.
- **SB 1.5.25**: "Once only, by their permission, I took the remnants of their food, and by so doing all my sins were at once eradicated." *For me:* prasadam and good company change a child's heart.

## S4. Words to know
- **Brahmacari:** a celibate student living simply and serving his guru.
- **Gurukula:** a school for children run by devotees.
- **Prasadam:** food offered to Krishna.
- **Remnants:** the food left after a devotee has eaten.
- **Child-protection contact:** the person at a temple who receives concerns about children's safety.
- **Purport:** Srila Prabhupada's explanation of a verse.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *Can a brahmacari joke with children?* Yes, gently and in public. He should not become childish (BIKC).
2. *Who corrects a child?* The parent or teacher.
3. *What if a child tells me something troubling?* Listen, thank him, do not promise secrecy, and tell the child-protection contact or a senior the same day.
4. *What can I give a child?* A good example, prasadam, a short story and kirtana.
