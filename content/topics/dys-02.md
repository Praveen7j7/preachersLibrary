# DYS 5: Substance and Shadow

> **DYS · 180 min · review: sources-checked.** Fifth session of "Discover Your Self"; textbook chapter 5 of *Discover Your Self*
> (VOICE, Pune, © 2008–2011; text in `research/iyf-materials/dys-book/`). Summarise, never read long passages. Two of the
> chapter's illustrations need handling with care (see J and K): the "saintly girl and the purgatives" story and the
> husband-and-wife analogy.

## A. Topic overview

| | |
|---|---|
| Batch / level | DYS, session 5 of 6 (pitch ≈ VL1) |
| Suggested duration | About 3 hours: 90 min lecture + 90 min engagement |
| Preparation time | About 90 min the first time |
| Difficulty | Introductory |
| Prerequisites | DYS 1–4 (especially session 4, "you are not the body") |
| Audience | Newcomers who have accepted, at least as a hypothesis, that the self is not the body, and now ask: so where do I belong, and why does nothing here satisfy for long? |
| Teaching objective | Show that what we chase in this world is a reflection ("shadow") of something real ("substance"): the soul belongs to God, is happy only in relationship with Him, and the practical way to begin is to hear and to chant |

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Session 4 ended with "I am not this body". The natural next question is "then what am I, and where do I belong?" Chapter 5
answers in three moves: God is all-attractive and we are His parts; the soul left its home seeking independent enjoyment and
now mistakes reflections for the real thing; the way back is to serve the whole, beginning with hearing and chanting. The
pitch stays VL1: images, stories, one experiment; the word "surrender" is explained, not demanded.

**Where IYF places DYS** (IYF's own documents, `research/batch-criteria.md` §1 and §8 point 1): the VL2 welcome letter is headed
"WELCOMES YOU IN DYS", the VL2 lesson plan opens with DYS-1…6, and the OTP announcement uses a "DYS video presentation" to recruit
for VL2. DYS is therefore IYF's six-session **opening journey**, placed at the start of VL2 and pitched a shade above VL1: the
listener has chosen to come, so one honest argument per session is welcome, but no practice is yet expected of them.

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **DYS 5** (here) | Substance vs shadow through analogies; the soul's home; why serving the whole satisfies the part; a first taste of chanting |
| VL1 | Short form (45 min): the tree and its reflection, the limbs and the stomach, one experiment |
| VL2 | [vl2-03](vl2-03.md) "Quest for happiness" builds on this session with the Gita's analysis of pleasure and the three kinds of happiness |
| VL3 | Chanting as a practice; the maha-mantra (the "great chant" of Hare Krsna) taught properly |
| SPS | The six opulences studied from the text; devotion as the soul's natural function |
| DPS | The individual soul (jiva) as part of God; Brahman, Paramatma, Bhagavan |
| BSS | Service as the way a part is fed; the daily practice of hearing and chanting |

**Not done here:**
- A full theology of the soul's "fall" or of the spiritual world. Keep to the book's level: "the soul belongs with God and has
  forgotten".
- Body-shaming. The chapter's "beauty queens become raisins" and the purgative story can be heard as contempt for the body
  or for women. Teach the principle (beauty is borrowed from the soul; the body is temporary) without the contempt.
- Teaching the maha-mantra as a technique with promised results. Introduce it; VL3 teaches it.

## C. Central message

Everything attractive in this world borrows its attraction from the soul within and, beyond that, from God, who is
all-attractive. We are chasing the shadow and forgetting the substance. The soul is a part of God and is satisfied, like a hand
feeding the stomach, only when it serves the whole (SB 4.31.14); the simplest way to begin is to hear about Him and to chant His names.

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. Explain "substance and shadow" with one of the book's analogies (the reflected tree, BG 15.1; the mirage; the doll in the shop).
2. State what "Bhagavan" means (the possessor of six opulences in full) and why the tradition calls Krsna "all-attractive".
3. Explain, with the stomach-and-limbs image (SB 4.31.14), why the tradition says the soul is happy only in serving the whole.
4. Name the three steps the chapter gives: the soul's forgetting (BG 7.27), the need for help to come out (BG 7.14), and the
   recommended practice for this age, chanting (CC Adi 17.21: a deeper verse; the talk uses only its plain meaning).
5. Try one five-minute session of hearing the maha-mantra and report what they noticed.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** BG 2.2 purport (Bhagavan), BG 15.7, BG 15.1, SB 4.31.14 and CC Adi 17.21 carry the talk. The rest are for questions.

**BG 2.2 (purport)**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/2/
- **Context:** Krsna's first words to the grieving Arjuna. The verse itself is a rebuke; the purport is the source for the word
  *Bhagavan*.
- **Purport point:** "Bhagavān is the ultimate in the Absolute Truth"; the Absolute is realised as Brahman, Paramatma and
  Bhagavan (SB 1.2.11 quoted), compared to sunshine, the sun's surface and the sun planet. The book's definition of Bhagavan
  as the possessor of six opulences (beauty, wealth, knowledge, fame, strength, renunciation) is Parasara Muni's; this
  purport names him and gives the list: "The Supreme Personality who possesses all riches, all strength, all fame, all
  beauty, all knowledge and all renunciation is called Bhagavān."
- **Use:** in the talk, for the opening "why are we attracted to beauty, strength, wealth?"

**BG 15.7**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/15/7/
> "The living entities in this conditioned world are My eternal fragmental parts. Due to conditioned life, they are struggling very hard with the six senses, which include the mind."
- **Teaching:** We are eternal parts of the Supreme; in this world we struggle.
- **Purport point:** The part has fragmental portions of the whole's qualities, "of which independence is one"; misuse of that
  independence conditions the soul, proper use frees it.
- **Application (preacher's):** This is the chapter's "sun and sunlight" image (God's potencies as the sun, ours as rays) and its
  statement that the soul has "a minute independence" to love God or to try to be independent.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 2.12 (purport)**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/12/
- **Purport point:** Prabhupada quotes the Katha Upanisad (2.2.13), *nityo nityanam cetanas cetananam / eko bahunam yo vidadhati
  kaman*: the Lord is the eternal among eternals, the one who provides for the many. The book quotes this Upanisad line ("one
  amongst many souls who are all subordinate to Him"); cite it through this purport.
- **Use:** background; for the question "are we God?"

**BG 7.5**: Verified. The living entities are the Lord's superior energy; "Energies are always controlled by the energetic"
(purport). **Use:** for the book's "Are we Gods or God's?" (ch. 4–5): we control a little, we are controlled entirely.

**BG 15.1**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/15/1/
> "…there is an imperishable banyan tree that has its roots upward and its branches down…"
- **Purport point:** The upside-down tree is like a tree reflected in water: "this material world is a reflection of the
  spiritual world"; the reflection is temporary, the real tree stands on the bank. This is the chapter's mango-tree image
  (dive into the river for the reflected mangoes and you only hurt your head).
- **Use:** in the talk; the central picture (N).

**BG 15.6**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/15/6/
> "That supreme abode of Mine is not illumined by the sun or moon, nor by fire or electricity. Those who reach it never return to this material world."
- **Purport point:** The spiritual planets are self-luminous; one should "desire to transfer himself to that eternal world and
  extricate himself from this false reflection of reality"; association with devotees is the way to loosen attachment.
- **Use:** in the talk, briefly, as "the home the chapter speaks of".

**BG 7.27 and BG 7.14**: Verified.
- 7.27: "all living entities are born into delusion, bewildered by dualities arisen from desire and hate." Purport: the deluded
  think "This is my wife; this is my house; I am the master of this house." **Use:** the soul's forgetting (the chapter's "he forgets his true identity").
- 7.14: the divine energy of the three modes is hard to overcome, "but those who have surrendered unto Me can easily cross
  beyond it." Purport: a man bound hand and foot cannot free himself; the rescuer must be free. **Use:** why we need help, and
  what "surrender" means here: accepting help.

**SB 4.31.14**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/31/14/
> "As pouring water on the root of a tree energizes the trunk, branches, twigs and everything else, and as supplying food to the stomach enlivens the senses and limbs of the body, simply worshiping the Supreme Personality of Godhead through devotional service automatically satisfies the demigods, who are parts of that Supreme Personality."
- **Context:** Narada instructing the Pracetas.
- **Teaching:** Serving the root serves the whole tree; feeding the stomach nourishes every limb.
- **Purport point:** Trying to feed the eyes or ears separately "the result is only havoc"; serving Krsna, everything is complete.
- **Application (preacher's):** The chapter's Panchatantra story of the limbs that went on strike against the stomach is this
  verse in story form. Tell the story, then show the verse.
- **Use:** in the talk; the central argument.

**BG 2.16**: Verified. "Of the nonexistent [the material body] there is no endurance and of the eternal [the soul] there is no
change." **Use:** the one-line definition of substance (what endures) and shadow (what does not).

**BG 2.62, BG 2.63**: Verified. Contemplating sense objects → attachment → lust → anger (2.62) → delusion → loss of memory and
intelligence → fall (2.63). Purport to 2.62: the senses "require real engagements"; the way out is a higher taste. **Use:** for
the question "why can't I just enjoy in moderation?" and for the chapter's "anarthas" (lust, anger, greed, pride, envy, illusion).

**CC Adi 17.21**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/adi/17/21/
> "In this Age of Kali there is no other means, no other means, no other means for self-realization than chanting the holy name, chanting the holy name, chanting the holy name of Lord Hari."
- **Context:** Sri Caitanya quoting the Brhan-naradiya Purana (the verse the chapter cites, *harer nama harer nama…*).
- **Teaching:** Chanting the holy name is the recommended process for this age.
- **Use:** in the talk, for its plain meaning only (deeper verse; flagged for the head).

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **Why are we attracted?** The chapter opens with film stars, boxers and billionaires: we are drawn to beauty, strength and
   wealth. Its claim: these qualities exist in full in Bhagavan, the possessor of six opulences (BG 2.2 purport; Parasara's
   definition), and what we meet here are fractions and reflections. *Krsna* means "all-attractive". Present this as the
   tradition's explanation of a universal experience, not as a proof.
2. **Substance and shadow.** What endures is substance; what passes is its shadow (BG 2.16). The soul is substance; the body
   and its beauty are shadow. The material world as a whole is a reflection of the spiritual world, like a tree in water
   (BG 15.1 purport). You cannot eat reflected mangoes, drink mirage water, or enjoy a doll (the chapter's three images).
3. **Where does the soul come from?** The soul is an eternal part of God (BG 15.7) with a minute independence (15.7 purport).
   Choosing to enjoy apart from God, it forgets itself (BG 7.27) and mistakes "my body, my house" for identity. The chapter's
   image: the child who insists on cooking is given a toy kitchen; when hungry, he returns to his mother. The material world is
   that toy kitchen. (Keep the chapter's prison analogy light: it is Prabhupada's, but at DYS "a school" or "a hospital" lands
   better than "a jail", and the chapter itself uses the hospital element.)
4. **Why the part is happy only in serving the whole.** The stomach and the limbs (SB 4.31.14): no limb is nourished by feeding
   itself. The chapter's Panchatantra strike story makes the point memorable. Its second analogy (husband at the office, wife at
   home) is dated and lands badly with this audience; replace it with any team where everyone benefits by serving the shared
   goal (an orchestra, a kitchen, a relay team).
5. **Why we cannot climb out alone.** The modes of nature are hard to overcome; those who accept the Lord's help cross them
   (BG 7.14, with the bound-man image from the purport). "Surrender" at DYS means accepting help from the one who is free.
6. **The goal: purification and glorification.** The chapter: no one can become desireless; we change the *quality* of desire,
   from "for me" to "for Him", and clear the six *anarthas* (lust, anger, greed, pride, envy, illusion; BG 2.62–63 shows the
   chain) by hearing about Krsna and glorifying Him. The chapter's examples (anger at blasphemy "as Hanuman burnt Lanka")
   are for the preacher's background; do not teach anger at DYS.
7. **The practice.** For this age, chanting the holy name (CC Adi 17.21: a deeper verse; the talk uses only its plain meaning). The chapter's lens image: diffused sunlight
   warms paper; focused through a lens it burns; attention makes the difference. Offer one five-minute hearing session.
8. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - "The body and the world are evil." No: shadow is not evil; it is *borrowed* light. The right response is gratitude and right use.
   - "Serving God means giving up everything." The chapter asks for a change of direction, not of occupation.
   - "Chanting is a Hindu ritual." The chapter's claim is that sound addressed to God works for anyone; Prabhupada's students
     came from every background. Offer it as an experiment.
9. **Pitch for DYS.** Images first, verse second, one experiment. Define *Bhagavan*, *anartha*, *maha-mantra*.

## G. Lecture flow

Session format: about 3 hours; first half lecture, second half engagement (M.2).

| Time | Segment | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0-10 | H.1 start: "Who are you attracted to, and why?" (H.2); collect qualities: beauty, strength, wealth, fame | Write them up |
| 10-30 | Bhagavan: the six opulences in full; "Krsna" = all-attractive; sun and rays (BG 15.7) | The tradition's explanation, not proof |
| 30-50 | Substance and shadow: BG 2.16; the reflected tree (BG 15.1); mirage; the fish out of water | Draw N |
| 50-65 | Where the soul comes from and why it forgot: BG 15.7 purport (independence), BG 7.27; the toy-kitchen story | Light touch |
| 65-80 | Why serving the whole satisfies: the limbs' strike story, then SB 4.31.14; why we need help (BG 7.14) | Use a team example, not the husband/wife analogy |
| 80-90 | The way to begin: hearing and chanting (CC Adi 17.21); five minutes of hearing the maha-mantra; the experiment of the week | Invite, do not require |
| 90-180 | Engagement block: choose from M.2 (limbs role-play, shadow-and-substance map, listening circle) | Praise good answers by name |

**Alternative: up to a 2-hour lecture, activities skipped:** give 30-50 and 65-80 about ten minutes more each (add the centrifugal and
centripetal image and the root-watering image from J), keep five minutes of hearing, and close with M.1.
**If time is short, drop first:** the "where the soul comes from" segment (two sentences), then the mirage.

**Exit check, from the Preaching Encyclopedia (HG Radheshyam Das, VOICE, © 2008-2011)** (`research/encyclopedia/Discover Your Self/0018 Substance and Shadow.txt`): students can name the six opulences and say why Krsna means "all-attractive"; distinguish material beauty ("fleeting, like a counterfeit note") from spiritual beauty; give a reason why the soul is in this world; show with the limbs' story why the part is nourished by serving the whole; and state the conclusion: purify the heart by hearing about Krsna and chanting His names. The Encyclopedia also wants the material world "proved" to be a prison: at DYS say "the tradition describes", not "we have proved".

**Alternative structure (story-led):** open with the limbs' strike story, let groups extract the lesson, then build the three ideas (parts of God; shadow vs substance; service nourishes) around it, ending with chanting.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Welcome people by name; recall last session's one idea: "I am not this body".
2. Say: "Tonight's question: if I am not the body, where do I belong, and why does nothing here satisfy for long?"
3. Ask H.2 question 1; write the qualities on the board; thank each answer.
4. Ask: "Where do these qualities come from, and why do they fade?" Do not answer yet.
5. Say: "Today we look for the real thing behind the reflection. At the end you will get one experiment to try."

**Adapting for your audience:** the "next phone" works wherever there are shops or screens. For a village or farming group use the harvest that is eaten within weeks; for professionals use the promotion celebrated for a week before the next target arrives.

**H.2 Opening questions**
- Think of someone millions admire. What is it about them?
- Think of something you wanted badly. How long did the joy last, and what did you want next?
- When you feel restless although "nothing is wrong", what do you think is missing?

**More on opening and engagement**


- **Opening question:** "Think of someone millions admire. What is it about them?" Write up the qualities. "Where do you think
  those qualities come from, and why do they fade?"
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** Someone saves for a year for a phone; the joy lasts a week; the next model is already out.
  What exactly did they want?
- **Transition:** "The Gita says we are chasing a reflection. Today: what is the real thing, and how do we reach it?"
- **Why it matters to this audience:** the cycle of wanting, getting and wanting again is the shape of most young lives; naming
  it is a relief.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **God is all-attractive; we are attracted to His reflections.** BG 2.2 purport (Bhagavan), BG 15.7. *Example:* the qualities
   on the board. *Transition:* so what is real and what is reflection?
2. **Substance and shadow.** BG 2.16; the reflected tree (BG 15.1). *Example:* mangoes in the river. *Transition:* how did we end up
   diving for reflections?
3. **We forgot.** Minute independence misused (BG 15.7 purport; BG 7.27). *Example:* the toy kitchen. *Transition:* so how is a part
   satisfied?
4. **Serve the whole.** SB 4.31.14; the limbs' strike. *Transition:* we cannot climb out alone (BG 7.14).
5. **Begin by hearing and chanting.** CC Adi 17.21; the lens image. *Transition:* to the experiment.

## J. Stories and illustrations

- **The limbs' strike against the stomach** (book ch. 5; a Panchatantra-type fable). Tell it fully; it is the best piece in the chapter.
- **The toy kitchen** (book ch. 5; a teaching analogy). The child who insists on cooking.
- **The reflected mango tree** (BG 15.1 purport; book ch. 5).
- **Srila Prabhupada and the doll in the shop window** (book ch. 5): "Can you enjoy that woman?" **Requires source review:**
  the anecdote's source is not given; tell it as "a story told about Srila Prabhupada" or omit.
- **The saintly girl and the purgatives** (book ch. 5). This is Srila Prabhupada's own essay "Truth and Beauty" (published in
  *The Science of Self-Realization*), retold. **Partially verified:** check the essay before using. **Handle with care:** at DYS,
  and in mixed groups, its graphic detail and its gendering (a woman's body as the lesson) can offend or shame. Prefer the doll or
  the reflected tree; if you use it, tell it briefly, name the source, and make the point about *every* body including your own.
- **Hanuman burning Lanka** (book ch. 5) is background only; do not use anger examples at DYS.
- **The fish out of water** (Preaching Encyclopedia (HG Radheshyam Das, VOICE, © 2008–2011) DYS 5 lecture notes, `research/encyclopedia/_other/DYS - Substance and
  Shadow - DYS 5 - Substance _ Shadow.txt`): offer the fish money, magazines and drink; it is still unhappy, because it is out
  of its element. Lesson: the soul's restlessness here is a sign of where it belongs. Pair it with the hostel student going home.
- **Watering the root** (same notes; the image is SB 4.31.14's own): leaves and branches are fed by watering the root, not
  each leaf. Lesson: serving the source nourishes everyone; "serve humanity instead of God" waters the leaves.
- **Centrifugal and centripetal** (*Spiritual Scientist* article "Two-point formula for happiness", attached to DYS 5 in the
  Encyclopedia, `…/Discover Your Self/0019 SS articles supplementing DYS.txt`): two pulls in us, outward to sense objects and
  inward to the all-attractive; and the Mercedes owner who is lost beside the Fiat owner who knows the way home. A modern image
  for "substance and shadow"; attribute it to the article.
- **Durvasa, the Pandavas and the leaf of śāk** (same DYS 5 notes: "when Krsna is satisfied, everyone is satisfied"). A
  Mahabharata narrative; **Requires source review** against a BBT retelling before telling it in full. The notes' claim that the
  Adam-and-Eve story "is originally from the Puranas" is unsourced: do not use.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"Why would an all-attractive God let us forget Him?"**
   - The tradition's answer: love needs freedom; a minute independence is part of being a person (BG 15.7 purport). The chapter's
     toy kitchen: the mother lets the child try. Be honest that this is an answer *within* the tradition, not a proof.
2. **"Isn't calling the world a 'shadow' life-denying?"**
   - A shadow is real light, borrowed. The teaching asks for right use and gratitude, not contempt. BG 2.16 says only that the
     body does not endure; it does not say it is evil.
3. **"I enjoy my relationships. Are they fake?"**
   - No. The chapter says they are *reflections* of relationships that do not end; the pain in them is that they end. The claim is
     that the original is available, not that the reflection is nothing.
4. **"Why serve? I'd rather be free."**
   - SB 4.31.14: no limb is nourished by feeding itself. The chapter's claim is that service to the whole is what freedom feels like
     from the inside. Test it with one week of small service.
5. **"Chanting sounds like magic words."**
   - The chapter's claim: the holy name is God Himself as sound, and attention (the lens) matters. At DYS: try five minutes and
     notice; the tradition invites the experiment (CC Adi 17.21).
6. **"Is this body-shaming?"** (if the purgative story is used)
   - Acknowledge the discomfort. The point is about every body, including the speaker's: beauty is borrowed from the soul.
     If the story landed badly, say so and move to the reflected tree.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The follower count.** Meera's account crosses 50,000 followers. For a day she is elated; by the weekend she is
anxious about engagement dropping.
- **Principles:** substance vs shadow (BG 2.16); the chain of attachment (BG 2.62–63).
- **Questions:** What did Meera think the number would give her? Why did it not stay? What would "watering the root" look like in her week?
- **Takeaway:** A reflection cannot hold what it reflects.

**Case 2: The team that fed itself.** In a hostel kitchen rota everyone cooks only their own meal; the kitchen is chaos and
nobody eats well. One week they cook together for the whole floor.
- **Principles:** SB 4.31.14; the limbs' strike.
- **Questions:** Why did serving the whole feed everyone better? Where else does this hold? Where does it not?
- **Takeaway:** The part is nourished by serving the whole; the tradition extends this to the soul and God.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first)
1. What does "Bhagavan" mean? *Expected:* the possessor of all opulences in full (beauty, wealth, knowledge, fame, strength, renunciation).
2. What does the reflected tree teach (BG 15.1)? *Expected:* what we chase here is a reflection of a real thing; diving for it only hurts.
3. What did the limbs learn (SB 4.31.14)? *Expected:* they weakened themselves by not feeding the stomach; a part is nourished by serving the whole.
4. According to BG 15.7, what are we in relation to God? *Expected:* His eternal parts.
5. Does "shadow" mean the body is bad? *Expected:* No. It is borrowed light that passes (BG 2.16).
6. What practice does the tradition suggest to begin? *Expected:* hearing and chanting. Praise any honest answer.

**M.2 Activities (engagement block, about 90 min; choose two)**
- **A. The limbs' strike, role-play (30 min).** Five volunteers play hand, leg, mouth, eyes and stomach. 10 min to stage the fable from J, 5 min performing, 15 min in pairs: "Where in life does a part try to feed only itself?" Praise every honest example; nobody shares anything private.
- **B. Shadow-and-substance map (30 min).** Groups of four draw two columns, "things I chase" and "what I was really looking for", and list five items (10 min). Each group reads out two and the room guesses the deeper want (10 min). The preacher links the wants to the soul's search (10 min).
- **C. Five-minute hearing and listening circle (25 min).** Hear the maha-mantra in silence (5 min); each person who wishes says one word for what they noticed (10 min); the preacher explains the week's experiment and answers questions (10 min). Invite only; nobody is required to chant.

**M.3 Reflection (private, optional).** Which "shadow" have I chased hardest this year? What was I really looking for? What one small act of "feeding the stomach", serving the whole, would I try this week?

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Why are we drawn to beauty, strength, wealth and fame? The tradition says all these qualities exist in full in God, called Bhagavan ("the one who has all opulences"), and what we meet in this world are small reflections. A reflection is a "shadow"; the real thing is the "substance". The Bhagavad-gita (an ancient Sanskrit scripture) compares the material world to a tree reflected in water (BG 15.1): dive for the reflected mangoes and you only bump your head on the riverbed. Substance lasts, shadow passes (BG 2.16). This does not make the world or the body bad; a shadow is borrowed light.

The tradition says we are eternal parts of God (BG 15.7) who used our small independence to try enjoying apart from Him and forgot where we belong (BG 7.27). That explains the restlessness that remains when we get what we wanted: like a fish on the shore, we are out of our element. We need help to come back (BG 7.14).

A part is nourished by serving the whole, as every limb is fed when the stomach is fed, or every leaf when the root is watered (SB 4.31.14). The simplest way to begin, the tradition says, is to hear about God and chant His names (CC Adi 17.21); a recorded five minutes is enough to start the experiment. Nobody is asked to believe more than they have tried.

## S2. Key ideas

- Substance endures; shadow passes (BG 2.16). The soul is substance; the body and its qualities are shadow.
- "Krsna" means all-attractive: every quality we admire is a fraction of His.
- We forgot our home by choosing to enjoy apart from God; the toy kitchen story.
- The part is satisfied by serving the whole, like limbs feeding the stomach.
- Chanting the holy name is the recommended practice for this age; attention is the lens.

## S3. Verses to remember

- **BG 15.7:** "The living entities in this conditioned world are My eternal fragmental parts…" *For me:* I belong to someone.
- **SB 4.31.14:** Water the root and the whole tree is fed; feed the stomach and every limb is nourished. *For me:* serving the whole is how I am fed.
- **CC Adi 17.21:** In this age there is no other way than chanting the holy name. *For me:* the practice is simple enough to try today.

## S4. Words to know

- **Bhagavan:** God as the possessor of all opulences in full (beauty, wealth, knowledge, fame, strength, renunciation).
- **Substance / shadow:** what endures / what only reflects it and passes.
- **Anartha:** an unwanted thing in the heart: lust, anger, greed, pride, envy, illusion.
- **Maha-mantra:** the "great chant": Hare Krsna, Hare Krsna, Krsna Krsna, Hare Hare / Hare Rama, Hare Rama, Rama Rama, Hare Hare.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Why would God let us forget Him?** Love needs freedom; we have a small independence and misused it (BG 15.7 purport).
2. **Is the world evil, then?** No. A shadow is borrowed light. The teaching asks for right use, not contempt.
3. **Are my relationships fake?** No. They are reflections of relationships that do not end; what hurts is that these end.
4. **Why serve? I'd rather be free.** No limb is fed by feeding itself (SB 4.31.14). Test it: serve the whole for a week and see.
5. **Isn't chanting just words?** The tradition says the name is God as sound, and attention matters. Try five minutes and notice.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. "Bhagavan" means: (a) a teacher, (b) the possessor of all opulences in full, (c) a temple.
2. In BG 15.1, what is the material world compared to?
3. What did the limbs discover when they stopped feeding the stomach (SB 4.31.14)?
4. According to BG 15.7, what are we in relation to God?
5. What practice does CC Adi 17.21 recommend for this age?
6. True or false: "shadow" means the body is evil.

**Answers:** 1. (b). 2. A tree reflected in water, roots up, branches down (BG 15.1). 3. That they themselves grew weak; serving the whole nourishes the parts (SB 4.31.14).
4. His eternal fragmental parts (BG 15.7). 5. Chanting the holy name (CC Adi 17.21). 6. False: a shadow is borrowed light; it passes (BG 2.16) but is not evil.
