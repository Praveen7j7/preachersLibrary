# Realisations of Maharaja Yayati

> Status line: BSS · 120 min · drafted · not marked sensitive (the page speaks plainly about desire; the head may wish to set `sensitive: true`). Builds on bss-10; bss-21 to bss-24 treat the BIKC chapters on desire in depth.

## A. Topic overview
| | |
|---|---|
| Batch / level | BSS, brahmacari training |

| Teaching objective | Listeners hear a king describe from experience why desire cannot be fed to silence, and how he then turned fully to the Lord; they leave with a plain understanding and a hopeful practice |

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level
The brahmacari is training to live without the very thing the world offers as the fulfilment of desire. The warning
carries weight only when the person who gives it is believable. Yayati is: he had everything for a thousand years.

| Level | Treatment |

## C. Central message
A man who enjoyed for a thousand years concluded that enjoyment feeds desire like butter fed to a fire, and he gave
himself fully to the Lord; a brahmacari can accept that conclusion on the strength of his experience, and pass on
to chanting, hearing and service.

## D. Learning outcomes
Participants can:
1. Retell the story of Yayati in five steps.
2. Explain the fire-and-butter verse in their own words and why its conclusion is "voluntary cessation" with Krsna's help.
3. Explain the he-goat parable as Yayati's self-description.
4. Explain the caution of SB 9.19.17 in the words of its purport.
5. State what Yayati did after his realisation (SB 9.19.19-25).

## E. Scriptural foundation
**SB 9.18.39**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/9/18/39/
> "My dear son, I am not yet satisfied in my sexual desires."
- **Context:** Yayati, afflicted by premature old age, asks his sons to exchange his old age for their youth (SB 9.18.38-39).
- **Teaching:** He asks his eldest, Yadu; Yadu declines (SB 9.18.40, not quoted here).
- **Use:** in the talk, as the start of the thousand years.

**SB 9.18.43**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/9/18/43/
> "Pūru replied: O Your Majesty, who in this world can repay his debt to his father?"
- **Teaching:** The youngest son Puru agrees; the verse says the father's mercy gives the human form which can enable one to become an associate of the Supreme Lord.
- **Application (preacher's):** Puru's service is a picture of obedience; note that SB 9.19.21 later shows Yayati returning the youth.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 9.18.51**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/9/18/51/
> "Although Mahārāja Yayāti was the king of the entire world and he engaged his mind and five senses in enjoying material possessions for one thousand years, he was unable to be satisfied."
- **Use:** in the talk; the whole case in one sentence.

**SB 9.19.1**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/9/19/1/
> "Yayāti was very much attached to woman. In due course of time, however, when disgusted with sexual enjoyment and its bad effects, he renounced this way of life and narrated the following story to his beloved wife."
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 9.19.3**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/9/19/3/
> "While wandering in the forest, eating to satisfy his senses, a he-goat by chance approached a well"
- **Teaching:** In the well he saw a she-goat fallen by the results of fruitive activities; the parable goes on in the following verses (SB 9.19.4-11).
- **Use:** in the talk, told briefly.

**SB 9.19.12**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/9/19/12/
> "I am exactly like that he-goat, for I am so poor in intelligence that I am captivated by your beauty and have forgotten the real task of self-realization."
- **Teaching:** Yayati applies the parable to himself.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 9.19.13**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/9/19/13/
> "A person who is lusty cannot satisfy his mind even if he has enough of everything in this world, including rice, barley and other food grains, gold, animals and women."
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 9.19.14**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/9/19/14/
> "As supplying butter to a fire does not diminish the fire but instead increases it more and more, the endeavor to stop lusty desires by continual enjoyment can never be successful."
- **Teaching:** The bracketed text adds: one must voluntarily cease from material desires.
- **Purport point:** One may have enough money and resources to satisfy the senses and still not be satisfied; the example of butter on a blazing fire is very appropriate.
- **Application (preacher's):** The verse says enjoyment cannot end desire. The "voluntary" stop is made possible by a higher taste, which is Krsna consciousness.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 9.19.16**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/9/19/16/
> "For those who are too attached to material enjoyment, sense gratification is very difficult to give up."
- **Teaching:** Even an invalid with old age cannot give up such desires; one who desires happiness must give them up.
- **Use:** background.

**SB 9.19.17**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/9/19/17/
> "One should not allow oneself to sit on the same seat even with one’s own mother, sister or daughter, for the senses are so strong that even though one is very advanced in knowledge, he may be attracted by sex."
- **Purport point:** "Learning the etiquette of how to deal with women does not free one from sexual attraction." "if one allows himself to sit very close to such a woman, one may be attracted. This is a psychological fact." "even if one is highly advanced, materially or spiritually, he may be attracted by lusty desires. The object of attraction may even be one’s mother, sister or daughter. Therefore, one should be extremely careful in dealings with women." "A brahmacārī is forbidden even to see the wife of his spiritual master if she happens to be young."
- **Application (preacher's):** BIKC (chapter "Restricting Association with Women") says: "For those interested in spiritual advancement, association with women must be restricted to the minimum. In the presence of a woman, the consciousness of a man changes."
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 9.19.18**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/9/19/18/
> "I have spent a full one thousand years enjoying sense gratification, yet my desire to enjoy such pleasure increases daily."
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 9.19.19**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/9/19/19/
> "Therefore, I shall now give up all these desires and meditate upon the Supreme Personality of Godhead."
- **Teaching:** Free from false prestige, he will wander in the forest with the animals.
- **Use:** in the talk; the turn.

**SB 9.19.21**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/9/19/21/
> "After speaking in this way to his wife, Devayānī, King Yayāti, who was now free from all material desires, called his youngest son, Pūru, and returned Pūru’s youth in exchange for his own old age."
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 9.19.25**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/9/19/25/
> "Because King Yayāti completely surrendered unto the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Vāsudeva, he was freed from all contamination of the material modes of nature."
- **Use:** closing; the devotional end of the story.

## F. Philosophical explanation
**Principle.** Desire does not reduce by feeding; a higher taste replaces it. **Why it matters.** The brahmacari may think a lifetime of enjoyment would end the longing; Yayati's account answers this with a thousand years of evidence. **Connections.** bss-09 (the soul's happiness is within), bss-10 (the forest and its plunderers), here the king who stayed in the forest for a thousand years. **Consequences.** Those who miss it argue, "just a bit more", or treat their own strong desire as a sign of unfitness. Those who see it recognise a universal condition, ask for help and take shelter of chanting. **Misunderstandings.** (1) That the caution of SB 9.19.17 is only for the weak: the purport says "even if one is highly advanced, materially or spiritually, he may be attracted by lusty desires." (2) That sexual desire is solved by more discipline alone: the remedy is turning the heart to Vasudeva (SB 9.19.25). (3) That a person who has fallen is finished: Yayati himself changed. **Pitch.** Frank, hopeful, unembarrassed; use plain words.

## H. Opening and engagement
**H.1 How to start.** Light a small lamp or candle. Put a spoonful of ghee near it, not on it. Ask: "If I add ghee, what happens?" Read SB 9.19.14 aloud after the answers. Say: "A king tested this for a thousand years and came back to tell us."
**H.2 Opening questions.** (1) "Who is someone you know who always says 'just a little more'?" (2) "Why do you think a king who had everything was still not satisfied?" (3) "What does a person need if enjoyment cannot satisfy?"

## I. Core teaching points
**I.1 A thousand years (SB 9.18.51, 9.19.18).** The king had a kingdom, a young body and a thousand years. The result: the desire increased daily. End: the experiment has been done by the one best placed to do it.
**I.2 The he-goat (SB 9.19.3-12).** Yayati tells his wife a parable of a he-goat who wandered to a well, helped a she-goat out, and became bound in a life of rivalry and sorrow, never satisfied for many years. He then says "I am exactly like that he-goat." Tell it as the verses tell it (SB 9.19.3-11); the point is Yayati's self-description. End: he sees his own condition without hiding.
**I.3 Butter on fire (SB 9.19.13-14).** Rice, grain, gold, animals and women together cannot satisfy a lusty mind. The remedy named is voluntary cessation, made possible by a taste for the Lord. Illustration (preacher's, labelled): a person eating salt water to quench thirst; the more drunk the thirstier. Devotional point: Krsna's name, hearing and service are the sweet water.
**I.4 Caution (SB 9.19.16-17).** The senses are strong, even for an advanced person; the purport: "Therefore, one should be extremely careful in dealings with women." BIKC (chapter "Restricting Association with Women"): "The brahmacārī has no real business associating with women—whatever must be there, he keeps as brief as possible." Anyone who struggles asks for help from a senior or a counsellor.

## J. Stories and illustrations
**J.1 Yayati (SB 9.18-9.19; key verse 9.19.14).** Yayati, a great king, married Devayani, daughter of Sukracarya. As told in SB 9.18, he was afflicted with premature old age and asked his sons to exchange it for their youth. Puru, the youngest, agreed (SB 9.18.43). Yayati enjoyed for a thousand years and was unsatisfied (9.18.51). He then told his wife the parable of the he-goat, concluded that desire is not ended by enjoyment (9.19.14), returned Puru's youth, gave Puru the kingdom (9.19.21-23), and took shelter of Vasudeva (9.19.25). Devotional lesson: the end of lust is not suppression but surrender to the Lord.
**J.2 Invented illustration (labelled):** a traveller drinks sea water on a boat because he is thirsty; each cup makes him thirstier. The sea is the world; fresh water is the name of Krsna, heard and chanted. This repeats the point of SB 9.19.14 and ends in the Lord's name.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections
1. **Isn't sex a natural thing?** The verse speaks about satisfying lust, not about natural functions; its point is that more enjoyment does not end the hunger.
2. **Why did Yayati get so many chances?** The story shows his mercy-received change; we cannot assume the same time.
3. **Does SB 9.19.17 distrust family?** The purport: "Generally, of course, one is not sexually attracted to his mother, sister or daughter, but if one allows himself to sit very close to such a woman, one may be attracted. This is a psychological fact."
4. **What if I have fallen?** Yayati also returned; Krsna consciousness is open to all (see bss-39, bss-40 for support and guidance).
5. **How can I stop?** By a higher taste: chanting, hearing, service and association (SB 5.5.2, 5.13.22).

## L. Case studies
**L.1 Invented case for discussion.** A brahmacari feels strong desire after a film scene. *Principles:* SB 9.19.14, 9.19.17. *Questions:* what can he do in the first five minutes? *Analysis:* leave the situation, chant aloud, speak to a senior, take prasadam. *Takeaway:* turn toward Krsna, not away from help.
**L.2 Invented case (extension).** A young man says, "I'll enjoy for some years and renounce later like Yayati." *Principles:* SB 9.19.14, 9.18.51. *Questions:* what did Yayati's thousand years give? *Analysis:* he came back to say it fails; the verse offers no guarantee for a second try. *Takeaway:* there is no better time than now.

## M. Discussion and reflection
**M.1 Closing check questions.**
1. *How long did Yayati enjoy?* One thousand years (SB 9.18.51).
2. *Did he become satisfied?* No (SB 9.18.51, 9.19.18).
3. *What is the example of SB 9.19.14?* Butter fed to a fire.
4. *Who in the parable does Yayati say he is like?* The he-goat (SB 9.19.12).
5. *What did he finally do?* Gave up the desires, returned Puru's youth and surrendered to Vasudeva (SB 9.19.19-25).
6. *What is the lesson of SB 9.19.17?* The senses are strong even for the learned.
**M.2 Activities.** (a) *Lamp and ghee (5 min):* repeat the opening demonstration with a volunteer and ask them to explain in a sentence. (b) *Write a verse in your own words (10 min):* pairs paraphrase SB 9.19.14 for a friend. (c) *Letter from Yayati (15 min, extension):* groups write a short imagined letter from Yayati to a young person, ending in the surrender of 9.19.25 (labelled as an exercise).
**M.3 Reflection.** "What is the taste that could replace what I keep reaching for?"

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary
King Yayati ruled the whole world and enjoyed with his mind and senses for one thousand years, and he was still not satisfied (SB 9.18.51). When he was afflicted with old age he asked his sons to give him their youth; his youngest son Puru agreed (SB 9.18.43). At the end Yayati told his wife a story of a he-goat who never became satisfied, and said, "I am exactly like that he-goat" (SB 9.19.12).
He concluded that no amount of things can satisfy lust, and that trying to end desire by enjoying is like pouring butter on a fire (SB 9.19.13-14). He warned that the senses are so strong that even a learned person may be attracted (SB 9.19.17). Then he decided to give up his desires and meditate on the Lord (SB 9.19.19), returned Puru's youth (SB 9.19.21), and, completely surrendered to Vasudeva, was freed from the modes of nature (SB 9.19.25). For a brahmacari the point is hopeful: a higher taste replaces desire.

## S2. Key ideas
- Enjoyment feeds desire; it cannot end it.
- The king saw it by experience.
- The senses are strong, so "one should be extremely careful in dealings with women" (SB 9.19.17 purport).
- Surrender to Vasudeva is the end of lust.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **SB 9.19.14**: butter on fire. *For me:* more enjoyment will not end desire.
- **SB 9.19.18**: after a thousand years the desire increased. *For me:* the experiment has been done.
- **SB 9.19.25**: surrender frees from the modes. *For me:* turn to Krsna.

## S4. Words to know
- **Realisation:** truth known by experience.
- **Lust:** the strong desire for sense pleasure.
- **Surrender:** giving oneself to the Lord's service.
- **Voluntary:** by one's own choice.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *Is desire wrong?* The story says enjoyment cannot satisfy it; the cure is a higher taste.
2. *What if I struggle?* Chant, serve, and talk to a senior; many struggle.
3. *What does Yayati say about himself?* That he is like the he-goat, "so poor in intelligence that I am captivated by your beauty" (SB 9.19.12); the chapter opens: "Yayāti was very much attached to woman" (SB 9.19.1).
4. *Can anyone change?* Yayati did.

## S6. Check your understanding
1. How many years did Yayati enjoy? 2. What example shows desire grows? 3. Who is the he-goat? 4. What did Yayati do at the end? 5. What does SB 9.19.17 say about the senses?

**Answers:** 1. One thousand (SB 9.18.51). 2. Butter on a fire (SB 9.19.14). 3. Yayati himself (SB 9.19.12). 4. Gave up desires and surrendered to Vasudeva (SB 9.19.19, 9.19.25). 5. They are so strong that even a knowledgeable person may be attracted (SB 9.19.17).
