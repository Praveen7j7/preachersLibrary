# Lifestyle management

> **VL1 · 45–60 min · review: sources checked, awaiting senior review.** IYF holds two decks for this topic: a short "Lifestyle management from Bhagwat
> Gita" (six dimensions of wellness; most slides are images) and HG Sundar Gopal Prabhu's "Topic-5 Lifestyle
> Management from Bhagavad Gita" (time in four buckets; three spokes of a transformed life; the student's three
> fields: spiritual practice, missionary spirit, studies). The Preaching Encyclopedia chapter *Lifestyle
> Management* adds four human needs: good animal, good craftsman, good friend, good saint. This page keeps the
> four buckets and the four needs, and supports every rule from the Gita and *The Nectar of Instruction*. The
> Sundar Gopal deck's later slides (the nine stages of bhakti, the missionary section) are VL3/SPS material and are
> left out. Not a sensitive topic.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

"Lifestyle" is a word this audience uses about clothes, gadgets and holidays. The Gita uses the idea for something
plainer: how a person eats, sleeps, works and rests, and whether those habits serve a purpose. A VL1 room will
accept a talk that begins with their tiredness and ends with a practical audit. Nobody has to believe anything to
try regular sleep.

Builds on vl1-09 (regulating the four bodily demands as a skill) and vl1-24 (priorities and hours): this page does not repeat their arguments; it adds the audit of a whole day, the four needs and the six leaks. The BG 18.37–38 "poison first" idea is argued in vl1-26 and here gets one sentence.

Three VL1 talks share this ground:

- Judging anyone's food, sleep or entertainment. Regulation is taught as a skill.
- Asking for a morning programme or a fixed number of rounds. VL3 does that.

## C. Central message

A lifestyle is what your hours add up to. The Gita asks for regulation in four places (eating, sleeping,
recreation, work), warns of six leaks, and gives the one purpose that tells you what "enough" is. Regulate the
body and the day becomes yours.

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. Sort a day's hours into four buckets (bodily needs, occupational duties, unnecessary activities, constitutional
   duties) and name the Encyclopedia's four needs.
2. State the Gita's four regulations (BG 6.17) and its warning about extremes (BG 6.16).
3. Explain, from BG 17.8–10, how the Gita reads food, and from BG 2.69 what it says about sleep and waking.
4. Name the six lifestyle leaks of NOI 2 in plain words.
5. Choose one change to a daily habit, small enough to keep for a week.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** BG 6.16–17, 17.8–10, NOI 2 and SB 1.2.10 carry the talk. The rest prepare you for questions.

**BG 6.16**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/16/
> "There is no possibility of one's becoming a yogī, O Arjuna, if one eats too much or eats too little, sleeps too much or does not sleep enough."
- **Context:** Chapter 6, the practice of yoga; Krsna describes the conditions for it.
- **Teaching:** Both extremes, in eating and in sleeping, block progress.
- **Purport point:** "Too much eating means eating more than is required to keep the body and soul together."
  "One who eats more than required will dream very much while sleeping, and he must consequently sleep more than is
  required." The purport gives a standard: "One should not sleep more than six hours daily."
- **Application (preacher's):** The verse is about *both* ends: the exam-week all-nighter and the Sunday
  twelve-hour sleep. The six-hour figure is Srila Prabhupada's standard for a yogi; at VL1, present it as such and
  ask for regularity first (O).
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 6.17**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/17/
> "He who is regulated in his habits of eating, sleeping, recreation and work can mitigate all material pains by practicing the yoga system."
- **Teaching:** Four areas of regulation; the result is relief from material pains.
- **Purport point:** "Extravagance in the matter of eating, sleeping, defending and mating… can block advancement";
  "any unnecessary time spent sleeping is considered a great loss." Rupa Gosvami and Haridasa Thakura are given as
  examples of regulated devotees.
- **Application (preacher's):** The whole talk in one verse: lifestyle management is these four words. "Recreation"
  is in the list; the Gita does not abolish rest, it regulates it. The examples of Rupa Gosvami and Haridasa Thakura
  are the standard of great devotees, not a demand on the room.
- **Use:** in the talk (the spine).

**BG 17.8**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/17/8/
> "Foods dear to those in the mode of goodness increase the duration of life, purify one's existence and give strength, health, happiness and satisfaction. Such foods are juicy, fatty, wholesome, and pleasing to the heart."
- **Use:** in the talk, with 17.9–10.

**BG 17.9**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/17/9/
> "Foods that are too bitter, too sour, salty, hot, pungent, dry and burning are dear to those in the mode of passion. Such foods cause distress, misery and disease."
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 17.10**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/17/10/
> "Food prepared more than three hours before being eaten, food that is tasteless, decomposed and putrid, and food consisting of remnants and untouchable things is dear to those in the mode of darkness."
- **Teaching (17.8–10):** Food is read by its effect: life, purity, strength and satisfaction (goodness); distress
  and disease (passion); staleness and infection (ignorance).
- **Purport point (17.10):** "The purpose of food is to increase the duration of life, purify the mind and aid
  bodily strength. This is its only purpose." Milk products, grains, fruits and vegetables are the foods "very dear
  to those in the mode of goodness"; prasadam is the best food.
- **Application (preacher's):** Three questions for any plate: does it give strength and satisfaction, or a burn
  and a crash, or is it simply stale? No diet plan is needed; the categories are enough for a first audit. The
  purport's vegetarian argument may be stated as the text's position; don't press it at VL1.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 2.69**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/69/
> "What is night for all beings is the time of awakening for the self-controlled; and the time of awakening for all beings is night for the introspective sage."
- **Purport point:** "There are two classes of intelligent men"; the introspective sage "remains alert in the
  'night' of the materialistic men" and "feels transcendental pleasure in the gradual advancement of spiritual
  culture".
- **Application (preacher's):** The verse is about two kinds of wakefulness, not about clock time. Its practical
  echo is that the hours others waste can be the hours one grows. For this audience: the early hour is the only one
  nobody else wants.
- **Use:** in the talk, one line.

**BG 3.8**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/8/
> "Perform your prescribed duty, for doing so is better than not working. One cannot even maintain one's physical body without work."
- **Purport point:** The Lord "did not want Arjuna to become a pretender"; "one has to maintain one's body and
  soul together by some work."
- **Application (preacher's):** The "occupational duties" bucket is not the enemy of spiritual life. Studies and
  work belong in a good lifestyle; the Gita forbids escaping them.
- **Use:** in the talk, one line.

**BG 2.41**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/41/
> "Those who are on this path are resolute in purpose, and their aim is one. O beloved child of the Kurus, the intelligence of those who are irresolute is many-branched."
- **Teaching:** One aim gives resolve; many aims scatter intelligence.
- **Application (preacher's):** The lifestyle problem in one image: a many-branched day. The verse is about the
  resolve of the devotee; the application to a scattered timetable is ours.
- **Use:** in the talk, one line.

**BG 18.37**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/37/
> "That which in the beginning may be just like poison but at the end is just like nectar… is said to be happiness in the mode of goodness."
- **Use:** in the talk, with 18.38.

**BG 18.38**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/38/
> "That happiness which is derived from contact of the senses with their objects and which appears like nectar at first but poison at the end is said to be of the nature of passion."
- **Application (preacher's):** Every lifestyle change feels like poison for a week. The verse tells the listener
  which curve they are on, and why the first week proves nothing.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**NOI 2**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/2/
> "One's devotional service is spoiled when he becomes too entangled in the following six activities: (1) eating more than necessary or collecting more funds than required; (2) over-endeavoring for mundane things that are very difficult to obtain; (3) talking unnecessarily about mundane subject matters; (4) practicing the scriptural rules and regulations only for the sake of following them…; (5) associating with worldly-minded persons…; and (6) being greedy for mundane achievements."
- **Context:** Rupa Gosvami's *Upadesamrta*, text 2.
- **Teaching:** Six activities that spoil spiritual life: over-eating and over-collecting, over-endeavour, idle
  talk, rule-following for its own sake (or rejecting rules), worldly company, greed.
- **Purport point:** "Human life is meant for plain living and high thinking." Birds eat a few grains and leave;
  "a human being, however, will take away the whole bag."
- **Application (preacher's):** Read as a lifestyle audit, these are the six leaks of a day: overeating and
  over-shopping, chasing what is out of reach, gossip and feeds, mechanical routine, draining company, and the
  itch for more. The verse is written for devotees; the leaks are visible to anyone.
- **Use:** in the talk (second half).

**NOI 3**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/3/
> "There are six principles favorable to the execution of pure devotional service: (1) being enthusiastic, (2) endeavoring with confidence, (3) being patient, (4) acting according to regulative principles…, (5) abandoning the association of nondevotees, and (6) following in the footsteps of the previous ācāryas."
- **Purport point:** "Even in the material world one has to be very enthusiastic in his particular field of
  activity in order to become successful."
- **Use:** in the talk, one line (the positive six beside the six leaks).

**SB 1.2.10**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/10/
> "Life's desires should never be directed toward sense gratification. One should desire only a healthy life, or self-preservation, since a human being is meant for inquiry about the Absolute Truth. Nothing else should be the goal of one's works."
- **Teaching:** Health and self-preservation are desired *for* inquiry; that purpose defines "enough".
- **Purport point:** "One should satisfy the senses only insomuch as required for self-preservation, and not for
  sense gratification."
- **Application (preacher's):** Without a purpose, no amount of food, sleep or entertainment is "enough". This
  verse supplies the measure for the whole audit.
- **Use:** in the talk (the organising purpose).

**SB 1.1.10**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/1/10/
> "O learned one, in this iron Age of Kali men almost always have but short lives. They are quarrelsome, lazy, misguided, unlucky and, above all, always disturbed."
- **Purport point:** "In Kali-yuga, the duration of life is shortened not so much because of insufficient food but
  because of irregular habits. By keeping regular habits and eating simple food, any man can maintain his health."
- **Application (preacher's):** The purport names the problem of this age in lifestyle terms: *irregular habits*.
  The remedy is in the same sentence: regular habits and simple food. This is the talk's thesis from the scripture
  itself.
- **Use:** in the talk (the opening diagnosis).

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** The body is a vehicle with four demands: eating, sleeping, mating, defending (BG 6.17
   purport). Regulated, they free the person; extravagant, they block him. The Gita's method is regulation at both
   ends (BG 6.16), with food read by its effects (BG 17.8–10) and work accepted as necessary (BG 3.8). The measure
   of "enough" is purpose: a human life is for inquiry, and the body is maintained for that (SB 1.2.10).
2. **Why it matters.** The purport to SB 1.1.10 says the shortness and disturbance of life in this age come "not
   so much because of insufficient food but because of irregular habits". That is a lifestyle diagnosis, and it
   describes a hostel. The remedy is simple and in the same sentence.
3. **Connections.** To time (VL1-24), self-management (VL1-09), habits (VL1-26), and later to a devotional daily
   routine (VL3).
4. **Consequences.** A regulated day has fewer decisions in it; energy that went into negotiating with oneself
   about sleep and snacks goes into study and relationships. The six leaks of NOI 2, once named, become visible.
5. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - **"Regulation means austerity."** BG 6.17 lists recreation among the things to regulate, not abolish. BG 6.16
     forbids too little as firmly as too much.
   - **"Spiritual life means leaving studies or work."** BG 3.8: one cannot maintain the body without work; the
     purport calls renunciation for the sake of maintenance pretence.
   - **"Six hours of sleep is a rule for me tonight."** It is the purport's standard for a yogi. Start with a
     regular time (O); let the amount follow.
   - **"Food rules are about purity and sin."** At VL1, keep to the text's own frame: effect on life, strength,
     satisfaction and disease.
6. **Pitch for VL1.** Words: "regulate", "enough", "leak", "bucket". *Prasadam* may be named once, as food offered
   to God, when the chanting slot's refreshments are mentioned. No programme is prescribed; one change is invited.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet, give your name, and say today is a practical audit, not a lecture on discipline.
2. Ask H.2 question 1 with a show of hands.
3. Thank the room for honesty; say nobody will be asked to share their routine.
4. Ask for one volunteer word for how they feel by 4 p.m. (tired, wired, fine); write the words.
5. Give the transition line below and start with the four needs.

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the room's own life)
1. Did you go to bed at about the same time every night this week?
2. What is the first thing you do after waking, and who chose it?
3. Which part of your day would you give back if you could?

- **Opening question:** "Hands up if you slept at a different time every night this week." Pause. "Keep it up if
  you felt tired anyway." Most hands stay up. "The Bhagavatam has a one-line explanation. Then it has a one-line
  cure."
- **Relatable scenario (invented; adapt to your room: a shift worker, a farmer in harvest season, a new parent, an executive on constant travel):** Karan's day: wakes at 10 after a 3 a.m. series, skips breakfast, eats a
  packet at 1, class at 2, canteen at 8, gym at 10, phone till 3. He is 21, healthy and permanently exhausted. He
  calls this his lifestyle.
- **Transition:** "The word lifestyle usually means what you buy. Today it means what your hours add up to. The
  Gita manages that in four words."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** Exams, placements, fitness, friendships and mood all rest on sleep, food and
  the use of hours. Nobody taught them this; everybody assumed they knew.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **The age's disease is irregular habits.** "By keeping regular habits and eating simple food, any man can
   maintain his health" (SB 1.1.10 purport). *Example:* Karan. *Transition:* so what are the needs we are managing?
2. **Four needs.** A good animal (body), a good craftsman (skills and intellect), a good friend (relationships), a
   good saint (the self) (Preaching Encyclopedia, *Lifestyle Management*). *Example:* a day that feeds only one of
   the four. *Transition:* let's look at where the hours go.
3. **Four buckets.** Bodily needs, occupational duties, unnecessary activities (some can go now; some must be
   sublimated), constitutional duties (the self's needs) (Sundar Gopal deck). *Example:* the audit on the board.
   *Transition:* what does the Gita say about the first bucket?
4. **Regulate at both ends.** Not too much, not too little, in eating and sleeping (BG 6.16); regulated eating,
   sleeping, recreation and work mitigate material pains (BG 6.17). *Example:* the all-nighter and the twelve-hour
   Sunday are the same mistake. *Transition:* how to read food?
5. **Read food by its effect.** Strength and satisfaction; distress and disease; staleness (BG 17.8–10). *Example:*
   three canteen choices. *Transition:* and sleep?
6. **Claim the early hour.** Two kinds of wakefulness (BG 2.69); the hour nobody else wants. Work stays in the day
   (BG 3.8). *Example:* the only quiet hour in a hostel. *Transition:* where does the day leak?
7. **Six leaks, six supports.** Overeating and over-collecting, over-endeavour, idle talk, mechanical routine,
   draining company, the itch for more (NOI 2); enthusiasm, confidence, patience, regulation, good company, good
   models (NOI 3). *Example:* the feed as "talking unnecessarily about mundane subject matters". *Transition:* why
   does the first week hurt?
8. **Poison first, nectar later.** BG 18.37–38; one aim versus many branches (BG 2.41). *Example:* the first week of
   a fixed bedtime. *Transition:* what tells me what "enough" is?
9. **Purpose defines enough.** The body is maintained for inquiry (SB 1.2.10). *Transition:* to the takeaway.

## J. Stories and illustrations

- **The birds and the bag of rice** (NOI 2 purport). Birds eat a few grains and leave; a human carries off the
  whole bag. *Connection:* over-collecting as the first leak.
- **Rupa Gosvami and Haridasa Thakura** (BG 6.17 purport). The purport gives their sleep and chanting as the ideal
  of regulated devotees: Rupa Gosvami "could not sleep more than two hours a day", Haridasa Thakura would not eat
  or sleep before finishing his chanting. Present as the standard of saints, not as the room's target.
  *Connection:* regulation at its highest.
- **Contemporary (invented):** Karan's day (H); the three canteen plates (I.5).

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

> **Before you teach:** eating and sleep are health matters. Someone may have an eating disorder, insomnia, or a
> medical condition; a talk is not a prescription. Teach regulation as a skill; refer anything medical to a doctor;
> never comment on anyone's body or plate.

1. **"Six hours of sleep? I need eight."**
   - The six-hour line is Srila Prabhupada's standard for a yogi (BG 6.16 purport), given with the reason
     (overeating → dreaming → oversleeping). The verse's own emphasis is *both* extremes. Start with a regular
     bedtime and waking time (the research in O also finds regularity important for health), then move step by step
     toward the purport's measure.
2. **"Is the Gita telling me to become vegetarian?"**
   - BG 17.8–10 and the purport describe foods by their effects and recommend grains, milk products, fruits and
     vegetables as foods in goodness; the purport argues against slaughter. State the text's position honestly. At
     VL1 the invitation is to notice effects on your own plate, and to taste the prasadam after the programme.
3. **"Isn't regulation boring? Where's the fun?"**
   - BG 6.17 regulates recreation; it does not remove it. BG 18.37–38 says the regulated life starts like poison and
     ends like nectar; the unregulated life does the reverse. Ask which curve they would rather be on at 30.
4. **"I have exams. Regular sleep is impossible."**
   - BG 6.16 says too little sleep blocks progress as surely as too much. The study in O found that irregular sleep
     timing is itself a health risk. Fix the waking time first; it is the easiest anchor.
5. **"Should I give up studies to focus on spiritual life?"**
   - No. BG 3.8: one cannot maintain the body without work; the purport calls false renunciation pretence. The
     Sundar Gopal deck's three fields for a student are spiritual practice, missionary spirit *and* studies.
6. **"What are the 'constitutional duties' in the fourth bucket?"**
   - The needs of the self: inquiry about who I am (SB 1.2.10), and the practices that feed it. At VL1, the
     ten-minute meditation at the end of the programme is one example practitioners use; it is offered, not
     required.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The hostel night.** Tanvi studies best after midnight, she says, so she sleeps at 3 and wakes at 10, misses
breakfast and the first lecture, and eats one large meal at 2 p.m. She is irritable and her marks are falling.
- **Dilemma:** Is "I study best at night" a fact or a habit?
- **Principles:** BG 6.16 (both extremes); SB 1.1.10 purport (irregular habits); BG 2.69 (two wakefulnesses); the
  four buckets.
- **Questions:**
  - Which bucket is swallowing Tanvi's day?
  - What is the one anchor that would reorder the rest?
  - What does the first week of the change feel like, and what does BG 18.37 say about that?
- **Suggested analysis:** The anchor is a fixed waking time; the rest (breakfast, lecture, two meals) follows.
  The first week is poison; that is expected.
- **Takeaway:** Anchor the waking hour; the day reorganises itself.

**Case 2: The over-collector.** Rishabh earns well in his first job and his flat fills with gadgets, subscriptions
and deliveries. He works late to afford them and has no time to use them. He calls it lifestyle.
- **Principles:** NOI 2 (collecting more than required; over-endeavour; greed); SB 1.2.10 (enough is defined by
  purpose); BG 2.41 (many-branched intelligence).
- **Questions:**
  - Which of the six leaks does Rishabh show?
  - What would "enough" mean for him if his purpose were clear?
  - What one subscription or purchase could he drop this week?
- **Takeaway:** Without a purpose, "enough" has no meaning.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first; praise every try)
1. What four things does BG 6.17 say to regulate? *Eating, sleeping, recreation, work.*
2. What does the SB 1.1.10 purport give as the cause of short life in this age? *Irregular habits.*
3. Does BG 6.16 praise too little sleep? *No: it warns against both too much and too little.*
4. Name two of the six leaks in NOI 2. *E.g. over-collecting, idle talk, worldly company, greed.*
5. What orders the whole day? *Purpose: inquiry about who we are (SB 1.2.10).*

**M.2 Activity (about 10 minutes): "Colour the day"**
1. Give each person a 24-hour strip; 4 min: colour yesterday in four colours (bodily needs, duties, unnecessary, constitutional).
2. 3 min, pairs: each says which colour surprised them (optional to share).
3. 2 min: each writes one change small enough to keep (for example, the same bedtime).
4. 1 min: thank everyone; no one is judged for their colours.

**M.3 Reflection**
- **Reflection:** Which of the six leaks (NOI 2) takes most of your week?
- **Application (private, optional):** Colour yesterday's 24 hours into the four buckets. Which bucket surprised
  you? *(Nobody needs to share.)*

## P. Practical takeaway

- **Main lesson:** A lifestyle is what your hours add up to. Regulate four things; watch six leaks; let purpose

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

"Lifestyle" usually means what we buy. Here it means what our hours add up to. The Bhagavatam says that in this age life is shortened and disturbed "because of irregular habits", and that regular habits and simple food restore health (SB 1.1.10 purport). A simple audit helps: sketch yesterday and sort the hours into bodily needs, duties, unnecessary activities and constitutional (spiritual) duties.

The Gita asks for regulation in four places, eating, sleeping, recreation and work (BG 6.17), and warns against both extremes: eating or sleeping too much or too little (BG 6.16). It reads food by its effects on the person (BG 17.8–10) and claims the quiet early hour for growth (BG 2.69). Six things leak a day away: overeating and over-collecting, chasing the out-of-reach, idle talk, mechanical routine or none, draining company, and the itch for more (NOI 2). Six things help: enthusiasm, confident effort, patience, regulated practice, good company and good examples (NOI 3).

The first week of a new routine proves little: happiness in goodness is like poison at first and nectar later (BG 18.37), so give it time. The measure of "enough" is purpose: a human life is for inquiry about who we are (SB 1.2.10). Choose one small, keepable change and try it for a week.

## S2. Key ideas

- Irregular habits, not lack of food, shorten and disturb life in this age (SB 1.1.10 purport).
- Four buckets of time: bodily needs, duties, unnecessary activities, the self's needs. Audit one day.
- Regulate eating, sleeping, recreation and work (BG 6.17); not too much, not too little (BG 6.16).
- Food is read by its effect: strength and satisfaction, or distress and disease, or staleness (BG 17.8–10).
- Six leaks (NOI 2); six supports (NOI 3).
- Every good change tastes like poison for a week (BG 18.37). "Enough" is defined by purpose (SB 1.2.10).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **BG 6.17:** "He who is regulated in his habits of eating, sleeping, recreation and work can mitigate all material
  pains…" *For me:* four words to manage; start with one.
- **BG 6.16:** no yoga "if one eats too much or eats too little, sleeps too much or does not sleep enough." *For me:*
  the all-nighter and the twelve-hour Sunday are the same mistake.
- **SB 1.2.10:** "One should desire only a healthy life, or self-preservation, since a human being is meant for
  inquiry about the Absolute Truth." *For me:* the body is kept well *for* something.

## S4. Words to know

- **Regulation:** a fixed, moderate pattern; not austerity, not indulgence (BG 6.16–17).
- **The six leaks (NOI 2):** over-eating/over-collecting, over-endeavour, idle talk, mechanical rules, draining
  company, greed for more.
- **Prasadam:** food offered to God and then eaten; what the programme serves afterwards.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Six hours of sleep? I need eight.**
   The six-hour line is Srila Prabhupada's standard for a serious yogi (BG 6.16 purport). The verse itself warns
   against too little as much as too much. Start with a regular bedtime and waking time,
   then move step by step toward the measure.
2. **Is the Gita telling me to be vegetarian?**
   BG 17.8–10 and the purport recommend grains, milk products, fruits and vegetables as foods in goodness and argue
   against slaughter. That is the text's position. Notice the effects on your own plate; taste the prasadam.
3. **Isn't regulation boring?**
   The Gita regulates recreation; it doesn't remove it (BG 6.17). Regulated life tastes like poison first and nectar
   later; the opposite life reverses (BG 18.37–38).
4. **Should I drop studies for spiritual life?**
   No. "One cannot even maintain one's physical body without work" (BG 3.8). Studies belong in a good day.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. BG 6.17 lists four things to regulate. Which is NOT one of them: (a) eating, (b) recreation, (c) speaking?
2. True or false: BG 6.16 says only too much sleep is a problem.
3. According to the SB 1.1.10 purport, what shortens life in this age?
4. Name three of the six leaks in NOI 2.
5. In your own words: why does the first week of a new bedtime prove nothing (BG 18.37)?

**Answers:** 1. (c) (BG 6.17: eating, sleeping, recreation, work). 2. False: "sleeps too much or does not sleep
enough" (BG 6.16). 3. Irregular habits (SB 1.1.10 purport). 4. Any three of: eating or collecting more than
necessary, over-endeavour, idle talk, following rules mechanically (or rejecting them), worldly company, greed (NOI 2).
5. Happiness in goodness is "like poison" in the beginning and "nectar" at the end; the first week is the poison
stage (BG 18.37).

## S7. Try this week

- **Practice:** Same waking time for seven days, Sunday included. Attach one good thing to it: breakfast, ten
  minutes of reading, or a short walk.
- **Reflection (private):** Colour yesterday into four buckets. Which bucket is eating my day? What in it can I leave now?
