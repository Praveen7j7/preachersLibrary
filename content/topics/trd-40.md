## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on trd-24 (why Srimad-Bhagavatam is the final evidence and the natural commentary on the Vedanta-sutra) and trd-35 (the
refutation of Sankara's reading). Those pages give the conclusion and the defence. This page gives the *book*: what the Vedanta-sutra
is, how Lord Caitanya treated it in Benares and Puri, and why and how Baladeva Vidyabhusana wrote the Gaudiya commentary that the
other sampradayas asked for.

TRD residents represent the sampradaya to the educated. Every Vedic school is expected to have a commentary on the Vedanta-sutra;
Srila Prabhupada wrote that "a Vaiṣṇava should be completely conversant with Vedānta philosophy" and that "unless one knows Vedānta
philosophy he cannot be an ācārya" (CC Adi 7.72, 7.102 purports). A resident who can only say "we chant" when asked about Vedanta
has not understood his own acharyas; one who can name *Govinda-bhasya*, say what it establishes and open the Bhagavatam as its
source, represents Lord Caitanya as He represented Himself to Prakasananda.

## C. Central message

The Vedanta-sutra is Narayana's own words through Vyasadeva, free of the four human defects (CC Adi 7.106–107); its direct meaning
is "as clear as sunshine" (Madhya 6.130 purport) and its true commentary is Vyasadeva's own Srimad-Bhagavatam (Madhya 25.91–97). Yet
because the world asks every school for its bhasya, and because the sahajiyas despise study, the Gaudiyas have *Govinda-bhasya*,
which Baladeva says Govinda Himself spoke to him in a dream; it establishes that every sutra speaks of the Supreme Person, that the
soul is His atomic part and servant, and that one who reaches Him never returns. A Vaisnava studies it and then chants; study that
does not end in the holy name has missed Vedanta (Adi 7.72 purport).

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Describe the Vedanta-sutra from the CC Adi 7.106 purport: its author, its six names, four chapters and sixteen padas, the five
   parts of an adhikarana, its first sutra (*athato brahma-jijnasa*) and its place as nyaya-prasthana beside sruti and smrti.
2. State Lord Caitanya's position: direct meaning is self-evident (Madhya 6.130–134, 6.138); interpretation covers the sun with a
   cloud; Brahman means Krishna (6.139–147); the Bhagavatam is Vyasadeva's own commentary (Madhya 25.91–97, 25.153).
3. Say why the Gaudiyas have *Govinda-bhasya* and what the book says of its own origin (closing verses), while marking the Jaipur
   account "Requires source review".
4. Give three conclusions from the *Govinda-bhasya* text in research/books/: the soul is atomic and a "part" as a potency (2.3); the
   path is devotion with knowledge and renunciation (chapter 3 introduction); "No return, because of the scriptures" (4.4.22, with
   Chandogya 4.15 and BG 8.15–16).
5. Answer "why don't Vaisnavas study Vedanta?" from CC Adi 7.72 and 7.102, and keep the order: study, then chant.

***Govinda-bhasya* (Srila Baladeva Vidyabhusana) on the Vedanta-sutra, Desire Tree edition (OCR), translator not named** — read
against the file, 2 Nov 2026 (`research/books/Baladeva_Vidyabhusana_Sri_Vedanta-sutra.txt`). **The file's readable text starts in
Chapter 2, Pada 3**; Chapter 1 (including the catuh-sutri and *sastra-yonitvat*, *tat tu samanvayat*) is **Requires source review** in
this edition and is cited only as Prabhupada quotes those sutras.
- **Method, visible on every page:** each adhikarana gives the sruti text, *samsaya* (doubt), *purvapaksa* (the opponent speaks),
  *siddhanta* (conclusion) and the sutra's purport; e.g. 2.3 on the soul's size: "Is the individual spirit soul atomic or
  all-pervading?"; opponent: "The individual spirit soul is all-pervading"; conclusion: "The individual spirit soul is atomic and not
  all-pervading."
- **The soul as "part" (2.3):** "The individual spirit soul is thus a part and parcel of the Supreme. The truth is that the individual
  spirit soul is a potency of the Supreme Personality of Godhead", citing Visnu Purana 6.7.61. "the Supreme Personality of Godhead is
  the master of all potencies, and the individual spirit soul is a part of the Lord's spiritual potency." And on *tat tvam asi*: "The
  Chandogya Upanisad's statement 'tat tvam asi' (You are that) therefore means 'You are dependent on the Supreme.'"
- **Chapter 3, introduction:** "The glorious Supreme Personality of Godhead does not give residence in His abode to they who do not
  follow the path of devotion, knowledge, and renunciation. Therefore the wise should take shelter of that path." "In the previous two
  chapters was explained the truth that the entire Vedanta philosophy describes the Supreme Personality of Godhead, who is the only
  creator of the material world, completely faultless, a jewel mine of transcendental virtues".
- **The last sutra (4.4.22):** "anävrttih śabdad anävrttih śabdat"; "No return, because of the scriptures. No return, because of the
  scriptures." Baladeva: "A devotee who faithfully worships and serves the Supreme Lord and then goes to the Lord's spiritual world,
  never returns. How is that known? The sütra explains, 'sabdät' (because of the scriptures)", citing Chandogya 4.15.6 ("They who
  enter the spiritual world never return to the world of men") and BG 8.15–16. His closing summary: "Once He brings back to Himself His
  dear devotees, who are His parts and parcels, the Supreme Personality of Godhead will not again let them go." "The words of the
  sutra are repeated to indicate the conclusion of the book."
- **Closing verses (the book on its own origin):** "Let they whose hearts are greedy to taste the honey of the lotus flower that is
  glorious Lord Govinda's feet study this Govinda-bhäsya." And: "All glories to graceful and handsome Lord Govinda, who is the dear
  friend of Sri Radh, who kindly gave me the name Vidyabhüşana, and who spoke this commentary to me in a dream."
- **Use:** in the talk: the method, the three conclusions, and the closing verses; cite chapter and sutra, and the edition.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **What the Vedanta-sutra is.** Vyasadeva's 550-odd aphorisms summarising the Upanisads (Madhya 6.133), in four chapters and sixteen
   padas (Adi 7.106 purport), beginning *athato brahma-jijnasa* and *janmady asya yatah* (Adi 7.106; Madhya 6.143) and ending
   *anavrttih sabdat* (Govinda-bhasya 4.4.22). It is nyaya-prasthana, the logical presentation of what sruti says (Adi 7.106 purport;
   BG 13.5). Its author is Narayana as Vyasa (Adi 7.106), so its words have none of the four defects (7.107).
2. **Why there are commentaries, and why Sankara's is set aside.** A sutra is deliberately brief; schools explain it. Sankara's
   *Sariraka-bhasya* explains it indirectly, "covering" the sun with a cloud (Madhya 6.138), on the Lord's own order (trd-35). Lord
   Caitanya therefore would not "discuss" Vedanta on that basis (Adi 7.101–102 purport), but He discussed it at length on its own.
3. **Lord Caitanya's position on commentary.** The direct meaning is self-evident (Madhya 6.130–134); Brahman means Krishna (6.139–147;
   BG 15.15); and the author's own commentary exists: Vyasadeva, on Narada's order, wrote Srimad-Bhagavatam "as a commentary on the
   Brahma-sūtra" (Madhya 25.97), passing the meaning of omkara through the Gayatri and the catuh-sloki (25.94–97). So: "Study
   Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam very scrutinizingly. Then you will understand the actual meaning of the Brahma-sūtra" (25.153).
4. **Why, then, a Gaudiya bhasya?** Because the sampradaya must answer the world in the world's form; because Prabhupada says a
   Vaisnava "should be completely conversant with Vedānta philosophy" and that one who cannot study it cannot be an acharya (Adi 7.72,
   7.102 purports); and because the sahajiyas' contempt for such study is a road "to hell" (7.72). *Govinda-bhasya* is the
   Bhagavatam's conclusions set out sutra by sutra in the classical method, so that a scholar of any school can follow the argument.
   Baladeva says Govinda spoke it to him in a dream (closing verses); the Jaipur circumstances are "Requires source review".
5. **What it establishes.** (a) Every sutra speaks of the Supreme Person, "the only creator of the material world, completely
   faultless, a jewel mine of transcendental virtues" (chapter 3 introduction). (b) The soul is atomic, a part of the Lord's spiritual
   potency, dependent on Him; *tat tvam asi* means "You are dependent on the Supreme" (2.3). (c) The path is devotion with knowledge and
   renunciation (chapter 3 introduction). (d) The goal is the Lord's abode, from which there is "No return, because of the scriptures"
   (4.4.22; Chandogya 4.15; BG 8.15–16). These are the Bhagavatam's conclusions in sutra form.
6. **The method as a preacher's tool.** Each adhikarana states the doubt, lets the opponent speak fully, then concludes with sruti.
   Residents who learn this form argue honestly: the other side is stated before it is answered. *Vedanta-syamantaka* gives the
   compact version: eight pramanas, five topics.
7. **Study, then chant.** Prabhupada's order is exact: "if after studying Vedānta one does not adopt the chanting of the holy name of
   the Lord, he is no better than a Māyāvādī" (Adi 7.72); and the Lord's last words to Prakasananda join the two: "Always discuss
   Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam and constantly chant the holy name" (Madhya 25.154). The resident who studies *Govinda-bhasya* does so to chant
   with understanding and to preach with authority, never to become a scholar in place of a servant.
8. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *"Lord Caitanya rejected Vedanta."* He rejected the Sariraka-bhasya and explained the sutras Himself (Adi 7.102 purport).
   - *"The Bhagavatam makes a bhasya unnecessary."* The Bhagavatam is the commentary; *Govinda-bhasya* presents its conclusions in the
     form the schools recognise. Both come from the same source.
   - *"Govinda-bhasya is only for scholars."* Its conclusions are the resident's daily siddhanta; its method is how he should argue.
   - *"Studying Vedanta makes one dry."* Only if one stops before the holy name (Adi 7.72). The Lord's own formula is study and
     chanting together (Madhya 25.154).
9. **Pitch for TRD.** Give the book's facts from Prabhupada's purport, the Lord's position from the two debates, Baladeva's three
   conclusions from the text with chapter numbers, and the order "study, then chant". Mark the Jaipur story honestly.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**

## I. Core teaching points

1. **A Vaisnava studies Vedanta, then chants** (Adi 7.72, 7.102 purports; SB 1.2.12). *Transition:* so what is the book?
2. **Narayana's words through Vyasa, without defect; six names, four chapters, sixteen padas; first sutra, the human mission**
   (Adi 7.106–107). *Transition:* why commentaries?
3. **Direct meaning is self-evident; interpretation is a cloud over the sun; Brahman is Krishna** (Madhya 6.130–147; BG 15.15).
   *Transition:* who is the right commentator?
4. **Vyasadeva himself, in Srimad-Bhagavatam, by Narada's order, in the parampara from omkara** (Madhya 25.91–97). *Transition:* then
   why *Govinda-bhasya*?
5. **To answer the world in its own form, and because contempt for study is the sahajiya's road** (Adi 7.72); Govinda spoke it in a
   dream (closing verses); Jaipur: Requires source review. *Transition:* what does it say?
6. **The Lord is the subject of every sutra; the soul is His atomic, dependent part; the path is devotion; no return from His abode**
   (2.3; ch. 3 intro; 4.4.22). *Transition:* the compact map.
7. **Eight pramanas, five topics** (*Vedanta-syamantaka*, Rays 1–2); and the Lord's last word: study the Bhagavatam and chant (Madhya
   25.153–154).

## J. Stories and illustrations

**Seven days of silence** (CC Madhya 6.121–147). Sarvabhauma Bhattacarya, the greatest Vedantist of his time, teaches the young
sannyasi Vedanta for seven days; the Lord listens without a word. Asked why He says nothing, He answers: "I can understand the meaning
of each sūtra very clearly, but your explanations have simply agitated My mind" (6.130); the sutras' meaning is "as radiant as the sun"
and interpretation "covers that sunshine with a cloud" (6.138); "Brahman" means Krishna (6.147). *Devotional lesson:* the Lord
honoured the teacher by hearing to the end, then taught the sutras as they are; a resident does both.

**"Why do You avoid the Vedanta-sutra?"** (CC Adi 7.101–107). At Benares the sannyasis ask the Lord why He only chants; He asks their
permission (7.102) and explains that Vedanta is Narayana's own words (7.106), free of defect (7.107), before refuting their reading.
Years later He tells Prakasananda that the Bhagavatam is Vyasa's own commentary (Madhya 25.91–97) and sends him to study it and chant
(25.153–154). *Devotional lesson:* Prabhupada: "Caitanya Mahāprabhu exhibited His knowledge of Vedānta in His discourses with
Prakāśānanda Sarasvatī. Thus it is to be understood that a Vaiṣṇava should be completely conversant with Vedānta philosophy" (Adi 7.72
purport).

**Baladeva's own words on his book** (closing verses, *Govinda-bhasya*, Desire Tree edition). He ends the commentary with verses to
Govinda, "who kindly gave me the name Vidyabhüşana, and who spoke this commentary to me in a dream", and invites "they whose hearts
are greedy to taste the honey of the lotus flower that is glorious Lord Govinda's feet" to study it. *Devotional lesson:* the Gaudiya
Vedanta commentary presents itself as Govinda's mercy, written for devotees. *The Jaipur circumstances:* Requires source review; tell
them only as "the tradition says", if at all, until a source is checked.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"Lord Caitanya said chant, not study. Why study Vedanta?"** He explained the Vedanta-sutra Himself to two assemblies, and
   Prabhupada writes that "unless one knows Vedānta philosophy he cannot be an ācārya" and that one who refuses to study and then
   chant is a sahajiya (Adi 7.72, 7.102 purports). The order is study, then chant.
2. **"If the Bhagavatam is the commentary, why did Baladeva write another?"** Because the world asks each school for a bhasya in the
   sutra form, and the Bhagavatam's conclusions can be shown there sutra by sutra. Both say the same; *Govinda-bhasya* is for the
   scholar who will not yet open the Bhagavatam, and for the devotee who must answer him.
3. **"What is the Jaipur story, exactly?"** Our sources in research/ do not contain it; it is "Requires source review". What we can say
   is what the book says: Govinda gave Baladeva his name and spoke the commentary to him in a dream.
4. **"Why does our copy start in chapter 2?"** The Desire Tree file's first pages did not OCR. Chapter 1 and the catuh-sutri are read
   through Prabhupada's purports (Adi 7.106; Madhya 6.143) until a complete edition is checked. Say so plainly; honesty about sources
   is part of siddhanta.
5. **"Is *anavrttih sabdat* not contradicted by the fall of the jiva?"** Baladeva answers the opponent who says one may fall from the
   spiritual world as from Svargaloka: "No return, because of the scriptures", with Chandogya 4.15 and BG 8.15–16. The jiva's original
   fall is dps-43's question, treated there; this sutra is about the devotee who has reached the Lord.
6. **"Does Govinda-bhasya teach acintya-bhedabheda?"** It teaches the soul as a "part" that is "a potency of the Supreme", "identical
   with the whole in substance" yet dependent (2.3): the same conclusion trd-26 gives from CC Madhya 20 and Madhya 6.
7. **"Where do I start reading?"** With Prabhupada's purport to Adi 7.106 and the Madhya 25 passage; then the 2.3 and 4.4.22 passages
   of *Govinda-bhasya* in the file, with a senior devotee; *Vedanta-syamantaka* Rays 1–2 for the map.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The professor's question.** At a university programme a philosophy professor asks the visiting resident, "What is the
Gaudiya commentary on the Brahma-sutra, and what does it say the first sutra means?"
- **Dilemma:** the resident knows the Bhagavatam well but has never named *Govinda-bhasya*.
- **Principles:** Adi 7.106 purport (first sutra, structure); Madhya 25.91–97 (Bhagavatam as Vyasa's commentary); Adi 7.72 (the Gaudiya
  bhasya named); *Govinda-bhasya* ch. 3 intro and 4.4.22.
- **Questions:** What three sentences answer him correctly? What must the resident not claim (Jaipur details; chapter 1 quotations)?
  How does he turn the answer toward the Bhagavatam and the holy name (Madhya 25.153–154)?
- **Takeaway:** name the book, state the first sutra and the conclusion, and offer the Bhagavatam.

**Case 2: The anti-intellectual.** A senior resident tells juniors that "philosophy is for Mayavadis; devotees just chant", and
discourages a bhakta from reading the Sandarbhas.
- **Principles:** Adi 7.72 purport (sahajiyas "do not care even to touch the Vedānta-sūtra"; a Vaisnava should be conversant); Adi 7.102
  purport (acharyas were learned; bhakti "based upon the philosophy of the Upaniṣads and Vedānta-sūtra" is "factual and steady");
  Madhya 25.154 (study and chanting together).
- **Questions:** Which half of Prabhupada's instruction has he kept and which dropped? What would he say to a junior's question about
  Vedanta? How can the juniors be encouraged without disrespecting him (trd-50)?
- **Takeaway:** the order is study, then chant; dropping either half departs from Prabhupada.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Every Vedic school is expected to have a commentary on the Vedanta-sutra, Vyasadeva's aphorisms that summarise the Upanisads. Lord
Caitanya told the sannyasis of Benares that this book "consists of words spoken by the Supreme Personality of Godhead Nārāyaṇa in the
form of Vyāsadeva" and is free of "mistakes, illusions, cheating and sensory inefficiency" (CC Adi 7.106–107). Srila Prabhupada's
purport describes it: it begins "Now is the time to inquire about the Absolute Truth" (*athato brahma-jijnasa*), has four chapters of
four divisions each, is also called Brahma-sutra, Sariraka, Vyasa-sutra, Badarayana-sutra, Uttara-mimamsa and Vedanta-darsana, and
is the logical presentation (nyaya-prasthana) of what the Upanisads (sruti) and the Gita and Puranas (smrti) teach. Krishna says He is
the compiler of Vedanta and the knower of the Vedas (BG 15.15).

Lord Caitanya's position on commentary was simple. After hearing Sarvabhauma Bhattacarya teach Vedanta for seven days, He said: "I can
understand the meaning of each sūtra very clearly, but your explanations have simply agitated My mind" (CC Madhya 6.130). The
Brahma-sutra "is as radiant as the sun. One who tries to interpret its meaning simply covers that sunshine with a cloud" (6.138); the
word "Brahman" means Krishna (6.147). And the real commentary already exists: Vyasadeva, instructed by Narada, wrote Srimad-Bhagavatam
"as a commentary on the Brahma-sūtra" (Madhya 25.97), carrying the meaning of omkara through the Gayatri and the four seed verses of
the Bhagavatam in the disciplic succession. So His instruction to Prakasananda was: "Study Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam very scrutinizingly. Then
you will understand the actual meaning of the Brahma-sūtra" (25.153), and "Always discuss Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam and constantly chant the
holy name of Lord Kṛṣṇa" (25.154).

Why, then, do the Gaudiyas also have a commentary of their own? Prabhupada writes that "a Vaiṣṇava should be completely conversant
with Vedānta philosophy", that "unless one knows Vedānta philosophy he cannot be an ācārya", and that cheap devotees who refuse to
"touch the Vedānta-sūtra" and call the acharyas "mixed devotees" "clear their way to hell" (CC Adi 7.72, 7.102 purports). "In the
Gauḍīya-sampradāya there is a Vedānta commentary called the Govinda-bhāṣya" (Adi 7.72 purport), written by Baladeva Vidyabhusana,
who says at its end that Govinda "kindly gave me the name Vidyabhüşana, and who spoke this commentary to me in a dream". (The
tradition about its writing at Jaipur is not in our sources and needs review.) It establishes, sutra by sutra, that the whole Vedanta
describes the Supreme Person, "the only creator of the material world, completely faultless, a jewel mine of transcendental virtues"
(chapter 3 introduction); that the soul is atomic, "a potency of the Supreme Personality of Godhead", so that "tat tvam asi" means
"You are dependent on the Supreme" (chapter 2); that the path is "devotion, knowledge, and renunciation" (chapter 3); and, in its last
sutra, that one who reaches the Lord's abode never returns: "No return, because of the scriptures" (4.4.22). Baladeva's short
*Vedanta-syamantaka* gives the map in one page: eight kinds of evidence and five topics (the Lord, the soul, matter, time, karma).
Prabhupada's order is exact: study Vedanta, then chant; "if after studying Vedānta one does not adopt the chanting of the holy name of
the Lord, he is no better than a Māyāvādī" (Adi 7.72 purport).

## S2. Key ideas

- The Vedanta-sutra is Narayana's own summary of the Upanisads through Vyasa, free of the four defects (CC Adi 7.106–107).
- Its direct meaning is self-evident; interpretation covers the sun with a cloud; Brahman means Krishna (Madhya 6.130–147; BG 15.15).
- Its true commentary is Vyasadeva's own Srimad-Bhagavatam (Madhya 25.91–97, 25.153).
- The Gaudiyas also have *Govinda-bhasya* by Baladeva Vidyabhusana, which Prabhupada names (Adi 7.72); it establishes the Lord as the
  subject of every sutra, the soul as His atomic dependent part, devotion as the path, and no return from His abode (4.4.22).
- A Vaisnava studies Vedanta and then chants; study without the holy name is Mayavada (Adi 7.72 purport).

  Vyāsadeva." *For me:* I treat the sutras as the Lord's words.
- **CC Madhya 6.138:** "The Brahma-sūtra, compiled by Śrīla Vyāsadeva, is as radiant as the sun. One who tries to interpret its meaning
  simply covers that sunshine with a cloud." *For me:* I read as it is.
- **CC Madhya 25.153:** "Study Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam very scrutinizingly. Then you will understand the actual meaning of the Brahma-sūtra."
  *For me:* my Bhagavatam class is Vedanta study.
- **BG 15.15:** "I am the compiler of Vedānta, and I am the knower of the Vedas." *For me:* the end of knowledge is a person.

## S4. Words to know

- **Vedanta-sutra (Brahma-sutra):** Vyasadeva's aphorisms summarising the Upanisads; also called Sariraka, Vyasa-sutra,
  Badarayana-sutra, Uttara-mimamsa, Vedanta-darsana.
- **Sutra:** an aphorism giving "the essence of all knowledge in a minimum of words" (Adi 7.106 purport).
- **Prasthana-traya:** the three sources: sruti (Upanisads), smrti (Gita, Puranas), nyaya (Vedanta-sutra).
- **Bhasya:** a commentary; Sankara's is the Sariraka-bhasya; the Gaudiya one is *Govinda-bhasya*.
- **Adhikarana:** a topic-section of the sutras, treated through doubt, opponent's view, conclusion and proof.
- **Anavrttih sabdat:** "No return, because of the scriptures", the Vedanta-sutra's last words (4.4.22).

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Lord Caitanya said to chant, not to study. Why learn Vedanta?** He explained the Vedanta-sutra Himself to two assemblies, and
   Prabhupada writes that a Vaisnava "should be completely conversant with Vedānta philosophy" and then chant (Adi 7.72 purport).
2. **If the Bhagavatam is the commentary, why did Baladeva write another?** Because the world asks every school for a commentary in
   the sutra form, and the Bhagavatam's conclusions can be shown there sutra by sutra; both say the same.
3. **What is the Jaipur story?** It is not in our sources and needs review. What the book itself says is that Govinda gave Baladeva his
   name and spoke the commentary to him in a dream.
4. **What is the last word of the Vedanta-sutra, and what does it mean?** "No return, because of the scriptures" (4.4.22): one who
   reaches the Lord's abode by devotion never returns to this world; Baladeva proves it from the Chandogya Upanisad and BG 8.15–16.
5. **Does studying philosophy make a devotee dry?** Only if he stops before the holy name. The Lord's formula joins the two: discuss the
   Bhagavatam always and chant constantly (Madhya 25.154).
6. **Where should I begin?** With Prabhupada's purport to CC Adi 7.106 and CC Madhya 25.91–97; then one chapter of the Bhagavatam a day
   with purports, which is the commentary itself.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. Who is the author of the Vedanta-sutra, and what are its first words?
2. True or false: Lord Caitanya refused to discuss Vedanta at all.
3. What did the Lord say interpretation does to the Brahma-sutra?
4. Which book did Vyasadeva write as the commentary on his own sutras, and on whose order?
5. Name the Gaudiya commentary, its author, and its last sutra's meaning.
6. What is Prabhupada's order for a Vaisnava regarding Vedanta study and chanting?

**Answers:** 1. Vyasadeva, who is Narayana Himself; *athato brahma-jijnasa*, "Now is the time to inquire about the Absolute Truth"
(CC Adi 7.106 purport). 2. False: He refused Sankara's reading and explained the sutras Himself (Adi 7.102 purport). 3. It covers the
sunshine with a cloud (Madhya 6.138). 4. Srimad-Bhagavatam, on Narada's order (Madhya 25.91, 25.97). 5. *Govinda-bhasya*, Baladeva
Vidyabhusana; "No return, because of the scriptures": one who reaches the Lord never comes back (4.4.22). 6. Study Vedanta, then chant;
study without the holy name makes one no better than a Mayavadi (Adi 7.72 purport).
