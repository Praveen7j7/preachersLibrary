# From contemplation to fall down - How to avoid it? - Ajamila drama

> **BSS · 2 hours · review: drafted · sensitive (celibacy, falldown).** No sexual detail. The topic is how a fall begins in the mind, and the remedy: association, the holy name, service. Women are never the object of blame. The drama in M.2 uses the scripture's sequence only.

## A. Topic overview

| | |
|---|---|

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

For a brahmacari the question is not "will I be tempted" but "what do I do at the first thought". Ajamila's life is the Bhagavatam's
case study of a learned, disciplined man whose fall began with a single sight and a moment of contemplation, and who rose again through the holy name and association.

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| VL1 | The power of the holy name, in one story |
| VL2 | Why even learned persons fall (reasoning) |
| VL3 | Association and the holy name as protection |
| SPS | Fall and recovery in a settled practice |
| DPS | Fall among responsibilities; leaders' protection |
| BSS | This page: the ladder from contemplation to fall, and how to stop it |

**Not done here:** the detailed pastime of Visnu's and Yama's messengers (VL level), the power of the holy name in the last moment as a promise to be abused (K.2); falldown counselling in detail (later BSS pages bss-40).

## C. Central message

A fall does not begin at the action; it begins at contemplation (BG 2.62-63). Ajamila knew scripture and still fell, because knowledge alone did not hold his mind (SB 6.1.62). What stops the ladder is
association with devotees and the holy name, which are available at every step, including after a fall.

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants will be able to:
1. Name the steps of BG 2.62-63 and match them to Ajamila's story.
2. Say at which step each protection works (eyes, mind, association, chanting).
3. Explain why a fall is not final (SB 6.2.38-40, 6.2.49).
4. Perform or lead the drama and the debrief.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**BG 2.62-63** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/62/
> "While contemplating the objects of the senses, a person develops attachment for them, and from such attachment lust develops, and from lust anger arises."
> "From anger, complete delusion arises, and from delusion bewilderment of memory."
- Context: Krishna's description of the downward ladder in the section on the steady mind.
- Purport point: Srila Prabhupada explains that one who is not Krishna conscious is subject to material desires while contemplating the objects of the senses; the senses require real engagement, and only engagement in the Lord's service gives it. (*Read the full purport before teaching.*)
- Application (preacher's): The ladder gives a map: contemplation, attachment, lust, anger, delusion, memory lost, fall.
- Use: in the talk

**SB 6.1.56-57, 6.1.58-60** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/6/1/56-57/
> "In the beginning this brāhmaṇa named Ajāmila studied all the Vedic literatures. He was a reservoir of good character, good conduct and good qualities."
> "Once this brāhmaṇa Ajāmila, following the order of his father, went to the forest to collect fruit, flowers and two kinds of grass"
- Context: Ajamila's starting point: learned, controlled, truthful (6.1.56-57); on an errand for his father he sees a scene he should not (6.1.58-60).
- Teaching: He fell from a high start, in the middle of service, through the eyes.
- Use: in the talk

**SB 6.1.61-63** — Verified
> "When Ajāmila saw her, the dormant lusty desires in his heart awakened, and in illusion he fell under their control."
> "he tried to control his lusty desires, but because of the force of Cupid within his heart, he failed to control his mind."
> "In the same way that the sun and moon are eclipsed by a low planet, the brāhmaṇa lost all his good sense."
- Teaching: The desires were already "dormant" in his heart (6.1.61): the sight woke them. He struggled with remembered instructions (6.1.62) and lost (6.1.63). The text locates the cause in the desires in his own heart (6.1.61); the lesson is drawn from there, not from blaming the woman (*Application, preacher's*).
- Application (preacher's): Willpower and knowledge alone are not the remedy; the next verses show what is.
- Use: in the talk

**SB 6.1.55** — Verified
> "if in the human form of life he is taught how to associate with the Supreme Personality of Godhead or His devotee, this position can be overcome."
- Teaching: The remedy stated: association with the Lord and His devotee.
- Use: in the talk

**SB 6.2.38-40** — Verified
> "Simply because I chanted the holy name of the Lord in the association of devotees, my heart is now becoming purified."
> "Because of a moment’s association with devotees [the Viṣṇudūtas], Ajāmila detached himself from the material conception of life with determination."
> "In Hardwar, Ajāmila took shelter at a Viṣṇu temple, where he executed the process of bhakti-yoga."
- Teaching: The turning point is the holy name and devotee association; the response is full-time service at a holy place.
- Use: in the talk

**SB 6.2.49** — Verified
> "Therefore if one faithfully and inoffensively chants the holy name of the Lord, where is the doubt that he will return to Godhead?"
- Teaching: Chant faithfully and inoffensively. The verse is not a licence to sin and chant later (see K.2).
- Use: in the talk

**SB 5.8.26 and SB 9.6.51** — Verified (for the closing)
> "Due to the fruitive results of his past deeds, he fell down from mystic yoga, austerity and worship of the Supreme Personality of Godhead."
> "A person desiring liberation from material bondage must give up the association of persons interested in sex life"
- Context: Bharata Maharaja's attachment to the deer (SB 5.8.26) shows a fall through a small attachment; the Saubhari verse (SB 9.6.51) gives the standard of association.
- Use: in the talk (one minute each, as supporting stories)

## F. Philosophical explanation

**Principle.** The mind descends step by step (BG 2.62-63); the desires are latent and are awakened by contact. The remedy is the higher taste of the holy name and association (SB 6.1.55; 6.2.38).

**Why it matters.** A brahmacari sees the first step (a glance, a scroll) as small; the text shows its consequence.

**Connections.** bss-03 (guard of the vow), later BIKC pages (sense control, falldown bss-40).

**Consequences.** (1) Guard the eyes and ears (Encyclopedia note in O). (2) At the first thought, chant aloud, move, call a devotee. (3) After any slip, return at once; Ajamila's turn came in one conversation.

**Misunderstandings.**
1. "He fell because he was a bad person." The text begins with his qualities (6.1.56-57).
2. "Knowledge protects." It did not by itself (6.1.62); Chaitanya Charan has a question on exactly this (O).
3. "I can fall now and chant at death." SB 6.2.49 asks for faithful and inoffensive chanting; Ajamila's repentance and service (6.2.38-40) is the model.
4. "A fall ends everything." Ajamila's life after the fall is the second half of the story.

**Pitch.** Speak as a fellow practitioner, never as a judge; use only the scripture's sequence for the drama.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start.** Say: "Everyone here has been somewhere you did not mean to go: a website, a conversation, a shop. The question today is not whether the first thought comes. It is what we do in the first ten seconds." Then say the K.1 note: "If anything in this session touches you personally, you are welcome to talk with me or a senior in private. Falling is not the end of the story; the story we study has a second half."

**H.2 Opening questions.**
1. When you walk past a street full of advertisements, what do your eyes do?
2. Which do you think is harder to control: the eyes or the mind?
3. What is the first thing you do when a distracting thought arrives?

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Start high, still fall** (SB 6.1.56-57). No one is beyond risk.
2. **The ladder** (BG 2.62-63): contemplation, attachment, lust, anger, delusion, forgetfulness, fall.
3. **Dormant desire, awakened by contact** (6.1.61).
4. **Knowledge alone struggles** (6.1.62-63).
5. **Five protections, matched to steps:** (a) guard the eyes and ears; (b) chant aloud at the first thought; (c) devotee association and a guide you tell early (SB 6.1.55); (d) full engagement in service; (e) after a slip, return at once (SB 6.2.38-40). *Application (preacher's).*
6. **The second half:** repentance and service in Hardwar (6.2.39-40). Transition to J.

## J. Stories and illustrations

*Source: SB 6.1-2; story index (ajamila, key verse SB 6.2.49; bharata-deer SB 5.8; saubhari SB 9.6).*

**J.1 The learned brahmana** (6.1.56-57): good character and conduct.
**J.2 The errand and the sight** (6.1.58-60): a scene on the road.
**J.3 The awakening and the struggle** (6.1.61-62).
**J.4 The eclipse and the descent** (6.1.63 onward): gives up his duties and his family to live with her (story index; 6.1.64-66).
**J.5 The turning** (6.2.38-40): the Visnudutas' teaching and the holy name; he leaves for Hardwar and serves at a Visnu temple.
**J.6 Two short parallels.** Bharata Maharaja fell through attachment to a deer (SB 5.8.26) and Saubhari through association (SB 9.6.51; the story index summary: he returned to renunciation in the end).
**Devotional lesson.** The Lord's messengers protect the one who calls His name, and the Lord's devotees raise a fallen soul; the story is a story of the Lord's mercy.
**Illustration (preacher's).** Someone who falls on a train station stair does not stay on the floor; he stands and holds the rail. The rail is the holy name and a devotee's hand.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"I have fallen in the past. Is there hope?"** Yes: SB 6.2.38-40 and 6.2.49. Tell a senior or your guide, chant, and return to service. If you feel hopeless or unsafe, talk to someone you trust today and contact a crisis service (Tele-MANAS 14416 in India; elsewhere your local crisis line). Shame is not required; honesty is.
2. **"Can I just chant at the end like Ajamila?"** The verse speaks of faithful and inoffensive chanting (6.2.49), and Ajamila afterwards gave up everything for service. Deliberate sin on the strength of chanting is an offence.
3. **"Ajamila knew scripture. Why did he fall?"** Chaitanya Charan Das answers this (O); the text itself shows desire awakened by sight (6.1.61-62).
4. **"Is the woman at fault?"** No; the text speaks of the desires in Ajamila's heart.
5. **"What is the first thing to do?"** Look away, chant aloud, call or message a devotee.

## L. Case studies

**Invented case for discussion.** Nikhil, a brahmacari, is waiting for a train and an advertisement catches his eye; later in the day the picture returns during his japa, and he opens his phone to look for more. He stops, feels ashamed and avoids his guide for a week.
- Principles: BG 2.62-63; SB 6.1.55; 6.2.38.
- Questions: At which step did he stop? Where could he stop earlier? What should he do now?
- Analysis: He stopped at the third step; earlier stops were the eyes and the japa moment (chant louder; call a friend). Now: tell the guide, return to association and service.
- Takeaway: the ladder can be stopped at any step, and the best step is the first.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check (answers).**
1. What does BG 2.62 say begins the descent? *Contemplating objects of the senses.*
2. What were Ajamila's qualities at the start? *Learned, controlled, truthful (6.1.56-57).*
3. What woke his desire? *Seeing (6.1.61).*
4. What did he do after the Visnudutas' teaching? *Left for Hardwar and served at a Visnu temple (6.2.39-40).*
5. What does SB 6.1.55 give as the remedy? *Association with the Lord and His devotee.*
6. Is a fall final? *No.*

**M.2 Activities.**
- **Drama: Ajamila in four scenes (20 min + 10 min debrief).** Roles: Narrator (reads verse paraphrases), Ajamila, Father (offstage voice), "Mind" (a player who personifies each step of BG 2.62-63 holding a card), Visnuduta, a devotee. Scene 1: the learned youth. Scene 2: the errand and the sight (no embrace or touching; the sight is narrated). Scene 3: the Mind's cards (contemplation, attachment, lust, anger, delusion, forgetfulness) as Ajamila struggles (6.1.62). Scene 4: the Visnudutas' teaching, the holy name, Hardwar. *The "Mind" device and all dialogue are invented for the drama, labelled as such; the sequence is from SB 6.1-2.* Debrief: "At which card could he stop? What would you have done?"
- **Five protections (10 min, pairs).** Write one concrete protection for each of the five in I.5 for your own week; share one.

**M.3 Reflection.** "What is my first ten seconds when a distracting thought comes? What will it be from now?"

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Ajamila was a learned, well-behaved brahmana (SB 6.1.56-57). One day, on an errand for his father, he saw a scene on the road, and the lust that was dormant in his heart woke up (6.1.61). He tried to remember the instructions of scripture, but he failed to control his mind (6.1.62), and he lost his good sense (6.1.63). He gave up his duties and lived a sinful life for many years.

Krishna describes how this happens: contemplating the objects of the senses leads to attachment, lust, anger, delusion, loss of memory and fall (BG 2.62-63). At the end of life, the holy name was chanted and the Lord's messengers came; Ajamila heard their teaching, chanted in the association of devotees, and his heart began to be purified (6.2.38). He left for Hardwar and served at a Visnu temple (6.2.40).

We learn to stop the ladder early: guard the eyes and ears, chant aloud at the first thought, tell a devotee, and after any slip return at once.

## S2. Key ideas
- A fall begins with contemplation (BG 2.62).
- Knowledge alone struggles; association and the holy name help (SB 6.1.55).
- Ajamila rose again: a fall is not final (SB 6.2.38-40).
- Chant faithfully and inoffensively (SB 6.2.49).

## S3. Verses to remember
- **BG 2.62** — contemplation leads to attachment, lust and anger. For me: guard the first step.
- **SB 6.1.55** — association with the Lord and His devotee overcomes the position. For me: tell a devotee early.
- **SB 6.2.38** — chanting in the association of devotees purifies the heart. For me: return to the name.

## S4. Words to know
- *Contemplation*: thinking again and again about something.
- *Visnudutas*: the Lord Visnu's messengers.
- *Dormant*: sleeping, not yet active.
- *Association*: company that shapes the mind.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. **Was Ajamila a bad man from the start?** No; he was learned and controlled (6.1.56-57).
2. **How do I stop a distracting thought?** Look away, chant aloud, call a devotee.
3. **If I fall, is it the end?** No: Ajamila returned (6.2.38-40). Tell a senior; get help if you feel unsafe (Tele-MANAS 14416 in India).
4. **Can I sin and chant later?** No; chant faithfully and inoffensively (6.2.49).

## S6. Check your understanding
1. What does BG 2.62 say comes from contemplation?
2. What woke Ajamila's desire?
3. What does SB 6.1.55 say overcomes the position?
4. Where did Ajamila go after his change of heart?
5. Is a fall final?

**Answers:** 1. Attachment, then lust and anger. 2. Seeing a scene on the road (6.1.61). 3. Association with the Lord or His devotee. 4. Hardwar (6.2.39-40). 5. No.
