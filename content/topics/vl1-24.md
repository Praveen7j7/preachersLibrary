# Time Management

> **VL1 · 45–60 min · review: sources checked, awaiting senior review.** A relevance talk for students and young professionals. Scripture light but
> exact: five verses carry the talk, the rest prepare the preacher. IYF's three slide decks (VOICE seminar; a 2020
> lockdown talk, "Time Management: the Art of Smart Work"; a short deck) give the spine; their unsourced statistics
> and popular-book material are labelled or dropped (see J and O).

## A. Topic overview

| | |
|---|---|
| Batch / level | VL1 (Vedic Level 1), a stand-alone talk |
| Suggested duration | 45–60 min (G gives the 60-minute flow and a 30-minute cut) |
| Preparation time | About 90 min, the first time |
| Difficulty | Introductory |
| Prerequisites | None (VL1-23 helps) |
| Audience | Students who "never have time" yet scroll for hours; young professionals in permanent crisis mode; many with no settled faith |
| Teaching objective | Show that the problem is not time but self-management and priorities; give the Bhagavatam's sober arithmetic of a human life and the Gita's two tools (regulation and one aim); send everyone home with one scheduling experiment |

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

"Time management" is the most requested soft-skill talk in colleges and offices, which makes it a natural VL1 door.
The room expects quadrants and to-do lists. It gets them, briefly, and then something no corporate trainer offers: a
5,000-year-old audit of how a hundred-year life is actually spent (SB 7.6.6–8), and the claim that one moment of full
consciousness outweighs years of drift (SB 2.1.12). The aim is **attraction through a longer view**: the student should
leave with a better Monday and a question about the whole.

IYF's decks share a spine: time is priceless and irreversible; we cannot manage time, only ourselves and our
priorities; the urgent/important matrix; the "big rocks" jar; routine and morning ritual; procrastination and the
restless mind (BG 6.34–35); and the Bhagavatam's count of wasted years. This page keeps that spine and the verses,
keeps the matrix and the jar as popular illustrations, and drops the decks' unsourced figures (the "2% can multitask"
claim, the hormone slide, "33 years in bed", the rising-negativity statistic).

*Builds on vl1-23 (PARTHA), which uses BG 2.41 (one aim) and BG 6.35 (practice and detachment): here they serve different points, namely priorities (what you value decides what is important) and procrastination; the dam, kite and camera images of that talk are not repeated.*

How the subject grows at other levels:

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **VL1** (here) | The audit of a life (SB 7.6.6–8); a moment of full consciousness (SB 2.1.12); regulation (BG 6.17); one aim (BG 2.41); practice against procrastination (BG 6.35); one experiment |
| VL2 | Time as the Lord's energy (BG 11.32; 10.33); why the sun "decreases the duration of life" except for one use of time (SB 2.3.17) |
| VL3 | A daily schedule built round a practice; *avyartha-kālatvam* (not wasting a moment, BG 6.17 purport) |
| SPS | Keeping a schedule when enthusiasm drops; time given to hearing and service |
| DPS | Time for leaders: delegating, protecting the team's time, and one's own practice |
| BSS | The ashram day as a worked example of regulated time |

**Not done here:**
- Productivity hacks as the point. The matrix and the jar take six minutes together, as illustration.
- Asking anyone to wake at 4 a.m. or chant. Practitioners' routines are mentioned as what they do.
- Statistics without sources. If a figure is used, it carries author, year and venue (O).

## C. Central message

Time cannot be managed; it passes whatever we do. What can be managed is the self and its priorities. The Bhagavatam
does the arithmetic: of a hundred years, half go to sleep, twenty to childhood and youth, twenty to old age, and the
rest to distraction (SB 7.6.6–8). So "better a moment of full consciousness" than a long life "wasted" (SB 2.1.12).
Two tools make the difference: regulated habits (BG 6.17) and one aim (BG 2.41).

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. Reproduce the Bhagavatam's audit of a hundred-year life (SB 7.6.6–8) and apply the same arithmetic to their own
   week.
2. Explain why "time management" is really self-management, and name the Gita's two tools: regulation of eating,
   sleeping, recreation and work (BG 6.17) and one aim (BG 2.41).
3. State what the Gita says about the restless mind and procrastination, and the remedy, "suitable practice and
   detachment" (BG 6.35).
4. Design one week with the "big rocks" placed first, including one rock that is not academic or professional.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** five references are enough for the talk. The rest prepare you for questions.

**SB 7.6.6**, **SB 7.6.7**, **SB 7.6.8**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/6/6/ (and 7, 8)
> 7.6.6: "Every human being has a maximum duration of life of one hundred years, but for one who cannot control his senses, half of those years are completely lost because at night he sleeps twelve hours, being covered by ignorance. Therefore such a person has a lifetime of only fifty years."
> 7.6.7: "In the tender age of childhood, when everyone is bewildered, one passes ten years. Similarly, in boyhood, engaged in sporting and playing, one passes another ten years. In this way, twenty years are wasted. Similarly, in old age, when one is an invalid, unable to perform even material activities, one passes another twenty years wastefully."
- **Context:** Prahlada, a boy of five, speaks to his schoolmates (SB 7.6) and urges them to begin spiritual life now.
- **Teaching:** A hundred-year life, honestly counted, leaves very little.
- **Purport point (7.6.7):** "seventy out of one hundred years are wasted by a person who does not know the aim of life
  and how to utilize this human form." **(7.6.8):** "those years are also spent with no purpose" because of
  attachment; the purport notes that in this age a hundred years "is generally not possible", which sharpens the
  point.
- **Application (preacher's):** The arithmetic is the talk's centrepiece. Do it on the board. At VL1 the lesson drawn
  is "the remaining years are few and precious", not the purport's conclusions about rebirth, which belong to VL2.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 2.1.12**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/2/1/12/
> "What is the value of a prolonged life which is wasted, inexperienced by years in this world? Better a moment of full consciousness, because that gives one a start in searching after his supreme interest."
- **Context:** Sukadeva Gosvami to King Pariksit, who has seven days to live.
- **Purport point:** "there is no use in living hundreds of years without any knowledge of the problems of life — better
  to live for a moment with full consciousness". Those in "the eat-drink-and-be-merry type of life are simply
  wasting their lives by the unseen passing away of valuable years."
- **Application (preacher's):** The counter to "I'll think about the big questions later". Said to a man with a
  week left, it is also said to a room with decades, who feel they have none.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 6.17**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/17/
> "He who is regulated in his habits of eating, sleeping, recreation and work can mitigate all material pains by practicing the yoga system."
- **Purport point:** "any unnecessary time spent sleeping is considered a great loss. Avyartha-kālatvam: a Kṛṣṇa
  conscious person cannot bear to pass a minute of his life without being engaged in the service of the Lord."
- **Application (preacher's):** The Gita's time management in one word: regulation. Not heroics; fixed times for the
  four basics. IYF's "routine brings freedom" rests here. The purport's examples (Rupa Gosvami, Haridasa Thakura) are
  for the preacher's inspiration, not VL1 targets.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 2.41**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/41/
> "Those who are on this path are resolute in purpose, and their aim is one. O beloved child of the Kurus, the intelligence of those who are irresolute is many-branched."
- **Purport point:** Resolute intelligence is rooted in knowledge; "by watering the root of a tree one automatically
  distributes water to the leaves and branches".
- **Application (preacher's):** Priorities need a root. The matrix sorts tasks; this verse asks what they are *for*.
  IYF's "clarity is the power" and "what to value" slides rest here.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 6.35**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/35/
> "…it is undoubtedly very difficult to curb the restless mind, but it is possible by suitable practice and by detachment."
- **Context:** Arjuna has just said the mind is harder to control than the wind (6.34).
- **Purport point:** Krsna accepts the difficulty "but at the same time He suggests that by practice and detachment it
  is possible." The purport recommends hearing about Krsna as the most practical practice.
- **Application (preacher's):** Procrastination is the restless mind in the calendar. The Gita's answer is neither
  guilt nor an app: practice, repeated, plus detachment from what pulls.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**For questions (K):**
- **SB 2.3.17** (Verified): "Both by rising and by setting, the sun decreases the duration of life of everyone, except
  one who utilizes the time by discussing topics of the all-good Personality of Godhead." Purport: "Even a fraction
  of the duration of life wasted cannot be compensated by any amount of gold." For "what use of time is not a
  loss?" At VL1 give the verse as the Bhagavatam's claim.
- **BG 11.32** (Verified): "Time I am, the great destroyer of the worlds". IYF's decks open with it ("there is no such
  thing as time management because the Lord says I am time"). Purport: "Time is destruction, and all manifestations
  are to be vanquished by the desire of the Supreme Lord." Background; one sentence at VL1.
- **BG 10.33** (Verified): "I am also inexhaustible time". Purport: "time kills everything. Time is the representative
  of Kṛṣṇa". Background.
- **BG 6.16** (Verified): no yoga for one who "eats too much or eats too little, sleeps too much or does not sleep
  enough." Purport: "One should not sleep more than six hours daily." For "how much sleep?"; give the purport's
  figure as Prabhupada's guidance for yogis, not as a rule for the room.
- **SB 7.6.1** (Verified): one "should use the human form of body from the very beginning of life… to practice the
  activities of devotional service… Even a slight amount of sincere devotional service can give one complete
  perfection." The chapter's thesis; for "why start now?"
- **SB 7.6.5** (Verified): one "must endeavor to achieve the highest goal of life as long as the body is stout and
  strong". For "I'll do this when I'm older."

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** Time is not ours to manage; the Gita identifies it with the Lord's own destroying power
   (BG 11.32; 10.33). What we manage is ourselves. The Bhagavatam audits a life (SB 7.6.6–8): half to sleep, twenty
   years to childhood and youth, twenty to old age, the remainder to attachment and distraction. Hence "better a
   moment of full consciousness" than a long wasted life (SB 2.1.12), and hence the urgency of beginning now, while
   the body is strong (SB 7.6.1, 7.6.5). The Gita's tools are regulation of the four basics (BG 6.16–17), one aim
   that orders every priority (BG 2.41), and repeated practice against the restless mind (BG 6.35). The Bhagavatam
   names one use of time that is never a loss (SB 2.3.17).
2. **Why it matters.** The audience's complaint is universal ("no time") and false (hours of scrolling). The
   Bhagavatam's arithmetic is the honest mirror, and the two tools are usable on Monday.
3. **Connections.** PARTHA (VL1-23) is the character behind the calendar; smart work (VL1-25) is how the hours are
   used; the pleasure hunt (VL1-21) explains where time goes.
4. **Consequences.** A person with regulated basics and one aim has fewer crises (IYF's "I postpone because I am in
   crisis, and I am in crisis because I postpone"), more energy and a quieter mind. Tendency, not guarantee.
5. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - **"Spiritual time management means more hours of practice."** BG 6.17 regulates eating, sleeping, recreation and
     work; it does not abolish recreation. The purport's heroes are inspiration, not quotas.
   - **"The Bhagavatam is anti-childhood."** SB 7.6.7 describes how years *pass*; its speaker is a child urging
     children to begin. The point is early beginning, not grim childhoods.
   - **The matrix as the teaching.** Quadrants sort tasks; only an aim decides what is important (BG 2.41).
   - **Guilt about sleep.** BG 6.16 forbids too little as much as too much.
6. **Pitch for VL1.** Say "regulation", "one aim", "practice", "a moment of full consciousness". *Kāla* may be said
   once with BG 11.32. Present the Bhagavatam's arithmetic as an audit to check, not a doctrine to accept.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet the room, say your name, and say the talk is about choosing, not about guilt.
2. Write on the board: "I don't have time".
3. Ask H.2 question 1 by show of hands; thank the room.
4. Ask question 2 as a private guess; pause so people can think.
5. Give the transition line below and go to "time cannot be managed".

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the room's own life)
1. Did you say "I don't have time" this week? About what?
2. Privately: how many hours did you spend on your phone or screen last week?
3. If you got one free hour every day, what would you do with it?

- **Opening question:** "Raise your hand if you said 'I don't have time' this week." Nearly all. "Now, privately,
  guess your screen time for the same week." Pause. "Both are true. That is the subject."
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** Ritika has an assignment due Friday, a friend's birthday, a gym plan and a
  series to finish. She opens the assignment Monday, checks her phone, and surfaces at midnight having done
  everything except the assignment. Thursday night she does it in a panic, skips the birthday, and promises to "manage
  time better". Next week is the same.
- **Transition:** "A five-year-old in the Bhagavatam did the arithmetic of a whole human life and reached a startling
  conclusion. Let's do his sum."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** Deadlines, job-hunting, the first job's permanent urgency (and, for a farmer or a market trader, the season that will not wait), and the quiet sense
  that years are passing without the things that mattered.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Time is not managed; the self is.** BG 11.32 (one sentence). *Plain:* time passes regardless; we decide what we
   do in it. *Example:* IYF's loop: "I postpone because I'm in crisis; I'm in crisis because I postpone." *Transition:*
   so how much of it do we actually have?
2. **The audit of a life.** SB 7.6.6–8. *Plain:* 100 years; 50 to sleep; 20 to childhood and youth; 20 to old age;
   the rest to distraction. *Example:* do the same for one week on the board. *Transition:* if so little is left, what
   is it for?
3. **A moment of full consciousness.** SB 2.1.12. *Plain:* quality beats quantity; the "later" never comes.
   *Example:* said to a king with seven days left. *Transition:* the Gita's first tool.
4. **Regulation.** BG 6.17; BG 6.16. *Plain:* fixed times for eating, sleeping, recreation and work; routine brings
   freedom. *Example:* IYF's "routine means you don't have to think, just do". The matrix and the jar (O) illustrate
   priorities. *Transition:* but what decides which rock is big?
5. **One aim.** BG 2.41. *Plain:* priorities need a root; the resolute mind has one. *Example:* the student with
   four "passions" and no week that fits any. *Transition:* why we still don't do it.
6. **The restless mind and procrastination.** BG 6.35. *Plain:* the mind is hard to curb, and the remedy is practice
   plus detachment. *Example:* the "five more minutes" of scrolling. *Transition:* to the experiment.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"If I can't manage time, why call it time management?"**
   - IYF's decks say exactly this: it is a misnomer; the challenge is to manage ourselves. The Gita identifies time
     with the Lord's destroying power (BG 11.32), so the only thing in our hands is what we do with the hours.
2. **"Sleeping half your life is normal. Why call it waste?"**
   - SB 7.6.6 counts twelve hours a night; its purport says one "desiring to advance in spiritual consciousness must
     reduce these activities", and BG 6.16 forbids too little sleep as much as too much. The point is not to feel
     guilty about rest but to notice how little is left for what matters and to stop wasting *that*.
3. **"Isn't the matrix just corporate stuff? Why is it in a spiritual talk?"**
   - It is corporate stuff (O), and it is useful. What it cannot do is tell you what is important. BG 2.41 does: an aim
     with a root. Use the tool; get the aim elsewhere.
4. **"I'll get serious about the big questions after my career is settled."**
   - SB 2.1.12 was spoken to a man with a week left; SB 7.6.5 says to pursue the goal "as long as the body is stout and
     strong"; SB 7.6.1 says from the beginning of life. The arithmetic of SB 7.6.7–8 shows why "later" shrinks. Not a
     threat; a calculation to check.
5. **"How do people who practise actually schedule?"**
   - Many fix the four basics (BG 6.17), rise early, and begin the day with a fixed period of hearing or chanting
     before anything else; IYF's decks call this a morning ritual. Offered as what practitioners do; nobody is asked
     to adopt it tonight.
6. **"What about procrastination? I know all this and still don't do it."**
   - So did Arjuna: the mind is harder to control than the wind (BG 6.34). Krsna's reply is "suitable practice and
     detachment" (BG 6.35): a repeated small action and a loosening of the pull. Start with one fixed time this week.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The crisis loop.** Aditya is always behind. He does assignments the night before, replies to messages
instantly, and says yes to every request. He sleeps five hours on weekdays and twelve on Sundays. He is talented and
exhausted.
- **Dilemma:** He says he has no time. Does he?
- **Principles:** BG 6.16–17 (regulation of sleep and work); BG 2.41 (one aim); SB 7.6.6 (the cost of unregulated
  sleep).
- **Questions:**
  - What would his week's audit (SB 7.6.6–8 style) show?
  - Which of his habits are urgent-and-unimportant?
  - What one fixed time (BG 6.17) would break the loop?
- **Suggested analysis:** The problem is not hours but the absence of fixed points and an aim. One fixed sleep time
  and one protected morning block change the whole shape.
- **Takeaway:** Crisis is a schedule, not a fate.

**Case 2: The postponed question.** Sneha is organised and successful; her calendar is full and colour-coded. A
friend's sudden illness shakes her, and she realises nothing in her calendar addresses the questions it raised. "I'll
think about that after the promotion."
- **Principles:** SB 2.1.12 (a moment of full consciousness); SB 7.6.5 (while the body is strong); BG 2.41 (what the
  aim is).
- **Questions:**
  - Is Sneha's calendar well managed?
  - What is the "big rock" missing from her jar?
  - What would one weekly hour for the question look like?
- **Suggested analysis:** Her management is excellent and her aim is unexamined. SB 2.1.12 does not ask her to
  abandon the calendar; it asks for one protected moment of full consciousness, regularly.
- **Takeaway:** A full calendar with no root is a well-run drift.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first; praise every try)
1. Can time be managed? *No; only ourselves and our priorities.*
2. In the Bhagavatam's arithmetic (SB 7.6.6–8), how many of a hundred years go to sleep? *Fifty.*
3. What does SB 2.1.12 prefer to a long life? *A moment of full consciousness.*
4. What four things does BG 6.17 regulate? *Eating, sleeping, recreation and work.*
5. What does BG 2.41 say of the irresolute mind? *It is many-branched.*
6. What two remedies does BG 6.35 give for the restless mind? *Practice and detachment.*

**M.2 Activity (about 10 minutes): "Audit one week"**
1. 3 min: each person lists last week under five heads: sleep, work, screens, people, big questions (rough hours).
2. 3 min, in pairs: each names the surprise and one hour they could move (the numbers stay private).
3. 3 min: volunteers share one change. Thank each volunteer.
4. 1 min: link to SB 7.6.6–8 and the "big rock" in P.

**M.3 Reflection**
- **Reflection:** Audit last week honestly: sleep, work, screens, people, the big questions. What surprised you?
- **Application (private, optional):** Name your one "big rock" that is not a deadline. Where in the week will it go
  first? *(Nobody needs to share.)*

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Time cannot be managed; it passes whatever we do. The Bhagavad-gita (an ancient Sanskrit scripture, a conversation between Krsna and Arjuna) even identifies time with God's own power (BG 11.32). What we can manage is ourselves and our priorities.

The Srimad-Bhagavatam, another Sanskrit scripture, does the arithmetic of a hundred-year life: fifty years to sleep, twenty to childhood and youth, twenty to old age, and the rest mostly to distraction (SB 7.6.6–8). So it says: "Better a moment of full consciousness" than a long life "wasted" (SB 2.1.12). Do the same sum for your own week and see where the hours go.

The Gita gives two tools. First, regulation: fixed habits of eating, sleeping, recreation and work (BG 6.17). Second, one aim: the resolute mind has one purpose; the irresolute mind is "many-branched" (BG 2.41), so what you value decides what is important. Procrastination is the restless mind in the calendar, and the remedy is "suitable practice and detachment" (BG 6.35), not guilt. This week, put your one "big rock" in the calendar first.

## S2. Key ideas

- We do not manage time; we manage ourselves and our priorities.
- Honestly counted, a hundred-year life leaves very little (SB 7.6.6–8).
- A moment of full consciousness is worth more than years of drift (SB 2.1.12).
- Tool 1: regulate the four basics; routine brings freedom (BG 6.17).
- Tool 2: one aim decides what is important (BG 2.41).
- Against procrastination: practice and detachment, not guilt (BG 6.35).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **SB 2.1.12:** "What is the value of a prolonged life which is wasted…? Better a moment of full consciousness…"
  *For me:* "later" is not a plan.
- **BG 6.17:** "He who is regulated in his habits of eating, sleeping, recreation and work can mitigate all material
  pains…" *For me:* fixed times are freedom, not a cage.
- **BG 2.41:** "Those who are on this path are resolute in purpose, and their aim is one…" *For me:* my calendar needs
  a root, not just a sort.

## S4. Words to know

- **Regulation:** fixed, moderate habits of eating, sleeping, recreation and work (BG 6.17). Too little is as bad as
  too much (BG 6.16).
- **Big rock:** a popular image (from management writing, not scripture) for the thing that must go into the week
  first.
- **Full consciousness:** attention to what life is for; the opposite of drift (SB 2.1.12).

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **If time can't be managed, what is this talk about?**
   Managing myself. Time is not in my hands (BG 11.32); what I do in it is.
2. **Is sleep a waste?**
   No. The Gita forbids too little sleep as much as too much (BG 6.16). The Bhagavatam's count (SB 7.6.6) only shows
   how little is left for what matters, so that we stop wasting that.
3. **Isn't the urgent/important matrix just corporate stuff?**
   Yes, and useful. It sorts tasks; it cannot tell you what is important. That comes from an aim (BG 2.41).
4. **Can't I think about the big questions later?**
   SB 2.1.12 was spoken to a king with a week to live; the Bhagavatam says to pursue the goal "as long as the body is
   stout and strong" (SB 7.6.5). "Later" shrinks.
5. **I know all this and still procrastinate.**
   Arjuna too said the mind is harder to control than the wind. Krsna's reply: it is hard, "but it is possible by suitable practice and by detachment" (BG 6.35). One
   fixed time this week is practice.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. In SB 7.6.6, how many of a hundred years does an unregulated person lose to sleep: (a) 20, (b) 33, (c) 50?
2. According to SB 7.6.7, how many years go to childhood and youth, and how many to old age?
3. Which four habits does BG 6.17 say should be regulated?
4. True or false: BG 6.35 says the restless mind cannot be controlled.
5. In your own words: why does the talk say the matrix is not enough?

**Answers:** 1. (c) (SB 7.6.6). 2. Twenty and twenty (SB 7.6.7). 3. Eating, sleeping, recreation and work (BG 6.17).
4. False: it is "very difficult… but it is possible by suitable practice and by detachment" (BG 6.35). 5. The matrix
sorts tasks as urgent or important but cannot say what is important; that needs one aim (BG 2.41).
