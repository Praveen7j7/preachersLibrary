# DYS 6: If God Is One, Then Why So Many Religions? (and the DYS test)

> **DYS · 180 min + a 15-min review test · review: sources-checked · SENSITIVE.** Sixth and last session of "Discover Your Self";
> textbook chapter 6 of *Discover Your Self* (VOICE, Pune, © 2008–2011) plus Appendix 1 (evolution) and Appendix 2 (the
> origin of the universe); text in `research/iyf-materials/dys-book/`. **Sensitive because it compares religions.** Read K
> before teaching: several passages of the chapter characterise other faiths and their founders' audiences in ways that are
> disrespectful and historically careless; they must not be repeated. The head reviews this page after the build. IYF's own
> six DYS test papers are in `research/iyf-materials/dys-03/`; Part 2 S6 gives a review quiz in their spirit.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

After "there is a designer", "we can hear from authority", "human life is for inquiry", "I am not the body" and "I belong to
God", the newcomer's practical question is "which religion, then?" Chapter 6 answers that genuine religions share one aim, that
God speaks to the capacity of the hearer, and that the Gita and Bhagavatam present the fullest form of that one aim. The course
also attaches two appendices on evolution and cosmology. At DYS the aim is **not** to win a comparison but to free the
newcomer from the false choice "either one religion is right and all others worthless, or all are the same and none matters".

**Where IYF places DYS** (IYF's own documents, `research/batch-criteria.md` §1 and §8 point 1): the VL2 welcome letter is headed
"WELCOMES YOU IN DYS", the VL2 lesson plan opens with DYS-1…6, and the OTP announcement uses a "DYS video presentation" to recruit
for VL2. DYS is therefore IYF's six-session **opening journey**, placed at the start of VL2 and pitched a shade above VL1: the
listener has chosen to come, so one honest argument per session is welcome, but no practice is yet expected of them.

**Not done here:**
- Any characterisation of other religions' founders, scriptures or followers as "degraded", "perverted" or "of little faith".
  Chapter 6 does this (its remarks on the Qur'an's audience and on Jesus' disciples). It is forbidden on this page: the course
  profile requires respectful handling of other faiths, and these remarks are also unfair readings of the texts cited.
- Proving that Krsna is God. State the tradition's claim with its own sources (BG 10.12–13; SB 1.3.28; Bs 5.1) and say that
  it is the claim the course invites you to examine in VL1–VL2.
- Teaching anti-evolution or anti-big-bang arguments. The appendices make them; O explains why the preacher should not.

## C. Central message

The tradition's answer to "why so many religions?" is that God speaks to each hearer according to what they can receive, and
that every genuine religion aims at the same goal: love of God, unmotivated and uninterrupted (SB 1.2.6). The differences are
of depth and emphasis, not of the goal. Respect for all who sincerely approach God follows from this (SB 1.2.26 purport), and so
does the invitation to go as deep as the Gita and the Bhagavatam go.

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. State the chapter's principle, "the message is given according to the capacity of the hearer", with the mathematics-teacher
   analogy, and give the tradition's scriptural ground for it (BG 4.11).
2. Name three of the common essentials the chapter lists across religions (one God; obedience and service; the kingdom of God as
   shelter; giving up sin; chanting God's names; renouncing flickering pleasure for lasting happiness; the need for a guide).
3. Explain the "fear, desire, duty, love" ladder of motives and locate the Bhagavatam's definition of the highest religion (SB 1.2.6).
4. Say what the tradition claims about Krsna and *who* in the texts says it (Arjuna, BG 10.12–13; the Bhagavatam, SB 1.3.28;
   Brahma, Bs 5.1), without claiming it is proven.
5. Respond to "aren't all religions the same?" and "is yours the only true one?" respectfully.
6. Pass a short review test on the six sessions.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** SB 1.2.6, BG 4.11, BG 9.23, SB 1.2.26 and BG 10.12–13 carry the talk.

**SB 1.2.6**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/6/
> "The supreme occupation [dharma] for all humanity is that by which men can attain to loving devotional service unto the transcendent Lord. Such devotional service must be unmotivated and uninterrupted to completely satisfy the self."
- **Context:** Suta Gosvami's answer to the sages' first question: what is the essence of all scripture?
- **Teaching:** The highest dharma for *all* humanity is unmotivated, uninterrupted loving service to God.
- **Purport point:** "We have purposely denoted dharma as occupation because the root meaning of the word dharma is 'that which
  sustains one's existence'"; devotion mixed with desire for material gain is an obstruction.
- **Application (preacher's):** This is the chapter's test question ("the topmost religion is that which teaches love of God,
  *ahaituki* and *apratihata*"). It is a definition of the goal, not a ranking of communities.
- **Use:** in the talk; the central verse.

**BG 4.11**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/4/11/
> "As all surrender unto Me, I reward them accordingly. Everyone follows My path in all respects, O son of Pṛthā."
- **Teaching:** The Lord reciprocates with each person according to how they approach Him.
- **Purport point:** "Everyone is searching for Kṛṣṇa in the different aspects of His manifestations… anyone and everyone is
  satisfied according to one's desire to have Him."
- **Application (preacher's):** The scriptural ground for the chapter's principle that God meets people where they are.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 9.23**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/9/23/
> "Those who are devotees of other gods and who worship them with faith actually worship only Me, O son of Kuntī, but they do so in a wrong way."
- **Teaching:** Faithful worship directed elsewhere reaches the Lord indirectly, though not by the proper method.
- **Purport point:** The image of watering leaves instead of the root; the demigods as "officers" of the Lord's government.
- **Application (preacher's):** The verse is about demigod worship within the Vedic frame; applying it to other religions is an
  *extension* and should be said as one. Its tone matters: faith is honoured, the method is corrected.
- **Use:** in the talk, carefully.

**SB 1.2.23 and SB 1.2.26**: Verified.
- 1.2.23: the Lord accepts the qualitative forms of Brahma, Visnu and Siva for creation, maintenance and destruction;
  "all human beings can derive ultimate benefit from Viṣṇu, the form of the quality of goodness." The chapter's "worship according
  to the modes" rests here.
- 1.2.26: those serious about liberation "are certainly nonenvious, and they respect all", yet worship the forms of Visnu.
  **Purport point:** "Highly qualified brāhmaṇas situated in the mode of goodness have no grudges against the mode of worship of
  others. They have all respect…" **Use:** this sentence sets the tone of the whole session.

**BG 10.12-13**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/10/12-13/
> "Arjuna said: You are the Supreme Personality of Godhead… All the great sages such as Nārada, Asita, Devala and Vyāsa confirm this truth about You…"
- **Teaching:** Arjuna, having heard, accepts Krsna as the Supreme and cites the sages. (The chapter cites this as BG 10.11; the
  verses are 10.12–13.)
- **Purport point:** "It is not that because Kṛṣṇa is Arjuna's intimate friend Arjuna is flattering Him"; the statement is held to
  agree with the Vedic texts.
- **Use:** in the talk, as "what the Gita claims, and who says it".

**SB 1.3.28**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/3/28/
> "All of the above-mentioned incarnations are either plenary portions or portions of the plenary portions of the Lord, but Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa is the original Personality of Godhead."
- **Purport point:** The Lord is "full of all opulences, all prowess, all fame, all beauty, all knowledge and all renunciation"
  (the six opulences the chapter uses for *Bhagavan*); incarnations display what the occasion needs, like bulbs from one powerhouse.
- **Use:** in the talk, with Bs 5.1.

**Bs 5.1**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bs/5/1/
> "Kṛṣṇa who is known as Govinda is the Supreme Godhead. He has an eternal blissful spiritual body. He is the origin of all…"
- **Context:** Brahma's prayer; the chapter quotes it (*isvarah paramah krsnah*).
- **Use:** in the talk, briefly.

**BG 10.8, BG 7.2, BG 15.6, BG 18.66**: Verified. 10.8: Krsna the source of all worlds. 7.2: complete knowledge "both
phenomenal and numinous" (the chapter's "nothing further shall remain to be known"). 15.6: the abode "not illumined by the sun or
moon" (the chapter sets it beside Revelation 22:5; see K on comparing scriptures). 18.66: "Abandon all varieties of religion and
just surrender unto Me" (the chapter's "Islam means surrender" parallel). **Use:** background and for questions.

**BG 7.16, BG 2.40**: Verified. 7.16: four kinds of people begin devotional service: the distressed, the desirer of wealth, the
inquisitive, the seeker of knowledge; purport: "These are not pure devotees, because they have some aspiration to fulfill." **Use:** the
chapter's ladder from fear and desire towards love has a cousin here; present the ladder as the chapter's framework, with 7.16 and
SB 1.2.6 as its scriptural ends. 2.40: "a little advancement on this path can protect one from the most dangerous type of fear";
purport: "One percent done in Kṛṣṇa consciousness bears permanent results, so that the next beginning is from the point of two
percent." **Use:** the chapter's "20% in this life, continue from 21%" is this purport's idea; cite the purport, not a percentage.

**BG 3.13, BG 9.26**: Verified. 3.13: food offered first in sacrifice frees one from sin; others "eat only sin". 9.26: "a leaf, a
flower, a fruit or water" offered with love is accepted. **Use:** the chapter's closing "ABCD" (association, books, chanting, diet) for D.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle: the message fits the hearer.** The chapter's analogy: the same mathematics is taught as "you cannot subtract 5
   from 2" in primary school and as negative numbers later; the teacher adjusts to the student, not the truth to the teacher.
   The tradition grounds this in BG 4.11 (the Lord reciprocates as each approaches) and reads other faiths through it. Present
   the principle; **do not** present the chapter's illustrations of it (what the Qur'an's or Jesus' audiences were like). They
   are unfair, unnecessary, and will rightly offend. If you need an illustration, use the Gita's own: Krsna teaches Arjuna in
   stages, from the soul (ch. 2) to surrender (ch. 18).
2. **One goal, many depths.** The chapter lists what genuine religions share: one God worthy of love; obedience and service;
   God's kingdom as the final shelter; giving up sin; chanting His names; preferring lasting to flickering happiness; the need
   for a guide. Its "pocket dictionary vs full dictionary" image says the differences are of depth, not of direction. Say this
   carefully: the tradition *claims* that the Gita and Bhagavatam present the goal in its fullest form (SB 1.2.6; BG 7.2); a
   Christian or Muslim will make the parallel claim for their scripture, and that is their right. Our job is to state ours
   honestly and invite examination, not to grade theirs.
3. **The ladder of motives.** Fear (*bhaya*), desire (*asa*), duty (*kartavya-buddhi*), love (*raga*). The chapter's point: God
   can be approached at every rung, and each rung is better than not approaching; the highest religion is love without motive
   (SB 1.2.6). BG 7.16 shows the same movement from need to love. The chapter's story of the farmer who refused payment for
   hospitality illustrates unmotivated service.
4. **Worship according to the modes.** The Puranas recommend different worship for people in ignorance, passion and goodness
   (SB 1.2.23; 1.2.26 and purport). Teach the structure; drop the chapter's examples of other worshippers' practices
   (intoxicants, the "five M's"), which read as mockery. The purport's own tone is the model: "no grudges… all respect".
5. **Why Krsna?** The tradition's claim, with its sources: Arjuna's confession and the sages (BG 10.12–13); the Bhagavatam's
   statement that Krsna is the original Personality of Godhead (SB 1.3.28); Brahma's prayer (Bs 5.1); Krsna's own words
   (BG 10.8). State who says it. At DYS this is the course's invitation, not its conclusion; VL1–VL2 argue it.
6. **"Sanatana-dharma, not Hinduism."** The chapter's point that "Hindu" is a later geographical name and that the texts call
   their teaching *sanatana-dharma* is a fair historical note. Its "supermarket of religions" and "oldest religion" framing, and
   its dates for other religions, add nothing; omit them. The Gita's own account of its antiquity (BG 4.1–3) can be mentioned
   as the tradition's account (see VL1-08).
7. **The appendices.** Appendix 1 argues against the origin of life by chance and against Darwinian evolution; Appendix 2 argues
   that the big bang is "mathematically indescribable" and that science also rests on faith. Two honest observations for the
   preacher: (a) the *philosophical* points (chance is not a cause; every method rests on axioms; the singularity is where the
   model stops) are fair and are made by scientists themselves; (b) the *scientific* claims (no transitional fossils; "humans
   existed millions of years ago"; Darwin dated modern man to 40,000 years; the probability figures) are not accepted by
   mainstream science and several are simply wrong (O). The tradition's own teaching, the evolution of the *soul* through
   species (VL2-02), does not depend on refuting Darwin. Do not stake a newcomer's faith on arguments that a biology
   undergraduate can dismantle.
8. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - "All religions are the same." No: same goal, different depths and methods, says the chapter.
   - "Only ours is true; others are false." No: SB 1.2.26 purport; BG 4.11; the chapter itself says "we do not criticize such religions".
   - "The Gita condemns other gods." BG 9.23 honours the faith and corrects the method.
   - "A devotee must disprove evolution." No; see 7.
9. **Pitch for DYS.** Warm, respectful, honest about what is claim and what is established; one principle, one ladder, one claim,
   one invitation.

**Alternative: up to a 2-hour lecture, activities skipped:** extend 10-25 and 40-55 by ten minutes each (add Dhruva and the parrots from J), keep the 15-minute test, and close with M.1.
**If time is short, drop first:** the modes segment and the appendices sentence; keep principle, ladder, claim, close.

**Exit check, from the Preaching Encyclopedia (HG Radheshyam Das, VOICE, © 2008-2011)** (`research/encyclopedia/Discover Your Self/0020 If God is one Why are there so many religions.txt`): students can give an example of a teaching fitted to its hearer; name the four motives for approaching God (fear, desire, duty, love) and describe love without motive; distinguish worship in the three modes; and state the ABCD formula with an example each. The Encyclopedia also asks students to "establish with quotes from Bible, Quran and Gita the loftiness of Sanatana Dharma" and to compare the ages of religions: **do not** set those as objectives (see K's warning and O).

**Alternative structure (dialogue):** two volunteers, one saying "all religions are the same", the other "only mine is true". The group finds what is right and wrong in each; the preacher then introduces the principle and SB 1.2.6.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Welcome people by name; say this is the sixth and last session, with a short review test later.
2. Ask: "Hands up if you have a close friend of another faith or of none." Thank them.
3. Ask H.2 question 1; take three answers without comment.
4. Say: "Today's rule: nobody's faith is made small in this room, including yours."
5. Write on the board: "same goal? different roads? what do I owe my neighbour's faith?"

**Adapting for your audience:** if the group is of one faith, use a workplace, village or neighbourhood with several; if it is very mixed, invite each person to speak only for themselves.

**H.2 Opening questions**
- Who is a person of a different faith or none whom you respect? What do you admire in how they live?
- Have you ever prayed, or wanted to pray, in a hard moment? To whom, and why?
- Do you think God hears the prayers of people who pray differently from you?

**More on opening and engagement**


- **Opening question:** "Hands up if you have a close friend of another religion." Most hands. "Do you think God hears their
  prayers?" Most say yes. "Then why so many religions?"
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** In a hostel, a Hindu, a Muslim and a Christian student each fast on different days for
  different reasons and tease each other about it; then they discover each one's fast is about self-control and remembering God.
- **Transition:** "The Gita and the Bhagavatam give one principle that makes sense of the differences without pretending they
  are not there."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** young people in most cities and many villages live among several faiths (adapt the example to your place); a spirituality that despises their friends'
  faith will be rejected, and should be.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **The message fits the hearer.** BG 4.11; the mathematics teacher. *Transition:* so do the messages agree on anything?
2. **One goal, many depths.** The shared essentials; SB 1.2.6 defines the goal. *Transition:* why do people approach God at all?
3. **From fear to love.** Fear, desire, duty, love; BG 7.16; the farmer. *Transition:* what about the many forms of worship within India itself?
4. **Worship by the modes, respect for all.** SB 1.2.23; 1.2.26 purport; BG 9.23. *Transition:* so what does *this* tradition claim?
5. **The claim about Krsna, and who makes it.** BG 10.12–13; SB 1.3.28; Bs 5.1. *Transition:* the course ends; the inquiry begins (ABCD).

## J. Stories and illustrations

- **The farmer who refused payment** (book ch. 6, told of a devotee traveller). Lesson: service without motive. Say "a story told
  in the course book".
- **The two parrots, the butcher's and the king's** (book ch. 6; a traditional tale with a Sanskrit verse). Lesson: association shapes
  speech and character. The verse's source is not given: **Requires source review**; tell the tale without the verse.
- **Dhruva's austerities and his regret at having asked for a kingdom** (book ch. 6; Srimad-Bhagavatam Canto 4, chs. 8–9).

- **Krsna lifting Govardhana; the universal form (BG ch. 11)** (book ch. 6). Mention as the tradition's narratives, briefly.
- **Not to be used:** the chapter's characterisations of the Prophet's audience, of Jesus' disciples, and of Saiva and Sakta worship.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

> **Before you teach:** this session can wound or inflame. Assume someone in the room belongs to, or loves someone who belongs
> to, every faith you mention. Never quote or paraphrase another scripture to its disadvantage; if you quote one at all (the
> chapter sets BG 15.6 beside Revelation 22:5 and a Qur'anic passage on gardens), quote it to show agreement, and only if you
> have checked the wording in a standard translation. The chapter's comparisons of scriptures are **Requires source review** as
> wording and must not be used as claims about what those faiths teach.

1. **"Aren't all religions the same?"**
   - Same goal, says the tradition (SB 1.2.6: love of God), but not the same depth or method. Pretending there are no differences
     is as disrespectful as pretending there is no common ground.
2. **"Are you saying your religion is the best?"**
   - "I am saying what this tradition claims about itself: that it presents the goal in its fullest form (BG 7.2). Every tradition
     makes a claim like that for its scripture. I am inviting you to examine ours, not to grade theirs." SB 1.2.26 purport: all respect.
3. **"Does the Gita say worshipping other gods is wrong?"**
   - BG 9.23 says such worship, done with faith, reaches Krsna indirectly, but not by the proper method. The verse is about demigods
     in the Vedic frame; applying it to other religions is an extension. Its tone honours faith.
4. **"If God speaks to each according to capacity, is our capacity being insulted?"**
   - The principle cuts both ways: *we* too receive what we can digest. The Gita itself is taught in stages (ch. 2 → ch. 18). No one
     is called degraded; the chapter's own examples that do so are not part of this course.
5. **"Why Krsna and not Siva or Devi?"**
   - The Bhagavatam's framework (SB 1.2.23): Brahma, Visnu and Siva preside over the modes; all benefit from Visnu, the form of
     goodness. The tradition worships Krsna as the source (SB 1.3.28); it does not disrespect Siva or Devi, and nor should the preacher.
6. **"Does accepting this mean leaving my religion?"**
   - At DYS, no one is asked to leave anything. The invitation is to inquire and to try the practices (hearing, chanting). Many
     devotees were born in other faiths; Prabhupada's students came from every background.
7. **"What about evolution and the big bang in the appendices?"**
   - "The appendices raise questions. Some of their points are fair philosophical ones; some of their scientific claims are not
     accepted by scientists, and I will not ask you to accept them. The tradition's own teaching, about the soul, does not depend on
     them." Then point to VL2-02.
8. **"Why does the book speak harshly about other faiths?"** (if a participant has read it)
   - Acknowledge it plainly: "Those passages are not how we teach, and I am sorry if they hurt you. The tradition's own standard is
     'no grudges… all respect' (SB 1.2.26 purport)."
9. **"How do I know which rung I am really on? I think I serve out of love."**
   - A realisation to add, attributed: S.B. Keshava Swami (ISKCON), "Acid Test", *Tattva*, 13 Oct 2015,
     https://tattva.keshavaswami.com/p/acid-test-1294872: the test of our motive is what happens when praise and results
     disappear; "That's the acid test to measure the sum and substance of our spirituality." He points to Prabhupada's years
     with almost no support. Said simply at DYS: notice what you do when nobody notices. The ladder (fear, desire, duty, love)
     is for looking at oneself, never at the person beside you.
10. **"Hasn't religion caused most of the world's violence? Wouldn't we be better off without it?"**
   - Acknowledge the violence done in religion's name; do not minimise it. Then the fair point made in the *Spiritual
     Scientist* article attached to this session in the Encyclopedia ("Religion: is it for violence or peace?",
     `research/encyclopedia/Discover Your Self/0022 SS articles supplementing DYS.txt`): people have also fought over
     nationality, language, wealth and power, and officially atheist states were not free of violence, so religion is not the
     *only* cause. Stop there. The article's further claims ("studies in psychology have confirmed…") are unsourced: do not
     use them. SB 1.2.6's definition gives the positive answer: religion that produces love and no motive to harm.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The hostel debate.** Rohan tells his Christian roommate Thomas that "Hinduism is the oldest and most complete religion,
yours is a pocket dictionary." Thomas stops coming to the Gita sessions.
- **Principles:** SB 1.2.26 purport (all respect); BG 4.11 (God meets each where they are); the difference between stating a claim and
  ranking a friend.
- **Questions:** What was true in what Rohan said? What was wrong in how he said it? How could he have invited Thomas instead?
- **Takeaway:** A claim about our scripture is not a verdict on a friend's faith.

**Case 2: The biology student.** Ananya loves the sessions but reads Appendix 1 and finds its fossil claims contradict her
textbook. She asks whether she must choose between the course and her degree.
- **Principles:** F.7; the tradition's teaching is about the soul's journey (VL2-02), not a biology hypothesis; honesty about claims (O).
- **Questions:** Which parts of the appendix are philosophy and which are science? What does the Gita's teaching about the self
  actually require her to believe about fossils (nothing)?
- **Takeaway:** Do not make a newcomer's faith depend on a disputed scientific claim.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first)
1. What is the principle for the variety of religions? *Expected:* God speaks to each hearer according to what they can receive (BG 4.11).

**M.2 Activities (engagement block, about 75 min after the test; choose two)**
- **A. Two voices (35 min).** Two volunteers play "all religions are the same" and "only mine is true" for 5 minutes each; groups of four list what each gets right and wrong (15 min); the preacher draws the answer from SB 1.2.6 and BG 4.11 (10 min). Rule: speak for yourself, not for a faith.
- **B. Listening interviews (35 min).** Pairs: one tells what they love about their own practice or way of living, the other listens and repeats back, no correcting (8 min each); swap; whole-room round: "one thing I learned" (15 min). Praise the best listening.

**M.3 Reflection (private, optional).** Which rung (fear, desire, duty, love) do I usually approach God from? What would one step up look like? How can I show respect for a friend's faith while being honest about my own inquiry?

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

If God is one, why so many religions? The tradition answers with one principle: God speaks to each person according to what they can receive, and He reciprocates with each as they approach Him (BG 4.11), as a good teacher gives each class the lesson it can follow. Genuine religions share a goal, which the Bhagavatam (an ancient Sanskrit scripture) defines as loving service to God, "unmotivated and uninterrupted" (SB 1.2.6); they differ in depth and method.

People approach God out of fear, desire, duty or love (BG 7.16 names several kinds); every rung is better than none, and love, asking nothing, is the top. Faithful worship offered elsewhere is not worthless: it reaches God, though not by the best method (BG 9.23). Those who are serious about God respect all (SB 1.2.26), so nobody is asked to leave their faith.

This tradition claims that Krsna (God as the all-attractive Supreme Person) is that Supreme: Arjuna says it (BG 10.12-13), the Bhagavatam says it (SB 1.3.28), Brahma says it (Bs 5.1). You are invited to examine the claim, not to accept it on the spot; every tradition makes a claim for its own scripture, and you are not asked to grade theirs. The course book also has two appendices on evolution and the big bang; the tradition's teaching is about the soul and does not depend on disproving science, so open questions can stay open.

The way to continue is the ABCD of practice: good association, good books, chanting, and prasadam (food offered with love) (BG 9.26). The course closes with a short review test; sincerity is the qualification.

## S2. Key ideas

- The message fits the hearer; the truth does not change, the teaching does.
- Same goal, different depths: love of God, unmotivated and uninterrupted (SB 1.2.6).
- Four motives: fear, desire, duty, love. Every religion has people on every rung.
- Faithful worship directed elsewhere still reaches God, though not by the best method (BG 9.23).
- Respect for all who approach God is part of the teaching, not a concession (SB 1.2.26).
- The claim about Krsna is a claim with sources; the course invites you to test it.

## S3. Verses to remember

- **SB 1.2.6:** The supreme dharma for all humanity is loving service to God, "unmotivated and uninterrupted". *For me:* the goal is love, not reward.
- **BG 4.11:** "As all surrender unto Me, I reward them accordingly." *For me:* God meets me where I am.
- **BG 9.23:** Those who worship other gods with faith "actually worship only Me… but they do so in a wrong way." *For me:* honour faith; seek the best method.

## S4. Words to know

- **Dharma:** here, "that which sustains one's existence"; one's real occupation (SB 1.2.6 purport).
- **Sanatana-dharma:** the eternal occupation of the soul; the tradition's own name for its teaching.
- **Bhaya, asa, kartavya, raga:** fear, desire, duty, love: four motives for approaching God.
- **Bhagavan:** the possessor of all opulences in full.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Aren't all religions the same?** Same goal, says the tradition (SB 1.2.6), but not the same depth or method.
2. **Is yours the only true one?** This tradition claims to present the goal in full (BG 7.2); other traditions claim the same for theirs. You are invited to examine, not to grade.
3. **Does the Gita condemn other gods?** No. BG 9.23 honours the faith and corrects the method.
4. **Must I leave my religion?** No one at DYS is asked to leave anything. Inquire; try the practices.
5. **Why Krsna?** Because the texts say so (BG 10.12–13; SB 1.3.28; Bs 5.1); whether they are right is what the next courses examine.
6. **What about evolution and the big bang?** The tradition's teaching is about the soul; it does not depend on disproving science. Open questions can stay open.
