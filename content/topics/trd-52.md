## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on trd-20, which plans a sustainable life of service (sleep, food, study, inspiration), on trd-51, where
*apratipatti* is the dull inability whose sign is "a lack of enthusiasm", and on dps-34, which teaches NOI 3 generally.
This page takes the long form: enthusiasm lost not for a week but for years, in a devotee whose practice and service
continue. Only a senior can have this problem, and only scripture names its cause honestly.

**Not done here:** the measure of sleep, food and rest (trd-20); the five obstacles in general (trd-51); dryness as a

## C. Central message

Vyasadeva had compiled the Vedas "for the total welfare of all people", and "still his mind was not satisfied"
(SB 1.4.26). Narada's diagnosis: "You have not actually broadcast the sublime and spotless glories of the Personality of
Godhead" (SB 1.5.8). Enthusiasm is lost when, under all the work, Krishna's glorification has thinned out; it is
restored by hearing and glorifying Him directly. Srila Prabhupada: "maya is always trying to take away our enthusiasm to
serve Krsna, because without enthusiasm everything else is finished" (letter to Dhananjaya, 31 December 1972).

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Define *utsaha* as Prabhupada does in the NOI 3 purport ("endeavor executed with intelligence in Krsna
   consciousness") and name the six favourable items it heads.
2. Retell Vyasadeva's despondency and Narada's diagnosis (SB 1.4.26-32, 1.5.8-9) and state the lesson Prabhupada's
   purports draw.
3. Quote what Prabhupada said maintains enthusiasm (letter to Dhananjaya, 1972) and what he said about slow results
   (letter to Mukunda, 1973).
4. Distinguish three causes of lost enthusiasm that scripture and the purports name: Krishna's glorification thinned
   out (SB 1.5.9); over-endeavour and over-collection (NOI 2); and bodily or mental illness, for which a doctor is seen.
5. Use BG 2.40 and BG 9.22 against the two thoughts that feed burnout: "nothing I did lasts" and "no one carries me".

## E. Scriptural foundation

**NOI 3**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/3/
> "There are six principles favorable to the execution of pure devotional service: (1) being enthusiastic, (2) endeavoring with confidence, (3) being patient, (4) acting according to regulative principles…, (5) abandoning the association of nondevotees, and (6) following in the footsteps of the previous ācāryas. These six principles undoubtedly assure the complete success of pure devotional service."
- **Purport points:** "Without enthusiasm, one cannot be successful. Even in the material world one has to be very
  enthusiastic in his particular field of activity in order to become successful." "Endeavor executed with intelligence
  in Kṛṣṇa consciousness is called utsāha, or enthusiasm." "The execution of devotional service is not a matter of idle
  meditation but practical action in the foreground of spiritual life." dps-15 cites the same purport for patience
  (the movement's own slow beginning).
- **Application (preacher's):** utsaha is defined as an intelligent act, not a feeling. A resident who waits to feel
  enthusiastic before acting has reversed the definition.
- **Use:** in the talk, the central verse.

**NOI 2**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/2/
> "One's devotional service is spoiled when he becomes too entangled in the following six activities: (1) eating more than necessary or collecting more funds than required; (2) over-endeavoring for mundane things that are very difficult to obtain…"
- **Purport point:** "If our endeavor (prayāsa) is not to inquire about the Absolute Truth, we will simply increase our
  endeavor to satisfy our artificial needs. A spiritual aspirant should avoid mundane endeavor."
- **Use:** in the talk: *atyahara* (over-collecting) and *prayasa* (over-endeavour) are named by Rupa Gosvami as
  spoilers; a resident burnt out by projects should ask whether the projects were *prayasa*. trd-53 uses the same verse
  for *laulya* and *niyamagraha*.

**SB 1.4.26, 1.4.27**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/4/26/ (and /27/)
> 1.4.26: "O twice-born brāhmaṇas, still his mind was not satisfied, although he engaged himself in working for the total welfare of all people."
> 1.4.27: "Thus the sage, being dissatisfied at heart, at once began to reflect, because he knew the essence of religion, and he said within himself:"
- **Purport points:** "Śrī Vyāsadeva was not satisfied with himself, although he had prepared literatures of Vedic
  value for the all-around welfare of the general mass of people… ultimately he was not satisfied." "Perfection is never
  attained until one is satisfied at heart. This satisfaction of heart has to be searched out beyond matter."
- **Use:** in the talk (J): the greatest worker in history, dissatisfied after the greatest work.

**SB 1.4.31, 1.4.32**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/4/31/ (and /32/)
> 1.4.31: "This may be because I did not specifically point out the devotional service of the Lord, which is dear both to perfect beings and to the infallible Lord."
> 1.4.32: "As mentioned before, Nārada reached the cottage of Kṛṣṇa-dvaipāyana Vyāsa on the banks of the Sarasvatī just as Vyāsadeva was regretting his defects."
- **Purport points:** "Unless one is fixed in the normal condition of service, neither the Lord nor the living being
  can become fully satisfied." "The vacuum felt by Vyāsadeva was not due to his lack of knowledge… without the
  transcendental loving service of the Lord, everything is void; but in the transcendental service of the Lord,
  everything is tangible."
- **Use:** in the talk: Vyasadeva's own first diagnosis, and the arrival of the spiritual master at that moment.

**SB 1.5.8, 1.5.9**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/5/8/ (and /9/)
> 1.5.8: "Śrī Nārada said: You have not actually broadcast the sublime and spotless glories of the Personality of Godhead. That philosophy which does not satisfy the transcendental senses of the Lord is considered worthless."
> 1.5.9: "Although, great sage, you have very broadly described the four principles beginning with religious performances, you have not described the glories of the Supreme Personality, Vāsudeva."
- **Purport points:** "Dry philosophical speculations even on the transcendental subject of the Absolute have very
  little attraction without directly dealing with the glorification of the Lord." "The root cause of the despondency of
  Vyāsadeva was his deliberate avoidance of glorifying the Lord… he had more or less improperly used his valuable time,
  and thus he was despondent. From this it is clearly indicated that no one can be pleased substantially without being
  engaged in the devotional service of the Lord."
- **Use:** in the talk: the diagnosis in Narada's words and Prabhupada's.

**BG 6.20-23**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/20-23/
> "…Being situated in such a position, one is never shaken, even in the midst of greatest difficulty. This indeed is actual freedom from all miseries arising from material contact."
- **Use:** in the talk, for 6.23: the state the acharyas describe is not shaken by difficulty; a devotee shaken by years
  of difficulty has not yet reached it, and that is information, not condemnation. trd-21 used the same passage for the

**BG 2.3, 2.7**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/3/ (and /7/)

- **Use:** in the talk: Krishna's words to a hero who could not go on, and the hero's own words of surrender. The

**BG 2.40**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/40/

- **Purport point:** "Kṣema refers to the merciful protection of the Lord. The Lord helps the devotee to achieve Kṛṣṇa

- **Use:** in the talk, against "no one carries me".

- **Use:** in the talk: the resident is not a passive victim of his mind; he can act on it.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** Enthusiasm in devotional service is not a temperament; it is the first of six items that "assure
   the complete success of pure devotional service" (NOI 3), and Prabhupada defines it as an intelligent act. It is fed
   by the regulative programme ("the sources and the maintainers of our enthusiasm", letter to Dhananjaya) and by direct
   glorification of Krishna (SB 1.5.8-9). It is drained by maya "always trying to take away our enthusiasm", by
   over-endeavour and over-collection (NOI 2), and by a service that has thinned to management.
2. **Vyasadeva's case as the senior's case.** Vyasadeva had done everything: the Vedas divided, the Puranas, the
   Mahabharata, "for the total welfare of all people" (SB 1.4.26). He was not lazy, not fallen, not unregulated. He was
   dissatisfied. His own first thought was right (1.4.31): he had not specifically pointed out devotional service.
   Narada confirmed it (1.5.8-9): the four principles were described broadly, "but you have not described the glories of
   the Supreme Personality, Vasudeva." Prabhupada: "no one can be pleased substantially without being engaged in the
   devotional service of the Lord." The senior resident who has built, taught and managed for years and feels empty is
   in Vyasadeva's cottage. The remedy was not rest; it was the Bhagavatam.
3. **Three causes to separate honestly.**
   - *Krishna's glorification has thinned out.* The talks are about programmes, the meetings about funds, the japa
     mechanical (trd-51). SB 1.5.9 names this; the remedy is direct hearing and glorifying, and the regulative programme
     Prabhupada lists.
   - *Over-endeavour.* NOI 2: *prayasa* and *atyahara*. Projects beyond what service needs exhaust body and mind and
     are not bhakti's demand but one's own. trd-20 gives the measures (BG 6.16-17).
   - *The body or mind is ill.* Long low mood with disturbed sleep, appetite and concentration is also a medical
     matter. Prabhupada's letters repeatedly told sick disciples to see a doctor; this page keeps the plain rule: if you
     are always unwell, see a doctor. In India, Tele-MANAS 14416 is free and confidential. Seeing a doctor is not a loss
     of faith; it is BG 6.17's regulation of the body.
4. **Two thoughts that feed burnout, and their answers.** "Nothing I did lasted": BG 2.40, "one percent done in Krsna
   consciousness bears permanent results". "No one carries me": BG 9.22, "to them I carry what they lack, and I preserve
   what they have." Both are answered by Krishna Himself, not by encouragement.
5. **Arjuna as the pattern.** The Gita is spoken to a man who has lost the will to act (BG 2.7, "I have lost all
   composure"). Krishna's first word is not comfort but "do not yield to this degrading impotence… arise" (BG 2.3);
   Arjuna's answer is surrender and inquiry (2.7). The resident who has lost enthusiasm follows Arjuna: he says it to his
   spiritual master or a senior Vaisnava in Arjuna's words, "please instruct me", and hears.
6. **Why utsaha is an act.** "Endeavor executed with intelligence in Krsna consciousness is called utsaha." The order
   is: act intelligently (rise, chant, read, go out, preach, offer prasadam), and enthusiasm is maintained; not: feel
   enthusiastic, then act. The letter to Dhananjaya says exactly this.
7. **Connections.** trd-20 plans the sustainable life this page presupposes. trd-51's *apratipatti* is this obstacle's
   short form. trd-53's complacency is the other response to the same emptiness: instead of despair, comfort. trd-21:
   the happiness that is "never shaken" is the state to aim for, not the state to pretend.
8. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *A devotee of twenty years should not feel this.* Vyasadeva felt it after compiling the Vedas.
   - *I need a long rest from service.* Vyasadeva's remedy was more direct glorification, not less service; rest is
     regulated by BG 6.16-17, not by withdrawal. Where illness is present, medical rest is a different matter and is
     taken on advice.
   - *Enthusiasm is a feeling I either have or lack.* Prabhupada defines it as intelligent endeavour.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes).** Say: "Tonight's topic is one that senior devotees rarely admit and juniors
rarely believe: enthusiasm lost after many years, while the service continues. Before anyone feels judged, hear whom
scripture gives as the example: Srila Vyasadeva, after compiling the Vedas, 'still his mind was not satisfied'
(SB 1.4.26). If it happened to him, it can happen here, and the Bhagavatam tells us what Narada said to him. Srila
Prabhupada defined enthusiasm not as a feeling but as 'endeavor executed with intelligence in Krsna consciousness'
(NOI 3 purport). So tonight we look for the intelligent endeavour that restores it." Then: "One rule for the evening:
nothing anyone writes is shared unless he chooses."

**H.2 Opening questions:**
1. "What service did you once wait for all year, and how do you feel about it now?"
2. "In the last month, how many minutes a day did you spend hearing or speaking directly about Krishna's glories, as
   against planning, managing and collecting?"
3. "When Arjuna said 'I have lost all composure… please instruct me' (BG 2.7), to whom could you say the same sentence?"

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Enthusiasm is an intelligent act, and the first of six supports.** NOI 3 and purport. Plain explanation: act,
   and it is maintained. Example (preacher's application): the resident who goes on sankirtana on the morning he least
   wants to and returns lighter. Transition: the greatest example of its loss.
2. **Vyasadeva was dissatisfied after the greatest work ever done.** SB 1.4.26-27, 1.4.31-32. Example: the temple
   builder after the opening. Transition: the diagnosis.
3. **Narada's diagnosis: Krishna's glories were not directly broadcast.** SB 1.5.8-9 and purports. Example: a year of
   talks on management and none on Krishna's pastimes. Transition: Prabhupada's own prescription.
4. **The regulative programme is "the sources and the maintainers of our enthusiasm".** Letter to Dhananjaya. Example:
   check the week against the letter's list. Transition: slow results.
5. **"Please do not be discouraged."** Letter to Mukunda; BG 2.40. Example: a programme that took five years to
   bear fruit. Transition: the body.
6. **Three causes, three responses.** SB 1.5.9; NOI 2; the medical rule (Tele-MANAS 14416). Transition: Arjuna.
7. **Arjuna's pattern: say it, surrender, hear.** BG 2.3, 2.7; BG 9.22; BG 6.5. Transition to M.

## J. Stories and illustrations

- **Vyasadeva and Narada (SB 1.4.26 to 1.5.9).** Tell it from the verses in E, in their own words, in sequence;
  Vyasadeva rises and worships Narada as he would Brahma (1.4.33). Devotional lesson (the purports' own): "no one can
  be pleased substantially without being engaged in the devotional service of the Lord"; the remedy for the
  dissatisfaction of a lifetime's work was to glorify Krishna directly, which became Srimad-Bhagavatam. Read SB 1.4.24
  to 1.5.40 before teaching; tell nothing the verses do not.
- **Arjuna at Kuruksetra (BG 1-2).** The warrior who had trained all his life cannot act; Krishna tells him to give up
  "petty weakness of heart and arise" (2.3); Arjuna surrenders and asks for instruction (2.7). Devotional lesson:
  the Gita's whole teaching is Krishna's answer to a man who had lost his will; the path out is inquiry from the
  spiritual master, not self-management.
- **Contemporary illustration (label it as the preacher's own):** the letter to Dhananjaya was written to a temple
  president of the London temple; Prabhupada's answer to a report of enthusiasm was a warning to maintain it by the
  programme. Say only what the letter says.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. *"I follow the whole programme Prabhupada lists and I am still empty."* Then look at Vyasadeva: his programme was
   complete too. Ask Narada's question: how much of your day is direct glorification of Krishna (hearing His pastimes,
   speaking them), as against the four principles broadly? And ask the medical question honestly (K.2).
2. *"How do I know whether this is spiritual or medical?"* Scripture gives the spiritual signs: glorification thinned
   out (SB 1.5.9), over-endeavour (NOI 2). Medicine gives the bodily ones: weeks of poor sleep, appetite and
   concentration, loss of interest in everything, not only in service. If the second set is present, see a doctor and
   also do the first; they do not compete. Tele-MANAS 14416 (India) is free and confidential. Never tell a resident
   that seeing a doctor shows weak faith.
3. *"Isn't admitting lost enthusiasm discouraging to juniors?"* Arjuna admitted it to Krishna, Vyasadeva to Narada,
   each to his spiritual master, not to the troops. Say it upward (dps-02), act on the remedy, and the juniors see
   recovery, not collapse.
4. *"Should I leave my post for a simpler service?"* Possibly, if the post is *prayasa* beyond what service needs
   (NOI 2); ask your authority. But Vyasadeva did not leave his work; he added what was missing.
5. *"Prabhupada says results will come; mine have not in ten years."* BG 2.40: no loss, permanent results, even when
   unfinished. The letter to Mukunda: "please do not be discouraged." Results in bhakti are measured by Krishna's
   pleasure (SB 1.5.8), not by numbers.

## L. Case studies

**Case 1 (Invented case for discussion).** A resident of eighteen years heads outreach, trains preachers and chairs
three committees. He chants, attends the programme, and has not read a Bhagavatam chapter for pleasure in two years.
He says he feels nothing and is ashamed of it. *Principles:* SB 1.4.26 (dissatisfied though working for all); SB 1.5.9
(glorification not described); NOI 3 (utsaha as act). *Questions:* What is his Narada question? What would Vyasadeva's
remedy look like for him? *Analysis:* the programme is kept, but direct glorification has thinned to nothing.
*Takeaway:* one hour of Bhagavatam daily, and one class a week on a pastime, not a project; the shame is answered by
Vyasadeva's example.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions (expected answers in brackets):**
1. How does Prabhupada define utsaha in the NOI 3 purport? [Endeavour executed with intelligence in Krishna
   consciousness.]
2. What did Narada say was the cause of Vyasadeva's dissatisfaction (SB 1.5.8-9)? [He had not broadcast the Lord's
   glories; he had described the four principles broadly but not Vasudeva's glories.]
3. What did Prabhupada call "the sources and the maintainers of our enthusiasm" (letter to Dhananjaya)? [The
   regulative principles: rising before 4 a.m., sixteen rounds, reading, sankirtana, preaching, offering prasadam.]
4. What does BG 2.40 say about work begun in Krishna consciousness? [There is no loss; a little advancement protects
   from the greatest fear; the purport: one percent bears permanent results.]
5. What did Krishna promise in BG 9.22? [To carry what His devotees lack and preserve what they have.]
6. Name the three causes this session separated. [Glorification thinned out; over-endeavour; illness of body or mind,
   for which a doctor is seen.]

**M.2 Activity (25 min): the Narada question and the Dhananjaya list.** Each resident writes privately: (a) his week
against the letter's list (rising, rounds, reading, sankirtana, preaching, prasadam), marking what has slipped; (b)
the minutes per day of direct glorification of Krishna (hearing or speaking His pastimes); (c) honestly, whether any
medical signs are present; (d) one intelligent endeavour for this week. Pairs discuss only (d), never (a)-(c).

**M.3 Reflection.** "If Narada came to my cottage tonight, what would he say I have broadly described, and what have I
left out?"

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

After compiling the Vedas and the Puranas "for the total welfare of all people", Srila Vyasadeva "still… was not
satisfied" (SB 1.4.26). He reflected and thought it might be because he "did not specifically point out the devotional
service of the Lord" (1.4.31). At that moment his spiritual master Narada arrived and said plainly, "You have not
actually broadcast the sublime and spotless glories of the Personality of Godhead… you have not described the glories of
the Supreme Personality, Vasudeva" (SB 1.5.8-9). Srila Prabhupada writes that "no one can be pleased substantially
without being engaged in the devotional service of the Lord." Vyasadeva's remedy was not rest but Srimad-Bhagavatam:
direct glorification of Krishna. A senior devotee who has worked for years and feels empty is in the same cottage,
and the same question applies: how much of my day is Krishna's glories, as against programmes and management?

Rupa Gosvami lists enthusiasm, *utsaha*, first among six things that "assure the complete success of pure devotional
service" (NOI 3). Prabhupada defines it: "Endeavor executed with intelligence in Krsna consciousness is called utsaha."
It is an act, not a mood. In a letter of 31 December 1972 he wrote: "maya is always trying to take away our enthusiasm
to serve Krsna, because without enthusiasm everything else is finished", and he named what maintains it: rising before
4 a.m., sixteen rounds, reading, sankirtana, preaching, offering prasadam. "As soon as someone is not following them
regularly, it may be certain that his enthusiasm will gradually disappear." To a disciple whose results were slow he
wrote, "please do not be discouraged" (21 February 1973). Rupa Gosvami also warns that over-endeavour and
over-collecting spoil devotion (NOI 2); some exhaustion is from projects service never asked for.

Krishna answers the two thoughts that feed despair. "Nothing I did lasted": "In this endeavor there is no loss or
diminution" (BG 2.40). "No one carries me": "to them I carry what they lack, and I preserve what they have" (BG 9.22).
Arjuna, who had lost all composure, said "please instruct me" (BG 2.7), and the Gita is Krishna's answer; a devotee who
has lost enthusiasm says the same to his spiritual master or a senior devotee and hears. One more honest step: if low
mood has lasted weeks and affects sleep, appetite and interest in everything, see a doctor as well. In India Tele-MANAS
14416 is free and confidential. Regulating the body (BG 6.16-17) and hearing about Krishna go together; neither
replaces the other.

## S2. Key ideas
- Vyasadeva was dissatisfied after the greatest work; the cause was that Krishna's glories were not directly described
  (SB 1.5.9); the remedy was the Bhagavatam.
- Utsaha is "endeavor executed with intelligence in Krsna consciousness" (NOI 3 purport): an act, not a feeling.
- The regulative programme is "the sources and the maintainers of our enthusiasm" (Prabhupada, 1972).
- Over-endeavour spoils devotion (NOI 2); not all exhaustion is service.
- There is no loss in this endeavour (BG 2.40); Krishna carries what we lack (BG 9.22); a sick body or mind is also
  taken to a doctor.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **SB 1.5.9**: "you have not described the glories of the Supreme Personality, Vasudeva." For me: when I am empty, I
  ask how much of my day was Krishna's glories.
- **NOI 3**: enthusiasm, confidence, patience, regulative principles, giving up bad association, following the acharyas.
  For me: enthusiasm is the first act, not the last feeling.
- **BG 9.22**: "to them I carry what they lack, and I preserve what they have." For me: I am carried.

## S4. Words to know
- **Utsaha:** enthusiasm; "endeavor executed with intelligence in Krsna consciousness".
- **Prayasa, atyahara:** over-endeavour; over-eating or over-collecting (NOI 2).
- **Apratipatti:** the obstacle of dull inability, whose sign is lost enthusiasm (trd-51).
- **Tele-MANAS 14416:** India's free, confidential mental-health helpline.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *I follow the whole programme and I am still empty.* So did Vyasadeva. Ask Narada's question: how much of your day
   is direct glorification of Krishna? And ask the medical question honestly.
2. *How do I know whether it is spiritual or medical?* Spiritual signs: glorification thinned out, over-endeavour.
   Medical signs: weeks of poor sleep, appetite and concentration, no interest in anything. If the second set is
   present, see a doctor and keep hearing; they do not compete.
3. *Isn't admitting this a weakness?* Arjuna admitted it to Krishna and Vyasadeva to Narada. Say it to your spiritual
   master or a senior devotee, as they did.
4. *Should I leave my post?* Ask your authority; if the post is over-endeavour (NOI 2), perhaps. Vyasadeva did not leave
   his work; he added what was missing.

## S6. Check your understanding
1. What was the cause of Vyasadeva's dissatisfaction, according to Narada?
2. How does Prabhupada define utsaha?
3. Name four of the activities Prabhupada called "the sources and the maintainers of our enthusiasm".
4. What does BG 2.40 say about a little advancement on this path?
5. True or false: seeing a doctor for long low mood shows weak faith.

**Answers:** 1. He had not broadcast the Lord's glories; he described the four principles broadly but not Vasudeva's
glories (SB 1.5.8-9). 2. "Endeavor executed with intelligence in Krsna consciousness" (NOI 3 purport). 3. Any four of:
rising before 4 a.m., sixteen rounds, reading books, street sankirtana, preaching, offering prasadam (letter to
Dhananjaya, 1972). 4. It "can protect one from the most dangerous type of fear"; there is no loss (BG 2.40). 5. False:
regulating the body is part of the path (BG 6.16-17), and hearing continues alongside.
