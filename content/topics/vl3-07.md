# Importance of Morning Program & Meaning of Mangala Aroti

> **VL3 · 180 min · review: sources-checked.** IYF's material is a six-page VOICE study sheet, *Importance of Morning Sadhana
> Program* (`research/iyf-materials/vl3-07/`), and two decks, *Srila Prabhupada on Importance of Morning Program* and
> *Morning Program Absorption* (`research/iyf-materials/iyf-decks/`). The Preaching Encyclopedia's "First Year Morning
> Program" section is a list of twenty short morning-class topics for new devotees, not a treatment of the programme
> itself; it is noted in O. The IYF sheet is strong on Srila Prabhupada's letters and on *why* the programme is
> non-negotiable for temple residents. This page keeps that spine, verifies what can be verified, and re-pitches it for
> the VL3 student: a college boy who chants four rounds, lives at home, and has never attended mangala-arati.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on vl3-04, vl3-05 and vl3-06: those teach chanting, reading and hearing one by one; this page does not re-teach them, it shows how they sit together in one morning.

VL3 is where practice begins in earnest (KRA tab: "started practising K.C … chanting min. 4 rounds … start reading
prescribed Srila Prabhupada books and hearing prescribed lecture"). VL3-04 to VL3-06 taught chanting, reading and
hearing one by one. This topic shows them as one daily *programme*, in the order Srila Prabhupada arranged them, and
gives the student his first look at the temple's morning worship, which many have only seen on a festival evening.

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| VL1 | Not covered; the first invitation to chant (vl1-series) |
| VL2 | "Chanting do's and don'ts" (2015 syllabus): the practice is introduced |
| **VL3** (here) | The morning programme as a whole; the meaning of mangala-arati; a home version a student can keep |
| SPS | Deity worship as a limb of bhakti (sps-14); the nine processes (sps-09); quality of japa (sps-13) |
| DPS | Keeping the programme steady while carrying service responsibilities |
| BSS | The programme as the frame of brahmacari (celibate student) life in the ashram |

**Not done here:**
- Demanding the temple standard (rise by 4 a.m., attend every day) of a VL3 student who lives at home and has
  classes at 8. Srila Prabhupada's letters quoted in the IYF sheet address **temple residents** and **initiated
  disciples**. Quote them as what the standard *is*, not as what a VL3 boy is failing to do.
- The sixteen-round vow in full. It is explained as the goal (CC Madhya 22.113 purport), not imposed; VL3's KRA is
  "increment in the chanting rounds", from four.
- The technical rules of Deity worship (arcana). The students are learning to attend, not to perform.
- Rasa. The Gurv-astaka's sixth verse is mentioned in one line and left for later batches.

## C. Central message

Srila Prabhupada arranged the most powerful limbs of bhakti into one compact morning programme: mangala-arati,
kirtana, japa, guru-puja, Deity greeting and Srimad-Bhagavatam class. Attending it attentively "almost completely"
destroys "all that is troublesome to the heart" (SB 1.2.18), and even a small, regular home version of it gives the
day its direction (BG 6.17).

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. Name the parts of the ISKCON morning programme in order and say what each does (CC Madhya 22.128–129; SB 1.2.18).
2. Explain the word *mangala-arati* and what happens in the ceremony, and give the gist of the song sung at it
   (Sri Gurv-astaka).
3. Explain why Srila Prabhupada made early rising and the morning programme non-negotiable for temple residents,
   and why regulation itself is a gift rather than a burden (BG 6.17; NOI 3; SB 4.8.72 purport).
4. Say why the Bhagavatam class is "the heart" of the programme and how to hear it (SB 1.2.17–18; SB 1.1.6).
5. Design a 20–30 minute home morning programme for themselves and commit to it for a week.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** BG 6.17, SB 1.2.18 and CC Madhya 22.128–129 carry the talk; CC Madhya 22.113 and CC Antya 11.23 for the
rounds; the rest is background.

**BG 6.17** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/17/
> "He who is regulated in his habits of eating, sleeping, recreation and work can mitigate all material pains by practicing the yoga system."
- **Context:** Krsna describes the yogi's regulated life (6.16–17) before describing meditation.
- **Teaching:** One regulated in eating, sleeping, recreation and work can mitigate all material pains by yoga.
- **Purport point:** "Any unnecessary time spent sleeping is considered a great loss"; Rupa Gosvami slept barely two
  hours; Haridasa Thakura "would not even accept prasādam nor even sleep for a moment without finishing his daily
  routine of chanting"; one who is regulated "in all his work, speech, sleep, wakefulness" has "no material misery".
- **Application (preacher's):** The morning programme is the Gita's *yukta-svapnāvabodha* (regulated sleeping and
  waking) made into a daily form. Do not quote Rupa Gosvami's two hours as a target; quote it as the direction.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 2.14** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/14/
> "…one must learn to tolerate them without being disturbed."
- **Purport point:** "According to Vedic injunction, one has to take his bath early in the morning even during the
  month of Māgha (January-February). It is very cold at that time, but in spite of that a man who abides by the
  religious principles does not hesitate to take his bath."
- **Application (preacher's):** The winter mangala-arati is exactly this. The purport makes early rising a matter of
  tolerance, not of temperament ("I'm not a morning person").
- **Use:** in the talk, for the "it's too early" objection.

**NOI 3** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/3/
> "There are six principles favorable to the execution of pure devotional service: (1) being enthusiastic, (2) endeavoring with confidence, (3) being patient, (4) acting according to regulative principles [such as śravaṇaṁ kīrtanaṁ viṣṇoḥ smaraṇam – hearing, chanting and remembering Kṛṣṇa], (5) abandoning the association of nondevotees, and (6) following in the footsteps of the previous ācāryas."
- **Purport point:** "Neglect of the regulative principles will destroy devotional service." Besides the four
  prohibitions (*yama*) "there are positive regulative principles (niyama), such as the daily chanting of sixteen
  rounds". ISKCON "is opening many centers just to invite people to live in the company of devotees and practice the
  regulative principles of spiritual life." Enthusiasm "means action".
- **Application (preacher's):** The programme is *tat-tat-karma-pravartana*: the positive regulations, done
  together, in the company of devotees. The IYF sheet's first heading, "Without following spiritual principles,
  enthusiasm dwindles", is this purport's point in reverse.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**CC Madhya 22.128-129** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/22/128/
> "One should associate with devotees, chant the holy name of the Lord, hear Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, reside at Mathurā and worship the Deity with faith and veneration." (128)
- **Context:** Sri Caitanya teaching Sanatana Gosvami the sixty-four limbs; 129 says these five are the best, and
  that even slight association with them awakens love for Krsna.
- **Teaching:** These five limbs are the best of all; even a slight performance of them awakens love for Krsna (22.129).
- **Application (preacher's):** The IYF deck *Morning Program Absorption* makes the key observation: all five are in
  the morning programme at once. Devotees (sadhu-sanga), kirtana and japa (nama-kirtana), the class
  (bhagavata-sravana), the temple as the Lord's dhama (the principle of Mathura-vasa), and arati and darsana
  (sri-murtira sraddhaya sevana). "Srila Prabhupada has systematically integrated the most powerful elements of bhakti
  into a compact, power-packed, variety-filled, dynamic morning program" (the deck's sentence; a fair summary).
- **Use:** in the talk; the central idea.

**CC Madhya 22.113** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/22/113/
> "Kṛṣṇa is the origin of Lord Viṣṇu. He should always be remembered and never forgotten at any time. All the rules and prohibitions mentioned in the śāstras should be the servants of these two principles."
- **Purport point:** "One may have other duties to perform under the direction of the spiritual master, but he must
  first abide by the spiritual master's order to chant a certain number of rounds. In our Kṛṣṇa consciousness
  movement, we have recommended that the neophyte chant at least sixteen rounds." Service such as selling books is
  "an impetus for remembering Kṛṣṇa".
- **Application (preacher's):** Every item of the programme is a servant of one principle: remember Krsna, never
  forget Him. This is the answer to "why so many rituals": they are not ends; they are reminders.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**CC Antya 11.23** and **CC Antya 11.24** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/11/23/
> 11.23 (Sri Caitanya to Haridasa): "Can you ascertain what your disease is?"
- **Purport point (11.23):** "Haridāsa Ṭhākura replied, 'My disease is that I cannot complete my rounds.' If one
  cannot complete the fixed number of rounds he is assigned, he should be considered to be in a diseased condition of
  spiritual life… In our Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement we have fixed sixteen rounds as the minimum so that the
  Westerners will not feel burdened." (The IYF sheet quotes this correctly.)
- **Purport point (11.24):** "Unless one has come to the platform of spontaneous love of God, he must follow the
  regulative principles"; Haridasa and Raghunatha dasa Gosvami were "living examples".
- **Application (preacher's):** A fixed, kept number is the measure of health. For a VL3 student the fixed number is
  the one he agreed with his facilitator (four), not sixteen; the principle is the same.
- **Use:** in the talk (the story) and in K.

**SB 1.2.17** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/17/
> "Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the Personality of Godhead, who is the Paramātmā [Supersoul] in everyone's heart and the benefactor of the truthful devotee, cleanses desire for material enjoyment from the heart of the devotee who has developed the urge to hear His messages, which are in themselves virtuous when properly heard and chanted."
- **Purport point:** The Lord "is present there in the form of transcendental sound"; "if one is engaged in hearing
  the glories of the Lord, gradually he realizes his real position"; "The Lord is more anxious to take us back into
  His kingdom than we can desire."
- **Use:** in the talk, for the class.

**SB 1.2.18** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/18/
> "By regular attendance in classes on the Bhāgavatam and by rendering of service to the pure devotee, all that is troublesome to the heart is almost completely destroyed, and loving service unto the Personality of Godhead, who is praised with transcendental songs, is established as an irrevocable fact."
- **Purport point:** Two Bhagavatas, the book and the devotee; "the messages of the book Bhāgavata, therefore, have
  to be received from the devotee Bhāgavata"; Narada as the maidservant's son who advanced by serving sages.
- **Application (preacher's):** "Regular attendance in classes on the Bhāgavatam" is the verse's own phrase; the
  morning Bhagavatam class is its most literal fulfilment.
- **Use:** in the talk; the verse to remember.

**SB 1.1.6** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/1/6/
> "…for you have gone through them under proper guidance and have also explained them."
- **Purport point:** "To hear and explain them is more important than reading them. One can assimilate the knowledge
  of the revealed scriptures only by hearing and explaining." (The IYF sheet quotes this.) Also: the speaker must be
  free from the four vices before sitting on the vyasasana.
- **Use:** background; why a class is not replaced by private reading.

**SB 4.8.72** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/8/72/
> "For the first month Dhruva Mahārāja ate only fruits and berries on every third day, only to keep his body and soul together…"
- **Purport point:** "It is not possible in this age to follow Dhruva Mahārāja in his austerity, but the principles
  must be followed… As far as our ISKCON movement is concerned, we simply ask that one observe the four prohibitive
  rules, chant sixteen rounds and, instead of indulging in luxurious eating for the tongue, simply accept prasāda."
- **Application (preacher's):** The IYF sheet's "compared to the austerities of previous yogīs… our austerities are
  insignificant" is this purport. Rising for mangala-arati is a small austerity; Dhruva is the scale.
- **Use:** in the talk, briefly.

**BG 18.5** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/5/
> "Acts of sacrifice, charity and penance are not to be given up; they must be performed. Indeed, sacrifice, charity and penance purify even the great souls."
- **Application (preacher's):** Early rising is a penance that "purifies even the great souls"; the IYF sheet's
  point that exalted Vaisnavas never gave up the basic practices rests here.
- **Use:** background.

**Srila Prabhupada's letters quoted in the IYF sheet** — Verified (read at vedabase.io/en/library/letters/, 28 Oct
2026; quote the vedabase wording, which differs slightly from the sheet's). Letter to Mukunda, Los Angeles, 12 Jan
1974: "all the temple members, without exception must rise by 4:AM and attend mangala arati… We cannot expect our
guests to follow all our principles, but whoever lives in the temple must follow." Letter to Hayagriva, Madras,
13 Feb 1972: "impressive but not repressive, that is the system". Letter to Madhudvisa, Los Angeles, 16 Jun 1972:

complete our sixteen rounds." Letter to Cakravarti, New Delhi, 2 Dec 1975: "If we follow this simple program along
with regular sankirtana, distributing the books and preaching, then there will be no fall down… Krishna
consciousness is the medicine for the material disease." The sheet's other letters (O) are not yet checked.

**Sri Gurv-astaka (Visvanatha Cakravarti Thakura)** — Partially verified. Sung at every mangala-arati; text and BBT
translation in *Songs of the Vaiṣṇava Ācāryas* (BBT). Not on vedabase in a form the checker reads; verify the wording
against the songbook before quoting. Paraphrase in F.4.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **What the programme is.** In ISKCON temples the day begins at 4:30 a.m.: *mangala-arati* (the auspicious
   first worship of the Deities, with Sri Gurv-astaka), *tulasi-arati*, two hours of *japa*, *guru-puja* (worship of
   Srila Prabhupada with Sri Guru-vandana), the greeting of the Deities (*darsana-arati*, with Govindam), and the
   *Srimad-Bhagavatam class*, then breakfast prasadam. Times vary slightly by temple; the order does not. *Application
   (preacher's):* this is practice, not scripture; describe your own temple's timings.
2. **Why this shape.** CC Madhya 22.128–129 names five limbs as the best of all; the programme contains all five
   (E). SB 1.2.17–18 says hearing the Bhagavatam in the company of devotees destroys "all that is troublesome to the
   heart". NOI 3 says the positive regulations, done with enthusiasm, assure success, and that neglecting them
   destroys devotion. Srila Prabhupada did not invent new practices; he packed the strongest existing ones into a form
   a working person can keep. The IYF deck calls this "one of Srila Prabhupada's greatest gifts". Fair.
3. **Why early.** Three reasons the texts give. (a) Regulation of sleep is yoga (BG 6.17); the purport counts
   needless sleep "a great loss". (b) Austerity purifies (BG 18.5), and cold-morning discipline is the Gita's own
   example of tolerance (BG 2.14 purport). (c) The mind is quiet before the day's noise begins; chanting and hearing
   land. *Application (preacher's):* the third is experience, not a verse; say so. The traditional name for the
   pre-dawn period, *brahma-muhurta*, may be mentioned; a Prabhupada purport for it is not cited here (Requires source
   review).
4. **What "mangala-arati" means.** *Mangala* = auspicious; *arati* (from *aratrika*) = the ceremony in which lamps,
   incense, water, cloth, flowers and a fan are offered to the Deity to the sound of kirtana. It is "mangala" because it
   is the first worship of the day and sets the day's auspiciousness. The song is **Sri Gurv-astaka**, eight verses
   by Visvanatha Cakravarti Thakura glorifying the spiritual master (paraphrase; verify against the songbook):
   the guru extinguishes the forest fire of material existence like a rain-cloud (1); he is absorbed in kirtana of the
   holy name (2); he worships the Deity and engages his disciples in that worship (3); he is satisfied when devotees
   honour prasadam (4); he is eager to hear and chant about the Lord's pastimes (5); he is dear to the Lord because he
   assists in the Lord's most confidential service (6; one line only at VL3); he is honoured as good as the Lord
   because he is the Lord's dearest servant (7); by his mercy one receives Krsna's mercy, and without it no progress is
   possible (8). *Application (preacher's):* mangala-arati is therefore not Deity worship alone; it is the morning's
   first act of remembering *through whom* we approach Krsna. The *Morning Program Absorption* deck's advice,
   "concentrate on the meanings of the morning program songs", only works if the student knows them: give the sheet.
5. **The heart of it: the class.** The IYF sheet is right to put the Bhagavatam class at the centre. SB 1.2.18 names
   "regular attendance in classes on the Bhāgavatam"; SB 1.1.6 purport says hearing and explaining assimilate
   scripture better than reading; Srila Prabhupada's letter to Madhudvisa (16 Jun 1972, Verified) gives the method: one
   sloka a day, Sanskrit repeated, discussed "from all angles of approach". VL3-06 taught how to hear and take notes; this is where
   it is used.
6. **Japa inside the programme.** The two hours of japa are where the day's rounds are mostly finished. CC Madhya
   22.113 purport makes the rounds "most essential" of all regulations; CC Antya 11.23 purport makes a kept number the
   measure of health. For the VL3 student the number is four, agreed with the facilitator (VL3-04), and the morning is
   the best time to finish them before the phone starts.
7. **Impressive, not repressive.** Srila Prabhupada's letter to Hayagriva (13 Feb 1972, Verified) gives the mood for
   this topic: the programme should attract, not coerce. The IYF sheet draws the consequence: leaders inspire by
   attending joyfully themselves. For VL3, the preacher is that leader. If you have not been to mangala-arati this
   month, do not teach this topic yet.
8. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - "The morning programme is for temple devotees." The *standard* in Srila Prabhupada's letters is for temple
     residents; the *principle* (begin the day with Krsna) is for everyone. A home programme is real.
   - "Arati is idol worship." Deity worship (*sri-murtira sraddhaya sevana*) is one of the five best limbs by Sri
     Caitanya's own list (CC Madhya 22.128); the Deity is the Lord's authorised form for our worship. Refer to sps-14
     for the full treatment; here one sentence.
   - "I'll attend when I'm serious." Attending is how one becomes serious (SB 1.2.18: the attendance comes first,
     the fixity follows).
   - "Rituals are mechanical." *The Nectar of Devotion* (ch. 2; the IYF sheet's "Bhakti-rasamrta-sindhu pgs. 21–22"):
     "by the order of his spiritual master, one rises early in the morning and offers ārati, but then he develops real
     attachment" (Verified, vedabase.io/en/library/nod/2/). The order is: practice, then taste.
9. **Pitch for VL3.** Practice terms defined (arati, japa, guru-puja, darsana). One practice to establish: a home
   morning programme this week; the temple programme once this month. Encouraging: "small, kept" beats "big, dropped"
   (CC Antya 11.23 purport's logic).

## G. Lecture flow

**Main plan (about 3 hours: 90 min lecture + 90 min engagement)**

| Time | Segment | Notes |

**Alternative: up to a 2-hour lecture, activities skipped.** Give the segments above 120 minutes: add 10 minutes each to

**Alternative structure (experience-led):** if the class can meet at the temple, hold it after a mangala-arati the

(F.1, F.4), then why (F.2–3), then the home version (P).

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet, say your name, and say today is about one ordinary hour, the first one of the day.
2. Write the title on the board; explain "mangala-arati" in one line (an auspicious first worship of the day, with lamps and singing).
3. Ask H.2 question 1; take two or three answers and thank each by name.
4. Say that nobody is asked to wake at 4 a.m. today; the goal is a plan each person can keep.
5. Use the transition below and begin F.1.

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the room's own life)
1. What is the first thing you touch or look at after waking?
2. Is there one part of your day you protect, such as a prayer, a walk, a meal with family? What does it give you?
3. If you could add 30 calm minutes before the day starts, what would you put in them?

- **Opening question:** "First thirty minutes after waking: what happened?" Collect answers without comment. Then:
  "The Gita says the yogi regulates his sleeping and waking (BG 6.17). Srila Prabhupada designed a first ninety
  minutes for his disciples. Let us see what is in it and why."
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** Rahul, a second-year student, chants his four rounds "whenever": sometimes at
  11 p.m., half-asleep. His facilitator asks him to try them at 6 a.m. for a week. Ask the class: what will change?
- **Transition:** "What Rahul is trying is a small piece of what every ISKCON temple does every morning."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** their mornings are the only unclaimed hour of the day; evenings belong to
  college, friends and the phone. The one hour they control decides the rest.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **One programme, five best limbs.** CC Madhya 22.128–129; the programme contains all five. *Example:* walk through
   the timetable. *Transition:* what is the first item, and why that song?
2. **Mangala-arati: auspicious beginning, through the guru.** The ceremony and Sri Gurv-astaka (F.4). *Example:*
   verse 8, "by the guru's mercy, Krsna's mercy". *Transition:* why at 4:30?
3. **Regulation is yoga; early rising is austerity.** BG 6.17; BG 2.14 purport; BG 18.5. *Example:* Dhruva's first
   month (SB 4.8.72) against our "simple austerities". *Transition:* what holds it together?
4. **A fixed number, kept.** CC Madhya 22.113 purport; CC Antya 11.23 purport. *Example:* Haridasa's "disease".
   *Transition:* and the heart of the programme?
5. **The Bhagavatam class.** SB 1.2.17–18; SB 1.1.6 purport. *Example:* Prabhupada's one-sloka-a-day method
   (letter to Madhudvisa, 16 Jun 1972). *Transition:* to the home version (P).

## J. Stories and illustrations

- **Haridasa Thakura's "disease"** (CC Antya 11.20–24, with Srila Prabhupada's purports to 11.23–24). Sri Caitanya
  asks what his disease is; Haridasa answers that he cannot complete his rounds. Tell it from the text; the point is
  that a kept number is the measure of health, and that only one on the platform of spontaneous love may reduce it.
- **Dhruva Maharaja's austerity** (SB 4.8.72–76; purport to 4.8.72 relates it to ISKCON's "simple" requirements).
  One minute; the scale, not the model.
- **Narada as the maidservant's son** (SB 1.2.18 purport): advancement by serving and hearing from sages. One line.
- **"What is your morning program?"** The IYF deck quotes a disciple's memoir (Bhurijana dasa, *My Glorious
  Master*) in which Srila Prabhupada, in a car in Hong Kong, asks a temple president why his temple has no morning
  programme: "Is your temple in ISKCON? Whose disciple are you?" **Partially verified:** a disciple's memoir, quoted
  in IYF's deck; read the book's passage before telling it, and tell it as his account.
- **Srila Prabhupada in Vrndavana** (the deck, from *Srila Prabhupada-lilamrta*, "Return to Vrindavan"): he calls the
  temple president and asks why devotees are absent from mangala-arati; "everyone must attend. Punctuality was also
  important." **Partially verified:** check the Lilamrta chapter before quoting.
- **Contemporary image (the preacher's):** a phone that is charged every night without fail. Nobody calls that a
  burden; it is what keeps the phone usable. The morning programme is the devotee's charging.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

> **Before you teach:** decide what you will ask of a VL3 student who lives at home. The honest answer is: a home
> programme daily, the temple programme as often as he can, and never a guilt trip about 4 a.m.

1. **"I live at home and college starts at 8. How can I attend mangala-arati?"**
   - Most VL3 students cannot attend daily, and Srila Prabhupada's letters about 4 a.m. are addressed to temple
     residents. Do a home programme (P): wake earlier than you do now, bathe, chant your rounds, sing Gurv-astaka or
     one kirtana, read ten minutes. Attend the temple programme on Sundays or at a Japa Retreat. Direction matters
     more than distance.
2. **"I'm not a morning person."**
   - BG 2.14 purport: the cold Magha bath is taken regardless. Nobody is a morning person at first; one becomes one
     by going to bed earlier (BG 6.17 regulates sleep, not only waking). Try it for seven days before deciding.
3. **"Isn't arati idol worship?"**
   - Worship of the Deity is one of the five most powerful limbs of bhakti in Sri Caitanya's own teaching (CC Madhya
     22.128). The Deity is the Lord's form accepted by scripture for our worship, not an image we made God out of.
     sps-14 treats this fully; here, one sentence and move on.
4. **"Why sing to the guru first, not to Krsna?"**
   - Gurv-astaka verse 8 (paraphrase): by the guru's mercy one receives Krsna's mercy. The programme begins by
     remembering through whom we approach Krsna; the Deity greeting (Govindam) follows. Tradition, not a rule we
     invented.
5. **"Why so many rituals? Isn't chanting enough?"**
   - CC Madhya 22.113: all rules are servants of two principles, remember Krsna and never forget Him. Each item is a
     reminder; none is an end. And chanting is in the programme, two hours of it.
6. **"What if I miss my rounds one day?"**
   - CC Antya 11.23 purport calls an unkept number a "diseased condition"; *The Nectar of Devotion* (ch. 7; Verified,
     vedabase.io/en/library/nod/7/): "if one is not even able to chant sixteen rounds, then he must make it up the next
     day. He must be sure to keep his vow." Make it up, tell
     your facilitator (VL3-08), do not build a debt of weeks.
7. **"The class is too philosophical / I can't follow the Sanskrit."**
   - SB 1.1.6 purport: hearing and explaining is how scripture is assimilated. Sit near the front, bring the notebook
     from VL3-06, and ask one question afterwards. Prabhupada's method repeats the Sanskrit precisely so that
     beginners learn it.
8. **"Is a temple devotee who skips mangala-arati a bad devotee?"**
   - Not your question to judge (BG 9.30–31 teach that a devotee is not to be derided for a lapse). Srila Prabhupada
     set the standard for residents and asked leaders to make it "impressive but not repressive". Keep your eyes on
     your own card.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The 11 p.m. rounds.** Rahul finishes his four rounds at night, half-asleep, and feels nothing. He wonders
whether chanting "works".
- **Principles:** BG 6.17 (regulated sleep and waking); CC Antya 11.23 purport (chant loudly enough to hear yourself);
  SB 1.2.17 (the Lord acts from within one eager to hear).
- **Questions:** Is the problem the mantra or the hour? What would a 6 a.m. week test? What else could he change?
- **Takeaway:** Move the rounds to the morning before judging them.

**Case 2: The first mangala-arati.** Sneha's brother attends his first mangala-arati at a Japa Retreat. He is moved by
the kirtana but has no idea what the song meant or why everyone bowed when the conch sounded.
- **Principles:** F.1 and F.4 (the parts and the song); CC Madhya 22.128 (all five limbs present).
- **Questions:** What should someone explain to him *before* his next visit? Which one line of Gurv-astaka would you
  give him first?
- **Takeaway:** Attendance with understanding; give newcomers the words.

**Case 3: The hostel.** Arjun shares a hostel room; his roommate sleeps till 9 and objects to any sound before 7.
- **Principles:** BG 17.15 (speech not agitating to others; from vl3-13); BG 6.17; NOI 3 (enthusiasm, patience).
- **Questions:** What can he do silently (japa under the breath, reading, headphones for a recorded class)? Where
  else could he go? Who could he ask (VL3-08)?
- **Takeaway:** Regulation adapts to the room; it does not wait for a perfect room.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Every ISKCON temple begins its day the same way: *mangala-arati* (an auspicious first worship of the Deity, with lamps, incense and singing) at about 4:30, then *japa* (quiet chanting of the Hare Krsna mantra on beads, 108 mantras a round), worship of Srila Prabhupada, the greeting of the Deities, and the Srimad-Bhagavatam class. Srila Prabhupada arranged it so that the five practices Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu names as best, associating with devotees, chanting the holy name, hearing the Bhagavatam, living in the Lord's place and worshipping the Deity (CC Madhya 22.128), all happen in one morning.

The song at mangala-arati is Sri Gurv-astaka, eight verses glorifying the spiritual master, because we approach Krsna through the guru. All the rules of the programme serve two principles: always remember Krsna, never forget Him (CC Madhya 22.113). Keeping a fixed number of rounds is the sign of health; Haridasa Thakura called an unfinished quota his "disease" (CC Antya 11.23 purport).

Rising early is an austerity (BG 18.5) and regulated eating, sleeping, recreation and work is yoga (BG 6.17). The Bhagavatam promises that regular attendance at its classes, with service to devotees, almost completely destroys "all that is troublesome to the heart" (SB 1.2.18).

You may not be able to attend daily yet. You can begin your day the same way at home, small and kept, and come to the temple programme when you can.

## S2. Key ideas

- Five best limbs of bhakti, one morning (CC Madhya 22.128–129).
- Regular attendance in Bhagavatam classes clears the heart (SB 1.2.18).
- Mangala = auspicious; arati = the lamp-and-kirtana worship of the Deity; Gurv-astaka = the guru's glories.
- All rules serve two principles: remember Krsna, never forget Him (CC Madhya 22.113).
- A fixed number of rounds, kept, is the sign of health (CC Antya 11.23 purport).
- Small and kept beats big and dropped.

## S3. Verses to remember

- **SB 1.2.18:** "By regular attendance in classes on the Bhāgavatam and by rendering of service to the pure devotee, all that is troublesome to the heart is almost completely destroyed…" *For me:* the class is medicine; attendance is the dose.
- **BG 6.17:** "He who is regulated in his habits of eating, sleeping, recreation and work can mitigate all material pains by practicing the yoga system." *For me:* my bedtime is part of my sadhana.
- **CC Madhya 22.113:** "…All the rules and prohibitions mentioned in the śāstras should be the servants of these two principles." *For me:* every item of the programme is a reminder of Krsna, not a ritual for its own sake.

## S4. Words to know

- **Mangala-arati:** the first, auspicious worship of the Deities each morning, with lamps, incense, flowers and kirtana.
- **Sri Gurv-astaka:** eight verses by Visvanatha Cakravarti Thakura glorifying the spiritual master, sung at mangala-arati.
- **Japa:** chanting the Hare Krsna maha-mantra on beads; a "round" is 108 mantras.
- **Guru-puja:** the morning worship of Srila Prabhupada.
- **Darsana:** seeing the Deities when the curtains open.
- **Srimad-Bhagavatam class:** the daily verse-by-verse talk that is the heart of the programme.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **I can't come at 4:30. Is there any point?** Yes. Do a home programme: wake earlier, bathe, chant your rounds before the phone, one verse of Gurv-astaka, ten minutes of reading. Come to the temple programme on a Sunday or at a retreat.
2. **Why sing to the guru before Krsna?** Gurv-astaka's last verse: by the guru's mercy we receive Krsna's mercy. We begin by remembering through whom we approach Him.
3. **Isn't arati idol worship?** Worship of the Deity is one of the five best limbs of bhakti in Sri Caitanya's own teaching (CC Madhya 22.128). The Deity is the Lord's form given in scripture for our worship.
4. **Why so many items? Isn't chanting enough?** Every item is a servant of one principle: remember Krsna (CC Madhya 22.113). And chanting is two hours of the programme.
5. **I missed my rounds yesterday.** Make them up today and tell your facilitator. Haridasa Thakura called an unfinished quota his "disease" (CC Antya 11.23 purport); the cure is to finish it, not to hide it.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. Name the five limbs of bhakti that CC Madhya 22.128 calls the best.
2. What does SB 1.2.18 say happens through regular attendance in Bhagavatam classes?
3. What does "mangala" mean, and what is sung at mangala-arati?
4. According to CC Madhya 22.113, what two principles do all rules serve?
5. True or false: the Gita says a yogi should sleep as little as possible without any regulation (BG 6.17).
6. What did Haridasa Thakura say his "disease" was (CC Antya 11.23 purport)?

**Answers:** 1. Associating with devotees, chanting the holy name, hearing Srimad-Bhagavatam, residing at Mathura (the Lord's place), worshipping the Deity with faith (CC Madhya 22.128). 2. "All that is troublesome to the heart is almost completely destroyed" and loving service is established (SB 1.2.18). 3. Auspicious; Sri Gurv-astaka, glorifying the spiritual master. 4. Always remember Krsna; never forget Him (CC Madhya 22.113). 5. False: it says *regulated* sleeping and waking (BG 6.17). 6. That he could not complete his rounds (CC Antya 11.23 purport).
