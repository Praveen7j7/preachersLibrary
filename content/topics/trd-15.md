# Pratistha: the preacher's subtle desire for name and fame

> **TRD · up to 180 min · review: drafted.** Group C (non-duplicity), second page. Builds on trd-14 (the four cheatings)
> and on DPS-11 (humility) and DPS-19 (false ego), which it does not repeat. trd-49 later treats all five weeds of
> CC Madhya 19.159 together; this page takes only *pratistha* (prestige), the one that grows with preaching success.

## A. Topic overview

| | |
|---|---|

| Suggested duration | Up to 3 hours; main plan about 150 minutes |
| Preparation time | About 2½ hours: CC Madhya 19.158–161 with purports (the 19.160 purport in full), CC Antya 20.21 and 20.29 with purports, BG 17.18 |
| Difficulty | Advanced |

| Teaching objective | Participants recognise *pratistha* as a weed that is watered by the very chanting and preaching that should grow bhakti, know its everyday forms in a preacher's life from Prabhupada's own list, and adopt the two remedies Lord Caitanya gives: cut it as soon as it is seen (CC Madhya 19.161) and expect no honour while offering all honour (CC Antya 20.21) |

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on trd-14: the four cheatings are the hidden motives of religion; *pratistha* is the one that becomes
dangerous precisely when a devotee is doing well. A new devotee rarely has a reputation to protect; a resident who has
preached for years does. Lord Caitanya names it in His instruction to Rupa Gosvami as a weed beside the creeper of
devotion, and Prabhupada's purport (CC Madhya 19.160) lists the forms it takes in a religious person: exactly the
forms available to a temple preacher.

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| VL1 | Not taught; only "serve without expecting praise" in passing |
| VL2 | Why doing good for recognition is still self-centred (BG 17.18: austerity for honour is in passion) |
| VL3 | CC Antya 20.21 as the mood for chanting: expect no honour, give all honour |
| SPS | A new preacher's wish to be appreciated: SB 1.1.2 purport (devotees are nonenvious, above competition) |
| DPS | Humility (DPS-11) and false ego (DPS-19) in general; the weeds of CC Madhya 19.159 appear in the stages series (DPS-14) |
| BSS | BIKC on renunciation and the brahmacari's simplicity (bss-26); the wish to be known as a strict brahmacari |
| **TRD (here)** | *Pratistha* in a preacher's life: Prabhupada's list of its forms (CC Madhya 19.160 purport), why it is watered by success, and the Lord's remedies |

**Not done here:** the other four weeds (trd-49); humility as a quality (DPS-11); the stool comparison attributed to
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura (see E, "Requires source review").

## C. Central message

The desire for name and fame (*pratistha*) is a weed that grows beside devotion and is watered by the same chanting
and preaching; if not cut at once it stops the real creeper. Lord Caitanya's remedy is to cut it as soon as it is seen
and to live by His verse: expect no honour for oneself, and offer all honour to others.

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Quote CC Madhya 19.159 and name *pratistha* among the weeds, explaining the image of the misused water (19.160).
2. List at least five forms of *pratistha* from Prabhupada's purport to CC Madhya 19.160 and recognise which could appear in a preacher's own life.
3. Explain from BG 17.18 why austerity or service done for honour is "neither stable nor permanent."
4. State the two remedies: cutting at once (CC Madhya 19.161) and *amanina manadena* (CC Antya 20.21), and show how CC Antya 6.237 applies the second to a renunciant.
5. Tell, as the text tells it, the Lord's rebuke of Jagadananda's pride (CC Antya 4.158–160) and draw the lesson Prabhupada's text draws.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**CC Madhya 19.158–159** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/19/159/
> 19.159: "Some unnecessary creepers growing with the bhakti creeper are the creepers of behavior unacceptable for those trying to attain perfection, diplomatic behavior, animal-killing, mundane profiteering, mundane adoration and mundane importance. All these are unwanted creepers."
- **Context:** Lord Caitanya teaching Rupa Gosvami at Prayaga: the seed of devotion, the creeper, the weeds.
- **Teaching:** Mundane adoration (*puja*) and mundane importance (*pratistha*) are weeds that grow *with* the bhakti creeper, not away from it.
- **Purport point (19.159):** "Material adoration and titles are decorations that cannot be carried over to the next body. In the next life, everything is forgotten." And: "Sometimes these unwanted creepers look exactly like the bhakti creeper."
- **Use:** in the talk, the central text.

**CC Madhya 19.160** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/19/160/
> "If one does not distinguish between the bhakti creeper and the other creepers, the sprinkling of water is misused because the other creepers are nourished while the bhakti creeper is curtailed."
- **Teaching:** The water is hearing and chanting. The same water grows the weed if the weed is not recognised.
- **Purport point:** Prabhupada gives Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura's description of the weeds. Among them: "One may make a show of devotional service like the prākṛta-sahajiyās… With the support of family tradition, one may become a pseudo guru… One may also try to carry out a professional business by means of chanting the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra or reading Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam… one may become a cheap Vaiṣṇava by trying to chant in a secluded place for material adoration, or one may desire mundane reputation by making compromises with nondevotees concerning one's philosophy or spiritual life… Just to cheat some innocent people, one makes a show of advanced spiritual life and becomes known as a sādhu, mahātmā or religious person."
- **Application (preacher's):** Each item on this list is a door open to a *preacher*, because preaching gives a stage. The list is for self-examination, not for labelling others.
- **Use:** in the talk; read the list aloud from the purport.

**CC Madhya 19.161** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/19/161/
> "As soon as an intelligent devotee sees an unwanted creeper growing beside the original creeper, he must cut it down instantly. Then the real creeper, the bhakti-latā, grows nicely, returns home, back to Godhead, and seeks shelter under the lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa."
- **Purport point:** "If one is misled by unwanted creepers and is victimized, he cannot make progress back to Godhead."
- **Use:** in the talk, the first remedy: at once, not after the programme, not after the festival.

**CC Antya 20.21** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/20/21/
> "One who thinks himself lower than the grass, who is more tolerant than a tree, and who does not expect personal honor but is always prepared to give all respect to others can very easily always chant the holy name of the Lord."
- **Teaching:** *Amanina manadena*: not expecting honour, giving honour. This is the condition for constant chanting; the proud chanter's chanting is interrupted.
- **Use:** in the talk, the second remedy.

**CC Antya 20.29** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/20/29/
> "I do not desire material wealth, materialistic followers, a beautiful wife or fruitive activities described in flowery language. All I want, life after life, is unmotivated devotional service to You."
- **Teaching:** *Na janam*: not even followers. A preacher's "followers" are the most respectable form of *pratistha*.
- **Use:** in the talk and as the closing prayer (full Siksastaka: trd-12).

**CC Antya 6.237** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/6/237/
> "Do not expect honor, but offer all respect to others. Always chant the holy name of Lord Kṛṣṇa, and within your mind render service to Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa in Vṛndāvana."
- **Context:** Lord Caitanya's final instruction to Raghunatha dasa Gosvami, a renunciant.
- **Purport point:** Bhaktivinoda Thakura (quoted): a person in the renounced order "should show all respect to others, but should n[ot expect honour]" (the sentence continues in the purport; read it in full).
- **Use:** in the talk: the Lord applied CC Antya 20.21 personally to a renounced disciple.

**BG 17.18** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/17/18/
> "Penance performed out of pride and for the sake of gaining respect, honor and worship is said to be in the mode of passion. It is neither stable nor permanent."
- **Purport point:** "Persons in the mode of passion arrange to be worshiped by subordinates and let them wash their feet and offer riches."
- **Use:** in the talk: Krishna's own classification of service done for honour.

**BG 16.1–3** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/16/1-3/
> Among the divine qualities: "modesty… and freedom from envy and from the passion for honor."
- **Use:** in the talk, briefly: freedom from the passion for honour is a *divine* quality, so its opposite is not a small fault.

**BG 13.8–12** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/13/8-12/
> "Humility; pridelessness; nonviolence; tolerance; simplicity…" are declared to be knowledge, "and besides this whatever there may be is ignorance."
- **Use:** background: Krishna begins the list of knowledge with *amanitvam* (humility) and *adambhitvam* (pridelessness).

**SB 1.1.2** — Verified. The Bhagavatam is for the "nonenvious" (*nirmatsaranam*); purport: devotees "rise above such competitions." *Use:* link to trd-14 (one line).

**CC Madhya 13.80** — Verified. "I identify Myself only as the servant of the servant of the servant of the lotus feet of Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa." *Use:* the identity that leaves no room for *pratistha* (fuller use: trd-18).

**CC Antya 4.158, 4.160** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/4/158/
> 4.158: "Jagā [Jagadānanda Paṇḍita] is only a new boy, but he has become so proud that he thinks himself competent to advise a person like you."
> 4.160: "But Jagā wants to advise you. This is but the impudence of a naughty boy."
- **Use:** story in J (told as the text tells it).

**"Pratistha is like stool" (attributed to Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura)** — **Requires source review.** The
saying is widely repeated in ISKCON classes, and the TRD topic list names it from Prabhupada's purports in CC Antya 1.
No passage with this wording was found in `research/books/` or on the vedabase pages read for this page. Until an
editor locates it, do not quote it as Prabhupada's or Bhaktisiddhanta's words; teach the same point from CC Madhya
19.159–160, which is verified.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** Devotion is a creeper that grows by the water of hearing and chanting (CC Madhya 19.152–155;
   DPS-14 covers the image). Weeds grow beside it, fed by the same water (19.160). *Pratistha*, the wish to be
   important, is one of them (19.159). Its special danger: it "look[s] exactly like the bhakti creeper" (19.159
   purport). Enthusiasm for preaching and hunger for recognition can be mistaken for each other, by others and by
   oneself.
2. **Why it grows with success.** The purport to 19.160 lists how a religious person seeks adoration: making a show of
   advancement, becoming known as a sadhu, chanting in seclusion for the reputation of it, compromising philosophy to
   be liked, turning chanting or Bhagavatam reading into a profession. Every item presupposes an audience. A resident
   who preaches gains an audience; so the weed finds water.
3. **Why it is serious, not cosmetic.** Krishna classes austerity done for honour as passion, "neither stable nor
   permanent" (BG 17.18); freedom from "the passion for honor" is among the divine qualities (BG 16.1–3); humility
   and pridelessness open the list of what He calls knowledge (BG 13.8–12). And in Lord Caitanya's verse, the proud
   person simply cannot chant constantly (CC Antya 20.21): *pratistha* interrupts the very practice it feeds on.
4. **The two remedies.** (a) *Cut it instantly* (19.161): the moment one notices "I want them to know it was me,"
   that thought is turned into service for another's credit, or confessed to a senior (trd-16). (b) *Amanina manadena*
   (CC Antya 20.21; applied to a renunciant in CC Antya 6.237): expect no honour, give all honour. The second is the
   positive habit that starves the weed over years.
5. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *"If I avoid pratistha I should refuse all responsibility."* No. Lord Caitanya praised Sanatana Gosvami as "an
     experienced authority" (CC Antya 4.167–170) and the Lord Himself led the Gundica cleaning (trd-18). Position is
     not the weed; wanting the position for oneself is.
   - *"Appreciating a devotee's service feeds his pratistha, so I should withhold praise."* CC Antya 20.21 says give
     all respect to others. Praise given sincerely is the giver's duty; wanting it is the receiver's weed.
   - *"The humble mood means thinking I am useless."* The Lord's identity is "servant of the servant of the servant"
     (CC Madhya 13.80): a full identity with no vacancy for self-importance.
6. **Pitch for TRD.** The speaker begins with himself: where this weed has grown in his own preaching. Nothing in the
   session rates another resident.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet the residents. Say: "Today we take one weed from Lord Caitanya's garden: *pratistha*, the wish to be
   important. It is the weed that grows best in a preacher."
2. Write CC Madhya 19.159's list on the board and circle *pratistha*.
3. Say: "Everything today is a mirror for ourselves. No one will be asked to speak about anyone else."
4. Ask H.2 question 1 for private writing (two minutes).
5. Ask question 2 aloud; take two or three answers; thank each by name; begin with the creeper image.

**H.2 Opening questions**
1. (Private.) Remember the last time your service was praised in front of others. What did you feel in the next
   hour? Write one honest line.
2. Why does a weed in a garden get water? (Expected: it is watered along with the plant; the gardener does not see the
   difference.)
3. Which is harder: to be criticised for a service, or to see another devotee praised for the same service? (Let them
   answer; no judgment.)

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Pratistha is a weed beside the creeper, fed by the same water.** CC Madhya 19.159–160. Hearing and chanting
   nourish it when it goes unrecognised, and the bhakti creeper "is curtailed." *Example:* a preacher who chants his
   rounds while planning how his report will sound in the meeting. *Transition:* what does the weed look like?
2. **Prabhupada's list is a preacher's list.** The 19.160 purport: making a show of advanced life, becoming known as a
   sadhu, chanting for reputation, compromising philosophy to be liked, professional chanting or Bhagavatam reading,
   pseudo-guru by family or position. *Application (preacher's):* a stage, a title, a WhatsApp following and a
   fundraising target are the modern soil for each. *Transition:* is it really so serious?
3. **Krishna calls service for honour passion, and freedom from it divine.** BG 17.18 (not stable, not permanent);
   BG 16.1–3 (freedom from the passion for honour is a divine quality); BG 13.8–12 (humility and pridelessness begin
   knowledge). *Transition:* and Lord Caitanya shows how He treats it.
4. **The Lord rebukes pride even in a dear associate.** Jagadananda's advice to Sanatana (J). *Transition:* what do
   we do when we see it in ourselves?
5. **Cut it instantly, and live by "expect none, give all."** CC Madhya 19.161; CC Antya 20.21; the Lord's personal
   order to Raghunatha dasa, CC Antya 6.237. The identity that makes this natural: "servant of the servant of the
   servant" (CC Madhya 13.80). *Transition:* the prayer that asks for no followers (CC Antya 20.29).

## J. Stories and illustrations

**Jagadananda Pandita advises Sanatana Gosvami (CC Antya 4.136–170).** Told as the text tells it. Sanatana, suffering
from itching sores and unhappy that the Lord embraced him despite them, consulted Jagadananda Pandita (4.136).
Jagadananda advised him to return to Vrndavana after the Ratha-yatra (4.141). When Sanatana told the Lord that
Jagadananda had advised this (4.156), "Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, in an angry mood, began to chastise Jagadānanda
Paṇḍita" (4.157): "Jagā is only a new boy, but he has become so proud that he thinks himself competent to advise a
person like you" (4.158); "you are on the level of his spiritual master. Yet not knowing his own value, he dares to
advise you" (4.159); "this is but the impudence of a naughty boy" (4.160). Sanatana fell at the Lord's feet and said
he now understood Jagadananda's fortune: the Lord made Jagadananda "drink the nectar of affectionate relationships,"
while honouring Sanatana with prayers was like "the bitter juice of nimba and niśindā" (4.161–163). The Lord replied
that He did not love Jagadananda more, "however, I cannot tolerate transgressions of the standard etiquette"
(4.166); Sanatana is "an experienced authority in the śāstras, whereas Jagā is just a young boy" (4.167); "I offer
you praise… because you are actually so qualified" (4.170).
- *Lesson (as the text gives it):* the Lord does not tolerate a junior's presumption even when the junior is dear to
  Him; and the one rebuked is the more fortunate. *Application (preacher's):* for a resident, being corrected for
  self-importance is mercy, not loss; and honour given to a senior is etiquette, not flattery.
- *Use:* in the talk, fifteen minutes; keep every detail as in the verses.

**Lord Caitanya's instruction to Raghunatha dasa (CC Antya 6.236–237).** The Lord's own summary for a renunciant:
"Do not talk like people in general or hear what they say. You should not eat very palatable food, nor should you
dress very nicely… Do not expect honor, but offer all respect to others. Always chant the holy name." *Connection:*
the remedy for *pratistha* is given as a daily rule to a young renunciant, in the same breath as food and dress.

**The Siksastaka's fourth prayer (CC Antya 20.29).** "Na janam": not even followers. *Connection:* the Lord in a
devotee's mood refuses the very thing a preacher most easily collects.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"I work hard for Prabhupada's mission. Is it wrong to want my service to be noticed?"**
   - Wanting it noticed by Krishna is devotion; wanting it noticed by people is the weed (CC Madhya 19.159). The test
     is what happens inside when another is praised for the same work (H.2 question 3). The remedy is not to work
     less but to cut the thought at once (19.161) and offer the credit to others (CC Antya 20.21).
2. **"Prabhupada accepted honour: a vyasasana, garlands, obeisances. Why can't a preacher?"**
   - Honour offered to the guru is the disciple's duty and the guru accepts it on behalf of his own guru; BG 17.18's
     warning is about *arranging* to be worshipped. A resident who is not a guru has no such role; and CC Antya 6.237
     gives the rule for the renounced: expect none.
3. **"Isn't this list (19.160 purport) about pseudo-gurus and sahajiyas, not about us?"**
   - It includes them, and it also includes "chant in a secluded place for material adoration" and "making compromises
     with nondevotees concerning one's philosophy" to gain "mundane reputation": ordinary temptations of ordinary
     preachers.
4. **"If I feel the weed, should I stop preaching until I am pure?"**
   - No: the Lord's instruction is to *cut the weed*, not the creeper (19.161). Keep preaching, hear more, and reveal
     the thought to a senior devotee (trd-16).
5. **"How do I give honour to a devotee whose behaviour I know to be poor?"**
   - CC Antya 20.21 asks for *respect*, not for approval of faults; NOI 5 explains dealing with devotees by level, and
     NOI 6 forbids judging a devotee by bodily faults (fuller treatment: trd-19, trd-50).

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real people.*

**Case 1: The report.** Nitai leads the college programme. Every month he writes the report. He notices he writes "I"
often, mentions his own talks by name, and leaves out that two junior brahmacaris did most of the follow-up calls.
- **Dilemma:** The report is accurate in facts. What is wrong?
- **Principles:** CC Madhya 19.159 (mundane importance), 19.161 (cut instantly), CC Antya 20.21 (give all respect).
- **Questions:** Which item of the 19.160 list is nearest? What would "cutting at once" look like in this report? What
  changes if he rewrites it to honour the two juniors?
- **Analysis:** The weed is in the pronoun. Cutting means rewriting before sending, naming the juniors, and telling
  his authority what he noticed. The programme loses nothing; the creeper gains.
- **Takeaway:** *Amanina manadena* can be practised in a document.

**Case 2: The soft talk.** Haridasa is invited to speak at a professional gathering. He plans to leave out the
regulative principles and Krishna's name "so they don't switch off" and so he is invited again.
- **Principles:** 19.160 purport ("mundane reputation by making compromises with nondevotees concerning one's
  philosophy"); SB 1.1.2 (the nonenvious devotee is above competition for approval).
- **Questions and analysis:** Adjusting language for the audience is skill; removing the message to be liked is the
  weed. He should speak Krishna's message in their words (preaching method: trd-02) and accept not being invited back.
- **Takeaway:** The wish to be asked again is *pratistha* wearing the dress of outreach.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first; thank each answer)
1. What does *pratistha* mean? *Expected:* the desire for mundane importance, name and fame.
2. In CC Madhya 19.160, what is the "water," and why is it "misused"? *Expected:* hearing and chanting; they feed the weed when the weed is not recognised.
3. What does Lord Caitanya say to do when a weed is seen? *Expected:* cut it down instantly (19.161).
4. According to BG 17.18, what mode is austerity done for honour in, and what is its result? *Expected:* passion; neither stable nor permanent.
5. What are the two halves of *amanina manadena* (CC Antya 20.21)? *Expected:* expect no honour for oneself; give all respect to others.
6. Why did the Lord rebuke Jagadananda, in His own words? *Expected:* a "new boy" presuming to advise his senior; "I cannot tolerate transgressions of the standard etiquette" (CC Antya 4.158, 4.166).

**M.2 Activities**
- **Option 1: The mirror list (15 min).** Each resident reads Prabhupada's list (19.160 purport) privately and marks,
  for himself only, the one item nearest to his own temptation, then writes one sentence of how he would "cut it
  instantly" this week. Nothing is shared.
- **Option 2: Credit exchange (15 min).** In pairs, each tells the other one service that a *third* devotee did well
  this month and that went unnoticed; then each writes a two-line note of appreciation to that devotee and delivers it
  the same day. (Practises the second half of CC Antya 20.21.)

**M.3 Reflection**
- Why does the Lord make "not expecting honour" the condition for *constant* chanting (CC Antya 20.21)? What does
  the wish for honour do to the holy name in my mouth?

## P. Practical takeaway

- **Main lesson:** *Pratistha* grows on the preacher's own stage, watered by his own chanting, and must be cut the

  and leave your own name out where you can. Each evening note in one line any moment the wish for recognition arose

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Lord Caitanya taught Rupa Gosvami that devotion is a creeper watered by hearing and chanting. Weeds grow beside it,
fed by the same water. He named them: forbidden behaviour, diplomacy, violence to living beings, profit, adoration and
*pratistha*, "mundane importance" (CC Madhya 19.159). If the gardener does not tell the weed from the creeper, "the
sprinkling of water is misused" and the creeper "is curtailed" (19.160). Srila Prabhupada's purport warns that these
weeds "look exactly like the bhakti creeper," and lists how a religious person seeks importance: making a show of
advanced spiritual life, becoming known as a sadhu, chanting in seclusion for reputation, compromising philosophy to
gain reputation, and turning chanting or Bhagavatam reading into a profession (19.160 purport). A preacher has a stage,
so this weed grows well in him.

Krishna says that austerity done "for the sake of gaining respect, honor and worship" is in the mode of passion and
is "neither stable nor permanent" (BG 17.18), while "freedom from… the passion for honor" is a divine quality
(BG 16.1–3) and humility and pridelessness begin the list of knowledge (BG 13.8–12). Lord Caitanya showed how
seriously He takes this: when Jagadananda Pandita, a dear associate, advised the senior Sanatana Gosvami, the Lord
rebuked him as "a new boy" who "has become so proud," saying "I cannot tolerate transgressions of the standard
etiquette" (CC Antya 4.158, 4.166).

The Lord gives two remedies. First, when a devotee sees a weed, "he must cut it down instantly" (CC Madhya 19.161).
Second, "one who… does not expect personal honor but is always prepared to give all respect to others can very easily
always chant the holy name" (CC Antya 20.21); He gave this same rule personally to Raghunatha dasa: "Do not expect
honor, but offer all respect to others" (CC Antya 6.237). The session closed with His prayer that asks for no wealth,
no followers and no fame, only "unmotivated devotional service… life after life" (CC Antya 20.29).

## S2. Key ideas

- *Pratistha*, the wish for importance, is a weed that grows beside devotion and is watered by chanting and preaching (CC Madhya 19.159–160).
- It looks like devotion; Prabhupada's list of its forms is a mirror for a preacher (19.160 purport).
- Service for honour is passion, "neither stable nor permanent" (BG 17.18); freedom from it is divine (BG 16.1–3).
- Remedy 1: cut the weed instantly (CC Madhya 19.161). Remedy 2: expect no honour, give all honour (CC Antya 20.21; 6.237).
- The Lord rebuked pride even in a dear associate (CC Antya 4.158–166); correction is mercy.

## S3. Verses to remember

- **CC Madhya 19.159:** "…mundane profiteering, mundane adoration and mundane importance. All these are unwanted creepers." *For me:* my wish to be known is a weed, not a small habit.
- **CC Madhya 19.161:** "he must cut it down instantly." *For me:* not after the programme; now.
- **CC Antya 20.21:** "…who does not expect personal honor but is always prepared to give all respect to others can very easily always chant the holy name." *For me:* pride interrupts my chanting.
- **BG 17.18:** penance "for the sake of gaining respect, honor and worship… is neither stable nor permanent." *For me:* what I do for praise will not last.

## S4. Words to know

- **Pratistha:** mundane importance, prestige, the desire for name and fame (CC Madhya 19.159).
- **Upasakha:** the unwanted creepers (weeds) that grow beside the bhakti creeper (CC Madhya 19.158–160).
- **Amanina manadena:** not expecting honour, giving honour (CC Antya 20.21).
- **Bhakti-lata:** the creeper of devotion (CC Madhya 19.161).

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Is it wrong to want my service noticed?** Wanting Krishna to notice is devotion; wanting people to notice is the weed (CC Madhya 19.159). Cut the thought at once and give the credit to others (19.161; CC Antya 20.21).
2. **Should I stop preaching until the wish for fame is gone?** No. The Lord says cut the weed, not the creeper (19.161). Keep preaching, hear more, and tell a senior devotee what you noticed.
3. **Prabhupada accepted honour. Why not a preacher?** Honour to the guru is the disciple's duty; BG 17.18 warns against arranging to be worshipped. For a renunciant the Lord's rule is "do not expect honor" (CC Antya 6.237).
4. **How do I respect a devotee whose faults I know?** Respect is not approval of faults; CC Antya 20.21 asks for respect to all. Dealing with faults is a separate topic (trd-19).

## S6. Check your understanding

1. Name three of the weeds in CC Madhya 19.159.
2. In CC Madhya 19.160, why is the water "misused"?
3. What must a devotee do "as soon as" he sees a weed (CC Madhya 19.161)?
4. True or false: BG 17.18 says austerity done for honour is in the mode of goodness.
5. What are the two halves of the instruction in CC Antya 20.21 about honour?
6. In the Lord's own words, why did He rebuke Jagadananda Pandita?

**Answers:** 1. Any three of: behaviour unacceptable for those seeking perfection, diplomatic behaviour, animal-killing,
mundane profiteering, mundane adoration, mundane importance (CC Madhya 19.159). 2. Because the water feeds the weeds
while the bhakti creeper is curtailed (19.160). 3. Cut it down instantly (19.161). 4. False: it is in the mode of
passion and is "neither stable nor permanent" (BG 17.18). 5. Not expecting personal honour; always giving all respect to
others (CC Antya 20.21). 6. He was "a new boy" who "has become so proud" as to advise his senior; "I cannot tolerate
transgressions of the standard etiquette" (CC Antya 4.158, 4.166).
