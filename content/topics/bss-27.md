# Discipline

> BSS · 120 min · drafted. Follows BIKC chapter 15. Builds on bss-26 (austerity and its measure): this page is about the mind, the teacher and the habit of obedience that make a steady life possible.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level
Brahmacarya depends on thousands of small repeated acts. BIKC chapter 15 states the aim of discipline: to remember Krsna; the obstacle: a mind full of material thoughts; the method: the intelligence controls the mind under the expert guidance of the guru.

## C. Central message
The mind is either our best friend or our worst enemy; discipline under a guru, with devotees, in chanting, hearing and service, trains it to become a friend that remembers Krsna.

## D. Learning outcomes
1. Quote BG 6.5–6 and say when the mind is friend and when enemy.
2. Explain BG 6.35: practice and detachment.
3. Describe in two minutes the Avanti brahmana's lesson (SB 11.23).
4. Draft a daily structure with chanting, hearing, service and association.
5. State why asking a senior for accountability is strength.

## E. Scriptural foundation
Translations from BBT, saved in `research/verification/bss-27.json`. SB 11 translations and purports are completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples (SB 11.23.42, 44, 46, 57, 58 have no purport by Srila Prabhupada himself, except that the SB 11.23.57 purport quotes his own comment from CC Madhya 3.6). Purport points below were written after reading each full purport on vedabase.io; the extracts quoted are saved under `purportExtracts` in the same file. SB 5.6.4 is the verse BIKC chapter 15 cites for discipline of the mind; its translation and purport are given as they stand.

**BG 6.5** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/5/
> "One must deliver himself with the help of his mind, and not degrade himself. The mind is the friend of the conditioned soul, and his enemy as well."
- **Purport point:** "The more one is attracted by sense objects, the more one becomes entangled in material existence. The best way to disentangle oneself is to always engage the mind in Kṛṣṇa consciousness." It quotes: "For man, mind is the cause of bondage and mind is the cause of liberation. Mind absorbed in sense objects is the cause of bondage, and mind detached from the sense objects is the cause of liberation", and concludes, "the mind which is always engaged in Kṛṣṇa consciousness is the cause of supreme liberation."

**BG 6.6** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/6/
> "For him who has conquered the mind, the mind is the best of friends; but for one who has failed to do so, his mind will remain the greatest enemy."
- **Purport point:** "As long as one's mind remains an unconquered enemy, one has to serve the dictations of lust, anger, avarice, illusion, etc. But when the mind is conquered, one voluntarily agrees to abide by the dictation of the Personality of Godhead, who is situated within the heart of everyone as Paramātmā." "For one who takes to Kṛṣṇa consciousness directly, perfect surrender to the dictation of the Lord follows automatically." So the mind's friendship is following the Lord's direction.

**BG 6.34** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/34/
> "The mind is restless, turbulent, obstinate and very strong, O Kṛṣṇa, and to subdue it, I think, is more difficult than controlling the wind."
- Context: Arjuna's honest objection.
- **Purport point:** "The easiest way to control the mind, as suggested by Lord Caitanya, is chanting 'Hare Kṛṣṇa,' the great mantra for deliverance, in all humility… one must engage one's mind fully in Kṛṣṇa. Only then will there remain no other engagements to agitate the mind." The purport calls the mind's strength like an infection that "may surpass the efficacy of medicine", and says the Kaṭha Upaniṣad's picture: intelligence is the driver, the mind the reins, the senses the horses.

**BG 6.35** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/35/
> "…it is undoubtedly very difficult to curb the restless mind, but it is possible by suitable practice and by detachment."
- **Purport point:** the practice is Krsna consciousness: "one engages in nine types of devotional service to the Lord. The first and foremost of such devotional engagements is hearing about Kṛṣṇa. This is a very powerful transcendental method for purging the mind… The more one hears about Kṛṣṇa, the more one becomes enlightened and detached from everything that draws the mind away from Kṛṣṇa." Detachment is the result: "Impersonal spiritual detachment is more difficult than attaching the mind to the activities of Kṛṣṇa." "Hearing of the transcendental activities of Lord Kṛṣṇa is therefore expert treatment for the mad mind, and eating the foodstuff offered to Kṛṣṇa is the appropriate diet for the suffering patient."

**BG 6.36** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/36/
> "…he whose mind is controlled and who strives by appropriate means is assured of success."
- **Purport point:** "one must control the mind by engaging it constantly in the transcendental loving service of the Lord. Unless one is engaged in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, he cannot steadily control the mind." The "appropriate means" is therefore service, not willpower.

**SB 5.6.4** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/6/4/
> "An unchaste woman is very easily carried away by paramours, and it sometimes happens that her husband is violently killed by her paramours. If the yogī gives his mind a chance and does not restrain it, his mind will give facility to enemies like lust, anger and greed, and they will doubtlessly kill the yogī."
- Context: Lord Rsabhadeva's teaching; the verse BIKC (ch. 15) cites for the discipline of the mind.
- **Purport (Srila Prabhupada's words, in full):** "In this verse the word puṁścalī refers to a woman who is easily carried away by men. Such a woman is never to be trusted. Unfortunately, in the present age, women are never controlled. According to the directions of the śāstras, women are never to be given freedom. When a child, a woman must be strictly controlled by her father. When she is young, she must be strictly controlled by her husband, and when she is old, she must be controlled by her elderly sons. If she is given independence and allowed to mingle unrestrictedly with men, she will be spoiled. A spoiled woman, being manipulated by paramours, might even kill her husband. This example is given here because a yogī desiring to get free from material conditions must always keep his mind under control. Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura used to say that in the morning our first business should be to beat the mind with shoes a hundred times, and, before going to bed, to beat the mind a hundred times with a broomstick. In this way one's mind can be kept under control. An uncontrolled mind and an unchaste wife are the same. An unchaste wife can kill her husband at any time, and an uncontrolled mind, followed by lust, anger, greed, madness, envy and illusion, can certainly kill the yogī. When the yogī is controlled by the mind, he falls down into the material condition. One should be very careful of the mind, just as a husband should be careful of an unchaste wife."
- **BIKC (ch. 15) says:** "Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura has advised us to discipline the rascal mind by beating it a hundred times in the morning with shoes, and a hundred times at night with a broom! (SB 5.6.4) As long as our intelligence is not completely fixed at the lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa, such stringent discipline is necessary."
- Use: in the talk (Point 1) and in the handout.

**BG 2.61** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/61/
> "One who restrains his senses, keeping them under full control, and fixes his consciousness upon Me, is known as a man of steady intelligence."
- **Purport point:** "unless one is Kṛṣṇa conscious it is not at all possible to control the senses." The purport's example is Maharaja Ambarisa, who "silently tolerated all the sage's injustices and thereby emerged victorious" over Durvasa Muni, "because of the following qualifications" of engaging every sense in service (SB 9.4.18–20, on bss-24); quoting Baladeva Vidyabhusana: "The senses can be completely controlled only by the strength of devotional service to Kṛṣṇa."
- Use: in the talk; discipline is for fixing consciousness on Krsna.

**BG 3.41** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/41/
> "…in the very beginning curb this great symbol of sin [lust] by regulating the senses…"
- **Purport point:** "Lust is only the perverted reflection of the love of God which is natural for every living entity… Kṛṣṇa consciousness is so powerful that even a late beginner can become a lover of God by following the regulative principles of devotional service… one can begin regulating the senses in Kṛṣṇa consciousness… and turn the lust into love of Godhead." Regulation is a beginning of love, and it is never too late to start.
- Use: in the talk; regulation at the start.

**NOI 1** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/1/
> "A sober person who can tolerate the urge to speak, the mind's demands, the actions of anger and the urges of the tongue, belly and genitals is qualified to make disciples all over the world."
- **Purport point:** each urge is met positively. Speech: "Controlling speech, however, does not mean self-imposed silence… the positive process of kṛṣṇa-kathā, engaging the speaking process in glorifying the Supreme Lord." Mind: "if one always thinks of Kṛṣṇa and how to serve Kṛṣṇa best, one's mind will naturally be controlled"; "To try to create a vacuum in the mind is artificial. The vacuum will not remain." Anger: "We cannot stop anger altogether"; one tolerates insults to oneself, and anger is controlled "when utilized in the service of the Lord" (Hanuman, Arjuna). Tongue and belly: "we should… control the tongue by supplying prasāda"; "The devotee's attitude is that he will eat only when Kṛṣṇa gives him prasāda." Genitals: "the sex impulse, can be controlled when not used unnecessarily"; the Krsna consciousness movement encourages marriage "for the begetting of Kṛṣṇa conscious children." And: "if one is fortunate enough to get the association of a pure devotee, he can easily surpass all the practices for controlling the mind… by engaging in the service of the Supreme Lord under the direction of the bona fide spiritual master." The purport also gives Pariksit Maharaja's comparison of atonement to an elephant's bathing and says: "many spiritual practitioners chant the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra and at the same time commit many forbidden things, thinking that their chanting will counteract their offenses" (the offence nāmno balād yasya hi pāpa-buddhiḥ); "Similarly, certain Christians go to church to confess their sins, thinking that confessing their sins before a priest and performing some penance will relieve them from the results of their weekly sins. As soon as Saturday is over and Sunday comes, they again begin their sinful activities, expecting to be forgiven the next Saturday."
- Use: in the talk; the six urges.

**SB 11.23.42** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/23/42/
> "…it is the mind alone that causes happiness and distress and perpetuates the rotation of material life."

**SB 11.23.46** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/23/46/
> "If one's mind is perfectly fixed and pacified, then tell me what need does one have to perform ritualistic charity and other pious rituals? And if one's mind remains uncontrolled… of what use are these engagements for him?"

**SB 11.23.57–58** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/23/57/ and /58/
> "I shall cross over the insurmountable ocean of nescience by being firmly fixed in the service of the lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa…" (57) "Even when insulted by foolish rascals he remained unswerved from his duty and chanted this song." (58)
- **Purport point** (57 quotes Srila Prabhupada's own comment, from CC Madhya 3.6): "It is not simply a matter of changing dress… The dress is not sannyāsa, but the attitude of service to Kṛṣṇa is." "If one does not completely devote his mind and body to the service of the Lord, he does not actually become a sannyāsī." (58, by disciples): this song may be chanted by those "becoming free from the materialistic way of life."

**SB 9.9.1–3** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/9/9/1/ , /2/ , /3/
> Aṁśumān "performed austerities for a very long time. Nonetheless, he could not bring the Ganges" (9.9.1); Dilīpa "was unable to bring the Ganges" (9.9.2); then "Dilīpa’s son, Bhagīratha, performed very severe austerities to bring the Ganges" (9.9.2), and "mother Ganges appeared before King Bhagīratha" (9.9.3).

**BG 12.8** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/12/8/
> "Just fix your mind upon Me, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, and engage all your intelligence in Me."
- **Purport point:** "A devotee does not live on the material plane – he lives in Kṛṣṇa. The holy name of the Lord and the Lord are nondifferent; therefore when a devotee chants Hare Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa and His internal potency are dancing on the tongue of the devotee. When he offers Kṛṣṇa food, Kṛṣṇa directly accepts these eatables, and the devotee becomes Kṛṣṇa-ized by eating the remnants." Discipline in chanting and prasadam is thus direct contact with Krsna.
- Use: close of the talk; the goal of discipline.

## F. Philosophical explanation
**F.1 Why discipline.** BIKC (ch. 15) says: "Our heads are full of all kinds of nonsense which blocks remembrance of Kṛṣṇa. These contaminations begin with sex desire." "Until we completely reject sense gratification, physical and mental, once and for all, we will not be able to fix the intelligence." "One whose intelligence remains controlled by his mind is no better than an animal or a child; nothing auspicious is possible for him." BIKC reports a letter in which Srila Prabhupada says one free from sex desire is 50 percent liberated (Letter, 15 Feb 1968, as cited in BIKC; Requires source review, check the letter on vedabase before quoting).

**F.2 The mind friend or enemy.** BG 6.5–6. The mind is not the soul; it can be trained. SB 5.6.4 and its purport give the measure of the danger: "An uncontrolled mind and an unchaste wife are the same"; the mind, "followed by lust, anger, greed, madness, envy and illusion, can certainly kill the yogī"; hence Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura's shoes in the morning and broomstick at night. BG 6.34 is honest: it is hard. BG 6.35 answers: practice and detachment, and its purport says what the practice is: the nine processes of devotional service, hearing about Krsna first, with prasadam as the diet; detachment follows automatically. This is why the daily structure matters, and why it is built from chanting, hearing and service.

**F.3 Under a guru.** BIKC: the brahmacari voluntarily agrees to be disciplined by the expert spiritual master, and by his mercy the impossible becomes possible. Application (preacher's): obedience is not for a boss's convenience but a way of receiving help that I cannot give myself.

**F.4 The mind decides.** SB 11.23.42: neither people, nor stars, nor body, but the mind causes happiness and distress. The Avanti brahmana's conclusion: fix on the Lord's lotus feet (SB 11.23.57).

**F.5 Rules for the six urges.** NOI 1: speech, mind, anger, tongue, belly, genitals. A day's structure addresses each, as the NOI 1 purport does: speech (krsna-katha: kirtan, hearing, speaking of Krsna, not silence), mind (fixed on Krsna's service, japa), anger (tolerating insult to oneself; anger used only in the Lord's cause, never as a personal weapon), tongue and belly (prasadam at scheduled times), genitals (for a brahmacari, celibacy, supported by all the above).

**F.6 Practical supports.** Fixed times for japa, mangala-arati, hearing; sleep in time; accountability to a senior; devotee friends. These support chanting; they do not replace it.

**F.7 Misunderstandings.** (1) "Discipline kills spontaneity": BIKC (ch. 14) says, "in the neophyte stage we are not fixed. Sometimes we feel like performing our devotional duties and sometimes we don't. But sādhana-bhakti means that we must follow." "As soon as we think that we are safe, māyā will smash us. So better to be as strict as possible in performing devotional service." (2) "A slip cancels everything": BG 6.35 says practise again. (3) "The shoes and the broom are too strong": the purport of SB 5.6.4 says, "In this way one's mind can be kept under control"; BIKC (ch. 15): "such stringent discipline is necessary."

## I. Core teaching points
1. **Friend or enemy.** BG 6.5–6; SB 5.6.4 with its purport read in full: the uncontrolled mind is like an unchaste wife who can kill her husband; Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura's instruction to beat the mind with shoes a hundred times in the morning and a hundred times with a broomstick before bed.
2. **Hard, but possible.** BG 6.34–36.
3. **Six urges.** NOI 1.
4. **The mind causes distress or peace.** SB 11.23.42, 46.
5. **Perseverance and mercy.** SB 9.9.1–3.

## J. Stories and illustrations
- **The Avanti brahmana** (SB 11.23). He lost his property, was insulted by people, yet concluded that the mind is the cause of happiness and distress (SB 11.23.42), took sannyasa, and resolved to cross the ocean of nescience by serving Krsna's feet (SB 11.23.57–58). **Devotional lesson:** his tolerance was based on service to Krsna, not on grimness; the purport of SB 11.23.57 (Srila Prabhupada's own words) says what the sannyasa he took stands for: "The dress is not sannyāsa, but the attitude of service to Kṛṣṇa is."
- **Bhagiratha** (SB 9.9.1–3). Two generations failed to bring the Ganges; Bhagiratha, with severe austerities, succeeded, and Ganga appeared (SB 9.9.3). The purport of SB 9.9.3 says what he wanted: "to deliver his forefathers, who had been burnt to ashes because of disrespecting Kapila Muni." **Devotional lesson:** his austerity was for the deliverance of others, carried on through three generations, and the success came as the favour of the Ganges, who said she was "very much satisfied"; the lesson for the brahmacari is that discipline is for service to others and the Lord's pleasure, and the result is mercy, not our own power. (The page does not claim more than SB 9.9.1–3 and its purport; read the rest of SB 9.9 before expanding.)
- Everyday image (invented, label it): a gardener who waters daily. It ends in the Krsna conscious point: our daily chanting and hearing waters the creeper of devotion.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections
- *Isn't discipline external?* BIKC (ch. 14): "in the neophyte stage we are not fixed … But sādhana-bhakti means that we must follow. For instance, we may not feel like rising for maṅgala-ārati. But the stricture is there: we must get up. In this way the irrational mind is conquered."
- *I cannot wake early.* Ask a senior for a plan; sleep earlier; there is no shame.
- *What are the shoes and the broom (SB 5.6.4)?* Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura's instruction, as the purport gives it: "in the morning our first business should be to beat the mind with shoes a hundred times, and, before going to bed, to beat the mind a hundred times with a broomstick. In this way one's mind can be kept under control." The beating is of the mind.
- *What if I fail?* BG 6.35: practise again; talk to a senior.
- *I am overwhelmed.* A qualified counsellor can help alongside seniors; Tele-MANAS 14416 (India).

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary
Krsna says the mind is the friend of the conditioned soul and also his enemy (BG 6.5). For the one who has conquered it, it is the best of friends; for the one who has failed, the greatest enemy (BG 6.6). Arjuna says the mind is harder to control than the wind; Krsna agrees it is difficult but says it is possible by practice and detachment (BG 6.34–35). Srimad-Bhagavatam 5.6.4 says: "If the yogī gives his mind a chance and does not restrain it, his mind will give facility to enemies like lust, anger and greed, and they will doubtlessly kill the yogī"; Srila Prabhupada's purport: "An uncontrolled mind and an unchaste wife are the same", and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura "used to say that in the morning our first business should be to beat the mind with shoes a hundred times, and, before going to bed, to beat the mind a hundred times with a broomstick."

Discipline is that practice. We put chanting, hearing and service on a timetable, and we accept the help of a guru and devotees. The Avanti brahmana lost his property and was insulted, yet saw that the mind causes happiness and distress, and resolved to cross the ocean of ignorance by serving Krsna's feet (SB 11.23). Bhagiratha's perseverance brought the Ganga (SB 9.9). The aim of all this is to fix the mind on Krsna (BG 12.8). When you slip, start again and talk to a senior.

## S2. Key ideas
- The mind is friend or enemy.
- Practice and detachment make it a friend.
- Discipline is mercy, not punishment.
- Accountability is strength.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **BG 6.6** · conquered mind is the best of friends · my aim.
- **BG 6.35** · difficult, but possible by practice and detachment · I can begin again.
- **SB 5.6.4** · an unrestrained mind gives facility to lust, anger and greed, and they kill the yogi · shoes in the morning, broom at night (purport).
- **BG 12.8** · fix the mind on Me · the goal of discipline.

## S4. Words to know
- **Discipline:** following a regulated practice.
- **Sadhana:** daily devotional practice.
- **Accountability:** letting a senior check my practice.
- **Nescience:** ignorance.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *Is the mind bad?* No: it is the friend that needs training.
2. *Why discipline if I love Krsna?* The beginner's mood changes; discipline carries us.
3. *What if I miss a week?* Restart at once.
4. *Is a guru's order a burden?* It is help I cannot give myself.
5. *What if I am overwhelmed?* A senior and a qualified counsellor can help.

## S6. Check your understanding
1. When is the mind an enemy (BG 6.6)?
2. What two means are given in BG 6.35?
3. According to SB 11.23.42, what causes happiness and distress?
4. True or false: discipline replaces chanting.
5. What is the goal of discipline (BG 12.8)?

**Answers:** 1. When not conquered. 2. Practice and detachment. 3. The mind. 4. False: it supports chanting. 5. To fix the mind on Krsna.
