## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on trd-15: *pratistha* is the weed; this page is the healthy plant. Position arrives early in temple life: a
two-year resident may be given ten new bhaktas. The world's model of leadership is to be served; Lord Caitanya's model,
shown in His own body at the Gundica temple, is to serve first and most. Krishna states the principle: "whatever action a
great man performs, common men follow" (BG 3.21). This page gives a resident the identity and the method before the
position shapes him.

## C. Central message

A Vaisnava leader is "the servant of the servant of the servant" of Krishna (CC Madhya 13.80). Lord Caitanya showed what
that means when He cleansed the Gundica temple: He gave out the brooms, did the work Himself "just to instruct others,"
and collected more dirt than everyone else. A resident leads by doing first what he asks of others.

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Quote CC Madhya 13.80 and explain why the Lord refuses every social and ashrama identity before stating His own.
2. Tell the Gundica cleansing as CC Madhya 12 tells it, with the four features that define the Lord's leadership (I.2).
3. Explain BG 3.21 with its purport: a leader "cannot teach the public to stop smoking if he himself smokes."
4. Apply NOI 5's principle of dealing with devotees according to their level to leading a mixed group.
5. State the Lord's standard of etiquette between junior and senior (CC Antya 4.166) and apply it to a young leader with senior members.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**CC Madhya 13.80** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/13/80/
> "I am not a brāhmaṇa, I am not a kṣatriya, I am not a vaiśya or a śūdra. Nor am I a brahmacārī, a householder, a vānaprastha or a sannyāsī. I identify Myself only as the servant of the servant of the servant of the lotus feet of Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the maintainer of the gopīs."
- **Context:** Lord Caitanya reciting at the Ratha-yatra; the purport notes it is Padyavali 74, compiled by Rupa Gosvami.
- **Teaching:** The Lord names every identity that gives status and denies each; what remains is service to the servants.
- **Use:** in the talk, the leader's identity.

**BG 3.21** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/21/
> "Whatever action a great man performs, common men follow. And whatever standards he sets by exemplary acts, all the world pursues."
- **Purport points:** "People in general always require a leader who can teach the public by practical behavior. A leader cannot teach the public to stop smoking if he himself smokes. Lord Caitanya said that a teacher should behave properly before he begins teaching. One who teaches in that way is called ācārya." "The teacher cannot manufacture rules against the principles of revealed scriptures." "All such natural leaders have a great responsibility to their dependents."
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 3.20** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/20/
> "Kings such as Janaka attained perfection solely by performance of prescribed duties. Therefore, just for the sake of educating the people in general, you should perform your work."
- **Purport point:** self-realised kings "performed all prescribed activities just to set examples for the people in general."
- **Use:** in the talk: even one who needs nothing for himself works, for the sake of those who follow.

**CC Madhya 12.84, 12.86, 12.88, 12.91, 12.115** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/12/84/
> 12.84: "Indeed, hundreds of devotees were engaged in cleansing all around the temple, and Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu was personally carrying out the operation just to instruct others."

> 12.115: "It appeared as though Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu were cleansing and washing with a hundred hands. He approached every devotee just to teach him how to work."
- **Use:** story J.1, told as the chapter tells it.

**CC Madhya 12.135** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/12/135/
- **Purport point:** "Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura says that Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, as the world leader, was personally giving instructions on how one should receive Lord Kṛṣṇa… within one's cleansed and pacified heart… If a devotee at all wants to cleanse his heart, he must chant and hear the glories of the Lord."
- **Use:** in the talk: the acarya reads the episode as leadership, and as the cleansing of the heart by chanting.

**NOI 5** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/5/

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **Identity before method.** The Lord's verse (CC Madhya 13.80) first removes every identity that confers rank, then
   states the one that remains: servant of Krishna's servants. A leader who begins from this identity cannot be
   corrupted by the position, because the position adds nothing to who he is. One who begins from "I am the coordinator"
   has already planted the weed of trd-15.
2. **Method: do it first, do it most, do it lowest.** At Gundica the Lord gave out the brooms with His own hand, cleansed
   "just to instruct others," was covered in dust, collected more dirt than anyone, and "approached every devotee just to
   teach him how to work" (CC Madhya 12.80–115). Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura calls Him there "the world leader"
   (12.135 purport). The acarya's reading: leadership is shown in the lowest service done best.
3. **Why example is the only teaching that works.** BG 3.21 and purport: a leader cannot teach what he does not do;
   "a teacher should behave properly before he begins teaching." Janaka, who needed nothing, still worked "to set
   examples" (BG 3.20 purport). A resident's juniors will chant as he chants and rise as he rises, whatever he says.
4. **Leading people of different levels.** NOI 5: honour the neophyte in the mind, bow to the initiated, serve the
   advanced. A leader's group has all three; he deals with each "according to their particular status" (purport) and
   gives respect to all (CC Antya 20.21). Where a junior leads seniors, the Lord's rule holds: standard etiquette is not
   transgressed (CC Antya 4.166); the junior coordinates the service and honours the senior's seniority.
5. **The leader's authority.** The purport to BG 3.21: "The teacher cannot manufacture rules against the principles of
   revealed scriptures." A Vaisnava leader's authority is borrowed from sastra and guru; the moment he invents, he has
   nothing to stand on.
6. **Misunderstandings.** *"Servant-leader means never deciding or correcting":* the Lord assigned areas, inspected
   the work and fined those who collected less (12.92–93, 12.132); service includes decision. *"Humility means doing
   everything myself":* He gave each devotee a broom (12.80); the leader engages others. *"Seniority is abolished by
   service":* CC Antya 4.166.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet the residents. Say: "Most of you already lead something: a kirtana, a group of bhaktas, a programme. Today we
   ask Lord Caitanya how He led, on the one day the Caitanya-caritamrta shows Him managing hundreds of devotees."
2. Write on the board: *gopi-bhartuh pada-kamalayor dasa-dasanudasah*, "servant of the servant of the servant" (CC Madhya 13.80).
3. Ask H.2 question 1 for private writing.
4. Ask question 2 aloud; take two or three answers; thank each; begin with 13.80.

**H.2 Opening questions**
1. (Private.) Name one responsibility you hold. Write what you ask of the people under you, and whether you do each of
   those things yourself.
2. Think of the best leader you have served under, inside or outside the temple. What did he *do*, not say, that made you
   follow him?
3. When a leader is seen doing the lowest task, what happens to the team? (Let them answer; the Gundica story confirms it.)

## I. Core teaching points

1. **The leader's identity is servant of the servants.** CC Madhya 13.80: every rank denied, service kept. *Transition:*
   what does that look like in action?
2. **The Lord's four features at Gundica (CC Madhya 12).** (a) He gave each devotee a broom with His own hand (12.80).
   (b) He did the work Himself "just to instruct others" (12.84). (c) He did more than anyone: His pile was "much greater"
   (12.91). (d) He went to each devotee "to teach him how to work" (12.115). Bhaktisiddhanta: "the world leader"
   (12.135 purport). *Transition:* why does this work where orders fail?
3. **Example teaches; words alone do not.** BG 3.21 purport: "A leader cannot teach the public to stop smoking if he
   himself smokes"; Janaka worked to set the example (BG 3.20). *Application (preacher's):* a bhakta leader's own
   mangala-arati attendance is his real class on sadhana. *Transition:* the people he leads are not all alike.
4. **Deal with each by level; honour all.** NOI 5 and purport; CC Antya 20.21. *Transition:* and when the leader is junior
   to some of them?
5. **Etiquette is not transgressed.** CC Antya 4.166: the Lord would not tolerate a junior presuming over a senior (story:
   trd-15). A junior coordinator asks a senior's help; he does not instruct him. *Transition:* the leader's authority is
   borrowed from sastra (BG 3.21 purport).

## J. Stories and illustrations

**J.1 The cleansing of the Gundica temple (CC Madhya 12.73–135).** Told as the chapter tells it. Before the Ratha-yatra,
the Lord asked permission to cleanse the Gundica temple and gathered the devotees. "He then gave each devotee a broom
with His own hand, and taking all of them personally with Him, the Lord went to Guṇḍicā" (12.80). "The Lord cleansed
everything inside the temple very nicely, including the ceiling" (12.82). "Hundreds of devotees were engaged in
cleansing all around the temple, and Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu was personally carrying out the operation just to instruct
others" (12.84). He "washed and cleansed the temple in great jubilation, chanting the holy name of Lord Kṛṣṇa all the
time" (12.85). "The entire beautiful body of the Lord was covered with dust and dirt… At times, when cleansing the
temple, the Lord shed tears, and in some places He even cleansed with those tears" (12.86). He gathered the straw and
dust in His own cloth and threw it outside, and the devotees followed His example (12.88–89). "I can tell how much you
have labored and how well you have cleansed the temple simply by seeing all the straw and dust you have collected
outside," He said (12.90); and "even though all the devotees collected dirt in one pile, the dirt collected by Śrī
Caitanya Mahāprabhu was much greater" (12.91). He allotted areas for a second, finer cleansing (12.92–93). Then, with a
hundred waterpots, they washed; "after the rooms had been cleansed, the minds of the devotees were as clean as the
rooms" (12.105). "It appeared as though Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu were cleansing and washing with a hundred hands. He
approached every devotee just to teach him how to work" (12.115). Finally He Himself sat in the middle picking up the
last straws and grains of sand, saying that whoever had collected less than the others would pay "a fine of sweet
cakes and sweet rice" (12.131–132). The temple became "cool and spotless, like one's cleansed and pacified mind"
(12.133).
- *The acarya's lesson (CC Madhya 12.135 purport):* the Lord, "as the world leader, was personally giving instructions on
  how one should receive Lord Kṛṣṇa… within one's cleansed and pacified heart"; the heart is cleansed by chanting and
  hearing (ceto-darpana-marjanam; SB 1.2.17).
- *Application (preacher's), outside the narration:* the four features in I.2 are a complete method for a temple
  leader: equip everyone, work first, work most, teach each one; and the work itself is a chanting, heart-cleansing
  service, not management for its own sake.
- *Use:* thirty-five minutes; every detail as in the verses.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"If I do the lowest work myself, when do I plan and manage?"**
   - The Lord did both: He allotted areas, inspected, and fined (12.92–93, 12.132) while sweeping. Doing the work first
     earns the right to allot it; it does not replace allotting it.
2. **"Some of my team are senior to me. How do I lead them?"**
   - Coordinate the service, honour the person. CC Antya 4.166: etiquette is not transgressed. Ask a senior's help with
     the plan rather than instructing him; NOI 5: serve the advanced devotee.
3. **"My juniors don't follow my instructions."**
   - BG 3.21 purport: they follow what the leader *does*. Check first whether you do what you ask. Then teach each one
     personally, as the Lord "approached every devotee" (12.115).
4. **"Isn't 'servant of the servant' just a humble phrase, while someone has to be in charge?"**
   - It is an identity, and the Lord held it while being, in Bhaktisiddhanta's words, "the world leader" (12.135 purport).
     Being in charge and being the servant are the same act when the one in charge works most.

## L. Case studies

*Invented case for discussion. Not real people.*

**Case 1: The new bhakta leader.** Vraja, three years a resident, is made leader of eight new bhaktas. He draws up a
strict schedule, checks their rounds daily, and is often absent from mangala-arati himself because of "preaching
preparation." Two bhaktas have started arriving late; one asked to be moved.
- **Dilemma:** His schedule is correct. Why is it failing?
- **Principles:** BG 3.21 (they follow what he does); CC Madhya 12.84 and 12.115 (the Lord worked first and taught each
  one); CC Madhya 13.80 (identity); NOI 5 (each by level).
- **Questions:** What does Vraja's own attendance teach, whatever his schedule says? Which of the four Gundica features
  is missing? What would "giving each a broom" mean here?
- **Analysis:** The Lord's first feature, doing the work "just to instruct others," is absent; the schedule has become an
  instrument of rank. The remedy begins with Vraja's own mangala-arati and with sitting beside each bhakta during japa.
- **Takeaway:** A leader's conduct is the only schedule his team reads.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first; thank each answer)
1. In CC Madhya 13.80, what does the Lord say He is? *Expected:* the servant of the servant of the servant of Krishna's lotus feet.
2. Why, according to 12.84, did the Lord personally carry out the cleansing? *Expected:* "just to instruct others."
3. Whose pile of dirt was the largest (12.91)? *Expected:* the Lord's.
4. What does the BG 3.21 purport say a leader cannot do? *Expected:* teach the public to stop smoking while he smokes.
5. How does NOI 5 tell us to deal with devotees? *Expected:* according to their level: honour the chanter in the mind, bow to the initiated, serve the advanced.
6. What would the Lord not tolerate in CC Antya 4.166? *Expected:* transgressions of standard etiquette.

**M.2 Activities**
- **Option 1: My Gundica (10 min, private).** Each leader lists the tasks he asks of his team and marks which he has done
  himself this month. He chooses one he will do first, this week, in front of the team.
- **Option 2: The four features (20 min).** Groups take one feature each (equip, work first, work most, teach each one)
  and prepare two concrete examples from temple departments; each reports in two minutes.

**M.3 Reflection**
- The Lord's body was covered in dust and He cleansed with His tears (12.86). What does it mean that the "world leader"
  wept while sweeping? What is my service for, if not for that?

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Lord Caitanya described Himself in this way: "I am not a brāhmaṇa, I am not a kṣatriya… Nor am I a brahmacārī, a
householder, a vānaprastha or a sannyāsī. I identify Myself only as the servant of the servant of the servant of the
lotus feet of Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa" (CC Madhya 13.80). He denied every identity that gives rank and kept only service. This is
the identity of a Vaisnava leader.

He showed what it means when He cleansed the Gundica temple before the Ratha-yatra (CC Madhya 12). He "gave each devotee
a broom with His own hand" (12.80). Among hundreds of devotees He "was personally carrying out the operation just to
instruct others" (12.84). His body "was covered with dust and dirt," and He cleansed some places with His tears (12.86).
"The dirt collected by Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu was much greater" than everyone else's (12.91). He "approached every
devotee just to teach him how to work" (12.115), and He also allotted areas and announced a fine of sweets for whoever
collected least (12.92, 12.132). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura calls Him there "the world leader" who was
teaching how to receive Krishna in a cleansed heart (12.135 purport).

Krishna gives the principle: "Whatever action a great man performs, common men follow" (BG 3.21). Prabhupada's purport:
"A leader cannot teach the public to stop smoking if he himself smokes," and a teacher "cannot manufacture rules against
the principles of revealed scriptures." Even King Janaka, who needed nothing, worked "to set examples" (BG 3.20). A
leader deals with devotees according to their level, honouring all (NOI 5; CC Antya 20.21), and a junior leading seniors
keeps standard etiquette, which the Lord "cannot tolerate" being transgressed (CC Antya 4.166). The session's four
features of leadership: equip everyone, work first, work most, teach each one.

## S2. Key ideas

- A Vaisnava leader's identity: servant of the servant of the servant (CC Madhya 13.80).
- The Lord's method at Gundica: a broom to each, work first "to instruct others," the largest pile, teaching each one (CC Madhya 12.80–115).
- People follow what a leader does, not what he says (BG 3.21 and purport).
- Deal with each devotee by level and honour all (NOI 5; CC Antya 20.21); keep etiquette with seniors (CC Antya 4.166).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **CC Madhya 13.80:** "the servant of the servant of the servant of the lotus feet of Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa." *For me:* my position adds nothing to who I am.
- **CC Madhya 12.84:** "personally carrying out the operation just to instruct others." *For me:* do first what I ask.
- **BG 3.21:** "Whatever action a great man performs, common men follow." *For me:* my conduct is my real instruction.

## S4. Words to know

- **Dasa-dasanudasa:** servant of the servant of the servant (CC Madhya 13.80).
- **Gundica-marjana:** the cleansing of the Gundica temple before Ratha-yatra (CC Madhya 12).
- **Acarya:** one who teaches by behaving properly before teaching (BG 3.21 purport).
- **Kanistha, madhyama, uttama:** the three levels of devotees, each dealt with appropriately (NOI 5).

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **If a leader does the lowest work, who manages?** The Lord did both: He allotted areas and fined the slow while sweeping (CC Madhya 12.92, 12.132). Working first earns the right to allot.
2. **How do I lead people senior to me?** Coordinate the service, honour the person; ask a senior's help rather than instructing him (CC Antya 4.166; NOI 5).
3. **My juniors don't follow my instructions.** They follow what you do (BG 3.21 purport). Check your own conduct first, then teach each one personally (12.115).

## S6. Check your understanding

1. List the identities the Lord denies in CC Madhya 13.80, and the one He keeps.
2. Why, in 12.84, did the Lord personally carry out the cleansing?
3. Whose pile of dirt was the largest (12.91)?
4. What example does the BG 3.21 purport give of a leader who cannot teach?
5. True or false: NOI 5 says all devotees should be dealt with in exactly the same way.

**Answers:** 1. Brahmana, ksatriya, vaisya, sudra; brahmacari, householder, vanaprastha, sannyasi; kept: servant of
the servant of the servant of Krishna (CC Madhya 13.80). 2. "Just to instruct others" (12.84). 3. The Lord's (12.91).
4. One who smokes cannot teach the public to stop smoking (BG 3.21 purport). 5. False: according to their level, honouring
all (NOI 5).
