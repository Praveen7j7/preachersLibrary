## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on vl3-10 (purpose decides the value of an act) and vl3-01 (training the mind): this page applies that lesson to two debates and does not repeat it.

IYF asked for "games in classes for VL-2 and VL-3" and for "higher topics… satisfying their intellectual need" (Vision
tab; `research/batch-criteria.md` §0). A debate does both. These two motions are chosen because VL3 students meet them
weekly: the friend who says "feed the poor, don't build temples", and their own phone. Both are tests of the lesson
VL3-10 taught: the value of an act is decided by whose purpose it serves.

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| VL1 | Technology and the modern mind as relevance talks (VL1 lifestyle topics) |
| VL2 (2015 syllabus) | The same two debates, as exercises in reasoning |
| **VL3** (here) | The debates run properly, concluded from scripture, each ending in a practice |

**Not done here:**
- "Proving" God from intelligent design or attacking evolution (the deck's "Science discovers spirituality" slides).
  VL2-02 explains why; it is not repeated. The Gita's claim does not need it.
- The deck's statistics on war deaths, suicide, divorce and lifespan: unsourced or dated; not used (O).
- Condemning technology or social work. Both debates end with "use it rightly", not "give it up".
- Proposed topic 9 ("Attention, phones and sadhana", Stage 2 §7) in full; Debate 2's practice points towards it.

## C. Central message

Debate 1: service to the Supreme includes every living being, because He is in every heart (SB 3.29.21–27), and
service to man that forgets Him cannot reach the root of his suffering (SB 4.31.14; BG 5.25). Debate 2: a tool has no
purpose of its own; the same knife cuts vegetables or throats (Srila Prabhupada, Chicago 1975), and the devotee's
intelligence is to dovetail every tool in Krsna's service (NOI 3 purport) while taking only his quota (ISO 1).

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. Argue either side of each motion fairly for two minutes, with at least one real point for the side they do not hold.
2. State Kapiladeva's teaching that worship of the Deity while neglecting living beings is "simply imitation" (SB
   3.29.21–22), and the complementary point that service to the root reaches every branch (SB 4.31.14).
3. Explain why the Gita calls the Krsna conscious person "the best philanthropist" (BG 6.32 purport) and why
   prasadam distribution is where manav seva and madhav seva meet (BG 3.13; SB 3.29.24 purport).
4. Give Srila Prabhupada's knife and dictaphone analogies and the Isopanisad's "quota" and "side by side"
   principles as the tradition's view of technology (ISO 1, 9, 11; NOI 3 purport).
5. Name one personal practice from each debate: one act of service that is both; one rule for their phone.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** SB 3.29.21–27 and SB 4.31.14 carry Debate 1; NOI 3 purport, ISO 1 and ISO 11 carry Debate 2; the rest
supports particular arguments.

### Debate 1: Manav seva or Madhav seva

**SB 3.29.21** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/29/21/
> "I am present in every living entity as the Supersoul. If someone neglects or disregards that Supersoul everywhere and engages himself in the worship of the Deity in the temple, that is simply imitation."
- **Context:** Lord Kapila teaching His mother Devahuti the kinds of devotional service (3.29.7–27).
- **Teaching:** Deity worship that ignores living beings is imitation.
- **Purport point:** "One who worships the Deity in the temple and does not show respect to others is a devotee on
  the material platform, in the lowest stage of devotional service… To serve everything means to engage everything in
  the service of Kṛṣṇa."
- **Application (preacher's):** The strongest text *for* the Manav seva side, and it is in the Bhagavatam. Give it
  to that team. Note the purport's definition of service: to engage everything in Krsna's service, not to replace Him
  with man.
- **Use:** in the conclusion.

**SB 3.29.22** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/29/22/
> "One who worships the Deity of Godhead in the temples but does not know that the Supreme Lord, as Paramātmā, is situated in every living entity's heart, must be in ignorance and is compared to one who offers oblations into ashes."
- **Purport point:** "persons who profess to belong to some religious sect but who do not feel the presence of the
  Supreme Personality of Godhead in every living entity… are in the mode of ignorance"; "a devotee should not ignore
  any living entity."
- **Use:** in the conclusion.

**SB 3.29.24** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/29/24/
> "My dear mother, even if he worships with proper rituals and paraphernalia, a person who is ignorant of My presence in all living entities never pleases Me by the worship of My Deities in the temple."
- **Purport point:** "As such, temple worship necessarily includes distribution of prasāda. It is not that one should
  create a temple in his private apartment… offer something to the Lord, and then eat."
- **Application (preacher's):** The purport names prasadam distribution as the built-in human face of temple worship.
  This is where the two sides of the debate meet.
- **Use:** in the conclusion.

**SB 3.29.25** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/29/25/
> "Performing his prescribed duties, one should worship the Deity of the Supreme Personality of Godhead until one realizes My presence in his own heart and in the hearts of other living entities as well."
- **Purport point:** "No one should be lazy under the plea of devotional service."
- **Use:** background.

**SB 3.29.27** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/29/27/
> "Therefore, through charitable gifts and attention, as well as through friendly behavior and by viewing all to be alike, one should propitiate Me, who abide in all creatures as their very Self."
- **Purport point:** "The impersonalist sometimes accepts a poor individual soul as being daridra-nārāyaṇa, meaning
  that Nārāyaṇa, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, has become poor. This is a contradiction… We cannot treat the
  Supreme Personality of Godhead as an inferior who is dependent on our charitable gifts." *Māna* to superiors, *dāna*
  to those in need.
- **Application (preacher's):** The verse commands charity and friendliness to all as worship of the Lord *in* them;
  the purport corrects the slogan "service to man is service to God" where it makes the poor man God. Both halves are
  needed in the conclusion.
- **Use:** in the conclusion; the verse to remember for Debate 1.

**SB 4.31.14** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/31/14/
> "As pouring water on the root of a tree energizes the trunk, branches, twigs and everything else, and as supplying food to the stomach enlivens the senses and limbs of the body, simply worshiping the Supreme Personality of Godhead through devotional service automatically satisfies the demigods, who are parts of that Supreme Personality."
- **Application (preacher's):** The strongest text *for* the Madhav seva side: serve the root and every part is
  served. Give it to that team. (The verse speaks of demigods; the principle of root and branch extends to all parts;
  label the extension as the preacher's.)
- **Use:** in the conclusion.

**BG 5.25** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/5/25/
> "Those who are beyond the dualities that arise from doubts, whose minds are engaged within, who are always busy working for the welfare of all living beings and who are free from all sins achieve liberation in the Supreme."
- **Purport point:** "to act to revive this consciousness within the entire human society is the highest welfare
  work… A person engaged only in ministering to the physical welfare of human society cannot factually help anyone.
  Temporary relief of the external body and the mind is not satisfactory."
- **Application (preacher's):** The verse itself praises those "always busy working for the welfare of all living
  beings"; the purport ranks the kinds of welfare. Quote both; the Manav side will otherwise hear only the second.
- **Use:** in the conclusion.

**BG 6.32** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/32/
> "He is a perfect yogī who, by comparison to his own self, sees the true equality of all beings, in both their happiness and their distress, O Arjuna!"
- **Purport point:** "Since the perfect yogī tries to broadcast the importance of becoming Kṛṣṇa conscious, he is the
  best philanthropist in the world… a devotee of the Lord always looks to the welfare of all living entities, and in
  this way he is factually the friend of everyone."
- **Use:** in the conclusion.

**BG 3.13** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/13/
> "The devotees of the Lord are released from all kinds of sins because they eat food which is offered first for sacrifice. Others, who prepare food for personal sense enjoyment, verily eat only sin."
- **Purport point:** "in order for people to become happy in all respects, they must be taught to perform the easy
  process of saṅkīrtana-yajña".
- **Application (preacher's):** Prasadam distribution feeds the body and sanctifies the eater: the one act that is
  wholly manav seva and wholly madhav seva.
- **Use:** in the conclusion.

**BG 17.20** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/17/20/
> "Charity given out of duty, without expectation of return, at the proper time and place, and to a worthy person is considered to be in the mode of goodness."
- **Purport point:** "Charity to the poor is sometimes given out of compassion, but if a poor man is not worth giving
  charity to, then there is no spiritual advancement. In other words, indiscriminate charity is not recommended."
- **Application (preacher's):** The Gita grades charity; it does not forbid it. Use for the Manav side's question
  "then should we give nothing?" The answer is: give, with intelligence, and give what lasts.
- **Use:** in K.

**BG 12.13-14** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/12/13-14/
> "One who is not envious but is a kind friend to all living entities… such a devotee of Mine is very dear to Me."
- **Use:** background; the devotee is "a kind friend to all living entities" by definition.

### Debate 2: Technology, boon or curse

**NOI 3** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/3/
> "…(4) acting according to regulative principles… These six principles undoubtedly assure the complete success of pure devotional service."
- **Purport point:** "For example, at present we are using a dictaphone. The materialist who invented this machine
  intended it for businessmen or writers of mundane subject matters. He certainly never thought of using the
  dictaphone in God's service, but we are using this dictaphone to write Kṛṣṇa conscious literature… Thus the devotee
  can understand that since nothing is independent of Kṛṣṇa's energy, everything should be dovetailed in His service.
  Endeavor executed with intelligence in Kṛṣṇa consciousness is called utsāha, or enthusiasm."
- **Application (preacher's):** Srila Prabhupada's own practice: the inventor's intention does not fix the tool's use;
  the user's intelligence does. The clearest text for the conclusion of Debate 2.
- **Use:** in the conclusion; the passage to remember for Debate 2.

**ISO 1** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/iso/1/
> "Everything animate or inanimate that is within the universe is controlled and owned by the Lord. One should therefore accept only those things necessary for himself, which are set aside as his quota, and one should not accept other things, knowing well to whom they belong."
- **Application (preacher's):** The measure for technology: *quota*. Not "none", not "all I can get": what is
  necessary, knowing whose it is. A student's phone passes or fails this test daily.
- **Use:** in the conclusion.

**ISO 9** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/iso/9/
> "Those who engage in the culture of nescient activities shall enter into the darkest region of ignorance. Worse still are those engaged in the culture of so-called knowledge."
- **Purport point:** "vidyā, or knowledge, is even more dangerous when mistaken or misguided… a godless civilization
  directed toward the so-called advancement of education is more dangerous than a civilization in which the masses of
  people are less 'educated.'"
- **Application (preacher's):** The strongest text *for* the Curse side. Give it to that team, with its exact
  wording: the danger is *godless* culture of knowledge, not knowledge.
- **Use:** in the conclusion.

**ISO 11** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/iso/11/
> "Only one who can learn the process of nescience and that of transcendental knowledge side by side can transcend the influence of repeated birth and death and enjoy the full blessings of immortality."
- **Purport point:** "Material science can discover the nuclear bomb to accelerate the process of death, but it cannot
  discover anything that can protect man from the cruel hands of old age, disease and death."
- **Application (preacher's):** The resolution of Debate 2 in one word: *side by side*. Material knowledge is not
  rejected; it is kept in its place beside the knowledge that answers death.
- **Use:** in the conclusion; the verse to remember.

**NOI 2** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/2/
> "One's devotional service is spoiled when he becomes too entangled in the following six activities: (1) eating more than necessary or collecting more funds than required; (2) over-endeavoring for mundane things that are very difficult to obtain; (3) talking unnecessarily about mundane subject matters…"
- **Application (preacher's):** Items 1–3 describe a phone used badly: *atyāhāra* (consuming more than needed),
  *prayāsa* (over-endeavour), *prajalpa* (idle talk). The Upadesamrta anticipated the feed.
- **Use:** in the conclusion and P.

**CC Madhya 23.105** (deeper verse; the talk uses only its plain meaning: dry renunciation is not asked) — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/23/105/
> "Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu then told Sanātana Gosvāmī about proper renunciation according to a particular situation, and the Lord forbade dry renunciation and speculative knowledge in all respects."
- **Purport point:** "This is the technique for understanding śuṣka-vairāgya and yukta-vairāgya… Dry renunciation is
  forbidden by Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu."
- **Application (preacher's):** "Throw away your phone" is *śuṣka-vairāgya*, dry renunciation, which Sri Caitanya
  forbade. The devotee's path is *yukta-vairāgya*: engaged renunciation.
- **Use:** in the conclusion.

**BG 18.48** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/48/
> "Every endeavor is covered by some fault, just as fire is covered by smoke. Therefore one should not give up the work born of his nature, O son of Kuntī, even if such work is full of fault."
- **Application (preacher's):** Srila Prabhupada quotes this verse in the Geneva conversation on technology and
  unemployment (O). Every tool has smoke; that is not a reason to put out the fire.
- **Use:** in K.

**SB 5.5.1** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/5/1/
> "…one who has been awarded this human form should not work hard day and night simply for sense gratification, which is available even for dogs and hogs that eat stool. One should engage in penance and austerity to attain the divine position of devotional service…"
- **Application (preacher's):** The Curse side's deepest point: technology that only multiplies sense gratification
  spends the human form on what dogs and hogs already have. Srila Prabhupada's "The dog is also running; they are also
  running… because they can run on by car, they are thinking they are civilized" (Chicago, 8 Jul 1975) is this verse in
  an image.
- **Use:** in the conclusion.

**Srila Prabhupada, Morning Walk, Chicago, 8 Jul 1975** — Verified against the transcript,
https://vedabase.io/en/library/transcripts/750708mwchi/. The *Back to Godhead* version ("Srila Prabhupada Speaks Out:
Seeing Technology in a Spiritual Light", Preaching Encyclopedia `…/0243`) is an edited text; quote the transcript:
"There is a nice car. Why shall I condemn it? Utilize it for Kṛṣṇa." (The Māyāvādīs, he adds, say "Give it up"; he
says "No.") "With a knife you are cutting vegetables and utilizing, but if you use it for cutting your throat, that is
bad… So they are using the knife for cutting their own throat." "The dog is also running; they are also running…
because they can run on by car, they are thinking they are civilized."

**Srila Prabhupada, conversation, Geneva (c. 1974)** — Partially verified (*Back to Godhead*, "On Technology and
Unemployment", `…/0235`). "Technology is not freedom. Rather, it is a free way to hell." "Everyone should be employed;
otherwise there will be trouble." He quotes BG 18.48. Use for the Curse side's social argument (machines and idleness);
note it is one conversation about Indian farm policy, not a general ruling.

## F. Philosophical explanation

### F.1 How to run a debate at VL3 (both debates)

- **Teams of 3–4**, chosen a week ahead; each team is briefed with the verses for *its* side (E) and told it will
  also hear the other side's verses in the conclusion. Students argue the side they are given, not the one they hold.
- **Format (30 min):** opening statements 3 min each; two rebuttals 2 min each; one question from the floor to each
  side; closing 2 min each. A timekeeper. No interruptions; "we will hear with mood of learning and not challenging"
  (IYF classroom norms).
- **Judging (3 criteria, announced in advance):** scripture used accurately; the other side's best point answered,
  not ignored; manner (BG 17.15: truthful, pleasing, beneficial, not agitating).
- **Conclusion (15 min):** the preacher, not a vote. IYF's recordings have HG Sundar Gopal Prabhu give a "Debate
  Conclusion"; hear one before you give yours (O). The conclusion names what each side got right, then resolves from
  scripture, then gives one practice.

### F.2 Debate 1: "Manav seva or Madhav seva?"

**Motion:** *Service to humanity is the real service to God.*

**The Manav seva side's best case (give them these):** SB 3.29.21–22: Deity worship that neglects the Lord in living
beings is "simply imitation", "oblations into ashes"; 3.29.27 commands "charitable gifts and attention… friendly
behavior" to all as worship of the Lord in them; BG 5.25 praises those "always busy working for the welfare of all
living beings"; BG 12.13–14: the devotee is "a kind friend to all living entities". Plus experience: hunger is real,
and a hungry man cannot hear philosophy.

**The Madhav seva side's best case (give them these):** SB 4.31.14: water the root, and every branch is nourished;
BG 5.25 purport: "A person engaged only in ministering to the physical welfare of human society cannot factually help
anyone. Temporary relief of the external body and the mind is not satisfactory"; BG 6.32 purport: the Krsna conscious
person is "the best philanthropist in the world"; SB 3.29.27 purport: the poor man is not *daridra-nārāyaṇa*; the
Supreme is not dependent on our charity. Plus experience: a century of welfare has not reduced anxiety.

**The conclusion (preacher's, from the texts):**
1. *The motion is half true, and the half matters.* Kapiladeva says in His own voice that worship which neglects
   living beings is imitation (3.29.21). Any devotee who sneers at social service has read neither this chapter nor
   its purport ("a devotee should not ignore any living entity").
2. *But the motion inverts the order.* The Lord is in every being; service to beings is worship of Him *because He
   is there* (3.29.27), not because man is God (the purport's *daridra-nārāyaṇa* correction). Serve the root and the
   branches live (4.31.14); water only leaves and the tree dies with its leaves green for a day.
3. *What man needs most.* BG 5.25 purport: the root of suffering is forgetfulness of Krsna; relief of the body is
   real and temporary. The devotee who gives Krsna consciousness is "the best philanthropist" (6.32 purport) because he
   treats the cause, and the purport adds that he "always looks to the welfare of all living entities": body included.
4. *Where both meet.* Prasadam. SB 3.29.24 purport: "temple worship necessarily includes distribution of prasāda";
   BG 3.13: offered food frees the eater from sin. ISKCON's free-meal programmes are this verse at scale (give no
   figures you have not checked). Likewise books, education, hospitals run in devotion: *manav seva* done as *madhav
   seva*.
5. *The grading of charity.* BG 17.20: charity in goodness is given as duty, at the right time and place, to a worthy
   recipient. Not "give nothing", not "give anything": give what lasts, and give food, knowledge and shelter in a way
   that connects the receiver to the Lord.
6. *Resolution:* **Madhav seva, done rightly, is the only manav seva that reaches the whole man; manav seva, done
   without Madhav, is sincere and incomplete; and the devotee who neglects man while worshipping God has misread
   both.**

### F.3 Debate 2: "Technology: boon or curse?"

**Motion:** *Technology has done humanity more harm than good.*

**The Curse side's best case (give them these):** ISO 9: the culture of godless knowledge leads to "the darkest region
of ignorance"; its purport: people "more unhappy than ever before" despite education; SB 5.5.1: the human form spent on
what "dogs and hogs" already have; NOI 2: *atyāhāra*, *prayāsa*, *prajalpa*, the three things a feed does to a mind;
Srila Prabhupada, Geneva: "Technology is not freedom. Rather, it is a free way to hell"; machines and idleness. Plus
experience: the phone in their pocket and the hours it takes. Plus the one piece of research in O (attention).

**The Boon side's best case (give them these):** NOI 3 purport: Srila Prabhupada dictating his books on a dictaphone
and explaining why; CC Madhya 23.105 purport: dry renunciation is forbidden by Sri Caitanya; BG 18.48: every endeavour
has smoke, fire is still fire; Srila Prabhupada, Chicago: "There is a nice car. Why shall I condemn it? Utilize it
for Kṛṣṇa." Plus experience: medicine, clean water, this class's recorded lectures, the
Vedabase on which every verse here was checked.

**The conclusion (preacher's, from the texts):**
1. *A tool has no purpose; the hand does.* The knife (Chicago 1975), the dictaphone (NOI 3 purport). The inventor's
   intention does not bind the user; "everything should be dovetailed in His service". The motion asks the wrong
   question: not *what has technology done* but *what have we done with it*.
2. *The real danger is named precisely.* ISO 9 does not say knowledge is dangerous; it says *godless* culture of
   knowledge is. The deck's phrase "technology makes spirituality appear unnecessary" is this purport's point and may be
   used with the verse.
3. *The measure is quota.* ISO 1: take what is necessary, "knowing well to whom they belong". NOI 2's first three
   items describe excess, not use. A phone used for class, maps, calls and the Vedabase is quota; the same phone at
   1 a.m. is *atyāhāra*.
4. *Side by side.* ISO 11: learn material knowledge and transcendental knowledge together; material science cannot
   answer death (purport). Technology is a boon exactly as far as it is kept beside the knowledge that answers what
   it cannot.
5. *Not dry renunciation.* CC Madhya 23.105 purport: Sri Caitanya forbade *śuṣka-vairāgya*. "Delete everything" is
   usually pride or despair; *yukta-vairāgya* is harder and is the instruction.
6. *Resolution:* **Technology is a boon in a hand that knows its quota and its purpose, and a curse in a hand that
   does not; the debate is not about technology but about the hand, and the hand is trained by sadhana.**

### F.4 Misunderstandings to prevent

- "Devotees oppose social service." SB 3.29.21–27 forbids that reading.
- "Devotees oppose technology." NOI 3 purport, CC Madhya 23.105 purport and Srila Prabhupada's own practice forbid it.
- "Science is the enemy." Not said anywhere here; VL2-02's rule applies: no anti-science tone, no intelligent-design
  "proofs" (the deck's slides are dropped).
- "Winning the debate is the point." The judging criteria reward accuracy and manner; the conclusion is the point.

### F.5 Pitch for VL3

Practical and encouraging: both debates end in one small, kept practice (P). Terms: *seva*, *yukta-vairāgya*,
*atyāhāra*, *prajalpa*, *quota*.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet, say your name, and say today is a fair contest of ideas, not of people.
2. Write the two motions on the board: "Manav seva (service to humanity) or Madhav seva (service to God)?" and "Technology: boon or curse?"
3. Ask H.2 question 1; take two or three answers.
4. Explain the rules in one line each: time limits, respect, every side must say what the other gets right.
5. Say that both sides will be thanked and the conclusion will come from scripture.

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the room's own life)
1. Last week, did you help anyone in a way that cost you something? Why did you do it?
2. What is one tool, such as a phone, a tractor, a spreadsheet, a pen, that changed your work for the better, and one way it also took something?
3. Do you decide what a tool does, or does it decide for you?

- **Opening question (Debate 1):** "Last week, did you help anyone in a way that cost you something? Did you think of
  Krsna while doing it?" No answers aloud. "Today we ask whether those are two things or one."
- **Opening question (Debate 2):** "Hands up if your phone was the first thing you touched this morning." (Most.)

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

> **Before you teach:** hear one of HG Sundar Gopal Prabhu's debate conclusions (O) and decide in advance what each
> side got right; the students will remember whether you were fair.

1. **"So feeding the poor is a waste if we don't preach to them?"**
   - No. SB 3.29.27 commands charity and friendliness to all; BG 17.20 grades charity, it does not forbid it. Feed
     them, and feed them prasadam (BG 3.13); then it is both services at once. What the Gita calls incomplete is relief
     that *stops* at the body (BG 5.25 purport).
2. **"Then why build marble temples instead of hospitals?"**
   - SB 3.29.24 purport: temple worship "necessarily includes distribution of prasāda"; a temple that only worships
     has misread Kapila. Temples feed, teach and shelter; the question "why not both?" has the answer "both". For the
     money question itself, vl3-09, K.4.
3. **"Isn't 'service to man is service to God' exactly what the Bhagavatam says in 3.29.27?"**
   - Read the whole verse: propitiate *Me*, "who abide in all creatures". Service to beings is worship of the Lord
     *in* them; the purport rejects the step that makes the poor man God. Direction matters: through man to God, not
     man instead of God.
4. **"Srila Prabhupada said technology is 'a free way to hell'. Doesn't that settle it?"**
   - He said it in a conversation about farm mechanisation and idleness (Geneva; Partially verified), and in Chicago
     he said of the car "Why shall I condemn it? Utilize it for Kṛṣṇa", and he dictated his books on a machine (NOI 3
     purport). The texts together say: a curse in a godless hand, a boon in a devotee's.
5. **"Should I give up my phone, then?"**
   - CC Madhya 23.105 purport: dry renunciation is forbidden. ISO 1: take your quota. Decide what your phone is *for*
     and when it is off (P). Giving it up is easier to say and harder to mean than regulating it.
6. **"Everything has side effects (BG 18.48). So why single out technology?"**
   - Agreed; the verse says fire has smoke and one does not therefore put out the fire. The debate's lesson is the
     same: judge the hand and the purpose, not the tool.
7. **"The deck says science has proved God through intelligent design."**
   - This course does not rely on that claim (VL2-02, O). The Gita's case for God does not need a contested
     scientific movement, and a biology student will dismantle it. Drop the slide.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Two debates, one lesson: the value of an act is decided by whose purpose it serves.

**Manav seva or Madhav seva?** (*Manav seva* means service to people; *Madhav seva* means service to God, Madhava being a name of Krsna.) Lord Kapila says that worshipping the Deity while neglecting living beings is "simply imitation", because He is present in every heart (SB 3.29.21); He also tells us to please Him, who abides in all creatures, through charity and friendly behaviour (SB 3.29.27). But the poor man is not God: the purport to SB 3.29.27 calls the slogan *daridra-narayana* ("the poor man as God") a contradiction, since the Supreme does not depend on our charity. Relief of the body alone is temporary; the highest welfare work is to revive Krsna consciousness in human society (BG 5.25 purport). Watering the root nourishes every branch (SB 4.31.14). Prasadam distribution (sharing food offered to Krsna) is where both services become one act (BG 3.13).

**Technology: boon or curse?** Srila Prabhupada dictated his books on a dictaphone, saying that everything should be dovetailed in Krsna's service (NOI 3 purport), and compared technology to a knife that cuts vegetables or throats. The Isopanisad gives the measure: take your quota (ISO 1), and learn material and spiritual knowledge "side by side" (ISO 11). Dry renunciation is not asked (CC Madhya 23.105 purport); a trained hand is the instruction. Choose one rule for your phone and one service you can do as service to God as well as to people.

## S2. Key ideas

- Worship that neglects living beings is imitation (SB 3.29.21–22).
- Serve the Lord *in* all beings; the poor man is not God (SB 3.29.27 and purport).
- Water the root; the branches live (SB 4.31.14). Bodily relief alone is temporary (BG 5.25 purport).
- Prasadam: one act, both services (BG 3.13).
- A tool serves the hand: the knife, the dictaphone (NOI 3 purport).
- Take your quota (ISO 1); learn material and spiritual knowledge side by side (ISO 11).
- Regulate, don't renounce dryly (CC Madhya 23.105 purport).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **SB 3.29.21:** "I am present in every living entity as the Supersoul. If someone neglects or disregards that Supersoul everywhere and engages himself in the worship of the Deity in the temple, that is simply imitation." *For me:* my worship is tested by how I treat people.
- **SB 4.31.14:** "As pouring water on the root of a tree energizes the trunk, branches, twigs and everything else… simply worshiping the Supreme Personality of Godhead through devotional service automatically satisfies the demigods…" *For me:* serve the root.
- **ISO 1:** "…One should therefore accept only those things necessary for himself, which are set aside as his quota…" *For me:* my phone has a quota.

## S4. Words to know

- **Manav seva / Madhav seva:** service to humanity / service to God (Madhava, a name of Krsna).
- **Daridra-narayana:** "the poor man as God", a slogan the Bhagavatam's purport corrects (SB 3.29.27).
- **Prasadam:** food offered to the Lord; feeding someone prasadam is both services at once (BG 3.13).
- **Yukta-vairagya:** engaged renunciation: using things for Krsna; opposite of *suska-vairagya*, dry renunciation.
- **Atyahara, prajalpa:** consuming more than needed; idle talk (NOI 2): what a feed does to a mind.
- **Quota:** what is necessary for me, knowing whose everything is (ISO 1).

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Is feeding the poor a waste if we don't preach to them?** No. SB 3.29.27 commands charity and friendliness to all. Feed them, and make it prasadam (BG 3.13); then it is both services.
2. **Why temples and not hospitals?** Both. Temple worship "necessarily includes distribution of prasāda" (SB 3.29.24 purport); temples feed, teach and shelter.
3. **Doesn't the Bhagavatam say service to man is service to God?** It says serve the Lord *who abides in* all beings (SB 3.29.27). Through man to God; not man instead of God.
4. **Should I give up my phone?** Dry renunciation is forbidden (CC Madhya 23.105 purport). Decide what it is for and when it is off (ISO 1: quota).
5. **Didn't Srila Prabhupada call technology "a free way to hell"?** In one conversation about machines and idleness. He also said of a car, "Why shall I condemn it? Utilize it for Kṛṣṇa", and wrote his books on a dictaphone (NOI 3 purport).
