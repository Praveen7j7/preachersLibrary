# Overcoming depression

> **VL1 · 45–60 min · review: sources checked, awaiting senior review · SENSITIVE (mental health).** The head reviews this page after the build.
> **Read K before anything else.** The preacher is not a therapist. This talk is about discouragement and low
> mood, which everyone knows; it says plainly, more than once, that depression as an illness deserves
> professional care, that such care is fully compatible with spiritual life, and when to refer. IYF has no
> folder for this topic; the page is built from the Gita's own account of Arjuna's collapse and recovery.

## A. Topic overview

| | |
|---|---|
| Batch / level | VL1 (Vedic Level 1), a stand-alone talk |
| Suggested duration | 45–60 min (G gives a 30-minute cut) |
| Preparation time | About 120 min, the first time, including reading K twice |
| Difficulty | Introductory to deliver; demanding in care |
| Prerequisites | None for the audience. For the preacher: VL1-16 (the mind) and VL1-06 (suffering) |
| Audience | Students and young professionals under academic, career and family pressure; some are discouraged, some grieving, and some in the room are clinically depressed or have been. Assume this |
| Teaching objective | Use Arjuna's collapse and recovery to show that despair is not a disgrace, that asking for help is the first act of recovery, and that the Gita's tools (truth about who I am, regulated life, engaging the mind, a shelter) support a person alongside, never instead of, the care an illness needs |

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on vl1-16 and vl1-18: the chariot and BG 6.5-6 (the mind as friend or enemy) and the "light against darkness" picture of fear are taken as known; here only Arjuna's collapse, asking for help and the care statement are taught. Do not retell them.

Low mood, discouragement and the feeling that nothing is worth doing are common in this age group, and the
word "depression" is used for all of it, from a bad fortnight to a serious illness. IYF's title asks the
preacher to speak to this. The Gita opens with a man whose body shakes, whose bow slips from his hand, who sits
down and weeps and says he cannot go on (BG 1.28–30, 1.46, 2.1). It does not mock him; it listens, and then
teaches. That makes it the right text, if handled with care.

How the subject grows at other levels:

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **VL1** (here) | Arjuna's collapse as a mirror; despair is not disgrace; asking for help; four supports; when to refer |
| VL1-06, VL1-11, VL1-16, VL1-18 | Suffering, positive thinking, the mind, fear: each takes one thread further |
| VL3 | Small daily supports (routine, sleep, movement, hearing) and a trusted mentor |
| SPS | The devotee in dryness and discouragement; tolerance (BG 2.14) as a practice; the role of association (VL3-08) |
| DPS | Noticing a struggling team member, listening, and referring to professional help (DPS-02) |
| BSS | Homesickness and discouragement in training; the guide's role |

**Not done here:**
- Diagnosing anyone, or explaining depression's causes.
- Reading BG 2.3 ("do not yield to this degrading impotence") at a depressed person. See E.
- Claiming that chanting, diet or faith cures depression. They support; they do not replace treatment.
- Any "mode of ignorance" framing of a listener's mood.

## C. Central message

The Gita begins with a strong man who collapses, weeps and says he cannot go on, and it does not shame him; it
listens and helps him stand again. Despair is not a disgrace, asking for help is the first step of recovery, and
spiritual life walks alongside the care an illness needs, never in its place.

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. Describe Arjuna's state in chapter 1 in their own words (body, mind, action) and say why the Gita treats it
   with respect.
2. Explain what Arjuna did that began his recovery: he spoke, and he asked someone he trusted (BG 2.7).
3. Name four supports the Gita offers for a low mind: truth about the self (2.14), regulated habits (6.17),
   engaging the mind (6.5–6), and hearing/a shelter (SB 1.7.7, 9.22), and say what each does and does not do.
4. State the difference between a low patch and an illness that needs professional care, and know where to turn.
5. Choose one small, kind step for this week.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** the Arjuna narrative (BG 1.28–30, 1.46, 2.1, 2.7, 18.73) carries the talk; BG 2.14, 6.5–6, 6.17 and
SB 1.7.7 give the supports. BG 2.3 is for the preacher's understanding, not for the room.

**BG 1.28–30**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/1/28/ (and 29, 30)
> 1.29: "My whole body is trembling, my hair is standing on end, my bow Gāṇḍīva is slipping from my hand, and my skin is burning."
> 1.30: "I am now unable to stand here any longer. I am forgetting myself, and my mind is reeling. I see only causes of misfortune…"
- **Context:** Arjuna sees his relatives arrayed for battle.
- **Teaching:** The text records a collapse of body and mind in a capable man: trembling, weakness, the grip
  failing, "my mind is reeling", seeing only misfortune.
- **Application (preacher's):** Read these verses aloud. Many listeners will recognise the body-and-mind
  description. The Gita does not begin with a saint; it begins here.
- **Use:** in the talk (opening).

**BG 1.46**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/1/46/
> "Arjuna… cast aside his bow and arrows and sat down on the chariot, his mind overwhelmed with grief."
- **Teaching:** He stops acting; he sits down.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 2.1**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/1/
> "Seeing Arjuna full of compassion, his mind depressed, his eyes full of tears, Madhusūdana, Kṛṣṇa, spoke the following words."
- **Teaching:** The text's own words: "his mind depressed, his eyes full of tears". Krsna sees and then speaks.
- **Purport point:** The purport says material compassion and tears for the body are "signs of ignorance of the
  real self" and that "compassion for the eternal soul is self-realization". It also notes that Arjuna wanted
  Krsna "to kill the demon of misunderstanding".
- **Application (preacher's):** The purport diagnoses the *root* (misidentification), not the person's worth.
  Use that distinction carefully: it explains why the Gita's medicine is knowledge, but it is never a reason to
  tell a sad person they are "in ignorance". Keep the purport for your understanding; in the room, say "Krsna
  saw him, and spoke".
- **Use:** in the talk (translation); purport background only.

**BG 2.3**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/3/
> "O son of Pṛthā, do not yield to this degrading impotence. It does not become you. Give up such petty weakness of heart and arise, O chastiser of the enemy."
- **Teaching:** Krsna's first words are a sharp call to Arjuna's strength.
- **Purport point:** Krsna "considered that sort of magnanimity mere weakness of heart"; abandoning the battle
  "would be committing an infamous act".
- **Application (preacher's):** **Do not use this verse on a depressed person.** Krsna speaks it to a warrior,
  as his friend and charioteer, knowing him completely, and Arjuna's reply (2.7) shows it drew out a request
  for help, not shame. A preacher does not have that knowledge or relationship. "Snap out of it" from us wounds.
  Know the verse; do not quote it in this talk.
- **Use:** background only (K).

**BG 2.7**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/7/
> "Now I am confused about my duty and have lost all composure because of miserly weakness. In this condition I am asking You to tell me for certain what is best for me. Now I am Your disciple, and a soul surrendered unto You. Please instruct me."
- **Teaching:** Arjuna names his state honestly and asks someone he trusts for help.
- **Purport point:** "By nature's own way the complete system of material activities is a source of perplexity
  for everyone." The remedy: "approach a bona fide spiritual master to get free from the perplexities of life".
- **Application (preacher's):** This is the turning point of the whole Gita, and it is a request for help. The
  strongest man on the field says "I am confused… please instruct me." Asking is not weakness; in the text it
  is the beginning of everything. The same applies to asking a mentor, a doctor or a counsellor.
- **Use:** in the talk (the key verse).

**BG 2.14**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/14/
> "…the nonpermanent appearance of happiness and distress, and their disappearance in due course, are like the appearance and disappearance of winter and summer seasons… one must learn to tolerate them without being disturbed."
- **Purport point:** The examples are duties done despite the weather: bathing in a cold month, cooking in
  summer. Tolerance means carrying on with what matters, not feeling nothing.
- **Application (preacher's):** For a low patch: "this is a season" is true and steadying. For an illness: a
  season that does not pass needs a doctor; do not use the verse to tell someone to wait it out.
- **Use:** in the talk, with the caution.

**BG 6.5–6**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/5/ · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/6/
> 6.5: "One must deliver himself with the help of his mind, and not degrade himself. The mind is the friend of the conditioned soul, and his enemy as well."
- **Purport point (6.6):** An unconquered mind means one "has to serve the dictations of lust, anger, avarice,
  illusion, etc."
- **Application (preacher's):** A low mind replays the same thoughts. The text says the same mind can be
  turned into a friend; engaging it (a task, a sound, a service) is one support. Not a cure.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 6.17**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/17/
> "He who is regulated in his habits of eating, sleeping, recreation and work can mitigate all material pains by practicing the yoga system."
- **Purport point:** Extravagance in "eating, sleeping, defending and mating" obstructs progress; the devotee's
  habits are regulated.
- **Application (preacher's):** The Gita puts the body's routine first: eating, sleeping, recreation, work. Low
  mood wrecks all four, and all four worsen low mood. Regular meals, sleep and daylight are spiritual as well as
  medical advice.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 1.7.7**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/7/7/
> "Simply by giving aural reception to this Vedic literature, the feeling for loving devotional service to Lord Kṛṣṇa… sprouts up at once to extinguish the fire of lamentation, illusion and fearfulness."
- **Purport point:** Lamentation, illusion and fear are "the primary symptoms of the material disease"; hearing
  removes "this false complexity of materialism".
- **Application (preacher's):** Hearing is a gentle practice a low person can do when they can do nothing else:
  listen. Offer it as support, not as a promise of cure.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 9.22**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/9/22/
> "…to them I carry what they lack, and I preserve what they have."
- **Teaching:** The Lord looks after His devotee's needs.
- **Application (preacher's):** A shelter: "I am not carrying this alone." Do not turn it into "pray and it
  will go away".
- **Use:** in the talk, one line.

**BG 18.58**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/58/
> "If you become conscious of Me, you will pass over all the obstacles of conditioned life by My grace…"
- **Use:** background; the promise of passage, for the preacher's own steadiness.

**BG 18.73**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/73/
> "My dear Kṛṣṇa, O infallible one, my illusion is now gone. I have regained my memory by Your mercy. I am now firm and free from doubt and am prepared to act according to Your instructions."
- **Context:** Arjuna's last words in the Gita.
- **Teaching:** The man who sat down weeping stands and acts, "by Your mercy".
- **Purport point:** "This illusion can be overcome by the mercy of the Lord or by the mercy of a pure devotee."
- **Application (preacher's):** Recovery is real and the text shows it. It came through a relationship and
  seventeen chapters of patient conversation, not through a slogan. Hold that out as hope, not as a timetable.
- **Use:** in the talk (close).

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** The Gita records a collapse (1.28–30, 1.46, 2.1), honours the request for help (2.7),
   and shows recovery (18.73). Its supports for the mind are: truth about who I am and the seasons of
   experience (2.14), regulated habits (6.17), engaging the mind so it becomes a friend (6.5–6), hearing
   (SB 1.7.7) and a shelter (9.22). These are supports for everyone; for an illness they stand *alongside*
   treatment.
2. **Why it matters.** Two errors harm people: "depression is a spiritual failure" and "spiritual practice is
   enough". The first shames; the second delays care. The Gita's own story cuts both: Arjuna's despair is not
   mocked, and his recovery comes through help he asked for.
3. **Connections.** The mind as friend or enemy (VL1-16); suffering and tolerance (VL1-06); fear (VL1-18);
   the eight tips (VL1-11). At VL3/DPS, the mentor relationship the Gita models (2.7 purport) becomes concrete.
4. **Consequences.** A listener who hears this talk well will (a) stop despising themselves for low moods,
   (b) know that asking for help is the first act of strength, (c) have four small supports to try, and
   (d) know when to see a professional and how to help a friend get there.
5. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - **"Arjuna snapped out of it when Krsna scolded him."** He did not; 2.3 drew out 2.7, a request for help,
     and recovery took the whole Gita.
   - **"A devotee shouldn't be depressed."** The text's hero was "his mind depressed, his eyes full of tears"
     (2.1). Great devotees have grieved (VL1-06: Bhisma, Kunti).
   - **"Depression is the mode of ignorance / bad karma."** Never say this to or about a person. It is not the
     preacher's to diagnose, and it shames. The Gita's medicine for Arjuna was knowledge given with love.
   - **"Chanting/diet cures depression."** Supports, not cures. Say so.
   - **"Tolerate it (2.14) and it will pass."** A low patch passes; an illness that persists needs a doctor.
6. **Pitch for VL1.** Use "low", "down", "discouraged", "depressed" as the audience does; distinguish once,
   clearly, the illness from the mood. No Sanskrit is needed. Tone: warm, slow, unhurried; this is not a
   motivational talk.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet, say your name, and say plainly that low mood is common and nobody is asked to share anything personal.
2. Say where help is (Tele-MANAS 14416 in India, free, 24x7; elsewhere, a local helpline or doctor), so no one waits for it.
3. Read BG 1.28-30 slowly, without comment; do not ask for hands.
4. Ask H.2 question 1 as a quiet thought, not for answers.
5. Give the transition and begin with what the Gita does not do.

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the room's own life; thought, not shared)
1. Think of a friend who went quiet. What would have helped them most?
2. What small thing lifts your own mood a little (a walk, a call, music)?
3. When someone is low, what do people often say that does not help?

- **Opening:** Read BG 1.28–30 and 1.46 slowly, without comment. Then: "This is how the most famous spiritual
  text in India begins. Not with a saint. With a strong, capable man whose body is shaking, whose hands won't
  hold, who sits down and cannot go on. Does that sound like anyone you know? Does it sound like you, some
  weeks?" Pause. Do not ask for hands.
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** Dev used to top his class. This semester he cannot get out of bed for
  lectures, has stopped answering friends, and tells himself he is lazy and useless. His friends tell him to
  "be positive". What does Dev need to hear, and what does he not?
- **Transition:** "Let's see what Krsna did with Arjuna, and what he did not do."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** Everyone here will be, or will sit beside, someone like Dev. What we say
  then matters. (Adapt the example: Dev can be any student or young worker in any country; replace the campus counsellor with a local doctor or counsellor.)

## I. Core teaching points

1. **The Gita begins with a collapse.** Body, mind and action all fail (1.28–30, 1.46; 2.1 "his mind
   depressed"). *Transition:* and the text does not sneer.
2. **Despair is not disgrace.** Krsna sees and speaks; the hero of the Gita wept. *Example:* Dev calling himself
   useless; the text would not. *Transition:* what changed things?
3. **Asking is the first act of recovery.** "I am confused… please instruct me" (2.7). *Example:* the strongest
   man present asks. For us: a mentor, a friend, a doctor. *Transition:* what did Arjuna receive?
4. **Four supports for a low mind.** Seasons (2.14): this passes, and carry on with what matters. Routine
   (6.17): eat, sleep, move, work, regularly. Engage the mind (6.5–6): a task, a sound, a service. Hear, and
   rest in a shelter (SB 1.7.7; 9.22): "I am not carrying this alone." *Each is support; none is a cure for an
   illness.* *Transition:* does it work?
5. **Recovery is real, and it took time and a relationship.** 18.73: "my illusion is now gone… by Your mercy."
   Seventeen chapters, not a slogan. *Transition:* to one kind step and how to help a friend.

## J. Stories and illustrations

- **Arjuna's collapse and recovery** (BG 1.28–2.10; 18.73). Tell it from the verses: his body (1.28–29), his
  mind (1.30), his action (1.46), Krsna seeing him (2.1), his request (2.7), and his standing up at the end
  (18.73). Add nothing. *Connection:* the whole talk.
- **Contemporary (invented, labelled):** Dev, above. Use to practise "what to say and what not to say".
- **For later levels, not here:** Bhisma and Kunti on suffering (VL1-06) are already in that talk; the devotee's
  own dryness and discouragement (SPS/DPS) belong where there is a mentor relationship in place.
- No "miracle recovery" stories, from any source. If a preacher knows a real person's story, they do not tell it
  without that person's permission, and never as proof that practice replaces treatment.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

> **Before you teach (read twice):**
> 1. **Say this early, in your own words:** "Everyone has low patches. Some people have depression, which is an
>    illness, like diabetes is an illness. If your low mood has lasted weeks, if you cannot sleep or eat or
>    function, if you feel hopeless or have thoughts of harming yourself, please see a doctor or counsellor, and
>    tell someone you trust today. That is not a failure of faith; it is the right and brave thing to do, and
>    spiritual life walks alongside it." *(Pastoral guidance; general descriptions, not a diagnostic tool.)*
> 2. **Referral:** Tele-MANAS, the Government of India's free 24×7 mental-health helpline: **14416** or
>    **1-800-891-4416** (https://telemanas.mohfw.gov.in/). Also your campus counsellor or a general physician.
>    Keep the number on your slide.
> 3. **If someone speaks of wanting to die or harm themselves:** take it seriously every time; stay with them;
>    do not leave them alone; do not argue theology; help them contact the helpline, a doctor or emergency
>    services, and involve someone who can stay with them. Follow up the next day.
> 4. **In the room:** never analyse a person's situation publicly; never say "it's your karma", "you lack
>    faith", "just chant more", or "be positive". Acknowledge, and talk afterwards.
> 5. **You are not a therapist.** Your gifts here are presence, honesty, the text's compassion, and a good
>    referral. That is enough, and it is a lot.

1. **"Isn't depression a lack of faith or a spiritual weakness?"**
   - No. The Gita's hero is described as "his mind depressed, his eyes full of tears" (2.1) and is not shamed.
     Illness of the mind is illness. Faith supports recovery; its absence does not cause depression, and its
     presence does not guarantee immunity.
2. **"Krsna told Arjuna to stop being weak (2.3). Shouldn't we just toughen up?"**
   - Krsna spoke as Arjuna's intimate friend who knew him fully, and the effect was that Arjuna *asked for help*
     (2.7), after which Krsna taught him patiently for seventeen chapters. None of us has Krsna's knowledge of
     another person. "Toughen up" from us is a wound. The text's method was listening and teaching.
3. **"Will chanting cure my depression?"**
   - Chanting, hearing and routine support a low mind (6.5–6, 6.17, SB 1.7.7). If what you have is an illness,
     they support treatment; they do not replace it. Taking treatment is itself a spiritual act of caring for
     the body you have been given.
4. **"My friend is depressed. What should I say?"**
   - Less than you think. Be present; ask how they are and listen; don't fix or preach; help with practical
     things (a meal, a walk, accompanying them to a doctor); check the red flags above and help them get care;
     keep coming back. Arjuna's recovery began with someone who stayed on the chariot with him.
5. **"I've been told antidepressants or therapy are 'unspiritual'."**
   - They are not. Medicine for the mind is medicine. The Gita asks for regulated eating, sleeping and work
     (6.17) and the use of intelligence (VL1-16); accepting a doctor's help is both.
6. **"Why would God let me feel like this?"**
   - *Pastoral:* honest pain honestly spoken is better than pretended peace; Arjuna spoke his. VL1-06 takes up
     the question of suffering properly. Here: you are not alone in it (9.22), and help is at hand.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: Dev.** A former topper now cannot get up for lectures, avoids friends, sleeps badly, eats little, and
calls himself lazy and useless. It has been six weeks. Friends say "be positive".
- **Dilemma:** What does Dev need first, and what should his friends stop saying?
- **Principles:** K (red flags: duration, sleep, appetite, function, self-contempt); BG 2.1 (the text does not
  shame); BG 2.7 (asking); BG 6.17 (routine as support).
- **Questions:** Which signs suggest more than a low patch? What should a friend do this week? What is the
  place of spiritual practice for Dev right now?
- **Suggested analysis:** Dev's friends should help him see a doctor or counsellor and go with him; "be positive"
  stops. Routine (meals, sleep, a daily walk), company, and gentle hearing can accompany care. Nobody tells him
  his mood is his fault.
- **Takeaway:** Presence and referral first; philosophy serves care, never replaces it.

**Case 2: The low fortnight.** Meher failed an exam, feels flat and discouraged, is sleeping and eating normally,
and keeps replaying the failure.
- **Principles:** BG 2.14 (a season; carry on with what matters); 6.5–6 (engage the replaying mind); 6.17
  (keep the routine); SB 1.7.7 (hear).
- **Questions:** How is Meher's situation different from Dev's? What would "this is a season" do for her? What
  small engagement would help the replaying mind?
- **Takeaway:** For a low patch, the Gita's supports are the right medicine; and if it does not lift, see Case 1.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first; praise every try)
1. How does BG 2.1 describe Arjuna? *"His mind depressed, his eyes full of tears."*
2. Did Krishna shame him? *No: He listened, then taught.*
3. What did Arjuna do in BG 2.7 that began his recovery? *He named his confusion and asked for instruction.*
4. Name two habits BG 6.17 says to regulate. *Eating, sleeping, recreation, work.*
5. When should a low mood be taken to a doctor or counsellor? *When it lasts weeks, affects sleep, eating or function, or brings hopelessness or thoughts of self-harm; tell someone today.*

**M.2 Activity (about 8 minutes): "What I would say to a friend"**
1. In pairs, 2 min: imagine a friend like Dev (invented, not anyone present); each writes two helpful sentences.
2. 3 min: swap and read; the listener says which sentence sounds kind and why.
3. 2 min: write one practical offer (a walk, going with them to a doctor).
4. 1 min: volunteers share a sentence. Nobody shares personal experience. Close with the helpline.

**M.3 Reflection**
- **Reflection (private, optional):** When I am low, what do I tell myself about myself? Would the Gita say that
  to Arjuna?
- **Application:** If a friend were like Dev, what two things would I do this week? *(Nobody needs to share
  anything personal.)*

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

The Bhagavad-gita does not begin with a saint. It begins with Arjuna, a strong and capable man, whose body trembles, whose bow slips from his hand, who sits down on his chariot "his mind depressed, his eyes full of tears" and says he cannot go on (BG 1.28-30, 1.46, 2.1). Krishna does not mock him. He listens, and when Arjuna says "I am confused… please instruct me" (BG 2.7), He teaches him patiently for seventeen chapters, until Arjuna stands up again (BG 18.73). Feeling low is not a disgrace, and asking for help is the first step of recovery.

The Gita's supports walk alongside the care an illness needs. Moods come and go like seasons (BG 2.14). A regular routine of eating, sleeping, recreation and work steadies us (BG 6.17). The mind can be a friend when it is engaged well (BG 6.5-6). Hearing and a shelter give strength (SB 1.7.7).

If low mood has lasted weeks, or you cannot sleep, eat or function, or you have thoughts of harming yourself, please tell someone today and see a doctor or counsellor. That is not a failure of faith; it is the brave and right thing to do. Tele-MANAS (14416, free, 24x7) helps in India; elsewhere, use your local helpline or doctor. If a friend is struggling, be there, listen, do not preach, and help them reach care.

## S2. Key ideas

- The Gita's hero collapsed and wept (BG 1.28–30, 2.1). Despair is not a disgrace.
- Asking for help was the turning point (BG 2.7). Asking is strength.
- Moods are seasons (BG 2.14): they pass, and we carry on with what matters.
- Routine matters: regular eating, sleeping, movement and work (BG 6.17).
- The same mind that drags us down can be made a friend by engaging it (BG 6.5–6).
- Hearing helps (SB 1.7.7), and I am not carrying this alone (BG 9.22).
- Depression as an illness needs professional care, like any illness. Spiritual life walks beside it.

## S3. Verses to remember

- **BG 2.7:** "Now I am confused about my duty and have lost all composure… I am asking You to tell me for
  certain what is best for me… Please instruct me." *For me:* the strongest man on the field asked for help.
  So can I.
- **BG 2.14:** Happiness and distress come and go "like the appearance and disappearance of winter and summer
  seasons". *For me:* this is a season, not my identity; and a season that does not pass needs a doctor.
- **BG 18.73:** "My illusion is now gone… I am now firm and free from doubt." *For me:* recovery is real; it
  took time and a relationship, not a slogan.

## S4. Words to know

- **Low mood / low patch:** feeling down, flat or discouraged for a while; everyone has these.
- **Depression (the illness):** low mood that lasts weeks, with changes in sleep, appetite, energy and the
  ability to function, often with hopelessness. Needs professional care, like any illness.
- **Support:** something that helps alongside care (routine, hearing, company). Not a cure.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Is depression a lack of faith?**
   No. The Gita's hero is described as "his mind depressed, his eyes full of tears" (BG 2.1) and is not shamed.
   Illness of the mind is illness.
2. **Krsna told Arjuna not to be weak. Shouldn't I just toughen up?**
   Krsna spoke as Arjuna's closest friend who knew him completely, and the result was that Arjuna asked for
   help (BG 2.7), after which Krsna taught him patiently. Nobody else has that knowledge of you. Being told to
   "toughen up" is not the Gita's method; listening and teaching is.
3. **Will chanting cure my depression?**
   Chanting, hearing and routine support a low mind. If you have an illness, they support treatment; they do not
   replace it. Taking treatment is itself caring for the body you were given.
4. **My friend seems depressed. What do I say?**
   Less than you think. Be there, ask and listen, don't fix or preach, help with practical things, and help them
   see a doctor or counsellor; go with them. Keep coming back.
5. **Are medicines and therapy "unspiritual"?**
   No. Medicine for the mind is medicine. The Gita asks for regulated eating, sleeping and work (BG 6.17) and
   for using our intelligence; accepting a doctor's help is both.
6. **Where can I get help?**
   Tele-MANAS, free, 24×7: 14416 or 1-800-891-4416. Also your campus counsellor or any doctor.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. In BG 2.1, Arjuna is described as: (a) calm and ready, (b) "his mind depressed, his eyes full of tears",
   (c) angry at Krsna.
2. True or false: Arjuna recovered the moment Krsna told him to stop being weak.
3. What does Arjuna do in BG 2.7 that begins his recovery?
4. Name two of the four habits BG 6.17 says should be regulated.
5. In your own words: when should a low mood be taken to a doctor or counsellor?

**Answers:** 1. (b) (BG 2.1). 2. False: his recovery came through asking for help (BG 2.7) and seventeen
chapters of teaching, ending in BG 18.73. 3. He names his confusion honestly and asks for instruction (BG 2.7).
4. Any two of eating, sleeping, recreation and work (BG 6.17). 5. When it has lasted weeks, or affects sleep,
eating or the ability to function, or brings hopelessness or thoughts of self-harm; then tell someone today.
