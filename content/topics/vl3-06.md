# Importance of Sravanam & Art of Making effective notes

> **VL3 · 180 min · review: sources-checked.** *Śravaṇam*, hearing, is the first of the nine processes of devotion (SB 7.5.23–24) and the VL3
> motive includes "hearing prescribed lecture" (batch-criteria §3). This page gives the texts' case for hearing from devotees and a
> concrete method for taking notes in a class so that hearing lasts past the door. SPS-11 treats the art of hearing in depth; this page
> begins it. IYF has no notes folder for this row; the IYF lecture "Sravanam is the cure for madness" (HG Sundar Gopal Prabhu) is the
> "hear next". One research item on note-taking is cited with its limits (O). No special sensitivity.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

VL3-05 taught the hearing chain and the daily page. This page takes the other half of *śravaṇam*: the class, the lecture, the kirtan.
VL3 participants hear a great deal (a weekly lecture, daily audio, Sunday kirtan) and retain little, because no one has distinguished
hearing from being present while sound occurs. The texts make the distinction: hearing is "attentively and submissively" (SB 1.1.2),
"with faith and without envy" (BG 18.71), "with mind attached to Me" (BG 7.1), from those "no longer interested in the false, temporary
glorification" (SB 10.1.4), as a disciple (BG 2.7). Notes are the VL3 practice that trains those qualities: a pen keeps the mind in the
room, a question keeps it submissive, a review keeps the class alive for a week, and one shared point (BG 18.68) makes the hearer a
speaker. SPS-11 will deepen the inner art; this page gives the outer skill.

Builds on VL3-05: its hearing chain (SB 1.2.16–21) and "book and person are identical" are only recalled in one line; this page teaches the class, the lecture and the note page.

## C. Central message

Hearing about Krsna from His devotees is the first of the nine practices of devotion (SB 7.5.23–24) and, in this age, the Gita's own
"suitable practice" for controlling the mind (BG 6.35 purport); it is the right medicine for the conditioned soul (SB 10.1.4), and the
hour spent in it is the one hour the sun cannot take away (SB 2.3.17). Hear attentively, submissively, with faith (SB 1.1.2; BG 18.71),
and make the class last with simple notes: a question before, a page during, a review and one shared point after.

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. Name hearing as the first of the nine processes (SB 7.5.23–24) and say from the BG 6.35 purport why it is the practice for this age.
2. State the four qualities of hearing the texts ask for (attentive and submissive, SB 1.1.2; faithful and without envy, BG 18.71; as a
   disciple, BG 2.7; from the right source, SB 10.1.4) and describe Pariksit Maharaja as the model hearer (SB 1.19.15–16).
3. Explain what the gṛhamedhī hears instead (SB 2.1.2–3) and why SB 2.3.17 calls the hour of hearing the one the sun cannot take.
4. Use the three-part note method (before: one question; during: one page in three columns; after: a one-line review and one shared
   point) on a live class.
5. Answer "I remember nothing after class", "notes distract me", "I just record it" and "can I hear online lectures instead?"

**SB 7.5.23–24**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/5/23-24/
> "Prahlāda Mahārāja said: Hearing and chanting about the transcendental holy name, form, qualities, paraphernalia and pastimes of Lord Viṣṇu, remembering them, serving the lotus feet of the Lord, offering the Lord respectful worship with sixteen types of paraphernalia, offering prayers to the Lord, becoming His servant, considering the Lord one’s best friend, and surrendering everything unto Him (in other words, serving Him with the body, mind and words) — these nine processes are accepted as pure devotional service…"
- **Context:** Prahlada answers his father's demand to say "the best thing" he has learnt.
- **Purport point:** Prahlada considered "what he had learned from his spiritual master was the best of all teachings"; "to execute

- **Teaching:** Nine processes; hearing is named first.

**BG 6.35**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/35/
> "Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa said: O mighty-armed son of Kuntī, it is undoubtedly very difficult to curb the restless mind, but it is possible by suitable practice and by detachment."
- **Purport point (VL3-01):** In this age the practice is the nine processes, and "the first and foremost of such devotional engagements
  is hearing about Kṛṣṇa. This is a very powerful transcendental method for purging the mind of all misgivings."
- **Use:** in the talk; why hearing is "first and foremost".

**SB 1.2.16–18**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/16/ (and following)
> 1.2.16: "O twice-born sages, by serving those devotees who are completely freed from all vice, great service is done. By such service, one gains affinity for hearing the messages of Vāsudeva."

- **Purport point (1.2.16):** The servants of God "preach according to particular time and place" and "urge people to become devotees";

**SB 2.1.2–3**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/2/1/2/ and /3/

> 2.1.3: "The lifetime of such an envious householder is passed at night either in sleeping or in sex indulgence, and in the daytime either in making money or maintaining family members."
- **Purport point (2.1.2):** The *gṛhamedhī* "set aside the question of the ultimate miseries of life"; they have "many subject matters

- **Teaching:** One who wants freedom from all miseries must hear about, glorify and remember the Lord.
- **Application (preacher's):** The three verbs give the shape of the note method (hear in class, speak one point, review to remember).

- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 10.1**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/10/1/

- **Application (preacher's):** "Listen again": repetition is the Gita's own method, and the answer to "I’ve heard this before".
- **Use:** in the talk and K.6.

**BG 13.26**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/13/26/
> "Again there are those who, although not conversant in spiritual knowledge, begin to worship the Supreme Person upon hearing about Him from others. Because of their tendency to hear from authorities, they also transcend the path of birth and death."
- **Purport point:** "This hearing process is very important. Lord Caitanya… gave great stress to hearing because if the common man
  simply hears from authoritative sources he can progress"; "faithful hearing from an authoritative person will help one transcend this
  material existence".
- **Use:** in the talk; hearing is enough to begin.

**BG 18.70–71**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/70/ and /71/
> 18.70: "And I declare that he who studies this sacred conversation of ours worships Me by his intelligence."
> 18.71: "And one who listens with faith and without envy becomes free from sinful reactions and attains to the auspicious planets where the pious dwell."
- **Purport point (18.71):** A devotee "will hold open class, and in that class not all the students are expected to be devotees"; those
  who hear "with faith" and are not envious "become at once free from all sinful reactions"; "simply by hearing Bhagavad-gītā, even a
  person who does not try to be a pure devotee attains the result of righteous activities".
- **Teaching:** The hearer's two qualities: faith, and freedom from envy.
- **Application (preacher's):** In a class, envy shows as auditing the speaker (L, Case 2).
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 18.68**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/68/
> "For one who explains this supreme secret to the devotees, pure devotional service is guaranteed, and at the end he will come back to Me."
- **Application (preacher's):** The ground for the method's last step: tell one person one point from the class. Hearing becomes
  speaking, and speaking fixes hearing.
- **Use:** in the talk; the "after" step.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **Hearing is first.** Prahlada names nine processes and puts hearing first (SB 7.5.23–24); Suta Gosvami's chain begins with the urge
   to hear (SB 1.2.16–17) and names "regular attendance in classes" as the remedy (1.2.18); Sukadeva tells Pariksit to "hear about,
   glorify and also remember" (2.1.5); Krsna Himself says "hear" (BG 7.1) and "listen again" (10.1); and the BG 6.35 purport makes
   hearing "the first and foremost" practice for controlling the mind in this age. Hearing is not the beginners' step before practice;
   it is the practice.
2. **Why it works.** The messages "are nondifferent from Him" (SB 1.2.17 purport, VL3-05); hearing them is "the right medicine for the
   conditioned soul" (10.1.4); it lifts one above hunger, thirst, fear and lamentation (4.29.40); it fixes devotion (BG 10.1 purport);
   and even one who merely hears from authorities "transcend[s] the path of birth and death" (13.26). The hour of class is the hour the sun
   cannot subtract (2.3.17).
3. **What is heard instead.** SB 2.1.2–3 describes the *gṛhamedhī*: "many subject matters for hearing", nights in sleep and sex, days
   in money and family. A VL3 participant will recognise the feed. The purport's remedy is not to stop hearing but to add the one
   subject that was missing. Say this without contempt; it is the Bhagavatam's description of a condition, not an insult to anyone's
   family.
4. **What kind of hearing.** The texts give the hearer's qualities, and together they are a method:
   - *attentively and submissively* (SB 1.1.2): the mind in the room;
   - *with faith and without envy* (BG 18.71): not auditing the speaker;
   - *as a disciple* (BG 2.7; 4.34: "inquire… submissively"): with a question, not a verdict;
   - *with mind attached to Me* (BG 7.1): the subject is Krsna, whatever the title;
   - *eager* (SB 4.29.40: "great eagerness"): arriving wanting something;
   - *from the right source* (SB 10.1.4; CC Madhya 22.131 at VL3-05): devotees in the line, through IYF's classes and prescribed lectures.
   Pariksit is the model (1.19.15–16): seven days left, and he wants only to go on hearing.
5. **Notes as the VL3 training of those qualities.** *Application (preacher's):* a pen is the simplest way to keep the mind in the room
   (attention), a written question is the simplest way to arrive as a disciple (submission), a review within a day is the simplest way to
   remember (2.1.5: "remember"), and one point told to someone is the simplest way to glorify (2.1.5: "glorify"; BG 18.68: "explains…
   to the devotees"). The method is not a study technique borrowed from college; it is the three verbs of SB 2.1.5 given a page.
6. **The method (teach it with a demonstration).**
   - **Before (two minutes):** write the date, the speaker, the verse or topic if announced, and *one question I bring* (BG 2.7). Arrive
     early enough to do this; phone silent and face down.
   - **During (one page, three columns):**
     - *Left (narrow):* the verses quoted, exactly as given (chapter and text), so you can open them later (VL3-05: verse, translation,
       purport).
     - *Middle (wide):* the points, in the speaker's order, in your own short phrases; a story's name and its one-line point, not the whole
       story; a quotation only if you can get it exactly, otherwise "check".
     - *Right (narrow):* your marks: **?** for a question, **!** for what struck you, **→** for *what this asks of me*. Nothing else.
     Do not write everything; write what you would want in a week. If the mind leaves, write the next verse number and return (BG 6.26,
     VL3-01).
   - **After (ten minutes, same day):** read the page once; write one line at the bottom, *the class in one sentence*; pick one **→**
     as this week's application; take one **?** to your facilitator or the speaker (BG 4.34); and tell one person one point (BG 18.68).
     On the sadhana card, note the class and the lecture heard.
   - **Weekly:** before the next class, reread last week's line. "Listen again" (BG 10.1).
7. **Hearing at home.** The daily "prescribed lecture" and kirtan follow the same shape in miniature: one question, one line at the end,
   one point told. For kirtan, SB 2.1.5's "glorify" is the practice itself; follow the words, respond, and let the note be a single line
   afterwards. (SPS takes kirtan further.)
8. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - "Hearing is passive; the real practice is chanting." SB 7.5.23–24 puts hearing first; the BG 6.35 purport calls it "first and
     foremost"; chanting and hearing are one pair in Prahlada's list.
   - "Notes mean I’m not absorbing." Notes done this way *are* the absorbing; the page is a tool for attention, not a transcript.
   - "I’ll record it and listen later." Recordings are good for review; they are not hearing. SB 4.29.40's "great eagerness" is a quality
     of the present hour. Pariksit did not ask Sukadeva for a recording.
   - "Any lecture online is as good as class." SB 10.1.4 and the 22.131 purport say the source matters, and SB 3.25.25 says the hearing
     is "in the association of pure devotees". At VL3: IYF's classes and prescribed lectures; the wider question is SPS material.
   - "I’ve heard this topic before." BG 10.1: "Listen again." The hearer is different each time.
9. **Pitch for VL3.** Practical, encouraging. Terms: *śravaṇam*, *kīrtanam*, *smaraṇam*, *paramparā* (defined once). The session includes
   a real ten-minute hearing exercise with the three-column page, so everyone leaves having done it once.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet and say the session helps us keep more of what we hear; no one will be tested on memory.
2. Ask H.2 question 1; let the silence sit, then take two or three answers.
3. Thank the honest blanks as warmly as the good answers.
4. Say the Bhagavatam calls that hour the one the sun cannot take from you (SB 2.3.17).
5. Give the transition line and begin with Prahlada's list.

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the group's own lives)
1. What was the last class or lecture you heard about, in one sentence?
2. When you remember something well from a talk, what helped: a story, a note, telling someone?
3. What do you do with your hands and phone while someone speaks?

- **Opening question:** "What was last Sunday’s class about? One sentence." Let the silence sit a moment; take the two or three who
  remember, and thank the honest blanks. "The Bhagavatam says that hour was the one hour the sun could not take from you (SB 2.3.17).
  Today is about keeping it."
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** Aarav attends every Sunday, photographs the slides, and has 300 photos he has never opened. In
  class his hand is on his phone and his mind is on Monday. He is present; he is not hearing.
- **Transition:** "Prahlada Maharaja, asked for the best thing he had learnt, listed nine practices and began with hearing. Let's see why,
  and then how."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** they hear more spiritual sound in a week than most generations heard in a year, and keep little
  of it. A method that keeps one point per class gives them fifty points a year.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Hearing is first.** SB 7.5.23–24; BG 6.35 purport; 2.1.5. *Example:* the three verbs on the board. *Transition:* what do we hear

2. **Many subject matters.** SB 2.1.2–3. *Example:* the feed. *Transition:* why does this one subject matter?
3. **Medicine, nectar, the hour not lost.** SB 10.1.4; 4.29.40; 2.3.17; BG 13.26. *Example:* the businessman eager for the market (4.29.40

4. **The hearer's qualities.** SB 1.1.2; BG 18.71; BG 2.7; BG 7.1; Pariksit (1.19.15). *Example:* Arjuna's turn at 2.7 from arguing to

5. **Before, during, after.** The three-column page; one question; one line; one point told (BG 4.34; 18.68). *Example:* the

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"I remember nothing after class. Is hearing wasted on me?"**
   - BG 13.26 and 18.71: hearing with faith acts even on one who is "not conversant in spiritual knowledge"; SB 6.2.19 (VL3-04) said the
     same of the name. Not wasted. But remembering is the third verb of SB 2.1.5, and the method exists for it: one line the same day, reread
     before the next class.
2. **"Taking notes distracts me from listening."**
   - Writing everything does. The three-column page writes little: verse numbers, short phrases, three marks. Try it once in the
     exercise and judge. For most people a pen keeps the mind in the room (*application, preacher's*; see O for what research can and
     cannot say).
3. **"I just record the class and listen later."**
   - Good for review, not a substitute. SB 4.29.40 speaks of "great eagerness" in the hour; BG 18.71 of listening "with faith"; Pariksit
     did not ask for a recording. Hear now; use the recording to fill the "check" marks.
4. **"Can I hear lectures online instead of coming to class?"**
   - Hear both, but class is not optional: SB 1.2.18 says "regular attendance in classes", 3.25.25 says the hearing is "in the association
     of pure devotees", and 10.1.4 says the source matters. IYF's prescribed lectures are the home hearing; the question of other online
     sources is for SPS. Ask your facilitator before adding.
5. **"The speaker was boring / I disagreed with him."**
   - BG 18.71: "with faith and without envy". You may have a question; write it as a **?** and ask it (BG 4.34), not as a verdict. The
     subject is Krsna whatever the speaker's skill (BG 7.1), and the 10.1 purport says hearing in the association of devotees fixes
     devotion even when the talk is plain.
6. **"I’ve heard this topic five times."**
   - BG 10.1: "Listen again." The Gita repeats on purpose, and the hearer changes. Write what is new to you *this* time in the **!**
     column; there is always something.
7. **"How is kirtan 'hearing'?"**
   - SB 2.1.5: hear, glorify, remember. Kirtan is the second verb done aloud; following the words and responding is hearing with the whole
     body. One line afterwards is enough of a note. SPS takes it further.
8. **"Should I type notes on my phone?"**
   - The texts do not care; your attention does. A phone brings the rest of the phone. Paper, or at least a dedicated notes app with
     everything else silent. The research on pen versus keyboard is mixed (O); the practical argument is about distraction.

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The 300 photos.** Aarav has attended for a year, photographs every slide, remembers no class, and feels the programme "isn't

- **Questions:** What is Meghna's mind attached to during class? How would a **?** column change what she does with a disagreement? What
  would she lose by writing less?
- **Takeaway:** Hear the subject, not the speaker; ask, don't audit.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

When Prahlada Maharaja was asked for the best thing he had learnt, he named nine practices of devotion and put hearing first (SB 7.5.23–24). Srila Prabhupada's purport to BG 6.35 calls hearing about Krsna "the first and foremost" practice for controlling the mind in this age. The Bhagavatam says hearing about the Lord is "the right medicine for the conditioned soul", conveyed from the spiritual master to the disciple in disciplic succession (SB 10.1.4), that the hour spent in it is the one hour the sun cannot take away (SB 2.3.17), and that we are to hear, glorify and remember (SB 2.1.5). Most people have "many subject matters for hearing" (SB 2.1.2); the remedy is to add the one that is missing. King Pariksit, with seven days to live, asked that the sages continue singing the deeds of Lord Visnu (SB 1.19.15); the Lord also says "listen again" (BG 10.1), so a repeated topic is not a wasted one.

Hear "attentively and submissively" (SB 1.1.2), "with faith and without envy" (BG 18.71), as a disciple with a question (BG 2.7). The class will stay with you if you keep a simple page: one question before, short notes in three columns during, and after it one line, one application, one question for your facilitator (BG 4.34) and one point told to someone (BG 18.68). Write little: verse numbers, short phrases, three marks.

A recording is good for review but does not replace the class: hearing asks for eagerness in the hour (SB 4.29.40). If a speaker is dull or you disagree, turn it into a question and ask it.

## S2. Key ideas

- Hearing is the first of the nine practices (SB 7.5.23–24) and "first and foremost" for the mind (BG 6.35 purport).
- Hear, glorify, remember (SB 2.1.5): the three verbs of a class.
- Hearing is medicine (SB 10.1.4); the hour of hearing is the hour the sun cannot take (SB 2.3.17).
- We already hear a lot: "many subject matters for hearing" (SB 2.1.2). Add the missing one.
- Hear attentively and submissively (SB 1.1.2), with faith and without envy (BG 18.71), with a question (BG 2.7).
- Pariksit had seven days and wanted only to hear (SB 1.19.15). What do I want from this Sunday's hour?
- A page keeps the hour: one question before, three columns during, one line and one shared point after.

## S3. Verses to remember

- **SB 7.5.23–24:** "Hearing and chanting about the transcendental holy name, form, qualities, paraphernalia and pastimes of Lord Viṣṇu, remembering them…" *For me:* hearing is where my practice begins.
- **SB 2.3.17:** the sun "decreases the duration of life of everyone, except one who utilizes the time by discussing topics of the all-good Personality of Godhead." *For me:* class is the hour I keep.
- **BG 18.71:** "one who listens with faith and without envy becomes free from sinful reactions…" *For me:* how to sit in a class.
- **BG 2.7:** "Now I am Your disciple, and a soul surrendered unto You. Please instruct me." *For me:* arrive with a question, not a verdict.

## S4. Words to know

- **Śravaṇam:** hearing about the Lord; the first of the nine processes (SB 7.5.23–24).
- **Kīrtanam:** glorifying, chanting, telling; the second.
- **Smaraṇam:** remembering; the third (SB 2.1.5 gives all three).
- **Paramparā:** the line of teachers through which the glorification of the Lord is passed down (SB 10.1.4).
- **Gṛhamedhī:** one absorbed in home and sense enjoyment, with "many subject matters for hearing" (SB 2.1.2 purport).

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **I remember nothing after class. Is it wasted?** No. Hearing with faith acts even on one "not conversant in spiritual knowledge" (BG 13.26; 18.71). But remembering is the third verb (SB 2.1.5): write one line the same day and reread it before the next class.
2. **Notes distract me.** Writing everything does. Write little: verse numbers, short phrases, three marks. Try it once.
3. **I record the class and listen later.** Good for review, not a substitute. Hearing asks for eagerness in the hour (SB 4.29.40). Pariksit did not ask for a recording.
4. **Can I hear online lectures instead of class?** Hear both, but class is not optional: "regular attendance in classes" (SB 1.2.18), "in the association of pure devotees" (SB 3.25.25), and the source matters (SB 10.1.4). Ask your facilitator before adding other sources.
5. **The speaker was boring, or I disagreed.** Listen "with faith and without envy" (BG 18.71). Turn the disagreement into a question and ask it (BG 4.34). The subject is Krsna whatever the speaker's skill (BG 7.1).
6. **I've heard this topic before.** "Listen again" (BG 10.1). Write what is new to you this time.
