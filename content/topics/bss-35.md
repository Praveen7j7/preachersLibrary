# 39. Dealings with Nondevotees in General

> BSS · 120 min · drafted · SENSITIVE (association, family and workplace; no contempt for any person; no private disclosure). Follows BIKC chapter 39. Builds on bss-22 and bss-23 (association as the second danger; the modern atmosphere).

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level
The brahmacari does not leave the world on the day he decides to join. He still sits in classrooms, offices, buses and family kitchens. BIKC chapter 39 gives a frank rule (give up the company of nondevotees, deal only as far as necessary); the Bhagavatam gives a warm one (show mercy to the innocent). This page holds both together, so that protection of the vow never turns into contempt and kindness never turns into mixing.

## C. Central message
The Bhagavatam does not tell us to hate anyone. It tells us to love the Lord, to befriend His devotees, to show mercy to innocent people and to keep our distance from people who are hostile to Him. A brahmacari protects his vow by choosing his company carefully and by being kind and honest with everyone.

## D. Learning outcomes
1. State the four attitudes of the intermediate devotee from SB 11.2.46 and give one example of each.
2. Explain, from SB 7.9.43-44, why compassion and distance can live together in one heart.
3. Name two things a brahmacari gives up (BIKC) and two things he never gives up (respect, honesty, kindness).
4. Describe in one minute how a devotee's presence can change a person's life (Narada, SB 1.5.23-26).
5. Make a personal plan for one situation: study, workplace, home or neighbourhood.

## E. Scriptural foundation
Quotations are the BBT translations kept in `research/verification/bss-35.json`. Purport points were read in full on vedabase.io. SB Canto 11 is translated by Srila Prabhupada's disciples (BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples).

**SB 11.2.46** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/2/46/
> "An intermediate or second-class devotee, called madhyama-adhikārī, offers his love to the Supreme Personality of Godhead, is a sincere friend to all the devotees of the Lord, shows mercy to ignorant people who are innocent and disregards those who are envious of the Supreme Personality of Godhead."
- Context: King Nimi asks the nine Yogendras how to tell the levels of devotee apart (SB 11.2.44); Sri Havir answers, and this verse describes the middle level (SB 11.2.45 describes the highest).
- Teaching: four relationships: love for the Lord, friendship with devotees, mercy to the innocent, disregard of the envious.
- Purport point: Prabhupada's purport says every living being is a part of the Lord (BG 15.7) and the Vaisnavas are "sincerely eager to serve the conditioned souls"; quoting Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, the second-class devotee, though eager to preach to the innocent, "should avoid the atheistic class of men" so as not to be disturbed or polluted; quoting Visvanatha Cakravarti, the preacher may stay far from an envious person yet "meditate upon ways to save" him, which is called *sad-acara*, saintly behaviour, with Prahlada as the example (SB 7.9.43).
- Application (preacher's): the verse sorts attitudes, not people into labels. One person may be "innocent" today and hostile tomorrow, or the reverse; the verse asks us to act on what a person is doing now, never to rank him.
- Use: in the talk (the frame of the whole session).

**SB 7.9.43** and **SB 7.9.44** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/9/43/ and /44/
> "I am not at all afraid of material existence, for wherever I stay I am fully absorbed in thoughts of Your glories and activities. My concern is only for the fools and rascals who are making elaborate plans for material happiness…" (SB 7.9.43)
> "…I do not wish to be liberated alone, leaving aside all these poor fools and rascals. I know that without Kṛṣṇa consciousness, without taking shelter of Your lotus feet, one cannot be happy." (SB 7.9.44)
- Context: Prahlada's prayer to Lord Nrsimhadeva after the Lord has killed his father.
- Purport point (7.9.43): Prahlada "was happy and unhappy simultaneously": happy in Krishna consciousness, unhappy for those struggling for happiness without it. (7.9.44): the devotee's interest is "in the busiest part of the world, where he teaches people Kṛṣṇa consciousness"; the movement does not teach showy meditation in seclusion.
- Teaching: the word "fools and rascals" in the translation is Prabhupada's rendering of a prayer; Prahlada speaks it with love ("simply concerned with love for them").
- Application (preacher's): when we feel superior to a classmate or colleague, this prayer is the correction; we may only use the word if we also feel Prahlada's concern.
- Use: in the talk.

**CC Madhya 22.87** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/22/87/
> "A Vaiṣṇava should always avoid the association of ordinary people. Common people are very much materially attached, especially to women. Vaiṣṇavas should also avoid the company of those who are not devotees of Lord Kṛṣṇa."
- Context: part of Lord Caitanya's instructions to Sanatana Gosvami on devotional practice (CC Madhya 22); the verse names this avoidance the Vaisnava's *acara*, his standard conduct.
- Teaching: avoiding *asat-sanga*, the company of the materially absorbed, is the Vaisnava's standard conduct.
- Application (preacher's): "avoid" is about the depth of intimacy, not courtesy. The verse is addressed to a devotee who is trying to protect his devotion; it is not an instruction to be rude.
- Use: in the talk.

**SB 5.5.2** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/5/2/
> "…For those who are not interested in such activities, who associate with people fond of women and sex, the path to hell is wide open."
- Context: Lord Rsabhadeva to His sons (bss-07 tells the whole teaching).
- Purport point: the purport quotes CC Madhya 22.87 and says there are "two kinds of materialists. One is attached to women and sense gratification, and the other is simply a nondevotee"; on the positive side is association with *mahatmas*.
- Use: in the talk, with CC Madhya 22.87, as the second half of the same instruction: avoidance is paired with association of mahatmas.

**NOI 2** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/2/
> "…(5) associating with worldly-minded persons who are not interested in Kṛṣṇa consciousness…"
- Context: Rupa Gosvami's list of six things that spoil devotional service (six things that spoil devotional service).
- Use: in the talk, one line only (the fifth item).

**NOI 4** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/4/
> "Offering gifts in charity, accepting charitable gifts, revealing one’s mind in confidence, inquiring confidentially, accepting prasāda and offering prasāda are the six symptoms of love shared by one devotee and another."
- Purport point: these are the exchanges that deepen devotees' bond; the purport also says ordinary people "must be given the chance to associate with the devotees", since by such exchanges "an ordinary man can fully revive his dormant Kṛṣṇa consciousness" and quotes BG 2.62 (desires develop according to the company one keeps).
- Application (preacher's): the six exchanges are a measuring stick. Gifts, confidences and food shared freely with devotees are healthy; the same exchanges with the materially absorbed are where the contamination (CC Antya 6.278) enters. With nondevotees we may give prasada, which is the mercy (the purport's own example).
- Use: in the talk.

**CC Antya 6.278** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/6/278/
> "When one eats food offered by a materialistic man, one’s mind becomes contaminated, and when the mind is contaminated, one is unable to think of Kṛṣṇa properly."
- Context: Lord Caitanya's advice to Raghunatha dasa Gosvami.
- Purport point: Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati's note lists "six kinds of association" (giving charity, accepting charity, accepting food, offering food, talking confidentially, inquiring confidentially) and warns against both materialists and casual pseudo-Vaisnavas (*sahajiyas*).
- Use: in the talk, for "food and close exchange" only.

**CC Antya 6.237** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/6/237/
> "Do not expect honor, but offer all respect to others. Always chant the holy name of Lord Kṛṣṇa, and within your mind render service to Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa in Vṛndāvana."
- Purport point: the purport explains *gramya-katha* as talk concerning ordinary family life, which a person in the renounced order does not indulge in hearing or speaking; the verse before it (CC Antya 6.236) says: do not talk like people in general or hear what they say.
- Application (preacher's): note the pairing in this very verse: "offer all respect to others" with "always chant". Distance is kept by the heart's engagement, not by disrespect.
- Use: in the talk.

**NOD 7** (the sections "Giving up the company of nondevotees", "Straightforwardness in ordinary dealings and equilibrium in loss and gain", "Not giving pain to any living entity") · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/nod/7/
- Purport point: Lord Caitanya says a Vaisnava should give up the company of nondevotees, of two kinds: those against the supremacy of Krishna and those too materialistic. The strongest statements (cage of iron, embracing a snake) are quoted about persons "through and through against the supremacy of the Lord" and about worshippers of demigods impelled by material desire. The same chapter says a Vaisnava should not cause pain to any living being and should not neglect to offer respect to the demigods.
- Application (preacher's): BIKC's summary (do not neglect ordinary dealings, yet give up company rigidly) rests on these sections. Read the sections before quoting the strong lines, which are about hostile persons, not about family or classmates.
- Use: background, and one line in the talk.

**BG 12.13-14** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/12/13-14/
> "One who is not envious but is a kind friend to all living entities… such a devotee of Mine is very dear to Me."
- Purport point: the purport says that nor does a devotee become his enemy's enemy, and that he is "always kind to everyone, even to his enemy".
- Use: in the talk, the ground of every dealing.

**SB 1.5.23 to 1.5.26 (Narada's earlier life)** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/5/23/ (and /24, /25, /26)
- SB 1.5.23: "…I was born as the son of a certain maidservant engaged in the service of brāhmaṇas who were following the principles of Vedānta…"
- SB 1.5.24: "Although they were impartial by nature, those followers of the Vedānta blessed me with their causeless mercy."
- SB 1.5.25: "Once only, by their permission, I took the remnants of their food, and by so doing all my sins were at once eradicated."
- SB 1.5.26: "…I could hear them describe the attractive activities of Lord Kṛṣṇa. And thus listening attentively, my taste for hearing of the Personality of Godhead increased at every step."
- Purport point (1.5.23): "the service of the Lord begins with the service of the Lord’s bona fide servants". (1.5.25): "Pure devotion is as much infectious, in a good sense, as infectious diseases"; the purport also warns that "there are sometimes pseudodevotees, and one should be very much cautious about them".
- Use: in the talk (J.1).

## F. Philosophical explanation
**F.1 Two directions of one love.** BIKC says the intermediate devotee shows mercy to the innocent and avoids the inimical (SB 11.2.46). The Bhagavatam gives the engine behind it: every living being is a part of the Lord (the purport, via BG 15.7), so no one is despised as a person. What is avoided is a *kind of association* (CC Madhya 22.87, SB 5.5.2), because "one’s desires and ambitions develop according to the company one keeps" (the NOI 4 purport, citing BG 2.62; bss-23 has the chain).

**F.2 Sorting by behaviour, not by person.** The verse names four dealings with four objects. A brahmacari does not need to decide whether his classmate is a "nondevotee"; he needs to ask three questions: *Is he innocent (open, curious) or hostile (ridicules Krishna)? Am I strong enough in this setting? What is the topic and the time?* (Application (preacher's).) BIKC itself warns that "not all nondevotees are out-and-out demons or rogues and cannot be so generalized. Nor can we assume that we are really devotees, beyond that we are trying to be so." (BIKC, summarised and quoted briefly.)

**F.3 Why a new devotee is vulnerable.** BIKC notes that a beginner may feel bliss in the morning programme and lose it outside the temple, and may react defensively. The remedy in the scriptures is not a wall but a stronger tie to devotees (NOI 4) and a regular engagement of the mind (CC Antya 6.237).

**F.4 The standard has two legs.** (1) *Give up* what feeds the old consciousness: intimate company of the materially absorbed, shared food and confidences (CC Antya 6.278, NOI 4), idle worldly talk (CC Antya 6.236-237). (2) *Never give up* respect (CC Antya 6.237), non-envy and kindness (BG 12.13-14), honest dealings and not giving pain to anyone (NOD 7). A brahmacari who is rude has misunderstood both.

**F.5 Giving up, and what replaces it.** BIKC lists worldly entertainment, news, sports, fashion and food cooked by nondevotees as things a serious devotee sets aside, and insists the replacement is positive: Krishna conscious reading, kirtana, drama, discussion and cooking for Krishna. Read this as BIKC's summary of ISKCON practice for committed celibates (it says so itself) and as the author's strong opinion on how far to go. Prabhupada's own words on the principle: attachment "cannot be killed; it has simply to be transferred" (SB 3.25.20 purport). So the question for each habit is: *what am I transferring it to?* (Application (preacher's).)

**F.6 Global and local forms.** BIKC's examples come from India and the West of the 1990s. A student in Lagos, a farm-worker in a village, a software engineer in Delhi all meet the same three things: a pull to join conversations that fill the mind, invitations that mix food and fellowship, and workplace pressure. The principles are the same; ask a senior how to apply them in your setting.

**F.7 What this is not.** It is not permission to look down on parents, relatives or teachers; those relationships carry gratitude and duty (bss-34). It is not a reason to refuse help, to leave a job without a plan, or to cut a friendship cruelly. When in doubt, ask a trusted senior.

## H. Opening and engagement
**H.1 How to start.** (1) Greet; say "Today we speak about the people around us, not against them." (2) Read SB 11.2.46 slowly, saying it is Krishna's own devotees speaking to a king. (3) Ask H.2. (4) Say: "Nothing today needs a personal story; if you wish to talk about your own family or job situation, I am glad to talk privately afterwards."

**H.2 Opening questions.**
1. Who is the person outside our devotee circle you spend the most time with each week? (Easy; no names needed.)
2. When you come back from that person's company, is your mind lighter or heavier?
3. Can you be a good friend to someone without sharing everything he shares?

## I. Core teaching points
1. **Four attitudes, one love.** SB 11.2.46. Transition: "How can mercy and distance live together?"
2. **Prahlada shows how.** SB 7.9.43-44: fear of nothing, concern for everyone. Transition: "What exactly do we guard?"
3. **We guard the mind through three doors: company, food and talk.** CC Madhya 22.87, SB 5.5.2, NOI 4, CC Antya 6.278, CC Antya 6.236-237. Transition: "Can the right company change a life?"
4. **Narada's story.** SB 1.5.23-26: a servant's son becomes a sage through serving devotees. Transition: "What replaces what we give up?"
5. **Respect never leaves.** BG 12.13-14, CC Antya 6.237, NOD 7. Close with the personal plan.

## J. Stories and illustrations
**J.1 Narada, the maidservant's son (SB 1.5.23-26).** The story is told in full on bss-44; here tell it in two sentences. The son of a maidservant served Vedanta-following brahmanas who stayed four months in the rainy season, and by serving them and hearing them speak of Krishna his taste for hearing grew (SB 1.5.23-26). Prabhupada's purport to 1.5.24 says these saints "are impartial in distributing the transcendental knowledge" and "see that the people in general are wasting time in false sensuous things". **Devotional lesson:** a person with no education or status was lifted by service to devotees and by hearing of Krishna; nothing in the story is about avoiding people, and everything in it is about *whom he served*. The story answers "what replaces the old company?": better company, offered by the Lord's mercy through His servants. (Do not add details about Narada's mother's death or his later vision; they are in SB 1.6 and not needed here.)

**J.2 Prahlada's compassion (SB 7.9.43-44).** Told in four sentences: Nrsimhadeva has killed Hiranyakasipu; the demigods have prayed (SB 7.8); Prahlada then prays (SB 7.9); his prayer says he fears nothing and is concerned for those without Krishna (SB 7.9.43), and he wishes to bring them to the Lord's lotus feet (SB 7.9.44). **Devotional lesson:** the boy who was tormented by his own father prays for people absorbed in material plans. (bss-06 tells Prahlada's teaching to his classmates; this page uses only his prayer.)

**J.3 Everyday image (Application (preacher's)).** A doctor who works among sick patients washes his hands, wears gloves, and then sits with each patient tenderly. The gloves are not contempt; they are what lets him serve. Ends in the Krishna conscious point: protection of the mind (chanting, hearing, devotees) is what lets a devotee serve even the hostile with a calm heart.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections
- *Does this mean I must cut off my family?* No. CC Antya 6.237 puts respect beside chanting; BG 12.13-14 asks kindness to all. Duties to parents and relatives are not "nondevotee dealings" in the sense of CC Madhya 22.87; bss-34 treats family and friends; ask your senior about the specifics.
- *Are all nondevotees hostile?* No. SB 11.2.46 itself names the "innocent". BIKC says they cannot be generalised.
- *Is "fools and rascals" permission to call people names?* No; SB 7.9.43 is a prayer in love. If you cannot say it with Prahlada's concern, do not say it.
- *My job needs me to join dinners and events. What do I do?* Take part in what the work needs, politely decline what serves only enjoyment, offer to bring prasada, and speak to a senior if the pressure is constant. Seeking another job without a plan is a decision for you and your senior, not for this page.
- *Is eating food cooked by nondevotees really that harmful?* Lord Caitanya's words (CC Antya 6.278) say it affects the mind; the purport lists the six exchanges. For travellers, students or those in hospital, ask your senior what is practical. Do not insult a host.
- *Is the Nectar of Devotion statement that one should accept a cage of iron rather than live with such persons about everyone?* No. The text speaks of those "through and through against the supremacy of the Lord" (NOD 7).
- *What if I feel alone because my old friends drift away?* This is common and it is not a failure. Ask your senior to connect you with devotee friends; if loneliness becomes heavy or you feel low for weeks, speak with a trusted senior and a qualified counsellor (Tele-MANAS 14416 in India, or your local crisis service).

## L. Case studies
**L.1 Invented case for discussion.** *Situation:* Imran is a brahmacari-in-training with a job. His team goes out every Friday; the manager says attendance is "part of the culture". *Dilemma:* he does not want to damage his work relationships, and he knows the evening ends with drinking and loud talk. *Principles:* SB 11.2.46 (mercy to the innocent), NOI 2 and CC Antya 6.278 (guard company and food), CC Antya 6.237 (respect). *Questions:* (a) What is he trying to protect? (b) What can he give? (c) What can he decline without contempt? *Analysis:* attend the first part occasionally if the work needs it, bring a gift of prasada for the team on another day, say plainly he does not drink, give the manager a reason he can repeat in one sentence, and tell his senior so that he is not carrying it alone. *Takeaway:* Imran can be polite and friendly in his manner, and still be firm about the one thing he must protect, which is the company and the drink that would disturb his mind.

## M. Discussion and reflection
**M.1 Closing check questions.**
1. Name the four attitudes of the madhyama-adhikari. *Love for the Lord, friendship with devotees, mercy to the innocent, disregard for the envious (SB 11.2.46).*
2. What was Prahlada's concern in SB 7.9.43? *The fools making plans for material happiness; he is concerned "with love for them".*
3. What does CC Antya 6.278 say about food? *Food offered by a materialistic man contaminates the mind and makes it hard to think of Krishna.*
4. What did Narada do in his earlier life? *Served the Vedanta brahmanas, ate their remnants once, heard of Krishna (SB 1.5.23-26).*
5. What two things go together in CC Antya 6.237? *Respect for others and chanting.*
6. Whom can you talk to about a hard situation? *A trusted senior; a counsellor if you feel low for long.*

**M.2 Activities.**
(a) *Four sorts (15 min):* in groups of three, read four short invented sentences the preacher gives (a neighbour asks about your clothes; a colleague mocks Krishna; a friend asks to come to a kirtana; a relative offers a party invitation) and decide which attitude of SB 11.2.46 each calls for. Groups report; praise good reasons by name.
(b) *My plan (10 min):* each writes one situation this week (class, work, family) and one sentence he will say, one he will not. Pairs read to each other; no one is asked to share with the room.
(c) *Replace it (10 min):* groups list one worldly habit and the Krishna conscious engagement that could take its place (kirtana, reading, cooking for Krishna), the BIKC method.

**M.3 Reflection.** "Whom do I look down on, and what would Prahlada's concern for him look like in one small act?" Private.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary
A brahmacari still lives among people who do not follow Krishna consciousness: classmates, colleagues, neighbours and family. The Srimad-Bhagavatam gives four attitudes (SB 11.2.46): love the Lord, be a friend to His devotees, show mercy to people who are innocent and curious, and keep away from those who are hostile to the Lord. Prahlada Maharaja shows that this is love, not hatred: he was afraid of nothing and prayed for the people absorbed in material plans (SB 7.9.43-44).

To protect devotion, Lord Caitanya and the Upadesamrta ask us to be careful with three things: company, food and talk. The materially absorbed person's close company, food and private conversations draw the mind away from Krishna (CC Madhya 22.87, SB 5.5.2, CC Antya 6.278). But the same Lord Caitanya tells us to give respect to all (CC Antya 6.237), and Krishna says the dear devotee is a kind friend to every living being (BG 12.13-14).

The Bhagavatam also shows the positive side: Narada Muni, a servant's son, became a great sage because he served devotees and heard about Krishna (SB 1.5.23-26). So when we give something up, we replace it with kirtana, reading and service. If you find it hard, or you feel lonely, speak with a trusted senior; if you feel low for a long time, a qualified counsellor can help (Tele-MANAS 14416 in India, or your local crisis service).

## S2. Key ideas
- The Bhagavatam gives four attitudes: love for the Lord, friendship with devotees, mercy to innocent people, and distance from people who are hostile to the Lord.
- We take most care with three things: the company we keep, the food we accept and the talk we listen to. We never give up respect or kindness.
- When we give something up, we replace it with a Krishna conscious activity such as kirtana, reading or cooking for Krishna.
- Good company can change a whole life, as it did for Narada.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **SB 11.2.46**: the madhyama-adhikari shows mercy to the innocent and disregards the envious. *For me: act on what a person is doing, not on a label.*
- **SB 7.9.44**: "…I do not wish to be liberated alone…". *For me: care for others is part of devotion.*
- **CC Antya 6.237**: offer all respect to others; always chant. *For me: respect and chanting go together.*

## S4. Words to know
- **Nondevotee:** a person who has not taken up devotional service; not a bad person.
- **Madhyama-adhikari:** the intermediate devotee, with four attitudes.
- **Innocent:** open and curious about Krishna, not hostile.
- **Prasada:** food offered to Krishna; a mercy to share.
- **Brahmacari:** a celibate student in devotional training.

## S5. Questions and answers
- *Must I cut off my family?* No. Respect and kindness remain; ask a senior how to deal with specific situations.
- *Are all non-devotees bad?* No. The Bhagavatam speaks of "innocent" people and asks for mercy.
- *Can I eat with colleagues?* Ask a senior. The Lord says food from a materialistic person affects the mind (CC Antya 6.278); you can bring prasada and be polite.
- *What if people laugh at me?* Do not argue; stay respectful and chant. If they are only curious, answer simply.
- *What if I feel lonely?* Tell a senior; he can connect you with devotee friends; if it is heavy, see a counsellor.
