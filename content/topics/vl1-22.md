# IQ, EQ & SQ: How to Make Balance

> **VL1 · 45–60 min · review: sources checked, awaiting senior review.** A relevance talk for students and young professionals. The IQ/EQ/SQ frame is
> modern vocabulary (see O); the Gita's own model, senses → mind → intelligence → self (BG 3.42), carries the talk.
> IYF's folder for this topic is large (slides, a doc, research PDFs on EI and SI); this page keeps its spine and
> drops its unsourced statistics and anecdotes (see J and O).

## A. Topic overview

| | |
|---|---|
| Batch / level | VL1 (Vedic Level 1), a stand-alone talk |
| Suggested duration | 45–60 min (G gives the 60-minute flow and a 30-minute cut) |
| Preparation time | About 90 min, the first time |
| Difficulty | Introductory |
| Prerequisites | None |
| Audience | Students measured by marks and placements; young professionals who have met brilliant colleagues who are miserable; many with no settled faith |
| Teaching objective | Use the familiar IQ/EQ/SQ vocabulary to show that intelligence is more than reasoning, that emotions need a governor, and that the Gita's hierarchy (senses, mind, intelligence, self) gives a working model of balance; leave the listener with one practice |

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Every student has been ranked by something like IQ and told, in corporate training, that EQ matters more. "SQ" is
the newest term. A VL1 room will accept a talk that takes these words seriously and then shows that an older text
has a sharper version of the same map. The aim is **attraction through a better model**: the listener should leave
able to draw the Gita's four-level hierarchy and see where their own imbalance sits.

IYF's material does this in three steps: IQ (why intelligent people fail), EQ (emotions as information, the
mind–intelligence dialogue, the chariot analogy), SQ (the Gita's eighteen items of knowledge, BG 13.8–12). This
page keeps that spine and the chariot. It drops the slides' claims about particular historical figures and their
IQs, the stress statistics, and the magazine article on "eight signs of high SQ", none of which could be sourced.

*Builds on vl1-03, vl1-09 and vl1-16 (the mind as friend or enemy, BG 6.5–6): here BG 6.5–6 is used once, as the "EQ" step. The IQ/EQ/SQ map and the chariot are this page's own.*

How the subject grows at other levels:

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **VL1** (here) | Senses, mind, intelligence, self (BG 3.42); the chariot (SB 7.15.41); the mind as friend or enemy (BG 6.5–6); the Gita's definition of knowledge (BG 13.8–12) as "SQ"; one practice |
| VL2 | What the self is (BG 7.4 lists mind, intelligence and false ego as *material* elements, so "I" am none of them); intelligence as a gift of the Lord (BG 10.4–5) |
| VL3 | Training the intelligence by hearing; the eighteen items as a self-check (BG 13.8–12 purport) |
| SPS | Balance between enthusiasm and patience, confidence and humility in a growing practice |
| DPS | Balance of the three for leaders who guide others: head, heart, meaning |
| BSS | Governing the senses and mind in a student ashram so the intelligence can drive |

**Not done here:**
- A psychology lecture. Say what IQ, EQ and SQ mean in two sentences each and move to the Gita.
- Diagnosing anyone's mental health. The chain in BG 2.62–63 is taught as a pattern everyone recognises, not as a
  judgement on a person in difficulty.
- Reproducing IYF's "why intelligent people fail" list as research; it is a popular list without a source here.

## C. Central message

Reasoning power, emotional skill and the search for meaning are three different capacities, and a life can be rich
in one and bankrupt in another. The Gita arranges them: the senses, then the mind (feeling), then the intelligence
(judgement), and above all three the self (BG 3.42). Balance means the higher governs the lower, and the Gita calls
the knowledge that reaches the self "knowledge"; "besides this whatever there may be is ignorance" (BG 13.12).

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. Say in plain words what IQ, EQ and SQ each refer to, and give one example of a life strong in one and weak in
   another.
2. Draw the Gita's hierarchy, senses → mind → intelligence → self (BG 3.42), and place "EQ" at the mind–intelligence
   boundary and "SQ" at the self.
3. Explain the chariot analogy (SB 7.15.41) and the chain from contemplation to loss of intelligence (BG 2.62–63) as
   descriptions of imbalance.
4. Name two items from the Gita's own definition of knowledge (BG 13.8–12) and choose one to practise this week.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** five references are enough for the talk. The rest prepare you for questions.

**BG 3.42**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/42/
> "The working senses are superior to dull matter; mind is higher than the senses; intelligence is still higher than the mind; and he [the soul] is even higher than the intelligence."
- **Context:** Krsna has told Arjuna to curb lust, "the great symbol of sin", and now gives the structure through
  which it works.
- **Teaching:** A four-level hierarchy: senses, mind, intelligence, self.
- **Purport point:** "above the mind is the determination of the intelligence, and above the intelligence is the soul
  proper." If the soul is engaged with the Supreme, "all other subordinates, namely, the intelligence, mind and senses,
  will be automatically engaged." Prabhupada notes the similar passage in the Katha Upanisad.
- **Application (preacher's):** This verse is the talk's map. IQ lives mostly in the intelligence, EQ at the border of
  mind and intelligence, SQ with the self. Balance is a question of who governs whom.
- **Use:** in the talk; the anchor verse.

**SB 7.15.41**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/15/41/
> "…compare the body… to a chariot. The senses are like the horses; the mind, the master of the senses, is like the reins; the objects of the senses are the destinations; intelligence is the chariot driver…"
- **Context:** Narada to Yudhisthira; the verse itself attributes the comparison to "transcendentalists who are
  advanced in knowledge".
- **Purport point:** Prabhupada quotes the Katha Upanisad (1.3.3–4, 9): the soul is the chariot's occupant, the
  intelligence the driver, the mind the reins, the senses the horses. "The human body, therefore, may be used in two
  ways — for going to the darkest regions of ignorance or for going forward".
- **Application (preacher's):** The picture everyone remembers. Horses without reins = senses without mind; reins
  without a driver = a mind with no judgement; a driver who forgets the passenger = brilliant intelligence serving no
  one. The verse itself does not name the soul as passenger; the purport's Upanisad quotation does.
- **Use:** in the talk (with the diagram in N).

**BG 6.5** and **BG 6.6**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/5/ · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/6/
> 6.5: "One must deliver himself with the help of his mind, and not degrade himself. The mind is the friend of the conditioned soul, and his enemy as well."
> 6.6: "For him who has conquered the mind, the mind is the best of friends; but for one who has failed to do so, his mind will remain the greatest enemy."
- **Teaching:** The mind is neither good nor bad; it is powerful, and it must be trained.
- **Purport point (6.5):** "the mind should be trained so that it will not be attracted by the glitter of material
  nature". **(6.6):** "As long as one's mind remains an unconquered enemy, one has to serve the dictations of lust,
  anger, avarice, illusion, etc."
- **Application (preacher's):** The Gita's "EQ" in two verses: not suppressing the mind, not obeying it, but training
  it into a friend.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 2.62** and **BG 2.63**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/62/ · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/63/
> 2.62: "While contemplating the objects of the senses, a person develops attachment for them, and from such attachment lust develops, and from lust anger arises."
> 2.63: "From anger, complete delusion arises, and from delusion bewilderment of memory. When memory is bewildered, intelligence is lost, and when intelligence is lost one falls down again into the material pool."
- **Teaching:** A chain: contemplation → attachment → desire → anger → delusion → confused memory → loss of
  intelligence → fall.
- **Purport point (2.62):** "The senses require real engagements, and if they are not engaged in the transcendental
  loving service of the Lord, they will certainly seek engagement in the service of materialism." The purport names a
  devotee's "higher taste" as the secret of success.
- **Application (preacher's):** The clearest description of how a clever person does a stupid thing: the intelligence
  is the *last* thing to go, after emotion has been running the show for several steps. This is why IQ alone does
  not protect anyone.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 13.8-12**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/13/8-12/ (vedabase prints the five verses on one page)
> "Humility; pridelessness; nonviolence; tolerance; simplicity; approaching a bona fide spiritual master; cleanliness; steadiness; self-control; renunciation of the objects of sense gratification; absence of false ego; the perception of the evil of birth, death, old age and disease; detachment; freedom from entanglement with children, wife, home and the rest; even-mindedness amid pleasant and unpleasant events; constant and unalloyed devotion to Me; aspiring to live in a solitary place; detachment from the general mass of people; accepting the importance of self-realization; and philosophical search for the Absolute Truth – all these I declare to be knowledge, and besides this whatever there may be is ignorance."
- **Teaching:** Krsna's own list of what counts as knowledge: largely qualities of character, ending in devotion and
  philosophical search.
- **Purport point:** The "most important point" is unalloyed devotion (verse 11); if one takes to it, "the other
  nineteen items automatically develop". The purport then defines each item; e.g. humility means "one should not be
  anxious to have the satisfaction of being honored by others"; tolerance means "to bear insult and dishonor from
  others"; simplicity means being "so straightforward that he can disclose the real truth even to an enemy".
- **Application (preacher's):** IYF presents these as the "SQ" items, and the fit is fair: they are about meaning,
  value and self-knowledge, not calculation. At VL1 read four or five (humility, tolerance, simplicity, cleanliness,
  steadiness, even-mindedness), not all twenty, and do not press items on family or solitude.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**For questions (K):**
- **BG 18.30** (Verified): understanding "by which one knows what ought to be done and what ought not to be done, what
  is to be feared and what is not to be feared, what is binding and what is liberating, is in the mode of goodness."
  The Gita's working definition of good intelligence: knowing what to do, not only how.
- **BG 10.4-5** (Verified): "Intelligence, knowledge, freedom from doubt and delusion, forgiveness, truthfulness,
  control of the senses, control of the mind…" are created by the Lord. Purport: "Intelligence refers to the power
  to analyze things in their proper perspective, and knowledge refers to understanding what is spirit and what is
  matter." For "isn't intelligence just genetic?"
- **BG 7.4** (Verified): "Earth, water, fire, air, ether, mind, intelligence and false ego – all together these eight
  constitute My separated material energies." IYF cites it to show that mind and intelligence are *subtle matter*,
  instruments of the self rather than the self. One sentence at VL1; the full point is VL2.
- **BG 5.23** (Verified): one able "to tolerate the urges of the material senses and check the force of desire and
  anger… is well situated and is happy in this world." Purport lists "the forces of talk, forces of anger, forces of
  mind, forces of the stomach, forces of the genitals, and forces of the tongue". For "what does EQ look like in
  practice?"
- **BG 2.14** (Verified): happiness and distress come and go like seasons; "one must learn to tolerate them without
  being disturbed." For "isn't tolerance just bottling up?"

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** The Gita distinguishes four levels in a person: senses, mind, intelligence and self
   (BG 3.42), and pictures them as horses, reins, driver and passenger (SB 7.15.41 and purport). Reasoning power
   (IQ) is a function of intelligence; emotional skill (EQ) is intelligence governing the mind rather than the mind
   dragging the intelligence (BG 6.5–6; BG 2.62–63); the search for meaning (SQ) is the self's own question, and the
   Gita's definition of knowledge (BG 13.8–12) is mostly a list of character and orientation, ending in devotion
   and philosophical search. Balance is the right chain of command.
2. **Why it matters.** Students are trained for one level and promoted for another, and nobody trains the third.
   Giving them the map lets them diagnose their own week: brilliant work, bad temper, no idea why.
3. **Connections.** The PARTHA success formula (VL1-23) trains the same hierarchy; smart work and the modes (VL1-25)
   classify the driver's style; the pleasure hunt (VL1-21) is the horses choosing the destination.
4. **Consequences.** A person whose intelligence governs the mind makes fewer regretted decisions; a person who asks
   the self's question has a direction for the intelligence to serve. In experience, such people are steadier
   colleagues and better friends. A claim about tendency, not a guarantee of success.
5. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - **"Emotions are the enemy."** The mind is "the friend of the conditioned soul, and his enemy as well" (BG 6.5).
     The Gita trains the mind; it does not amputate it.
   - **"Intelligence will protect me."** In BG 2.62–63 the intelligence is lost *last*, after several emotional steps.
     Clever people fall by this route.
   - **"SQ means religion."** The Gita's list (BG 13.8–12) begins with humility, nonviolence, tolerance and
     simplicity. Present SQ as the self's question about meaning, and devotion as the item the Gita calls central.
   - **Psychology as proof.** IQ, EQ and SQ are modern frames (O); they illustrate, they do not establish the Gita.
6. **Pitch for VL1.** Say "senses, mind, intelligence, self", "the chariot", "who is driving". *Buddhi* and *manas*
   may be said once each. Present Krsna as the Gita's speaker without arguing His divinity; "who is the passenger?"
   is a VL2 door.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet the room, say your name, and say the talk offers a map, not a verdict on anyone.
2. Write on the board: IQ, EQ, SQ (reasoning, handling feelings, asking what life is for).
3. Ask H.2 question 1 by show of hands; thank the room.
4. Ask question 2; take two or three answers (no names) and thank each speaker.
5. Give the transition line below and go to the two-sentence definitions.

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the room's own life)
1. Think of the most intelligent person you know. Are they also the happiest?
2. Think of the happiest person you know. Were they the one with the top marks or the best job?
3. When did a clear head last lose to a strong feeling in your own life?

- **Opening question:** "Think of the most intelligent person you know. Raise your hand if they are also the
  happiest." Pause. "Now think of the happiest person you know. Were they the topper?" Most rooms laugh. "Something
  other than marks is deciding happiness. Tonight we name it."
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** Ishaan tops his batch and lands the dream offer. Within a year he has quarrelled
  with his team lead, ghosted two friends, and lies awake asking what the point of any of it is. His IQ did not
  change. What did he lack?
- **Transition:** "Modern psychology answers with two more letters, EQ and SQ. The Bhagavad-gita answered with a
  picture of a chariot."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** Job interviews now test "soft skills" (placement interviews, in Indian campuses); family and friendships test
  something else; and the 3 a.m. question ("what is this all for?") tests something no exam measures.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Three capacities, not one.** *Plain:* IQ reasons, EQ handles feeling, SQ asks what it is all for (O). *Example:*
   Ishaan (H). *Transition:* the Gita has an older and sharper version.
2. **The Gita's map.** BG 3.42; SB 7.15.41. *Plain:* senses, mind, intelligence, self; horses, reins, driver,
   passenger. *Example:* a brilliant driver with no idea where the passenger wants to go. *Transition:* what happens
   when the order is reversed?
3. **The mind can be friend or enemy.** BG 6.5–6. *Plain:* feelings are not the problem; untrained feelings in charge
   are. *Example:* the message sent in anger at midnight. *Transition:* the Gita traces exactly how clever people
   lose their judgement.
4. **How intelligence is lost.** BG 2.62–63. *Plain:* contemplate → attach → desire → anger → confusion → forgotten
   memory → intelligence gone. *Example:* the "one quick look" at the feed that ends an evening of study.
   *Transition:* so what does the Gita call real knowledge?
5. **Krsna's own list.** BG 13.8–12. *Plain:* humility, tolerance, simplicity, cleanliness, steadiness,
   even-mindedness, and at the centre a relationship with the Supreme and the search for truth. *Example:* each
   person picks one item they would like more of. *Transition:* to balance.
6. **Balance is the right chain of command.** BG 18.30. *Plain:* good intelligence knows what ought to be done, not
   just how to do it; it needs the self's question to know that. *Transition:* to the practice.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"Isn't SQ just a fancy word for religion?"**
   - The Gita's list of knowledge (BG 13.8–12) starts with humility, pridelessness, nonviolence, tolerance and
     simplicity; most of it is character. The Gita does make devotion the central item (purport), and says so
     openly. At VL1: SQ is the self's question about meaning; where the answer leads is for you to explore.
2. **"Can EQ be trained, or are you born with it?"**
   - The Gita's whole premise is training: "the mind should be trained" (BG 6.5 purport), and controlling the mind is
     called possible "by suitable practice and by detachment" in the same chapter. Modern EI research also treats it
     as skills (O). The method here: engage the mind in something higher so the senses have "real engagements"
     (BG 2.62 purport).
3. **"Isn't intelligence genetic? What can I do?"**
   - BG 10.4–5 lists intelligence among qualities "created by Me alone", and its purport defines it as "the power to
     analyze things in their proper perspective". Whatever one's raw capacity, the Gita's concern is its *use*: who
     it serves and whether it governs the mind (BG 3.42). That is trainable.
4. **"Doesn't tolerance mean suppressing emotions?"**
   - BG 2.14 asks us to tolerate happiness and distress as we tolerate seasons: feel them, carry on. BG 5.23 speaks of
     checking "the force of desire and anger", not denying that they exist. Suppression is the reins with no driver;
     tolerance is the driver holding the reins.
5. **"Am I my mind? Where is the 'self' in all this?"**
   - BG 7.4 lists mind, intelligence and false ego among material energies, so the Gita says the self is none of them.
     That is exactly VL2's topic. For tonight: there is a passenger, and the talk is about letting the passenger
     choose the destination.
6. **"My friend is really struggling emotionally. Is this the answer?"**
   - *Pastoral guidance, not a scriptural claim:* this talk is about everyday balance. Serious or persistent
     distress deserves professional help, which is fully compatible with spiritual life. Encourage the friend to seek
     it, and stay a friend.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The topper's temper.** Riya is the best engineer on her team and knows it. In reviews she corrects
colleagues bluntly; twice she has sent furious messages at night and regretted them by morning. Her manager says
promotion depends on "how you work with people".
- **Dilemma:** She thinks the feedback is unfair: her work is the best. Is it?
- **Principles:** BG 3.42 (intelligence should govern the mind); BG 2.62–63 (anger leads to lost judgement);
  BG 13.8–12 (humility, tolerance, simplicity as knowledge).
- **Questions:**
  - At which step of BG 2.62–63 does Riya's night message happen?
  - Which item from BG 13.8–12 would most change her reviews?
  - What would "the driver taking the reins" look like for her, concretely, this week?
- **Suggested analysis:** Her IQ is not in question; her chain of command is. One rule (no messages after a trigger
  until morning) is intelligence governing the mind. Humility, as the purport defines it (not anxious for honour),
  removes the fuel.
- **Takeaway:** Excellence that cannot be received is not yet excellence.

**Case 2: The meaningless success.** Karan has done everything right: good college, good job, good salary. He is
kind and well-liked. He tells a friend he feels like "a very efficient machine with no idea what it is for".
- **Principles:** BG 3.42 (the self above the intelligence); BG 13.8–12 ("accepting the importance of
  self-realization… philosophical search for the Absolute Truth"); BG 18.30 (knowing what ought to be done).
- **Questions:**
  - Which capacity is Karan missing, in the talk's terms?
  - Why can neither more IQ nor more EQ answer his question?
  - What is one honest first step for someone who wants to ask the self's question?
- **Suggested analysis:** Nothing is broken in the driver or the reins; the passenger has never been asked where he
  wants to go. The Gita's last two items of knowledge are exactly his need. First step: a regular time to hear and
  ask, in company that takes the question seriously.
- **Takeaway:** Efficiency without direction is the specific emptiness of the capable.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first; praise every try)
1. What does IQ measure, in your own words? *Reasoning power, the ability to solve problems.*
2. What are the four levels in BG 3.42? *Senses, mind, intelligence, self.*
3. In the chariot (SB 7.15.41), who are the horses and who is the driver? *Senses are the horses, intelligence the driver (the mind holds the reins).*
4. Which verse calls the mind a friend or an enemy? *BG 6.5–6.*
5. What is the first step in the chain of BG 2.62–63? *Dwelling on sense objects.*
6. Name one of the items of knowledge in BG 13.8–12. *Any of: humility, tolerance, simplicity, cleanliness, steadiness.*

**M.2 Activity (about 10 minutes): "Who is driving?"**
1. 2 min: each person recalls, silently, one recent decision they regret and draws the chariot (four boxes) on paper.
2. 4 min, in pairs: each marks which part was in charge at that moment (horses, reins, driver or passenger); sharing the decision is optional.
3. 3 min: volunteers say what they noticed. Thank each volunteer; no judgement.
4. 1 min: link to BG 6.5–6 and the one practice in P.

**M.3 Reflection**
- **Reflection:** In the last month, which part of the chariot has been driving you most: horses, reins, driver or
  passenger? What is the evidence?
- **Application (private, optional):** Pick one item from BG 13.8–12 that you would like more of. What is one
  situation this week where it will be tested? *(Nobody needs to share.)*

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

We are used to measuring intelligence by marks (IQ). Psychologists added emotional intelligence (EQ), the skill of handling feelings, and writers added spiritual intelligence (SQ), the capacity to ask what life is for. These three are modern words. They describe real differences, but they illustrate behaviour; they do not prove anything in the scripture.

The Bhagavad-gita (an ancient Sanskrit scripture, a conversation between Krsna and Arjuna) has an older map of the same territory: the senses, above them the mind (feeling), above that the intelligence (judgement), and above all three the self (BG 3.42). The Bhagavatam, another Sanskrit scripture, pictures it as a chariot: senses as horses, mind as reins, intelligence as driver (SB 7.15.41).

Imbalance is when the lower drives the higher: the mind becomes "the greatest enemy" (BG 6.6), and the Gita traces how a clever person loses judgement step by step (BG 2.62–63). Balance is the right chain of command. And the Gita's own definition of knowledge is mostly a list of character: humility, tolerance, simplicity, steadiness, with devotion and the search for truth at its centre (BG 13.8–12). This week, notice who is driving at one decision.

## S2. Key ideas

- IQ, EQ and SQ are three different capacities; a life can be rich in one and poor in another.
- The Gita's map: senses → mind → intelligence → self (BG 3.42); horses, reins, driver, passenger (SB 7.15.41).
- The mind is a friend when trained and an enemy when not (BG 6.5–6).
- Clever people lose judgement by a chain, and intelligence is lost last (BG 2.62–63).
- The Gita calls character and the search for truth "knowledge" (BG 13.8–12).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **BG 3.42:** "…mind is higher than the senses; intelligence is still higher than the mind; and he [the soul] is
  even higher than the intelligence." *For me:* when I feel pulled, I can ask who is driving.
- **BG 6.6:** "For him who has conquered the mind, the mind is the best of friends; but for one who has failed to do
  so, his mind will remain the greatest enemy." *For me:* my feelings are not the problem; untrained feelings in
  charge are.
- **BG 13.8–12 (opening):** "Humility; pridelessness; nonviolence; tolerance; simplicity…" *For me:* the Gita's idea
  of being intelligent includes how I treat people.

## S4. Words to know

- **IQ / EQ / SQ:** modern names for reasoning power, emotional skill and the search for meaning. Useful words, not
  scripture.
- **Mind (manas):** the thinking-feeling-wanting part; the reins in the chariot picture.
- **Intelligence (buddhi):** judgement, the capacity to decide what ought to be done (BG 18.30); the driver.
- **Self:** the passenger; who I am beyond senses, mind and intelligence (BG 3.42). What the self is comes at the next
  level.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Isn't SQ just religion?**
   The Gita's list of knowledge (BG 13.8–12) begins with humility, nonviolence, tolerance and simplicity. It does put
   devotion at the centre, and says so. SQ is the self's question about meaning; where the answer leads is yours to
   explore.
2. **Can EQ be trained?**
   The Gita's whole approach is training: "the mind should be trained" (BG 6.5 purport). Give the senses "real
   engagements" (BG 2.62 purport) and the mind follows.
3. **Isn't intelligence genetic?**
   Whatever your raw capacity, the Gita's concern is how it is used: whether it governs the mind (BG 3.42) and whom
   it serves. That part is trainable.
4. **Does tolerance mean suppressing emotions?**
   No. BG 2.14 asks us to carry happiness and distress like seasons: feel them, carry on. Suppression is reins with
   no driver; tolerance is the driver holding the reins.
5. **Am I my mind?**
   The Gita lists mind and intelligence among material energies (BG 7.4), so it says the self is neither. That is
   the next level's topic.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. In BG 3.42, which is higher: (a) the mind, (b) the intelligence, (c) the senses?
2. In the chariot picture (SB 7.15.41), what are the senses, the mind and the intelligence?
3. Put these in the Gita's order (BG 2.62–63): anger, attachment, contemplating sense objects, loss of intelligence,
   lust.
4. True or false: the Gita's list of knowledge in BG 13.8–12 is mainly about facts and calculation.
5. In your own words: what does "balance" mean in this talk?

**Answers:** 1. (b) (BG 3.42). 2. Horses, reins, driver (SB 7.15.41). 3. Contemplating sense objects → attachment →
lust → anger → (delusion, bewildered memory) → loss of intelligence (BG 2.62–63). 4. False: it is mainly character
(humility, tolerance, simplicity…) with devotion and the search for truth at the centre (BG 13.8–12). 5. The higher
governs the lower: intelligence holds the reins of the mind, and the self's question gives the intelligence its
direction (BG 3.42).
