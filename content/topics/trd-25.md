## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on sps-03 (God is a person) and sps-32 (three kinds of transcendentalists): those pages establish that Krishna is a
person and that bhakti is the highest path. This page gives the exact siddhanta behind both: how one Absolute is seen as
three, and why Bhagavan is "the last word". Builds on trd-24: the proof is from the Bhagavatam, now accepted as evidence.

A resident is asked, by educated people, to explain how the Upanisads' impersonal Brahman and the Gita's personal Krishna
can both be true. A loose answer ("it is all the same") loses Krishna; a harsh answer ("Brahman is false") denies scripture.
SB 1.2.11 gives the precise answer, and Jiva Gosvami's *Bhagavat-sandarbha* is built on that one verse. No lower batch takes
the three features as a subject.

## C. Central message

"Learned transcendentalists who know the Absolute Truth call this nondual substance Brahman, Paramātmā or Bhagavān" (SB 1.2.11). The three are one Absolute Truth seen by three processes: speculative knowledge sees Brahman, yoga sees Paramatma, devotion sees Bhagavan (CC Madhya 20.157). "Bhagavān, or the Personality of Godhead, is the last word of the Absolute Truth. Paramātmā is the partial representation of the Personality of Godhead, and impersonal Brahman is the glowing effulgence of the Personality of Godhead, as the sun rays are to the sun-god" (SB 1.2.11 purport).

## D. Learning outcomes

Residents should be able to:
1. Recite SB 1.2.11 and explain each term from its purport: nondual substance, three features, "different perspective views".
2. Match each feature with its process and its scriptural seat: Brahman and jnana (Upanisads, BG 14.27), Paramatma and yoga
   (BG 13.23, 18.61), Bhagavan and bhakti (CC Madhya 20.157–159).
3. Show why Bhagavan is the complete feature: "I am the basis of the impersonal Brahman" (BG 14.27); the effulgence is His
   bodily rays (CC Adi 2.5, Bs 5.40); the Supersoul is His plenary portion (CC Adi 2.5).
4. Explain the meaning of the word *bhagavan* as Jiva Gosvami gives it and Prabhupada repeats it (CC Adi 2.10 purport).
5. Answer "all paths reach the same God" and "God is just energy" without denying Brahman or losing Krishna.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**SB 1.2.11**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/11/
> "Learned transcendentalists who know the Absolute Truth call this nondual substance Brahman, Paramātmā or Bhagavān."
- **Context:** Suta Gosvami answering the sages of Naimisaranya on the essence of all scriptures.
- **Teaching:** One nondual substance (*advaya-jnana*), three names, known by those who know the truth (*tattva-vidah*).
- **Purport point:** "The same substance is realized as impersonal Brahman by the students of the Upaniṣads, as localized Paramātmā by the Hiraṇyagarbhas or the yogīs, and as Bhagavān by the devotees." "Bhagavān, or the Personality of Godhead, is the last word of the Absolute Truth. Paramātmā is the partial representation of the Personality of Godhead, and impersonal Brahman is the glowing effulgence of the Personality of Godhead, as the sun rays are to the sun-god." "Less intelligent students of either of the above schools sometimes argue in favor of their own respective realization, but those who are perfect seers of the Absolute Truth know well that the above three features of the one Absolute Truth are different perspective views seen from different angles of vision." The purport adds that in the Absolute "both the knower and the known are one and the same thing", and that "There is no difference between the energy and energetic, but there is a difference of quality of energies."
- **Use:** in the talk. The central verse; have it on a slide with the purport sentences above.

**CC Adi 2.5, 2.10, 2.11**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/adi/2/5/
> 2.5: "What the Upaniṣads describe as the impersonal Brahman is but the effulgence of His body, and the Lord known as the Supersoul is but His localized plenary portion. Lord Caitanya is the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Kṛṣṇa Himself, full with six opulences. He is the Absolute Truth, and no other truth is greater than or equal to Him."
> 2.10: "In terms of His various manifestations, He is known in three features, called the impersonal Brahman, the localized Paramātmā and the original Personality of Godhead."
- **Context:** Krsnadasa Kaviraja Gosvami establishing who Lord Caitanya is, through SB 1.2.11.
- **Purport point (2.5):** "Impersonal Brahman realization is the partial, distant experience of the Absolute Truth that one achieves through the rational approach. It is compared to one's seeing a hill from a distance and taking it to be a smoky cloud." "The impersonal glowing effulgence of Brahman consists only of the personal bodily rays of the Supreme Godhead, Śrī Kṛṣṇa."
- **Purport point (2.10):** "Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī has explained the word bhagavān in his Bhagavat-sandarbha." The syllables: "bha, which means 'sustainer' and 'protector.' The next letter, ga, means 'leader,' 'pusher' and 'creator.' Va means 'dwelling' (all living beings dwell in the Supreme Lord, and the Supreme Lord dwells within the heart of every living being)." So the word "carries the import of inconceivable potency in knowledge, energy, strength, opulence, power and influence, devoid of all varieties of inferiority." Impersonal Brahman "is a partial manifestation of the Absolute Truth realized in the absence of such complete potencies."
- **Purport point (2.11):** "one who tries to approach the Absolute simply by mental speculation will ultimately realize the impersonal Brahman, and one who tries to approach the Absolute through yoga practice will be able to realize Paramātmā, but one who has complete knowledge and spiritual understanding realizes the spiritual form of Bhagavān". The devotee "does not discriminate between Śrī Kṛṣṇa's name, form, qualities and pastimes"; "A pure devotee knows that when he chants the transcendental name 'Kṛṣṇa,' Śrī Kṛṣṇa is present as transcendental sound." "This lack of absolute knowledge is called māyā."
- **Use:** in the talk. The 2.10 purport is the only place on this page where *Bhagavat-sandarbha* is quoted, through Prabhupada.

**CC Adi 2.63–65**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/adi/2/65/
> 2.65: "Lord Kṛṣṇa Himself is the one undivided Absolute Truth, the ultimate reality. He manifests Himself in three features — as Brahman, Paramātmā and Bhagavān."
- **Teaching (2.63–64):** SB 1.2.11 is quoted again and the author asks his readers to "consider its meaning: the one original entity is known in His three different features."
- **Purport point (2.65):** "the principal word, bhagavān, indicates the Personality of Godhead, and Brahman and Paramātmā are concomitants deduced from the Absolute Personality, as a government and its ministers are deductions from the supreme executive head."
- **Use:** in the talk (the government and its ministers).

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** The Absolute Truth is one, nondual (*advaya*). It is not divided into three gods or three levels of
   reality. The three names describe how the one truth appears to three kinds of seekers (SB 1.2.11 and purport; CC Madhya
   20.157, 24.80). The differences are in the seers, "different perspective views seen from different angles of vision", not
   in the truth seen.
2. **Three processes, three features.**
   - *Jnana*, speculative knowledge by negation of matter, reaches **Brahman**: the Lord's effulgence, without variety
     (CC Madhya 20.159; Bs 5.40; CC Adi 2.5). It is real, and it is His (BG 14.27: "I am the basis").
   - *Yoga*, meditation within, reaches **Paramatma**: the Lord in the heart, "the overseer and permitter" (BG 13.23), who
     directs the wanderings of all beings (BG 18.61). He is His "localized plenary portion" (CC Adi 2.5), different from
     the atma (BG 13.23 purport).
   - *Bhakti* reaches **Bhagavan**: the Person full of six opulences, "the last word of the Absolute Truth" (SB 1.2.11
     purport), who is Krishna (CC Adi 2.65; BG 7.7; BG 10.12–13).
3. **Why Bhagavan is complete.** The word itself says so: sustainer, leader, dwelling-place, "inconceivable potency in knowledge, energy, strength, opulence, power and influence" (CC Adi 2.10 purport). Brahman is this truth "realized in the absence of such complete potencies"; Paramatma is His partial representation. "Both Paramātmā and the impersonal Brahman are within the Supreme Person" (BG 14.27 purport). Krsnadasa Kaviraja's image: a government and its ministers are deductions from the chief executive (CC Adi 2.65 purport).
4. **Nondual rightly understood.** The oneness is not the merging of the soul into Brahman; it is that the Lord's name, form,
   qualities and pastimes are nondifferent from Him, and that "both the knower and the known are one and the same thing" in
   the Absolute (SB 1.2.11 purport). Hence the devotee chanting Krishna's name knows "Śrī Kṛṣṇa is present as transcendental sound" (CC Adi 2.11 purport). To separate the name from the person is "lacking in absolute knowledge", and that lack "is called māyā".
5. **Connections.**
   - trd-23: the three features belong to sambandha; Prabhupada says spiritual knowledge "means fully understanding the Absolute Truth in three features" (CC Madhya 25.103 purport).
   - trd-24: Vyasadeva in trance "did not take notice of the effulgence of Brahman because that is not absolute vision" (SB 1.7.4 purport). The Bhagavatam is written from the Bhagavan vision.
   - trd-26: the Lord's energies and the jiva are explained inside the Paramatma feature.
   - trd-27: which person is Bhagavan Himself; this page establishes only that Bhagavan is the complete feature.
   - sps-32: the three transcendentalists are the three seers of this page.
6. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *"Brahman is false."* No: Krishna says "I am the basis of the impersonal Brahman" (BG 14.27) and calls it "immortal, imperishable and eternal". It is His effulgence (CC Adi 2.5).
   - *"The three are equal, so all paths are equal."* The features are of one truth, but they are not equally complete; the
     impersonal path is "very troublesome" (BG 12.5) and stopping there "runs the risk of falling down" (BG 14.27 purport).
   - *"God was impersonal first and became a person."* This is exactly the view of "unintelligent men" in BG 7.24.
   - *"Paramatma is the soul itself."* "His name is Paramātmā, not ātmā" (BG 13.23 purport).
   - *"Bhagavan is one more feature among three."* He is the whole of which the other two are parts (CC Adi 2.65 purport).
7. **Pitch for TRD.** Residents must hold two things at once: deep respect for what the Upanisads say about Brahman, and
   unshaken clarity that Krishna is the complete truth. Teach from SB 1.2.11 outwards and keep the sun analogy in view; it
   lets a preacher honour the sunshine without forgetting the sun.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet the residents and say that today's session gives the exact answer to the most common educated objection they
   meet: "all paths lead to the same formless God."
2. Ask them to write privately, in one sentence, how they answered that objection the last time they heard it.
3. Read SB 1.2.11 slowly, twice. Ask: does this verse say there are three truths, or one? Take two answers.
4. Say that Lord Caitanya quoted this one verse to Sanatana Gosvami, that Krsnadasa Kaviraja built the second chapter of
   the Caitanya-caritamrta on it, and that Jiva Gosvami wrote a whole book on it.
5. Ask H.2 question 1 and take three answers.

**H.2 Opening questions**
1. When someone says "God is just energy, not a person", what exactly are they right about, and what are they missing?
2. On a bright day you feel the sun on your skin without looking at it. What have you experienced of the sun, and what
   have you not?
3. Why do you think different sincere seekers describe the Absolute so differently?

## I. Core teaching points

1. **One substance, three names.** SB 1.2.11: the Absolute Truth is nondual; those who know it call it Brahman, Paramatma
   or Bhagavan. Not three gods, not three levels of reality. *Example:* the sun seen by ordinary eyes "appears to consist simply of effulgence" (CC Madhya 20.159). *Transition:* what decides which feature a seeker meets?
2. **The process decides the feature.** Speculative knowledge reaches Brahman, yoga reaches Paramatma, bhakti reaches
   Bhagavan (CC Madhya 20.157; 24.80; SB 1.2.11 purport). *Transition:* let us define each precisely.
3. **Brahman is His effulgence; He is its basis.** "I am the basis of the impersonal Brahman" (BG 14.27); the effulgence is
   "the rays of Kṛṣṇa's bodily effulgence" (CC Madhya 20.159; Bs 5.40; CC Adi 2.5). *Transition:* and the Lord within?
4. **Paramatma is the Lord in the heart, not the soul.** "The overseer and permitter" (BG 13.23), directing all beings
   (BG 18.61); "His name is Paramātmā, not ātmā" (BG 13.23 purport). *Transition:* and the complete feature?
5. **Bhagavan is the whole.** The word means sustainer, leader, dwelling-place, full of "inconceivable potency" (CC Adi 2.10
   purport, from *Bhagavat-sandarbha*); "the last word of the Absolute Truth" (SB 1.2.11 purport); Brahman and Paramatma
   are "within the Supreme Person" (BG 14.27 purport). He is Krishna (CC Adi 2.65; BG 7.7; BG 10.12–13). *Transition:* so
   what does "nondual" really mean?
6. **Nondual means His name, form and qualities are Himself.** The devotee chanting knows Krishna "is present as transcendental sound" (CC Adi 2.11 purport). Not the soul merging, but the Lord fully present in His name. *Transition:* why not simply stay with Brahman?
7. **Stopping at Brahman is incomplete and risky.** The Kumaras rose from Brahman to bhakti; one who stops "runs the risk of falling down" (BG 14.27 purport); the impersonal path is "very troublesome" for the embodied (BG 12.5); to think God was impersonal first is the view of "unintelligent men" (BG 7.24). The remedy is hearing and chanting, which bring the complete feature.

## J. Stories and illustrations

**The four Kumaras** (BG 14.27 purport). Srila Prabhupada states: "The four Kumāras were situated first in the impersonal Brahman conception of truth, but then they gradually rose to the platform of devotional service." He adds the warning: "One who cannot elevate himself beyond the impersonal conception of Brahman runs the risk of falling down." *Devotional lesson:* even perfect sages who had Brahman realisation went further to bhakti; Brahman is a stage, Bhagavan the destination. (The narration of how the Kumaras were attracted by the fragrance of tulasi at the Lord's lotus feet is in SB 3.15; read it there before telling it.)

**The sun seen from the ground** (CC Madhya 20.159; SB 1.2.11 purport). Lord Caitanya: the Brahman effulgence "is exactly like the sun. When the sun is seen by our ordinary eyes, it appears to consist simply of effulgence." Prabhupada: Brahman is to Bhagavan "as the sun rays are to the sun-god." *Devotional lesson:* the sunshine is real and is the sun's; but the one who knows only the sunshine has not met the sun-god. Devotion is the eye that sees the person.

**The hill and the smoky cloud** (CC Adi 2.5 purport). Impersonal realisation "is compared to one's seeing a hill from a distance and taking it to be a smoky cloud. A hill is not a smoky cloud, but it appears to be one from a distance because of our imperfect vision." *Devotional lesson:* the impersonalist is not seeing nothing; he is seeing the Lord from far away. The preacher's work is to bring him closer, not to tell him he saw nothing.

**Vyasadeva passes over the effulgence** (SB 1.7.4 and purport). In trance, fixed in bhakti-yoga, Vyasadeva "saw the Absolute Personality of Godhead along with His external energy". Prabhupada: he "did not take notice of the effulgence of Brahman because that is not absolute vision." *Devotional lesson:* the author of the Vedanta-sutra himself looked past Brahman to the Person; the Bhagavatam is written from that vision.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. *"All religions and paths reach the same God. Why insist on a person?"* The Bhagavatam agrees there is one truth
   (SB 1.2.11) and says what each path reaches: knowledge reaches the effulgence, yoga the Lord in the heart, devotion the
   Person (CC Madhya 20.157). The paths are not equal in what they see; Krishna is the basis of Brahman (BG 14.27).
2. *"Isn't 'God is energy' the higher, more scientific view?"* Energy belongs to an energetic; Brahman is the effulgence of
   Krishna's body (CC Adi 2.5; Bs 5.40). The view that God was impersonal and later took a form is what Krishna calls the view
   of "unintelligent men" (BG 7.24).
3. *"The Upanisads say Brahman is formless. Are they wrong?"* No. They describe the effulgence correctly (CC Adi 2.5: "What the Upaniṣads describe as the impersonal Brahman is but the effulgence of His body"). The Gita, Bhagavatam and Brahma-samhita then say whose effulgence it is.
4. *"Is Paramatma the same as my soul?"* No: "His name is Paramātmā, not ātmā... It is distinctly clear that the ātmā and Paramātmā are different" (BG 13.23 purport). He is the overseer and permitter in the heart (BG 13.23, 18.61).
5. *"If Brahman realisation is real, why not aim for it? It seems safer than devotion to a person."* It is "very troublesome" for the embodied (BG 12.5), and stopping there "runs the risk of falling down" (BG 14.27 purport). The Kumaras went on to bhakti.
6. *"How can the name of Krishna be Krishna? That sounds like idol worship of a sound."* In the Absolute, knower and known are one (SB 1.2.11 purport); the pure devotee knows Krishna "is present as transcendental sound" (CC Adi 2.11 purport). Separating name from person is the lack of absolute knowledge, "called māyā".
7. *"Why study *Bhagavat-sandarbha* when Prabhupada's purports say all this?"* Because Prabhupada draws on it himself (CC Adi 2.10 purport) and because residents who teach siddhanta should know its systematic form; but the principle is already fully given in the purports on this page.

## L. Case studies

**Case 1: The yoga teacher.** *Invented case for discussion.* A yoga teacher visits the temple and says: "I experience a
presence within in meditation. That is God. Your Krishna is a symbol of that presence."
- *Dilemma:* deny his experience, or accept his conclusion.
- *Principles:* SB 1.2.11 and purport; BG 13.23, 18.61; CC Madhya 20.157–158.
- *Questions:* What has he realised, in the Bhagavatam's terms? What is true in his statement? What has he reversed?
- *Analysis:* He has approached Paramatma by yoga, a real feature of the one truth (CC Madhya 20.157). The Supersoul in the
  heart is the Lord's "localized plenary portion" (CC Adi 2.5), not a symbol pointing to a feeling; Krishna is the whole of
  which the presence is a part. The resident honours the experience, defines it from BG 13.23, and invites him to hear how
  the Lord in the heart is a person who can be served.
- *Takeaway:* never deny a real realisation; place it and point beyond it.

**Case 2: The resident who went too far.** *Invented case for discussion.* Madhava, a brahmacari, tells a Vedanta student
that Brahman "does not exist" and that the Upanisads are "for fools". The student leaves offended, and Madhava feels he
defended Krishna.
- *Dilemma:* zeal without siddhanta.
- *Principles:* BG 14.27 ("I am the basis of the impersonal Brahman"); CC Adi 2.5; SB 1.2.11 purport ("different perspective views").
- *Questions:* What did Madhava deny that Krishna affirms? How could he have said the same truth without denying scripture?
- *Analysis:* Krishna calls Brahman "immortal, imperishable and eternal" and Himself its basis. The siddhanta is not that
  Brahman is false but that it is the Lord's effulgence, seen from far. Madhava should have said what Lord Caitanya said to
  Sanatana (CC Madhya 20.159), honouring the sunshine and naming the sun.
- *Takeaway:* precision is a form of respect; the exact siddhanta wins where zeal loses.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Many educated people say that all paths lead to the same formless God, and that Krishna is one symbol among many. The Srimad-Bhagavatam answers precisely: "Learned transcendentalists who know the Absolute Truth call this nondual substance Brahman, Paramātmā or Bhagavān" (SB 1.2.11). There is one Absolute Truth, not three; the three names describe how it is seen by three kinds of seekers. Lord Caitanya explained to Sanatana Gosvami: speculative knowledge reaches Brahman, mystic yoga reaches Paramatma, and bhakti-yoga reaches Bhagavan (CC Madhya 20.157).

Brahman, the formless effulgence described in the Upanisads, is real; Krishna says, "I am the basis of the impersonal Brahman" (BG 14.27), and the Caitanya-caritamrta says it "is but the effulgence of His body" (CC Adi 2.5). Lord Caitanya compared it to the sun seen by ordinary eyes, which "appears to consist simply of effulgence" (CC Madhya 20.159). Paramatma is the Lord within every heart, "the overseer and permitter" (BG 13.23), who directs all living beings (BG 18.61); He is not the soul itself. Bhagavan is the complete Person. Srila Prabhupada writes: "Bhagavān, or the Personality of Godhead, is the last word of the Absolute Truth. Paramātmā is the partial representation of the Personality of Godhead, and impersonal Brahman is the glowing effulgence of the Personality of Godhead, as the sun rays are to the sun-god" (SB 1.2.11 purport). Srila Jiva Gosvami explains the word *bhagavan* in his *Bhagavat-sandarbha*: *bha* means sustainer and protector, *ga* leader and creator, *va* dwelling-place (CC Adi 2.10 purport).

"Nondual" does not mean the soul merges into Brahman. It means that Krishna's name, form, qualities and pastimes are not different from Him; the pure devotee chanting knows that "Śrī Kṛṣṇa is present as transcendental sound" (CC Adi 2.11 purport). The four Kumaras rose from Brahman realisation to devotional service, and one who stops at Brahman "runs the risk of falling down" (BG 14.27 purport). The impersonal path is "very troublesome" for the embodied, while bhakti is natural (BG 12.5 and purport). So the preacher honours what every sincere seeker has seen, and points to the Person who is the basis of it all.

## S2. Key ideas
- One nondual Absolute Truth, three features: Brahman, Paramatma, Bhagavan (SB 1.2.11).
- The process decides the feature: knowledge sees Brahman, yoga sees Paramatma, devotion sees Bhagavan (CC Madhya 20.157).
- Brahman is Krishna's effulgence; He is its basis (BG 14.27; CC Adi 2.5). Paramatma is His portion in the heart, not the soul (BG 13.23).
- Bhagavan, Krishna, is the complete truth, "the last word" (SB 1.2.11 purport).
- Krishna's name is Krishna present (CC Adi 2.11 purport).

## S3. Verses to remember
- **SB 1.2.11:** "Learned transcendentalists who know the Absolute Truth call this nondual substance Brahman, Paramātmā or Bhagavān." *For me:* one truth; I seek the complete feature.
- **BG 14.27:** "And I am the basis of the impersonal Brahman". *For me:* when someone says "God is light", I say whose light.
- **CC Madhya 20.157:** three processes, three features. *For me:* devotion is the process that meets the Person.

## S4. Words to know
- **Brahman:** the impersonal, all-pervading spiritual effulgence; Krishna's bodily rays.
- **Paramatma:** the Supersoul, the Lord's expansion within every heart.
- **Bhagavan:** the Supreme Person full of six opulences; Krishna.
- **Advaya-jnana:** nondual knowledge; the one Absolute Truth without a second.
- **Jnana, yoga, bhakti:** speculative knowledge, meditation, devotional service; the three processes.
- **Bhagavat-sandarbha:** Jiva Gosvami's second Sandarbha, built on SB 1.2.11.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *Don't all paths reach the same God?* There is one truth, but the paths see different features of it; devotion sees the complete Person (SB 1.2.11; CC Madhya 20.157).
2. *Isn't "God is energy" the higher view?* Energy belongs to someone; Brahman is the effulgence of Krishna's body, and He is its basis (CC Adi 2.5; BG 14.27).
3. *Are the Upanisads wrong about formless Brahman?* No; they describe the effulgence correctly. The Gita and Bhagavatam say whose effulgence it is (CC Adi 2.5).
4. *Is Paramatma my soul?* No; "His name is Paramātmā, not ātmā" (BG 13.23 purport).
5. *Why not stay with Brahman?* It is "very troublesome" for the embodied (BG 12.5), and stopping there risks falling (BG 14.27 purport); the Kumaras went on to bhakti.

## S6. Check your understanding

2. Which process reaches Paramatma?
3. Complete the sentence from BG 14.27: And I am the ________ of the impersonal Brahman.
4. True or false: Paramatma and the individual soul are the same.
5. What do the syllables *bha*, *ga* and *va* mean in *bhagavan*?
6. Short answer: what does "nondual" mean for the person chanting Krishna's name?

**Answers:** 1. (b). 2. Mystic yoga (CC Madhya 20.157). 3. basis. 4. False (BG 13.23 purport). 5. Sustainer and protector;
leader, pusher and creator; dwelling (CC Adi 2.10 purport). 6. Krishna is present in His name as transcendental sound; the
name is not different from Him (CC Adi 2.11 purport).
