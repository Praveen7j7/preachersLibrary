# Who should be object of my worship

> **VL2 · 180 min · review: sources-checked.** Sensitive: the talk touches how listeners' families worship (demigods,
> other faiths). Read F.6 and K before teaching. The same title is taught at SPS as sps-01; B says what SPS adds.
> IYF's own notes: `research/iyf-materials/vl2-04/` (slides "Who is God", "Why do we accept Krishna as supreme")
> and the EBG chapter in `research/iyf-materials/sps2-01/`.

## A. Topic overview

| | |
|---|---|
| Batch / level | VL2 (Vedic Level 2: reasoned faith) |
| Suggested duration | About 3 hours: 90 min lecture + 90 min engagement |
| Preparation time | About 120 min the first time: the Gita chapters 7, 9 and 10 must be read, not skimmed |
| Difficulty | Intermediate |
| Prerequisites | VL2-01 (the soul), VL2-02 (a basic idea of God as a person) help; not required |
| Audience | Young people who already grant that God may exist and now ask: *which* God, and does it matter whom I worship? Many come from families that worship several deities |
| Teaching objective | Give a reasoned, respectful case that the Gita presents Krsna as the one Supreme Person, the source of the demigods, and that worship of Him is the recommended path, while honouring the sincerity of other worship |

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

**Builds on [dys-03](dys-03.md) (DYS 6, If God is one, why so many religions?):** that session gave the principle (God meets each hearer as they approach, BG 4.11), the ladder of fear, desire, duty and love, respect for all faiths, and stated the claim about Krsna (BG 10.12-13; SB 1.3.28). This page does not repeat them. It takes up the Gita's own reasoning about the *object* of worship: the real but limited role of the demigods (BG 3.12, 7.20-23, 9.23-25), how Arjuna accepts the claim, and a practical test (BG 9.26).


At VL1 the question is "Why explore spirituality at all?". At VL2 the listener grants that much and asks
"Why accept *this*?". "Whom should I worship?" is the first place that question becomes concrete: the listener's
family altar, the temple down the road, a friend's church or mosque. A VL2 talk must therefore do three things
at once: present the Gita's claim with its reasons, distinguish honest questions from misconceptions, and speak
about every other object of worship with respect.

**IYF's VL2 frame** (IYF's own documents, `research/batch-criteria.md` §2 and §8 point 2): course name "Technical Science
for Spiritual Value", mood *questioning, reasoned*; motive "developing strong faith in practicing spirituality" and "removing all
misconceptions about ISKCON and spirituality"; KRAs "Start doing chanting", "Coming to regular classes", "Meeting with
facilitators". The 2015 VL2 lesson plan runs DYS 1–6 first and then the EBG study-sessions; this page mirrors the EBG sessions "Who should be the Object of My Worship", "Different Platforms of Worship" and "God and Gods" (Encyclopedia transcripts, O). Practice is
expected to *begin* during VL2 (by its end: 2–4 rounds, 15 min of Prabhupada's books and 15–30 min of kirtan hearing a day), so
this page may carry a first practical step without breaking IYF's level (P).

**Not done here:**
- Running through the eighteen Puranas by mode: SPS material, and the Matsya Purana list needs a source check.
- Any mockery of demigod worship or of other faiths.
- Treating "God is one" as if it ended the discussion: the Gita's point is *who* that one is and *how* one approaches Him.

## C. Central message

The Gita does not say "worship anyone, it is all the same". It says there is one Supreme Person who is the source
of everything, including the demigods (BG 7.7, 10.8, 10.2); that worship offered elsewhere reaches Him indirectly
and yields limited results (BG 7.23, 9.23–25); and that He accepts even a leaf or water offered with love (BG 9.26).
A reasonable person can test this claim by reading the text and by trying the practice.

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. State the Gita's claim about Krsna in its own words (BG 7.7, 10.8) and name who confirms it in the text (BG 10.12–13).
2. Explain the difference the Gita draws between God and the demigods, and between the results of worshipping each (BG 7.20–23, 9.25).
3. Say accurately what BG 9.23 does and does not say about those who worship other gods.
4. Respond to "Isn't all worship the same?" with the Gita's answer (BG 4.11, 9.23) without disrespecting anyone.
5. Choose one way to test the claim this week (BG 9.26).

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** eight references carry the talk (BG 7.7, 10.8, 10.12–13, 7.20, 7.23, 9.23, 9.25, 9.26). The rest prepare you for K and for SPS.

**BG 7.7**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/7/7/
> "O conqueror of wealth, there is no truth superior to Me. Everything rests upon Me, as pearls are strung on a thread."
- **Context:** Chapter 7, where Krsna explains His energies and how He is to be known.
- **Teaching:** Krsna states that nothing is superior to Him and that everything rests on Him.
- **Application (preacher's):** The claim is explicit. The honest question for a VL2 listener is not "Is this in the text?" but "Is it true?" Say so.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 10.8**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/10/8/
> "I am the source of all spiritual and material worlds. Everything emanates from Me. The wise who perfectly know this engage in My devotional service and worship Me with all their hearts."
- **Teaching:** Krsna is the source of both worlds; those who know this worship Him.
- **Purport point:** Prabhupada collects supporting statements from the Upanisads and Puranas (Gopala-tapani, Narayana and Maha Upanisads, Varaha Purana) that Narayana is the source of Brahma and Siva, and states that this Narayana is an expansion of Krsna. He adds that one who understands this "from a bona fide spiritual master, with references from Vedic literature" becomes fixed in devotion.
- **Application (preacher's):** For a VL2 audience the purport is useful because it shows that the claim is not one verse standing alone but a reading of the wider literature. Say "the tradition reads these texts together", not "every Hindu text agrees".
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 10.12–13, 10.14**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/10/12-13/ and /10/14/
> 10.12–13: "…All the great sages such as Nārada, Asita, Devala and Vyāsa confirm this truth about You, and now You Yourself are declaring it to me."
> 10.14: "O Kṛṣṇa, I totally accept as truth all that You have told me…"
- **Teaching:** Arjuna accepts Krsna's statement, citing the sages' testimony and Krsna's own declaration.
- **Application (preacher's):** This is the Gita's own model of reasoned faith: testimony of trusted authorities plus the speaker's self-disclosure, then acceptance. Present it as a model the listener can examine, not as a demand.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 7.20, 7.21, 7.22, 7.23**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/7/20/ to /23/
> 7.20: "Those whose intelligence has been stolen by material desires surrender unto demigods and follow the particular rules and regulations of worship according to their own natures."
> 7.22: "…he endeavors to worship a particular demigod and obtains his desires. But in actuality these benefits are bestowed by Me alone."
> 7.23: "Men of small intelligence worship the demigods, and their fruits are limited and temporary. Those who worship the demigods go to the planets of the demigods, but My devotees ultimately reach My supreme planet."
- **Teaching:** Demigod worship is driven by material desire; the Lord Himself steadies the worshipper's faith (7.21) and grants the result (7.22); the fruits are limited and temporary; destinations differ (7.23).
- **Purport point (7.20):** Prabhupada notes that the Vedic literature itself recommends worship of various gods for particular purposes ("a diseased man is recommended to worship the sun"), which is why some think demigods are better for certain ends; a pure devotee depends on the Supreme Lord and "is satisfied with whatever He gives".
- **Purport point (7.23):** Prabhupada answers the objection "if the demigods are parts of the Lord's body, worshipping them should give the same result": food must be given to the stomach, not the ears or eyes; demigods are parts of the Lord's universal body, and so are we; the results of their benedictions are perishable because the planets, the demigods and their worshippers are all perishable.
- **Application (preacher's):** Keep the tone the verse has: it is about the *result* and the *motive*, not a verdict on the worshipper's worth. The Lord Himself steadies their faith (7.21), which is hardly contempt.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 9.23, 9.25**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/9/23/ and /9/25/
> 9.23: "Those who are devotees of other gods and who worship them with faith actually worship only Me, O son of Kuntī, but they do so in a wrong way."
> 9.25: "Those who worship the demigods will take birth among the demigods… and those who worship Me will live with Me."
- **Teaching:** Worship of other gods, done with faith, reaches Krsna indirectly but by an improper method; the destination follows the object of worship.
- **Purport point (9.23):** The purport's images: watering the leaves and branches instead of the root; feeding the stomach rather than the limbs; the demigods as "officers and directors in the government of the Supreme Lord", whom one does not bribe. The purport ends: "Kṛṣṇa does not approve the unnecessary worship of the demigods."
- **Application (preacher's):** 9.23 is the verse that lets you be both clear and kind: the faith is real and it does reach God; the method is the issue. Never shorten it to "they worship wrongly".
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 9.26**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/9/26/
> "If one offers Me with love and devotion a leaf, a flower, a fruit or water, I will accept it."
- **Teaching:** The Lord accepts a simple offering made with love.
- **Purport point:** "No one, therefore, can be barred from Kṛṣṇa consciousness, because it is so easy and universal"; what Krsna wants is "only loving service and nothing more".
- **Application (preacher's):** This is the testable step for P: the claim can be tried, not only argued.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 10.2, BG 3.12, BG 14.27, BG 5.29**: Verified.
- 10.2: neither the demigods nor the sages know Krsna's origin; He is their source. 3.12: the demigods, satisfied by yajna, supply necessities; enjoying without offering is theft (purport: the demigods are "authorized supplying agents" of the Lord; all yajnas are "ultimately offered to the Supreme Personality of Godhead"). 14.27: Krsna is the basis of the impersonal Brahman. 5.29: knowing Him as the Lord of all planets and demigods brings peace. **Use:** for K.

**BG 4.11**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/4/11/
> "As all surrender unto Me, I reward them accordingly. Everyone follows My path in all respects, O son of Pṛthā."
- **Purport point:** "Kṛṣṇa is the object of everyone's realization, and thus anyone and everyone is satisfied according to one's desire to have Him"; "all kinds of spiritual processes are but different degrees of success on the same path".
- **Application (preacher's):** The purport's "different degrees of success on the same path" is the respectful frame for other faiths. It is also a claim about *degrees*, so do not flatten it into "all paths are equal".
- **Use:** for K.4.

**BG 18.66**: Verified. "Abandon all varieties of religion and just surrender unto Me." **Use:** background: the Gita's conclusion; introduce only at the end, as where the argument leads.

**SB 1.3.28, SB 1.9.18, SB 1.2.11**: Verified.
- 1.3.28: the incarnations are plenary portions or portions of portions, "but Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa is the original Personality of Godhead" (purport: Krsna "exhibited the complete features of Godhead", so He is the source of the other incarnations). 1.9.18: Bhisma calls Krsna "the first Nārāyaṇa". 1.2.11: the one nondual substance is called Brahman, Paramatma or Bhagavan. **Use:** for K.5 ("Krsna or Visnu?") and SPS.

**SB 10.89.1, 10.89.2, 10.89.14–17**: Verified · *BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples.*
- 10.89.1–2: sages at a sacrifice on the Sarasvati dispute "which of the three chief deities is supreme" and send Bhrgu to test them. 10.89.14–17: hearing his account, the sages "were freed from all doubts and became convinced that Viṣṇu is the greatest Lord"; from Him come peace, fearlessness, the principles of religion and "detachment with knowledge".
- **Use:** J, briefly. The full narrative is SPS (sps-01) material; IYF's EBG chapter retells it from *Krsna* book.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** The Gita's claim is specific: one Supreme Person is the source of everything (7.7, 10.8), including the demigods and the sages (10.2) and even the impersonal Brahman (14.27). The demigods are real and have real functions (3.12), but they are not the source; worship directed to them is, at best, indirect (9.23) and its results are limited (7.23).
2. **Why it matters.** Whom you worship shapes what you ask for and what you become. Worship driven by material desire keeps the desire at the centre (7.20); worship of the source turns the heart towards the source (10.8). The Gita links the two: object and transformation.
3. **How the Gita argues it (reasoned faith).** Three kinds of ground appear in the text, and a VL2 talk should name them:
   - *Testimony:* Arjuna cites Narada, Asita, Devala and Vyasa (10.12–13).
   - *Self-disclosure:* Krsna states who He is (7.7, 10.8); Arjuna accepts "all that You have told me" (10.14).
   - *Experience:* the invitation to offer something and see (9.26); the Bhagavatam's promise of knowledge and detachment following devotion (SB 10.89.14–17 purport quotes SB 11.2.42).
   None of these compels a sceptic. Together they are what the tradition means by faith that is reasonable.
4. **Connections.** Forward to VL2-05 (what the Vedic "manual" is for) and VL2-07 (why a guide is needed to read these texts), and to sps-01, which adds Bhrgu's test, the Puranas by mode and the Krsna–Visnu question.
5. **Consequences.** A person who accepts this will not abandon respect for other deities (3.12 forbids ingratitude to them) but will redirect worship to the root. Family tension can follow; see K.6.
6. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - **"Demigod worshippers are fools."** 7.23 says "small intelligence" about the *choice of goal*, and 7.21 says the Lord Himself steadies their faith. The text is sharper about motive than about people. Preachers who sneer misrepresent it.
   - **"All paths are the same."** 4.11 purport: "different degrees of success on the same path". Degrees, not sameness; 7.23 says destinations differ.
   - **"ISKCON invented Krsna's supremacy."** The claim is in the Gita (7.7, 10.8) and the Bhagavatam (1.3.28). Others read these texts differently; say so, and point to the verses.
   - **"God is one, so it does not matter."** The Gita agrees God is one and then spends chapters 7–10 on who He is.
7. **Pitch for VL2.** Define *demigod* (deva: an empowered being in charge of a function), *Bhagavan*, *Brahman*, *Paramatma* once each (SB 1.2.11). Argue from the text; invite the listener to open the Gita and check.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Welcome people by name; recall dys-03 in one line: "God meets each person as they come".
2. Ask H.2 question 1; collect answers without comment.
3. Say: "Tonight is not about who is right. We ask what the Gita itself says about the object of worship, and why."
4. Ask the room to promise one rule: no one's family altar is made small.
5. Write on the board: "whom, what for, how".

**Adapting for your audience:** if the group is not from a family with several deities, use "who or what do you turn to in a hard time?"; in a professional setting use "whom do you consult when a decision matters, and why that person?".

**H.2 Opening questions**
- At home, who is on the altar, or who do you turn to in a hard time? Who chose, and why?
- When you pray or hope, what do you usually ask for?
- If a source had made everything you rely on, would you thank that source or the helpers?

**More on opening and engagement**


- **Opening question:** "At home, who is on the altar? Who chose, and why?" Collect answers without comment. "Today we ask the Gita a question most families never ask out loud."
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** Before an exam a student prays to one deity for memory, to another for luck, and his grandmother tells him to light a lamp for a third. He wonders: is anyone listening, and does it matter whom I ask?
- **Transition:** "The Gita does not laugh at him. It says his prayer is heard (BG 7.21–22). Then it asks him a harder question: what are you asking *for*?"
- **Why it matters to this audience:** Young adults are deciding what of their family's practice to keep. A reasoned answer respects both the family and the question.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **One truth, known in three ways.** Brahman, Paramatma, Bhagavan (SB 1.2.11). *Example:* sunlight, the sun in the sky, the sun-god personally; an image of the preacher, not of the verse. *Transition:* the Gita says which of these is the source.
2. **The Gita's claim is explicit.** Nothing is superior to Krsna; everything rests on Him (7.7); He is the source of both worlds and of the demigods (10.8, 10.2). *Transition:* how does Arjuna respond?
3. **Arjuna's acceptance is reasoned** (dys-03 states the claim; here we examine how it is accepted). Testimony of sages plus Krsna's own words (10.12–14). *Example:* how we accept a doctor's diagnosis: credentials plus explanation. *Transition:* so what about the demigods?
4. **Demigods are real, with real jobs, but they are not the source.** They supply necessities and deserve gratitude (3.12); worship aimed at them for material desires brings limited results (7.20–23); it reaches the Lord indirectly and by a wrong method (9.23); destinations differ (9.25). *Example:* the purport's officers of a government. *Transition:* and other religions?
5. **Different points on the same path.** BG 4.11 and purport are taught in [dys-03](dys-03.md); here one sentence only, then straight to the degrees of 7.23. *Transition:* can I test any of this?
6. **The claim is testable.** Offer a leaf, a flower, fruit or water with love (9.26) and see what happens in your heart over weeks. *Transition:* to P.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

> **Before you teach:** someone's family deity will be named. Never say a deity is "false" or that worshippers are foolish. The Gita's own words are about motive, method and result (7.20–23, 9.23); stay there.

1. **"Isn't all worship the same? God is one."**
   - God is one; the Gita agrees and then asks *who* He is (7.7, 10.8). The principle of BG 4.11 is taught in [dys-03](dys-03.md); the purport calls the processes "different degrees of success on the same path". Degrees, not sameness (7.23: different destinations).
2. **"Why can't I worship Ganesh or Durga? My family always has."**
   - You can respect them; the Gita itself forbids ingratitude to the demigods (3.12). The question is what you ask for and whom you treat as the source. Worship with faith reaches Krsna indirectly (9.23); worship of the source is direct. Offer this as the Gita's position, not as a command to change the family altar tonight. Honest question, not objection.
3. **"Is this just ISKCON saying its God is the best?"**
   - The claim is in the text (7.7, 10.8, SB 1.3.28), older than any organisation. Others read the same texts differently (some traditions take Siva or the impersonal Brahman as supreme); the honest answer is "read the verses and judge". Avoid "our God is bigger" language entirely.
4. **"What about Christians and Muslims?"**
   - Answered in [dys-03](dys-03.md) (the principle; its K.1 and K.2). Speak with respect; do not rank religions in a talk. Chaitanya Charan's "Do all religions lead to God?" (https://www.thespiritualscientist.com/do-all-religions-lead-to-god/, 14 Apr 2017, linked from chaitanyacharan.com) argues for an "inclusivist" middle: many ways, tested by whether they move a person towards God. Supporting source, not authority.
5. **"Krsna or Visnu? Isn't Krsna one of Visnu's avatars?"**
   - SB 1.3.28 lists the incarnations and then says Krsna is "the original Personality of Godhead"; Bhisma calls Him "the first Nārāyaṇa" (SB 1.9.18). This is the Bhagavatam's position; the Krsna–Visnu relation is developed at SPS (sps-01).
6. **"If I change whom I worship, my parents will be hurt."**
   - *Pastoral guidance, not a scriptural claim:* nothing in this talk asks anyone to remove a family altar or argue at home. Begin with what adds (an offering, BG 9.26), not with what removes. Gratitude to parents is part of the culture being taught.
7. **"Isn't 'small intelligence' (7.23) an insult?"**
   - The verse judges a *goal* ("limited and temporary") and the purport explains the reasoning (feeding the body through the ears). It is not a statement about anyone's IQ or worth. If you cannot say this kindly, leave the verse out.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The exam altar.** Priya lights a lamp to three deities before every exam. After this talk she asks, "Should I stop?"
- **Principles:** BG 7.21–22 (her faith is steadied and her prayers answered by the Lord); 9.23 (indirect worship); 9.26 (add a loving offering to the source).
- **Questions:** What is Priya actually asking for? What would change if she offered the same lamp to Krsna with the same love? Must anything be removed?
- **Takeaway:** Change the direction and the motive before changing the furniture.

**Case 2: The argument at dinner.** Arjun tells his uncle, a lifelong Siva devotee, that "demigod worship is for the less intelligent" and quotes BG 7.23. The evening ends badly.
- **Principles:** BG 3.12 (gratitude to the demigods); 9.23 (faith that reaches Krsna); 4.11 purport (degrees on one path).
- **Questions:** Was Arjun's quotation accurate? Was it kind? How could he have raised the subject?
- **Takeaway:** A verse quoted without its purport and its tone can do harm.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first)
1. What does BG 10.8 say Krsna is the source of? *Expected:* everything, spiritual and material, including the demigods.
2. What does BG 3.12 say about the demigods? *Expected:* they supply our needs and deserve gratitude.
3. What two things differ in BG 7.23 between demigod worship and worship of Krsna? *Expected:* the result and the destination.
4. What does BG 9.23 say about worship of other gods with faith? *Expected:* it reaches Krsna, but by a wrong method.
5. What test does BG 9.26 offer? *Expected:* offer a leaf, flower, fruit or water with love and observe.
6. Does anyone have to change their family altar? *Expected:* No; begin with what adds. Praise respectful answers.

**M.2 Activities (engagement block, about 90 min; choose two)**
- **A. Verse-by-verse reading (35 min).** Groups of four receive one of BG 7.20-23, 9.23-25 or 10.12-14 with the purport. They write in their own words: motive, method, result (15 min); each group reads out one sentence and hears one question from the room (20 min).
- **B. Family-altar conversation practice (30 min).** Pairs rehearse the K.2 and K.6 answers: one plays a relative who asks, the other answers kindly, then swap. Rule: no verse is quoted as a weapon. Close by naming phrases that worked (10 min).
- **C. Offering plan (25 min).** Each person writes one offering they could make this week "with love rather than with a request" (a flower, a fruit, a glass of water, a song), when and where; nobody is asked to share. The preacher explains how to offer simply (10 min).

**M.3 Reflection (private, optional).** When I pray, what do I usually ask for? What does that say about whom I treat as the source? Which one offering could I make this week with love rather than with a request?

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Most of us grew up worshipping several deities without asking why. The Bhagavad-gita takes the question
seriously. It says there is one Supreme Person, Krsna, who is the source of everything, including the demigods
(BG 7.7, 10.8). Demigods are real and do real work, and we owe them gratitude (BG 3.12), but worship aimed at them
for material wishes gives limited, temporary results (BG 7.23), and worship of other gods reaches Krsna only
indirectly (BG 9.23). Everyone who sincerely worships is rewarded according to their approach (BG 4.11). The Gita
asks for faith that is reasoned: Arjuna accepts Krsna's claim because trusted sages confirm it and Krsna Himself
declares it (BG 10.12–14). And it gives a simple test: offer a leaf, a flower, a fruit or water with love (BG 9.26).

## S2. Key ideas

- God is one; the Gita's question is *who* He is and *how* we approach Him.
- Krsna says He is the source of everything, including the demigods (BG 7.7, 10.8, 10.2).
- Demigods deserve respect, but worshipping them for material desires gives limited results (BG 3.12, 7.20–23).
- Faith in other gods reaches Krsna indirectly (BG 9.23); sincere worship anywhere is never wasted (BG 4.11).
- Faith can be reasoned (BG 10.12–14) and tested (BG 9.26).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **BG 7.7:** "There is no truth superior to Me. Everything rests upon Me, as pearls are strung on a thread." *For me:* the claim is clear; my job is to examine it honestly.
- **BG 9.23:** Those who worship other gods with faith "actually worship only Me… but they do so in a wrong way." *For me:* no one's sincere worship is mocked; the method is the question.
- **BG 9.26:** "If one offers Me with love and devotion a leaf, a flower, a fruit or water, I will accept it." *For me:* I can test this.

## S4. Words to know

- **Demigod (deva):** a powerful being empowered by the Lord to manage a part of the universe (BG 3.12).
- **Bhagavan:** the Supreme Person, full in all opulence; one of three names for the one Absolute Truth, with Brahman and Paramatma (SB 1.2.11).
- **Brahman:** the all-pervading impersonal aspect of the Absolute; Krsna says He is its basis (BG 14.27).

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Isn't all worship the same?** Everyone who surrenders is rewarded accordingly (BG 4.11), but results and destinations differ (BG 7.23, 9.25). Same path, different points on it.
2. **Can I still respect Ganesh, Durga or Siva?** Yes. The Gita forbids ingratitude to the demigods (BG 3.12). The question is whom I treat as the source and what I ask for.
3. **What about my friends of other religions?** Sincere worship is never wasted (BG 4.11). We speak of every faith with respect.
4. **Is Krsna an avatar of Visnu?** The Bhagavatam lists the incarnations and then says Krsna is the original Personality of Godhead (SB 1.3.28). This is studied further at SPS.
5. **Do I have to change my family's altar?** No one asked that. Start by adding one loving offering (BG 9.26), not by removing anything.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. According to BG 10.8, what does Krsna say He is the source of? (a) the material world only, (b) the spiritual world only, (c) both.
2. True or false: BG 9.23 says worship of other gods reaches no one.
3. What two things does BG 7.23 say differ between demigod worshippers and Krsna's devotees?
4. Which verse in this session gives a test you can do this week, and what is the test?
5. What does the Gita say about enjoying what the demigods supply without offering anything back (BG 3.12)?

**Answers:** 1. (c) (BG 10.8). 2. False: it "actually" reaches Krsna, but by a wrong method (BG 9.23). 3. The result (limited and temporary vs lasting) and the destination (demigods' planets vs Krsna's abode) (BG 7.23). 4. BG 9.26: offer a leaf, a flower, a fruit or water with love and notice what happens in your heart. 5. Such a person "is certainly a thief" (BG 3.12).
