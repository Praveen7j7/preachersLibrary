# 36. Dealings with Nondevotee Women

> BSS (brahmacari training) · 120 min · drafted · SENSITIVE (celibacy and relations between men and women; adult audience; verses, purports and BIKC are given in their own words; no private disclosure). Follows BIKC chapter 36. Builds on bss-31 (respect and daily habits with women devotees) and bss-05 (Ajamila).

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level
Brahmacaris preach, study and work among people who are not devotees. Such contact is unavoidable, so a brahmacari
needs a plain method. The IYF motive for BSS includes "following the principles of brahmachari life" and "serving more
dedicatedly" (research/batch-criteria.md, section 6). This page adds what bss-31 does not cover: public life, sankirtana,
campus and work.

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| VL1 | Not taught as a topic; basic courtesy |
| VL2 | Preaching to everyone with respect |
| VL3 | Clean conduct in the workplace and on campus |
| SPS | Boundaries in preaching and book distribution |
| DPS | Leading outreach teams with care for each member |
| BSS | This page: protection of celibacy in public life, with compassion (BIKC ch. 36) |

Not done here: the daily habits with women devotees (bss-31); the stages of the fall (bss-05); methods of sense control (bss-24).

## C. Central message
Every woman a brahmacari meets is a soul, and she is also a person who usually knows nothing about the life of a celibate. The brahmacari treats her with courtesy, keeps contact brief and clear, guards his eyes and mind, asks the Lord for protection, and takes honest help from his mentor. The Lord alone can save a mind that has been captured, so he takes shelter of the holy name early.

## D. Learning outcomes
By the end, participants can:
1. Retell, from SB 11.26, how King Pururava came to see that his own ignorance, and not Urvasi, was the cause of his suffering, and what he then saw about the body (SB 11.26.18-21, with the purports' words).
2. Explain SB 11.26.22-23: restricting the senses from their objects slowly quiets the mind, "like a fire without fuel" (purport).
3. Explain the warning of SB 6.1.65 and say why Ajamila's fall began with a glance.
4. List the practical habits for public life (section I.4), including what a sankirtana devotee does when he feels agitated.
5. Say where to go for help: a mentor, the temple authority, a counsellor, Tele-MANAS 14416.

## E. Scriptural foundation
Use SB 11.26.1, 11.26.4, 11.26.5, 11.26.15, 11.26.16, 11.26.17, 11.26.18-21, 11.26.22, 11.26.23, 11.26.25, 11.26.26, SB 6.1.65 and SB 11.2.46 in the talk. The rest are for questions. All verses and purports were read on Vedabase. SB 11.26 is in Canto 11, so the translation is the BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples.

**SB 11.26.1**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/26/1/
> "…one can achieve Me, the reservoir of all pleasure and the Supreme Soul of all existence, residing within the heart of every living being."
- **Context:** Krishna begins to tell Uddhava the song of King Pururava.
- **Purport point:** The purport says that because of bad association even liberated souls may fall; that in the material world the association of women is especially dangerous; and that this chapter, the Aila-gita, is spoken to prevent such a fall. It says one can be saved from sex attraction by the association of saintly persons, who awaken real spiritual intelligence.
- **Application (preacher's):** Notice the order: the chapter opens with the positive goal, reaching Krishna in the heart, and then gives the warning as protection for that goal.
- **Use:** in the talk (I.1).

**SB 11.26.4**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/26/4/
> "…When deprived of his wife, Urvaśī, he was at first bewildered, but by controlling his lamentation he began to feel detachment."
- **Purport point:** The purport says the story is also told in the Ninth Canto, and that Aila (Pururava) was a great king.
- **Use:** in the talk (J.1).

**SB 11.26.5**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/26/5/
> "When she was leaving him, even though he was naked he ran after her just like a madman and called out in great distress, “O my wife, O terrible lady! Please stop!”"
- **Purport (exact):** "As his beloved wife was leaving him, the shocked king was crying out, “My dear wife, please think for a moment. Just stop! You terrible lady, can’t you stop? Why don’t we speak together for a while? Why are you killing me?” Thus lamenting, he followed her."
- **Use:** in the talk (J.1), as the verse tells it.

**SB 11.26.15**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/26/15/
> "Who but the Supreme Personality of Godhead, who lies beyond material perception and is the Lord of self-satisfied sages, can possibly save my consciousness"
- **Application (preacher's):** The king's turning point is to ask for the Lord. Teach the audience to do the same: the first thing to do when the mind is captured is to take shelter of the holy name and the Lord's mercy.
- **Use:** in the talk (I.3).

**SB 11.26.16**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/26/16/
> "…even though Urvaśī herself gave me wise counsel with well-spoken words."
- **Purport (exact):** "As described in the Ninth Canto of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, the goddess Urvaśī plainly told Purūravā that he should never trust women or believe their promises. Despite this frank advice, he became fully attached and thus suffered great mental anguish." (Urvasi's words are SB 9.14.36-38; bss-38 gives them with their purports.)
- **Use:** in the talk (J.1); good advice did not help when the senses were uncontrolled.

**SB 11.26.17**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/26/17/
> "How can I blame her for my trouble when I myself am ignorant of my real, spiritual nature?"
- **Purport point:** The purport says the king frankly admits that Urvasi is not to blame; he was the offender for trying to exploit her external form, and a person who thinks the Lord's material energy exists for his enjoyment will bring fear and anxiety on himself.
- **Use:** in the talk (I.1). This is the rule of both pages: the work is on one's own mind.

**SB 11.26.18**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/26/18/
> "What is this polluted body anyway — so filthy and full of bad odors? I was attracted by the fragrance and beauty of a woman’s body, but what are those so-called attractive features? They are simply a false covering created by illusion."
- **Purport (exact):** "Purūravā now understands that although he was madly attracted to the fragrant, shapely body of Urvaśī, in fact that body was a sack of stool, gas, bile, mucus, hairs and other repugnant elements. In other words, Purūravā is now becoming sane."
- **Use:** in the talk (I.1, J.1), read as it is.

**SB 11.26.19-20**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/26/19-20/
> "One can never decide whose property the body actually is. Does it belong to one’s parents, who have given birth to it, to one’s wife, who gives it pleasure, or to one’s employer, who orders the body around? Is it the property of the funeral fire or of the dogs and jackals who may ultimately devour it? Is it the property of the indwelling soul, who partakes in its happiness and distress, or does the body belong to intimate friends who encourage and help it? Although a man never definitely ascertains the proprietor of the body, he becomes most attached to it. The material body is a polluted material form heading toward a lowly destination, yet when a man stares at the face of a woman he thinks, “What a good-looking lady! What a charming nose she’s got, and see her beautiful smile!”"
- **Purport (exact):** "The phrase tuccha-niṣṭhe, or “headed toward a lowly destination,” indicates that if buried, the body will be consumed by worms; if burned, it will turn to ashes; and if dying in a lonely place, it will be consumed by dogs and vultures. The illusory potency of Māyā enters within the female form and bewilders a man’s mind. The man is attracted to Māyā appearing within the female form, but when he embraces the woman’s body he ends up with two handfuls of stool, blood, mucus, pus, skin, bone, hairs and flesh. People should not be like cats and dogs, in the ignorance of bodily consciousness. A human being should be enlightened in Kṛṣṇa consciousness and learn to serve the Supreme Lord without falsely trying to exploit His potencies."
- **Use:** in the talk (I.1, J.1), read as it is.

**SB 11.26.21**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/26/21/
> "What difference is there between ordinary worms and persons who try to enjoy this material body composed of skin, flesh, blood, muscle, fat, marrow, bone, stool, urine and pus?"
- **Use:** in the talk (I.1, J.1). No purport is printed on Vedabase for this verse.

**SB 11.26.22**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/26/22/
> "After all, the contact of the senses with their objects inevitably agitates the mind."
- **Use:** in the talk (I.2). No purport is printed on Vedabase for this verse.

**SB 11.26.23**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/26/23/
> "Because the mind is not disturbed by that which is neither seen nor heard, the mind of a person who restricts the material senses will automatically be checked in its material activities and become pacified."
- **Purport point:** The purport says that even with closed eyes or in a quiet place one may recall sense pleasure, but this comes from earlier seeing and hearing. When one keeps the senses away from their objects, especially intimate contact with women, the mind's material tendency slackens and, like a fire without fuel, eventually dies.
- **Application (preacher's):** This is the reason behind all the habits in I.4. Fewer impressions today mean fewer memories later, and the holy name fills the space.
- **Use:** in the talk (I.2).

**SB 11.26.24**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/26/24/
> "…Even those who are highly learned cannot trust the six enemies of the mind"
- **Use:** background; it parallels SB 9.19.17 (bss-31).

**SB 11.26.25**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/26/25/
> "His illusion cleansed away by transcendental knowledge, he understood Me to be the Supreme Soul within his heart and so at last achieved peace."
- **Use:** in the talk, the ending of the story (J.1): the king did find peace.

**SB 11.26.26 and 11.26.28**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/26/26/ (and /28/)
> "An intelligent person should therefore reject all bad association and instead take up the association of saintly devotees" (11.26.26)
- **Purport point (11.26.28):** The purport says that even without direct instruction, simply hearing a pure devotee glorify the Lord purifies one of sinful reactions caused by illusion.
- **Use:** in the talk (I.3); the cure is hearing and association.

**SB 6.1.65**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/6/1/65/
> "Because his intelligence was pierced by the lustful glance of the prostitute, the victimized brāhmaṇa Ajāmila engaged in sinful acts in her association."
- **Context:** Builds on bss-05, which tells Ajamila's whole story.
- **Purport point:** The purport says Ajamila was victimized by the dangerous lustful glance of a prostitute, and that he wasted his inheritance on her instead of using it in Krishna's service. BIKC quotes this verse for sankirtana devotees.
- **Use:** in the talk (I.2), one sentence; the lesson is the speed of the change.

**SB 11.2.46**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/2/46/
> "…shows mercy to ignorant people who are innocent and disregards those who are envious of the Supreme Personality of Godhead."
- **Purport point:** The purport says a second-class devotee is eager to preach to the innocent but should avoid the atheistic class so that he is not disturbed or polluted by their association. It adds that a preacher can still pray for the welfare of the envious.
- **Application (preacher's):** Most women whom a brahmacari meets are innocent, not envious. They deserve a kind answer and a book or prasadam, delivered briefly.
- **Use:** in the talk (I.3).

**SB 7.12.9**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/12/9/
- Builds on bss-31, where it is explained in full ("fire and butter"). BIKC mentions this principle again here: if a brahmacari repeatedly preaches to the same woman, the fire-and-butter principle acts. **Use:** reference only.

**SB 11.26.14**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/26/14/
> "…just like a fire that can never be extinguished by the oblations of ghee poured into its flames."
- Reserve (R.1); used in the talk of bss-53.

## F. Philosophical explanation
**Principle.** The purports on SB 11.26 give four points. The first is that bad association can make even liberated persons fall, that "within the material world the association of women is especially dangerous", and that sex attraction is overcome by the association of saintly persons (11.26.1 purport). The second is what the king saw once he "is now becoming sane" (11.26.18 purport): the body he was attracted to "was a sack of stool, gas, bile, mucus, hairs and other repugnant elements"; "The illusory potency of Māyā enters within the female form and bewilders a man’s mind" (11.26.19-20 purport). The third is that the senses, once drawn to their objects, agitate the mind, and that fewer impressions quiet it (11.26.22-23). The fourth is that the one who suffers must not blame the other, but must correct his own understanding (11.26.17 purport).
**BIKC's guidance (BIKC ch. 36), in its own words where it matters (see O).** BIKC (ch. 36) says: "Dealing with nondevotee women in the developed countries is an even bigger problem because they have no idea how to behave with brahmacārīs at all. Nor is it possible to explain to them. It is very, very dangerous and better to be avoided totally (which is impossible)." It says: "There is no stricture that brahmacārīs cannot preach to women, but after a woman’s interest in Kṛṣṇa consciousness is aroused, it is better that further preaching to her be done by women devotees. Otherwise, if a brahmacārī repeatedly preaches to the same woman, the fire and butter principle is sure to act." Sankirtana devotees "have no other recourse but to constantly pray for the special mercy of Lord Caitanya to protect them", keeping "their minds tightly under control, lest like Ajāmila they be 'victimized by the dangerous lustful glance of a prostitute'" (SB 6.1.65). They "should avoid visiting degraded places such as porno shops and sleazy bars", because "everything we see remains as an impression within the mind. Later on, maybe years later, those experiences may resurface in the consciousness and cause agitation." Those who feel agitation on sankirtana "are advised to concentrate on preaching and distributing books to men", and one whose position "is being threatened because of constant contact with nondevotee women ... should discuss with his temple authorities and if necessary adjust his service." BIKC reports that after preaching at a rock concert Srila Prabhupada said, "This is no place for a brahmacārī" (SPL ch. 22; *Requires source review* for the original).
**Connections.** bss-31 (daily habits and respect); bss-05 (Ajamila; contemplation to fall); bss-24 (sense gratification and its control); bss-12 (Haridasa Thakura's steadiness through chanting).
**Consequences.** Without care, a brahmacari may carry impressions that return later and disturb his service. With care, public life becomes service, and the mind stays peaceful.
**Common misunderstandings.**
1. "Avoid women completely." BIKC says contact is "better to be avoided totally (which is impossible)" and that there is no stricture against preaching to women.
2. "Politeness invites trouble, so be rude." BIKC does not say this; it says further preaching passes to women devotees. Polite and brief, then pass on.
3. "A good enough brahmacari does not need protection." SB 11.26.24 says even the highly learned cannot trust the six enemies of the mind.
4. "Only the woman is to blame." SB 11.26.17's purport says the king was the offender for trying to exploit her external form; the 11.26.1 purport says the association of women is especially dangerous; the 11.26.19-20 purport says Maya "enters within the female form and bewilders a man’s mind". Teach all three as they are.
**How to pitch it.** Read the verses and purports as they are, then the habits BIKC gives.

## H. Opening and engagement
**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes).**
1. One round of kirtana or a short prayer; say the title.
2. Say: "This is a talk between brothers. We will hear an old story of a great king who was very learned and still became confused. We will read what he said when he came to his senses, in the words of the Bhagavatam, and see what we can do. Nobody will be asked about private matters."
3. Read SB 11.26.17 slowly. Say: "The king says that he cannot blame anyone else. Listen for why."
**H.2 Opening questions.**
1. "Where do you meet people outside the temple in your week: campus, work, public transport, book distribution? Which of these needs the most care, in your own view?" (Take three answers.)
2. "What is a polite way to end a conversation quickly and kindly?" (Collect examples.)
3. "Why do you think the Lord's mercy comes first in a difficulty, and not our own cleverness?"

## I. Core teaching points
**I.1 A king's lament and what it taught him (SB 11.26.1, 11.26.4-5, 11.26.16-21).** Krishna tells Uddhava a song sung by King Pururava after he lost his wife Urvasi. The king, a great ruler, had lost his judgment: when she left, "even though he was naked he ran after her just like a madman" (11.26.5). Urvasi "plainly told Purūravā that he should never trust women or believe their promises", and still he "became fully attached and thus suffered great mental anguish" (11.26.16 purport). When he understood what had happened, he did not blame her: he saw his own ignorance of his real spiritual nature; the purport says the king was the offender for trying to exploit her outward form (11.26.17). Then read 11.26.18-21 as they are: the body he was attracted to is "polluted ... so filthy and full of bad odors", its attractive features "a false covering created by illusion" (11.26.18); nobody can say whose property the body is, it is "heading toward a lowly destination", yet a man stares at a woman's face and says "What a good-looking lady!" (11.26.19-20); those who try to enjoy a body of "skin, flesh, blood, muscle, fat, marrow, bone, stool, urine and pus" are no different from worms (11.26.21). Give the purports' reason in their words: "that body was a sack of stool, gas, bile, mucus, hairs and other repugnant elements. In other words, Purūravā is now becoming sane" (11.26.18); "The illusory potency of Māyā enters within the female form and bewilders a man’s mind ... A human being should be enlightened in Kṛṣṇa consciousness and learn to serve the Supreme Lord without falsely trying to exploit His potencies" (11.26.19-20). Transition: "How does an impression turn into a hold on the mind?"
**I.2 Impressions and the speed of a fall (SB 11.26.22-23; SB 6.1.65).** The senses meeting their objects agitate the mind. The mind that is not disturbed by what it does not see and hear becomes quiet, and the purport compares this to a fire without fuel. Ajamila's intelligence was pierced by one lustful glance, and he soon neglected his duties (bss-05 gives the details). The lesson is practical: what we see and hear today becomes tomorrow's memory.
**I.3 The cure is the Lord, the holy name and saintly association (SB 11.26.15, 11.26.26, 11.26.28; SB 11.2.46).** The king asks who but the Lord can save his consciousness. He ends with peace by transcendental knowledge (11.26.25). The chapter then tells us to take up the association of saintly devotees (11.26.26), and the purport to 11.26.28 says that simply hearing the Lord's glories purifies. SB 11.2.46 shows the attitude to outsiders: mercy to the innocent, and distance from the envious. So the brahmacari is courteous, gives a book or prasadam, and moves on to hear and chant.
**I.4 Practical habits for public life (Application, preacher's, based on BIKC).**
1. Be polite and brief. Smile, answer clearly, give the book or prasadam, and end the talk.
2. Keep your eyes down and your attention on your service. If you notice that you are looking, turn your eyes to your service, such as the book in your hand.
3. If a woman shows interest in Krishna consciousness, introduce her to women devotees or a family of devotees, and let them continue the preaching.
4. On sankirtana, work in a pair or a group, and "constantly pray for the special mercy of Lord Caitanya" (BIKC ch. 36) before you start. If you feel agitated, BIKC says to "concentrate on preaching and distributing books to men"; speak to the team leader or your mentor the same day, and "if necessary adjust" your service.
5. Avoid "degraded places such as porno shops and sleazy bars" (BIKC ch. 36). Choose other places where books can be distributed, as BIKC says there are plenty.
6. Take care with phones and the internet, where contact with strangers is easy and private. Use your phone in common areas, do not keep private conversations with strangers, and ask your mentor for help if a habit forms. If a habit has become hard to stop, a qualified counsellor, or Tele-MANAS 14416 (India) or your local crisis service, can help, and there is no shame in asking.
7. Keep your chanting and hearing strong. They are the main protection (bss-12).

## J. Stories and illustrations
**J.1 King Pururava's song (SB 11.26; the story is also told in SB 9.14, which bss-38 uses).** Krishna tells Uddhava that Pururava, a famous emperor, was bewildered when his wife Urvasi left him; "even though he was naked he ran after her just like a madman and called out in great distress, 'O my wife, O terrible lady! Please stop!'" (11.26.5). For many years he had enjoyed sex pleasure with her in the evening hours and was never satisfied, and did not notice how the nights came and went (11.26.6); he understood that he had wasted his days (11.26.8). He said that Urvasi had given him wise counsel and he had not listened because his senses were uncontrolled (11.26.16; the purport: she "plainly told Purūravā that he should never trust women or believe their promises"). He asked who but the Lord could save his consciousness (11.26.15). He did not blame her (11.26.17). He then saw the body as it is: "What is this polluted body anyway — so filthy and full of bad odors?" (11.26.18); nobody can say whose property the body is, it is "heading toward a lowly destination", "yet when a man stares at the face of a woman he thinks, 'What a good-looking lady!'" (11.26.19-20); those who try to enjoy a body of "skin, flesh, blood, muscle, fat, marrow, bone, stool, urine and pus" are no different from worms (11.26.21). He concluded that one should never let the senses associate freely with women or with men attached to women, and that the mind is quiet when it is not fed (11.26.22-24). He gave up, understood the Lord as the Supersoul in his heart, and attained peace (11.26.25). Krishna ends by telling Uddhava to take up the association of saintly devotees (11.26.26-28). **Devotional lesson:** even a great king can be captured by attraction; the Lord, saintly devotees and the holy name can free him, and the wise person takes shelter of them early. Read 11.26.18-21 with their purports (E): the purport to 11.26.18 says that in these words "Purūravā is now becoming sane".
**J.2 Ajamila (SB 6.1.65). Builds on bss-05.** One sentence only: a learned brahmana's intelligence was pierced by one glance. The rest is in bss-05. **Devotional lesson:** the Lord's messengers rescued him when he called the holy name; there is always a way back.
**Illustration (preacher's, labelled).** A person who carries a full cup of hot tea through a crowded station walks slowly, keeps his eyes on the cup and says "excuse me" politely to everyone. He is not afraid of the crowd and not rude to it. He wants to deliver the tea. The brahmacari carries a full cup, his vow, to the Lord's service, so he moves through the world politely and with attention.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections
1. **"Is it wrong to talk with women on sankirtana?"** No. BIKC says there is no rule against it. Be brief and kind, pass further preaching to women devotees, and pray for protection.
2. **"Won't being so careful look rude?"** Polite and brief is not rude. Smile, give the book, say thanks. Most people never notice.
3. **"What if I feel strongly attracted to someone I meet?"** SB 11.26.24 says even the highly learned cannot trust the six enemies of the mind. Tell your mentor soon, chant more attentively, and, as BIKC says, adjust service if needed. A counsellor or Tele-MANAS 14416 (India) can also help.
4. **"The king's verses are harsh about women. Can I use them?"** Yes. Read 11.26.17 (he blames himself) and 11.26.18-21 together, with their purports, in their own words. The purport to 11.26.18 gives the reason for the strong words: "Purūravā is now becoming sane." The purport to 11.26.19-20 says: "The illusory potency of Māyā enters within the female form and bewilders a man’s mind."
5. **"I feel I need to avoid every woman."** BIKC says contact with nondevotee women is "better to be avoided totally (which is impossible)", and that there is no stricture against preaching to them. SB 11.2.46 asks for mercy to the innocent. So: preach briefly, pass further preaching to women devotees, and keep the mind under control.

## L. Case studies
**L.1 Invented case for discussion.** A brahmacari distributes books at a station with a partner. A woman, interested, asks many questions and asks him for his phone number to continue the discussion. *Dilemma:* he is happy that someone is interested, and he does not want to lose her. *Principles:* BIKC (pass further preaching to women devotees), SB 7.12.9 (association only for important business), SB 11.26.17. *Questions:* What does he say? What does he offer in place of his number? Who should know? *Analysis:* he says kindly that the temple's women devotees would be glad to answer her, gives the temple's contact, and tells his mentor. *Takeaway:* his kindness and his vow work together.
**L.2 Invented case for discussion (extension).** A brahmacari working in an office finds that his colleague, a kind woman, often stays to talk after hours. He feels his attention drifting. *Principles:* SB 11.26.22-23. *Questions:* What steps can he take at work? Whom can he tell? *Takeaway:* early small adjustments, such as leaving on time and talking in common areas, and honesty with a mentor, keep the mind peaceful.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary
Brahmacaris meet many people who are not devotees, including women. The book *Brahmacarya in Krishna consciousness* (ch. 36) says that nondevotee women "have no idea how to behave with brahmacārīs at all", that such dealings are "very, very dangerous and better to be avoided totally (which is impossible)", that there is no stricture against preaching to women but further preaching to an interested woman should be done by women devotees, and that sankirtana devotees must constantly pray to Lord Caitanya for protection. It also says to avoid "degraded places such as porno shops and sleazy bars", because the impressions can come back years later.
Srimad-Bhagavatam 11.26 tells the story of King Pururava, a powerful emperor who lost his judgment through attachment: when Urvasi left, "even though he was naked he ran after her just like a madman" (SB 11.26.5). After he understood what had happened, he said, "How can I blame her for my trouble when I myself am ignorant of my real, spiritual nature?" (SB 11.26.17). He saw the body as it is: "What is this polluted body anyway — so filthy and full of bad odors? I was attracted by the fragrance and beauty of a woman’s body, but what are those so-called attractive features? They are simply a false covering created by illusion" (SB 11.26.18). The purport says that in these words "Purūravā is now becoming sane", and the purport to 11.26.19-20 says: "The illusory potency of Māyā enters within the female form and bewilders a man’s mind." He asked who but the Lord could save his consciousness (11.26.15). He found peace through knowledge (11.26.25). The chapter ends by telling us to take up the association of saintly devotees (11.26.26). The purport to SB 11.26.23 says that when the senses are kept from their objects, the mind slowly quiets, like a fire without fuel.
SB 6.1.65 reminds us how quickly a glance can change a person's intelligence, as it did for the learned Ajamila. A brahmacari therefore is polite and brief, keeps his eyes and attention on his service, refers interested women to women devotees, avoids disturbing places, and uses his phone with care. He prays for protection, chants and hears, and talks to his mentor early. If he is struggling, a counsellor, or Tele-MANAS 14416 (India) or your local crisis service, can also help. There is no shame in asking.

## S2. Key ideas
- Every person is a soul, and the brahmacari treats everyone with courtesy.
- When trouble comes in the mind, the work is on my own mind and I do not blame others.
- What I see and hear today becomes tomorrow's memory, so I choose my impressions with care.
- The cure is the Lord's mercy, the holy name, hearing and the company of saintly devotees.
- Early honest talk with a mentor, and a change of service if needed, are signs of strength.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **SB 11.26.17**: "How can I blame her for my trouble when I myself am ignorant of my real, spiritual nature?" *For me:* I look at my own mind first.
- **SB 11.26.18**: "What is this polluted body anyway — so filthy and full of bad odors? ... They are simply a false covering created by illusion." *For me:* what attracts the eye is a covering, as the purport says, and seeing this is "becoming sane".
- **SB 11.26.23**: "…the mind of a person who restricts the material senses will automatically be checked in its material activities and become pacified." *For me:* fewer disturbing impressions give a calmer mind.
- **SB 11.26.26**: "An intelligent person should therefore reject all bad association and instead take up the association of saintly devotees" *For me:* I choose to hear and sit with devotees.

## S4. Words to know
- **Brahmacari:** a celibate student living simply and serving his guru.
- **Sankirtana:** going out to chant and distribute books.
- **Purport:** Srila Prabhupada's explanation of a verse.
- **Mentor:** a senior devotee who guides and cares for you.
- **Saintly devotees:** devotees whose words and life show attachment to Krishna.
- **Impression:** what remains in the mind after seeing or hearing something.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *Should I avoid all women?* BIKC says such dealings are "better to be avoided totally (which is impossible)" and that there is no stricture against preaching to women. Be courteous and brief, and pass interested women to women devotees.
2. *What if I am attracted to someone?* Even the learned cannot trust the six enemies of the mind (SB 11.26.24). Tell your mentor soon, chant and hear more, and adjust your service if needed (BIKC).
3. *Why avoid certain places?* The impressions stay in the mind and can return later (BIKC).
4. *Who can help?* Your mentor, the temple authority, a counsellor, and Tele-MANAS 14416 (India).
