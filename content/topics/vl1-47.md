# India vs Bharat

> **VL1 · 45–60 min · review: sources checked, awaiting senior review · sensitive: true.** The Hindi-style title is kept as given. The talk is
> about two names for one land and the two ways of seeing it they stand for: "India", a place on a map with an
> economy and borders, and "Bharat", a name scripture traces to a devotee-king and values for one reason only:
> the ease of practising spiritual life there. **Sensitive** because the names are debated in public life. This
> page takes no position on what the country should be called, makes no political claim, and characterises no
> community. IYF's three Umang decks ("Glories of Vedic India") open with the same question ("What is Bharat?")
> and then list historical and scientific "facts"; the opening is used, the list is not (see B1). VL1-02 "Mera
> Bharat Mahan" and VL1-27 "Glories of Vedic culture" cover greatness and culture; this page covers only the
> *name and the identity*.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Young people carry the two names already: one on their passport, one in their grandmother's songs. Public
arguments about the names leave them with heat and no light. The Bhagavatam gives a way through: it says where
the name Bharata comes from (SB 5.4.9) and what, exactly, makes the land fortunate (SB 5.19.21), and the answer
has nothing to do with wealth, borders or who was born where. At VL1 the talk takes pride and irritation
seriously, then relocates both: the thing worth being proud of is an opportunity, and the only way to claim it
is to use it.

**Not done here:**
- Any view on what the country should be called, now or ever.
- Claims about history, science or other nations as facts.
- Any statement that one community, religion or nationality is spiritually superior; SB 5.19.19 says birth in
  this land is "exactly according to one's past karma" and produces every kind of person.
- Repeating VL1-02's greatness argument; this page links to it.

## C. Central message

"India" names a place; "Bharata" names a devotee-king and the culture that honoured him. The Bhagavatam does
not prize this land for its wealth, size or past; it prizes it for one thing: here, spiritual life is easy to
take up. That makes the real question personal, not national: not "what should the country be called?" but
"am I using the opportunity the old name stands for?"

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. Say where the Bhagavatam traces the name *Bharata-varsa* (SB 5.4.9) and what kind of person it honours.
2. State the one reason the demigods call birth in this land fortunate (SB 5.19.21), and note what that reason
   is *not* about.
3. Explain why this is not a claim of superiority: births here are of every kind by karma (SB 5.19.19), and the
   Lord "can manifest Himself anywhere" (BG 4.7 purport).
4. Restate Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu's instruction to those born here (CC Adi 9.41): make life successful *and*
   work for everyone's benefit.
5. Answer the deck's question "Who is an Indian?" for themselves, as a question about living, not about birth.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** SB 5.4.9, 5.19.21 and CC Adi 9.41 carry the talk; BG 4.7 purport and SB 5.19.19 are the safeguards.
The rest prepare you for questions.

**SB 5.4.9**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/4/9/
> "Of Ṛṣabhadeva's one hundred sons, the eldest, named Bharata, was a great, exalted devotee qualified with the best attributes. In his honor, this planet has become known as Bhārata-varṣa."
- **Context:** The narration of Rsabhadeva's dynasty.
- **Teaching:** The land is named in honour of Bharata, a great devotee.
- **Purport point:** "This planet known as Bhārata-varṣa is also called puṇya-bhūmi, the pious land. At the
  present moment Bhārata-bhūmi, or Bhārata-varṣa, is a small piece of land extending from the Himālaya Mountains
  to Cape Comorin." The purport then quotes CC Adi 9.41.
- **Application (preacher's):** The name honours a *quality* (devotion), not a conquest or a border. Note the
  purport's own distinction: "this planet" in the verse, "a small piece of land" now. The name has always been
  bigger than any map.
- **Use:** in the talk (the origin of the name).

**BG 2.14**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/14/
> "…They arise from sense perception, O scion of Bharata, and one must learn to tolerate them without being disturbed."
- **Teaching:** Krsna addresses Arjuna as "scion of Bharata", a descendant of the Bharata dynasty.
- **Application (preacher's):** Used only to show that "Bharata" in the Gita is a family name of honour that
  Krsna uses to call Arjuna to his best. There are several Bharatas in the tradition (the son of Rsabhadeva, the
  dynasty of the Mahabharata); the talk does not sort them out, it only notes that the name always carried an
  expectation.
- **Use:** in the talk, one line.

**BG 4.7**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/4/7/
> "Whenever and wherever there is a decline in religious practice, O descendant of Bharata, and a predominant rise of irreligion – at that time I descend Myself."
- **Teaching:** The Lord descends wherever dharma declines.
- **Purport point:** "It is not a fact that the Lord appears only on Indian soil. He can manifest Himself
  anywhere and everywhere, and whenever He desires to appear."
- **Application (preacher's):** The page's first safeguard. Whatever is said about this land's fortune, the Lord
  and His dharma are not national property.
- **Use:** in the talk (safeguard) and K.

**SB 5.19.19**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/19/19/
> "The people who take birth in this tract of land are divided according to the qualities of material nature… Some of them are born as exalted personalities, some are ordinary human beings, and some are extremely abominable, for in Bhārata-varṣa one takes birth exactly according to one's past karma."
- **Teaching:** Birth in this land produces every kind of person, by karma; the verse goes on to say life
  becomes perfect when one is trained to serve the Lord under a bona fide guide.
- **Purport point:** The purport discusses varnasrama and contains statements about present-day governments.
  **Not used at VL1**; the varnasrama theme is VL2.
- **Application (preacher's):** The page's second safeguard. Being born here guarantees nothing; the verse
  itself says so. The opportunity is real; the entitlement is not.
- **Use:** in the talk (safeguard), translation only.

**SB 5.19.20**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/19/20/
> "After many, many births, when the results of one's pious activities mature, one gets an opportunity to associate with pure devotees…"
- **Teaching:** After many births, when piety matures, one gets the association of pure devotees; through it
  one cuts the knot of ignorance and gradually serves Lord Vasudeva.
- **Purport point:** "Devotional service is actual liberation."
- **Application (preacher's):** This verse, between 19 and 21, tells us what the "fortune" of 21 consists of:
  association and service, not soil.
- **Use:** in the talk, one line.

**SB 5.19.21**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/19/21/
> "How wonderful it is for these human beings to have been born in the land of Bhārata-varṣa. They must have executed pious acts of austerity in the past, or the Supreme Personality of Godhead Himself must have been pleased with them. Otherwise, how could they engage in devotional service in so many ways?"
- **Context:** The demigods in heaven speak of the inhabitants of Bharata-varsa.
- **Teaching:** The demigods envy birth here for one reason: the many ways of devotional service available.
- **Purport point:** "There are many facilities in India, Bhārata-varṣa, for executing devotional service… From
  all points of view, Bhārata-varṣa is the special land where one can very easily understand the process of
  devotional service and adopt it to make his life successful."
- **Application (preacher's):** Read the verse slowly and ask the room what it does *not* mention: wealth,
  armies, inventions, borders. The praise is for an opportunity. An opportunity unused is not a possession.
- **Use:** in the talk (the spine). VL1-02 uses the same verse for greatness; here it is used for identity.

**CC Adi 9.41**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/adi/9/41/
> "One who has taken his birth as a human being in the land of India [Bhārata-varṣa] should make his life successful and work for the benefit of all other people."
- **Context:** Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu's instruction, in the allegory of the tree of devotion.
- **Teaching:** Birth here carries two duties: perfect your own life, and benefit everyone else.
- **Purport point:** "Anyone who takes birth in the land of India (Bhārata-varṣa) has the special privilege of
  being able to take advantage of the instruction and guidance of the Vedic civilization." The purport also
  says Mahaprabhu "is addressing not only Bengalis but all the inhabitants of India", and that the
  resulting welfare is for "the entire world".
- **Application (preacher's):** The verse turns pride into a job description. "Bharat" is the name of an
  assignment: *para-upakara*, the benefit of all others, with no community excluded. The purport contains
  remarks about the leaders of its time; **not used**.
- **Use:** in the talk (the assignment).

**BG 5.29**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/5/29/
> "…the benefactor and well-wisher of all living entities…"
- **Teaching:** The Lord is the well-wisher of all living beings.
- **Application (preacher's):** The culture named after His devotee cannot wish anyone ill. One line, for K.
- **Use:** background; one line.

**SB 5.8.26**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/8/26/
> "…Bharata Mahārāja was overwhelmed by an uncontrollable desire which was manifest in the form of the deer… he fell down from mystic yoga, austerity and worship of the Supreme Personality of Godhead."
- **Teaching:** Overwhelmed by a desire that took the form of the deer, Bharata fell from yoga, austerity and
  worship. (Context: this is the same Bharata after whom the land is named; the purport adds that he attained
  perfection in a brahmana body in the next life, as Jada Bharata.)
- **Application (preacher's):** Even the man the land is named after had to *keep* earning the name. IYF's two
  recordings on Jada Bharata belong here as "hear next"; the story itself is told in VL1-44.
- **Use:** in the talk, one line.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** Names point. "India" points to a geography and, today, a nation-state with an economy
   and borders; the name is commonly traced to the river Sindhu (Indus) through Persian and Greek, a derivation
   recorded by outsiders describing the land from its north-west edge (educational claim; see O). "Bharata"
   points to a person: the devotee-king Bharata, son of Rsabhadeva, in whose honour the Bhagavatam says the land
   is named (SB 5.4.9). The scripture's praise of the land follows from the second pointer, not the first: it is
   the place where "devotional service in so many ways" is easy (SB 5.19.21), where the chance to meet devotees
   is the real fortune (5.19.20), and where those born carry an assignment to benefit everyone (CC Adi 9.41).
2. **Why it matters.** Pride and embarrassment about the country both rest on the first pointer: GDP, rankings,
   headlines. The second pointer offers a pride that cannot be humiliated by a headline and a responsibility that
   cannot be discharged by a flag.
3. **Connections.** Follows VL1-46: the dharma the land is valued for. Sits beside VL1-02 (greatness), VL1-27
   (culture) and VL1-35 (Vedic India vs modern civilisation), which this page does not repeat. Leads to VL3's
   practice and to *para-upakara* as outreach at higher levels.
4. **Consequences.** Someone who holds this stops arguing about the name and asks whether he is living in
   "Bharat" by the only measure the text gives: is he using the opportunity (SB 5.19.21) and benefiting others
   (CC Adi 9.41)? The deck's question "Who is an Indian?" becomes a mirror, not a weapon.
5. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - **"So people born here are spiritually superior."** SB 5.19.19: births here are of every kind, by karma. The
     Lord "can manifest Himself anywhere and everywhere" (BG 4.7 purport). The verses praise an opportunity, not
     a people.
   - **"So people elsewhere are at a disadvantage."** The purport to SB 5.4.9 and the verse CC Adi 9.41 both
     point *outward*: the opportunity exists to be shared with the whole world. Srila Prabhupada's own life is the
     example (VL1-48 tells it).
   - **"This talk is about what the country should be called."** It is not. Both names appear in the country's
     founding document ("India, that is Bharat", Constitution of India, Article 1; educational fact, not a
     political claim), and this page has no view on usage. It asks what each name *points to*.
   - **"Loving Bharat means scorning 'India'."** The modern state is where the opportunity is lived today. The
     Bhagavatam's own purport names the present "small piece of land" as the same *punya-bhumi* (SB 5.4.9).
   - **"Bharata was a conqueror."** The Bharata of SB 5.4.9 is honoured as "a great, exalted devotee"; he even
     left his kingdom, and later fell and rose again (SB 5.8.26). The name honours devotion, and devotion has to
     be kept up.
6. **Pitch for VL1.** Say "Bharata-varsa" once with its meaning ("the land of Bharata"); otherwise "Bharat" and
   "India" as the audience uses them. No Sanskrit lists, no history lesson, no current affairs. The two
   pointers and the demigods' speech do the work. Keep the tone warm toward both names.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet, say your name, and say the talk takes no side on any name or politics; it asks what a name points to.
2. Write two words on the board: "India" and "Bharat" (Bharat is the Sanskrit-derived name used in Hindi and in the Constitution's wording "India, that is Bharat").
3. Ask H.2 question 1 as private thought.
4. Take two answers and thank each; write one word from each on the board.
5. Say the transition line below and go to SB 5.4.9.

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the room's own life; for a non-Indian room, use "your homeland")
1. When you hear the name of your country or homeland, what do you picture first: a map, a flag, a person, a place, a song?
2. When did you last feel proud of, or embarrassed by, your country? What exactly was it attached to: a result, a headline, a person?
3. If you had to pass on one thing from your land to a child, what would it be?

- **Opening question:** "You carry two names for your country. One is on your passport. The other is in a song
  your grandmother sings. Same land. Why two names, and do they mean the same thing?" Pause. "Today we are not
  going to argue about which name is correct. We are going to ask what each one points to."
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** Kabir's cousin in Toronto posts "proud to be Indian" under a cricket win and
  "ashamed of India" under a news story the same week. Kabir wonders what, exactly, the pride and the shame are
  attached to.
- **Transition:** "The Bhagavatam tells us where the older name comes from, and what it was ever praised for."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** Their pride and their embarrassment are both being managed by headlines.
  The text gives them something steadier to attach either to.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Two names, two pointers.** "India": a place, commonly traced to the river Sindhu, named from outside.
   "Bharata": a person, "a great, exalted devotee" in whose honour the land is named (SB 5.4.9); in the Gita a
   name Krsna uses to call Arjuna to his best (BG 2.14). *Example:* a school named after a scientist vs a school
   named after its street. *Transition:* so what did the old name's culture think the land was *for*?
2. **The land is prized for one thing.** SB 5.19.21: the demigods envy birth here because here one can "engage
   in devotional service in so many ways". Not wealth, not armies, not inventions. SB 5.19.20: the fortune is the
   chance to meet devotees. *Example:* read the verse and ask what is missing. *Transition:* does that make
   anyone superior?
3. **No one is superior by birth.** SB 5.19.19: every kind of person is born here, "exactly according to one's
   past karma". BG 4.7 purport: the Lord does not appear "only on Indian soil". *Example:* an opportunity is
   not a medal; a library card is not a degree. *Transition:* so what does the name ask of us?
4. **The name is an assignment.** CC Adi 9.41 (deeper verse; the talk uses only its plain meaning): make life successful and "work for the benefit of all other
   people". The culture named after Krsna's devotee cannot wish anyone ill (BG 5.29). *Example:* Srila
   Prabhupada took the opportunity and carried it out of the country (VL1-48). *Transition:* and even the man
   the land is named after had to keep earning it.
5. **Bharat is lived, not inherited.** SB 5.8.26: Bharata fell through attachment and rose again. IYF's
   question "Who is an Indian?" answered: the one who uses the opportunity and benefits others, wherever he was
   born. *Transition:* to P.

## J. Stories and illustrations

- **Bharata, son of Rsabhadeva** (SB 5.4.9; his later life SB 5.7–8, verified verse 5.8.26). The eldest of a
  hundred sons, a great devotee, "qualified with the best attributes"; the land is named in his honour. He later
  renounced his kingdom, fell from his practice through affection for a fawn, took a deer's body, remembered, and
  became the great Jada Bharata. *Connection:* the name honours devotion, and devotion is kept, not owned. Told
  in full in VL1-44; here one paragraph. IYF has two recordings (HG Sundar Gopal Prabhu, "Story of Jada Bharat",
  23 Nov 2017; "Jada Bharat and Maharaj Rahugana", 14 Dec 2017) for "hear next".
- **The demigods' speech** (SB 5.19.21): heavenly beings looking down and wishing for a human birth here, for
  the sake of devotional service. *Connection:* the land's value seen from outside it. Read, do not dramatise.
- **Srila Prabhupada leaving India at sixty-nine** to carry out CC Adi 9.41 is told, with its source, in VL1-48
  (*Srila Prabhupada-lilamrta*, vol. 1, ch. 9). *Connection:* the assignment points outward.
- **Contemporary illustration (invented, labelled):** Kabir's cousin (H); the two schools (I.1).
- **Left out from IYF's decks:** every historical and scientific claim (Rama's bridge, Sarasvati, *Vaimanika
  Sastra*, invention lists, plunder figures, quotations attributed to Einstein and Thoreau) and every example
  that names a person's diet or belief. *Requires source review* if any preacher wants them in another talk;
  none is needed here.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

> **Before you teach:** the two names are argued over in public life. Someone may want the talk to endorse or
> oppose a renaming, a party or a movement, or may feel that praising "Bharat" excludes him. The page's answers
> are the texts' own: the Lord is not tied to any soil (BG 4.7 purport); birth here makes nobody superior
> (SB 5.19.19); the name's assignment is the benefit of *all* others (CC Adi 9.41). Take no side on usage;
> characterise no group; if pressed, say "the talk is about what the names point to, not about what anyone should
> be made to say". If a listener from another community feels excluded, that is the moment to read BG 4.7
> purport aloud. *(Pastoral guidance, not a scriptural claim.)*

1. **"So which name is correct? What should the country be called?"**
   - The talk has no view, and the country's own founding document uses both ("India, that is Bharat",
     Article 1). We are asking a different question: what does each name point to, and which pointer do I live by?
2. **"Isn't this nationalism in religious clothing?"**
   - Test it against the verses. SB 5.19.21 praises an *opportunity*, not a people; SB 5.19.19 says birth here
     produces every kind of person; BG 4.7 purport says the Lord does not appear only on Indian soil; CC Adi 9.41
     assigns the benefit of *all* others. A nationalism would need the opposite of each.
3. **"I am not Hindu. Does 'Bharat' include me?"**
   - The Bhagavatam's "Bharata-varsa" is named for a devotee's quality and valued for an opportunity open to
     anyone who uses it (SB 5.19.21; 5.19.20). IYF's own slide says Bharat "is not restricted to a particular
     person, colour, language, society, land, politics, religion or country". By the text's measure, the question
     is never which community you belong to.
4. **"If this land is so spiritually blessed, why is it so troubled?"**
   - The texts never promised comfort; they promised opportunity (SB 5.19.21) and said plainly that every kind
     of person is born here by karma (SB 5.19.19). An opportunity can be wasted by a whole generation. The
     verse's question is still "how could they engage in devotional service in so many ways?", and that is still
     possible today.
5. **"People abroad are more disciplined, more honest. Isn't the West the real Bharat now?"**
   - Avoid the comparison; the texts do not make it. CC Adi 9.41 gives those born here a job, not a rank; the
     purport's own example of the job done is the Krsna consciousness movement carried *to* the West. Where the
     opportunity is used, there Bharat is lived, by the deck's own definition.
6. **"Why not include the inventions and the NASA facts? They make the point better."**
   - They make a *different* point (greatness, VL1-02) and many are disputed. This talk's claim stands without
     them: the text itself does not praise the land for inventions. Adding contested facts risks the whole talk on
     the weakest one.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The cousin in Toronto.** Kabir's cousin posts "proud to be Indian" and "ashamed of India" in one
week. Kabir asks: proud of what, ashamed of what?
- **Dilemma:** Both posts attach to headlines. What would a pride attach to that no headline can touch?
- **Principles:** SB 5.19.21 (what the land is praised for); CC Adi 9.41 (what it asks of us); SB 5.19.19
  (what it never promised).
- **Questions:** Which pointer is the cousin using? What would "proud to be from Bharat" mean by the text's
  measure, and what would it require of him in Toronto?
- **Takeaway:** Attach pride to the opportunity and it becomes a responsibility you can carry anywhere.

**Case 2: The classroom argument.** In a mixed hostel common room, one student insists "real Indians" follow
one set of customs; another, from a different community, goes quiet. Sana is asked what she thinks.
- **Principles:** SB 5.19.19 (every kind of person by karma; no birth-rank); BG 4.7 purport (not only on Indian

- **Takeaway:** The name's measure is a way of living that excludes nobody.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

You carry two names for your country: one on your passport, one in your grandmother's songs. This session does
not argue about which is correct; it asks what each name points to. "India" points to a place: geography, a
state, an economy, and the pride and shame that rise and fall with the news. "Bharata" points to a person: the
Bhagavatam says the land is named in honour of Bharata, "a great, exalted devotee" (SB 5.4.9). The scripture
praises this land for one thing only: here people can "engage in devotional service in so many ways"
(SB 5.19.21). It adds two safeguards: every kind of person is born here, by karma (SB 5.19.19), and the Lord
does not appear only on Indian soil (BG 4.7 purport). And it gives those born here a job: "make his life
successful and work for the benefit of all other people" (CC Adi 9.41). The talk's question "Who is an Indian?" becomes a mirror, not a weapon: a person is measured by using the opportunity and by benefiting others, not by a birth certificate or by news headlines (the words "great, exalted devotee" in SB 5.4.9 describe a person, not a border). Bharat is not inherited; it is lived.

## S2. Key ideas

- The land is named after a devotee, Bharata, "qualified with the best attributes" (SB 5.4.9).
- The demigods envy birth here for one reason: the opportunity for devotional service (SB 5.19.21), and the
  chance to meet devotees (SB 5.19.20). Not wealth, not borders, not inventions.
- Birth here makes nobody superior: every kind of person is born here by karma (SB 5.19.19).
- The Lord "can manifest Himself anywhere and everywhere" (BG 4.7 purport); dharma is not national property.
- The name is an assignment: perfect your life *and* benefit all others (CC Adi 9.41; deeper verse, the talk uses only its plain meaning).
- Even the Bharata the land is named after had to keep earning the name (SB 5.8.26).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **SB 5.4.9:** "In his honor, this planet has become known as Bhārata-varṣa." *For me:* the old name honours a
  quality, not a border.
- **SB 5.19.21:** "How wonderful it is for these human beings to have been born in the land of Bhārata-varṣa…
  how could they engage in devotional service in so many ways?" *For me:* the opportunity is the point.
- **CC Adi 9.41:** "…should make his life successful and work for the benefit of all other people." *For me:*
  a job, not a rank.

## S4. Words to know

- **Bharata-varsa:** "the land of Bharata", named after the devotee-king, son of Rsabhadeva (SB 5.4.9).
- **Punya-bhumi:** "the pious land", another name for it (SB 5.4.9 purport).
- **Para-upakara:** working for the benefit of all other people (CC Adi 9.41).

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **So which name is correct?**
   The session has no view; the Constitution uses both ("India, that is Bharat"). The question is what each name
   points to, and which one you live by.
2. **Isn't this nationalism?**
   Check the verses: an opportunity, not a people (SB 5.19.21); every kind of person born here (SB 5.19.19);
   the Lord not tied to any soil (BG 4.7 purport); the benefit of *all* others (CC Adi 9.41). Nationalism would
   need the opposite of each.
3. **I am from another community. Does "Bharat" include me?**
   By the text's measure the name is about an opportunity and a way of living, open to anyone who uses it. It
   is never about which community you belong to.
4. **If the land is blessed, why is it troubled?**
   The texts promised an opportunity, not comfort, and said every kind of person is born here. An opportunity can
   be wasted; it can also still be used.
5. **Why not the inventions and the NASA facts?**
   They make a different point, many are disputed, and the text itself never praises the land for them.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. According to SB 5.4.9, the land is named after: (a) a conqueror, (b) a river, (c) a great devotee.
2. What is the one reason the demigods envy birth in Bharata-varsa (SB 5.19.21)?
3. True or false: SB 5.19.19 says everyone born in Bharata-varsa is spiritually advanced.
4. What two things does CC Adi 9.41 ask of a person born here?
5. In your own words: what is the difference between "proud to be Indian" and "living in Bharat" by this
   session's measure?

**Answers:** 1. (c) (SB 5.4.9). 2. That people there can engage in devotional service in so many ways
(SB 5.19.21). 3. False: births there are of every kind, exactly according to past karma (SB 5.19.19). 4. Make
his life successful and work for the benefit of all other people (CC Adi 9.41). 5. The first attaches to a
place and its headlines; the second is a way of living, using the opportunity (SB 5.19.21) and benefiting
others (CC Adi 9.41), which anyone can do anywhere.
