## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

A resident has already given his life. The danger is no longer "will I join?" but "why am I here?" After a few years
the reason can quietly shift from compassion to comfort, position or routine. Lord Caitanya's one-line instruction
(CC Adi 9.41) and Prahlada Maharaja's two prayers (SB 7.9.43-44) give the reason back in its original strength.

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| VL1 | Not covered; the first talks give the value of human life |
| VL2 | "Is religion selfish?" answered with BG 18.68-69: the dearest servant is the one who shares the message |
| VL3 | The two kinds of welfare work (body and soul) from CC Adi 9.41-42; chanting and sharing as the practice |
| SPS | The preacher's compassion: SB 7.9.43-44 read fully; a student takes one friend to the programme |

| **TRD (here)** | The resident's **purpose**: para-upakara as the reason for his whole life, examined honestly against his present motives |

## C. Central message

Lord Caitanya says that one born as a human being in India should make his life successful and then work for the
benefit of all other people (CC Adi 9.41). The real benefit is to give people Krishna consciousness. Prahlada
Maharaja shows the mood: he is not afraid for himself, but he will not be liberated alone while others suffer
without Krishna (SB 7.9.43-44). A temple resident lives in the temple for this reason and no other.

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Quote CC Adi 9.41 and explain, from the purport, what "para-upakara" (benefit to others) means and why it is
   the duty of everyone, not only Indians.
2. Explain from SB 7.9.43-44 why a pure devotee is "happy and unhappy simultaneously" and why he does not go to the
   Himalayas.
3. State from BG 18.68-69 what Krishna promises the one who explains the Gita to devotees.
4. Name, privately, what their own reason for living in the temple has become, and test it against these verses.
5. Prepare the same topic for a BSS or DPS audience from section R.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**CC Adi 9.41** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/adi/9/41/
> "One who has taken his birth as a human being in the land of India [Bhārata-varṣa] should make his life successful and work for the benefit of all other people."
- **Context:** Krishnadasa Kaviraja describes Lord Caitanya as the gardener of the tree of devotion, who asks everyone to distribute its fruit. This verse is the Lord's own instruction.
- **Teaching:** Two steps, in order: first make your own life successful (janma sarthaka kari'), then do good to others (kara para-upakara).
- **Purport point:** "It is in the land of India that actual human civilization can be developed." Anyone born in India has the privilege of Vedic guidance; "if an Indian is educated in the Vedic principles, he is able to perform the most beneficial welfare activity for the entire world." "The best welfare activity for all of human society is to awaken man's God consciousness, or Krsna consciousness." Srila Prabhupada adds: "This is not only the duty of Indians but the duty of everyone, and we are very happy that American and European boys and girls are seriously cooperating with this movement." He also writes that India's leaders should "train many Indians to go outside of India to preach this cult."
- **Application (preacher's):** For a resident, "make your life successful" is his sadhana and training; "work for the benefit of others" is his preaching. Neither step can be skipped.
- **Use:** in the talk; the central verse. Have the Bengali and the translation on a slide.

**CC Adi 9.42** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/adi/9/42/
- **Teaching:** Lord Caitanya supports His instruction with Srimad-Bhagavatam: "It is the duty of every living being to perform welfare activities for the benefit of others with his life, wealth, intelligence and words."
- **Use:** in the talk, read directly after 9.41. The four tools (life, wealth, intelligence, words) make the activity concrete.

**SB 10.22.35** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/10/22/35/ · *BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples*
- **Context:** Krishna speaks to His cowherd friends about the trees of Vrindavana, which give everything to others. The BBT edition notes that its translation is taken from Srila Prabhupada's CC Adi 9.42.
- **Use:** background; cite the source verse when a student asks where Lord Caitanya's quotation comes from.

**SB 7.9.43** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/9/43/
> "O best of the great personalities, I am not at all afraid of material existence, for wherever I stay I am fully absorbed in thoughts of Your glories and activities. My concern is only for the fools and rascals who are making elaborate plans for material happiness and maintaining their families, societies and countries. I am simply concerned with love for them."
- **Context:** Prahlada Maharaja's prayers to Lord Nrsimhadeva after his father's death.
- **Teaching:** The devotee has no fear for himself; his only anxiety is love for those who plan material happiness without Krishna.
- **Purport point:** The planners are called *vimudha*, "first-class fools", because the material world is "temporary and miserable" (BG 8.15 cited there) and no plan can change that. "Prahlada Maharaja was happy and unhappy simultaneously. He felt happiness and transcendental bliss because of his being Krsna conscious, yet he felt great unhappiness for the fools and rascals who make elaborate plans to be happy in this material world."
- **Use:** in the talk. Read it as it is, including "fools and rascals"; the purport explains the word.

**SB 7.9.44** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/9/44/
> "My dear Lord Nṛsiṁhadeva, I see that there are many saintly persons indeed, but they are interested only in their own deliverance. Not caring for the big cities and towns, they go to the Himālayas or the forest to meditate with vows of silence [mauna-vrata]. They are not interested in delivering others. As for me, however, I do not wish to be liberated alone, leaving aside all these poor fools and rascals. I know that without Kṛṣṇa consciousness, without taking shelter of Your lotus feet, one cannot be happy. Therefore I wish to bring them back to shelter at Your lotus feet."
- **Teaching:** There are saints who seek only their own liberation; the Vaisnava will not leave others behind.
- **Purport point:** "This is the decision of the Vaisnava, the pure devotee of the Lord." His interest "is in the busiest part of the world, where he teaches people Krsna consciousness. The Krsna consciousness movement was started for this purpose." "Every member of the Krsna consciousness movement is interested in going door to door to try to convince people about the teachings of Bhagavad-gita As It Is." And: "The members of the Krsna consciousness movement must be fully convinced that without Krsna one cannot be happy."
- **Application (preacher's):** A resident who withdraws into private "bhajana" while the programme suffers has chosen the Himalayas in the middle of the city. The purport names the alternative: conviction that without Krishna nobody is happy, and the work that follows from it.
- **Use:** in the talk; the second central reference.

**BG 18.68** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/68/
> "For one who explains this supreme secret to the devotees, pure devotional service is guaranteed, and at the end he will come back to Me."
- **Purport point:** The Gita "should be explained to persons who are ready to accept Krsna as the Supreme Personality of Godhead"; whoever "tries sincerely to present Bhagavad-gita as it is will advance in devotional activities and reach the pure devotional state of life."
- **Use:** in the talk: the preacher's own gain is pure devotion, not fame.

**BG 18.69** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/69/
> "There is no servant in this world more dear to Me than he, nor will there ever be one more dear."
- **Teaching:** No purport; the verse itself is the promise. Krishna's dearest servant is the one who gives His message to others.
- **Use:** in the talk, read directly after 18.68.

**SB 6.17.28** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/6/17/28/
- **Teaching:** Devotees of Narayana "never fear any condition of life"; heaven, liberation and hell are the same to them. Srila Prabhupada quotes it in the SB 7.9.44 purport to explain Prahlada's fearlessness.
- **Use:** background; reserve for an advanced audience (R.1).

**SB 1.1.4** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/1/4/
- **Purport point:** The sages "are sincere friends to all living entities, and at the risk of great personal inconvenience they are always engaged in the service of the Lord for the good of all people."
- **Use:** one line in the talk (I.4); the verse is treated fully in trd-07.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** Human life is for God realisation (the CC Adi 9.41 purport cites Vedanta-sutra, *athato brahma-jijnasa*). One who has received that knowledge owes it to others. Lord Caitanya makes this a duty: make your life successful, then benefit others (CC Adi 9.41-42).
2. **Which benefit?** The purport is exact: "the best welfare activity for all of human society is to awaken man's God consciousness." Feeding, schooling and medicine are good but temporary; they do not stop birth and death. The resident does not despise them; he knows which help is the deepest.
3. **Why "Bharata"?** Because India inherited the Vedic culture and so has the training to give it (purport). Srila Prabhupada immediately widens the duty: "the duty of everyone". A resident from any country is included; an Indian resident has a special debt.
4. **The mood.** Prahlada Maharaja gives the inner side: no fear for himself, and love for those who have no Krishna (SB 7.9.43). This is not pity from above; the purport says he was "happy and unhappy simultaneously". Preaching that lacks this love becomes recruitment.
5. **The renunciation that is not Vaisnava.** Saints who go to the Himalayas for their own liberation are not condemned, but Prahlada will not follow them (SB 7.9.44). Srila Prabhupada: the movement "was started for this purpose", to work "in the busiest part of the world".
6. **The reward.** Krishna promises the one who explains the Gita to devotees pure devotion and His own dearest love (BG 18.68-69). The preacher's own bhakti is protected by preaching.
7. **Misunderstandings.** (a) "Para-upakara means social work": the purport says the best welfare is Krishna consciousness. (b) "First I must be perfect, then I will preach": the order in 9.41 is real, but "successful" means one has taken up the process seriously, not that one has finished it; Prabhupada's own disciples were preaching within months (the 9.41 purport thanks the Western youth who "are seriously cooperating"). (c) "Prahlada's words about fools are harsh": they are the Bhagavatam's words and the purport explains them; we read them as they are.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes).** Greet the residents and say: "Tonight we go back to the first reason any of us
moved into this building. We will read two short instructions, one from Lord Caitanya and one from Prahlada
Maharaja, and we will ask whether they are still the reason we are here." Then chant *jaya sri-krsna-caitanya*
once together and read CC Adi 9.41 in Bengali, slowly, before translating it.

**H.2 Opening questions**
1. "When you first decided to join the ashram, what did you want to do with your life? One sentence, to yourself; no one has to say it aloud."
2. "If a friend from outside asked you, 'What do you actually do all day in the temple, and why?', what would you answer in one sentence?"
3. "Lord Caitanya said that one born in India should make his life successful and then do good to others. Which of the two do you think He stressed more?" (Take two or three answers; the verse holds both.)

## I. Core teaching points

1. **The instruction (CC Adi 9.41).** Two steps: make your own life successful, then benefit others. Example: a resident's morning programme and rounds are step one; his evening programme and the people he follows up are step two. Transition: "What benefit did Lord Caitanya mean?"
2. **The real benefit (CC Adi 9.41 purport; 9.42).** Awakening God consciousness is "the best welfare activity for all of human society". The Bhagavatam says we do this "with life, wealth, intelligence and words". Example: a village volunteer who teaches children to read does real good; a resident who teaches the same children to chant gives something that goes with them beyond this life. Both are good; the second is what the temple exists for. Transition: "Who is the model of this mood?"
3. **No fear for myself (SB 7.9.43).** Prahlada is not afraid of material existence; he is concerned only with love for those who plan without Krishna. Example: a resident who worries daily about his own future ("will I remain?") has lost Prahlada's first half; one who feels nothing for the people outside has lost the second half. Transition: "Prahlada then looks at the other saints."
4. **Not the Himalayas (SB 7.9.44; SB 1.1.4 purport).** He will not be liberated alone. Srila Prabhupada: the movement works "in the busiest part of the world". The sages of Naimisaranya likewise serve "at the risk of great personal inconvenience" for the good of all. Example: the "Himalayas" in a temple can be a room, a laptop and a closed door while the Sunday programme runs short of speakers. Transition: "What does the preacher himself get?"
5. **The dearest servant (BG 18.68-69).** Pure devotion is "guaranteed" for the one who explains the Gita to devotees, and Krishna calls him His dearest servant. Example: residents often fear that preaching will dry up their own bhajana; Krishna says the opposite. Transition to J.

## J. Stories and illustrations

**Prahlada Maharaja's prayer (SB 7.9, told as the chapter tells it).** After Lord Nrsimhadeva kills Hiranyakasipu, the
demigods cannot approach Him; the boy Prahlada is sent forward and offers his prayers. In texts 43 and 44 he tells the
Lord that he is not afraid of material existence, that he is anxious only for the fools making plans for happiness,
and that he does not wish to be liberated alone like the sages who go to the Himalayas with vows of silence. Devotional
lesson (purport): "This is the decision of the Vaisnava."

**One Indian's humble service (CC Adi 9.41 purport).** Srila Prabhupada writes that the practical effect of spreading
the movement is that "the most degraded debauchees are becoming the most elevated saints. This is only one Indian's
humble service to the world. If all Indians had taken to this path, as advised by Lord Caitanya Mahaprabhu, India would
have given a unique gift to the world." Tell it in his words; it is his own account of para-upakara done. Lesson: one
person who takes the instruction seriously changes thousands of lives; the residents are his continuation.

**The Jaladuta (see trd-03).** Do not retell it here; mention that the next page gives the founder's mood on the ship.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"Isn't it proud to say India has a special duty?"** The purport says why: India inherited the Vedic culture, so it has the training to give. It immediately adds that the duty belongs to everyone and thanks the Western devotees. The special duty is a debt, not a rank.
2. **"Prahlada calls people fools and rascals. Should I speak like that?"** Read what the purport says the word means: people who try to make a temporary, miserable world into a place of happiness. Prahlada says it "with love for them" (7.9.43). We repeat the Bhagavatam's words as they are and keep the love that goes with them; we do not insult individuals.
3. **"I joined for my own spiritual life, not to preach. Is that wrong?"** The order in CC Adi 9.41 begins with your own success, so your motive is included. But the verse does not stop there, and SB 7.9.44 says the pure devotee does not stop there. Ask yourself where you are on the two steps, honestly.
4. **"What about devotees who serve in the kitchen or office and never speak publicly?"** CC Adi 9.42 names "life, wealth, intelligence and words". Cooking prasadam for the Sunday programme is para-upakara with one's life and intelligence. The test is the purpose it serves, not the microphone.
5. **"Social work gives visible results; preaching often does not. Why not do the visible good?"** Both are good; the purport says which is "the best welfare activity". The visible good ends with the body; the message goes with the soul. A resident can do both where the temple does, but he knows which one his life is for.

## L. Case studies

**Case 1: The quiet years (Invented case for discussion).** Nikhil has lived in the ashram for six years. He serves in
accounts, chants attentively, and has slowly stopped taking preaching programmes, saying he is "not the type". The
temple's Sunday programme has three speakers for twelve slots.
- **Dilemma:** Is Nikhil's withdrawal a legitimate choice of service, or the Himalayas of SB 7.9.44?
- **Principles:** CC Adi 9.41-42 (four tools: life, wealth, intelligence, words); SB 7.9.44 purport ("busiest part of the world").
- **Questions:** Which of the four tools is Nikhil using? Which has he put down, and why? What would Prahlada's mood look like in the accounts office?
- **Analysis:** His service is real para-upakara (intelligence, life). But "not the type" needs a test: is it humility about ability, or avoidance of the discomfort of speaking? The purport does not require every devotee to lecture; it does require conviction that people cannot be happy without Krishna and some step toward them.
- **Takeaway:** Keep the service; add one small, direct step toward people (co-hosting a programme, following up two guests).

**Case 2: The programme that became a project (Invented case for discussion).** A preaching centre's weekly programme
grew from fifteen to a hundred students in two years. The resident in charge now spends most of his time on
reports, sponsors and photographs for social media, and admits he has not spoken to a new student personally in months.
- **Dilemma:** Growth is good; what has been lost?
- **Principles:** SB 7.9.43 ("I am simply concerned with love for them"); BG 18.68 (explaining the message to devotees).
- **Questions:** What did the programme exist for at fifteen students? What does it exist for at a hundred? Who is explaining the Gita now, and to whom?
- **Takeaway:** Numbers serve para-upakara only if someone is still giving Krishna to a person. Protect that part of the week first.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions**
1. What are the two steps in CC Adi 9.41? (Make one's own life successful; work for the benefit of all others.)
2. According to the purport, what is the best welfare activity for human society? (To awaken God consciousness, Krishna consciousness.)
3. With which four things does the Bhagavatam say we benefit others? (Life, wealth, intelligence, words: CC Adi 9.42.)
4. Why is Prahlada "happy and unhappy simultaneously"? (Happy because Krishna conscious; unhappy for those making plans without Krishna: SB 7.9.43 purport.)
5. Where does the SB 7.9.44 purport say the devotee's interest lies? (In the busiest part of the world, teaching Krishna consciousness; going door to door.)
6. What does Krishna promise in BG 18.68-69? (Pure devotional service, return to Him, and that no servant is dearer.)

**M.2 Activity (25 min): "Four tools."** In groups of three, each resident lists his current services under the four
headings of CC Adi 9.42 (life, wealth, intelligence, words) and marks which heading is empty. Each group proposes one
realistic step for the empty heading for one of its members, with the senior's agreement to be sought afterwards.
Report back in one sentence per group.

**M.3 Reflection (private, written).** "The reason I live in the temple today is ___. The reason Lord Caitanya and
Prahlada give is ___. The difference, if any, is ___." Nobody reads it out.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Lord Caitanya gave one instruction that explains why a devotee lives in a temple. He said that a person born as a human
being in India should make his life successful and then work for the benefit of all other people (CC Adi 9.41). Srila
Prabhupada explains what that benefit is: "the best welfare activity for all of human society is to awaken man's God
consciousness." Feeding and teaching people are good, but they end with this body; giving a person Krishna goes with
his soul. Lord Caitanya then quotes the Bhagavatam: we help others "with life, wealth, intelligence and words"
(CC Adi 9.42). Srila Prabhupada adds that this is "not only the duty of Indians but the duty of everyone."

Prahlada Maharaja shows the mood behind the instruction. He tells Lord Nrsimhadeva that he is not afraid of material
existence, because he is always absorbed in the Lord; his only concern is love for the people who make big plans for
happiness without Krishna (SB 7.9.43). He sees saints who go to the Himalayas to be liberated alone and says he will
not do that: "I do not wish to be liberated alone, leaving aside all these poor fools and rascals" (SB 7.9.44). Srila
Prabhupada writes that the Krishna consciousness movement "was started for this purpose": to work in the busiest part
of the world, going door to door with the message of Bhagavad-gita.

Krishna Himself promises the result. Whoever explains the Gita to devotees is guaranteed pure devotional service and
will come back to Him, and "there is no servant in this world more dear to Me than he" (BG 18.68-69). A resident's
own spiritual life is not drained by preaching; it is protected by it.

## S2. Key ideas

- Two steps, in order: make your own life successful; then work for everyone's benefit (CC Adi 9.41).
- The best benefit is awakening Krishna consciousness (CC Adi 9.41 purport); the tools are life, wealth, intelligence and words (CC Adi 9.42).
- The pure devotee has no fear for himself and feels love for those without Krishna (SB 7.9.43).
- He does not seek liberation alone; he works where the people are (SB 7.9.44 and purport).
- The one who explains the Gita to devotees becomes Krishna's dearest servant (BG 18.68-69).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **CC Adi 9.41:** "One who has taken his birth as a human being in the land of India should make his life successful and work for the benefit of all other people." *For me:* my sadhana and my preaching are the two halves of one instruction.
- **SB 7.9.44:** "I do not wish to be liberated alone… I wish to bring them back to shelter at Your lotus feet." *For me:* the Vaisnava's decision, which I can make again today.
- **BG 18.69:** "There is no servant in this world more dear to Me than he." *For me:* Krishna's own reason for me to share His message.

## S4. Words to know

- **Para-upakara:** doing good to others; here, giving them Krishna consciousness.
- **Janma sarthaka:** making one's birth (life) successful.
- **Bharata-varsa:** India, the land that inherited the Vedic culture.
- **Mauna-vrata:** a vow of silence, taken by the sages Prahlada describes.
- **Vimudha:** "first-class fools" (SB 7.9.43 purport): people who try to make a temporary world a place of happiness.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Is it selfish to be a devotee?** Not if we follow the whole instruction. The first step is our own success; the second is everyone else's. Prahlada refused to be liberated alone (SB 7.9.44).
2. **I am not a speaker. Can I still do para-upakara?** Yes. The Bhagavatam names life, wealth, intelligence and words (CC Adi 9.42). Cooking for a programme or keeping the accounts that run it is included, if it serves that purpose.
3. **Why does Prahlada call people fools?** Srila Prabhupada explains: they try to make a temporary and miserable world a place of happiness. Prahlada says it "with love for them". We keep both the word and the love.
4. **Does preaching weaken my own spiritual life?** Krishna says the opposite: pure devotion is "guaranteed" for the one who explains the Gita to devotees (BG 18.68).
5. **Is this only for Indians?** No. Srila Prabhupada writes that it is "the duty of everyone" and thanks the Western devotees who cooperate.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. What are the two steps in CC Adi 9.41, in order?
2. According to Srila Prabhupada's purport, what is "the best welfare activity for all of human society"?
3. Name the four tools in CC Adi 9.42.
4. True or false: in SB 7.9.44 Prahlada says he wants to go to the Himalayas to meditate.
5. What two things does Krishna promise in BG 18.68?
6. Why is Prahlada "happy and unhappy simultaneously"?

**Answers:** 1. Make one's own life successful; then work for the benefit of all other people. 2. To awaken man's God
consciousness, Krishna consciousness. 3. Life, wealth, intelligence, words. 4. False: he says other saints do that and he
does not wish to be liberated alone. 5. Pure devotional service is guaranteed, and he will come back to Krishna.
6. Happy because he is Krishna conscious; unhappy for those making plans without Krishna (SB 7.9.43 purport).
