# Art of self management

> **VL1 · 45–60 min · review: sources checked, awaiting senior review.** Also taught at VL3 as VL3-01 (see B). No special sensitivity.

## A. Topic overview

| | |
|---|---|
| Batch / level | VL1 (Vedic Level 1), a stand-alone talk. Also at VL3 (vl3-01) |
| Suggested duration | 45–60 min |
| Preparation time | About 90 min, the first time |
| Difficulty | Introductory |
| Prerequisites | None |
| Audience | Students and young professionals; many have heard "time management" and "stress management" talks |
| Teaching objective | Show that managing others, time or work begins with managing oneself (mind, senses, habits), using the Gita's picture of the mind as friend or enemy, and give a few practical, testable regulations |

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

This topic is a good bridge for a first-time audience: a corporate-style subject (self-management) with the Gita's
practical advice, and no demand for belief. It follows VL1-07 (the heart can change) with the question "how do I actually
manage myself day to day?"

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **VL1** (here) | The mind as friend or enemy; senses, mind, intelligence, self; regulating eating, sleep, work, recreation; honest about how hard it is; one small experiment |
| **VL3** (vl3-01, same title) | *Preacher's note, to confirm with the VL3 page:* VL3 is a practice level. The natural addition is to place self-management inside a devotional routine (a fixed morning programme, chanting as the mind's anchor, accountability to a facilitator). This VL1 page does not require chanting practice |
| VL2 | Self as distinct from body and mind; reasoned basis for why the mind needs training |
| SPS | Self-management inside a growing spiritual routine |
| DPS | Self-management becomes accountable commitment and discernment of one's ashrama |
| BSS | A brahmacari's daily schedule as self-management in practice |

**Not done here:**
- A full course in discipline, or a diet or fitness plan.
- Moral lectures on what students "should not" do. IYF's longer notes include strong judgements about students and
  about specific indulgences; this page leaves them out. Teach regulation as a skill, not as a verdict on listeners.
- Claiming that restraint is easy. The Gita itself says the mind is hard to control (BG 6.34).
- Quoting IYF's unsourced figures (school-problem lists, "10,000 thoughts a day"); see O.

## C. Central message

Before we can manage anything outside us, we have to manage the one who is doing the managing. The Gita says the mind can be
our best friend or worst enemy, that it is hard to control, and that it can be trained through practice and detachment
and through simple regulation of daily habits.

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. Explain in plain words the Gita's picture of the mind as friend or enemy (BG 6.5–6).
2. Describe the order of the senses, mind, intelligence and self (BG 3.42) and use it to explain why willpower alone often fails.
3. Name the four areas of daily regulation (eating, sleeping, recreation, work) and one effect of each (BG 6.16–17).
4. Say that the mind is hard to control and what the Gita suggests for it (BG 6.34–35).
5. Choose one small self-management experiment for the week.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** six references suffice (BG 6.5, 6.6, 6.17, 3.42, 6.34, 6.35). The rest are for questions.

**BG 6.5**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/5/
> "One must deliver himself with the help of his mind, and not degrade himself. The mind is the friend of the conditioned soul, and his enemy as well."
- **Teaching:** The same mind can lift or lower us.
- **Application (preacher's):** Self-management starts with seeing that the mind is a tool and also a possible obstacle.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 6.6**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/6/
> "For him who has conquered the mind, the mind is the best of friends; but for one who has failed to do so, his mind will remain the greatest enemy."
- **Purport point:** The purpose of yoga practice is to control the mind so that it becomes a friend.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 3.42**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/42/
> "The working senses are superior to dull matter; mind is higher than the senses; intelligence is still higher than the mind; and he [the soul] is even higher than the intelligence."
- **Purport point:** Above the mind is the intelligence and above it the soul; if the soul is engaged with the
  Supreme, intelligence, mind and senses are "automatically engaged".
- **Application (preacher's):** The diagram in N: when the senses give orders, the mind follows; when intelligence (reason, values) leads, the mind obeys. The "who is in charge?" image helps with the common experience of knowing better and doing otherwise.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 6.34–35**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/34/ and /35/
> 6.34: "The mind is restless, turbulent, obstinate and very strong… to subdue it, I think, is more difficult than controlling the wind."
> 6.35: "…it is undoubtedly very difficult to curb the restless mind, but it is possible by suitable practice and by detachment."
- **Context:** Arjuna, a skilled warrior, confesses he finds the mind harder to control than the wind; Krsna agrees it is difficult and offers a way.
- **Teaching:** The difficulty is accepted openly; the remedy is practice (regular, suitable) and detachment.
- **Application (preacher's):** Removes shame. If Arjuna found the mind difficult, listeners need not be embarrassed about their struggle.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 6.16–17**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/16/ and /17/
> 6.16: "There is no possibility of one's becoming a yogī… if one eats too much or eats too little, sleeps too much or does not sleep enough."
> 6.17: "He who is regulated in his habits of eating, sleeping, recreation and work can mitigate all material pains by practicing the yoga system."
- **Teaching:** Regulation (balance), not extremes, of the four basic areas of daily life.
- **Application (preacher's):** 6.16 is about extremes; 6.17 about regulation. Present this as balanced rhythm and not as a rigid code.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 2.61**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/61/
> "One who restrains his senses, keeping them under full control, and fixes his consciousness upon Me, is known as a man of steady intelligence."
- **Purport point:** The purport says the highest yoga perfection is Krsna consciousness, and "unless one is Kṛṣṇa
  conscious it is not at all possible to control the senses" (the example is King Ambarisa).
- **Use:** background. At VL1, treat it as the tradition's deeper answer, which is developed at VL3 and later; do not press it.

**BG 17.15–16**: Verified.
- 17.15: austerity of speech is "truthful, pleasing, beneficial, and not agitating to others".
- 17.16: austerity of the mind is "satisfaction, simplicity, gravity, self-control and purification of one's existence".
- **Application (preacher's):** Self-management is about speech and thought as well as eating and sleeping. **Use:** for questions and as optional extension.

**SB 1.9.27 (purport)**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/9/27/
- **Purport point:** The purport, discussing salvation, lists short remedies: "To get freedom from anger, one should learn how to forgive… By tolerance only can one conquer desires and avarice. Disturbances from various diseases can be avoided by regulated diets. By self-control one can be free from false hopes…" (purport, quoted by IYF's notes).
- **Caution:** The verse's translation itself is about charity and duties of women and devotees. Only the purport carries this list. Cite it as "SB 1.9.27 purport".
- **Use:** optional, as a short list slide.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** The Gita describes a hierarchy of senses, mind, intelligence and self (BG 3.42). When the senses drive the mind, we feel
   pulled by cravings; when intelligence guides the mind, the mind is a friend (BG 6.5–6). Self-management means
   putting the higher faculty in charge, which takes both understanding and regular practice (BG 6.35).
2. **Why it matters.** Skills for work (time plans, goal charts) depend on the person using them. A student who knows the plan
   and still scrolls for hours knows the gap the Gita describes.
3. **Connections.** VL1-07 (heart transformation), VL1-03 "Art of mind control" and VL1-04 "Power of habits" (other VL1 topics on the same theme; check them to avoid
   repetition), VL3-01 (the same title at the practice level).
4. **Consequences.** Simple regulation (BG 6.17) is a base for study, work and any spiritual practice. Without it, higher goals lack a platform.
5. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - **"Self-control is suppression."** The Gita asks for balance (BG 6.16–17), and practice with detachment (BG 6.35). Do not teach harshness.
   - **"If I fail, I am weak."** Even Arjuna found the mind difficult (BG 6.34).
   - **"Self-management is only for executives."** Its subject is every person's daily life.
   - **"This replaces medical help."** Sleep, anxiety and appetite problems can have medical causes; encourage professional help where needed.
6. **Pitch for VL1.** Use "mind", "habits", "regulation", "balance". Use IYF's practical examples (the library board, the second
   sweet) as questions to the room. Skip Sanskrit except *yukta* (regulated) at most once. Treat the Gita as a source of ideas to test, not as an authority to obey.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet, say your name, and say this is a practical talk with one small experiment at the end.
2. Write "Who manages me?" on the board.
3. Read the dinner-tray scenario below and ask H.2 question 1; take two answers and thank each by name.
4. Note on the board the "voices" people name; you return to them at minute 13.
5. Give the transition line below.

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the room's own life)
1. What is the hardest hour of your day to stay on track?
2. When did you last keep a small promise to yourself? What helped?

- **Opening question:** "A special dinner. Everyone is allowed one sweet. The tray is nearly full and the server isn't looking. What happens in your head?" (IYF's scenario, adapted.) Take two answers. "That voice, whichever side you listen to, is what this talk is about."
- **Other prompts (from IYF's notes, adapted):** a free last period with no teacher; a message on your phone during study time.
- **Transition:** "We are all managers of someone: ourselves. The Gita has a very practical note on how."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** exams, phones, sleep, fitness, work habits, and the gap between plans and actions.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Manage the manager first.** Work and leadership depend on the person. *Example:* the prompts above. *Transition:* what is the manager made of?
2. **The mind is friend or enemy.** BG 6.5–6. *Example:* the driver who can be a friend or hazard (IYF's image). *Transition:* who is meant to be in charge?
3. **There is an order of command.** Senses, mind, intelligence, self (BG 3.42). *Example:* knowing you should sleep, and watching one more video. *Transition:* can it be fixed?
4. **It is hard, and it is possible.** BG 6.34–35. *Example:* learning an instrument: rarely quick, always possible. *Transition:* what practice?
5. **Regulate the four basics.** Eating, sleeping, recreation, work (BG 6.16–17). *Example:* ask what happens to your mood after four nights of short sleep (the audience knows). *Transition:* to the takeaway.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"Isn't this just suppressing myself?"**
   - The Gita asks for regulation, avoiding both too much and too little (BG 6.16). It also says detachment and practice (BG 6.35). Suppression without any positive replacement often fails; look for something you enjoy more, not just something you give up. *(Last sentence is a preacher's application.)*
2. **"I try and fail. What is wrong with me?"**
   - Nothing unusual. Arjuna describes the same difficulty (BG 6.34). Practice, not shame, is the Gita's answer.
3. **"Why is the soul above intelligence? What is the soul?"**
   - BG 3.42 makes this claim. At VL1, invite exploration; the full discussion is for VL2.
4. **"Do I have to give up everything fun?"**
   - No. Recreation is one of the four regulated areas (BG 6.17). The word is regulated, not removed.
5. **"What about my anxiety, insomnia or addiction?"**
   - Encourage professional medical or psychological help; spiritual and practical regulation can go with it, not replace it.
6. **"How is this different from a corporate self-management seminar?"**
   - Many tips overlap (sleep, balance, goals). The Gita adds a view of who the self is and what the goal is. State it as the tradition's addition and let the listener judge.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The late-night scroll.** Karan has an exam in two days. Every night he plans to study from nine, and every night he is still on his phone at midnight. He says, "I have no willpower."
- **Principles:** BG 3.42 (senses lead, intelligence should); BG 6.17 (regulated sleep, work); BG 6.35 (practice).
- **Questions:** Who is in charge at 9 pm? What one change in his setup would help (a place, a time, a friend)?
- **Suggested analysis:** Willpower is helped by arrangements. Start with one small, repeatable step (not "I'll change everything").
- **Takeaway:** Train the mind in small steps; shame does not help.

**Case 2: The new job.** Anita starts her first job, works long hours, skips meals and sleeps late. After a month she is irritable.
- **Principles:** BG 6.16–17 (balance of eating, sleeping, work).
- **Questions:** What is out of balance? What can she change this week without hurting her work?
- **Takeaway:** Self-management is about rhythm, not heroics.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first; praise every try)
1. In BG 6.5, can the mind be a friend? *Yes, and also an enemy.*
2. Name the four areas in BG 6.17. *Eating, sleeping, recreation, work.*
3. In BG 3.42, which is higher, the mind or the intelligence? *The intelligence.*
4. Did Arjuna find the mind easy to control? *No (BG 6.34); it can be done by practice and detachment (BG 6.35).*
5. Does the Gita ask us to give up all recreation? *No; recreation belongs to a balanced life (BG 6.17).*

**M.2 Activity (about 10 minutes): "Habit audit"**
1. 4 min, in pairs: each draws four boxes (eating, sleeping, recreation, work) and writes one thing that already works in each.
2. 3 min: each circles one box to improve by one small step this week.
3. 3 min: two volunteers share a step (sharing is optional). Praise every good idea, then link to BG 6.17.

**M.3 Reflection**
- **Reflection:** In which of the four areas am I most out of balance? What is one sign?
- **Application (private, optional):** What one small, repeatable step would help most this week?

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Before we manage a team, a project or our time, we have to manage ourselves. The Bhagavad-gita says the mind can be our best friend or our worst enemy (BG 6.5–6). When the mind is trained it helps us; when it runs free it works against us.

Who is in charge inside us? The Gita describes an order: the senses, then the mind, then the intelligence, and above them the self (BG 3.42). We do best when the intelligence leads and the mind follows, not when the senses pull us along.

Is this easy? No. Arjuna himself said the mind is very hard to control (BG 6.34). The answer is not harshness but regular practice and detachment (BG 6.35). A good start is balance in eating, sleeping, recreation and work (BG 6.16–17). It is not a diet or a punishment; it is a steady rhythm.

Try one small change in one of these four areas this week, and repeat it. Many tips here will sound like a management seminar; the Gita adds a view of who the self is and what the goal is, and you are free to judge for yourself.

## S2. Key ideas

- The mind can be a friend or an enemy.
- Senses, mind, intelligence, self: who is in charge?
- Self-control is hard; it can be learned.
- Balance in eating, sleeping, recreation and work helps everything else.
- Start small and repeat.

<p><em>BG 3.42: each level is higher than the one below it; self-management means the higher one leads.</em></p>


## S3. Verses to remember

- **BG 6.5:** "The mind is the friend of the conditioned soul, and his enemy as well." *For me:* I can train my mind to help me.
- **BG 6.17:** One who is regulated in eating, sleeping, recreation and work can lessen all material pains. *For me:* start with the basics.
- **BG 6.35:** The restless mind is difficult to curb, "but it is possible by suitable practice and by detachment". *For me:* difficulty is normal; practice helps.

## S4. Words to know

- **Mind:** the part of me that thinks, feels and wants.
- **Intelligence:** the part that reasons and decides.
- **Regulated:** in balance, neither too much nor too little.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Isn't self-control just suppression?**
   The Gita asks for balance and for practice with detachment, not harshness (BG 6.16, 6.35).
2. **I always fail. What is wrong with me?**
   Nothing unusual. Arjuna also found the mind hard (BG 6.34).
3. **Do I have to give up everything fun?**
   No. Recreation is part of a balanced life (BG 6.17).
4. **What if I have anxiety or sleep problems?**
   Get medical or psychological help. Habits can support you but do not replace care.
5. **How is this different from a management seminar?**
   Many tips are the same. The Gita adds a view of who the self is and what the goal is. Judge for yourself.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. In BG 6.5, the mind is called: (a) always an enemy, (b) a friend and an enemy, (c) neither.
2. Name the four areas of regulation in BG 6.17.
3. In BG 3.42, which is higher: the mind or the intelligence?
4. True or false: the Gita says controlling the mind is easy.
5. What is one small change you could make this week?

**Answers:** 1. (b) (BG 6.5). 2. Eating, sleeping, recreation and work (BG 6.17). 3. The intelligence (BG 3.42). 4. False; it is very difficult but possible by practice and detachment (BG 6.34–35). 5. Your own answer.

## S7. Try this week

- **Practice:** Choose one of the four areas (eating, sleeping, recreation, work) and make one small change for seven days.
- **Reflection (private):** When did my mind last act as my friend? What helped?
