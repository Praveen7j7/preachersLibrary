# The mad-elephant offence: Vaisnava-aparadha among seniors

> **TRD · up to 180 min · review: drafted (30 Oct 2026).** Group H (blockers for senior devotees), second of six.
> For residents who live and work closely with peers and seniors for years, where criticism and envy have time to
> grow. Verses checked on vedabase (research/verification/trd-50.json). dps-07 covers fault-finding in general and
> dps-06 the ten offences to the holy name; this page takes the offence that "uproots the creeper".

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on dps-07, which treats fault-finding as a habit to overcome, and on dps-06, which lists the ten offences to the
holy name. This page does not repeat them. It asks what happens when the fault-finder has been a devotee for twenty years
and the one he criticises is his godbrother, his temple president or a senior preacher: the "so-called mature devotee" of
Prabhupada's purport (19.157).

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| VL1 | Respect for devotees as part of good conduct; no offence theory |
| VL2 | Why criticising a devotee harms the critic: the reasoning from BG and SB on association |
| VL3 | The first offence to the holy name (blaspheming devotees) as part of chanting practice |
| SPS | Fault-finding as a habit; how to see good in others |

**Not done here:** the full ten offences (dps-06); conflict resolution between devotees (trd-19); the psychology of

## C. Central message

"If the devotee commits an offense at the feet of a Vaisnava while cultivating the creeper of devotional service in
the material world, his offense is compared to a mad elephant that uproots the creeper and breaks it" (CC Madhya
19.156). Twenty years of watering do not protect the creeper from one elephant. The fence (19.157) is the association
of pure devotees and the regulative principles; the remedy, when the offence is done, is Durvasa's: go to the devotee
"immediately, without a moment's delay" (SB 9.4.69).

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Explain the mad-elephant image (CC Madhya 19.156) and the three forms of the offence Prabhupada names in the
   purport: offending a Vaisnava, disobeying the spiritual master (*guru-avajna*), and giving up the regulative
   principles and the devotees' company.
2. Describe the fence of 19.157 and Prabhupada's instruction to "stick to the Society" even when one thinks its
   members are not pure.
3. State, from NOI 6, why criticising an empowered Vaisnava's bodily or behavioural defects is an offence, and the
   difference between the kanistha and madhyama vision.
4. Retell the Durvasa-Ambarisa history as the Bhagavatam tells it and draw its devotional lesson about the remedy for
   offence.
5. Apply SB 10.74.40 and SB 4.4.17 to the question of hearing criticism of devotees.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**CC Madhya 19.156**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/19/156/
> "If the devotee commits an offense at the feet of a Vaiṣṇava while cultivating the creeper of devotional service in the material world, his offense is compared to a mad elephant that uproots the creeper and breaks it. In this way the leaves of the creeper are dried up."
- **Context:** Lord Caitanya to Rupa Gosvami at Prayaga, between the creeper's growth (19.151-155) and the weeds
  (19.158-161, trd-49).
- **Purport point:** "if one thinks that he has become very mature and can live separate from the association of
  Vaiṣṇavas and thus gives up all the regulative principles due to offending a Vaiṣṇava, one's position becomes very
  dangerous… Giving up the regulative principles and living according to one's whims is compared to a mad elephant…
  Such an offense is especially created when one disobeys the instructions of the spiritual master. This is called
  guru-avajñā… As soon as one is deviated from the instructions of the spiritual master, the uprooting of the
  bhakti-latā begins, and gradually all the leaves dry up."
- **Use:** in the talk, the central verse.

**CC Madhya 19.157**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/19/157/
> "The gardener must defend the creeper by fencing it all around so that the powerful elephant of offenses may not enter."
- **Purport point:** "The neophyte devotee must be protected by being surrounded by pure devotees… A so-called mature
  devotee, however, commits a great offense by giving up the company of pure devotees… Even if one thinks that there
  are many pseudo devotees or nondevotees in the Kṛṣṇa Consciousness Society, still one should stick to the Society; if
  one thinks the Society's members are not pure devotees, one can keep direct company with the spiritual master, and if
  there is any doubt, one should consult the spiritual master… By one's mental concoctions, one falls down."
- **Use:** in the talk: the fence, and the specific warning to the "so-called mature devotee".

**NOI 6**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/6/
> "…Such a devotee should not be seen from a materialistic point of view. Indeed, one should overlook a devotee's having a body born in a low family, a body with a bad complexion, a deformed body, or a diseased or infirm body… It is exactly like the waters of the Ganges, which sometimes during the rainy season are full of bubbles, foam and mud. The Ganges waters do not become polluted…"
- **Purport points:** "No one should criticize the bodily defects of a pure devotee. If there are such defects, they
  should be overlooked." "If we consider the bodily defects of a Vaiṣṇava, we should understand that we are committing
  an offense at the lotus feet of the Vaiṣṇava. An offense at the lotus feet of a Vaiṣṇava is very serious. Indeed, Śrī
  Caitanya Mahāprabhu has described this offense as hātī-mātā, the mad elephant offense." "Everyone should therefore be
  very careful not to be jealous of an empowered Vaiṣṇava, or a śuddha-vaiṣṇava. It is also an offense to consider an
  empowered Vaiṣṇava an object of disciplinary action. It is offensive to try to give him advice or to correct him."
  Prabhupada also writes of "neophyte godbrothers who do not appreciate the extraordinary activities of spreading Kṛṣṇa
  consciousness all over the world. They simply try to bring us to their platform, and they try to criticize us in every
  respect." The purport distinguishes the kanistha, who "cannot distinguish between a devotee and nondevotee", from
  the madhyama, who treats the Lord, the devotee and the nondevotee differently.
- **Application (preacher's):** the senior's offence is often not against a devotee's body but against his way of
  preaching, his manner, his decisions: "to give him advice or to correct him" from a lower platform.
- **Use:** in the talk, in Prabhupada's words.

**CC Adi 8.24**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/adi/8/24/
> "There are offenses to be considered while chanting the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra. Therefore simply by chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa one does not become ecstatic."
- **Purport point (the first offence, Padma Purana as Prabhupada gives it):** "The first offense is to blaspheme great
  personalities who are engaged in distributing the holy name of the Lord… *satāṁ nindā nāmnaḥ paramam aparādhaṁ
  vitanute*… To blaspheme the great saintly persons who are engaged in preaching the glories of the Hare Kṛṣṇa
  mahā-mantra is the worst offense at the lotus feet of the holy name… The Nāma-prabhu, who is identical with Kṛṣṇa,
  will never tolerate such blasphemous activities, even from one who passes as a great devotee."
- **Use:** in the talk: the first of the ten offences (dps-06 gives all ten) is this very offence, and it is not excused
  by seniority: "even from one who passes as a great devotee".

**SB 10.74.40**: Verified · BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples ·
https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/10/74/40/
> "Anyone who fails to immediately leave the place where he hears criticism of the Supreme Lord or His faithful devotee will certainly fall down, bereft of his pious credit."
- **Context:** Sisupala blasphemes Krishna at Yudhisthira's Rajasuya sacrifice; the kings who could not bear to hear it
  leave.
- **Use:** in the talk: hearing is the senior's usual share in the offence.

**SB 4.4.17**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/4/17/
> "Satī continued: If one hears an irresponsible person blaspheme the master and controller of religion, one should block his ears and go away if unable to punish him…"
- **Purport point:** "the instruction set forth here in Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam is that one should not tolerate at any cost
  the activities of a person who vilifies or blasphemes an authority… a brāhmaṇa should leave the place or block his
  ears so that he will not hear the blasphemy." (The verse and purport also give the ksatriya's duty and Sati's own
  decision; read the whole purport, and teach the verse as it is.)
- **Use:** in the talk, together with SB 10.74.40.

**SB 9.4.36 to 9.5.24 (the Durvasa-Ambarisa history)**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/9/4/36/ and
following pages
- 9.4.36: Ambarisa receives Durvasa, offers worship, "sitting at his feet, the King requested the great sage to eat."
- 9.4.38: "only a muhūrta of the Dvādaśī day was left on which to break the fast… the King consulted learned
  brāhmaṇas."
- 9.4.39-40: the King's reasoning: transgressing respect for brahmanas is "a great offense", not breaking the fast in
  time is "a flaw in one's observance of the vow"; he drinks water, which "may be accepted as eating and also as not
  eating."
- 9.4.42: Durvasa returns and "by his mystic power, could understand that King Ambarīṣa had drunk water without his
  permission."
- 9.4.46: "his face became red with anger. Uprooting a bunch of hair from his head, he created a demon resembling the
  blazing fire of devastation to punish Mahārāja Ambarīṣa."
- 9.4.48: "by the previous order of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, His disc, the Sudarśana cakra, immediately
  burnt to ashes the created demon to protect the Lord's devotee."
- 9.4.50-51: the disc pursues Durvasa, who flees "in the sky, on the surface of the earth, in caves, in the ocean, on
  different planets… and even on the heavenly planets", and sees the fire following him everywhere.
- 9.4.63: the Lord to Durvasa: "I am completely under the control of My devotees. Indeed, I am not at all
  independent… What to speak of My devotee, even those who are devotees of My devotee are very dear to Me."
- 9.4.68: "The pure devotee is always within the core of My heart, and I am always in the heart of the pure devotee…"
- 9.4.69: "By offending Mahārāja Ambarīṣa, you have acted with self-envy. Therefore you should go to him immediately,
  without a moment's delay. One's so-called prowess, when employed against the devotee, certainly harms he who employs
  it. Thus it is the subject, not the object, who is harmed."
- 9.4.70: "For a brāhmaṇa, austerity and learning are certainly auspicious, but when acquired by a person who is not
  gentle, such austerity and learning are most dangerous."
- 9.4.71: "you should therefore go immediately to King Ambarīṣa… If you can satisfy Mahārāja Ambarīṣa, then there will
  be peace for you."
- 9.5.1: Durvasa "immediately approached Mahārāja Ambarīṣa. Being very much aggrieved, the muni fell down and clasped
  the King's lotus feet."
- 9.5.14: Durvasa: "today I have experienced the greatness of devotees of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, for
  although I have committed an offense, you have prayed for my good fortune."
- 9.5.24: after one year Ambarisa fed Durvasa and "then he himself also ate… he did not take any credit, for everything
  had been done by the Lord."
- **Purport points:** on 9.4.69, "A Vaiṣṇava is always an object of envy for nondevotees, even when the nondevotee
  happens to be his father"; on 9.4.70, "a jewel is very valuable, but when it is on the hood of a serpent, it is
  dangerous despite its value."
- **Use:** in the talk (J), told as the verses tell it.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** The creeper grows by watering (CC Madhya 19.152); weeds reduce it (19.158-161, trd-49); but one
   thing uproots it: an offence at a Vaisnava's feet (19.156). The image is exact. A weed competes for water; an
   elephant does not compete, it destroys. The leaves of decades can dry.
2. **What the offence is, in Prabhupada's words.** Three things, from the 19.156-157 and NOI 6 purports: (a) offending
   a Vaisnava, including seeing his bodily or external defects and criticising them, and "to give him advice or to
   correct him" when he is empowered (NOI 6); (b) *guru-avajna*, disobeying the spiritual master's instructions; (c)
   thinking oneself "very mature", giving up the association of devotees and the regulative principles, and living by
   "mental concoctions". The third is the one a senior is most likely to call by another name: independence, maturity,
   discernment.
3. **Why seniors are the ones at risk.** The 19.157 purport addresses "a so-called mature devotee" twice. The newcomer
   is surrounded and protected; the senior has the standing to form a verdict on a peer and the audience to share it.
   NOI 6 describes "neophyte godbrothers" who "try to criticize us in every respect": godbrothers, not strangers.
   CC Adi 8.24: the holy name "will never tolerate such blasphemous activities, even from one who passes as a great
   devotee."
4. **The fence.** 19.157: association of pure devotees and the regulative principles. Prabhupada's instruction is
   blunt: even if one thinks the Society is full of pseudo devotees, "still one should stick to the Society", keep the
   company of the spiritual master, and consult him when in doubt. Leaving is the elephant's open gate.
5. **Hearing is participation.** SB 10.74.40: one who does not leave the place where a devotee is criticised "will
   certainly fall down". SB 4.4.17: block the ears and go. In a temple, the senior's share in Vaisnava-aparadha is most
   often as the listener who stays.
6. **The remedy: Durvasa's path.** The Lord Himself could not protect Durvasa from the Sudarsana; He sent him back to
   the devotee he had offended (9.4.69, 71). The offence against a devotee is forgiven by that devotee. Ambarisa prayed
   for his offender (9.5.14) and waited a year to eat. For residents: if the offence was spoken, go to the person;
   if it was heard and enjoyed, go also.
7. **Connections.** Envy appears first as a weed (trd-49, jiva-himsa) and matures into the elephant. Honesty with
   authorities (trd-16) prevents the silent verdict. Conflicts resolved the Vaisnava way (trd-19) do not become
   offences. dps-07 gives the daily practice against fault-finding.
8. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *Correcting a senior is my duty if he is wrong.* NOI 6: it is offensive "to give him advice or to correct him"
     when he is an empowered Vaisnava; one raises a concern through the proper authority (dps-02, trd-16), never by
     verdict and broadcast.
   - *An advanced devotee cannot commit this offence.* CC Adi 8.24: "even from one who passes as a great devotee".
     Durvasa was a great sage.
   - *Not every devotee is a pure devotee, so criticism of most is safe.* The 19.157 purport forbids this reasoning as
     the ground for leaving the devotees' company; and the madhyama's duty (NOI 5, dps-25) is respect in proportion,
     never contempt.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes).** Say: "Last session we looked at weeds beside the creeper. Tonight is about the
one thing Lord Caitanya says does not compete with the creeper but uproots it: an offence at a Vaisnava's feet. He calls
it a mad elephant. Srila Prabhupada's purport on the fence that keeps the elephant out speaks twice about 'a so-called
mature devotee'. That is us: the ones who have been here long enough to have opinions about each other." Read
CC Madhya 19.156 aloud. Then: "We will read what the offence is in Prabhupada's words, how the fence is built, and what
Durvasa Muni did when he had already committed it."

**H.2 Opening questions:**
1. "Think of the devotee you find hardest to respect. Without naming him: is your difficulty with his service to
   Krishna, or with his body, his manner, his decisions?"
2. "When someone criticises a godbrother in your presence, what do you usually do: join, stay silent, or leave?"
3. "Have you ever been forgiven by someone you wronged? What did it take to go to him?"

## I. Core teaching points

1. **One offence can uproot decades.** CC Madhya 19.156. Plain explanation: the elephant does not compete, it
   destroys. Example (preacher's application): a devotee of long standing who left after a dispute with a godbrother
   and gave up his practice within a year; the purport describes exactly this sequence. Transition: what the offence is.
2. **Three forms: offending a Vaisnava, disobeying guru, leaving the fence.** 19.156-157 purports. Example: each in a
   senior's vocabulary ("he is not on the platform he claims", "I know what Prabhupada really meant", "I don't need the
   temple to practise"). Transition: the subtle form.
3. **Criticising an empowered Vaisnava's defects, or correcting him, is the offence.** NOI 6. Example: a resident who
   dismisses a senior preacher's results because of his manner. Transition: the holy name's own first offence.
4. **The holy name does not tolerate it, "even from one who passes as a great devotee".** CC Adi 8.24. Transition: the
   fence.
5. **Stick to the Society; consult the spiritual master; do not concoct.** 19.157 purport. Example: the devotee who
   stays, takes his doubt to his authority and is still chanting twenty years later. Transition: hearing.
6. **Leave the place where a devotee is criticised.** SB 10.74.40; SB 4.4.17. Example: a prasadam conversation.
   Transition: the remedy.
7. **Go to the devotee you offended, "without a moment's delay".** SB 9.4.69-71; 9.5.1, 9.5.14. Transition to M.

## J. Stories and illustrations

- **Durvasa Muni and Maharaja Ambarisa (SB 9.4.36 to 9.5.24).** Tell it from the verse sequence in E, in the verses'
  own words, nothing added. Devotional lesson, stated after the narration: the Lord does not override His devotee to
  forgive an offence against him; the offender is sent to the devotee (9.4.69, 71). "It is the subject, not the object,
  who is harmed" (9.4.69). Austerity and learning in one who is not gentle "are most dangerous" (9.4.70): the senior's
  attainments do not protect him; they sharpen the danger. Read SB 9.4.33 to 9.5.27 in full before teaching.
- **Sisupala at the Rajasuya (SB 10.74.40, context).** The kings who could not bear to hear Krishna blasphemed left the
  assembly. Devotional lesson: leaving is a devotional act. Tell only what the chapter tells; read SB 10.74.30-40 first.
- **Sati before Daksa (SB 4.4.17).** Sati, hearing her father blaspheme Lord Siva, declares what one must do on hearing
  blasphemy of an authority. The chapter then tells her decision; read it and tell it as it is; the purport explains
  the different duties by varna. Devotional lesson: hearing blasphemy is not neutral.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. *"If a senior is actually doing wrong, must I stay silent?"* No. Take the concern to the proper authority, in
   confidence, as NOI 4 and dps-02 describe, and let the authority act. What is forbidden is the private verdict shared
   around and the attempt to "correct" an empowered Vaisnava from below (NOI 6). Serious misconduct is reported to the
   authorities responsible; that is protection of the Society, not aparadha.
2. *"Not everyone in the temple is a pure devotee. Does NOI 6 protect them all?"* NOI 6 speaks of the pure and
   empowered devotee. For others, NOI 5 (dps-25) gives respect in proportion, and the 19.157 purport forbids using "they
   are not pure" as a reason to leave the devotees' company or to criticise freely. Criticism of any devotee who is
   sincerely practising harms the critic (dps-07).
3. *"I committed an offence years ago and the devotee has left or passed away."* Follow Durvasa as far as possible:
   acknowledge it to your spiritual master or a senior Vaisnava, pray for the devotee's welfare as Ambarisa prayed for
   Durvasa (9.5.14 in reverse), serve his service, and stop repeating the offence. Do not conclude it is hopeless; the
   Lord Himself gave Durvasa a path.
4. *"Is leaving a conversation rude?"* SB 10.74.40 and 4.4.17 make it a duty. Leave quietly, or change the subject
   once, then leave.
5. *"What about my own authority who, I believe, disobeys Prabhupada?"* The 19.157 purport: "if there is any doubt,
   one should consult the spiritual master." Doubt is taken upward, to the spiritual master and the Society's
   processes (dps-43 for the controversies), never resolved by private concoction and departure.

## L. Case studies

**Case 1 (Invented case for discussion).** Two residents of fifteen years each lead a department. One's programmes
grow; the other begins, in private talks with two juniors, to question the first one's "motives" and "standards".
Within months the juniors repeat it. *Principles:* CC Madhya 19.156 (the elephant); NOI 6 ("to criticize us in every
respect"; jealousy of an empowered Vaisnava); SB 10.74.40 (the hearers). *Questions:* Who is harmed first? What should
the juniors have done at the first conversation? *Analysis:* the critic feeds the elephant with his own creeper, and the
juniors' creepers are grazed by hearing. *Takeaway:* the critic goes to his godbrother (9.4.69); the juniors leave such
conversations (SB 10.74.40).

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions (expected answers in brackets):**
1. To what does Lord Caitanya compare an offence at a Vaisnava's feet (CC Madhya 19.156)? [A mad elephant that uproots
   and breaks the creeper.]
2. What is the fence (19.157 purport)? [Association of pure devotees and following the regulative principles.]
3. What does Prabhupada say to one who thinks the Society is full of pseudo devotees? [Still stick to the Society; keep
   the spiritual master's company; consult him when in doubt.]
4. According to NOI 6, what is offensive toward an empowered Vaisnava besides criticising his body? [To consider him an
   object of discipline, to give him advice or to correct him; to be jealous of him.]
5. Where did the Lord send Durvasa for relief (SB 9.4.69, 71)? [To Maharaja Ambarisa, the devotee he had offended,
   immediately.]
6. What should one do on hearing criticism of a devotee (SB 10.74.40; SB 4.4.17)? [Leave the place immediately; block
   the ears and go.]

**M.2 Activity (25 min): the two lists.** Each resident writes privately: (a) one devotee he has spoken against or
heard spoken against without leaving, in the last year; (b) the exact step he will take (go to the person; go to his
authority; stop a conversation). Then, in pairs, discuss only how to approach a godbrother to ask forgiveness, using
Durvasa's example (9.5.1), never the private content.

**M.3 Reflection.** "Which of the three gates (criticism, disobedience, independence) is open in my life?"

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Lord Caitanya told Rupa Gosvami that while the creeper of devotion grows in the heart, one thing can destroy it
entirely: "If the devotee commits an offense at the feet of a Vaisnava… his offense is compared to a mad elephant that
uproots the creeper and breaks it. In this way the leaves of the creeper are dried up" (CC Madhya 19.156). Srila
Prabhupada's purport names three forms of this offence: offending a Vaisnava; disobeying the spiritual master
(*guru-avajna*); and thinking oneself "very mature", giving up the association of devotees and the regulative
principles, and living by "mental concoctions". The Lord then said, "The gardener must defend the creeper by fencing it
all around" (19.157). The fence is the company of pure devotees and the regulative principles. Prabhupada writes that
even if one thinks the Society is full of pseudo devotees, "still one should stick to the Society", keep the spiritual
master's company and consult him in doubt.

In NOI 6, Rupa Gosvami says a pure devotee "should not be seen from a materialistic point of view": his bodily defects
are like foam and mud on the Ganges, which do not pollute it. Prabhupada's purport adds that considering a Vaisnava's
defects is "an offense at the lotus feet of the Vaisnava", the mad-elephant offence; that one must not be jealous of an
empowered Vaisnava; and that "it is offensive to try to give him advice or to correct him". The first of the ten
offences to the holy name is this same thing: to blaspheme those who distribute the holy name, which the holy name
"will never tolerate… even from one who passes as a great devotee" (CC Adi 8.24 purport). Hearing is also
participation: "Anyone who fails to immediately leave the place where he hears criticism of the Supreme Lord or His
faithful devotee will certainly fall down" (SB 10.74.40), and Sati says to block one's ears and go (SB 4.4.17).

The Bhagavatam shows the remedy. Durvasa Muni, angry that King Ambarisa had broken his Ekadasi fast with water before the
sage returned, created a demon to kill him; the Lord's Sudarsana disc burnt the demon and chased the sage through the
whole universe. Lord Visnu told him, "I am completely under the control of My devotees" and sent him back to the King
"immediately, without a moment's delay" (SB 9.4.63, 69). Durvasa fell at Ambarisa's feet; the King prayed for his
welfare; after a year the King fed him and only then ate himself (SB 9.5.1, 9.5.14, 9.5.24). An offence against a
devotee is settled at that devotee's feet.

## S2. Key ideas
- One offence against a Vaisnava can uproot decades of devotional growth (CC Madhya 19.156).
- Three forms: criticising a Vaisnava, disobeying guru, leaving the devotees' company as a "so-called mature devotee".
- The fence: association of pure devotees and the regulative principles; stick to the Society and consult the spiritual
  master (19.157 purport).
- Do not judge or "correct" an empowered Vaisnava from below (NOI 6); leave where devotees are criticised (SB 10.74.40).
- The remedy is Durvasa's: go to the devotee you offended, at once (SB 9.4.69).

## S3. Verses to remember
- **CC Madhya 19.156**: "a mad elephant that uproots the creeper and breaks it." For me: my years do not protect me.
- **NOI 6**: the Ganges "waters do not become polluted" by foam and mud. For me: I do not judge a devotee by his
  externals.
- **SB 9.4.69**: "you should go to him immediately, without a moment's delay." For me: an apology delayed is an elephant
  fed.

## S4. Words to know
- **Vaisnava-aparadha:** an offence against a devotee of the Lord.
- **Hati-mata:** the mad elephant (Lord Caitanya's image for the offence).
- **Guru-avajna:** disobeying or disregarding the spiritual master.
- **Kanistha, madhyama, uttama:** the neophyte, intermediate and advanced devotee (NOI 5-6).
- **Sudarsana cakra:** the Lord's disc weapon, which protected Ambarisa.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *If a senior is actually doing wrong, must I stay silent?* No: take the concern to the proper authority in
   confidence and let the authority act. What is forbidden is the private verdict shared around and "correcting" an
   empowered Vaisnava from below (NOI 6).
2. *Not everyone in the temple is a pure devotee. May I criticise the others?* Prabhupada forbids using "they are not
   pure" as a reason to leave the devotees' company or criticise freely (19.157 purport); respect is given in proportion
   (NOI 5), and fault-finding harms the critic (dps-07).
3. *I offended a devotee years ago and cannot reach him.* Acknowledge it to your spiritual master or a senior Vaisnava,
   pray for that devotee's welfare, serve his service, and stop repeating the offence.
4. *Is leaving a conversation rude?* The Bhagavatam makes it a duty (SB 10.74.40; 4.4.17). Leave quietly.

## S6. Check your understanding
1. To what does Lord Caitanya compare an offence at a Vaisnava's feet?
2. What two things make up the fence (19.157 purport)?
3. True or false: Prabhupada says that if one thinks the Society's members are not pure devotees, one should leave it.
4. What does NOI 6 say about trying to advise or correct an empowered Vaisnava?
5. Where did Lord Visnu send Durvasa for relief, and how quickly?

**Answers:** 1. A mad elephant that uproots and breaks the creeper (CC Madhya 19.156). 2. Association of pure devotees
and following the regulative principles (19.157 purport). 3. False: "still one should stick to the Society" and consult
the spiritual master (19.157 purport). 4. It is offensive (NOI 6 purport). 5. To Maharaja Ambarisa, "immediately,
without a moment's delay" (SB 9.4.69).
