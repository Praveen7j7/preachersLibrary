> **TRD · up to 180 min · review: drafted.** Group C (non-duplicity), fourth page. Residents handle donations, prasada

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on trd-14 and trd-16. A resident has no salary and no private property, yet money and goods pass through his
hands daily: donations, book-distribution collections, a temple phone, a vehicle, the prasada store. Nobody checks
every rupee. The Bhagavatam gives the measure of what he may use for himself, and Lord Caitanya showed, in the case of
Gopinatha Pattanayaka, what He thinks of a devotee who spends entrusted revenue on himself.

| VL3 | Simple living as a support for chanting; NOI 2's warning against over-collecting |

## C. Central message

Everything belongs to Krishna; a devotee may use only his quota, as much as maintains body and soul, and whoever
takes more "must be considered a thief" (SB 7.14.8; ISO 1). Lord Caitanya called misusing entrusted money "a greatly
sinful affair" He could not tolerate (CC Antya 9.92). A resident protects himself by simplicity and by transparency:
nothing spent that his authority would not know about.

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Quote SB 7.14.8 and ISO 1 and explain the measure they give (body and soul together; one's quota).
2. Explain from the SB 7.14.8 purport what should be done with "more than one requires" (spent for Krishna, not accumulated).
3. Tell the Gopinatha Pattanayaka episode as CC Antya 9 tells it and state the Lord's own verdict (9.89–92) and His final order (9.142–143).
4. Name NOI 2's first fault, *atyahara*, in both its meanings (overeating; collecting more funds than required).
5. Adopt three transparent habits for their own service (I.5) and one simplicity standard from CC Antya 6.236–237.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**SB 7.14.8** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/14/8/
> "One may claim proprietorship to as much wealth as required to maintain body and soul together, but one who desires proprietorship over more than that must be considered a thief, and he deserves to be punished by the laws of nature."
- **Purport points:** "There is no need to accumulate money in the bank merely to increase one's bank balance. Such a mentality is described in Bhagavad-gītā (16.13) as asuric, demoniac." "Actually, if one has more than one requires for his necessities, the extra money should be spent for Kṛṣṇa." "This verse is very important. As stated here, one who accumulates more money than needed is a thief, and by the laws of nature he will be punished." "One who acquires more money than necessary becomes desirous of enjoying material comforts more and more."
- **Use:** in the talk, the central text. *Application (preacher's):* a resident's "extra" is anything beyond maintenance that passes through his hands; its only destination is Krishna's service through the mission's channel.

**ISO 1** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/iso/1/
> "Everything animate or inanimate that is within the universe is controlled and owned by the Lord. One should therefore accept only those things necessary for himself, which are set aside as his quota, and one should not accept other things, knowing well to whom they belong."
- **Purport point:** "everything is the property of the Supreme Being." *Use:* the ownership behind the measure.

**BG 3.12** — Verified. "He who enjoys such gifts without offering them… in return is certainly a thief." *Use:* Krishna's own word for using what is supplied without offering it back.

**NOI 2** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/2/
> "One's devotional service is spoiled when he becomes too entangled in the following six activities: (1) eating more than necessary or collecting more funds than required; (2) over-endeavoring for mundane things that are very difficult to obtain…"
- **Purport point:** "Human life is meant for plain living and high thinking." (All six faults: DPS-34.)
- **Use:** in the talk: *atyahara* includes collecting, not only eating.

**SB 8.19.17** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/8/19/17/
> "I do not want anything from you that is unnecessary. If a learned brāhmaṇa takes charity from others only according to his needs, he does not become entangled in sinful activities."
- **Purport point:** "No one can use more of the Supreme Lord's property than necessary… This principle should especially be followed by brāhmaṇas and sannyāsīs who live at the cost of others. Thus Vāmanadeva was an ideal beggar." *Use:* the Lord as the model beggar.

**SB 8.19.21** — Verified. Even the three worlds "cannot satisfy a person whose senses are uncontrolled." *Use:* why "a little more" never ends.

**SB 1.2.9** — Verified. Never "use material gain to cultivate sense gratification." *Use:* link to trd-14.

**CC Antya 9.61–65, 9.89–92, 9.112, 9.142–143** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/9/
> 9.89: "The revenue of the government is more sacred than the property of a brāhmaṇa. One who misappropriates the government's money and uses it to enjoy sense gratification is most sinful."
> 9.91–92: "…the King is certainly very religious. But Gopīnātha Paṭṭanāyaka is a great cheat. He does not pay the revenue to the King, but he wants My help for release. This is a greatly sinful affair. I cannot tolerate it here."
- **Use:** story J.1, told as the chapter tells it.

**CC Antya 6.236–237** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/6/237/
> "You should not eat very palatable food, nor should you dress very nicely. Do not expect honor, but offer all respect to others. Always chant the holy name of Lord Kṛṣṇa…"
- **Use:** in the talk: the Lord's simplicity standard for a renunciant.

**Srila Prabhupada's letters on spending the Society's money** — **Requires source review.** Prabhupada's letters to
temple leaders on accounts, receipts and not spending Krishna's money for personal comfort are often quoted in classes.
Vedabase letters could not be searched for this page; cite a letter only after opening it on vedabase.io and copying
its date and recipient. The principle is fully established above from the books.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** Krishna owns everything (ISO 1). A living being may use his quota: what maintains body and soul
   (SB 7.14.8). Beyond that quota, possession is theft, whoever the possessor is; Krishna uses the word Himself for one
   who enjoys what is supplied without offering it back (BG 3.12). The resident does not escape this by owning nothing
   on paper: what passes through his hands is held in trust.
2. **The two directions of the fault.** *Taking*: using the mission's money or goods for comfort beyond maintenance
   (SB 7.14.8; SB 1.2.9: never use gain for sense gratification). *Hoarding*: collecting or keeping more than the service
   needs, even with good intentions, which NOI 2 names first among the faults that spoil bhakti (*atyahara*) and which the
   7.14.8 purport calls "asuric" when it is accumulation for its own sake. The extra "should be spent for Kṛṣṇa," through
   the mission's own channel, not by private decision.
3. **Why "a little" is not little.** The purport to SB 7.14.8: "One who acquires more money than necessary becomes
   desirous of enjoying material comforts more and more." Bali Maharaja was told that the three worlds cannot satisfy
   uncontrolled senses (SB 8.19.21). A resident who starts using the temple phone's balance for himself does not stop
   at the phone.
4. **The Lord's verdict.** In CC Antya 9 the Lord refused to intervene for a devotee's son who had spent entrusted
   revenue, called it "a greatly sinful affair" He "cannot tolerate," and said the King who wanted only his money was
   "very religious" (9.89–92). When the King forgave the debt, the text records that this "was not the desire of Lord
   Caitanya Mahāprabhu" (9.112). His final order to the man was not "renounce your post" but "do not spend any of the
   King's revenue; first pay what is due" (9.142–143). Entrusted money is to be accounted for before anything else.
5. **Transparency and simplicity.** Nothing used that the authority would not know about (trd-16 applied to things): a
   receipt and a question asked first protect the resident's heart and the donor's faith. And the Lord's rule to
   Raghunatha dasa, not very palatable food, not very nice dress (CC Antya 6.236): a resident who wants little has little
   reason to take.
6. **Misunderstandings.** *"It is all Krishna's, and I am Krishna's, so I may use it":* the measure is body and soul;
   beyond it, "thief" (SB 7.14.8). *"I spend it on service, so no accounting is needed":* the Lord did not want even the
   King's pardon to replace the account (9.112). *"Simplicity means neglecting health":* SB 1.2.10 asks for a healthy
   life (trd-20).

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet the residents. Say: "None of us has a salary, and all of us handle money and things that are not ours. Today
   the scriptures tell us how much of it is ours to use, and what Lord Caitanya said when a devotee's son misused what was
   entrusted to him."
2. Write on the board: *yavad bhriyeta jatharam* ("as much as required to maintain body and soul together", SB 7.14.8).
3. Say: "No one's accounts are being examined today except by himself."
4. Ask H.2 question 1 for private writing.
5. Ask question 2 aloud; take two or three answers; thank each; begin with ISO 1.

**H.2 Opening questions**
1. (Private.) List everything of the mission's you used this week that was not strictly needed for your service or
   maintenance. Write the list; keep it.
2. If a shop assistant takes a little from the till every day "because he works so hard," what do we call it? Why does
   the word feel harsher when the till is Krishna's?

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Krishna owns everything; I have a quota.** ISO 1; SB 7.14.8 (body and soul together); beyond it, "thief" (SB 7.14.8;
   BG 3.12). *Transition:* what about more that arrives honestly?
2. **Extra is for Krishna, through the right channel; keeping it is asuric.** SB 7.14.8 purport; NOI 2 (*atyahara*:
   collecting more funds than required). *Example:* a preacher who keeps a "float" from collections "for emergencies"
   without his authority's knowledge. *Transition:* who models the opposite?
3. **The Lord as the ideal beggar.** Vamanadeva asked three paces; "No one can use more of the Supreme Lord's property
   than necessary… especially… brāhmaṇas and sannyāsīs who live at the cost of others" (SB 8.19.17 purport). Uncontrolled
   senses are never satisfied (SB 8.19.21). *Transition:* and when entrusted money is misused?
4. **The Lord's verdict on misused revenue.** CC Antya 9.89–92; 9.112; 9.142–143 (J.1). *Transition:* so what habits keep
   a resident safe?
5. **Three transparent habits and one simplicity standard (Application, preacher's).** (a) Every rupee received or spent
   for the mission is written down the same day and shown to the person responsible. (b) Ask before using, never explain
   after. (c) Nothing of the mission's is used for oneself that the authority does not know about (trd-16). And the
   standard: not very palatable food, not very nice dress (CC Antya 6.236).

## J. Stories and illustrations

**J.1 Gopinatha Pattanayaka (CC Antya 9).** Told as the chapter tells it. Gopinatha, "the son of Bhavānanda Rāya, was
engaged in the service of the government, but he misappropriated some funds from the treasury," and the King's eldest
son "ordered that he be punished by death" (chapter summary). Devotees asked the Lord to protect him (9.14–17). He said:
"All the family members of Bhavānanda Rāya are engaged in government service, but they spend the government's revenue in
various ways. What is the fault on the part of the King? He wants the government's money. However, when they are punished
for failing to pay the government its due, they come to Me to release them" (9.61–62). "Jagannātha has saved him once from death today, but if
tomorrow he again does not pay what he owes the treasury, who will give him protection?" (9.65). To Kasi Misra He said: "The revenue of the government is more sacred than the property
of a brāhmaṇa. One who misappropriates the government's money and uses it to enjoy sense gratification is most sinful…
The King wanted his revenue paid and did not want to enforce punishment. Therefore the King is certainly very religious.
But Gopīnātha Paṭṭanāyaka is a great cheat. He does not pay the revenue to the King, but he wants My help for release.
This is a greatly sinful affair. I cannot tolerate it here" (9.89–92). The King, hearing of the Lord's displeasure,
excused all the debt and doubled Gopinatha's salary (9.100–106); the text adds: "It was not the desire of Lord Caitanya
Mahāprabhu that Gopīnātha Paṭṭanāyaka be excused of his debt to the government, nor was it His desire that his salary
be doubled" (9.112). When Gopinatha came to thank Him, the Lord gave one order: "Do not spend any of the King's revenue.
First you should pay the revenue due the King, and then you may spend the balance for religious and fruitive activities"
(9.142–143).
- *Connection:* the Lord would not cover misused funds even for a dear devotee's family; "religious activities" came
  only after the entrusted money was paid. *Use:* thirty minutes; read the Lord's words aloud.

**Vamanadeva's three paces (SB 8.19.17 and purport).** The Lord, able to take everything, asked only what He needed, "an
ideal beggar"; the resident, who also "live[s] at the cost of others," follows Him.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"I use the temple vehicle and phone for small personal things. Is that theft?"**
   - The measure is what maintains body and soul (SB 7.14.8). Small personal use beyond that, unknown to the authority,
     is what the purport describes as the start of wanting "more and more." Ask first; then it is the mission's gift, not
     your taking.
2. **"Prabhupada built big temples and wanted money for the mission. Isn't collecting good?"**
   - Collecting for Krishna is service; the 7.14.8 purport sends extra money exactly there. NOI 2 names the fault:
     collecting *more than required*, or keeping it. The test is whether it goes through the mission's channel at once.
3. **"If I spend collected money directly on preaching, why must I write it down?"**
   - Because entrusted money is accounted for before anything else (CC Antya 9.142–143), and because a record protects
     your own heart and the donor's faith. The Lord did not want even the King's forgiveness to replace the account (9.112).
4. **"Simplicity sounds like poverty. Won't it harm health or preaching?"**
   - The measure includes a healthy body (SB 1.2.10; trd-20). The Lord's rule is about *very* palatable food and *very*
     nice dress (CC Antya 6.236), not about neglect.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real people.*

**Case 1: The float.** Madhu runs book distribution. Collections come in cash. He keeps about two thousand rupees aside
"for emergencies," unknown to the treasurer, and sometimes buys snacks from it on long trips.
- **Dilemma:** He steals nothing on purpose. Where is he in the texts?
- **Principles:** SB 7.14.8 (more than maintenance, thief); NOI 2 (collecting more than required); CC Antya 9.142–143
  (pay what is due first); trd-16 (what the authority does not know).
- **Questions:** Which habit in I.5 would have prevented this? What should he do tomorrow morning? What is the right way
  to have a travel allowance?
- **Analysis:** The float is *atyahara*; the snacks are using gain for sense gratification (SB 1.2.9). Tomorrow: hand
  over the full amount with an account, and ask for a proper travel allowance, which the mission may gladly give.
- **Takeaway:** The same money, asked for openly, is service; kept quietly, it is the beginning of theft.

**Case 2: The gift.** A donor gives Nanda a phone "for your preaching"; he keeps it without telling anyone. *Analysis:*
the quota (ISO 1) is set by the authority, not the receiver; he reports it the same day and uses it as decided.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first; thank each answer)
1. According to SB 7.14.8, how much may one claim as one's own? *Expected:* as much as maintains body and soul together.
2. What does the same verse call one who wants more? *Expected:* a thief, punished by the laws of nature.
3. What are the two meanings of *atyahara* in NOI 2? *Expected:* eating more than necessary; collecting more funds than required.
4. In the Lord's own words, why was Gopinatha Pattanayaka "a great cheat"? *Expected:* he did not pay the revenue to the King but wanted the Lord's help for release (CC Antya 9.91–92).
5. What was the Lord's final order to him? *Expected:* do not spend the King's revenue; pay what is due first (9.142–143).

**M.2 Activities**
- **Option 1: The account (10 min, private).** Each resident writes a true account of all mission money and goods he
  holds right now, and one line on which item his authority does not know about. He decides to whom he will show it.
- **Option 2: Department standard (25 min).** Groups by department draft five plain rules for handling money and goods
  (receipts, who approves, personal use, gifts, travel). Each group reads its rules; the room checks each against SB
  7.14.8 and CC Antya 9.142–143. The drafts go to the temple authority.

**M.3 Reflection**
- Why did the Lord, who forgave Jagai and Madhai, refuse to help a devotee's son escape a debt? What does that tell me
  about Krishna's view of entrusted money?

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Everything in the universe "is controlled and owned by the Lord," so a person "should accept only those things
necessary for himself, which are set aside as his quota" (ISO 1). The Bhagavatam gives the measure: one may claim "as
much wealth as required to maintain body and soul together, but one who desires proprietorship over more than that
must be considered a thief" (SB 7.14.8). Krishna uses the same word for one who enjoys what is supplied without
offering it back (BG 3.12). Prabhupada's purport says that extra money "should be spent for Kṛṣṇa," and that keeping it
to grow a balance is "asuric." Rupa Gosvami names *atyahara*, which includes "collecting more funds than required,"
first among the six faults that spoil devotion (NOI 2). Vamanadeva asked Bali for only three paces, "an ideal beggar";
the rule applies "especially" to those "who live at the cost of others" (SB 8.19.17 purport).

In Caitanya-caritamrta, Gopinatha Pattanayaka, the son of a dear devotee, misused government revenue and was
sentenced to death. Devotees begged the Lord to save him. The Lord said the King was right to want his money, called
Gopinatha "a great cheat" who "does not pay the revenue to the King, but he wants My help for release," and said, "This
is a greatly sinful affair. I cannot tolerate it here" (CC Antya 9.62, 9.89–92). When the King excused the debt, the
text says this "was not the desire of Lord Caitanya Mahāprabhu" (9.112). The Lord's final order was: "Do not spend any
of the King's revenue. First you should pay the revenue due the King" (9.142–143).

Three habits follow: write down every sum the same day and show it to the person responsible; ask before using anything
of the mission's; use nothing for yourself that your authority does not know about. The positive side is the Lord's rule
to Raghunatha dasa: "You should not eat very palatable food, nor should you dress very nicely" (CC Antya 6.236).

## S2. Key ideas

- Everything is Krishna's; I may use my quota, what maintains body and soul (ISO 1; SB 7.14.8).
- More than that is theft, whether taken for comfort or kept "for later" (SB 7.14.8 and purport; BG 3.12; NOI 2).
- Extra goes to Krishna through the mission's channel, accounted first (SB 7.14.8 purport; CC Antya 9.142–143).
- The Lord would not cover a devotee's misuse of entrusted money (CC Antya 9.89–92, 9.112).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **SB 7.14.8:** "as much wealth as required to maintain body and soul together… more than that must be considered a thief." *For me:* the measure is clear.
- **ISO 1:** "accept only those things necessary for himself, which are set aside as his quota." *For me:* the rest has an owner.
- **CC Antya 9.143:** "First you should pay the revenue due the King." *For me:* account first, then everything else.

## S4. Words to know

- **Yavad bhriyeta jatharam:** "as much as fills the stomach": the measure of what one may claim (SB 7.14.8).
- **Atyahara:** over-eating, or collecting more funds than required (NOI 2).
- **Quota (ISO 1):** the portion set aside for one's needs by the Lord's arrangement.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Is small personal use of temple things theft?** Beyond what maintains body and soul, and unknown to the authority, it is the beginning of what SB 7.14.8's purport describes. Ask first, and it becomes the mission's gift.
2. **Collecting for Krishna is good. Where is the fault?** In collecting *more than required* or keeping it (NOI 2); extra goes to Krishna at once through the proper channel (SB 7.14.8 purport).
3. **If I spend it on preaching, why keep accounts?** Entrusted money is accounted for first (CC Antya 9.142–143); the Lord did not want even the King's pardon to replace the account (9.112).

## S6. Check your understanding

1. According to SB 7.14.8, how much may a person claim as his own?
2. What does ISO 1 say about things beyond one's quota?
3. Name the two meanings of *atyahara* in NOI 2.
4. In the Lord's own words, why did He call Gopinatha Pattanayaka "a great cheat"?
5. What was the Lord's final order to Gopinatha (CC Antya 9.142–143)?

**Answers:** 1. As much as required to maintain body and soul together (SB 7.14.8). 2. One should not accept them,
"knowing well to whom they belong" (ISO 1). 3. Eating more than necessary; collecting more funds than required (NOI 2).
4. He did not pay the revenue to the King but wanted the Lord's help for release (CC Antya 9.91–92). 5. Do not spend the
King's revenue; first pay what is due (9.142–143).
