# When sadhana feels dry: staying fresh in the ashram

> **TRD · up to 180 min · review: drafted (30 Oct 2026).** For residents in the long middle: the programme is kept, the
> service is done, and the heart is often dry. The stages themselves are DPS-12…18; the five obstacles in bhajana are
> TRD-51; lost enthusiasm after many years (burnout) is TRD-52. This page treats one thing: dryness of taste, what
> Srila Prabhupada's books say it is, and what they say to do.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on DPS-15 (how steadiness grows) and DPS-16 (what taste is): this page does not re-draw the stages or define ruci again; it meets the resident who stands between them and feels nothing.

A newcomer's dryness ends when he returns to the programme. A resident's dryness is harder: he never left the programme. He chants, hears, serves, and the sweetness others describe is not there. Two wrong conclusions wait for him: "I am not meant for this" and "this is all there is". Srila Prabhupada's books refuse both, and name the condition exactly.

## C. Central message

Dryness is the disease of the tongue, not the absence of the sweet. "The holy name, character, pastimes and activities of Krsna are all transcendentally sweet like sugar candy. Although the tongue of one afflicted by the jaundice of avidyā cannot taste anything sweet, it is wonderful that simply by carefully chanting these sweet names every day, a natural relish awakens within his tongue, and his disease is gradually destroyed at the root" (NOI 7). The remedy for no taste is the medicine taken more carefully, not less; and no sincere step on this path is ever lost (BG 2.40).

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. State what NOI 7 and *Madhurya-kadambini* (Fifth Shower, text 2) say dryness is, and why the remedy is the same practice continued with care.
2. Explain from SB 1.2.17–18 what Krishna does for the one who hears, and what the two inputs are that the Bhagavatam names.
3. Give the Gita's three assurances to a devotee who feels he is failing (BG 2.40; 6.40–45; SB 1.5.17) without turning them into permission for slackness.
4. Distinguish dryness from offence (TRD-08), from burnout (TRD-52) and from illness or low mood that needs a doctor.
5. Choose one change to their hearing for the next month, and one person to review it with.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**NOI 7**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/7/
> "The holy name, character, pastimes and activities of Kṛṣṇa are all transcendentally sweet like sugar candy. Although the tongue of one afflicted by the jaundice of avidyā [ignorance] cannot taste anything sweet, it is wonderful that simply by carefully chanting these sweet names every day, a natural relish awakens within his tongue, and his disease is gradually destroyed at the root."
- **Purport point:** "Attacked by jaundice, the tongue of a diseased person cannot palatably relish sugar candy. Rather, a person with jaundice considers something sweet to taste very bitter. Avidyā (ignorance) similarly perverts the ability to relish the transcendentally palatable name, quality, form and pastimes of Kṛṣṇa. Despite this disease, if one with great care and attention takes to Kṛṣṇa consciousness, chanting the holy name and hearing Kṛṣṇa's transcendental pastimes, his ignorance will be destroyed and his tongue enabled to taste the sweetness... Such a recovery of spiritual health is possible only by the regular cultivation of Kṛṣṇa consciousness."
- **Use:** in the talk; the central reference. Have it on a slide.

**SB 1.2.17–18**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/17/
> 1.2.17: "Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the Personality of Godhead, who is the Paramātmā [Supersoul] in everyone's heart and the benefactor of the truthful devotee, cleanses desire for material enjoyment from the heart of the devotee who has developed the urge to hear His messages, which are in themselves virtuous when properly heard and chanted."
- **Teaching:** The cleansing is Krishna's work; the devotee's part is "the urge to hear". 1.2.18 names the two inputs: "regular attendance in classes on the Bhāgavatam" and "service to the pure devotee"; by them "all that is troublesome to the heart is almost completely destroyed".
- **Purport point (1.2.18):** "The messages of the book Bhāgavata, therefore, have to be received from the devotee Bhāgavata, and the combination of these two Bhāgavatas will help the neophyte devotee to make progress on and on."
- **Application (preacher's):** when dryness comes, check the two inputs before checking the heart. DPS-15 uses these verses for steadiness; here they are the cause of taste.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 2.40**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/40/
> "In this endeavor there is no loss or diminution, and a little advancement on this path can protect one from the most dangerous type of fear."
- **Purport point:** "Even a small beginning of such activity finds no impediment, nor can that small beginning be lost at any stage... One percent done in Kṛṣṇa consciousness bears permanent results, so that the next beginning is from the point of two percent, whereas in material activity without a hundred percent success there is no profit." The purport then quotes SB 1.5.17.
- **Use:** in the talk, as the first assurance.

**BG 6.40–41, 6.43, 6.45**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/40/
> 6.40: "Son of Pṛthā, a transcendentalist engaged in auspicious activities does not meet with destruction either in this world or in the spiritual world; one who does good, My friend, is never overcome by evil."
- **Teaching:** Even the yogi who fails is given another birth suited to continuing (6.41); he "revives the divine consciousness of his previous life, and he again tries" (6.43); "achieving perfection after many, many births of practice, he attains the supreme goal" (6.45).
- **Purport point (6.43):** King Bharata, who "retired for spiritual perfection but failed", became Jada Bharata; "from his life it is understood that transcendental endeavors, or the practice of yoga, never go in vain."
- **Purport point (6.40):** quotes SB 1.5.17 again; "The Bhāgavatam assures the unsuccessful transcendentalist."
- **Application (preacher's):** Arjuna's question in 6.37–39 is the dry resident's question: what happens to the one who starts with faith and does not finish? Krishna's answer is the one we give.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 1.5.17**: Verified. "One who has forsaken his material occupations to engage in the devotional service of the Lord may sometimes fall down while in an immature stage, yet there is no danger of his being unsuccessful." *Purport:* warns also of surrender "by some temporary sentiment" that later falls "by undesirable association". *Use:* in the talk, with both halves.

**BG 18.37–38**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/37/
> 18.37: "That which in the beginning may be just like poison but at the end is just like nectar and which awakens one to self-realization is said to be happiness in the mode of goodness."
- **Purport point:** "All these procedures are very difficult, bitter like poison, but if one is successful in following the regulations and comes to the transcendental position, he begins to drink real nectar, and he enjoys life." 18.38 gives the opposite: sense happiness "appears like nectar at first but poison at the end".
- **Use:** in the talk (why dryness at the start is expected, and why the alternative is worse).

**BG 2.59; BG 9.2**: Verified. Restriction alone does not remove taste for sense objects; "by experiencing a higher taste, he is fixed in consciousness" (2.59). The purport: "restrictions are only good until one actually has a taste for Kṛṣṇa consciousness." Devotional service is "everlasting, and it is joyfully performed" (9.2, *su-sukham*). *Use:* in the talk: the dry resident's rules are meant to end in taste; the path is joyful by nature.

**NOI 3; BG 6.24; BG 6.35; BG 9.14**: Verified. Six principles favourable to bhakti, including "being patient" (NOI 3); the purport: "One should not be impatient in discharging devotional service but should take instructions from the spiritual master and execute them with patience." BG 6.24 purport: "not becoming discouraged if there is any delay in the attainment of success. Success is sure for the rigid practitioner", quoting NOI 3 and telling of the sparrow who set out to dry the ocean. BG 6.35 purport: the practice that curbs the mind is "hearing about Kṛṣṇa... a very powerful transcendental method for purging the mind". BG 9.14: the great souls endeavour "with great determination". *Use:* in the talk (the practical supports).

**SB 11.2.42**: Verified · *BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples.* "Devotion, direct experience of the Supreme Lord, and detachment from other things — these three occur simultaneously... in the same way that pleasure, nourishment and relief from hunger come simultaneously and increasingly, with each bite." *Use:* the honest self-check: is detachment growing even where relish is not felt?

**SB 1.1.19; SB 10.1.4; CC Madhya 25.278**: Verified. "We never tire of hearing the transcendental pastimes of the Personality of Godhead... Those who have developed a taste... relish hearing of His pastimes at every moment" (SB 1.1.19; the purport: transcendental news "remains fresh even after repeated readings"). "Who will cease hearing such glorification of the Lord except a butcher or one who is killing his own self?" (SB 10.1.4; BBT translation completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples). "The devotee who simply eats ordinary grains but does not taste the transcendental pastimes of Lord Caitanya Mahāprabhu and Kṛṣṇa gradually becomes weak and falls down from the transcendental position" (CC Madhya 25.278); its purport: "All the devotees connected with the Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement must read all the books that have been translated... otherwise, after some time, they will simply eat, sleep and fall down from their position." *Use:* in the talk: dryness untreated is dangerous, and the treatment is named.

**CC Antya 20.19, 20.21**: Verified. Lord Caitanya: "I am so unfortunate that I have no attachment for chanting Your holy names" (20.19); and the mood in which one can always chant (20.21). *Use:* the dry resident's prayer, in the Lord's own words (TRD-12 for the whole prayer).

***Madhurya-kadambini* (Srila Visvanatha Cakravarti Thakura)**: approved by the head (28 Oct 2026). Edition: with the *Bhavanuvada* and *Piyusa-varsini-vrtti* of Bhaktivedanta Narayana Gosvami Maharaja, English first edition, Gaudiya Vedanta Publications, 2018 (`research/books/`). Supporting; the principle rests on NOI 7 and SB 1.2.17–18.
- **Fifth Shower, text 1:** "the bhajana of sādhakas entering the path of bhakti is somewhat slack, because they do not experience relish in practising the limbs of bhajana. Bhajana seems extremely difficult to them. But by persisting in their practice, their heart gradually becomes cleansed and they relish bhajana-rasa." The book's example: a student first finds a scripture "abstruse" and "does not find his study relishable"; "when the actual meaning of the scripture dawns on him, he finds great satisfaction and delight". It also records Jiva Gosvami: "taste for hari-kathā is the first and also the highest step."
- **Fifth Shower, text 2:** "For the tongue afflicted by jaundice, the mere taste of natural rock candy is unpleasant. Nonetheless... such rock candy is actually the cure for jaundice. By regularly taking rock candy, one can gradually perceive its sweetness... In the same way, by continuously engaging in bhakti characterized by śravaṇa, kīrtana, etc., the heart of the living entity polluted by nescience... becomes cleansed of these defects, and ruci for bhakti is awakened." The commentary quotes SB 11.14.26 (hearing cleanses the mind as collyrium cures the eye).
- **Fifth Shower, text 4:** the devotee in whom taste has awoken looks back at his dry years and says: "By the mercy of the lotus feet of śrī guru, a most sinful person like me attained something as great as devotion to the Supreme Lord... Not understanding its value, I have forgotten about it, just as one may forget about a precious jewel bound within the hem of one's cloth."
- **Application (preacher's):** Visvanatha Cakravarti and Rupa Gosvami (NOI 7) give the same picture: the medicine tastes bitter until it has worked. Teach it as the book gives it, edition named.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **What dryness is.** Not a sign that the sweet is absent, nor that the path is wrong for me, but the condition of a tongue that cannot yet taste (NOI 7). The sweetness is in the object: "sweet like sugar candy." The defect is in the instrument, and the defect has a cause (avidya, the heart not yet cleansed) and a cure (the same hearing and chanting, done carefully).
2. **Why the cure looks like the disease.** The remedy for not relishing the name is the name; for not relishing the Bhagavatam, the Bhagavatam. This is why dryness is a trap: the dry devotee is tempted to do less of the one thing that heals him. Rupa Gosvami's word is "carefully"; Visvanatha's is "continuously"; the Bhagavatam's is "regular" (SB 1.2.18).
3. **The cause of taste is outside me.** Krishna "cleanses desire for material enjoyment from the heart" (SB 1.2.17). The two inputs that let Him do it are named (1.2.18). So the first question in a dry month is not "what is wrong with my heart?" but "am I hearing the Bhagavatam from devotees, and am I serving them?" The heart is Krishna's department.
4. **The three assurances.** (a) Nothing is lost: "one percent done... bears permanent results" (BG 2.40 purport). (b) Even the one who fails is carried forward: Bharata became Jada Bharata (BG 6.43 purport). (c) The immature who falls "faces no danger of being unsuccessful" (SB 1.5.17). None of these is a licence; the SB 1.5.17 purport names "undesirable association" as the way down.
5. **Why the alternative is worse.** Sense happiness is "nectar at first but poison at the end" (BG 18.38); the devotee who stops tasting the pastimes "becomes weak and falls down" (CC Madhya 25.278). Dryness is uncomfortable; it is not the danger. The danger is what the dry devotee reaches for instead.
6. **Connections.** The six kinds of unsteady practice (DPS-15) describe how dryness shows in behaviour; the five obstacles (TRD-51) describe how it shows during japa and hearing; burnout (TRD-52) is dryness plus exhaustion in service. This page is the root of all three: taste not yet awake.
7. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *"Taste is a feeling I should have by now."* Taste is a stage (ruci), after nistha (DPS-16). Before it, bhajana "seems extremely difficult" (MK Fifth Shower, 1). That is the normal report from the road.
   - *"I feel nothing, so nothing is happening."* SB 11.2.42: devotion, experience and detachment grow together, "with each bite". Detachment can be measured when relish cannot: what no longer pulls me that pulled me five years ago?
   - *"Dryness means offence."* Sometimes. CC Adi 8.29 (TRD-08) names offence as one cause. But Rupa Gosvami in NOI 7 names avidya, which every conditioned soul has. Check the offences (DPS-06), then continue.
   - *"A change of service will fix it."* It may help the body and mind; it does not cleanse the heart. Only hearing and service to devotees do that (SB 1.2.18).
8. **Pitch for TRD.** Speak as one who has had dry years. Residents know when a speaker is describing them from outside. Use the Lord's own words for the complaint (CC Antya 20.19) so that no one in the room is ashamed of it.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet everyone. Say: "Today's subject is the dry month. Everyone here has had one. Nothing personal is said aloud; everything personal is written."
2. Read NOI 7 aloud, slowly, once. Do not explain it yet.
3. Ask H.2 question 1 for private writing (two minutes).
4. Ask H.2 question 2; take three answers; thank each by name.
5. Say: "Rupa Gosvami calls this a disease of the tongue, and he names the cure. We will spend the morning on both."

**H.2 Opening questions**
1. (Private) Think of your driest month in the ashram. What did you do more of, and what did you do less of, in that month?
2. When a junior devotee says "I feel nothing when I chant", what is the first thing you say to him?
3. Is a dry devotee who keeps the programme doing better or worse than an enthusiastic devotee who skips it? Why?

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Dryness is the jaundiced tongue, and the sweet is real.** NOI 7; MK Fifth Shower 2. *Example:* the rock candy that tastes bitter to the sick and is itself the medicine. *Transition:* so where does taste come from?
2. **Taste is given, and its two inputs are named.** SB 1.2.17–18: Krishna cleanses the heart of one who has "the urge to hear"; the inputs are the Bhagavatam heard regularly and service to the devotee. *Example:* the student and the scripture (MK Fifth Shower 1): the meaning dawns, and then study is delight. *Transition:* but what if I fail first?
3. **Nothing is lost.** BG 2.40 (one percent bears permanent results); BG 6.40–45 (Bharata, Jada Bharata); SB 1.5.17. With the one warning: undesirable association. *Transition:* why not simply rest from it for a while?
4. **The alternative is worse.** BG 18.37–38 (poison first, nectar after; or nectar first, poison after); CC Madhya 25.278 (he who stops tasting the pastimes becomes weak and falls); SB 10.1.4 (only a butcher stops hearing). *Transition:* is anything happening while I feel nothing?
5. **Detachment is the sign one can measure.** SB 11.2.42: devotion, experience and detachment grow together. *Example:* what no longer attracts me that attracted me when I joined? *Transition:* the end of dryness, described by one who came through it.
6. **The devotee who tasted looks back.** MK Fifth Shower 4: the jewel bound in the cloth, forgotten. SB 1.1.19: "we never tire of hearing". *Transition:* the Lord's own prayer (CC Antya 20.19, 20.21) and P.

## J. Stories and illustrations

**Arjuna's question about the one who fails** (BG 6.37–45). Arjuna asks what becomes of the man who begins with faith but, his mind deviating, "fails to attain perfection in mysticism" (6.37). Krishna answers that such a person "does not meet with destruction either in this world or in the spiritual world" (6.40); he is born again in a pious or an aristocratic family (6.41), or, if he fell after long practice, "in a family of transcendentalists" (6.42); he "revives the divine consciousness of his previous life, and he again tries" (6.43); and after many births "he attains the supreme goal" (6.45). *Lesson:* the sastra does not threaten the dry devotee; it assures him, and then asks him to continue.

**King Bharata and Jada Bharata** (BG 6.43 purport, pointing to SB Canto 5). Bharata, emperor of the world, "retired for spiritual perfection but failed to achieve success" (the SB 1.5.17 purport adds: "due to his intimate attachment to a stag"). In his next life "he took birth in the family of a good brāhmaṇa and was known as Jaḍa Bharata because he always remained secluded and did not talk to anyone", and "later on he was discovered as the greatest transcendentalist by King Rahūgaṇa." *Lesson:* "transcendental endeavors, or the practice of yoga, never go in vain" (6.43 purport). Tell it as the purport tells it; the full narration is SB 5.7–13 and belongs to the Bhakti-vaibhava course.

**The sparrow and the ocean** (BG 6.24 purport). A sparrow's eggs were carried off by the ocean; she decided to dry the ocean with her beak; everyone laughed; Garuda, hearing of it, came to help, and the ocean returned the eggs. Prabhupada's point: "not becoming discouraged if there is any delay in the attainment of success. Success is sure for the rigid practitioner." *Lesson:* determination in a small creature draws the help of the great.

**Lord Caitanya's own complaint** (CC Antya 20.15–19). With "lamentation and humility", the Lord recites the second verse of the Siksastaka and explains: "You have invested Your full potencies in each individual holy name, but I am so unfortunate that I have no attachment for chanting Your holy names" (20.19). *Lesson:* the dry devotee's sentence is in the Lord's own mouth.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"I have kept the programme for ten years and feel nothing. Is the path simply not working for me?"**
   - NOI 7 says the sweet is real and the tongue is sick; the cure is the same practice done "carefully" every day. Check the two inputs of SB 1.2.18 first: regular Bhagavatam hearing from devotees, and service to devotees. Then check the offences (DPS-06). Then continue. BG 2.40: nothing done so far is lost.
2. **"Would a break from sadhana refresh me?"**
   - A break from a particular service, arranged with your authority, may rest the body. A break from hearing and chanting does the opposite: CC Madhya 25.278 says the devotee who stops tasting the pastimes "gradually becomes weak and falls down". Rest the service if needed; never the medicine.
3. **"Others describe tears and bliss. Am I behind?"**
   - The stages are a direction, not a race (DPS-15). Before ruci, bhajana "seems extremely difficult" (MK Fifth Shower 1); that is the normal report. Measure what can be measured: detachment (SB 11.2.42). Never produce symptoms (NOD 16, in TRD-13).
4. **"Is my dryness an offence?"**
   - It may include one; CC Adi 8.29 (TRD-08) names offence as a cause of love not awakening. But Rupa Gosvami names avidya (NOI 7), which we all carry. Review the ten with a senior devotee (DPS-06); do not sit in guilt; chant.
5. **"How is this different from burnout?"**
   - Burnout (TRD-52) is lost enthusiasm with exhaustion in service, often after many years of overwork; it needs rest, honest talk with authorities and reorganised service as well as sadhana. Dryness is the absence of relish while the programme is kept. They overlap; the remedy for the sadhana part is the same.
6. **"What if I cannot sleep, cannot eat, and feel low every day for weeks?"**
   - That is more than dryness of taste. Tell your authority or a senior devotee the same week, and see a doctor; in India the free helpline Tele-MANAS (14416) is available at any hour. Keep your rounds and hearing in the meantime; sastra and medical care are not rivals. Srila Prabhupada's measure for sleep (BG 6.16 purport: not more than six hours) is the standard we teach; a body that cannot sleep at all is a different matter and is treated as illness.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The dry cook.** Madhava dasa has cooked for the Deities for six years. He never misses mangala-arati or his rounds, but he leaves the morning class to start the kitchen and hears Bhagavatam only on festival days. He says the kitchen is his sadhana.
- **Dilemma:** Which input of SB 1.2.18 has quietly dropped out? Is "the kitchen is my sadhana" true, half true or false?
- **Principles:** SB 1.2.17–18 (two inputs); NOI 7 (care); MK Fifth Shower 1 (bhajana seems hard before relish).
- **Questions:** What has Madhava kept? What has he lost? What arrangement with the kitchen head would restore hearing without harming the Deities' service?
- **Takeaway:** service is real sadhana, and hearing is its food. Twenty minutes of recorded Bhagavatam class while cutting vegetables, or class on three fixed mornings, agreed with the kitchen head.

**Case 2: The resident who wants a sabbatical.** Kesava dasa, nine years in the ashram and a good preacher, asks for three months away from the temple "to find his inspiration again". He plans to stay with old college friends and read.
- **Principles:** SB 1.5.17 purport (falling "by undesirable association"); CC Madhya 25.278 (stopping the pastimes leads to falling); BG 6.40–45 (even if he fails, he is not lost); NOI 3 (patience; following the acaryas).
- **Questions:** What does he actually need rest from, the service or the hearing? What would the SB 1.5.17 purport say about the company he has chosen? What alternative would give rest without removing the medicine?
- **Takeaway:** the request is honest and the plan is dangerous. A changed service, a week in the dhama with devotees, or study under a senior devotee gives rest without undesirable association.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Everyone who lives for Krishna meets the dry month: the programme is kept, the service is done, and the heart feels nothing. Rupa Gosvami names this exactly. Krishna's name, qualities and pastimes "are all transcendentally sweet like sugar candy", but "the tongue of one afflicted by the jaundice of avidyā [ignorance] cannot taste anything sweet" (NOI 7). The sweet is real; the tongue is sick. And the cure is the sweet itself: "simply by carefully chanting these sweet names every day, a natural relish awakens within his tongue, and his disease is gradually destroyed at the root." Visvanatha Cakravarti says the same in *Madhurya-kadambini*: rock candy tastes bitter to a jaundiced tongue, yet rock candy is the medicine, and "by regularly taking rock candy, one can gradually perceive its sweetness" (Fifth Shower, text 2).

Where does taste come from? Not from us. Krishna "cleanses desire for material enjoyment from the heart of the devotee who has developed the urge to hear His messages" (SB 1.2.17). Our part is two things: "regular attendance in classes on the Bhāgavatam" and "service to the pure devotee" (SB 1.2.18). So in a dry month the first question is not "what is wrong with my heart?" but "am I hearing the Bhagavatam from devotees, and am I serving them?"

Krishna also answers the fear that we are failing. "In this endeavor there is no loss or diminution" (BG 2.40); the purport says one percent done bears permanent results. Even the yogi who fails is born again to continue and "again tries" (BG 6.40–45): King Bharata failed and became Jada Bharata, the great saint. One who falls while immature "faces no danger of being unsuccessful" (SB 1.5.17), though the purport warns of falling "by undesirable association". The alternative to dryness is worse: sense pleasure is "nectar at first but poison at the end" (BG 18.38), and the devotee who stops tasting Krishna's pastimes "gradually becomes weak and falls down" (CC Madhya 25.278). While we feel nothing, something can still be measured: devotion, experience of the Lord and detachment grow together (SB 11.2.42). Ask what no longer attracts you that once did. And pray in the Lord's own words: "I am so unfortunate that I have no attachment for chanting Your holy names" (CC Antya 20.19).

## S2. Key ideas

- Dryness is the sick tongue, not the missing sweet (NOI 7; *Madhurya-kadambini*, Fifth Shower 2).
- The cure is the same practice done carefully and continuously; never less of it.
- Taste is given by Krishna; our two inputs are Bhagavatam hearing and service to devotees (SB 1.2.17–18).
- Nothing is lost (BG 2.40); the one who fails is carried forward (BG 6.40–45; SB 1.5.17), but bad association is the way down.
- The alternative is worse: poison at the end (BG 18.38); weakness and falling (CC Madhya 25.278).
- Detachment is the sign one can measure before relish comes (SB 11.2.42).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **NOI 7:** "simply by carefully chanting these sweet names every day, a natural relish awakens within his tongue, and his disease is gradually destroyed at the root." *For me:* no taste means take the medicine carefully, not stop it.
- **SB 1.2.18:** "By regular attendance in classes on the Bhāgavatam and by rendering of service to the pure devotee, all that is troublesome to the heart is almost completely destroyed." *For me:* check my hearing and my service first.
- **BG 2.40:** "In this endeavor there is no loss or diminution, and a little advancement on this path can protect one from the most dangerous type of fear." *For me:* my dry years are not wasted.
- **BG 18.37:** "That which in the beginning may be just like poison but at the end is just like nectar... is said to be happiness in the mode of goodness." *For me:* bitterness at the start is expected.

## S4. Words to know

- **Avidya:** ignorance; the "jaundice" that stops the tongue tasting Krishna's sweetness (NOI 7).
- **Ruci:** taste; the stage of bhakti at which hearing and chanting become relishable (DPS-16).
- **Sravanam:** hearing about Krishna; the first of the nine processes and "the first and also the highest step" to taste (Jiva Gosvami, quoted in *Madhurya-kadambini*).
- **Bhagavata:** both the book Srimad-Bhagavatam and the devotee who lives by it (SB 1.2.18 purport).

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **I have practised for years and feel nothing. Is the path not for me?** NOI 7 says the sweet is real and the tongue is sick; the cure is the same practice done carefully. Check the two inputs of SB 1.2.18 (hearing from devotees, serving devotees), check the ten offences with a senior devotee, and continue. Nothing you have done is lost (BG 2.40).
2. **Would a break from sadhana help?** A break from a particular service, arranged with your authority, may rest you. A break from hearing and chanting makes you "weak" and leads to falling (CC Madhya 25.278). Rest the service, never the medicine.
3. **Others feel bliss. Am I behind?** Before taste awakens, bhajana "seems extremely difficult" (*Madhurya-kadambini*, Fifth Shower 1). That is normal. Measure detachment (SB 11.2.42), not feelings; never imitate symptoms.
4. **Is dryness an offence?** It may include one; review the ten offences (DPS-06). But Rupa Gosvami names ignorance (NOI 7), which we all carry. Do not sit in guilt; chant.
5. **What if I cannot sleep or eat and feel low every day for weeks?** That is more than dryness. Tell a senior devotee the same week and see a doctor (in India, Tele-MANAS 14416, free, any hour). Keep your rounds and hearing meanwhile.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. In NOI 7, what is the holy name compared to, and what is the devotee's tongue compared to?
2. True or false: the cure for not relishing the holy name is to chant less until the taste returns.
3. Name the two inputs SB 1.2.18 gives.
4. What happened to King Bharata after he failed (BG 6.43 purport)?
5. According to SB 11.2.42, which three things grow together?
6. What does BG 18.38 say about happiness from the senses?

**Answers:** 1. Sugar candy; a tongue with jaundice (NOI 7). 2. False: "carefully chanting these sweet names every day" awakens the relish (NOI 7). 3. Regular hearing of the Bhagavatam in class, and service to the pure devotee (SB 1.2.18).
4. He was born as Jada Bharata in a brahmana family and was later recognised as the greatest transcendentalist; yoga practice never goes in vain. 5. Devotion, direct experience of the Lord, and detachment (SB 11.2.42). 6. It is "like nectar at first but poison at the end" (BG 18.38).
