# Lord Caitanya and Milk-drinking Brahmacari

> **BSS · 2 hours · review: drafted.** Builds on bss-01 (renunciation flows from attachment to Krishna) and bss-03 (the vow). This page: pride in austerity, and what the Lord wants instead.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Brahmacaris take up many austerities (food, sleep, rules). The risk is a quiet pride: "I do more than others." This
pastime from Lord Caitanya's own Nabadwip kirtans is the sharpest scriptural picture of that risk, and of the Lord's mercy.

**Not done here:** the vow and association (bss-03); renunciation as fruit of attachment (bss-01); food rules in detail (a later BSS page); any claim that milk or diet is wrong.

## C. Central message

Austerity is welcome only when it serves bhakti. The Lord rebuked a milk-and-fruit brahmacari who was proud of his practice,
told him to surrender and render devotional service, and then blessed him. We accept the practices and keep ourselves humble and dependent on the Lord's mercy.

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants will be able to:
1. Narrate the pastime in order, and give the Lord's two statements (rebuke and blessing).
2. Say, with BG 17.5-6 and SB 1.2.8, why austerity and rules do not by themselves please the Lord.
3. Say what balanced practice is (BG 6.16-17).
4. Spot pride in their own practice and name one remedy.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Source of the pastime.** *Caitanya-bhagavata*, Madhya-lila, the chapter of the Lord's nocturnal kirtans at Srivasa's house (in the English translation in `research/books/Caitanya Bhagavata.txt` it is Madhya chapter 13; chapter numbers differ between editions: *Requires source review* for the exact citation). *Caitanya-bhagavata* is used here for narrative only (not for principles); the story index (data/stories.json) lists it as not yet fully indexed.

**BG 17.5-6** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/17/5-6/
> "Those who undergo severe austerities and penances not recommended in the scriptures, performing them out of pride and egoism"
- Context: Krishna describes austerities in the mode of passion and ignorance; the verses say that such practice is not recommended.
- Teaching: Austerity done from pride is a demoniac mark; the verse warns about pride, not about all austerity.
- Application (preacher's): The brahmacari's practices are scriptural; the thing to examine is the pride.
- Use: in the talk

**BG 6.16-17** — Verified
> "There is no possibility of one’s becoming a yogī, O Arjuna, if one eats too much or eats too little, sleeps too much or does not sleep enough."
> "He who is regulated in his habits of eating, sleeping, recreation and work can mitigate all material pains by practicing the yoga system."
- Teaching: Krishna's own standard is balance.
- Use: in the talk

**SB 1.2.8** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/8/
> "The occupational activities a man performs according to his own position are only so much useless labor if they do not provoke attraction for the message of the Personality of Godhead."
- Teaching: Even perfect rules are fruitless unless they lead to attraction to hearing about the Lord.
- Application (preacher's): The test of any practice is whether it increases attraction to hearing and chanting.
- Use: in the talk

**SB 7.9.10** — Verified
> "he is certainly lower than a devotee who is a dog-eater but who has dedicated everything"
- Context: Prahlada's prayer to Lord Nrsimha: a brahmana with all qualities but averse to the Lord is lower than a devotee who has dedicated everything to Him. The Lord's words in the pastime echo this.
- Use: background for the preacher

**BG 18.66** — Verified
> "Abandon all varieties of religion and just surrender unto Me."
- Teaching: The Lord's instruction to the brahmacari (render devotional service to Visnu-Krishna) is the same as Krishna's final instruction.
- Use: in the talk (closing)

## F. Philosophical explanation

**Principle.** Bhakti is by the Lord's mercy and the devotee's surrender. Preparation (austerity, rules, knowledge) is useful as it helps hearing and chanting; it becomes an obstacle when it makes the practitioner proud and independent.

**Why it matters.** A brahmacari is watched, praised and measured by his practices. Pride in them blocks the very humility bhakti needs.

**Connections.** The pride of learning is the same species as the pride of austerity (the IYF Encyclopedia note in O links this pastime to both).

**Consequences.** (1) Measure progress in attraction to hearing and chanting. (2) Take advice about diet and sleep (BG 6.16-17). (3) Say quietly, "By the Lord's mercy."

**Misunderstandings.**
1. "Milk is bad." Srila Prabhupada recommended milk for devotees (BIKC, quoting conversation 30/04/73 and lecture 15/01/74), and also warned that excess milk is rajasika. The Lord's rebuke is on pride.
2. "Austerity is useless." BG 17.14-16 praises austerity of body, speech and mind (not quoted here); the Lord objects to pride.
3. "The brahmacari was punished." The story ends with the Lord's feet on his head and a blessing.

**Pitch.** Humble, light, no mockery of the brahmacari; he is praised by the author for how he accepted the rebuke.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start.** Say: "Raise your hand if you have given up something for Krishna this year. Keep it up if someone knows. Now put it down if you want them to know." (Light laughter.) "Today the Lord Himself addresses this feeling. And He does it with love." Then ask the H.2 questions.

**H.2 Opening questions.**
1. What practices make a devotee admired in your community?
2. How do you feel when someone does more than you?
3. What helps you keep going when nobody sees?

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Austerity is a servant of bhakti** (SB 1.2.8; BG 17.5-6). Transition: "How does the story show it?"
2. **The Lord sees the hidden heart** (J.2). The Lord knew the brahmacari was hidden in the house.
3. **Surrender is the means to the Lord** (J.3, BG 18.66). The Lord names Gajendra, Hanuman and the gopis as those who surrendered (narrative).
4. **The Lord rebukes with love** (J.4). The brahmacari accepts and is blessed.
5. **Balance, not extreme** (BG 6.16-17).

## J. Stories and illustrations

*Source: Caitanya-bhagavata narrative (see E; narrative only). Chapter number to be confirmed.*

**J.1 The closed kirtans.** The Lord held nightly kirtans in Srivasa's house with only devotees admitted (narrative).
**J.2 The hidden guest.** A peaceful, honest brahmacari, who lived on milk and fruits, wanted to see the kirtan. Srivasa let him in on condition that he stay hidden. The Lord, who knew, said He felt no usual ecstasy and asked if someone was hiding.
**J.3 The rebuke.** Told of the brahmacari, the Lord was angry: no one reaches Him just by drinking milk. He mentioned those who attained Him by surrender (Gajendra, Hanuman, the gopis) and said that renunciants without surrender are not favoured.
**J.4 The mercy.** The brahmacari thought, "I have been punished, but I saw wonderful dancing." The Lord called him back, placed His feet on his head and told him to give up reliance on austerity and to render loving devotional service to Visnu-Krishna. The brahmacari wept with joy; the dance resumed.
**Devotional lesson.** The Lord rebukes the pride and receives the devotee who accepts it. The brahmacari's humility in accepting is what Vaisnava authors praise.
**Illustration (preacher's).** A student who studies every night but never asks teachers for help may do well on tests but not learn to ask. The brahmacari's practice without asking for the Lord's mercy was similar. End: ask the Lord and the devotees for help in hearing and chanting.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"Is the Lord against austerity?"** No: BG 6.16-17 and SB 1.2.8 teach balance and purpose. He is against austerity as a substitute for surrender.
2. **"The brahmacari was not proud outwardly. Is the story fair?"** The Lord sees the heart; see the IYF note (O) on pride in austerity. *Requires source review* for the exact words in other editions.
3. **"Should I drink milk?"** Srila Prabhupada recommended milk (BIKC); drink as advised by your temple and health needs.
4. **"How can I know I am proud?"** If you compare, or if your practice makes you look down on others, ask a senior to tell you; chant for humility.
5. **"Is this story from Prabhupada's books?"** Not that I have checked: it is from *Caitanya-bhagavata*, quoted for narrative only. *Requires source review* for a Prabhupada citation.

## L. Case studies

**Invented case for discussion.** Vimal has kept a strict diet for a year and tells the new boys what they eat is "too rajasika". He is strong in chanting but a little cold in the group. A senior asks him to cook for a guest event with a normal menu.
- Principles: BG 6.16-17; SB 1.2.8.
- Questions: What is the Lord's likely response? What can Vimal do?
- Analysis: He serves cheerfully, asks for the senior's mercy, and checks his motive; he continues his diet if healthy.
- Takeaway: service and humility make the practice bhakti.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check (answers).**
1. What did the brahmacari live on? *Milk and fruits.*
2. Why was he hidden? *The Lord had shut the doors; only devotees were admitted.*
3. What did the Lord say you cannot attain by drinking milk? *Him: only by surrender.*
4. What did He tell the brahmacari to do? *Render loving devotional service.*
5. What does BG 6.16 say about eating and sleeping? *Not too much, not too little.*
6. How did the story end? *With the Lord's blessing and renewed dance.*

**M.2 Activities.**
- **Practice and purpose (10 min, pairs).** List three practices; for each write "How does it increase my attraction to hearing the Lord's message?" (SB 1.2.8).
- **Drama: the hidden brahmacari (15 min, groups of 5).** Roles: Srivasa, the brahmacari, the Lord, two devotees. Use only the sequence in J (invented words allowed; mark them). End with the blessing.

**M.3 Reflection.** "In which of my practices am I proudest? What would I say to the Lord about it?"

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Lord Caitanya held night kirtans in Srivasa's house with devotees only. A peaceful brahmacari who lived on milk and fruits wanted to see them. Srivasa let him in, hidden. The Lord, who knew, said His ecstasy was missing and asked if someone was hiding. Told of the brahmacari, the Lord rebuked him: no one reaches Him just by drinking milk; those who surrender attain Him. The brahmacari accepted the rebuke without anger. The Lord then called him back, placed His feet on his head, told him to render loving devotional service, and the dancing continued.

Austerity is useful when it helps us to hear and chant (SB 1.2.8); it becomes a problem when it is done from pride (BG 17.5-6). Krishna asks for balance (BG 6.16-17) and for surrender (BG 18.66).

## S2. Key ideas
- Austerity serves bhakti; it does not replace surrender.
- Pride in austerity blocks the Lord's mercy.
- Balance in eating and sleeping (BG 6.16-17).
- The Lord rebukes and blesses together.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **SB 1.2.8** — duties are useless labour if they do not provoke attraction to the Lord's message. For me: check whether my practice makes me love hearing.
- **BG 6.17** — regulate eating, sleeping, recreation and work. For me: keep a steady balance.
- **BG 18.66** — surrender unto Me. For me: the goal of every practice.

## S4. Words to know
- *Austerity (tapasya)*: voluntary hardship for a spiritual aim.
- *Brahmacari*: celibate student.
- *Kirtan*: congregational singing.
- *Surrender*: giving oneself to the Lord's will.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. **Is milk wrong?** No: Srila Prabhupada recommended it; excess is not good.
2. **Is austerity wrong?** No; pride in it is the problem.
3. **Was the brahmacari rejected?** No; he was blessed.
4. **How do I avoid pride?** Ask for the Lord's mercy and thank others who help.

## S6. Check your understanding
1. Where were the kirtans held?
2. What did the brahmacari live on?
3. What did the Lord say about reaching Him?
4. What did the Lord tell the brahmacari to do?
5. What does SB 1.2.8 say about duties?

**Answers:** 1. Srivasa's house. 2. Milk and fruits. 3. Not by drinking milk but by surrender. 4. Render loving devotional service. 5. They are useless labour if they do not provoke attraction for the Lord's message.

Pick one practice and ask: "How does it increase my love for hearing about Krishna?" Private reflection: "Am I proud of anything I do?"
