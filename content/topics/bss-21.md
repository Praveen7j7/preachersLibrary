## B. Why this topic belongs at this level
Brahmacarya is not only a rule of conduct but an understanding. BIKC (ch. 5) says: "Brahmacarya is meant for developing clear, unsentimental, uncompromising understanding of these points as the basis for making rapid, determined progress in spiritual life." And: "All the advice given in this book is based on this understanding: sex cannot make us happy; only Kṛṣṇa can make us happy." This page gives that understanding in the words of the Gita, the Bhagavatam and BIKC. Page bss-22 builds on it with the consequences, bss-23 with the cultural environment, bss-24 with the other senses.

## C. Central message
Krsna calls lust the "all-devouring sinful enemy of this world" (BG 3.37), "never satisfied", burning "like fire" (BG 3.39); it is our love for Krsna transformed by contact with the mode of passion (BG 3.37 purport). BIKC (ch. 5): "Sex desire is the main symptom of insanity exhibited by the conditioned soul"; "sex cannot make us happy; only Kṛṣṇa can make us happy." The lasting remedy is a higher taste in Krsna consciousness (BG 2.59), not willpower alone.

## D. Learning outcomes
1. State in one sentence what the Gita says lust is and where it sits (BG 3.37, 3.40).
2. Explain, with the verse and one picture, why feeding desire does not end it (SB 9.19.14).
3. Distinguish desire for one's own sense pleasure from desire to please Krsna (CC Adi 4.165).
4. Name two things that make desire stronger and two that make it quieter, from their own routine.
5. Say whom they would talk to if the pressure feels bigger than their own strength.

## E. Scriptural foundation
Translations are quoted from vedabase.io (BBT, Srila Prabhupada), saved in `research/verification/bss-21.json`. Purport points below were written after reading each full purport on vedabase.io; the short extracts quoted are saved under `purportExtracts` in the same file.

**BG 3.37** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/37/
> "It is lust only, Arjuna, which is born of contact with the material mode of passion and later transformed into wrath, and which is the all-devouring sinful enemy of this world."
- Context: Arjuna asks what pushes a person to act wrongly even against his wish; Krsna's first answer is lust.
- Teaching: lust is born of contact with the mode of passion and turns into anger; Krsna calls it all-devouring.
- **Purport point:** "When a living entity comes in contact with the material creation, his eternal love for Kṛṣṇa is transformed into lust, in association with the mode of passion", "as milk in contact with sour tamarind is transformed into yogurt." So lust is love of Krsna misdirected; "if … lust is transformed into love for the Supreme, or transformed into Kṛṣṇa consciousness – or, in other words, desiring everything for Kṛṣṇa – then both lust and wrath can be spiritualized", and "when they are employed in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, become our friends instead of our enemies." Hanuman burning Ravana's city is the purport's example (wrath used in the Lord's service made him "the greatest devotee").
- Use: in the talk. The purport says this of every living entity who "comes in contact with the material creation".

**BG 3.39** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/39/
> "Thus the wise living entity's pure consciousness becomes covered by his eternal enemy in the form of lust, which is never satisfied and which burns like fire."
- Teaching: lust covers pure consciousness; it is never satisfied; it burns like fire.
- **Purport point:** "lust cannot be satisfied by any amount of sense enjoyment, just as fire is never extinguished by a constant supply of fuel"; "In the material world, the center of all activities is sex, and thus this material world is called maithunya-āgāra, or the shackles of sex life. In the ordinary prison house, criminals are kept within bars; similarly, the criminals who are disobedient to the laws of the Lord are shackled by sex life." And: "Advancement of material civilization on the basis of sense gratification means increasing the duration of the material existence of a living entity."
- Use: in the talk, paired with SB 9.19.14.

**BG 3.40** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/40/
> "The senses, the mind and the intelligence are the sitting places of this lust."
- Teaching: three seats: senses, mind, intelligence.
- **Purport point:** the Lord names the places "so that one who wants to conquer the enemy may know where he can be found." "when we hear about sense objects the mind generally becomes a reservoir of all ideas of sense gratification"; and "Lusty intelligence influences the spirit soul to acquire the false ego and identify itself with matter." The enemy is located so that it can be met; what is met is the false identification of the self with the body.
- Application (preacher's): this is why guarding only the eyes or only the body is not enough (see F.3).
- Use: in the talk.

**BG 7.11** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/7/11/
> "I am sex life which is not contrary to religious principles."
- Teaching: Krsna acknowledges regulated, dharmic family life; the verse keeps the discussion balanced.
- **Purport point:** "sex life, according to religious principles (dharma), should be for the propagation of children, not otherwise. The responsibility of parents is then to make their offspring Kṛṣṇa conscious." Even the permitted form is meant to serve Krsna consciousness; the purport does not present it as the source of happiness.
- Use: in the talk, as the purport reads it: sex life within dharma is for children who are then made Krsna conscious.

**SB 5.5.4** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/5/4/
> "When a person considers sense gratification the aim of life, he certainly becomes mad after materialistic living…"
- Context: Rsabhadeva's instruction to His sons, toward devotional service (full story: bss-07).
- Teaching: the verse is about a person who makes sense gratification the aim of life.
- **Purport point:** "Begging, borrowing and stealing to live for sense gratification is condemned in this verse because such consciousness leads one to a dark, hellish condition. The four sinful activities are illicit sex, meat-eating, intoxication and gambling." It also says "The living entity is not really connected with this material world, but due to his tendency to enjoy the material senses he is put into the material condition", and ends: "One should perfect his life by associating with devotees. He should not become further implicated in the material body."
- **BIKC (ch. 5) opens with this verse:** "The conditioned soul in the material world is in a state of madness. Nūnaṁ pramattaḥ kurute vikarma. (SB 5.5.4)"; "Sex desire is the main symptom of insanity exhibited by the conditioned soul."
- Use: in the talk.

**SB 5.5.8** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/5/8/
> "The attraction between male and female is the basic principle of material existence."
- Teaching: this attraction ties hearts together and extends to body, home, property, children, relatives, wealth: "I and mine".
- **Purport point:** this knot is "hṛdaya-granthi, 'the hard knot in the heart.'" "The attachment to the family is the strongest illusion", and "most people are bound by the knots of the marital relationship, and consequently they forget their relationship with Kṛṣṇa." The purport also says that in exalted cases such as Lord Brahma "the wife actually helps further spiritual life and liberation", so the binding is in the forgetfulness, not in the person.
- Application (preacher's): sex desire is the root of a large tree of attachments; this is why BIKC calls it the main binding force.
- Use: in the talk.

**SB 7.9.45** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/9/45/
> "Sex life is compared to the rubbing of two hands to relieve an itch."
- Context: Prahlada's prayer to Lord Nrsimha.
- Teaching: the relief is temporary, and the persons who think it the greatest happiness are not satisfied by repeating it. Those who are "dhīra, however, who are sober and who tolerate this itching, are not subjected to the sufferings of fools and rascals."
- **Purport point:** "Materialists think that sexual indulgence is the greatest happiness in this material world, and therefore they make elaborate plans to satisfy their senses, especially the genitals." Prahlada "advises one not to be misled by this civilization of sense gratification, and especially not by sex life. Rather, one should be sober, avoid sense gratification and be Kṛṣṇa conscious", and "if one voluntarily submits to the lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa, he can be saved very easily" (BG 7.14). The purport then quotes Yamunacarya in full: "Since I have been engaged in the transcendental loving service of Kṛṣṇa, realizing ever-new pleasure in Him, whenever I think of sex pleasure, I spit at the thought, and my lips curl with distaste." And: "Yāmunācārya had formerly been a great king who enjoyed sexual happiness in various ways, but since he later engaged himself in the service of the Lord, he enjoyed spiritual bliss and hated to think of sex life. If sexual thoughts came to him, he would spit with disgust."
- Use: in the talk, with the whole Yamunacarya verse as the purport gives it.

**SB 9.19.13** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/9/19/13/
> "A person who is lusty cannot satisfy his mind even if he has enough of everything in this world…"
- Context: Yayati's own conclusion after a thousand years of enjoyment (story: bss-11, do not retell).
- **Purport point:** "To have a satisfied mind, one must give up his heart disease of lusty desires. This can be done only when one is Kṛṣṇa conscious"; otherwise "this disease of lusty desires will continue, and one cannot have peace in his mind." The purport calls the condition a heart disease, which has a cure: devotional service.
- Use: in the talk.

**SB 9.19.14** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/9/19/14/
> "As supplying butter to a fire does not diminish the fire but instead increases it more and more, the endeavor to stop lusty desires by continual enjoyment can never be successful."
- Teaching: the verse then adds that one must voluntarily cease from material desires.
- **Purport point:** the purport only restates the example: "One cannot stop a blazing fire by trying to extinguish it with butter." The way to cease is given in the purport of SB 9.19.13 above and in BG 2.59.
- Use: in the talk, the key verse of this page.

## F. Philosophical explanation
**F.1 What it is.** The Gita names the force (BG 3.37), says where it is born (contact with passion), what it does (covers knowledge, BG 3.39) and where it lives (senses, mind, intelligence, BG 3.40). BIKC (ch. 5) says: "Forgetting his eternal, blissful relationship with Kṛṣṇa, he is suffering life after life, being kicked and spat upon by māyā. But he is smiling, taking it as enjoyment." Of youths: "their consciousness is always saturated with thoughts of touching the bodies of the opposite sex, seeing their naked forms, and engaging in sexual intercourse. They say 'love,' but they have only lust." Of society: "In human society sex desire is expanded into many forms, under the headings of profit, adoration, and distinction"; "society, friendship, love, house, cars, clothes, position, power, prestige, money, and so on—are simply for facilitating and expanding this animal instinct called sex desire."

**F.2 Why it is not satisfied by indulgence.** SB 9.19.14: fuel on a fire. BG 3.39 purport: "lust cannot be satisfied by any amount of sense enjoyment". BIKC (ch. 5): "as surveys show, many karmīs are actually bored at the time of sex. Then why don't they give it up and take to Kṛṣṇa consciousness, the real nectar of life? Because they know nothing better".

**F.3 Why the remedy must reach the intelligence and the heart.** BG 3.40 gives three seats. A rule governs the body; study and discussion inform the intelligence; the mind needs engagement and the heart needs a higher object. BG 2.55 (R.1) says the mind, purified, finds satisfaction in the self, and its purport adds: "Artificially, such sense desires cannot be stopped. But if one is engaged in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, then, automatically, sense desires subside without extraneous efforts." BG 2.59's purport likewise: restrictions are for neophytes "only good until one actually has a taste for Kṛṣṇa consciousness."

**F.4 Lust and love, one longing.** CC Adi 4.165 (R.1): "The desire to gratify one's own senses is kāma [lust], but the desire to please the senses of Lord Kṛṣṇa is prema [love]." BIKC ch. 7 says our sexual desire is a "perverted reflection" of the heart's longing to love Krsna. This is why the instruction is positive: not to kill a longing but to give it its true object. The BG 3.37 purport says the same: lust is love of Krsna "transformed" by contact with passion, and desiring everything for Krsna spiritualizes it. The CC Adi 4.165 purport adds that acts done "for the purpose of enjoying its fruits or results" are sense gratification, "sometimes in gross and sometimes in subtle forms" (even when done under the cover of religion or public welfare), while service felt as "an eternal servitor of Kṛṣṇa" is pure love.

**F.5 Misunderstandings to correct.**
- "All sex is forbidden." BG 7.11: Krsna is "sex life which is not contrary to religious principles"; its purport: "for the propagation of children, not otherwise." BIKC (ch. 46, quoted in bss-42): "If they want sex life, that is not forbidden—in the gṛhastha-āśrama."
- "The body is beautiful." BIKC (ch. 5): "there is nothing intrinsically beautiful about any material body. Even the bodies of beauty queens are simply bags of skin filled with foul-smelling, revolting substances." "Sexual happiness is the happiness of uniting the two urine-producing parts." "As a pig is attracted to a sow, or a male cockroach to a female, so is the sexual desire of a man for a woman. It is not even slightly more elevated than that of the pigs." SB 7.9.45 purport says the same in Yamunacarya's words (E).
- "Sex is special." BIKC (ch. 5): "There is really nothing special about sex. It is not wonderful, it is not noble, it is not romantic—it is just a bodily function, a response to raw gut feeling." "The president of America is doing it, the bums on the street are doing it, the cats and dogs are doing it. Only a few great souls are attempting to conquer over it."
- "A strong desire means I cannot succeed." BIKC (ch. 5): "Even a neophyte devotee can be confident of gradually conquering over lust, because he has got the right method; that is, to revive his natural, real love—his love for Kṛṣṇa." BG 2.60 (bss-24): the senses carry away even a discriminating man.

**F.6 Pitch for BSS.** These are adults who chose this life. Speak to them as such: frank, calm, practical, encouraging.

## I. Core teaching points
1. **Lust: the eternal enemy, never satisfied.** BG 3.37, 3.39, read with their purports (E). BIKC (ch. 5): "Sex desire is the main symptom of insanity exhibited by the conditioned soul." Transition: "If it is an enemy, how does it behave?"
2. **It does not shrink when fed.** SB 9.19.13–14, 7.9.45 (with Yamunacarya's verse from the purport). BIKC (ch. 5): "Even the bodies of beauty queens are simply bags of skin filled with foul-smelling, revolting substances." Illustration (invented, label it): a thirsty person drinking salt water. It ends in the Krsna conscious point: only the water of the holy name and service quenches the soul's thirst; SB 9.19.14 continues that one must voluntarily cease from material desires, and BG 2.59 gives the higher taste. Transition: "Where does it go from there?"
3. **It is the root of a tree of attachments.** SB 5.5.4, 5.5.8: body, home, property, children, wealth, "I and mine". Application (preacher's): a young man's ambition for money, status and looks often serves the same drive; BIKC makes this point.
4. **Three seats.** BG 3.40: protect senses, mind and intelligence together.
5. **Same energy, new direction.** CC Adi 4.165: the longing is real; it needs a worthy object. BIKC: Krsna consciousness gives a method that makes gradual victory possible even for a beginner.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections
- *Is the desire itself a sin?* The Gita calls lust the "all-devouring sinful enemy of this world" (BG 3.37) and the sinful act is illicit sex (SB 5.5.4 purport: "The four sinful activities are illicit sex, meat-eating, intoxication and gambling"). BG 7.11 gives sex life within religious principles. BIKC (ch. 5): "Even a neophyte devotee can be confident of gradually conquering over lust, because he has got the right method."
- *Why does BIKC speak so bluntly about the body?* Give BIKC's own words and its own reason (ch. 5): "by the mighty power of illusion, an arrangement of skin and flesh overrides all logic and sanity and brings even a person of intelligence down to the level of a dog"; "Brahmacarya is meant for developing clear, unsentimental, uncompromising understanding of these points". Srila Prabhupada's purport to SB 7.9.45 speaks the same way (Yamunacarya: "I spit at the thought").
- *Does marriage mean illusion?* No: BG 7.11.
- *Can a person who has already been involved before joining take this path?* Yes; past patterns can be changed; see bss-40 (falldown) for recovery.
- *What if I feel this strongly all the time?* The Gita itself says the senses are strong enough to carry away even a discriminating man (BG 2.60), so feeling this is not a failure. Talk to a trusted senior; if it feels compulsive or distressing, see a qualified counsellor as well: this is care, not a failure of faith.

## L. Case studies
**L.1 Invented case for discussion.** *Situation:* Arun, 22, has joined the ashram four months ago. He keeps his rules, but evenings are hard; his phone has long videos that start innocently. *Dilemma:* he feels his sadhana is "fake" because the thoughts return. *Principles:* BG 3.40 (three seats), SB 9.19.14. *Questions:* (a) What is working in his life already? (b) Which seat is not being protected? (c) What one change would you suggest? *Analysis:* strength is not measured by absence of thought but by what he does next. The Krsna conscious response: chant extra rounds with attention in the evening, hear Srila Prabhupada's lectures or the Bhagavatam with devotees, take a defined service, and speak to a senior; practical aids such as handing the phone to the ashram routine only support this. *Takeaway:* the remedy is a higher taste through chanting, hearing, association and service, not willpower alone.

## M. Discussion and reflection
**M.1 Closing check questions.**
1. According to BG 3.37, what is lust born of? *Contact with the mode of passion.*
2. In SB 9.19.14, what does butter do to a fire? *Increases it.*
3. Name the three seats of lust (BG 3.40). *Senses, mind, intelligence.*
4. What is the difference between kama and prema (CC Adi 4.165)? *Own senses versus Krsna's pleasure.*
5. Does Krsna approve of all sex? *He is regulated sex life which is not contrary to religious principles (BG 7.11); the brahmacari vow is chosen.*
6. Whom can I talk to? *A trusted senior; a counsellor for compulsive patterns.*

**M.2 Activities.** (a) *Pair talk (3 min):* "Which hour of my day is empty of Krsna's service, and what service could fill it?" General answers only; no stories. (b) *Engagement list (5 min):* in groups list three Krsna conscious engagements (chanting, hearing, a service) that fill a restless hour, and say which seat of BG 3.40 each protects. (c) *Q&A round (10 min):* anonymous written questions into a box.

**M.3 Reflection.** "What is one thing that I now see differently about the drive I carry?" Private.

## P. Practical takeaway
- **Lesson:** desire is a force that grows when fed; it quiets when the heart has a higher taste.
- **One action:** pick one habit that "feeds the fire" (late-night phone, idle time) and replace it for one week with a defined service, attentive chanting, or hearing the Bhagavatam with devotees.
- **Reflection question:** "What do I really long for when this pressure comes?"
- **Help:** if it feels bigger than you can manage, talk to a trusted senior and consider a qualified counsellor. Seeking help is a sign of seriousness.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary
Today we asked: what is sex desire, and what does Srila Prabhupada's teaching say about it? Krsna tells Arjuna that lust is born of contact with the mode of passion, that it covers our clear thinking, that it is never satisfied and burns like fire, and that it sits in the senses, the mind and the intelligence (BG 3.37–40).

The Bhagavatam says that trying to put out this fire by giving it more is like pouring butter on a fire: it grows (SB 9.19.14). King Yayati learned this after a thousand years of enjoyment (SB 9.19.13). Prahlada compares sex life to "the rubbing of two hands to relieve an itch" (SB 7.9.45), and Srila Prabhupada's purport quotes Yamunacarya: "Since I have been engaged in the transcendental loving service of Kṛṣṇa, realizing ever-new pleasure in Him, whenever I think of sex pleasure, I spit at the thought, and my lips curl with distaste." Sex attraction is also the root of other attachments: body, home, family, money and the thought "I and mine" (SB 5.5.8). BIKC (ch. 5) says: "Sex desire is the main symptom of insanity exhibited by the conditioned soul"; "there is nothing intrinsically beautiful about any material body. Even the bodies of beauty queens are simply bags of skin filled with foul-smelling, revolting substances"; "sex cannot make us happy; only Kṛṣṇa can make us happy."

Krsna also says: "I am sex life which is not contrary to religious principles" (BG 7.11), and the purport says it "should be for the propagation of children, not otherwise." CC Adi 4.165 shows the direction: wanting to please my own senses is lust, wanting to please Krsna is love. The same longing, given the right object, becomes love.

## S2. Key ideas
- Lust is a force that grows when fed (SB 9.19.14).
- It sits in senses, mind and intelligence, so all three need care (BG 3.40).
- The remedy is a higher taste, found in chanting, hearing, devotees' company and service, not only force (BG 2.59).
- BIKC (ch. 5): "sex cannot make us happy; only Kṛṣṇa can make us happy."
- Asking for help is normal and wise.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **BG 3.39** · lust "is never satisfied and … burns like fire" · It will not be solved by giving it more.
- **SB 9.19.14** · butter on a fire increases it · I should not expect indulgence to end the pressure.
- **CC Adi 4.165** · lust is wanting to please my own senses, love is wanting to please Krsna · The longing can be redirected.

## S4. Words to know
- **Brahmacari:** a student living celibate, in training.
- **Kama:** lust; desire for one's own sense pleasure.
- **Prema:** pure love for Krsna.
- **Mode of passion:** the quality of nature that produces hankering and restlessness.
- **Intelligence:** the faculty that decides and discriminates.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *Is having this desire a sin?* The Gita calls lust "the all-devouring sinful enemy of this world" that covers our consciousness (BG 3.37, 3.39); the sinful act is illicit sex (SB 5.5.4 purport). BIKC (ch. 5): "Even a neophyte devotee can be confident of gradually conquering over lust, because he has got the right method."
2. *Is all sex forbidden?* No: BG 7.11 gives place to sex life not contrary to religious principles, which the purport says is "for the propagation of children, not otherwise." The brahmacari chooses another stage.
3. *Why not just give in to settle it?* SB 9.19.14: it does not settle; it increases.
4. *Where does it live?* Senses, mind, intelligence (BG 3.40).
5. *What if I need to talk?* A trusted senior; for repeated, distressing patterns, a qualified counsellor.

## S6. Check your understanding
1. Lust is born of contact with which mode (BG 3.37)? (a) goodness (b) passion (c) ignorance
2. In SB 9.19.14, supplying butter to a fire does what?
3. Name the three "sitting places" of lust.
4. What is the difference between kama and prema?
5. True or false: the Gita says all sex life is condemned.

**Answers:** 1. (b) passion (BG 3.37). 2. It increases the fire (SB 9.19.14). 3. Senses, mind, intelligence (BG 3.40). 4. Kama is wanting to please my own senses, prema is wanting to please Krsna (CC Adi 4.165). 5. False: BG 7.11.
