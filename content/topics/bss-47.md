# 23. Preaching

> Status line: BSS (brahmacari training) · 120 min · drafted · not sensitive. Based on chapter 23 of BIKC. Builds on bss-06 (Prahlada teaching his schoolmates, SB 7.5-7.7: not retold here; only Prahlada's prayer SB 7.9.43-44 is used, for a different point) and on bss-46 (service). Stories from the life of Lord Caitanya (Kurma and Vasudeva) are used here and not on earlier BSS pages.

## A. Topic overview

| Preparation time | About 2 hours: read CC Madhya 7.126-150 and CC Adi 9.41-42 with purports, SB 7.9.43-46, then this page |
| Difficulty | Intermediate |
| Prerequisites | None |
| Audience | Young men living or preparing to live as brahmacaris (celibate students of spiritual life); also any devotee who wants to share Krishna consciousness. "Preaching" here means sharing the message of Krishna by word, book or example, not arguing or pressing |
| Teaching objective | The audience understands why preaching is the devotee's own protection and a duty of gratitude, can say what Lord Caitanya asked of everyone, and leaves with a gentle, honest way to start |

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level
BIKC: the most effective weapon against illusion (*maya*) is the preaching of Krishna consciousness; by it we strike at the illusion around us and cleanse the illusion within. IYF's own BSS aim includes "serving more dedicatedly in Krishna consciousness way" (research/batch-criteria.md). IYF's own preaching decks add the missionary purpose: "Preaching is the Essence" (Viplava 2020) and "Youth Preaching - Future Hope of Our Movement" (ILEADS 2019).

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| VL1 | The joy of sharing something good with a friend; no pressure |
| VL2 | Why share: Caitanya's request to those in India (CC Adi 9.41) |
| VL3 | Starting small: telling one friend; the book and the invitation |
| SPS | Spreading Krishna consciousness as a duty and a joy |
| DPS | Approaching new people and building a team |

Not done here: the techniques of book distribution (see the engagement pages and O), debating, and the art of answering objections (the preaching toolkit pages).

## C. Central message
Lord Caitanya asked everyone to speak of Krishna wherever they are (CC Madhya 7.128), and Prahlada refused to be liberated alone (SB 7.9.44). For the brahmacari, preaching is both the repayment of mercy and the guard of his own renunciation (SB 7.9.46 purport; BIKC): speak of Krishna to others, and the heart is cleansed.

## D. Learning outcomes
1. State Lord Caitanya's instruction to the brahmana Kurma and what it means for people who cannot leave their homes (CC Madhya 7.126-130).
2. Explain what CC Madhya 7.148 says the Lord asked of the leper Vasudeva in return for mercy.
3. Say why Prahlada refused liberation alone (SB 7.9.43-44).
4. Explain the SB 7.9.46 purport: why one who avoids preaching finds the modes of nature "very difficult to surpass".
5. Give a short, respectful invitation to a friend.

## E. Scriptural foundation
Use six verses in the talk (CC Madhya 7.128, 7.148, CC Adi 9.41-42, SB 7.9.44, SB 7.9.46). "Verified" means the verse exists (research/verification/bss-47.json) and I read the translation and the purport used. CC Adi 9.41 and 9.42 are Srila Prabhupada's own translation; the same line is SB 10.22.35, so no Canto 10 note is needed.

**CC Madhya 7.128**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/7/128/
> "Instruct everyone to follow the orders of Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa as they are given in the Bhagavad-gītā and Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam. In this way become a spiritual master and try to liberate everyone in this land."
- **Context:** Lord Caitanya, on His tour of South India, speaks to the brahmana Kurma, who had asked to go with Him (7.126: "I can no longer tolerate the waves of misery caused by materialistic life"). The Lord answers: stay at home and chant (7.127) and instruct others (7.128).
- **Purport point:** "This is the sublime mission of the International Society for Krishna Consciousness." Many ask whether they must give up family life; "that is not our mission. One can remain comfortably in his residence." The purport: chant the maha-mantra, read the Gita and Bhagavatam if one can, refrain from illicit sex, meat-eating, gambling and intoxication, and follow Lord Caitanya's advice to Kurma: "stay at home, chant the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra and preach the instructions of Kṛṣṇa". (The purport also makes statements on marriage that are not used in this talk; this page does not rely on them.)
- **Use:** in the talk.

**CC Madhya 7.126-127, 7.129**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/7/126/
> "Better to remain at home and chant the holy name of Kṛṣṇa always." (7.127)
> "your materialistic life at home will not obstruct your spiritual advancement." (7.129)
- **Purport point (7.129):** if one follows Lord Caitanya's instructions under His representative, chanting and "teaching everyone as far as possible the same principle, the contamination of the materialistic way of life will not even touch him"; "wherever he lives, he converts that place into Vṛndāvana and Navadvīpa."
- **Use:** in the talk (J.1).

**CC Madhya 7.140, 7.141, 7.147, 7.148**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/7/148/
> "Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu also advised Vāsudeva to preach about Kṛṣṇa and thus liberate living entities. As a result, Kṛṣṇa would very soon accept him as His devotee." (7.148)
- **Context:** the leper brahmana Vasudeva fell unconscious when he learned the Lord had left (7.139); the Lord returned and embraced him (7.140) and the leprosy and his distress were gone (7.141); the Lord told him to chant Hare Krishna incessantly to avoid pride (7.147).
- **Purport point (7.148, read in full):** "the only return the Lord wanted was that Vāsudeva preach the instructions of Kṛṣṇa and liberate all human beings. That is the process of the International Society for Krishna Consciousness." "If one wants to be recognized as a devotee by Kṛṣṇa, he should take to preaching work."
- **Use:** in the talk (J.2).

**CC Adi 9.41-42**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/adi/9/41/ and /42/
> "It is the duty of every living being to perform welfare activities for the benefit of others with his life, wealth, intelligence and words." (9.42)
- **Purport point (9.41, read in full):** Lord Caitanya addresses people born in India, but "this is not only the duty of Indians but the duty of everyone". The purport says the best welfare activity for all human society is "to awaken man's God consciousness, or Kṛṣṇa consciousness"; and that the cult "request[s] every Indian to become a preacher". (9.42 purport, what I read:) unless one is interested in *sreyas*, the ultimately beneficial, in his own life, "he cannot preach of śreyas for the benefit of others."
- **Application (preacher's):** the order is first to care about one's own real welfare, then to share it. This suits a young brahmacari: his own practice is his first preaching.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 18.68-69**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/68/ and /69/
> "For one who explains this supreme secret to the devotees, pure devotional service is guaranteed, and at the end he will come back to Me." (18.68)
> "There is no servant in this world more dear to Me than he, nor will there ever be one more dear." (18.69)
- **Purport point (18.68):** Bhagavad-gita is generally discussed among devotees, for those who do not accept Krishna "as He is" will not understand; it should be explained to "persons who are ready to accept Kṛṣṇa as the Supreme Personality of Godhead"; but "anyone, however, who tries sincerely to present Bhagavad-gītā as it is will advance in devotional activities". (18.69 has no purport on vedabase.)
- **Application (preacher's):** present the Gita as it is, without whimsical explanation; that is what makes the preacher both honest and safe.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 7.9.43-44**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/9/43/ and /44/
> "I do not wish to be liberated alone, leaving aside all these poor fools and rascals." (7.9.44)
> "My concern is only for the fools and rascals who are making elaborate plans for material happiness ... I am simply concerned with love for them." (7.9.43)
- **Purport point (7.9.43, read in full):** Prahlada was "happy and unhappy simultaneously": happy because he was Krishna conscious, unhappy for those who plan to find happiness in the material world. Read the translation as it is, "fools and rascals" included; the purport gives Prahlada's mood towards them: "I am simply concerned with love for them." 7.9.44: translation only is used.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 7.9.46**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/9/46/ (the preaching statement is in the purport, not in the translation)
- **Translation:** ten methods of liberation (silence, vows, austerity, study, solitude, etc.) are often "only a professional practice and means of livelihood for those who have not conquered their senses".
- **Purport point (read in full):** the devotee's silence is not needed, because he speaks of the Lord's glories; "There are many who like to chant the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra in a silent, solitary place, but if one is not interested in preaching, talking constantly to the nondevotees, the influence of the modes of nature is very difficult to surpass. Therefore unless one is extremely advanced in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, one should not imitate Haridāsa Ṭhākura". Prahlada "does not condemn" such a process, but without active service "generally one cannot attain liberation."
- **Use:** in the talk. BIKC quotes the purport line; I checked it and it is as BIKC says.

**SB 4.7.44**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/7/44/
> "if one engages in hearing and chanting such topics, he can be delivered from illusion."
- **Purport point (read in full):** "For those who engage in the preaching of these two Vedic literatures it is very easy to get out of the illusory conditional life imposed upon us by māyā." Lord Caitanya "advised everyone to spread the word of Kṛṣṇa" ("All of you become spiritual masters. Your duty is simply to talk to whomever you meet of Kṛṣṇa or of the instructions given by Kṛṣṇa"); "We do not ask anyone to first change his position and then come to us."
- **Use:** in the talk. BIKC cites this verse; I checked it and it supports BIKC.

**BG 4.34**: Verified, for R.1 only (not for this level's talk or handout): https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/4/34/.

**Second-hand Prabhupada quotations (all Requires source review).** (1) IYF's deck "Youth Preaching - Future Hope of Our Movement" quotes a letter to Gajendra, 27 Jan 1970 ("Bamboo while it is green can be bent..."), and a letter to Satsvarupa and Hrdayananda of 9 Jan 1973 (also in the Preaching Encyclopedia's "Basis of College Preaching", "Srila Prabhupada's 16 Instructions on College Preaching"). ## F. Philosophical explanation
**F.1 Why preach.** Three reasons arise from the verses. (a) Compassion: Prahlada refuses to be liberated alone (SB 7.9.44) and Lord Caitanya asks everyone to benefit others (CC Adi 9.41-42). (b) Gratitude and order: Lord Caitanya's instruction is to become a spiritual master in one's own place (CC Madhya 7.128). (c) Protection: SB 7.9.46's purport says without interest in preaching the modes of nature are very difficult to surpass; BIKC adds that preaching gives a taste and realization which a brahmacari especially needs to maintain the high renunciation his ashram requires.

**F.2 What preaching is.** Not forcing, not debating to win, not displaying knowledge. Its content is the Lord's orders as given in the Gita and Bhagavatam (CC Madhya 7.128) and the holy name; its method is hearing and chanting (SB 4.7.44 purport). IYF's "Preaching is the Essence" deck frames it as personal benefit and shared responsibility, not as a favour: if a house in the village is on fire, the whole village runs with buckets (the deck's example; the deck is IYF's presentation, not scripture).

**F.3 Who may preach.** CC Adi 9.42's purport requires interest in one's own welfare first; BG 18.68's purport requires presenting the Gita as it is. Kurma was a householder and Vasudeva was a sick man; they were told to preach where they were. A brahmacari too need not wait for a degree.

**F.4 Preaching and renunciation.** The SB 7.9.46 purport warns that solitary chanting without interest in preaching leaves the modes of nature hard to cross, and that only the extremely advanced should imitate Haridasa Thakura. BIKC makes the practical point: "Preach to the karmis, preach to the devotees, preach to yourself." For a brahmacari who cannot go out daily, reading and teaching what he has learned are also preaching.

**F.5 Preaching about celibacy (BIKC).** BIKC advises that preaching to nondevotees should generally not be about celibacy but about the need to become Krishna conscious: encourage them to chant, take prasadam and associate with devotees; "everything else will follow". Among committed devotees the subject should be discussed regularly. This is BIKC's judgment. It fits the order of CC Madhya 7.128: start with the holy name and the Gita.

**F.6 Misunderstandings.** (a) "Preaching is only for senior devotees" (Kurma and Vasudeva). (b) "Preaching means defeating people in argument" (SB 4.7.44: hearing and chanting deliver people from illusion). (c) "I must first be perfect" (BG 18.68 purport: those who sincerely present the Gita advance). (d) "I should not preach until I have no doubts": CC Adi 9.42's purport asks for interest in one's own welfare, not perfection; keep practising and ask your seniors.

## H. Opening and engagement
**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes).**
1. Short prayer or kirtana verse. State the title and that "preaching" here means telling others about Krishna with love.
2. Say: "Think of the last time you told a friend about a film, a food or a place you loved. Did anyone have to order you? Today we ask what happens when what we love is Krishna."
3. Read the translation of CC Madhya 7.128.
4. Say: "The Lord said it to one man who wanted to leave everything. We will see what He asked of him."

**H.2 Opening questions.**
1. "What is one good thing you found that you could not help telling others about?"
2. "What stops you from talking about Krishna with your friends?" (Take three answers; note them for K.)
3. "Who first told you about Krishna consciousness, and how? What did you like about it?"

## I. Core teaching points
**I.1 Stay and speak (CC Madhya 7.126-129).** Kurma wanted to go with the Lord. The Lord's answer is not rejection. Read 7.127 and 7.128. Then the purport: "One can remain comfortably in his residence". Application (preacher's): whatever your circumstance (hostel, village, office), the order is the same: chant and speak of Krishna there. Next question for the group: who are we to speak about Krishna?

**I.2 The leper's return (CC Madhya 7.140-148).** Read J.2. The Lord's mercy was complete. The only thing He asked was that Vasudeva preach (7.148 purport). Lesson: preaching is the natural reply to being embraced. Next question for the group: why should this be my duty?

**I.3 For everyone's benefit (CC Adi 9.41-42; SB 7.9.43-44).** Read 9.42. Explain *sreyas* (the lasting good) from the purport. Then Prahlada's refusal to be liberated alone. Application (preacher's): tell the group that fear of imposing is natural; the verse removes it: this is a welfare activity. Next question for the group: what does preaching do for the preacher?

**I.4 Preaching protects the preacher (SB 7.9.46, SB 4.7.44, BIKC).** Read the key line of the SB 7.9.46 purport. Add SB 4.7.44's purport: preaching Gita and Bhagavatam is how illusion is crossed. BIKC: preaching gives taste; "ultimately every service is preaching", but contact with the public in book distribution, harinama sankirtana (public chanting) and college programmes lets us "directly experience the shower of Lord Caitanya's mercy" (BIKC). Application (preacher's): if you notice your sadhana drying up, ask whether you have shared Krishna with anyone recently.

**I.5 How to begin (preacher's application, from the verses and IYF's decks).** (a) Start with the holy name and prasadam, not with rules (CC Madhya 7.128 purport; BIKC). (b) Invite, do not press: IYF's Encyclopedia sheet "Do's and Don'ts of Book Distribution" says to inspire and persuade, not force or impose, to keep a humble, compassionate, grateful mood and to pray to Krishna to inspire the person; to thank the person even if no book is taken; and to invite to future sessions. (c) Tell what Krishna consciousness has done for you; IYF's deck asks "What positive change did Krishna consciousness bring in you?" (d) Speak what you have heard from the books; present the Gita as it is (BG 18.68 purport). (e) Ask your seniors; take part in the programmes already running (IYF's "Each one 'seench' one": every devotee connects one person to the programme and follows up).

## J. Stories and illustrations
**J.1 The brahmana Kurma (CC Madhya 7.126-135; key verses 7.126-129).** Retell in order from the verses: Lord Caitanya, travelling in South India, stayed at the house of the brahmana Kurma (7.138 says the leper Vasudeva came to see the Lord "at the house of Kūrma"). Kurma begged to be allowed to accompany the Lord because he could no longer bear materialistic misery (7.126). The Lord said: remain at home and chant (7.127); instruct everyone to follow Krishna's orders in the Gita and Bhagavatam, and become a spiritual master (7.128); if you do, material life at home will not obstruct your progress, and you will never lose My company (7.129). Later, when the Lord left, Kurma followed a great distance, but the Lord sent him home (7.135). **Devotional lesson:** the Lord does not ask Kurma to prove anything by renouncing; He asks him to speak of Krishna in his own place. The company of the Lord is given to the one who obeys.

**J.2 The leper Vasudeva (CC Madhya 7.136-148).** A brahmana named Vasudeva, suffering from leprosy, "enlightened" even in his sickness (7.137), came to see the Lord and found Him gone; he fell down unconscious (7.139). The Lord returned and embraced him (7.140); the leprosy and distress went away (7.141). Vasudeva, astonished, recited Sudama's verse "Who am I?" (7.142-143); fearing pride, he was told to chant Hare Krishna incessantly (7.147) and to preach (7.148). **Devotional lesson:** the Lord's mercy is not for show; the Lord's one request in return is to give that mercy on to others, and chanting protects the receiver from pride.

**J.3 Invented illustration (labelled).** A man is given a lamp on a dark road. A neighbour is stumbling in the dark. If the man keeps the lamp close to himself, he walks well but the neighbour may fall. If he walks beside the neighbour, both reach home. The Krishna conscious point is that the message of Krishna lights the way for the person who holds it and for the people near him. This is why Prahlada did not want to go alone (SB 7.9.44).

## K. Anticipated questions and objections
1. *I am not qualified. Can I preach?* Kurma and Vasudeva were told to do so; BG 18.68's purport: anyone who sincerely presents the Gita as it is will advance. Present what you have heard, and stay under seniors' guidance.
2. *Won't I be seen as imposing?* CC Adi 9.42: it is a welfare activity. Invite, do not press (I.5).
3. *Is preaching only book distribution?* No; BIKC names book distribution, harinama sankirtana and college programmes as direct forms, and says every service is preaching in a wide sense.
4. *What if I do not feel enthusiastic?* Read SB 7.9.46's purport: the problem is not shyness but the strength of the modes; also see bss-44 on enthusiasm.
5. *What if people argue?* The Encyclopedia sheet advises not entering long philosophical argument and inviting the person to a later session, which can be a practical choice for a street conversation; it is not a rule of scripture.
6. *Should I speak about celibacy to everyone?* BIKC's advice is no; start with chanting, prasadam and the Gita.
7. *Do Prabhupada's "fools and rascals" mean we look down on people?* The purport (SB 7.9.43) says Prahlada's feeling is love and concern for them; keep that mood.

## L. Case studies
**L.1 Invented case for discussion.** Sahil, a new brahmacari, has a friend who asks "Why do you chant?" Sahil gives a long lecture on karma and the friend goes quiet. *Principles:* CC Madhya 7.128 purport (the maha-mantra, the Gita), IYF/Encyclopedia (inspire, do not force). *Questions:* What would you say in thirty seconds? What did the friend actually ask? *Analysis:* answer the question asked, tell what it does for you, and invite him to taste prasadam and a kirtana. *Takeaway:* Sahil's friend should feel welcomed first, and informed second.

**L.2 Invented case for discussion (extension).** Nikhil's service is in the kitchen and he thinks he is "not a preacher". *Principles:* BIKC ("ultimately, every service is preaching"), BG 18.68 purport, CC Madhya 7.148. *Questions:* How could his work invite someone? *Takeaway:* offering prasadam is one of the best ways to invite someone to Krishna consciousness.

## M. Discussion and reflection
**M.1 Closing check questions (with expected answers).**
1. *What did Lord Caitanya tell the brahmana Kurma to do?* Remain at home, chant, and instruct everyone in the orders of Krishna (CC Madhya 7.127-128).
2. *What did the Lord ask of Vasudeva after healing him?* To preach Krishna consciousness (CC Madhya 7.148).
3. *Why did Prahlada not wish to be liberated alone?* He did not want to leave aside "all these poor fools and rascals"; his concern for them was love (SB 7.9.43-44).
4. *What does the SB 7.9.46 purport say about someone not interested in preaching?* The influence of the modes is very difficult to surpass.
5. *What does CC Adi 9.42 say about welfare activities?* It is the duty of every living being to do them with life, wealth, intelligence and words.
6. *In BIKC's advice, what should preaching to newcomers emphasise?* Chanting, prasadam and association, not the restrictions.

**M.2 Activities.**
(a) *My story in one minute (10 min, pairs).* Each tells, in one minute, what Krishna consciousness has changed in their life (IYF's "before and after"). The listener repeats it back; no advice.
(b) *Thirty-second invitations (10 min, groups of three).* One plays a friend who asks, "Why do you chant?" The answerer must reply in thirty seconds, end with an invitation (a kirtana, a meal) and say thanks. Rotate.
(c) *Kurma at home (10 min, extension).* Each writes one place they are (hostel, office, family) and one action there that follows CC Madhya 7.128. Share one.

**M.3 Reflection (private).** "Who is the one person I would like to share Krishna with, and what would I want them to taste first?"

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary
Lord Caitanya once met a brahmana named Kurma who wanted to leave everything and travel with Him. The Lord told him to remain at home, chant the holy name, and instruct everyone to follow Krishna's orders in the Bhagavad-gita and Srimad-Bhagavatam (CC Madhya 7.127-128). He added that if Kurma did so, family life would not obstruct his progress (7.129). Another man, Vasudeva, was sick with leprosy. The Lord embraced him and healed him, and the one thing He asked in return was that Vasudeva preach about Krishna (7.148).

Srila Prabhupada explains that it is the duty of everyone to do welfare work for others with life, wealth, intelligence and words (CC Adi 9.42), and that the best welfare is to awaken Krishna consciousness. Prahlada Maharaja said, "I do not wish to be liberated alone, leaving aside all these poor fools and rascals" (SB 7.9.44), and that his concern for them was love (SB 7.9.43).

Preaching also protects the preacher. Prabhupada writes that if one is not interested in preaching, the influence of the modes of nature is very difficult to surpass (SB 7.9.46, purport). Those who engage in preaching Bhagavad-gita and Bhagavatam find it very easy to get out of illusion (SB 4.7.44, purport). The best way to begin is simple: chant, offer prasadam, share what has helped you, invite people gently, and thank them whatever they say. Do not argue to win, and do not press.

## S2. Key ideas
- We can chant and speak about Krishna wherever we are, as Kurma was told to do.
- The one thing the Lord asked of Vasudeva in return for His mercy was to preach.
- Preaching helps other people, and it also protects the preacher.
- We start with the holy name and prasadam, and we invite people without forcing them.
- We present the Bhagavad-gita as it is.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **CC Madhya 7.128** · Instruct everyone to follow Krishna's orders in the Gita and Bhagavatam. · I can start to speak about Krishna right where I am.
- **CC Adi 9.42** · It is the duty of every living being to do welfare for others with life, wealth, intelligence and words. · Sharing Krishna consciousness is the best help I can give anyone.
- **SB 7.9.44** · "I do not wish to be liberated alone." · Caring for others is part of devotion.

## S4. Words to know
- **Preaching:** sharing the message of Krishna by words, books, prasadam or example.
- **Prasadam:** food offered to Krishna.
- **Harinama sankirtana:** public chanting of the holy names.
- **Brahmana:** a person of the priestly class (Kurma and Vasudeva were both).
- **Maya:** the illusion that makes us forget Krishna.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *Do I have to leave home to preach?* No. The Lord told Kurma to stay home and chant and instruct others (CC Madhya 7.127-128).
2. *Am I qualified?* Anyone who sincerely presents the Gita as it is will advance (BG 18.68, purport).
3. *Is preaching only for books and streets?* No; it also includes prasadam, kirtana and personal talk.
4. *What if someone argues?* Do not try to win; thank them and invite them to a later talk or a kirtana.
5. *Why preach when I have my own practice?* Preaching protects our own practice (SB 7.9.46, purport).

## S6. Check your understanding
1. What did the Lord tell Kurma to do?
2. What did the Lord ask Vasudeva to do after healing him?
3. Why did Prahlada not want to be liberated alone?
4. According to the SB 7.9.46 purport, what is hard for a person who has no interest in preaching?
5. What is a good first step with a new friend?

**Answers:** (1) Stay home, chant, and instruct others in Krishna's orders. (2) Chant Hare Krishna and preach. (3) He did not want to leave aside "all these poor fools and rascals"; he was concerned with love for them. (4) Crossing the influence of the modes of nature. (5) Chant, offer prasadam, share what has helped you, and invite gently.
