# Stress and depression management

> **VL1 · 45–60 min · review: sources checked, awaiting senior review · SENSITIVE (mental health).** The head reviews this
> page after the build. **Read K before anything else.** The preacher is not a therapist. This talk is mainly
> about **stress**, the everyday pressure of results, deadlines and expectations, which the Gita addresses
> directly (BG 2.47–48). It touches depression only to say clearly what it is, that it deserves professional
> care, that such care is fully compatible with spiritual life, and where to turn; VL1-19 *Overcoming
> depression* is the fuller page and should be read with this one. IYF's deck (*Stress and depression
> management*, 2022) is followed where it holds (desires regulated, not stopped; association; books; mantra
> meditation; diet; BG 3.36, 18.78); its statistics and two biographical stories are not repeated as fact (B1).

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Stress is the word this audience uses most about their own lives, and it needs no selling. The Gita was spoken
to a man under the heaviest pressure imaginable, minutes before a battle, and its first practical teaching is
about exactly this: do your part fully and let go of the result (BG 2.47–48). That is a teaching a VL1 room can
take home and test on Monday. Depression is a different thing, an illness, and the Gita's compassion toward
Arjuna's collapse is the model for how we speak about it (VL1-19). This page keeps the two distinct and says so
in the first ten minutes.

**Builds on vl1-10 and vl1-19:** [vl1-10](vl1-10.md) teaches BG 2.47 (the right to work, not to results) as a way of working; [vl1-19](vl1-19.md) covers depression through Arjuna's collapse. This page takes only stress: where it comes from, the body's routine, and where stress ends and illness begins. Do not re-teach those talks.

**Not done here:**
- Diagnosing anyone, or explaining depression's causes.
- Claiming that chanting, diet, lower expectations or positive thinking cure depression or an anxiety disorder.
  They support; they do not replace treatment.
- Any "mode of ignorance", "karma" or "lack of faith" framing of a listener's state.

## C. Central message

Most stress is about results we cannot control and expectations we never examined. The Gita's method is to do
our part fully and release the result, to keep the body's routine, and to steady the mind by practice, so that
pressure is carried rather than carried away by. When low mood lasts for weeks and life stops working, that is
not stress but an illness, and getting professional help is the brave, right and fully spiritual thing to do.

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. Explain in their own words the Gita's distinction between the work (ours) and the result (not ours), and say
   why that reduces stress (BG 2.47–48, 5.12).
2. Name two expectations in their own life that feed their stress, and say which are theirs and which borrowed.
3. Describe the body's part: regulated eating, sleeping, recreation and work (BG 6.16–17).
4. Say what "regulate desire, don't stop it" means, using the ocean image (BG 2.70).
5. State the difference between stress and depression as an illness, and know where to turn (K).

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** BG 2.47–48 carry the talk (work and result). 3.36–37, 2.70, 5.12, 6.16–17 and 6.26 give the method.
2.8, 2.14 and 9.22 are for the care statement and the close. 18.58 and 18.78 are the ending; 18.58's second half
is background only.

**BG 3.36**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/36/
> "Arjuna said: O descendant of Vṛṣṇi, by what is one impelled to sinful acts, even unwillingly, as if engaged by force?"
- **Context:** End of chapter 3. IYF's deck quotes it (slide 17); an earlier slide names "drinking or taking
  drugs when depressed or in anxiety".
- **Teaching:** A person can act against his own wish, "as if engaged by force".
- **Purport point:** "Although the living entity sometimes does not want to act in sin, he is still forced to
  act."
- **Application (preacher's):** Many stressed people reach for something: a drink, a screen, food. The verse
  names the experience without shaming it. Pair with 3.37 and move to the method; do not dwell.
- **Use:** in the talk, briefly.

**BG 3.37**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/37/
> "It is lust only, Arjuna, which is born of contact with the material mode of passion and later transformed into wrath…"
- **Purport point:** Desire is love of God "transformed into lust, as milk in contact with sour tamarind is
  transformed into yogurt"; when the mode of passion is "elevated to the mode of goodness by the prescribed
  method of living and acting", one is saved from its degradation.
- **Application (preacher's):** The deck's bicycle: desire is the pedal; the Gita does not say stop pedalling,
  it says change what you pedal toward. "Regulated and directed, not stopped" is a fair reading of this purport.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 2.47**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/47/
> "You have a right to perform your prescribed duty, but you are not entitled to the fruits of action. Never consider yourself the cause of the results of your activities, and never be attached to not doing your duty."
- **Context:** Krsna's first practical instruction to Arjuna after the teaching on the soul.
- **Teaching:** The work is ours; the result is not. Not doing the work is also attachment.
- **Purport point:** "One who is attached to the result of his work is also the cause of the action. Thus he is
  the enjoyer or sufferer of the result"; "Any attachment, positive or negative, is cause for bondage."
- **Application (preacher's):** Stress lives in the gap between effort and outcome. This verse closes the gap
  from our side: the effort is fully ours, the outcome is shared with many factors we do not hold. It is not
  "don't care"; the verse forbids giving up the duty too.
- **Use:** in the talk (the key verse).

**BG 2.48**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/48/
> "Perform your duty equipoised, O Arjuna, abandoning all attachment to success or failure. Such equanimity is called yoga."
- **Teaching:** Equanimity in success and failure *is* yoga.
- **Purport point:** "Yoga means to concentrate the mind upon the Supreme by controlling the ever-disturbing
  senses"; "Gain or victory are Kṛṣṇa's concern".
- **Application (preacher's):** The audience thinks yoga is postures; the Gita says it is this evenness. For a
  VL1 room, offer the practice: "do the work as an offering; let the result be the Lord's concern", and let
  them test it before they believe it.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 5.12**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/5/12/
> "The steadily devoted soul attains unadulterated peace because he offers the result of all activities to Me; whereas a person who is not in union with the Divine, who is greedy for the fruits of his labor, becomes entangled."
- **Purport point:** "The person who is attached to Kṛṣṇa and works for Him only is certainly a liberated person,
  and he has no anxiety over the results of his work."
- **Application (preacher's):** The purport uses the word *anxiety*: the Gita's peace is specifically freedom
  from result-anxiety. Say it that plainly.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 2.70**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/70/
> "A person who is not disturbed by the incessant flow of desires – that enter like rivers into the ocean, which is ever being filled but is always still – can alone achieve peace, and not the man who strives to satisfy such desires."
- **Teaching:** Desires keep flowing in; peace belongs to the one who is not disturbed by them, not to the one
  who tries to satisfy them all.
- **Purport point:** "As long as one has the material body, the demands of the body for sense gratification will
  continue. The devotee, however, is not disturbed by such desires, because of his fullness."
- **Application (preacher's):** The ocean image answers "should I have no desires?": the ocean does not refuse
  the rivers, and does not overflow. Expectations arrive; we need not be run by each one. This is where the
  deck's "decrease expectation" belongs: examine which expectations are yours.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 2.14**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/14/
> "…the nonpermanent appearance of happiness and distress, and their disappearance in due course, are like the appearance and disappearance of winter and summer seasons… one must learn to tolerate them without being disturbed."
- **Purport point:** The purport's examples are duties done despite the weather (a bath early in the cold month
  of Magha, cooking in the summer heat): "One has to execute his duty in spite of climatic inconveniences."
- **Application (preacher's):** Tolerance here means carrying on, not feeling nothing. For a stressful fortnight: "this is a season" is true and steadying. For a mood
  that does not lift in weeks: a season that does not pass needs a doctor (K). Say both.
- **Use:** in the talk, with the caution.

**BG 6.16**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/16/
> "There is no possibility of one's becoming a yogī, O Arjuna, if one eats too much or eats too little, sleeps too much or does not sleep enough."
- **Purport point:** The purport names simple food in the mode of goodness (grains, vegetables, fruits, milk)
  and regulated sleep.
- **Application (preacher's):** The deck's "sattvic diet" slide rests here. Stress wrecks eating and sleep, and
  wrecked eating and sleep worsen stress. Offer simple food and regular sleep as the first, least glamorous
  support.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 6.17**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/17/
> "He who is regulated in his habits of eating, sleeping, recreation and work can mitigate all material pains by practicing the yoga system."
- **Purport point:** Extravagance in "eating, sleeping, defending and mating" blocks advancement; the devotee's
  habits are regulated.
- **Application (preacher's):** Four items, in the Gita's order: eating, sleeping, recreation, work. Ask the room
  which of the four their exam season destroys first.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 6.26**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/26/
> "From wherever the mind wanders due to its flickering and unsteady nature, one must certainly withdraw it and bring it back under the control of the Self."
- **Purport point:** One who controls the mind is a *gosvami*; one controlled by it is "the servant of the
  senses".
- **Application (preacher's):** Stress is largely the mind replaying tomorrow's exam or yesterday's mistake. The
  verse gives the practice: notice, bring it back. Mantra meditation is one way of doing exactly this; offer it
  as a ten-minute experiment, not a prescription.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 2.8**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/8/
> "I can find no means to drive away this grief which is drying up my senses. I will not be able to dispel it even if I win a prosperous, unrivaled kingdom on earth…"
- **Context:** Arjuna's words just after asking Krsna for help (2.7).
- **Teaching:** Arjuna says that success itself would not remove his grief.
- **Purport point:** "The problems of material existence – birth, old age, disease and death – cannot be
  counteracted by accumulation of wealth and economic development."
- **Application (preacher's):** The stressed student believes the placement will fix everything; Arjuna, about
  to win a kingdom, says it would not. Not a put-down of ambition: a reminder that the result is not the cure
  for the anxiety about the result.
- **Use:** in the talk, one minute.

**BG 9.22**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/9/22/
> "…to them I carry what they lack, and I preserve what they have."
- **Application (preacher's):** A shelter: "I am not carrying this alone." Do not turn it into "pray and the
  stress will go".
- **Use:** in the talk, one line.

**BG 18.58**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/58/
> "If you become conscious of Me, you will pass over all the obstacles of conditioned life by My grace…"
- **Use:** background; the promise of passage, for the preacher's own steadiness. The verse's second half ("you
  will be lost") is not for a stressed room.

**BG 18.78**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/78/
> "Wherever there is Kṛṣṇa, the master of all mystics, and wherever there is Arjuna, the supreme archer, there will also certainly be opulence, victory, extraordinary power, and morality. That is my opinion."
- **Context:** The last verse of the Gita, spoken by Sanjaya; IYF's deck closes with it.
- **Purport point:** Sanjaya tells Dhrtarastra, "where Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna are present, there will be all good
  fortune."
- **Application (preacher's):** The deck's point: the man who was shaking in chapter 1 ends in chapter 18 on the
  side where "victory" is predicted, because he did his part and kept the right company. Close on it as hope,
  not as a guarantee of exam results.
- **Use:** in the talk (close).

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** Stress is the mind strained between effort and outcome, and between what I want and what
   others expect. The Gita separates the two cleanly: the work is mine, the result is not (2.47); evenness in
   both outcomes is yoga (2.48); offering the result removes "anxiety over the results" (5.12 purport).
   Desires and expectations will keep flowing in (2.70); the task is not to stop them but to regulate and
   direct them (3.37 purport: the mode of passion raised to goodness). The body's routine (6.16–17) and the
   practice of bringing the mind back (6.26) are the supports.
2. **Why it matters.** Two errors harm people: "stress means I'm failing" and "if I just achieve the result I'll
   be fine." The first shames; the second is refuted by Arjuna himself (2.8). The Gita offers a third way: do
   the duty wholeheartedly, release the outcome, keep the body regular, steady the mind by practice.
3. **The deck's frame, worded carefully.** IYF's presenter says "depression – past; anxiety – future" and "our
   inner screen represents our outside screen." As a *teaching image* this is useful: much stress is the mind
   living in a feared tomorrow or a regretted yesterday, and what we show ourselves inside shapes what we see
   outside. It is not a clinical account of either condition and should not be presented as one.
4. **Connections.** The mind as friend or enemy (VL1-16); work without anxiety for results (VL1-10); the four
   needs and routine (VL1-29, VL1-09); depression (VL1-19). At VL3 the offering of results becomes a devotional
   practice; at SPS/DPS the mentor relationship carries a person through dry periods.
5. **Consequences.** A listener who hears this well will (a) sort their expectations into "mine" and
   "borrowed", (b) put effort where it belongs and release the result as a daily practice, (c) protect sleep and
   simple food in exam season, (d) try bringing the mind back, perhaps with a mantra, and (e) know the line
   between stress and illness and how to help a friend across it.
6. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - **"Detachment from results means not trying."** 2.47 forbids inaction in the same breath. Full effort,
     released outcome.
   - **"Lower your expectations and depression goes."** Expectation work is for stress. Depression is an illness
     and is not caused by high standards; never say or imply that it is the person's fault.
   - **"Chanting/diet cures depression."** Supports, not cures. Say so.
   - **"A devotee shouldn't be stressed or sad."** Arjuna was both (2.8), and he is the Gita's hero.
   - **"Tolerate it (2.14) and it will pass."** A stressful fortnight passes; a mood that does not lift in weeks
     needs a doctor.
7. **Pitch for VL1.** Use the audience's words: "stress", "pressure", "burnout", "anxious", "down". Distinguish
   once, clearly, stress from the illness. Sanskrit: none needed beyond "yoga" redefined. Tone: warm, slow,
   practical; not a motivational talk.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet, give your name, and say today's talk is about everyday stress, and that you will say clearly where stress ends and illness begins.
2. Ask H.2 question 1 by show of hands.
3. Say: "If anything today feels too heavy, you may step out; I will be near, and I will give a helpline before the end."
4. Give the opening line below (the Gita, spoken before a battle).
5. Move to the care statement (segment 2).

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the room's own life)
1. Who is waiting on a result this month: an exam, interview, harvest, sale, contract or medical report? Hands only.
2. What is the hardest part of waiting: the work, or not knowing the result?
3. Whose expectation, yours or someone else's, weighs more on you right now?

- **Opening:** "Who here is under some pressure this month? Exams, placements, a target, a family expecting
  something?" Pause; most hands go up. "Good. The Bhagavad-gita was spoken to a man under more pressure than any
  of us, a few minutes before a battle. Its first practical advice is about exactly this. Let's see if it works."
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** Ananya has her final placement interview on Thursday. She has prepared well,
  cannot sleep, has stopped eating properly, snaps at her mother and replays every possible question. Her uncle
  says "just be positive". What is Ananya actually stressed about: the preparation, or the result? Whose
  expectation is she carrying?
- **Transition:** "Krsna's first instruction to Arjuna separates two things Ananya has mixed together."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** Everyone here will be Ananya this year, or sit beside her.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Stress lives in the gap.** Between effort and outcome, and between my wish and others' expectations.
   *Example:* Ananya has done the preparation; the stress is all about Thursday's result and her family's hopes.
   *Transition:* what does the Gita say about that gap?
2. **The work is mine; the result is not.** BG 2.47, and 2.48: evenness in success and failure *is* yoga; 5.12
   purport: "no anxiety over the results". *Example:* a surgeon operates fully and does not control every
   outcome; a batsman plays each ball and does not control the scoreboard. *Transition:* but my desires keep
   coming.
3. **Regulate, don't stop.** The ocean takes the rivers and stays full and still (2.70). Desire is the pedal;
   direct it, do not stop pedalling (the deck's image; 3.37 purport). Sort expectations: mine, borrowed.
   *Example:* Ananya's "I must get this job" is partly her uncle's. *Transition:* what about the body?
4. **The body first.** Eating, sleeping, recreation, work, regulated (6.16–17). *Example:* exam season destroys
   sleep first; protect it. *Transition:* and the replaying mind?
5. **Bring it back.** The mind wanders; withdraw it and bring it back (6.26). Mantra meditation is this practice
   with a sound to return to; ten minutes is an experiment anyone can run. *Transition:* and when it is more
   than stress?
6. **Stress passes like a season; an illness does not.** 2.14 with the caution; K. Asking for help is what
   Arjuna did (2.7, VL1-19). *Transition:* to the close.
7. **Where Krsna and Arjuna are** (18.78). The man who shook in chapter 1 ends on the side of "victory" because
   he did his part and kept the right company. Hope, not a guarantee.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

> **Before you teach (read twice):**
> 1. **Say this early, in your own words:** "Stress is normal and this talk is about carrying it better. Some
>    people have depression or an anxiety disorder, which are illnesses, like diabetes is an illness. If your low
>    mood or anxiety has lasted weeks, if you cannot sleep or eat or function, if you feel hopeless or have
>    thoughts of harming yourself, please see a doctor or counsellor, and tell someone you trust today. That is
>    not a failure of faith; it is the right and brave thing to do, and spiritual life walks alongside it."
>    *(Pastoral guidance; general descriptions, not a diagnostic tool.)*
> 2. **Referral:** Tele-MANAS, the Government of India's free 24×7 mental-health helpline: **14416** or
>    **1-800-891-4416** (https://telemanas.mohfw.gov.in/). Also the campus counsellor or a general physician.
>    Keep the number on your slide.
> 3. **If someone speaks of wanting to die or harm themselves:** take it seriously every time; stay with them;
>    do not leave them alone; do not argue theology; help them contact the helpline, a doctor or emergency
>    services, and involve someone who can stay with them. Follow up the next day.
> 4. **In the room:** never analyse a person's situation publicly; never say "it's your karma", "you lack
>    faith", "just chant more", "lower your expectations" or "be positive" to someone who is ill. Acknowledge,
>    and talk afterwards.
> 5. **You are not a therapist.** Your gifts here are presence, honesty, the text's method for stress, and a good
>    referral. That is enough.

1. **"Isn't detachment from results just an excuse not to try?"**
   - The same verse forbids giving up the duty: "never be attached to not doing your duty" (2.47). The Gita's
     person works harder, not less, because the energy that went into worrying goes into the work.
2. **"My parents' expectations are the stress. The Gita can't change them."**
   - No, and it does not ask you to abandon your family. It asks you to sort what is yours from what is borrowed
     (2.70: rivers come; the ocean stays itself), do your duty fully, and release the result. Honest
     conversation at home is part of the duty. VL3-13 takes up dealing with parents in depth.
3. **"Will chanting or diet cure my depression or anxiety?"**
   - They support a stressed mind (6.16–17, 6.26). If what you have is an illness, they support treatment and do
     not replace it. Taking treatment is itself caring for the body you were given.
4. **"The slide says lower your expectations. Isn't that giving up on ambition?"**
   - Keep the ambition; examine the expectation. 2.47 keeps the effort at full strength and drops only the
     clutching at the outcome. Many of the heaviest expectations turn out to be borrowed anyway.
5. **"My friend is very stressed / seems depressed. What do I say?"**
   - For stress: listen, help with the practical (a meal, a walk, a study plan), remind them the work is theirs
     and the result is not. For signs of illness (weeks of low mood, not sleeping or eating, hopelessness): help
     them see a doctor or counsellor, go with them, keep coming back. Arjuna's recovery began with someone who
     stayed on the chariot.
6. **"Why would God give me this pressure?"**
   - *Pastoral:* honest pain honestly spoken is better than pretended peace; Arjuna spoke his (2.8). VL1-06 takes
     up suffering properly. Here: you are not carrying this alone (9.22), and the method is in your hands.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: Ananya's interview.** Final placement interview on Thursday. Prepared well; not sleeping; not eating;
replaying questions; snapping at her mother; "just be positive" from her uncle.
- **Dilemma:** What is she stressed about, and what can she actually do before Thursday?
- **Principles:** BG 2.47 (effort hers, result not); 2.70 (whose expectation?); 6.16–17 (sleep and food first);
  6.26 (bring the mind back).
- **Questions:** Which of her stresses are about effort and which about result? Which expectations are hers?
  What would protecting sleep and meals look like this week?
- **Suggested analysis:** Her preparation is done; the result is shared with the panel, the market and luck.
  She protects sleep and simple food, does one last review each day and then stops, and tries ten minutes of
  bringing the mind back each evening. Her uncle's "be positive" is replaced by "do your part; release the rest".
- **Takeaway:** Effort, released result, routine, practice.

**Case 2: Karan's six weeks.** Karan missed a promotion two months ago. He has felt flat since, sleeps badly,
has stopped meeting friends, finds no interest in anything and calls himself a failure. Colleagues tell him to
"manage his stress".
- **Principles:** K (red flags: duration, sleep, withdrawal, self-contempt); BG 2.14 (a season that does not
  pass); VL1-19 (Arjuna asked for help).
- **Questions:** Is this stress? Which signs suggest more? What should a friend do this week?
- **Suggested analysis:** This is beyond stress management. A friend helps Karan see a doctor or counsellor and
  goes with him; routine and company accompany care; nobody tells him it is his expectations or his fault.
- **Takeaway:** Know the line; presence and referral first; the Gita's method serves care, never replaces it.

1. Is stress the same as depression, in this talk? *No: stress is pressure; depression is an illness needing professional care.*
2. What two things does BG 2.47 separate? *The work (ours) and the result (not ours).*
3. What does BG 2.48 call yoga? *Evenness of mind in success and failure.*
4. In BG 2.70, what does the ocean do with the rivers? *It receives them and stays full and still.*
5. Name two of the four things BG 6.17 says to regulate. *Eating, sleeping, recreation, work.*

**M.2 Activities (choose one; about 10 minutes)**
- **Effort or result?** (1) Each writes three current stresses privately (2 min). (2) Marks each "effort" or "result", and "my expectation" or "someone else's" (3 min). (3) In pairs, say only the marks, not the stresses (3 min). (4) Volunteers share one thing they noticed; thank each (2 min).
- **Ten-minute reset:** (1) Everyone sits with the feet on the floor. (2) Three slow breaths (1 min). (3) Mantra or a chosen calm word, repeated quietly for 5 minutes; when the mind runs, bring it back (BG 6.26). (4) One sentence in pairs: "what I noticed" (3 min).

**M.3 Reflection**
- **Reflection (private, optional):** Of the three things stressing me most, which are about effort and which about results? Which expectations are mine?
- **Application:** Which of the four (eating, sleeping, recreation, work) will I protect this week, and how? *(Nobody needs to share anything personal.)*

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Stress is the gap between the effort we make and the result we cannot control, widened by expectations we carry without ever examining them. The Bhagavad-gita (an ancient Indian text) was spoken to a man, Arjuna, under enormous pressure just before a battle, and its first practical advice is about this gap: "You have a right to perform your prescribed duty, but you are not entitled to the fruits of action" (BG 2.47). Do your part fully; the result depends on many things you do not hold. Evenness in success and failure, Krsna says, is what *yoga* (the practice of steady, linked-up awareness) really means (BG 2.48).

Desires and expectations will keep flowing in like rivers; peace belongs to the person who, like the ocean, stays full and still (BG 2.70). Look after the body's routine, especially sleep and simple food (BG 6.16–17), and when the mind runs to tomorrow's exam or interview, bring it back (BG 6.26); a mantra (a short sacred sound repeated quietly) is one way to do that. Stress passes like a season (BG 2.14).

But if low mood or anxiety has lasted weeks, or you cannot sleep, eat or function, or you have thoughts of
harming yourself, that is not stress but an illness: please tell someone today and see a doctor or counsellor.
That is the brave and right thing to do, and spiritual life walks beside it.

## S2. Key ideas

- The work is mine; the result is not (BG 2.47). Stress lives in the gap between them.
- Evenness in success and failure is yoga (BG 2.48).
- Even winning would not remove Arjuna's grief (BG 2.8): the result is not the cure for worry about the result.
- Desires and expectations keep coming; be the ocean, not the river (BG 2.70). Sort expectations: mine, borrowed.
- Protect eating, sleeping, recreation and work (BG 6.16–17).
- When the mind wanders, bring it back (BG 6.26).
- Depression and anxiety disorders are illnesses and need professional care. Spiritual life walks beside it.

## S3. Verses to remember

- **BG 2.47:** "You have a right to perform your prescribed duty, but you are not entitled to the fruits of
  action." *For me:* full effort, released result.
- **BG 2.48:** "Perform your duty equipoised, O Arjuna, abandoning all attachment to success or failure. Such
  equanimity is called yoga." *For me:* yoga is this evenness, not a posture.
- **BG 2.70:** Desires "enter like rivers into the ocean, which is ever being filled but is always still".
  *For me:* I can let expectations arrive without being run by them.

## S4. Words to know

- **Stress:** pressure from results we do not control and expectations we carry; everyone has it.
- **Depression / anxiety disorder (illness):** low mood or anxiety lasting weeks, with sleep, appetite, energy
  and daily life affected. Needs professional care, like any illness.
- **Yoga (in the Gita's sense):** evenness in success and failure while doing one's duty (BG 2.48).
- **Mantra meditation:** repeating a sacred sound and bringing the mind back to it; a practice of BG 6.26.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **If I detach from results, won't I stop trying?**
   The same verse says "never be attached to not doing your duty" (BG 2.47). The energy that went into worry
   goes into the work.
2. **My parents' expectations are the stress.**
   Sort what is yours from what is borrowed (BG 2.70); do your duty fully; talk honestly at home. The Gita does
   not ask you to abandon your family.
3. **Will chanting or diet cure my depression or anxiety?**
   They support a stressed mind. If you have an illness, they support treatment and do not replace it.
4. **Is lowering expectations giving up ambition?**
   Keep the ambition; examine the expectation. The Gita keeps the effort at full strength (BG 2.47).
5. **My friend seems depressed. What do I say?**
   Listen, help practically, and if the signs have lasted weeks, help them see a doctor or counsellor; go with
   them. Keep coming back.
6. **Where can I get help?**
   Tele-MANAS (India), free, 24×7: 14416 or 1-800-891-4416. In any other country, call your national crisis line or emergency number, or see your doctor or campus/workplace counsellor.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. BG 2.47 says I have a right to: (a) the result, (b) the work, (c) neither.
2. True or false: BG 2.48 says yoga is a set of postures.
3. In BG 2.70, what does the ocean do with the rivers?
4. Name two of the four habits BG 6.17 says should be regulated.
5. In your own words: when does stress become something to take to a doctor?

**Answers:** 1. (b) the work (BG 2.47). 2. False: equanimity in success and failure is called yoga (BG 2.48).
3. It receives them and stays full and still (BG 2.70). 4. Any two of eating, sleeping, recreation and work
(BG 6.17). 5. When low mood or anxiety has lasted weeks, or affects sleep, eating or the ability to function,
or brings hopelessness or thoughts of self-harm; then tell someone today.
