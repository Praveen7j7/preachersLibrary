# Teachings of Lord Rishabhadev to his hundred sons

> Status line: BSS (brahmacari training) · 120 min · drafted · not sensitive. Builds the base for bss-08 to bss-12: this page owns "human life, association, and the knot of attraction".

## A. Topic overview
| | |
|---|---|
| Batch / level | BSS, brahmacari training (Bhakti Siddhanta Sabha) |
| Suggested duration | 120 minutes (lecture, with an "if time allows" extension) |
| Preparation time | About 2 hours: read SB 5.5.1-26 with purports once, then this page |
| Difficulty | Intermediate |
| Prerequisites | None. A listener who knows the words "human form" and "devotional service" can follow |
| Audience | Young men living or preparing to live as brahmacaris; also useful to any seeker. Brahmacari = a celibate student who lives simply, serves the guru and studies scripture |
| Teaching objective | The audience sees why a brahmacari's training starts with the verse "do not work like dogs and hogs", how association decides direction, and why a guru is a heavy responsibility |

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level
A brahmacari is training to use the human form for the one purpose Rsabhadeva names in SB 5.5.1. This is the first
page of the BSS story group: it gives the aim of the training before the later pages (avadhuta, the forest of enjoyment,
Yayati, Haridasa) show what threatens it and what protects it.

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| VL1 | One verse (SB 5.5.1) as the answer to "what is life for?" (see vl1-44) |
| VL2 | The ladder of beings and the choice at the human junction (SB 5.5.21, 5.5.2) |
| VL3 | The practice of association and detachment from family illusion |
| SPS | The Rsabhadeva chapter as a whole, including the Lord's own conduct (SB 5.5.1-35) |
| DPS | The guru's duty and the fruit of service in sanctioned lineages |
| BSS | This page: training the heart to take up tapasya, seek association and not be bound by the knot of attraction |

Not done here: the full life of Rsabhadeva, the python-like conduct He later adopted (SB 5.5.32), or discussion of
other levels' material (see R).

## C. Central message
The human body is a rare chance to purify the heart through austerity and devotional service; the doorway to
liberation is service of advanced devotees, and the doorway to bondage is the knot of attraction. A brahmacari
chooses his association accordingly.

## D. Learning outcomes
By the end, participants can:
1. Explain in their own words what Rsabhadeva says human life is NOT for and what it is for (SB 5.5.1).
2. Identify the two doors in SB 5.5.2 and name which kind of association opens which.
3. Explain what "knot in the heart" means and why the verse's remedy is service and hearing, not suppression alone.
4. State the responsibility Rsabhadeva places on a guru or father (SB 5.5.18) and what a brahmacari can learn from it.
5. Choose one concrete change of association or daily service for the coming week.

## E. Scriptural foundation
Use six verses in the talk (SB 5.5.1, 5.5.2, 5.5.4, 5.5.8, 5.5.14, 5.5.18). The rest are for questions.

**SB 5.5.1**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/5/1/
> "…should not work hard day and night simply for sense gratification, which is available even for dogs and hogs that eat stool."
- **Context:** Lord Rsabhadeva, an incarnation of the Lord, is giving His last instructions to His hundred sons before leaving household life.
- **Teaching:** A living being with a human body should not spend day and night only on sense gratification. He should take up penance and austerity to attain devotional service; by that the heart is purified and he attains eternal blissful life.
- **Purport point:** The purport says the word *deha-bhak* refers to anyone who accepts a material body, but one given the human form must act differently from animals; dogs and hogs enjoy sense gratification in their own way.
- **Application (preacher's):** For a brahmacari, "penance" is not a punishment. It is the gate of a house he is entering: early rising, simple food, chanting, service.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 5.5.2**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/5/2/
> "One can attain the path of liberation from material bondage only by rendering service to highly advanced spiritual personalities."
- **Teaching:** Service to mahatmas is the door of liberation; association with those fond of women and sex opens the way to hell. The verse describes the mahatmas: equipoised, peaceful, without anger, working for the benefit of all.
- **Purport point:** The human body is like a junction. On one path one associates with mahatmas, on the other with those attached to sense gratification and women.
- **Application (preacher's):** The verse is about whom we serve and sit with, not about despising anyone. Every person is a soul (SB 5.5.26); association is a choice of what we absorb.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 5.5.3**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/5/3/
> "They are not attached to their homes, although they may be householders."
- **Teaching:** Those who want love of Godhead do not like to mingle with people absorbed only in maintaining the body; they collect only enough to keep body and soul together, yet are not indifferent to duty.
- **Purport point:** A person interested in advancing spiritually should not be attached to homely comforts, even if he is a householder.
- **Use:** background; shows that detachment is attitude, not address.

**SB 5.5.4**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/5/4/
> "…he certainly becomes mad after materialistic living and engages in all kinds of sinful activity."
- **Teaching:** One who takes sense gratification as life's aim becomes mad after it; the body already received is temporary and the cause of misery; Rsabhadeva thinks it unfit for an intelligent person to enter again the activities that bring body after body.
- **Purport point:** The purport names illicit sex, meat-eating, intoxication and gambling as the four sinful activities by which another miserable body is obtained.
- **Use:** in the talk, one sentence only; this is the four regulative principles stated as cause and effect.

**SB 5.5.5**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/5/5/
> "As long as one does not inquire about the spiritual values of life, one is defeated and subjected to miseries arising from ignorance."
- **Teaching:** Pious or sinful, karma gives results; as long as the mind is impure and absorbed in fruitive activity, one must accept a material body.
- **Purport point:** People think they should act piously to be relieved of misery, but speculative knowledge and pious activity do not solve the problem. The only aim should be emancipation from maya, and one should be inquisitive.
- **Use:** for questions about "just being a good person".

**SB 5.5.6**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/5/6/
> "…until one has love for Lord Vāsudeva, who is none other than Myself, he is certainly not delivered from having to accept a material body again and again."
- **Use:** background; gives the positive goal that answers 5.5.5.

**SB 5.5.7**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/5/7/
> "Even though one may be very learned and wise, he is mad if he does not understand that the endeavor for sense gratification is a useless waste of time."
- **Use:** for a brahmacari who is also a student or professional: learning is no protection by itself.

**SB 5.5.8**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/5/8/
> "The attraction between male and female is the basic principle of material existence."
- **Teaching:** On the basis of this misconception, which ties the hearts together, one becomes attracted to body, home, property, children, relatives and wealth, and thinks "I and mine".
- **Purport point:** The tie is called *hrdaya-granthi*, "the hard knot in the heart", which is very difficult to undo.
- **Application (preacher's):** The brahmacari's training is the slow, joyful loosening of the knot by service; the topic of how, and why willpower alone is weak, continues in bss-11.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 5.5.14**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/5/14/
> "…the knot of bondage in the heart will be completely severed."
- **Teaching:** If the sons follow the advice carefully, they will be freed from ignorance of the desire for fruitive activity and the knot will be cut; for further advancement they should not become attached even to the means of liberation.
- **Use:** in the talk; the promise of the section.

**SB 5.5.18**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/5/18/
> "One who cannot deliver his dependents from the path of repeated birth and death should never become a spiritual master, a father, a husband, a mother or a worshipable demigod."
- **Purport point:** Unless one is a pure devotee of Krsna he cannot save himself from the path of repeated birth and death; one can stop birth and death only by returning home, back to Godhead.
- **Application (preacher's):** The verse is first a mirror for those who guide others and then a standard for a student to appreciate: a guru is not a title, he is a lifeline. Do not read it as permission to criticise one's guru; read it as a reason to serve with gratitude.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 5.5.21**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/5/21-22/
> "Superior to animals are human beings"
- **Teaching:** The verse ranks beings upward from dull matter through plants, moving creatures and intelligent animals to human beings, and onward to ghosts, Gandharvas, demigods, Brahma and beyond. Use only its first steps in this talk.
- **Use:** background; the ladder behind the word "human".

**SB 5.5.26**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/5/26/
> "…you should not envy any living entity — be he moving or nonmoving. Knowing that I am situated in them, you should offer respect to all of them at every moment."
- **Use:** closing; keeps SB 5.5.2 from becoming contempt for anyone.

## F. Philosophical explanation
**Principle.** Rsabhadeva speaks as the Lord and as a father: the body is a gift to be used for devotional service (SB 5.5.1); sense gratification is available to all species and does not need a human body.
**Why it matters.** A brahmacari is asked to give up things the world calls normal. Without this verse, the training looks like loss. With it, the training is a trade for a larger happiness described as "transcendental to material happiness and which continues forever" (SB 5.5.1).
**Connections.** Association (5.5.2) is the practical means; the knot of attraction (5.5.8) is the obstacle; ignorance and karma (5.5.5) explain why willpower is not enough; love for Vasudeva (5.5.6) is the cure; the guru's responsibility (5.5.18) is what makes the cure safe to receive.
**Consequences.** If this is not understood, austerity is felt as a burden and a falldown is felt as the end. If it is understood, austerity becomes the cost of entering something better.
**Common misunderstandings.**
1. "Sense enjoyment is sin, so the body is bad." The text calls the body temporary and a cause of misery when used for gratification; it does not call the person bad. The body is meant for service.
2. "Mahatmas are those who look holy." SB 5.5.2 describes qualities: equipoise, peace, freedom from anger, work for the good of all, absorption in devotional service.
3. "Women are the enemy." The verses speak of association with those fond of sex, and of the male-female attraction that binds both persons. Respect for all is commanded in SB 5.5.26.
**How to pitch it.** Speak with warmth to men who are giving up a lot. Put the aim first, the cautions second.

## H. Opening and engagement
**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes).**
1. Offer a short prayer or one round of kirtana; say the title once.
2. Say: "Today a father is speaking to his hundred sons for the last time. He is the Lord Himself, and he has one thing to say. We will hear it as sons."
3. Read SB 5.5.1 slowly, English only. Ask nobody anything yet. Pause for three seconds.
4. Say: "Within two hours we will understand each word of this verse and see how it shapes a brahmacari's day."
**H.2 Opening questions.**
1. "When you joined this training (or when you first thought of it), what did you think you were giving up?" (Take three answers; do not comment.)
2. "What do you think the word 'austerity' means to someone outside the temple?"
3. "If you could give a hundred young people one sentence before leaving them, what would it be?" (This leads to the story: the Lord did exactly that.)

## I. Core teaching points
**I.1 Human life is for tapasya and devotion (SB 5.5.1).** Dogs and hogs have all the sense gratification they need; the human form has a different purpose. Illustration (preacher's): a person with a rare opportunity, for example a scholarship to a great school, who spends it only on sleeping and entertainment has missed the point; Rsabhadeva says the human form is a greater opportunity and its point is bhakti, which purifies the heart. End: ask what tapasya will mean for me this week. Transition: where do I learn it?
**I.2 Two doors, chosen by association (SB 5.5.2, 5.5.3, 5.5.26).** Service of mahatmas opens liberation; association with those attached to sense gratification opens the opposite. Explain the verse's qualities of a mahatma and give the verse's own contrast. Then SB 5.5.26: respect everyone as a soul, while choosing close association with care. Example (preacher's, labelled): a seed planted in different soil. The soil is the association; Krsna consciousness grows where it is heard and served. Transition: what is it that pulls us toward the wrong door?
**I.3 The knot of the heart (SB 5.5.4-5.5.8, 5.5.14).** Sense gratification as life's aim makes a person mad after it (5.5.4); learning does not rescue us (5.5.7); the male-female attraction ties the heart to body, home, family, wealth (5.5.8). Rsabhadeva's remedy is not to fight alone but to follow the instructions (5.5.14) and gain love for Vasudeva (5.5.6). The method is hearing and chanting, service and association, with regulation as support. Transition: who gives the instructions?
**I.4 The guru's responsibility (SB 5.5.18).** The standard is high and shows why we are taught by a pure devotee: only such a person can lead us out of birth and death. A brahmacari's response is gratitude and service, with the attitude described in the BIKC (Bhakti Vikasa Swami's *Brahmacarya in Krishna consciousness*): obedient, humble, cheerful (summary; see O).

## J. Stories and illustrations
**J.1 Rsabhadeva instructs his sons (SB 5.5; key verse 5.5.2).** Retell in order: the Lord appears as King Rsabhadeva; He has a hundred sons, of whom Bharata is the eldest and a great devotee (SB 5.5.20 names Bharata as the one to be served). He gathers them and gives the instructions of SB 5.5.1-26. Devotional lesson: the best inheritance a father can give is a path to Krsna, and association with devotees is that path's first step. Note: His later conduct (SB 5.5.32) is for advanced study, not this talk.
**J.2 Invented illustration (labelled):** a mountain village where a young man leaves each day to carry goods to a market town. He is given a place beside two cart drivers: one speaks only of profit and gossip, the other of the river's holy names. After a month he notices he repeats what he hears. The point is SB 5.5.2: what we hear regularly enters the heart, so we choose who we sit with, and offer the heart to the Name.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections
1. **"Isn't it extreme to say humans should not work for enjoyment?"** The verse says "simply for sense gratification" and "day and night". Work for maintenance is accepted (SB 5.5.3: "enough money to keep the body and soul together"); the question is the aim of life.
2. **"What if I have family duties?"** SB 5.5.3 says detachment applies even to householders; the brahmacari and householder both serve; their paths differ in practice.
3. **"Does this say women are dangerous?"** No. It speaks of male-female attraction binding both; and SB 5.5.26 says respect everyone. The brahmacari's practice is protection of one's own heart.
4. **"Can one skip the guru and read the book?"** The books are the authority; the guru is the person who makes them lived (SB 5.5.18 shows the responsibility). Study and service together.
5. **"I already feel pulled. Is it too late?"** SB 5.5.14 promises the knot can be cut by following the instruction; start with service, chanting, and the company of devotees; speak openly to your mentor.

## L. Case studies
**L.1 Invented case for discussion.** A new brahmacari finds that his closest friend in the hostel speaks mostly about films and cricket scores. He likes the friend. *Dilemma:* whether to leave the friendship. *Principles:* SB 5.5.2 (association), 5.5.26 (respect for all). *Questions:* Could he stay friendly but spend more time with devotees? How could he share prasadam and a kirtana with the friend? *Analysis:* Rsabhadeva gives no instruction to hate; he gives a direction. Take the friend to the Name, and take care that the friendship does not set the brahmacari's habits. *Takeaway:* the aim is to be useful to the friend and to Krsna.
**L.2 Invented case for discussion (extension).** A young man, learned and successful, feels spiritually restless despite his degrees. *Principles:* SB 5.5.5, 5.5.7. *Questions:* What does the verse say learning cannot do? What might add service to his learning? *Takeaway:* the cure is bhakti, and learning serves it.

## M. Discussion and reflection
**M.1 Closing check questions (with expected answers).**
1. *What does Rsabhadeva say is available even for dogs and hogs?* Sense gratification (SB 5.5.1).
2. *What should a human being do instead?* Penance and austerity to attain devotional service, which purifies the heart (SB 5.5.1).
3. *Which two doors does SB 5.5.2 name?* Service of mahatmas leads to liberation; association with those fond of women and sex leads to hell.
4. *What is the "knot in the heart"?* The male-female attraction that ties the heart to body, home, family and wealth (SB 5.5.8, with purport).
5. *Why does learning alone not solve the problem?* One may still be mad after sense gratification, and the problem is not solved by pious or speculative activity (SB 5.5.5, 5.5.7).
6. *What is the guru's responsibility?* To deliver dependents from repeated birth and death (SB 5.5.18).
**M.2 Activities.**
a) *Verse in two voices (10 min).* Pairs read SB 5.5.1; one reads the "do not", the other the "do". Each says in one sentence what that "do" will look like tomorrow morning.
b) *Association inventory (10 min, private).* On a card, list three people or sources I spend most time hearing from. No one reads their card out. Mark each with one plus or a minus, then one action.
c) *Short drama (15 min, extension).* Roles: Rsabhadeva, two sons, narrator. Script: a three-line parting speech from SB 5.5.1, 5.5.2, 5.5.26 in the characters' own words (labelled as dramatisation). Debrief: what would you most want to remember if this were the last talk?
**M.3 Reflection.** "What does my day show that I think life is for?" Private; nothing is shared.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary
Lord Rsabhadeva, the Supreme Lord appearing as a king, spoke to His hundred sons before He left household life. He told them that a human being should not work day and night only for sense gratification, which dogs and hogs also have. The human form is for penance and austerity, which lead to devotional service; by that the heart becomes pure and life becomes eternal and blissful (SB 5.5.1).
He then said that the door to liberation is serving advanced souls, the mahatmas, who are peaceful, free of anger and engaged in devotional service. The opposite door is association with those who are attached to women and sex (SB 5.5.2). Rsabhadeva explained that the attraction between man and woman is the basic principle of material existence; it ties the heart into a knot of "I and mine" (SB 5.5.8). If we follow His instructions carefully, the knot will be cut (SB 5.5.14). He added that no one should become a guru or parent unless he can save his dependents from repeated birth and death (SB 5.5.18). For a brahmacari this means: choose your association with care, serve with gratitude, and respect everyone as a soul.

## S2. Key ideas
- The human body is for devotional service, not for what dogs and hogs also enjoy.
- Association is a door: serving mahatmas leads toward liberation.
- Attraction between male and female ties the heart to body and home.
- The guru's job is to deliver, and the student's job is to serve with thanks.
- Respect every living being.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **SB 5.5.1**: Do not work day and night only for sense gratification; take up austerity for devotional service. *For me:* my day has a purpose.
- **SB 5.5.2**: Service to advanced souls is the way to liberation. *For me:* I choose whom I listen to.
- **SB 5.5.14**: By following the instruction the knot in the heart is cut. *For me:* there is a way out.

## S4. Words to know
- **Brahmacari:** a celibate student living simply and serving his guru.
- **Mahatma:** a great soul, peaceful and engaged in devotional service.
- **Tapasya / austerity:** voluntary simplicity to purify the heart.
- **Sense gratification:** acting only to please the senses.
- **Guru:** a spiritual teacher who can lead a student out of birth and death.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *Is the body bad?* No. It is to be used for service; it is temporary and gives misery when used only for gratification (SB 5.5.4).
2. *Must I stop having friends outside?* No. Respect everyone (SB 5.5.26). Choose close association with care.
3. *What if I feel pulled by attraction?* SB 5.5.14 says following the instruction cuts the knot. Seek devotees' company, chant, serve, and talk with your mentor.
4. *Why a guru?* To be led out of repeated birth and death (SB 5.5.18).

## S6. Check your understanding
1. What does the verse say dogs and hogs also have?
2. Name the two doors in SB 5.5.2.
3. What is the "knot" of SB 5.5.8?
4. Does Rsabhadeva tell the sons to envy anyone?
5. What should a guru be able to do (SB 5.5.18)?

**Answers:** 1. Sense gratification (SB 5.5.1). 2. Service to mahatmas / association with those attached to sense gratification and women (SB 5.5.2). 3. The attraction between male and female that ties the heart to "I and mine" (SB 5.5.8). 4. No: respect all (SB 5.5.26). 5. Deliver his dependents from birth and death (SB 5.5.18).
