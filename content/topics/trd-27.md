## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on trd-25 (Bhagavan is the complete feature) and trd-26 (the Lord is the master of all energies): this page asks *which*
person is that Bhagavan, and answers: Krishna Himself, not as one form among many but as the source of all. Builds on sps-01
(who should be the object of my worship), which gives the practical conclusion; here the siddhanta behind it.

Residents meet this question from every side: from Hindus for whom Krishna is one avatara of Visnu, from devotees of other
Vaisnava lines, from scholars who call the Gaudiya claim sectarian. The Bhagavatam's own statement, "Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa is the original Personality of Godhead" (SB 1.3.28), and Lord Caitanya's teaching to Sanatana (CC Madhya 20.152–167) settle it, and Jiva Gosvami's *Krsna-sandarbha* is the book that proves it in detail. Srila Prabhupada himself points to it in the very first purport of the Bhagavatam (SB 1.1.1). No lower batch takes the proof as a subject.

## C. Central message

"All of the above-mentioned incarnations are either plenary portions or portions of the plenary portions of the Lord, but Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa is the original Personality of Godhead" (SB 1.3.28). "Kṛṣṇa who is known as Govinda is the Supreme Godhead. He has an eternal blissful spiritual body. He is the origin of all. He has no other origin and He is the prime cause of all causes" (Bs 5.1). Lord Caitanya to Sanatana: "Kṛṣṇa is the original source of everything and the sum total of everything" (CC Madhya 20.153). Every form of Godhead is honoured as His; the heart is given to the source.

## D. Learning outcomes

Residents should be able to:
1. Recite SB 1.3.28 and explain from its purport why Krishna is "distinguished from other incarnations" and what the
   powerhouse and bulbs illustrate.
2. Give Krishna's own statements (BG 7.7, 10.8) and Arjuna's confirmation (BG 10.12–13) that He is the source of all.
3. Teach Lord Caitanya's words to Sanatana (CC Madhya 20.152–156) and the three kinds of forms, svayam-rupa, tad-ekatma-rupa
   and avesa, as the CC Madhya 20.165 purport defines them from *Laghu-bhagavatamrta*.
4. Show that Narayana is Krishna's expansion (SB 10.14.14; CC Madhya 9.143–144) and name Krishna's four extraordinary
   qualities (CC Madhya 9.144 purport).
5. Say what *Krsna-sandarbha* establishes and answer "why Krishna and not Visnu, Siva or the formless?" without offending a
   worshipper of those forms.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**SB 1.3.28**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/3/28/
> "All of the above-mentioned incarnations are either plenary portions or portions of the plenary portions of the Lord, but Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa is the original Personality of Godhead. All of them appear on planets whenever there is a disturbance created by the atheists. The Lord incarnates to protect the theists."
- **Context:** Suta Gosvami closes the list of incarnations (SB 1.3.6–27) with this verse: *krsnas tu bhagavan svayam*.
- **Purport point:** "In this particular stanza Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the Personality of Godhead, is distinguished from other incarnations. He is counted amongst the avatāras (incarnations) because out of His causeless mercy the Lord descends from His transcendental abode." "Originally the Lord is full of all opulences, all prowess, all fame, all beauty, all knowledge and all renunciation. When they are partly manifested through the plenary portions or parts of the plenary portions, it should be noted that certain manifestations of His different powers are required for those particular functions." The illustration: "When in the room small electric bulbs are displayed, it does not mean that the electric powerhouse is limited by the small bulbs." Parasurama and Nrsimha displayed the power their missions needed.
- **Use:** in the talk. The governing verse of the whole topic; Lord Caitanya quotes it twice (CC Madhya 9.143, 20.156).

**Bs 5.1 and 5.39**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bs/5/1/
> 5.1: "Kṛṣṇa who is known as Govinda is the Supreme Godhead. He has an eternal blissful spiritual body. He is the origin of all. He has no other origin and He is the prime cause of all causes."
> 5.39: "I worship Govinda, the primeval Lord, who manifested Himself personally as Kṛṣṇa and the different avatāras in the world in the forms of Rāma, Nṛsiṁha, Vāmana, etc., as His subjective portions."
- **Purport point (5.1, Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura):** "The mundane manifestive portion of His own Self is known as all-pervading Paramātmā, Īśvara (Superior Lord) or Viṣṇu (All-fostering). Hence it is evident that Kṛṣṇa is sole Supreme Godhead."

> 20.165: "The Supreme Personality of Godhead exists in three principal forms — svayaṁ-rūpa, tad-ekātma-rūpa and āveśa-rūpa."
> 20.166: "The original form of the Lord [svayaṁ-rūpa] is exhibited in two forms — svayaṁ-rūpa and svayaṁ-prakāśa. In His original form as svayaṁ-rūpa, Kṛṣṇa is observed as a cowherd boy in Vṛndāvana."

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** Among all forms of Godhead, Krishna, the son of Nanda Maharaja in Vrindavana, is the original: "Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa is the original Personality of Godhead" (SB 1.3.28); "He has no other origin and He is the prime cause of all causes" (Bs 5.1); "Although He is the source of everything, He Himself has no source" (CC Madhya 20.153 purport). The other forms are His plenary portions or portions of portions.
2. **Why this does not lower any form of God.** The incarnations are the same Godhead (BG 15.7 purport: visnu-tattva, "personal expansions"). The difference is in manifestation, not in divinity: the powerhouse is not limited by the bulbs, and each incarnation displays the power its mission needs (SB 1.3.28 purport). Narayana has sixty qualities in full; Krishna has four more (CC Madhya 9.144 purport). So a Gaudiya honours Rama, Nrsimha, Visnu and Narayana as God, and knows their source.
3. **The three kinds of forms** (CC Madhya 20.165 purport, from *Laghu-bhagavatamrta*). *Svayam-rupa*: the form that depends
   on no other form, Krishna the cowherd boy of Vrindavana (20.166). *Tad-ekatma-rupa*: forms nondifferent from Him that
   exist at the same time with different features and activities (svamsa and vilasa). *Avesa*: living entities specially
   empowered by the Lord. This frame lets a preacher place any form he is asked about.
4. **The proof in four voices.**
   - The Bhagavatam: SB 1.3.28 after the list of incarnations; SB 1.1.1 opening with Krishna, son of Vasudeva (purport).
   - Krishna Himself: "there is no truth superior to Me" (BG 7.7); "I am the source of all spiritual and material worlds" (BG 10.8).
   - Arjuna and the sages: "You are the Supreme Personality of Godhead... All the great sages such as Nārada, Asita, Devala and Vyāsa confirm this truth about You" (BG 10.12–13).
   - Brahma: "Lord Nārāyaṇa is Your expansion" (SB 10.14.14); Brahma-samhita 5.1, 5.39.
   Lord Caitanya gathers these for Sanatana (CC Madhya 20.152–156) and Krsnadasa Kaviraja concludes: "All the revealed scriptures accept Śrī Kṛṣṇa as the Supreme Lord" (CC Adi 2.106).
5. **Connections.**
   - trd-23: this closes sambandha-jnana: God is known not only as a person (trd-25) and as master of energies (trd-26), but
     as this person, Krishna.
   - trd-28 follows: knowing who He is, what is to be done (abhidheya) is His service.
   - trd-31 gives the ordered list of incarnations from *Laghu-bhagavatamrta*; trd-32 shows Lord Caitanya as Krishna Himself
     (Bs 5.39 purport already hints: "Kṛṣṇa Caitanya, identical with Kṛṣṇa Himself").
   - sps-33 and trd-33: Krishna's sweetness, the four qualities, belong to rasa and are not expanded here.
6. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *"Krishna is an avatara of Visnu."* The Bhagavatam counts Him among the avataras because He descends, but distinguishes
     Him: the others are His portions (SB 1.3.28 and purport). Brahma says Narayana is Krishna's expansion (SB 10.14.14).
   - *"To say Krishna is the source is to insult Rama or Siva."* Naming the source does not lower what comes from it; the
     Gaudiya honours every form of Godhead as Godhead (BG 15.7 purport) and knows the demigods' source (BG 10.8 purport).
   - *"Only the Gaudiyas say this."* Krishna says it (BG 7.7, 10.8), Arjuna and the sages confirm it (BG 10.12–13), Brahma
     prays it (SB 10.14.14; Bs 5.1), and the Bhagavatam states it (SB 1.3.28).
   - *"The cowherd boy is a lower, human form; the four-armed Visnu is higher."* The cowherd boy is the svayam-rupa, "the form of the Supreme Personality of Godhead that does not depend on other forms" (CC Madhya 20.165 purport); the Lord told Sanatana that Krishna "appears as the supreme youth" (20.153).
7. **Pitch for TRD.** Teach it as evidence, calmly, with the powerhouse image ready, and with respect for the worshippers of
   every form of God the residents will meet. The aim is not to win a quarrel about forms but to bring the hearer to the
   source, where, as the Lord says, "The wise who perfectly know this engage in My devotional service" (BG 10.8).

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet the residents and say that today's session proves, from scripture, who the Bhagavan of the last two sessions is.
2. Ask them to write privately the most recent time someone asked "why Krishna and not Visnu, Siva or the formless?", and
   what they answered.
3. Read SB 1.3.28. Ask: what does this verse say about all the incarnations listed before it, and what about Krishna?
4. Say that Lord Caitanya quoted this verse to a devotee of Narayana at Sri Rangam and to Sanatana Gosvami at Varanasi, and
   that Jiva Gosvami wrote the *Krsna-sandarbha* around it.
5. Ask H.2 question 1 and take three answers.

**H.2 Opening questions**
1. If every form of God is God, why does it matter which one is the source?
2. A powerhouse lights many bulbs of different brightness. What does the brightness of a bulb tell you about the
   powerhouse?
3. Who, in scripture, has the authority to say which form is the original: a philosopher, a devotee, Brahma, or the Lord
   Himself?

## I. Core teaching points

1. **The Bhagavatam's own conclusion.** After listing the incarnations, Suta Gosvami says they "are either plenary portions or portions of the plenary portions of the Lord, but Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa is the original Personality of Godhead" (SB 1.3.28). *Example:* the powerhouse and the bulbs (purport). *Transition:* what does Krishna say of Himself?
2. **Krishna's own word.** "There is no truth superior to Me" (BG 7.7); "I am the source of all spiritual and material worlds. Everything emanates from Me" (BG 10.8). *Transition:* is this only His claim?
3. **Confirmed by Arjuna and the sages.** "You are the Supreme Personality of Godhead... All the great sages such as Nārada, Asita, Devala and Vyāsa confirm this truth about You" (BG 10.12–13); not flattery but Vedic truth (purport). *Transition:* what did Lord Caitanya teach?
4. **Lord Caitanya to Sanatana.** Krishna is the Absolute Truth "present in Vṛndāvana as the son of Nanda Mahārāja" (CC Madhya 20.152), "the original source of everything and the sum total of everything" (20.153); His proofs are Bs 5.1 and SB 1.3.28 (20.154, 20.156). *Transition:* how are His many forms related to Him?
5. **Three kinds of forms.** Svayam-rupa (the cowherd boy of Vrindavana, depending on no other form), tad-ekatma-rupa
   (nondifferent forms with different features), avesa (empowered souls) (CC Madhya 20.165–166 and purport, from
   *Laghu-bhagavatamrta*). *Transition:* and Narayana, whom so many worship as the Supreme?
6. **Narayana is Krishna's expansion.** Brahma: "Lord Nārāyaṇa is Your expansion" (SB 10.14.14). Krishna has Narayana's
   sixty qualities and four more: His pastimes, His devotees in conjugal love, His flute, His beauty (CC Madhya 9.144 and
   purport). *Transition:* what does this change in a preacher's life?
7. **Application (preacher's).** Knowing the source, the devotee honours every form of Godhead and gives his heart to Krishna;
   "The wise who perfectly know this engage in My devotional service and worship Me with all their hearts" (BG 10.8). This is
   the end of sambandha and the door to abhidheya (trd-28).

## J. Stories and illustrations

**Brahma's prayer after stealing the calves** (SB 10.14.14 and purport). The purport, quoting Srila Prabhupada's CC Adi 2.30 comment, tells the setting: Brahma "had tried to test Lord Kṛṣṇa to see if He was really the Supreme Personality of Godhead playing as a cowherd boy. Brahmā stole all the other boys and their calves from the pasturing grounds, but when he returned to the pastures he saw that all the boys and calves were still there, for Lord Kṛṣṇa had created them all again. When Brahmā saw this mystic power of Lord Kṛṣṇa's, he admitted defeat and offered prayers to the Lord". In his prayer Brahma says: "Indeed, Lord Nārāyaṇa is Your expansion" (10.14.14). *Devotional lesson:* the creator of the universe, born from Narayana, learned by Krishna's mercy that his own father is an expansion of the cowherd boy before him. (Read SB 10.13–14 in full before telling more of the pastime.)

**Lord Caitanya and Venkata Bhatta at Sri Rangam** (CC Madhya 9.143–144). Speaking with Venkata Bhatta, a devotee of Laksmi-Narayana, the Lord quotes SB 1.3.28 (9.143) and says: "Because Kṛṣṇa has four extraordinary qualities not possessed by Lord Nārāyaṇa, the goddess of fortune, Lakṣmī, always desires His company" (9.144). *Devotional lesson:* the Lord did not tell a Narayana devotee that his Lord was lesser; He showed that Krishna and Narayana are the same Godhead and that Laksmi herself is drawn to the fuller sweetness. (Read CC Madhya 9.108–160 before telling the whole exchange.)

**Arjuna accepts** (BG 10.12–13). After hearing Krishna say "I am the source of all spiritual and material worlds" (10.8), Arjuna declares: "You are the Supreme Personality of Godhead, the ultimate abode, the purest, the Absolute Truth... All the great sages such as Nārada, Asita, Devala and Vyāsa confirm this truth about You, and now You Yourself are declaring it to me." *Devotional lesson:* the friend who had treated Krishna as a friend accepts Him as the Supreme on the evidence of the sages and of Krishna's own word; so should the preacher's hearer.

**The powerhouse and the bulbs** (SB 1.3.28 purport). "When in the room small electric bulbs are displayed, it does not mean that the electric powerhouse is limited by the small bulbs." *Devotional lesson:* each incarnation shows the power its mission needs; the source is never less than His manifestations.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. *"Krishna is one of the ten avataras of Visnu. Why make Him the source?"* The Bhagavatam lists the incarnations and then
   says: "All of the above-mentioned incarnations are either plenary portions or portions of the plenary portions of the Lord, but Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa is the original Personality of Godhead" (SB 1.3.28). Brahma says Narayana is Krishna's expansion (SB 10.14.14).
2. *"Isn't this sectarian? Other Vaisnavas worship Narayana as supreme."* Krishna and Narayana are the same Godhead; Krishna
   has Narayana's sixty qualities and four more (CC Madhya 9.144 purport). Lord Caitanya said this with love to Venkata Bhatta,
   a devotee of Narayana, and it is the Bhagavatam's statement, not ours.
3. *"Siva is also called supreme in some Puranas."* "All Vedic literature agrees that Kṛṣṇa is the source of Brahmā, Śiva and all other demigods" (BG 10.8 purport). The Bhagavatam, the ripe fruit of the Vedas (trd-24), settles what the Puranas say variously.
4. *"The two-armed cowherd boy looks like a human form; surely the four-armed Visnu is higher?"* The cowherd boy is the
   svayam-rupa, "the form of the Supreme Personality of Godhead that does not depend on other forms" (CC Madhya 20.165 purport); the Lord told Sanatana that Krishna "appears as the supreme youth, and His whole body is composed of spiritual bliss" (20.153).
5. *"Does saying Krishna is the source lower Rama or Nrsimha?"* No. They are visnu-tattva, the Lord's "personal expansions" (BG 15.7 purport), Godhead Himself. Each displays the power its mission needs; the powerhouse is not limited by the bulbs (SB 1.3.28 purport).
6. *"Why did Krishna have to prove Himself to Brahma?"* He did not have to; Brahma tested Him and was defeated, and the result
   was Brahma's prayer (SB 10.14.14 purport). The pastime is the Lord's mercy on the creator.
7. *"Why study *Krsna-sandarbha* when SB 1.3.28 is enough?"* Because Srila Prabhupada himself sends the reader there in the
   first purport of the Bhagavatam (SB 1.1.1), and because residents who teach siddhanta should know the systematic proof;
   until a readable edition is checked, teach from the purports here.

## L. Case studies

**Case 1: The Narayana devotee.** *Invented case for discussion.* A visitor from a Sri Vaisnava family tells Gopala, a
brahmacari: "We worship Narayana as the Supreme. Your Krishna is His avatara. Why do you reverse it?"
- *Dilemma:* argue, or follow Lord Caitanya's way with Venkata Bhatta.
- *Principles:* CC Madhya 9.143–144 and purport; SB 10.14.14; SB 1.3.28.
- *Questions:* What did Lord Caitanya affirm about Narayana? What did He add? How did He keep the friendship?
- *Analysis:* Gopala first affirms that Narayana is Godhead with sixty qualities in full, then says, as the Lord said, that
  Krishna has four more and that Laksmi herself desires His company (CC Madhya 9.144), and that Brahma, born from Narayana,
  calls Narayana Krishna's expansion (SB 10.14.14). He does not lower the visitor's Lord; he shows the fuller sweetness and
  invites him to hear Krishna's pastimes.
- *Takeaway:* the Gaudiya claim is made with love for every form of Godhead, as the Lord Himself made it.

**Case 2: The resident who quarrelled.** *Invented case for discussion.* Nanda, a full-time preacher, tells a Siva devotee at
a festival that Siva is "just a demigod" and "nothing compared to Krishna". The man leaves angry, and Nanda's book table is
avoided for the rest of the day.
- *Dilemma:* true siddhanta said without the Lord's manner.
- *Principles:* BG 10.8 and purport ("Kṛṣṇa is the source of Brahmā, Śiva and all other demigods"); CC Madhya 9.143–144 (the Lord's manner with Venkata Bhatta).
- *Questions:* What was true in Nanda's words? What did he leave out? How would the Lord have said it?
- *Analysis:* The source of Siva is Krishna (BG 10.8 purport); but the Lord, speaking to a worshipper of another form,
  affirmed first and added second, and kept the man's heart. Nanda should have honoured Siva as the great devotee and
  demigod scripture describes, then opened BG 10.8 and asked who Siva himself worships.
- *Takeaway:* siddhanta without the Lord's manner closes the door it was meant to open.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Having learned that God is a person (trd-25) and the master of all energies (trd-26), we ask: which person? The Srimad-Bhagavatam, after listing the incarnations of God, concludes: "All of the above-mentioned incarnations are either plenary portions or portions of the plenary portions of the Lord, but Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa is the original Personality of Godhead" (SB 1.3.28). Srila Prabhupada explains that each incarnation displays the power its mission needs, as small bulbs do not limit the powerhouse that lights them. Krishna Himself says: "there is no truth superior to Me" (BG 7.7) and "I am the source of all spiritual and material worlds. Everything emanates from Me" (BG 10.8). Arjuna accepts this, saying that the great sages Narada, Asita, Devala and Vyasa confirm it (BG 10.12–13).

Lord Caitanya taught Sanatana Gosvami that Krishna is the Absolute Truth "present in Vṛndāvana as the son of Nanda Mahārāja" (CC Madhya 20.152), "the original source of everything and the sum total of everything" (20.153), and He gave two proofs: the Brahma-samhita, "He has no other origin and He is the prime cause of all causes" (Bs 5.1), and SB 1.3.28. The Lord then explained three kinds of forms of Godhead: svayam-rupa, the original form, Krishna the cowherd boy of Vrindavana, which depends on no other form; tad-ekatma-rupa, forms nondifferent from Him with different features, such as Narayana and the Visnu expansions; and avesa, living entities specially empowered by the Lord (CC Madhya 20.165–166 and purport, quoting Rupa Gosvami's *Laghu-bhagavatamrta*). Brahma, after testing Krishna by stealing the calves and cowherd boys, prayed: "Indeed, Lord Nārāyaṇa is Your expansion" (SB 10.14.14). To Venkata Bhatta, a devotee of Narayana, Lord Caitanya said that Krishna has four extraordinary qualities beyond Narayana's sixty, His pastimes, His devotees in conjugal love, His flute and His beauty, and that Laksmi therefore desires His company (CC Madhya 9.144 and purport).

Srila Jiva Gosvami's *Krsna-sandarbha* proves all this systematically; Srila Prabhupada points to it in the first purport of the Bhagavatam (SB 1.1.1). For the preacher, the lesson is twofold: every form of Godhead is honoured as Godhead, and the heart is given to Krishna, the source. As Krishna says, "The wise who perfectly know this engage in My devotional service and worship Me with all their hearts" (BG 10.8).

## S2. Key ideas
- Krishna, the son of Nanda Maharaja, is the original Personality of Godhead; all incarnations are His portions (SB 1.3.28).
- Krishna says it Himself (BG 7.7, 10.8); Arjuna and the sages confirm it (BG 10.12–13); Brahma prays it (SB 10.14.14; Bs 5.1).
- Three kinds of forms: svayam-rupa (Krishna in Vrindavana), tad-ekatma-rupa (nondifferent forms such as Narayana), avesa (empowered souls) (CC Madhya 20.165–166).
- Krishna has Narayana's sixty qualities and four more (CC Madhya 9.144 purport).
- Every form of God is honoured; the heart is given to the source (BG 10.8).

## S3. Verses to remember
- **SB 1.3.28:** the incarnations are portions, "but Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa is the original Personality of Godhead." *For me:* the verse that settles "why Krishna?".
- **Bs 5.1:** Krishna, Govinda, "has no other origin and He is the prime cause of all causes." *For me:* Lord Caitanya's own proof-text.
- **BG 10.8:** "I am the source of all spiritual and material worlds... The wise who perfectly know this engage in My devotional service". *For me:* knowing the source leads to serving Him.

## S4. Words to know
- **Krsnas tu bhagavan svayam:** "but Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa is the original Personality of Godhead" (SB 1.3.28).
- **Svayam-rupa:** the Lord's original form, depending on no other form; Krishna in Vrindavana.
- **Tad-ekatma-rupa:** forms nondifferent from Him with different features; includes svamsa and vilasa expansions.
- **Avesa:** a living entity specially empowered by the Lord.
- **Visnu-tattva:** forms of Godhead Himself, as distinct from the living entities.
- **Krsna-sandarbha:** Jiva Gosvami's fourth Sandarbha, proving that Krishna is the original Godhead.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *Isn't Krishna one of Visnu's ten avataras?* The Bhagavatam lists the avataras and then says the others are Krishna's portions and He is the original (SB 1.3.28); Brahma calls Narayana Krishna's expansion (SB 10.14.14).
2. *Isn't this sectarian?* Krishna says it (BG 7.7, 10.8), Arjuna and the sages confirm it (BG 10.12–13), Brahma prays it (SB 10.14.14; Bs 5.1), the Bhagavatam states it (SB 1.3.28).
3. *Does this lower Rama, Nrsimha or Narayana?* No; they are Godhead Himself, visnu-tattva (BG 15.7 purport). The powerhouse is not limited by the bulbs (SB 1.3.28 purport).
4. *Isn't the four-armed form higher than the cowherd boy?* The cowherd boy is the svayam-rupa, the form that depends on no other (CC Madhya 20.165–166).
5. *What are Krishna's four qualities beyond Narayana's?* His pastimes, His devotees in conjugal love, His flute and His beauty (CC Madhya 9.144 purport).
