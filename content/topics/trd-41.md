# The five primary rasas: santa, dasya, sakhya, vatsalya, madhurya

> **TRD · up to 180 min · review: drafted · `sensitive: true` (rasa; madhurya-rasa is described; the head reviews).** Group G, the
> devotional mellows and the goal of devotion. trd-33 gave the whole map of twelve rasas in Srila Prabhupada's words; this page walks
> through the five primary (direct) rasas one by one, as The Nectar of Devotion (third division, chapters 35–44) and Ramananda Raya's
> conversation with Lord Caitanya (CC Madhya 8.68–89) present them: what each relationship is, who the devotees in it are, what each
> adds to the one before it, and why the comparison between them never makes one devotee lower than another. **Source note:** the
> Bhakti-rasamrta-sindhu file in `research/books/` (OCR) covers only the Eastern Division; the five rasas (Western Division) are therefore
> taken from Prabhupada's summary study and from the verses he quotes. Madhurya-kadambini is quoted from the approved Gaudiya Vedanta
> Publications edition. Ujjvala-nilamani stays the preacher's background (research/advanced-books.md §2).

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on trd-33. That page gave the twelve rasas as a map, with the Lord's own examples (CC Madhya 19.189–191), the gradation (CC Adi
4.42–46) and Prabhupada's rule for speaking of rasa (NOD 34). This page does not repeat the map. It takes the five direct rasas in order
and asks of each: what is the relationship like from the inside, who lives in it, and what does it add? The source is Prabhupada's own
summary of the Western Division of the Bhakti-rasamrta-sindhu (NOD 35–44) and the conversation at Kovvur, where Lord Caitanya kept
saying to Ramananda Raya, "This is all right, but still you can speak more on the subject" (CC Madhya 8.68), and the answer climbed from
servitude to friendship to parental love to conjugal love.

A resident needs this because he lives among these words. He hears "dasya" in the morning class, sings of the sakhas and of Yasoda in
the evening, and reads of the gopis in the Krsna book. If he knows only the names, he will either treat the rasas as ranks (and feel
inferior as a servant) or treat them as a menu (and pick one). Prabhupada's presentation removes both errors: each rasa is complete
happiness for the one in it, the higher ones contain the lower (8.85–86), and no one chooses; the rasa is "permanently situated in the
heart of the devotee" (CC Madhya 19.188) and is revealed. Lower batches hear only that Krishna has friends, parents and servants
(sps-33, dps-18); no lower batch studies the five rasas as a science.

## C. Central message

There are five direct relationships in which a soul loves Krishna: neutrality (santa), servitude (dasya), friendship (sakhya), parental
love (vatsalya) and conjugal love (madhurya). "These five transcendental mellows exist permanently. The devotee may be attracted to one
of these mellows, and thus he becomes happy. Kṛṣṇa also becomes inclined toward such a devotee and comes under his control" (CC Madhya
23.46). Each later rasa holds the qualities of the earlier ones, as earth holds the qualities of sky, air, fire and water (CC Madhya
8.85–88); yet "whatever relationship a particular devotee has with the Lord is the best for him" (8.83). A resident does not pick a
rasa. He serves as a servant now, and Krishna reveals the rest.

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Describe santa, dasya, sakhya, vatsalya and madhurya in one or two sentences each, using Prabhupada's own definitions (NOD 35, 36, 41, 43, 44).
2. Name the main devotees of each rasa as The Nectar of Devotion lists them (the four classes of servants, the four groups of friends, the parental devotees, the gopis headed by Radharani).
3. Explain from CC Madhya 8.71–79 purports what each rasa adds and what the previous one lacked (fear, equality, maintenance, complete freedom).
4. Give the sky-air-fire-water-earth analogy (CC Madhya 8.85–88) and say what it does and does not prove (CC Madhya 8.83 and purport).
5. Say what a resident does about his own rasa today, from CC Madhya 8.71–73, SB 10.9.20 purport and trd-13: serve as a servant, imitate nothing, let the rasa be revealed.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**NOD 35 ("Neutral Love of God")** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/nod/35/
- **Teaching:** "This third division of Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu describes the five primary kinds of devotional service – namely, neutrality, servitude, fraternity, parenthood and conjugal love." Santa-rasa: "When one is actually able to maintain the transcendental position, his stage is called neutrality in devotional service." Its devotees "are always neutral and have no affection for nor misidentification with anything material. They are always grave, but fully absorbed in thoughts of the Supreme Personality of Godhead."
- **Purport point:** the sages in santa-rasa appreciate the four-handed Visnu form; and "śānta-rasa can be attained by the impersonalists only when they are in association with pure devotees. Otherwise it is not possible."
- **Application (preacher's):** santa is a rasa, not a failure. Its mark is attachment to Krishna with detachment from everything material; its limit is that it lacks intimacy. Many sincere seekers who come to us from meditation and yoga are near this door; the devotees' company brings them through it.
- **Use:** in the talk; the first rasa.

**NOD 36 ("Transcendental Affection (Servitude)")** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/nod/36/
- **Teaching:** "Devotees engaged in servitude are attached to Kṛṣṇa in the affection of reverence." "The devotees of the Lord in servitude are divided into four classes: appointed servants (such as Lord Brahmā and Lord Śiva, who are appointed to control the material modes of passion and ignorance), devotees in servitude who are protected by the Lord, devotees who are always associates and devotees who are simply following in the footsteps of the Lord." In Dvaraka the close associates are Uddhava, Daruka, Satyaki and others; "Out of the many close associates of Lord Kṛṣṇa, Uddhava is considered the best."
- **Application (preacher's):** the fourth class, "devotees who are simply following in the footsteps of the Lord", is where every resident stands today. Dasya is not a waiting room; Brahma, Siva and Uddhava live in it eternally.
- **Use:** in the talk; the second rasa.

**NOD 41 ("Fraternal Devotion")** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/nod/41/
- **Teaching:** "When a devotee is permanently situated in devotional service, and by different symptoms of ecstasy he has developed and matured a fraternal mellow or flavor in relationship with the Personality of Godhead, his feeling is called fraternal love of Godhead." The friends (vayasyas) "are generally divided into four groups: (1) well-wishers, (2) friends, (3) confidential friends and (4) intimate friends." Of the confidential friends, "Śrīdāmā is considered to be the chief." The friends "are fearless, and on a level equal with Kṛṣṇa they discharge their transcendental loving devotional service."
- **Application (preacher's):** friendship here means equality and fearlessness toward Krishna, grown from mature devotional service, never casualness adopted as a style.
- **Use:** in the talk; the third rasa.

**NOD 43 ("Parenthood")** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/nod/43/
- **Teaching:** "When ecstatic love develops into the relationship of parenthood and becomes steadily established, the relationship is called vātsalya-rasa." "This kind of parental love is generated in a devotee out of his conviction, in love, that he himself is superior to Kṛṣṇa and that without being taken care of by such a devotee, Kṛṣṇa could not possibly live." The chapter lists the parental devotees: mother Yasoda, Nanda Maharaja, Rohini, the elderly gopis, Devaki, the other wives of Vasudeva, Kunti, Vasudeva and Sandipani Muni. A brahmana prays: "But as far as I am concerned, I wish only to worship Mahārāja Nanda, because the supreme absolute Personality of Godhead, Kṛṣṇa, is crawling in his courtyard as his own child."
- **Use:** in the talk; the fourth rasa.

**NOD 44 ("Devotional Service in Conjugal Love")** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/nod/44/
- **Teaching:** "Although such conjugal feelings are not at all material, there is some similarity between this spiritual love and material activities. Therefore, persons who are interested only in material activities are unable to understand this spiritual conjugal love, and these devotional reciprocations appear very mysterious to them. Rūpa Gosvāmī therefore describes conjugal love very briefly." "Out of all the young gopīs, Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī is the most prominent." "Conjugal love is divided into two portions: vipralambha, or conjugal love in separation, and sambhoga, or conjugal love in direct contact."
- **Application (preacher's):** Rupa Gosvami's brevity is itself the teaching for a speaker: name madhurya-rasa, place Radharani at its head, and leave the depth to trd-44 and trd-45 and to the Bhagavatam heard in the devotees' company.
- **Use:** in the talk; the fifth rasa, briefly.

**CC Madhya 8.70–73 (with purports)** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/8/71/
> 8.73: "'By serving You constantly, one is freed from all material desires and is completely pacified. When shall I engage as Your permanent eternal servant and always feel joyful to have such a perfect master?'"
- **Context:** at Kovvur on the Godavari, Lord Caitanya asks Ramananda Raya again and again to go further; from verse 70 (the price of intense greed) the talk is about spontaneous love.
- **Purport point (8.71):** "This intimacy is called mamatā." "When the devotee feels 'The Lord is my master' and renders service unto Him, Kṛṣṇa consciousness is awakened."
- **Use:** in the talk; dasya as the first fixed relationship.

**CC Madhya 8.74–75 (with purports)** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/8/74/
> 8.74: "Loving service to Kṛṣṇa rendered in fraternity is the highest perfection."
- **Purport point (8.74):** "As long as loving service is rendered to the Lord in the master-servant relationship, there is some fear, for the servant is always afraid of the master, despite the intimacy of self-interest." In friendship the devotee "considers the Lord and himself on an equal level"; "This understanding is called viśrambha, that is, devoid of a respectful attitude."
- **Teaching (8.75, SB 10.12.11):** neither the Brahman-realised nor those who accept the Lord as master can understand the cowherd boys "now playing with the Lord in friendship".
- **Use:** in the talk; what sakhya adds.

**CC Madhya 8.76–78 (with purports); SB 10.8.46; SB 10.9.20** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/8/76/ · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/10/9/20/
> SB 10.9.20: "Neither Lord Brahmā, nor Lord Śiva, nor even the goddess of fortune, who is always the better half of the Supreme Lord, can obtain from the Supreme Personality of Godhead, the deliverer from this material world, such mercy as received by mother Yaśodā."
- **Purport point (8.76):** "In the fraternal relationship there is a sense of equality, but when that sense of equality is advanced in affection, one attains the platform of parental love."
- **Purport point (SB 10.9.20):** "Kṛṣṇa has the transcendental quality of bhṛtya-vaśyatā, becoming subordinate to His bhṛtya, or servant." Yet "the position of mother Yaśodā is the greatest"; she is "a nitya-siddha devotee, an expansion of Kṛṣṇa's hlādinī potency".
- **Use:** in the talk; what vatsalya adds; J.2.

**CC Madhya 8.79–80 (with purports)** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/8/79/
> 8.79: "Your statements are certainly getting better and better one after the other, but surpassing all of them is another transcendental mellow, and you can speak of that as the most sublime."
- **Purport point (8.79):** "In general, love of Godhead is devoid of the intimacy of ownership. In the case of love in servitude, there is a want of confidence. There is a want of increased affection in the fraternal relationship, and even when this affection increases in the parental relationship, there is nonetheless a want of complete freedom. However, when one becomes a conjugal lover of Kṛṣṇa, everything lacking in the other relationships is completely manifest. Love of Godhead lacks nothing in the conjugal stage."
- **Teaching (8.80, SB 10.47.60):** Uddhava, seeing the gopis' love in separation, says that the favour they received "was never bestowed upon the goddess of fortune or the other consorts in the spiritual world."
- **Use:** in the talk; what madhurya adds, in one paragraph of Prabhupada's.

**CC Madhya 8.83 (with purport)** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/8/83/
> "It is true that whatever relationship a particular devotee has with the Lord is the best for him; still, when we study all the different methods from a neutral position, we can understand that there are higher and lower degrees of love."
- **Purport point:** Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura "explains that this verse does not advocate the whimsical invention of some methods of love of Godhead." Prabhupada then lists the offences of invented devotion: selling mantras, making disciples for a livelihood, professional kirtana bands.
- **Use:** in the talk; the comparison and its limit, read together.

**CC Madhya 8.85–88 (with purport)** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/8/88/
> 8.85: "There is a gradual order of improvement in transcendental mellows from the initial ones to the later ones. In each subsequent mellow the qualities of the previous mellows are manifested, counting from two, then three, and up to the point of five complete qualities."
> 8.86: "As the qualities increase, so the taste also increases in each and every mellow. Therefore the qualities found in śānta-rasa, dāsya-rasa, sakhya-rasa and vātsalya-rasa are all manifested in conjugal love [mādhurya-rasa]."
- **Teaching (8.87):** sky, air, fire, water and earth hold one, two, three, four and five qualities.
- **Purport point (8.88):** "In conjugal love there are the qualities of neutrality, servitorship, fraternity and parental affection, as well as those of conjugal love itself. The conclusion is that through conjugal love the Lord is completely satisfied." "The highest form of conjugal love is represented by Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī; therefore in the pastimes of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa we can see that Kṛṣṇa is always subjugated by Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī's influence."
- **Use:** in the talk; the analogy (N).

**CC Madhya 8.89** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/8/89/
> "Lord Kṛṣṇa told the gopīs, 'The means of attaining My favor is loving service unto Me, and fortunately you are all thus engaged.'"
- **Purport point:** "The aim of human life is to attain the natural position of eternal life. This eternal life can be achieved only by devotional service."
- **Use:** in the talk; the close of the climb: service, in every rasa.

**CC Madhya 23.45–46 (with purports)** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/23/46/

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **Rati becomes rasa, in five kinds.** Attachment (rati) to Krishna has five forms, one for each relationship (CC Madhya 23.45; Madhurya-kadambini
   7.2b). Each is a sthayi-bhava, a continuous love that rules the other emotions like a king (23.46 purport). The five "exist permanently"
   (23.46). How a sthayi-bhava is built into rasa is trd-43's subject; here the five kings are introduced.
2. **Santa: fixed in the Lord, free of the world.** Its devotee has attachment to Krishna and detachment from matter, is "always grave, but fully
   absorbed in thoughts of the Supreme Personality of Godhead" (NOD 35), and worships the four-handed Visnu form. The Kumaras and the Yogendras
   are its examples (trd-33). Its taste is "not concentrated" (CC Madhya 19.185 purport); what it lacks is intimacy, mamata (8.71 purport).
3. **Dasya: "The Lord is my master."** Reverent affection (NOD 36). With dasya comes mamata, the sense "He is mine, I am His", and so "the loving
   affairs between the Lord and His devotee are not actually fixed" without it (8.71 purport). Four classes: appointed servants (Brahma, Siva),
   protected servants, constant associates (Uddhava, Daruka), and those following in the Lord's footsteps (NOD 36). What remains is fear: "the
   servant is always afraid of the master" (8.74 purport).
4. **Sakhya: equality without fear.** Visrambha, confidence "devoid of a respectful attitude" (8.74 purport). Four groups of friends: well-wishers
   (older, slightly parental), friends, confidential friends (Sridama chief), intimate friends (NOD 41). The Pandavas are friends outside Vraja.
   What remains is the want of "increased affection" (8.79 purport): a friend does not maintain Krishna.
5. **Vatsalya: "He cannot live without my care."** Parental love grows when equality "is advanced in affection" (8.76 purport); it rests on the
   devotee's loving conviction "that he himself is superior to Kṛṣṇa" (NOD 43). Yasoda's mercy exceeds Brahma's, Siva's and Laksmi's (SB 10.9.20).
   What remains is a want of "complete freedom" (8.79 purport): a mother still commands and protects.
6. **Madhurya: nothing lacking.** "Love of Godhead lacks nothing in the conjugal stage" (8.79 purport). The gopis, headed by Radharani (NOD 44);
   in separation and in meeting (NOD 44). Rupa Gosvami "describes conjugal love very briefly" because the materialist finds it mysterious (NOD 44);
   the resident does the same in public and studies it in trd-44 and trd-45.
7. **The analogy and its limit.** Sky has one quality, earth five; each later rasa holds the earlier ones (8.85–88). This is why conjugal love is
   "superior to all others in sweetness" (CC Adi 4.44) and why "through conjugal love the Lord is completely satisfied" (8.88 purport). But the
   same speech says "whatever relationship a particular devotee has with the Lord is the best for him" (8.83), and the purport warns against
   inventing any method of love. The comparison is made "from a neutral position"; it describes taste, not rank.
8. **Krishna under control in every rasa.** "Kṛṣṇa also becomes inclined toward such a devotee and comes under his control" (23.46); Krishna's
   quality of bhrtya-vasyata applies to the servant too (SB 10.9.20 purport). No rasa is a one-way street.
9. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *Dasya is for beginners; advanced devotees move to friendship.* Brahma, Siva and Uddhava are eternally servants (NOD 36). Rasas are not stages one passes through; they are permanent (23.46).
   - *I can decide my rasa by what attracts me in the Krsna book.* Attraction to a pastime is not the revelation of one's eternal rati; symptoms outside real service are "raty-ābhāsa" (23.45 purport). The present business is service in the servant's mood (8.71–73; trd-13).
   - *Because madhurya is highest, speaking of it most is most advanced.* Rupa Gosvami spoke of it "very briefly" (NOD 44); Uddhava, a dasya-bhakta, honoured it by praying for the gopis' foot-dust, not by claiming it (Madhurya-kadambini 7.2b).
   - *Santa is impersonal.* It is attachment to the Lord's Visnu form in the devotees' company (NOD 35); without devotees it is not attained.
10. **Pitch for TRD.** Walk the five in order with Prabhupada's one-line definitions, one story each, and the "what remains" from the 8.74–79
    purports; end with 8.83 and 8.89: service is the means in every rasa, and ours today is the servant's.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet the devotees and say that last time (trd-33) we learned the map of twelve rasas; today we walk the five main roads on that map, one by one, with Srila Prabhupada as guide.
2. Say: "At Kovvur, Lord Caitanya kept telling Ramananda Raya, 'This is all right, but still you can speak more on the subject.' We will follow that conversation step by step and see what each step adds."
3. Ask H.2 question 1 for private writing (no sharing).
4. Ask H.2 question 2; take three or four answers and write the relationships named on the board under five headings.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Five permanent attachments.** Rati has five forms (CC Madhya 23.45; Madhurya-kadambini 7.2b); each rules like a king (23.46 purport). *Transition:* the first king.
2. **Santa: grave, fixed, free.** NOD 35: attachment to the Lord's Visnu form, detachment from matter, attained in the devotees' company. *Transition:* what does it lack?
3. **Dasya: mamata.** "The Lord is my master" (8.71 purport); reverent affection; four classes of servants (NOD 36). *Transition:* and what remains in dasya?
4. **Sakhya: visrambha.** Fear gone, equality (8.74 purport); four groups of friends, Sridama chief (NOD 41). *Transition:* and what remains?
5. **Vatsalya: maintenance.** Equality "advanced in affection" (8.76 purport); the parent's loving conviction of superiority (NOD 43); Yasoda's unmatched mercy (SB 10.9.20). *Transition:* and what remains?
6. **Madhurya: nothing lacking.** 8.79 purport; the gopis headed by Radharani (NOD 44); described briefly on purpose. *Transition:* how do the five relate?
7. **Sky to earth.** 8.85–88: each later rasa holds the earlier qualities; the Lord is "completely satisfied" in conjugal love. *Transition:* does that rank devotees?
8. **Best for him, and no inventions.** 8.83 and purport; service is the means in all (8.89). *Transition:* so what do I do today?
9. **Serve as a servant; the rest is revealed.** 8.71–73; Krishna's bhrtya-vasyata (SB 10.9.20 purport); Uddhava's example (J.3, J.4); trd-13.

## J. Stories and illustrations

**1. Durvasa Muni and Maharaja Ambarisa (SB 9.5.16, as quoted in CC Madhya 8.72 and purport).** Durvasa Muni, a caste brahmana and great yogi,
hated Maharaja Ambarisa and tried to punish him with his mystic power; the Lord's Sudarsana cakra chased Durvasa until he took shelter of the
king. Settled at last, Durvasa said, "A man becomes purified simply by hearing the holy name of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, whose lotus
feet create the holy places of pilgrimage. Therefore what remains to be attained by those who have become His servants?" *Lesson:* this is dasya
seen from outside by a yogi: the servant of the Lord owns the Lord's opulences and lacks nothing (8.72 purport). Ramananda Raya quotes it as the
first fixed relationship.

**2. Yasoda binds Krishna (SB 10.9.20 and purport; CC Madhya 8.78).** Mother Yasoda tried to bind Krishna with ropes and failed; when she gave
up trying, Krishna agreed to be bound (8.78 purport). Sukadeva Gosvami then says that neither Brahma, nor Siva, nor Laksmi "can obtain from the
Supreme Personality of Godhead, the deliverer from this material world, such mercy as received by mother Yaśodā" (SB 10.9.20). The purport
counts the three "na"s and says they mark "great stress on a fact". *Lesson:* vatsalya binds the unbindable; Krishna "has become completely
subordinate to her" (purport). The parent's "superiority" is love's conviction, not fact, and Krishna honours it.

**3. Uddhava among the gopis (SB 10.47.60, as quoted in CC Madhya 8.80 and purport).** Uddhava came to Vrindavana with Krishna's message and
stayed to watch the gopis. Seeing their love in separation, he said that the favour Krishna gave them in the rasa dance "was never bestowed upon
the goddess of fortune or the other consorts in the spiritual world." *Lesson:* a devotee in servitude recognises the summit without claiming it.

**4. Uddhava's prayer (Madhurya-kadambini, Seventh Shower, text 2b; SB 10.47).** Visvanatha Cakravarti writes that Uddhava, "a devotee endowed
with dāsya-rasa mixed with sakhya-rasa", seeing the gopis' attachment, "prayed to take birth in Vṛndāvana as a blade of grass or a shrub, so
that he may obtain a particle of their foot-dust." *Lesson:* the right response of a lower rasa to a higher one is reverence and the prayer for
dust, not imitation. This is the resident's exact position toward madhurya-rasa.

**Illustration (writer's, not scriptural):** five cups hold the same water; the first is plain, the second has sugar, the third sugar and
milk, and so on; the fifth has everything the others have and more. No cup is empty, and the one drinking from any cup is fully satisfied
(compare CC Madhya 8.83 and 8.85–86). The illustration ends where the verses end: each later rasa contains the earlier, and each devotee's own is best for him.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"Since the servant is 'always afraid of the master', is dasya a lesser love?"** The fear is reverence, and the same purport says dasya carries
   "the intimacy of self-interest" and mamata (8.71, 8.74 purports). Brahma, Siva and Uddhava are eternally in it (NOD 36). Lesser in sweetness
   compared "from a neutral position" (8.83); best for the one in it.
2. **"Can I find out my rasa by seeing which pastimes attract me?"** No. Attraction to a story is not the awakening of eternal rati; symptoms without
   real service are "raty-ābhāsa" (CC Madhya 23.45 purport). The rasa is permanent and is revealed as bhava awakens (dps-18); now we serve as
   servants (8.71–73; trd-13).
3. **"If madhurya is the highest, should I meditate on the gopis to progress faster?"** Rupa Gosvami described it "very briefly" for a reason
   (NOD 44), and inventing one's own method of love is "whimsical invention" (8.83 purport). Uddhava's way is ours: pray for the gopis'
   foot-dust (Madhurya-kadambini 7.2b), hear faithfully (trd-33), imitate nothing (trd-13, trd-39).
4. **"Is santa-rasa just impersonalism with a Vaisnava label?"** It is attachment to the Lord's four-handed form with detachment from matter, and
   it "can be attained by the impersonalists only when they are in association with pure devotees" (NOD 35). The impersonalist who meets devotees
   may enter santa; the one who does not stays outside rasa.
5. **"Does Krishna really come 'under the control' of a servant, or only of a mother and a beloved?"** Bhrtya-vasyata is His quality toward every
   servant (SB 10.9.20 purport); the degree differs (Yasoda's is greatest), the fact does not. 23.46 says it of all five.
6. **"Why study the higher rasas if they are not mine to claim?"** Because the resident teaches from the Krsna book and the Caitanya-caritamrta,
   where all five appear, and because reverent knowledge of the summit protects against both envy and imitation (J.3, J.4).

## L. Case studies

**Case 1 (Invented case for discussion).** A brahmacari of three years reads NOD 41 and tells his godbrothers he is "in sakhya-rasa"; he begins to
speak of the Deity casually and to joke about the spiritual master's instructions as "a friend would". *Dilemma:* is this friendship with Krishna or
its reflection? *Principles:* NOD 41 (friendship grows "when a devotee is permanently situated in devotional service" and the mellow has "matured");
CC Madhya 23.45 purport (raty-abhasa); CC Madhya 8.83 purport (invented methods); trd-13. *Questions:* What produced the "friendship", mature
service or a book and a wish? Whom does casualness toward guru please? *Analysis:* the symptom is raty-abhasa; real sakhya is fearless toward Krishna
because of love, not careless toward His representatives. The remedy is the servant's mood toward guru and Deity (8.71–73), which is the platform on
which every rasa is eventually revealed. *Takeaway:* nobody reads himself into a rasa.

**Case 2 (Invented case for discussion).** A resident who cleans the temple and distributes books hears a class on madhurya-rasa and comes away
thinking his service is "low" and that real spiritual life is elsewhere. *Dilemma:* has the class taught rasa or rank? *Principles:* CC Madhya 8.83
("best for him"); NOD 36 (servants who follow in the Lord's footsteps are a class of eternal associates); CC Madhya 8.89 (service is the means);
SB 10.9.20 purport (Krishna subordinate to His servant). *Questions:* Which verse did the speaker leave out? What does Uddhava show (J.3–4)?
*Analysis:* the gradation describes taste, not the worth of a devotee; the speaker should always read 8.83 with 8.85–88. The resident's cleaning
is dasya in Prabhupada's mission, and dasya already has mamata: "He is mine." *Takeaway:* teach the five as five kinds of full happiness.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Srila Prabhupada's Nectar of Devotion, following Rupa Gosvami, describes "the five primary kinds of devotional service – namely, neutrality,
servitude, fraternity, parenthood and conjugal love" (NOD 35). In Sanskrit they are santa, dasya, sakhya, vatsalya and madhurya. Lord Caitanya
told Sanatana Gosvami, "These five transcendental mellows exist permanently. The devotee may be attracted to one of these mellows, and thus he
becomes happy. Kṛṣṇa also becomes inclined toward such a devotee and comes under his control" (CC Madhya 23.46).

At Kovvur, Lord Caitanya asked Ramananda Raya again and again to speak further, and Ramananda Raya climbed through the relationships. In
neutrality the devotee is "always grave, but fully absorbed in thoughts of the Supreme Personality of Godhead" (NOD 35) and worships the Lord's
four-handed form. In servitude the devotee feels "The Lord is my master" (CC Madhya 8.71 purport); Brahma, Siva, Uddhava and all who follow in the
Lord's footsteps are servants (NOD 36), yet "the servant is always afraid of the master" (8.74 purport). In friendship that fear goes and the
devotee feels equal to Krishna; Sridama is the chief of the confidential friends (NOD 41). In parental love the devotee is convinced, out of
love, "that he himself is superior to Kṛṣṇa and that without being taken care of by such a devotee, Kṛṣṇa could not possibly live" (NOD 43);
mother Yasoda received a mercy that Brahma, Siva and the goddess of fortune never received (SB 10.9.20). In conjugal love, "everything lacking in
the other relationships is completely manifest" (8.79 purport); its devotees are the gopis, and "Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī is the most prominent" (NOD 44).
Rupa Gosvami describes this love "very briefly" because materialistic people cannot understand it (NOD 44).

Lord Caitanya then explained the order with an example: sky has one quality, air two, fire three, water four, and earth all five; in the same way
each later mellow holds the qualities of the earlier ones, and conjugal love holds all five (CC Madhya 8.85–88). But He also said, "It is true
that whatever relationship a particular devotee has with the Lord is the best for him" (8.83), and the purport warns against inventing any method
of love. So the five are five kinds of full happiness, not five ranks. For us today the lesson is Uddhava's: a servant of Krishna who saw the gopis'
love prayed for the dust of their feet and did not imitate them (CC Madhya 8.80; Madhurya-kadambini 7.2b). We serve as servants now; Krishna reveals
the rest.

## S2. Key ideas
- Five permanent relationships with Krishna; the devotee in any of them is happy and Krishna comes under his control (CC Madhya 23.46).
- Each later rasa adds what the earlier lacked: intimacy, then fearlessness, then care, then complete freedom (CC Madhya 8.71–79 purports).
- Sky to earth: each later mellow holds the qualities of the earlier (8.85–88).
- Each devotee's own relationship is best for him; no invented methods (8.83 and purport).
- Today: serve as a servant, honour the higher rasas as Uddhava did, imitate nothing.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **CC Madhya 23.46:** "These five transcendental mellows exist permanently." For me: my relationship with Krishna is eternal; I do not have to make one up.
- **CC Madhya 8.83:** "whatever relationship a particular devotee has with the Lord is the best for him". For me: no comparison can make my service small.
- **CC Madhya 8.73:** "When shall I engage as Your permanent eternal servant and always feel joyful to have such a perfect master?" For me: today's prayer.

## S4. Words to know
- **Santa, dasya, sakhya, vatsalya, madhurya:** neutrality, servitude, friendship, parental love, conjugal love (NOD 35).
- **Rati:** attachment to Krishna; it has five forms, one for each relationship (CC Madhya 23.45).
- **Mamata:** the sense "He is mine"; it begins in servitude (CC Madhya 8.71 purport).
- **Visrambha:** confidence without a respectful attitude; the friend's mood (8.74 purport).
- **Vipralambha and sambhoga:** love in separation and love in meeting, the two parts of conjugal love (NOD 44).

## S5. Questions and answers
1. **Is a servant of Krishna lower than His friend?** No. Each relationship is best for the one in it (CC Madhya 8.83); Brahma, Siva and Uddhava are eternal servants (NOD 36). The order describes sweetness of taste, not the worth of a devotee.
2. **How do I know which rasa is mine?** It is revealed as love awakens; attraction to a story is not the awakening of eternal attachment (CC Madhya 23.45 purport). Now we serve in the servant's mood (8.71–73).
3. **Why is conjugal love called the highest?** Because it holds the qualities of all the others, as earth holds the qualities of sky, air, fire and water (8.85–88), and in it "Love of Godhead lacks nothing" (8.79 purport).
4. **Why did Rupa Gosvami describe conjugal love so briefly?** Because materialistic people cannot understand it and it appears mysterious to them (NOD 44). We do the same in public.
5. **What should I do about the higher rasas?** What Uddhava did: honour them and pray for the dust of those devotees' feet, without imitating them (CC Madhya 8.80; Madhurya-kadambini 7.2b).

to obtain a particle of the gopis' foot-dust (Madhurya-kadambini 7.2b; SB 10.47).
