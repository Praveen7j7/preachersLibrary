**About the source.** The *Brahma-vaivarta Purana* is one of the eighteen major Puranas, but it is **not a book of Srila Prabhupada**. This page uses it for the narrative, in the English translation of Rajendra Nath Sen (*The Brahma-vaivarta Purana*, Sacred Books of the Hindus vol. XXIV, Allahabad 1920; a public-domain text, kept in `research/books/`). The story is told as Sen's translation gives it, without adding or leaving out (head, 30 Oct 2026); its words are labelled "the Purana says (Sen)". Every principle on this page is taken from Srila Prabhupada's books, and those are labelled as his.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level
BSS members are preparing for brahmacari life. In an ashram, tulasi is served every day, and a brahmacari meets questions about association with women, about the fall of a devotee, and about what the Lord does with a sincere servant who is passing through a difficult birth. This story touches all three, and it comes from the tradition's own narrative literature. Used carefully, it teaches clearly: a servant of Krishna is kept by Krishna, a person who remembers the Lord at the end is carried by that remembrance, and tulasi is a living servant of the Lord who deserves our daily care.

## C. Central message
In the *Brahma-vaivarta Purana*, a devotee who is cursed to take birth as a demon still remembers the Lord and returns to Him. The girl who prayed for years to attain Krishna is given the form of tulasi, the plant the Lord keeps at His feet. In Srila Prabhupada's teaching the same truths are plain: the Lord does not lose His servant, remembrance of Krishna at the end of life carries the devotee to Him, and tulasi is to be served every day with love.

## D. Learning outcomes
By the end, participants can:
1. Tell the story in order as Sen's translation gives it, saying which words come from the *Brahma-vaivarta Purana* (Sen 1920) and which principles come from Srila Prabhupada's books.
2. Say why the Lord, according to Srila Prabhupada's books, does not lose a servant who is passing through a curse (SB 3.16.26, 3.16.31).
3. Explain what BG 8.5 teaches about the end of life and how it applies to Sankhacuda in the story.
4. Describe how to serve tulasi each day (SB 3.15.19 purport) and why (BG 9.26, SB 2.3.23).
5. Answer honestly when someone asks about the harder parts of the story: quote what Sen's text says, and say what Srila Prabhupada's purports say, without making up details.

## E. Scriptural foundation
Use these in the talk: BG 8.5, SB 3.16.26, 3.16.29 and 3.16.31, SB 3.15.19, BG 9.26. The others are for questions.

**BG 8.5**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/8/5/
> "And whoever, at the end of his life, quits his body remembering Me alone at once attains My nature. Of this there is no doubt."
- **Context:** Krishna is answering Arjuna's question about how the Lord is known at the time of death.
- **Teaching:** Whoever leaves the body remembering Krishna attains His nature.
- **Purport point:** The purport says that remembrance is not possible for a person who has not practised Krishna consciousness in devotional service, so one should practise from the beginning of life, chanting the maha-mantra constantly, tolerating the impediments.
- **Application (preacher's):** This is the principle behind the end of Sankhacuda in the story. See bss-53 for more on remembrance at death.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 3.16.26**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/16/26/
> "…they will fall to a birth in a demoniac family. But they will be firmly united with Me in thought through mental concentration intensified by anger, and they will return to My presence shortly."
- **Context:** The Lord is speaking about Jaya and Vijaya, His doorkeepers, who had been cursed by the four Kumara sages.
- **Teaching:** The Lord says the punishment was ordained by Him, that the two will be born in a demoniac family, and that they will return to Him.
- **Purport point:** The purport says that the punishment inflicted by the sages was conceived by the Lord Himself, and that without His sanction nothing can happen.
- **Application (preacher's):** This is a parallel from Srila Prabhupada's books to the Purana's account of Sudama, who is cursed and sent as a demon, and who comes back. The cause of the curse and the details are different.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 3.16.29**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/16/29/
> "Though I am capable of nullifying the brāhmaṇas’ curse, I would not do so. On the contrary, it has My approval."
- **Purport point:** The purport says that all the incidents that took place had the approval of the Lord.
- **Use:** in the talk, with SB 3.16.26.

**SB 3.16.31**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/16/31/
> "…within a very short time return to Me."
- **Teaching:** The Lord assures Jaya and Vijaya that they will return to Him very soon.
- **Use:** in the talk, together with SB 3.16.26.

**SB 3.15.19**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/15/19/
> "…for tulasī is given special preference by the Lord, who garlands Himself with tulasī leaves."
- **Context:** The four Kumaras have entered Vaikuntha and are seeing its flowers and trees.
- **Teaching:** The flowers of Vaikuntha, though fragrant, are conscious of the austerities performed by tulasi, because the Lord gives her special preference.
- **Purport point:** The purport says that devotees are recommended to water the tulasi tree every day and to collect the leaves to worship the Lord. It also says there is no envy among the devotees in the spiritual world, even among the flowers.
- **Use:** in the talk. This is the verse for tulasi seva.

**SB 2.3.23**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/2/3/23/
> "…the person who has never experienced the aroma of the tulasī leaves from the lotus feet of the Lord is also a dead body, although breathing."
- **Teaching:** The verse says that a person who has never received the dust of a pure devotee's feet, and never smelled the tulasi at the Lord's feet, is as if dead while breathing.
- **Purport point:** "According to Śrīla Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura, the breathing dead body is a ghost." The purport says that the first condition of devotional service is to be a servant of a pure devotee.
- **Use:** for questions and for the closing, read as the purport reads it.

**BG 9.26**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/9/26/
> "If one offers Me with love and devotion a leaf, a flower, a fruit or water, I will accept it."
- **Purport point:** The purport says that the process is so easy that even the poorest person can attempt it, that Krishna wants only loving service, and that bhakti is the only means to approach Him. It also says that meat, fish and eggs should not be offered, because the Lord has asked for leaf, flower, fruit and water.
- **Use:** in the talk, for the offering of tulasi leaves.

**SB 5.5.26**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/5/26/
- **Teaching:** The verse says that we should not envy any living being and should offer respect to all (see bss-07, where it is quoted in full).
- **Use:** background.

**SB 3.31.42**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/31/42/
> "A woman, therefore, should consider her husband, her house and her children to be the arrangement of the external energy of the Lord for her death, just as the sweet singing of the hunter is death for the deer."
- **Purport point:** "In these instructions of Lord Kapiladeva it is explained that not only is woman the gateway to hell for man, but man is also the gateway to hell for woman. It is a question of attachment." It says that when the attachment is transferred to Krishna, both become Krishna conscious. See bss-52 for the whole chapter (SB 3.31.33-42, where Kapiladeva speaks of woman as "the gateway to hell" for a man).
- **Use:** in K.3, as Srila Prabhupada's own words on the subject the Purana's conversation raises; read next to Tulsi's words (Sen p. 132), not in place of them.

**SB 10.34.25 and 10.34.31**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/10/34/25/ · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/10/34/31/ (BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples)
- **Teaching:** These verses speak of a different Sankhacuda, a servant of Kuvera, who stole women at Vrindavana and was killed by Krishna, who took his jewel.
- **Use:** only to tell the audience that this is another person (see K.1).

## F. Philosophical explanation
**Principle.** Three principles are taught by Srila Prabhupada and illustrated by the story. First, the Lord is in control, and nothing happens without His sanction (SB 3.16.26 purport). A curse, a battle and a birth as a demon do not take a servant out of His hands. Second, what a person remembers at the end of life decides where he goes (BG 8.5). Third, tulasi is a devotee of the Lord, given special preference by Him (SB 3.15.19), and serving her every day is part of devotional service.

**Why it matters.** A brahmacari may be tempted to think that his faults, his past, or the circumstances he is born into, put him outside the Lord's care. The story, like the story of Jaya and Vijaya, says that a servant is kept by the Lord even in a difficult birth. A brahmacari may also wonder what his daily service means. Watering tulasi may look small. SB 3.15.19 and SB 2.3.23 show that the Lord and His devotees value her greatly.

**Connections.** This page connects to bss-53 and bss-05 on remembering Krishna at death and not falling by attachment, to bss-07 on association, and to bss-12 on how Haridasa Thakura lived with tulasi.

**Consequences.** The story is told as Sen's translation gives it, including the conversation about women, the married life and the Lord's approach to Tulsi, each labelled as the Purana's words. The principles are Srila Prabhupada's, labelled as his. Nothing is added from our own understanding.

**Common misunderstandings.**
1. "The *Brahma-vaivarta Purana* is Srila Prabhupada's teaching." It is not. He does quote Puranas on occasion, but the details here are in Sen's translation, not in his books.
2. "Tulsi's words about women are Srila Prabhupada's words." They are the Purana's words in Sen's translation (p. 132), and in the same passage Sankhacuda answers that "a part of your statement is true and a part, not true" (p. 133). Srila Prabhupada's own words on the subject are in the purport to SB 3.31.42: "not only is woman the gateway to hell for man, but man is also the gateway to hell for woman. It is a question of attachment."
3. "The Lord does wrong in the story." Read what the Purana says (Sen p. 147, table above): Tulsi curses Him, and He says "I, too, by your curse, will turn into a stone." How our acaryas explain it is not in Srila Prabhupada's books that I have read (K.4, requires source review).
4. "Sankhacuda here is the demon Krishna kills in Vrindavana." No. That is another person (SB 10.34.25-31). See K.1.

**How to pitch it.** Tell the story as the Purana tells it, in Sen's words, and say each time whose words they are. Give the lessons from Srila Prabhupada's books and let the story illustrate them.

## H. Opening and engagement
**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes).**
1. Begin with a short prayer or one round of kirtana. Say the title once.
2. Say: "Today's story comes from the *Brahma-vaivarta Purana*. It is a respected Purana, but it is not one of Srila Prabhupada's books. So we will do two things. We will tell the story as the translator Rajendra Nath Sen gives it, and we will take our teachings only from what Srila Prabhupada's books say."
3. Say: "Some parts of the story speak of marriage and of women in strong words. I will read them as the translator gives them, and I will say each time that they are the Purana's words in Sen's translation, not Srila Prabhupada's. Where Srila Prabhupada's purports speak on the same subject, I will read his words too."
4. Say: "Let us begin with a question."

**H.2 Opening questions.**
1. "Who looks after the tulasi plant in your temple or home? What do they do each day?" (Take two or three answers. Thank each speaker.)
2. "Have you ever been given a task you did not want, and later saw that it helped your service?" (Take two answers; no comment.)
3. "What do you think a person should remember at the end of life?" (This leads to BG 8.5.)

## I. Core teaching points
**I.1 A servant of the Lord is not lost (SB 3.16.26, 3.16.31; Sen pp. 136-137).** In the Purana, Sankhacuda was a servant of Krishna in Goloka named Sudama. He was cursed to take birth as a demon, and the Lord Himself says he is devoted to Him. Srila Prabhupada's books give the clearest statement of the same principle in the story of Jaya and Vijaya: the Lord says that their punishment was ordained by Him, that they will return shortly (SB 3.16.26), and that He could cancel the curse but will not, because it has His approval (SB 3.16.29). The two stories have different causes. Do not mix their details. The lesson is the same: a devotee in a hard birth is still the Lord's, and the Lord arranges his return. Transition: what keeps a person on the path?

**I.2 The aim of tapasya (Sen pp. 130-131; SB 3.15.19).** The Purana says that Tulsi performed severe austerities in the forest and asked Brahma for Krishna. Do not present her austerities as an example for us to copy. Srila Prabhupada's books give a balanced rule (see bss-04 and bss-49, which cover BG 6.16-17). What the story does show is that the **aim** of her tapasya was to reach Krishna. SB 3.15.19 says that the flowers of Vaikuntha are conscious of the austerities performed by tulasi. A brahmacari's tapasya has the same aim: it is a means to serve Krishna, and it has no value when it is only a show. Transition: how does a person speak and act with care?

**I.3 The conversation (Sen pp. 132-134; SB 3.31.42 purport).** Read the conversation from the table in E, in Sen's words. Tulsi asks who he is and says: "A good man, if he sees a virtuous woman alone, does not talk to her"; "The Śrutis hold that only a vile man desires a woman. For she is sweet at first, but afterwards proves fatal to him"; "She is an obstacle to the path of devotion, a hindrance to emancipation, an impediment to Hari's faith." Sankhacuda answers: "A part of your statement is true and a part, not true. God has divided the fair sex into two classes, viz., those who are chaste and those who may be easily seduced. The chaste woman is praise-worthy; the unchaste one should be condemned", and he names Laksmi, Sarasvati, Durga, Sita and Radha. He tells her who he is and says: "A virtuous man does not court the wife of another in public or in private." Tulsi: "A man subdued by a woman is unholy and contemptible." Then read Srila Prabhupada's own words on this subject, the purport to SB 3.31.42: "not only is woman the gateway to hell for man, but man is also the gateway to hell for woman. It is a question of attachment." The practical rules for a brahmacari's dealings with women are in bss-16 and bss-30 onward (BIKC). Transition: what happens at the end of the story?

**I.4 Remembering Hari at the end (BG 8.5; Sen pp. 145-146).** When Siva casts the spear, the demon, in the Purana's words, "wisely laid aside his bow, adopted a posture of meditation and remembered Hari". The Purana then says he goes to Goloka. BG 8.5 says that whoever quits the body remembering Krishna attains His nature, and the purport says this is only possible for a person who has practised Krishna consciousness. So the teaching for us is the practice, not the story: chant now, so that we remember Krishna then. Do not say that anyone is sure to go back at death without practice. Transition: what became of Tulsi?

**I.5 Tulasi, the servant at the Lord's feet (Sen pp. 146-150; SB 3.15.19; SB 2.3.23; BG 9.26).** In the Purana, the Lord consoles Tulsi in her grief and tells her that she will become the plant tulasi, whose leaf pleases Him more than "a thousand jars full of honey". Srila Prabhupada's books say the same in their own terms. The Lord garlands Himself with tulasi (SB 3.15.19). A person who has never smelled tulasi from the Lord's feet has not tasted spiritual life (SB 2.3.23). The Lord accepts a leaf offered with love (BG 9.26). **What to do:** the purport of SB 3.15.19 says to water the tulasi every day and collect her leaves to worship the Lord. In an ashram this is a daily duty. Do it with care and respect. Ending: ask the audience who will water tulasi tomorrow and how.

## J. Stories and illustrations
Tell the story as Sen's text tells it, and say "the Purana says". Do not add details and do not leave events out.

**J.1 Part 1: Tulsi and Sankhacuda (Sen pp. 130-135).** Tulsi, daughter of Dharmadhwaja, performs austerity in the forest for a very long time. Brahma appears and offers a boon. She tells him her past: she was a cowherd girl of Goloka, a companion of Radha; once she "fainted through excess of enjoyments afforded to me by the society of Govinda in the sphere of the Rasa", Radha saw her and "blinded with fury" cursed her to take birth as a human being; Govinda told her she would attain Narayana as her husband in India. She asks for Krishna. Brahma tells her that Sudama, a cowherd of Goloka, "was impatient with lust at your sight" but did not approach her "for fear of Rādhikā", that he is now born as Sankhacuda, that she will first be married to him, and that she will later become a sacred plant "dearer to Visnu than his life". Sankhacuda, having been granted a boon by Brahma, comes to the same hermitage. Tulsi asks him who he is and speaks the words about women quoted in I.3; he answers as quoted there and tells her plainly who he is. Tulsi says she "tested your knowledge simply"; Brahma comes and tells them to marry by the Gandharva rites, and they marry. The Purana then says that "the demon Śankha-chuda began to enjoy the society of his love", describes their married life in many places, and says: "Both of them remained unsated. Their desire increased like fire fed with ghee." He then rules "the gods, the demons, the Raksasas" and deprives the gods of their rights of worship.
**Devotional lesson:** both of them had a past with the Lord and both are given a future with Him. The Purana's own line, "their desire increased like fire fed with ghee", is the teaching Srila Prabhupada gives: "As supplying butter to a fire does not diminish the fire but instead increases it more and more, the endeavor to stop lusty desires by continual enjoyment can never be successful" (SB 9.19.14); and, quoting the *Manu-smṛti*, "lust cannot be satisfied by any amount of sense enjoyment, just as fire is never extinguished by a constant supply of fuel" (BG 3.39, purport).

**J.2 Part 2: the end of Sankhacuda (Sen pp. 137-146).** Sankhacuda rules for a long time and takes away the rights and positions of the gods. The gods go to Siva and Brahma, who take them to Visnu. Visnu tells them that Sankhacuda is a servant of His, cursed to take birth as a demon, and gives Siva a spear. In the war, when Sankhacuda sees Siva he bows to him. Visnu comes as an old brahmana and begs Sankhacuda's protective amulet, which he gives "at once". The Purana then says: "Hari, through the powers of his illusion, took the form of Śankha-chuda, approached Tulsi and violated her chastity" (told in J.3). Siva casts the spear. "The demon wisely laid aside his bow, adopted a posture of meditation and remembered Hari", and he is reduced to ashes. The Purana says he takes the form of a cowherd with a flute and goes to Goloka, where Radha and Krishna take him on their laps.
**Devotional lesson:** a devotee who is generous to the end and remembers Hari reaches the Lord (BG 8.5).

**J.3 Part 3: Tulsi becomes tulasi (Sen pp. 145-148).** The Purana says (Sen): Hari, "in order to accomplish the design of the gods, took the form of Śankha-chuda", took the demon's amulet, came to Tulsi's house with the announcement "may victory crown the King", and told her a story of the battle. "Hari cohabited with Tulsi but owing to the transgression of the former method of sexual intercourse she became suspicious and said, 'O Lord of dissemblers, who are you? You have violated my chastity. I must, therefore, curse you, whoever you are.'" Hari showed His own form. Tulsi said: "Lord, you are unkind to me and possess a heart of stone. You have fraudulently violated me and killed my husband. As you are hard-hearted like a stone, you will be worshipped in the world in the form of a stone. Those who call you merciful are deluded." Then she fell at His feet and wept. Hari consoled her: she had prayed for Him for a long time, and Sankhacuda had obtained her by his own prayer; "I should now give you the fruits of your own austerities." He told her to quit her body and take a celestial form; her body became the river Gandaki and her hair the plant tulasi; "Hari will not be so much pleased with the gift of a thousand jars full of honey as with the offer of a Tulsi leaf"; and "I, too, by your curse, will turn into a stone on the coast of the Gandaki."
**Note for the preacher:** tell it as the Purana does, in Sen's words, and say whose words they are. The Purana's own explanation is the Lord's words above (the design of the gods; the fruit of her austerities; Brahma's earlier promise, p. 131, that she would attain Narayana). How our acaryas explain the episode is **requires source review** (see K.4).
**Devotional lesson:** tulasi is the Lord's own servant, and He accepts her curse. A devotee's love is so strong that even her anger is received by Him.

**J.4 Invented illustration (labelled).** A brahmacari new to the ashram is told to water tulasi every morning. For a week he does it fast, because it seems minor. One day the temple president asks him to read SB 3.15.19 aloud first. The next morning he waters more slowly and sees that the leaves are fresh. The point: the Lord garlands Himself with tulasi (SB 3.15.19), so the small service is a service to a dear servant of the Lord.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections
1. **"Is this the Sankhacuda that Krishna kills in Vrindavana?"** No. In SB 10.34.25-31 Sankhacuda is a servant of Kuvera who tries to carry off women and is killed by Krishna, who takes his jewel. The Sankhacuda of the *Brahma-vaivarta Purana* is a different person (a cursed servant from Goloka).
2. **"Why was Sudama cursed?"** Give the Purana's own account (Sen pp. 130, 136-137): Tulsi, a cowherd girl of Goloka, fainted in the rasa dance "through excess of enjoyments afforded to me by the society of Govinda"; Radha, "blinded with fury", cursed her to be born as a human being; Sudama, "impatient with lust at your sight", was cursed by Radha too and became a demon. Visnu tells Brahma: "He has become a demon by Radha's curse." How our acaryas explain these Goloka pastimes is **requires source review**; say so if asked.
3. **"Does the Purana say women are bad?"** Read what it says (Sen pp. 132-133). Tulsi says: "only a vile man desires a woman. For she is sweet at first, but afterwards proves fatal to him"; "She is an obstacle to the path of devotion". Sankhacuda answers: "A part of your statement is true and a part, not true. God has divided the fair sex into two classes, viz., those who are chaste and those who may be easily seduced. The chaste woman is praise-worthy; the unchaste one should be condemned." Then read Srila Prabhupada's purport to SB 3.31.42: "not only is woman the gateway to hell for man, but man is also the gateway to hell for woman. It is a question of attachment."
4. **"The Lord appears to deceive Tulsi. How can this be?"** Read the Purana's account as it stands (Sen p. 147, J.3), with the Lord's own words in it: He acted "in order to accomplish the design of the gods"; "I should now give you the fruits of your own austerities"; "I, too, by your curse, will turn into a stone." Say that Srila Prabhupada's books do not tell this episode, and do not add an explanation of your own. How our acaryas explain it: ask a senior devotee (**Requires source review**).
5. **"Do we need to worship salagrama stones as the Purana says?"** This page does not give rules about salagrama. **Requires source review.** Ask your temple's senior priest or guru.
6. **"Is it true that a demon who remembers Hari at death goes to Goloka?"** BG 8.5 teaches that whoever quits the body remembering Krishna attains His nature. Whether a particular person did so is not for us to judge. We practise now.
7. **"How do I serve tulasi?"** Water the tulasi every day and collect the leaves to offer to the Lord (SB 3.15.19 purport). Ask your temple which days and hours are kept, and who may pick leaves.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary
The *Brahma-vaivarta Purana*, in the translation of Rajendra Nath Sen (1920), tells of a girl named Tulsi who performed long austerities in a forest to attain Krishna. She had been a cowherd girl of Goloka, cursed by Radha to be born as a human being. Brahma promised her that she would first marry Sankhacuda and that later she would become a sacred plant "dearer to Visnu than his life". Sankhacuda was a servant of Krishna in Goloka named Sudama, cursed by Radha to be born as a demon. When he came to her, Tulsi said: "A good man, if he sees a virtuous woman alone, does not talk to her", and spoke strongly about women: "only a vile man desires a woman. For she is sweet at first, but afterwards proves fatal to him." Sankhacuda answered: "A part of your statement is true and a part, not true"; chaste women such as Sita and Radha "are worthy of praise". He told her plainly who he was, and by Brahma's order they were married. The Purana says of their married life: "Both of them remained unsated. Their desire increased like fire fed with ghee." These are the Purana's words in Sen's translation, not Srila Prabhupada's. Srila Prabhupada's own words on the subject are in his purport to SB 3.31.42: "not only is woman the gateway to hell for man, but man is also the gateway to hell for woman. It is a question of attachment."
Later, Sankhacuda took away the gods' rights, and Siva, with a spear given by Visnu, fought him. Visnu, as an old brahmana, begged his protective amulet, and he gave it. The Purana says that at the end the demon "laid aside his bow, adopted a posture of meditation and remembered Hari", and that he went to Goloka. Srila Prabhupada teaches that whoever leaves the body remembering Krishna attains Him (BG 8.5), and that the Lord keeps His servants, even when they are cursed (SB 3.16.26).
The Purana says that Hari then took Sankhacuda's form, went to Tulsi and "violated her chastity"; when she knew Him, she cursed Him: "As you are hard-hearted like a stone, you will be worshipped in the world in the form of a stone." He consoled her, said "I should now give you the fruits of your own austerities", made her hair into the plant tulasi and her body into the river Gandaki, and said that He Himself, by her curse, would become a stone on its bank. Srila Prabhupada's books do not tell this episode; how our acaryas explain it, ask your teacher. Srila Prabhupada teaches that the Lord garlands Himself with tulasi (SB 3.15.19) and that He accepts a leaf offered with love (BG 9.26). The purport of SB 3.15.19 says that devotees should water tulasi every day and collect her leaves to worship the Lord.

## S2. Key ideas
- The story comes from a Purana translated by Sen in 1920. It is not Srila Prabhupada's book. The principles come from his books.
- The Lord keeps His servant through a curse (SB 3.16.26).
- Whoever remembers Krishna at the end attains Him (BG 8.5).
- Tulasi is dear to the Lord, and we serve her every day (SB 3.15.19).
- The Purana's words about women are Tulsi's words in Sen's translation, answered in the same text by Sankhacuda; Srila Prabhupada's own words are in the SB 3.31.42 purport.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **BG 8.5**: Whoever quits the body remembering Krishna alone attains His nature. *For me:* I chant daily so that I remember Him.
- **SB 3.15.19**: Tulasi is given special preference by the Lord. *For me:* I water tulasi every day.
- **BG 9.26**: The Lord accepts a leaf, a flower, a fruit or water offered with love. *For me:* a small offering with love pleases Him.

## S4. Words to know
- **Purana:** one of the old books of stories and teachings that follow the Vedas.
- **Tulasi:** the sacred basil plant that the Lord loves, used in His worship.
- **Goloka:** Krishna's own abode in the spiritual world.
- **Demon:** a being who is against the Lord.
- **Curse:** a statement by which a person is made to suffer a result.
- **Amulet:** a small protective object that is worn.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *Is this story in Srila Prabhupada's books?* No. It is from the *Brahma-vaivarta Purana* in Sen's translation. We take the principles from Srila Prabhupada's books.
2. *What does this Purana say about women?* Tulsi says to Sankhacuda: "only a vile man desires a woman. For she is sweet at first, but afterwards proves fatal to him"; "She is an obstacle to the path of devotion." Sankhacuda answers: "A part of your statement is true and a part, not true"; chaste women are "worthy of praise", unchaste women "should be condemned" (Sen pp. 132-133). Srila Prabhupada writes: "not only is woman the gateway to hell for man, but man is also the gateway to hell for woman. It is a question of attachment" (SB 3.31.42 purport).
3. *Is this Sankhacuda the one Krishna kills at Vrindavana?* No. That one was a servant of Kuvera (SB 10.34.25-31).
4. *How do I serve tulasi?* Water her every day and collect leaves to offer to the Lord (SB 3.15.19 purport).
5. *What if I do not understand a part of the story, such as the Lord taking Sankhacuda's form?* Read what the Purana says (Sen p. 147), say that it is the Purana's account and not in Srila Prabhupada's books, and ask a senior devotee. It is honest to say "I will find out".
