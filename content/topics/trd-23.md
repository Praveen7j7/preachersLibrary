## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on dps-12 to dps-18: those pages give the *stages* of bhakti from faith to love. This page gives the *structure*
of the whole philosophy inside which those stages sit. Builds on sps-03 and sps-32 (personal God, and the three kinds of
transcendentalists): their conclusions are here placed under "sambandha".

A temple resident preaches daily. He answers "who is God?", "what should I do?" and "what is the goal?" many times a week.
Lord Caitanya gave Sanatana Gosvami one frame that holds every such answer: the Vedic literature teaches our relationship
with Krishna (sambandha), the activity in that relationship (abhidheya) and the goal, love of God (prayojana)
(CC Madhya 20.124). A preacher who carries this map never loses the thread of an argument, never mistakes the means for the
goal, and can read Jiva Gosvami's Sandarbhas, which are built on it. No lower batch studies the map as a subject.

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| VL1 | Not covered as a subject; the three questions ("who am I, what should I do, where am I going") appear in plain words |
| VL2 | The three questions as the reason to study the Gita: BG 15.15, "by all the Vedas, I am to be known" |
| VL3 | The map as a reading guide for the Gita and Bhagavatam: which chapter answers which question |
| SPS | The three terms named and defined from CC Madhya 20.124–125; linked to sps-03 and sps-32 |
| DPS | The map placed beside the stages of bhakti (dps-12 to dps-18): prayojana is prema, the stages are the road |
| **TRD** (here) | The full teaching to Sanatana, Sarvabhauma and the Varanasi sannyasis; the same map in the catuh-sloki; the Sandarbhas as the systematic working out of the map; use in preaching |
| BSS | The map as the brahmacari's safeguard against speculation and against mistaking rules for the goal |

**Not done here:** the content of each Sandarbha (trd-24 to trd-29); the stages of bhakti (dps-12 to dps-18); the
answer to Mayavada in detail (trd-35); the Vedanta-sutra and Govinda-bhasya (trd-40).

## C. Central message

All Vedic literature teaches three things and nothing else: our eternal relationship with Krishna (sambandha), devotional
service as the activity in that relationship (abhidheya), and love of Krishna as the goal (prayojana) (CC Madhya 20.124).
"Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa's service and love of Kṛṣṇa are the three great riches of life" (CC Madhya 20.143). A preacher who knows
where each verse sits on this map can answer any question without losing Krishna from the centre.

## D. Learning outcomes

Residents should be able to:
1. Define sambandha, abhidheya and prayojana in Lord Caitanya's own words (CC Madhya 20.124–125) and in Srila Prabhupada's
   purport (CC Adi 7.146).
2. Explain the setting: Sanatana Gosvami's questions (CC Madhya 20.102–103) and Krishna's three helpers for the forgetful
   soul, scripture, guru and the Supersoul (CC Madhya 20.122–123).
3. Show that the same map is given to a logician (Sarvabhauma, CC Madhya 6.178), to Mayavadi sannyasis (CC Adi 7.146) and
   in the catuh-sloki of the Bhagavatam (CC Madhya 25.102–103, 25.131).
4. Place any verse, question or objection they meet in preaching under one of the three headings and say why.
5. Name the six Sandarbhas and say which heading each serves, so the next six sessions have a frame.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**CC Madhya 20.102–103**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/20/102/
> 20.102: "Who am I? Why do the threefold miseries always give me trouble? If I do not know this, how can I be benefited?"
> 20.103: "Actually I do not know how to inquire about the goal of life and the process for obtaining it. Being merciful upon me, please explain all these truths."
- **Context:** Sanatana Gosvami, the former minister of Nawab Hussain Shah, has surrendered to Lord Caitanya at Varanasi
  and asks the questions that open the Lord's long teaching (CC Madhya 20).
- **Teaching:** The three questions a human being must ask: who am I (sambandha), what is the process (abhidheya), what is
  the goal (prayojana). Sanatana asks them in this order.
- **Purport point (20.102):** The threefold miseries come from the body and mind, from other living beings and from nature.
  "There is danger in every step of life."
- **Use:** in the talk, as the opening of the whole map.

**CC Madhya 20.122–123**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/20/122/
> 20.122: "The conditioned soul cannot revive his Kṛṣṇa consciousness by his own effort. But out of causeless mercy, Lord Kṛṣṇa compiled the Vedic literature and its supplements, the Purāṇas."
> 20.123: "The forgetful conditioned soul is educated by Kṛṣṇa through the Vedic literatures, the realized spiritual master and the Supersoul."
- **Teaching:** The map is given by Krishna, not found by us. Our own effort cannot revive Krishna consciousness.
- **Purport point (20.123):** "Thus a conditioned soul is always helped by the Supreme Personality of Godhead in three ways — by the scriptures, the spiritual master and the Supersoul within the heart." Krishna as Vyasadeva compiles the Vedas;
  Krishna appears externally as the spiritual master; Krishna sits in the heart (BG 18.61 quoted).
- **Application (preacher's):** the preacher is one of the three helpers at work. He gives the map; he did not make it.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**CC Madhya 20.124–125**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/20/124/
> 20.124: "The Vedic literatures give information about the living entity's eternal relationship with Kṛṣṇa, which is called sambandha. The living entity's understanding of this relationship and his acting accordingly is called abhidheya. Returning home, back to Godhead, is the ultimate goal of life and is called prayojana."
> 20.125: "Devotional service, or sense activity for the satisfaction of the Lord, is called abhidheya because it can develop one's original love of Godhead, which is the goal of life."
- **Teaching:** The three terms defined by Lord Caitanya Himself. Note that abhidheya is defined twice: as understanding
  the relationship and acting in it (20.124), and as devotional service, sense activity for the Lord's satisfaction (20.125).
- **Purport point (20.125):** Krishna "wants His sons to return home, back to Godhead; therefore He personally comes to deliver Vedic literatures like the Bhagavad-gītā." Through study of the Vedic literatures "one comes to Kṛṣṇa consciousness. Thus one engages in devotional service, called abhidheya. When the living entity actually develops love of Godhead, he has reached the ultimate goal, prayojana."
- **Use:** in the talk. These two verses are the central reference; have them on a slide.

**CC Madhya 20.143–146**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/20/143/
> 20.143: "In the Vedic literatures, Kṛṣṇa is the central point of attraction, and His service is our activity. To attain the platform of love of Kṛṣṇa is life's ultimate goal. Therefore Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa's service and love of Kṛṣṇa are the three great riches of life."
> 20.144: "In all revealed scriptures, beginning with the Vedas, the central point of attraction is Kṛṣṇa. When complete knowledge of Him is realized, the bondage of māyā, the illusory energy, is automatically broken."
> 20.146: "When one accepts the Vedic literature by interpretation or even by dictionary meaning, directly or indirectly the ultimate declaration of Vedic knowledge points to Lord Kṛṣṇa."
- **Teaching:** The three subjects restated as three riches, with Krishna at the centre of each. The Vedas point to Krishna
  whether read directly or indirectly.
- **Use:** in the talk, as the summary line residents memorise.

**CC Adi 7.146**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/adi/7/146/
> "One's relationship with the Supreme Personality of Godhead, activities in terms of that relationship, and the ultimate goal of life [to develop love of God] — these three subjects are explained in every aphorism of the Vedānta-sūtra, for they form the culmination of the entire Vedānta philosophy."
- **Context:** Lord Caitanya speaking to Prakasananda Sarasvati and the Mayavadi sannyasis of Varanasi.
- **Purport point:** Srila Prabhupada defines the terms. Sambandha-jnana is knowledge of four items: "Knowledge of these four items — namely oneself, the universe, God, and their internal relationship — is called sambandha-jñāna, or the knowledge of one's relationship." Then: "When one's relationship with the Supreme Lord is established, the next program is to act in that relationship. This is called abhidheya, or activity in relationship with the Lord." Then prayojana-siddhi, love of Godhead, "the fulfillment of his human mission." The purport ends: "Therefore one who does not understand the Vedānta-sūtra in terms of these principles is simply wasting his time."
- **Use:** in the talk. This is the purport that gives the preacher his definitions.

**CC Madhya 6.178**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/6/178/
> "The Supreme Personality of Godhead is the central point of all relationships, acting in devotional service to Him is one's real occupation, and the attainment of love of Godhead is the ultimate goal of life. These three subject matters are described in the Vedic literature."
- **Context:** Lord Caitanya teaching Sarvabhauma Bhattacarya, the greatest logician of the day, the meaning of Vedanta.
- **Purport point:** "The actual purpose in reading the Vedas is to learn how to become a devotee of the Supreme Lord." The purport adds that varnasrama is "very difficult to establish in this age; therefore Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu advises that we not worry about the Vedic system of varṇāśrama-dharma. Rather, we should take directly to the chanting of the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra and simply hear about the Supreme Personality of Godhead from pure devotees."
- **Use:** in the talk: the same map given to a scholar.

**BG 15.15**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/15/15/
> "I am seated in everyone's heart, and from Me come remembrance, knowledge and forgetfulness. By all the Vedas, I am to be known. Indeed, I am the compiler of Vedānta, and I am the knower of the Vedas."
- **Teaching:** Krishna is the object of all the Vedas, their compiler (as Vyasadeva) and their knower. This is the verse
  the purports quote (CC Madhya 6.178, 20.125) for the whole map.
- **Purport point:** "The commentation on the Vedānta-sūtra by Vyāsadeva in the Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam gives the real understanding of Vedānta-sūtra."
- **Use:** in the talk and the handout.

**CC Madhya 25.98, 25.100, 25.102–103, 25.131, 25.142**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/25/102/
> 25.102: "The essence of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam — our relationship with the Supreme Lord, our activities in that connection and the goal of life — is manifest in the four verses of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam known as the catuḥ-ślokī. Everything is explained in those verses."
> 25.103: "[Lord Kṛṣṇa says:] 'I am the center of all relationships. Knowledge of Me and the practical application of that knowledge is actual knowledge. Approaching Me for devotional service is called abhidheya."
> 25.131: "Thus one's relationship with the Lord, activities in devotional service, and the attainment of the highest goal of life, love of Godhead, are the subject matters of Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam."
- **Context:** Lord Caitanya to Prakasananda Sarasvati and his followers at Varanasi, after they have been won over.
- **Teaching:** Vyasadeva placed the Vedic conclusions in the Vedanta-sutra (25.98); the Bhagavatam explains the sutra
  (25.100, 25.142); its essence, the four verses Krishna spoke to Brahma (SB 2.9.33–36), is itself the map (25.102–103).
- **Purport point (25.103):** "Spiritual knowledge means fully understanding the Absolute Truth in three features — impersonal Brahman, localized Paramātmā and the all-powerful Supreme Personality of Godhead." Service to the Lord is the practical application; "The practice of devotional service to attain that goal of life is called abhidheya."
- **Use:** in the talk (second half) and as the bridge to trd-24.

**SB 1.1.2**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/1/2/
> "As soon as one attentively and submissively hears the message of Bhāgavatam, by this culture of knowledge the Supreme Lord is established within his heart."
- **Teaching:** The Bhagavatam "propounds the highest truth" and "is sufficient in itself for God realization." Jiva Gosvami
  quotes this verse at the opening of *Bhakti-sandarbha* to show how the map is received: by hearing.
- **Use:** in the talk (how the map reaches the heart).

***Bhakti-sandarbha* (Srila Jiva Gosvami), anuccheda 1**: text in `research/books/Jiva_Goswami_Sri_Bhakti_Sandarbha (OCR).txt`
(Desire Tree edition; the file does not name the translator, so say "a published English translation" and confirm the edition
before printing). Read against the file, 30 Oct 2026.
- The book opens by summarising the four Sandarbhas before it as sambandha: "By the previous four sandarbhas the relationship between Krsna and the living entities was explained." It lists what was established: the Absolute Truth "appearing in three features: Brahman, Paramatma, and Bhagavan. Of these Bhagavan was proved to be the highest"; the avataras; "Sri Krsna was proved to be the original form of Bhagavan"; and the jivas, "who are the Lord's marginal potency, who are spiritual in nature".
- It then turns to the other two subjects: because the soul's disease is aversion to the Lord, "that relationship is one of turning one's face of the Lord and it is characterised by service to the Lord" (abhidheya), and the final goal is "directly seeing the Lord" (prayojana). It gives an old story: "Hearing "There is a treasure (hidden) in your house", a poverty stricken man searched for the treasure and eventually found it." The scriptures tell us the treasure is there (sambandha), how to dig (abhidheya) and what we will hold (prayojana).
- **Use:** in the talk, to show that Jiva Gosvami built the six books on Lord Caitanya's map. The principle rests on CC Madhya 20.124–125.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** Vedic knowledge is one subject seen three ways. *Sambandha* is the relationship: who Krishna is, who I
   am, what this world is, and how the three are related (CC Adi 7.146 purport: "oneself, the universe, God, and their internal relationship"). *Abhidheya* is what is to be done in that relationship: devotional service, "sense activity for the satisfaction of the Lord" (CC Madhya 20.125). *Prayojana* is the goal: love of Krishna, "returning home, back to Godhead" (CC Madhya 20.124).
2. **Why the order matters.** One cannot act rightly in a relationship one does not know, and one cannot reach a goal one
   has not been shown. So the Vedas first tell us the truth (sambandha), then the means (abhidheya), then the fruit
   (prayojana). Sanatana asked in this order (CC Madhya 20.102–103), and the Lord answered in this order through CC Madhya
   20–25: the Absolute Truth and the jiva (20), the avataras and Krishna's supremacy (20), the process of bhakti (22), the
   stages up to prema (23), and the Bhagavatam as the proof (25).
3. **Krishna is the centre of all three.** This is what makes the map Gaudiya and not merely Vedantic in a general sense.
   "Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa's service and love of Kṛṣṇa are the three great riches of life" (CC Madhya 20.143). The relationship is with
   Krishna, the activity is Krishna's service, the goal is love of Krishna. Impersonalism removes Krishna from the centre and
   therefore misreads all three.
4. **Connections.**
   - The six Sandarbhas follow the map: *Tattva* (what evidence we use and that the Bhagavatam is the final word; trd-24),
     *Bhagavat* (Brahman, Paramatma, Bhagavan; trd-25), *Paramatma* (the Lord's energies and the jiva; trd-26) and *Krsna*
     (Krishna is the original Godhead; trd-27) are sambandha. *Bhakti* (trd-28) is abhidheya. *Priti* (trd-29) is prayojana.
     Jiva Gosvami says so himself at the opening of *Bhakti-sandarbha* (E).
   - The stages of bhakti (dps-12 to dps-18) are the road inside abhidheya, and their last stage, prema, is prayojana.
   - The three kinds of transcendentalists (sps-32) and the three features of the Absolute (trd-25) belong to sambandha.
5. **Practical consequences for a preacher.**
   - Every question a student asks belongs to one heading. "Is there a God?" and "who am I?" are sambandha. "Why chant?"
     and "why these rules?" are abhidheya. "What happens after liberation?" is prayojana. Naming the heading, silently or
     aloud, tells the preacher which verses to open.
   - Mistakes happen when headings are confused: treating rules (abhidheya) as the goal; treating liberation (a stage short
     of prayojana) as the end; teaching the goal (prema) to someone who has not accepted the relationship.
   - The map keeps the preacher humble: he is one of Krishna's three helpers (CC Madhya 20.123), delivering what Krishna
     compiled, not his own ideas.
6. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *"Sambandha means only knowing God."* It also includes knowing oneself, the world and the relationship (CC Adi 7.146
     purport). A preacher who teaches God without teaching the soul and the world leaves the student unable to act.
   - *"Abhidheya means following rules."* The rules serve "sense activity for the satisfaction of the Lord" (CC Madhya
     20.125). Rules without this aim are not abhidheya.
   - *"Prayojana is liberation."* Lord Caitanya names love of Godhead, not liberation, as the goal (CC Madhya 20.124, 6.178).
     Liberation is passed on the way (trd-29).
   - *"The Vedanta-sutra is for impersonalists."* Lord Caitanya says the three subjects "are explained in every aphorism of the Vedānta-sūtra" (CC Adi 7.146); the Bhagavatam is Vyasadeva's own commentary (CC Madhya 25.142).
7. **Pitch for TRD.** Residents know the verses; the gain here is seeing the frame. Spend time on CC Madhya 20.102–146 as one
   argument rather than on isolated verses, and keep returning to the question "which heading is this?".

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet the residents and say that today's session gives the one map on which every verse they know has a place.
2. Ask them to write, privately, in two or three sentences, what they say when a college student asks "what do you
   actually believe?" Give two minutes.
3. Say: Sanatana Gosvami, a learned minister, came to Lord Caitanya and asked three questions. Read CC Madhya 20.102–103.
4. Say that the Lord's answer to those three questions is the whole of Gaudiya philosophy, and that by the end of the
   session each of them will be able to give the same answer in three sentences.
5. Ask H.2 question 1 and take three answers.

**H.2 Opening questions**
1. When a student asks you "what do you believe?", where do you usually start, and where do you run out of order?
2. Sanatana asked "who am I?" before asking "what should I do?" Why does that order matter?
3. If you had to name the one subject of all the Vedic literature in one word, which word would you choose, and why?

## I. Core teaching points

1. **The map is given, not invented.** The conditioned soul "cannot revive his Kṛṣṇa consciousness by his own effort"
   (CC Madhya 20.122). Krishna gives the knowledge through scripture, guru and the Supersoul (20.123). *Example:* Sanatana
   did not reason his way to the answer; he asked (20.102–103). *Transition:* what did the Lord answer?
2. **Three subjects: sambandha, abhidheya, prayojana.** Relationship, activity in it, goal (CC Madhya 20.124–125).
   Prabhupada's four items of sambandha-jnana: oneself, the universe, God, their relationship (CC Adi 7.146 purport).
   *Example:* a traveller needs to know where he is, which road to take and where he is going; the three headings are those
   three. (Illustration, not a scriptural image.) *Transition:* who is at the centre of each?
3. **Krishna at the centre of all three.** "Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa's service and love of Kṛṣṇa are the three great riches of life"
   (CC Madhya 20.143). All scriptures point to Him, directly or indirectly (20.144, 20.146); "By all the Vedas, I am to be known" (BG 15.15). *Transition:* did the Lord give this map only to Sanatana?
4. **One map for every audience.** To the logician Sarvabhauma (CC Madhya 6.178), to the Mayavadi sannyasis (CC Adi 7.146)
   and to Sanatana, the Lord gave the same three subjects. *Transition:* where is the map in the Bhagavatam?
5. **The Bhagavatam is the map explained.** Vyasadeva wrote the Vedanta-sutra and explained it himself in the Bhagavatam
   (CC Madhya 25.98, 25.142). Its essence, the catuh-sloki, states the three subjects (25.102–103, 25.131). Hearing it
   establishes the Lord in the heart (SB 1.1.2). *Transition:* how did the Gosvamis organise this?
6. **The Sandarbhas are the map worked out.** Jiva Gosvami says the first four Sandarbhas explained the relationship
   (*Bhakti-sandarbha*, anuccheda 1); the fifth is service, the sixth is love. The next six sessions follow that order.
   *Transition:* how does a preacher use this tomorrow?
7. **Preach from the map.** Name the heading of every question; answer from that heading's verses; never leave Krishna out
   of the centre. The preacher is one of the three helpers Krishna uses (CC Madhya 20.123), so he speaks with confidence
   and without pride.

## J. Stories and illustrations

**Sanatana Gosvami asks the three questions** (CC Madhya 20.102–103, 20.122–125). In Varanasi, after surrendering to Lord
Caitanya, Sanatana Gosvami asks: "Who am I? Why do the threefold miseries always give me trouble? If I do not know this, how can I be benefited?" (20.102) and "Actually I do not know how to inquire about the goal of life and the process for obtaining it. Being merciful upon me, please explain all these truths." (20.103). The Lord answers that the conditioned soul cannot revive his Krishna consciousness by his own effort, that Krishna compiled the Vedic literature out of mercy (20.122) and educates the forgetful soul through scripture, guru and the Supersoul (20.123); then He defines sambandha, abhidheya and prayojana (20.124–125). *Devotional lesson:* the greatest scholar of his court asked as a beginner, and the whole of Gaudiya siddhanta was given in answer to his humility.

**Lord Caitanya and Sarvabhauma Bhattacarya** (CC Madhya 6.178). Teaching Vedanta to the chief logician of Puri, the Lord
states that the Supreme Personality of Godhead "is the central point of all relationships, acting in devotional service to Him is one's real occupation, and the attainment of love of Godhead is the ultimate goal of life." *Devotional lesson:* the map is not a simplification for beginners; it is what the Lord gave the most learned man He met.

**Lord Caitanya and the Mayavadi sannyasis of Varanasi** (CC Adi 7.146). To Prakasananda Sarasvati and his followers the Lord says the three subjects "are explained in every aphorism of the Vedānta-sūtra". *Devotional lesson:* the Vedanta-sutra, which impersonalists claim, is itself a book about relationship, service and love of God.

**The treasure in the house** (*Bhakti-sandarbha*, anuccheda 1; Jiva Gosvami calls it an ancient story). "Hearing "There is a treasure (hidden) in your house", a poverty stricken man searched for the treasure and eventually found it." *Devotional lesson:* the scriptures tell the poor soul that the treasure of love for Krishna is already his by relationship; service is the digging; the treasure found is prema. Jiva Gosvami adds that the story "is told to dispel weakness" of heart.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. *"Is this map in Srila Prabhupada's books, or only in the Gosvamis'?"* It is Lord Caitanya's own teaching in
   Caitanya-caritamrta (CC Madhya 20.124–125, 6.178; CC Adi 7.146), with Prabhupada's purports defining each term. The
   Gosvamis systematised it.
2. *"Why three subjects and not one? Isn't Krishna the only subject?"* Krishna is the centre of all three (CC Madhya
   20.143–144). The three headings are the three ways the one subject is taught: who He is to me, what I do for Him, what I
   attain in Him.
3. *"A Mayavadi says the Vedanta-sutra teaches oneness. How do I answer from the map?"* Lord Caitanya told the Varanasi
   sannyasis that every aphorism teaches relationship, activity and goal (CC Adi 7.146); Vyasadeva explained his own sutras
   in the Bhagavatam (CC Madhya 25.142), and the Bhagavatam names love of God, not merging, as the goal (25.131). The full
   answer is trd-35.
4. *"Is liberation the prayojana?"* No. The Lord names love of Godhead (CC Madhya 20.124, 6.178, 25.131). trd-29 shows why
   liberation is not the end.
5. *"Where do the rules of the ashram fit?"* Under abhidheya, as "sense activity for the satisfaction of the Lord"
   (CC Madhya 20.125). The purport of CC Madhya 6.178 adds that in this age the direct means is chanting and hearing from pure
   devotees, with varnasrama hard to establish.
6. *"Can I reach the goal without knowing sambandha, by just chanting?"* Chanting is abhidheya and does purify, but Sanatana's
   first question was "who am I?", and the Lord answered it first. Knowing the relationship protects the chanting from wrong
   aims (CC Madhya 20.102, 20.124).
7. *"Why study six Sandarbhas when the Caitanya-caritamrta already gives the map?"* Because residents must answer scholars
   and sceptics with evidence, and the Sandarbhas give the systematic proof under each heading. Prabhupada himself refers the
   reader to *Tattva-sandarbha* on evidence (CC Madhya 6.135 purport).

## L. Case studies

**Case 1: The one-minute answer.** *Invented case for discussion.* Ravi, a brahmacari, is distributing books at a railway
station. A young engineer says: "You have one minute. What is your philosophy?" Ravi starts with the ten offences to the
holy name, then jumps to the spiritual world, then runs out of time.
- *Dilemma:* how to say the whole philosophy in one minute without leaving out Krishna or the goal.
- *Principles:* CC Madhya 20.124–125; CC Madhya 20.143.
- *Questions:* Which heading did Ravi begin with? Which did he leave out? What three sentences would cover all three?
- *Analysis:* He began in the middle (abhidheya, the rules of chanting) and skipped the relationship. Three sentences in the
  Lord's order: "You are an eternal soul, part of Krishna, the Supreme Person (sambandha). The way to revive that relationship
  is to serve Him, beginning with chanting His names (abhidheya). The result is love of God, which satisfies the soul
  completely (prayojana)."
- *Takeaway:* the map gives the order; the preacher adds the one verse he knows best for each heading.

**Case 2: The reader who lost the thread.** *Invented case for discussion.* Devaki, a full-time preacher, has read the
Caitanya-caritamrta twice but cannot say how CC Madhya 20, 22 and 23 relate. She reads each chapter as a separate list.
- *Dilemma:* much knowledge, no structure; she feels she "knows nothing" before scholars.
- *Principles:* CC Madhya 20.102–103 (the order of Sanatana's questions); CC Madhya 25.102–103.
- *Questions:* Which of Sanatana's three questions does each chapter answer? What would change in her reading if she wrote
  the heading at the top of each chapter?
- *Analysis:* Madhya 20 answers "who am I and who is Krishna" (sambandha); Madhya 22 answers "what is the process"
  (abhidheya); Madhya 23 answers "what is the goal" (prayojana); Madhya 25 proves it all from the Bhagavatam. With the headings
  written, the three chapters become one argument.
- *Takeaway:* study with the map in hand; then the Sandarbhas (trd-24 to trd-29) will read as six chapters of one book.

**M.2 Activities**
- **Sorting round (15 min).** Each pair receives eight written questions students actually ask (for example: "Is God a
  person?", "Why four rules?", "What is the soul?", "What happens after death?", "Why chant sixteen rounds?", "Is the world

  name one verse they would open for each. Pairs report; the group corrects placements together.
- **Three-sentence answer (10 min).** Each resident rewrites the answer he wrote at the start (H.1) in three sentences, one
  per heading, in Lord Caitanya's order. Three volunteers read theirs aloud; the group notes which sentence was hardest.

**M.3 Reflection** (private)
Which heading do I preach most, and which do I avoid? Is it because I know it least, or because I have not realised it?

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

When Sanatana Gosvami surrendered to Lord Caitanya at Varanasi, he asked: "Who am I? Why do the threefold miseries always give me trouble?" and "I do not know how to inquire about the goal of life and the process for obtaining it" (CC Madhya 20.102–103). The Lord answered that the conditioned soul cannot revive his Krishna consciousness by his own effort; out of mercy Krishna compiled the Vedic literature and teaches the forgetful soul through scripture, the spiritual master and the Supersoul in the heart (CC Madhya 20.122–123).

Then the Lord gave the map of all Vedic knowledge. It teaches three things: *sambandha*, our eternal relationship with Krishna; *abhidheya*, understanding that relationship and acting in it, which is devotional service; and *prayojana*, the goal, love of God, "returning home, back to Godhead" (CC Madhya 20.124–125). Krishna is the centre of all three: "Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa's service and love of Kṛṣṇa are the three great riches of life" (CC Madhya 20.143). Srila Prabhupada explains that sambandha-jnana means knowing four things: oneself, the universe, God and their relationship (CC Adi 7.146 purport).

The Lord gave the same three subjects to the logician Sarvabhauma Bhattacarya (CC Madhya 6.178) and to the Mayavadi sannyasis of Varanasi, telling them that every aphorism of the Vedanta-sutra teaches them (CC Adi 7.146). Vyasadeva, who wrote the Vedanta-sutra, explained it himself in the Srimad-Bhagavatam, whose four essential verses state the same three subjects (CC Madhya 25.98, 25.102–103, 25.131, 25.142). Srila Jiva Gosvami built his six Sandarbhas on this map: the first four explain the relationship, the fifth devotional service, the sixth love of God.

## S2. Key ideas
- All Vedic literature teaches three subjects: relationship (sambandha), activity in it (abhidheya), goal (prayojana).
- Krishna is at the centre of each: who He is to me, what I do for Him, what I attain in Him (CC Madhya 20.143).
- The knowledge is given by Krishna through scripture, guru and the Supersoul, not invented by us (CC Madhya 20.122–123).
- The goal is love of God, not liberation (CC Madhya 20.124, 6.178).
- A preacher answers every question from its heading, so Krishna is never left out.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **CC Madhya 20.124:** the Vedic literatures teach our eternal relationship with Krishna (sambandha), understanding and
  acting in it (abhidheya), and returning home, back to Godhead (prayojana). *For me:* my questions have one map.
- **CC Madhya 20.143:** "Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa's service and love of Kṛṣṇa are the three great riches of life." *For me:* the one line
  that holds the whole philosophy.
- **BG 15.15:** "By all the Vedas, I am to be known." *For me:* whatever I read in scripture, I am reading about Krishna.

## S4. Words to know
- **Sambandha:** relationship; here, the eternal relationship between the soul, the world and Krishna.
- **Abhidheya:** what is to be done; devotional service, acting in the relationship.
- **Prayojana:** the goal; love of Krishna.
- **Catuh-sloki:** the four verses Krishna spoke to Brahma (SB 2.9.33–36), the essence of the Bhagavatam.
- **Sandarbha:** "essay" or "treatise"; Jiva Gosvami's six systematic books on Gaudiya philosophy.
- **Siddhanta:** the settled conclusion of scripture.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *Is this map Srila Prabhupada's teaching or only the Gosvamis'?* It is Lord Caitanya's own teaching in Caitanya-caritamrta
   (CC Madhya 20.124–125), with Prabhupada's purports defining each term (CC Adi 7.146).
2. *Why three subjects when Krishna is the only subject?* The three are the three ways the one subject is taught: who He is
   to me, what I do for Him, what I attain (CC Madhya 20.143).
3. *Is liberation the goal?* No; the Lord names love of Godhead as the goal (CC Madhya 20.124, 6.178, 25.131).
4. *Where do the ashram's rules fit?* Under abhidheya, "sense activity for the satisfaction of the Lord" (CC Madhya 20.125).
5. *Why study the six Sandarbhas if the map is already in Caitanya-caritamrta?* They give the systematic proof under each
   heading, so a preacher can answer scholars with evidence.

## S6. Check your understanding
1. Which of these is sambandha? (a) chanting sixteen rounds (b) knowing that I am an eternal soul related to Krishna
   (c) attaining love of God.
2. Who asked Lord Caitanya "Who am I? Why do the threefold miseries always give me trouble?"
3. Name Krishna's three helpers for the forgetful soul (CC Madhya 20.123).
4. True or false: according to Lord Caitanya, the Vedanta-sutra teaches only impersonal oneness.
5. Under which heading does Jiva Gosvami's *Bhakti-sandarbha* fall?
6. Short answer: in one sentence, what is the prayojana?

**Answers:** 1. (b) (CC Madhya 20.124). 2. Sanatana Gosvami (CC Madhya 20.102). 3. Scripture, the spiritual master and the
Supersoul (CC Madhya 20.123). 4. False: every aphorism teaches relationship, activity and the goal of love of God
(CC Adi 7.146). 5. Abhidheya. 6. Love of Krishna, returning home, back to Godhead (CC Madhya 20.124).
