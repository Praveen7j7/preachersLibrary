## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on [DYS-3](dys-01-s3.md) (why the Vedas stay relevant: the problems technology leaves) and [VL2-05](vl2-05.md) (the manual and its benefits). Neither is repeated here: this page keeps to **why the teaching was lost, and ISKCON's ways of re-establishing practical Vedic culture**, with the benefits mentioned only in one line.

VL2-05 argued that the Vedic literature offers a manual for peace. The natural VL2 objection follows at once:
"Then why is almost no one using it? Why did I never hear of SB 1.2.10 at school?" A reasoned-faith talk must
take the objection seriously. It has three honest parts: the texts' own account of how the teaching gets lost
(BG 4.2) and of this age (SB 1.1.10, 12.2.1–2); a fair historical account of what happened in India, said as
history with its uncertainties; and a description of what ISKCON actually does, stated as its purposes and
practices rather than as a claim to be the only or best organisation.

**IYF's VL2 frame** (IYF's own documents, `research/batch-criteria.md` §2 and §8 point 2): course name "Technical Science
for Spiritual Value", mood *questioning, reasoned*; motive "developing strong faith in practicing spirituality" and "removing all
misconceptions about ISKCON and spirituality"; KRAs "Start doing chanting", "Coming to regular classes", "Meeting with
facilitators". The 2015 VL2 lesson plan runs DYS 1–6 first and then the EBG study-sessions; this page mirrors the DYS/EBG session "Are Vedas Relevant Today" (Encyclopedia transcript, O). Practice is
expected to *begin* during VL2 (by its end: 2–4 rounds, 15 min of Prabhupada's books and 15–30 min of kirtan hearing a day), so
this page may carry a first practical step without breaking IYF's level (P).

**Not done here:**
- Blaming any religion or community for India's history. The texts' own explanation of decline is internal ("in course of time the succession was broken", BG 4.2).
- Claiming ISKCON is "unlike" or "better than" other organisations. That is VL2-07's question, and it is handled by tests, not comparisons.
- Quoting numbers (books distributed, temples, meals served) without a current source.

## C. Central message

The Gita itself says that the teaching gets lost "in course of time" and is re-established by the Lord and His
representatives (BG 4.2, 4.7–8). The Bhagavatam describes this age as one where religion's four legs are broken
and only truthfulness hobbles on (SB 1.17.24–25), and names the remedy: the Bhagavatam itself (SB 1.3.43) and the
chanting of the holy name (SB 12.3.51–52). ISKCON's stated purposes are an attempt to do exactly that: teach,
chant, publish, build places of worship and a simpler way of life. Whether it succeeds is something the listener
can look at.

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. Explain the Gita's own account of how spiritual knowledge is lost and recovered (BG 4.1–3, 4.7–8).
2. Describe the symptoms of this age from SB 1.1.10, 1.17.24–25 and 12.2.1–2, and relate them to what they see.
3. State, fairly, two or three historical factors scholars discuss for the decline of Vedic learning in India, without presenting any as the whole story.
4. Name the remedy the texts prescribe for this age (SB 1.3.43, 12.3.51–52, CC Adi 17.21) and the practical benefits the texts promise (SB 1.2.17–18, BG 2.40).
5. List ISKCON's seven stated purposes and match each to something they can observe.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** eight references carry the talk (BG 4.2, 4.7, SB 1.1.10, 1.17.24–25, 1.17.38, 1.3.43, 12.3.51, CC Adi 9.41). The rest prepare you for K.

**BG 4.1, 4.2, 4.3**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/4/2/
> 4.2: "This supreme science was thus received through the chain of disciplic succession, and the saintly kings understood it in that way. But in course of time the succession was broken, and therefore the science as it is appears to be lost."
- **Context:** Krsna traces the Gita's transmission from the sun-god through Manu and Iksvaku (4.1) and says He now speaks it again to Arjuna (4.3).
- **Teaching:** Knowledge is handed down in succession; the succession can break in time; the science then "appears to be lost" and is spoken again.
- **Purport point (4.2):** "As soon as the original purpose was scattered by the motives of the unscrupulous commentators, there arose the need to reestablish the disciplic succession"; today "there are so many editions of the Gītā… but almost all of them are not according to authorized disciplic succession"; hence the need for an edition "as it is received by the paramparā".
- **Application (preacher's):** This is the texts' own answer to "why do we not hear of them": the first cause of loss is internal and gradual, not an enemy. Lead with it before any history.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 4.7, 4.8**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/4/7/
> 4.7: "Whenever and wherever there is a decline in religious practice, O descendant of Bharata, and a predominant rise of irreligion – at that time I descend Myself."
> 4.8: "To deliver the pious and to annihilate the miscreants, as well as to reestablish the principles of religion, I Myself appear, millennium after millennium."
- **Teaching:** The Lord appears when religion declines, to re-establish its principles.
- **Purport point (4.7):** "Principles of religion are laid down in the Vedas"; "Only the Lord can manufacture a system of religion"; "No one should be accepted as an avatāra unless he is referred to by scriptures"; "It is not a fact that the Lord appears only on Indian soil"; "Sometimes He descends personally, and sometimes He sends His bona fide representative in the form of His son, or servant, or Himself in some disguised form."
- **Application (preacher's):** Re-establishment is the Lord's work, done sometimes through representatives. That is the frame in which to speak of any organisation, including ours: an instrument, not the author.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 1.1.10, SB 12.2.1, SB 12.2.2**: Verified. (12.2.1–2: *BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples.*)
> 12.2.1: "…religion, truthfulness, cleanliness, tolerance, mercy, duration of life, physical strength and memory will all diminish day by day because of the powerful influence of the Age of Kali."
> 12.2.2: "In Kali-yuga, wealth alone will be considered the sign of a man's good birth, proper behavior and fine qualities. And law and justice will be applied only on the basis of one's power."
- **Teaching:** A list of what diminishes in this age, and the rule of wealth and power.
- **Purport point (SB 1.1.10):** "due to a bad system of education, men have no desire for self-realization"; "In the Kali-yuga the whole atmosphere is surcharged with faithlessness."
- **Application (preacher's):** The second answer to "why don't we hear of them": the age itself works against hearing. Present 12.2.2 and let the room react.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 1.17.24, 1.17.25**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/17/24/
> 1.17.24: "In the age of Satya [truthfulness] your four legs were established by the four principles of austerity, cleanliness, mercy and truthfulness. But it appears that three of your legs are broken due to rampant irreligion in the form of pride, lust for women, and intoxication."
- **Context:** King Pariksit speaks to the bull, religion personified.
- **Teaching:** Religion's four legs are austerity, cleanliness, mercy and truthfulness; three are broken by pride, lust and intoxication; truthfulness alone remains, under attack by Kali (1.17.25).
- **Purport point (1.17.24):** "Man cannot check all these evils simply by statutory acts and police vigilance, but he can cure the disease of the mind by the proper medicine, namely advocating the principles of brahminical culture or the principles of austerity, cleanliness, mercy and truthfulness."
- **Application (preacher's):** "Practical benefits" in IYF's subtitle: the texts define a healthy culture by these four qualities. A listener can ask whether more austerity, cleanliness, mercy and truthfulness would improve their street. That is the practical case.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 1.17.38, 1.17.39**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/17/38/
> 1.17.38: "…gave him permission to reside in places where gambling, drinking, prostitution and animal slaughter were performed."
> 1.17.39: "…wherever there is gold there is also falsity, intoxication, lust, envy and enmity."
- **Teaching:** Kali is permitted to live in four places (gambling, drinking, prostitution, slaughter) and a fifth, gold.
- **Purport point (1.17.38):** the four irreligious principles "counteract the four principles of religion"; the state "should categorically stop all sorts of gambling, drinking, prostitution and falsity"; scriptural concessions for those in ignorance "are not meant for the encouragement of such adharma, but for regulating" it.
- **Application (preacher's):** This is the scriptural root of the four regulative principles ISKCON members follow. Present them as the texts' map of where irreligion grows, not as a rule book.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 1.3.43**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/3/43/
> "This Bhāgavata Purāṇa is as brilliant as the sun, and it has arisen just after the departure of Lord Kṛṣṇa to His own abode… Persons who have lost their vision due to the dense darkness of ignorance in the Age of Kali shall get light from this Purāṇa."
- **Teaching:** The Bhagavatam is the light for those in the darkness of this age.
- **Use:** in the talk: the first remedy the texts name.

**SB 12.3.51, 12.3.52**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/12/3/51/ · *BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples.*
> 12.3.51: "My dear King, although Kali-yuga is an ocean of faults, there is still one good quality about this age: Simply by chanting the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra, one can become free from material bondage and be promoted to the transcendental kingdom."
- **Teaching:** The age's one good quality is that chanting frees one from bondage; chanting yields in this age what meditation, sacrifice and temple service yielded in earlier ages (12.3.52).
- **Purport point (12.3.51):** "Just as one powerful king can kill innumerable thieves, one brilliant spiritual quality can destroy all the contamination of this age."
- **Use:** in the talk: the second remedy.

**CC Adi 17.21**: Verified. "In this Age of Kali there is no other means… than chanting the holy name… of Lord Hari." (Caitanya Mahaprabhu quoting the Brhan-naradiya Purana.) **Use:** with 12.3.51.

**CC Adi 9.41**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/adi/9/41/
> "One who has taken his birth as a human being in the land of India [Bhārata-varṣa] should make his life successful and work for the benefit of all other people."
- **Teaching:** Caitanya Mahaprabhu's instruction to those born in India: perfect your life and do good to others.
- **Purport point:** "if an Indian is educated in the Vedic principles, he is able to perform the most beneficial welfare activity for the entire world"; "there is a need for vigorous propaganda to educate people to refrain from sinful activities"; Prabhupada reports the movement's "practical effect" as degraded people becoming devotees. He also writes that India's leaders were "influencing the people not to believe in God".
- **Application (preacher's):** The verse is the mandate ISKCON cites for its work. Quote the verse and the purport's claim as *Prabhupada's claim*, then point to what the listener can observe (K.5).
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 1.2.17, 1.2.18, BG 2.40, BG 9.2**: Verified.
- 1.2.17: hearing Krsna's messages cleanses the desire for material enjoyment; 1.2.18: regular hearing and service destroy "all that is troublesome to the heart"; BG 2.40: "a little advancement on this path can protect one from the most dangerous type of fear"; BG 9.2: this knowledge "gives direct perception of the self by realization". **Use:** the practical benefits the texts actually promise (F.2).

**BG 3.21, BG 18.68, 18.69**: Verified. 3.21: what a great man does, common men follow. 18.68–69: one who explains this to the devotees is most dear to the Lord. **Use:** K and P (why teaching and example are the method).

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **Why we do not hear of them: three honest answers.**
   - *The texts' own account.* Succession breaks "in course of time" (BG 4.2); the age diminishes religion, memory and truthfulness (SB 12.2.1); education does not aim at self-realisation (SB 1.1.10 purport; BG 9.2 purport: "no university or educational institution where the science of the spirit soul is instructed"). This is the first and sufficient explanation, and it blames no one outside.
   - *Fair history (claims, with the mainstream view).* Historians discuss several factors in the decline of traditional Vedic learning: the destruction or abandonment of great centres (Nalanda ceased to function around 1200 CE; the causes, including a raid recorded in a Persian chronicle, are debated); the loss of royal patronage under successive rulers; the colonial education policy that favoured English (Macaulay's Minute of 1835 is a real document; read it before quoting it); and, within Indian society, hereditary caste, which kept the texts from most people, and reform movements that criticised ritual. Mainstream historians describe a long, many-sided process with internal and external causes. "Systematically attacked" is an interpretation some writers make; present it as such. The Gita's own emphasis is on the internal cause.
   - *The present.* Consumer culture and an education built for employment leave no room for SB 1.2.10. This needs no conspiracy to explain.
2. **The practical benefits, from the texts.** Not "ancient India was rich" (VL2-05 handles that) but what the texts promise someone who takes them up: a cleansed heart (SB 1.2.17), the end of what troubles it (1.2.18), protection from fear even from a small beginning (BG 2.40), direct perception by realisation (BG 9.2), and a society measured by austerity, cleanliness, mercy and truthfulness (SB 1.17.24). Each is checkable in a person or a community.
3. **How re-establishment happens.** The Lord descends or sends a representative (BG 4.7–8 purport). Leaders set the example others follow (BG 3.21). Teaching is the method (BG 18.68–69). The age's special means is the holy name (SB 12.3.51–52, CC Adi 17.21) and the Bhagavatam (SB 1.3.43).
4. **What ISKCON does.** Its seven purposes, written into its certificate of incorporation (New York, July 1966) (https://iskconnews.org/the-seven-purposes-of-iskcon/): to propagate spiritual knowledge and "achieve real unity and peace in the world"; to propagate Krsna consciousness as revealed in the Gita and Bhagavatam; to bring members closer to Krsna; to teach and encourage sankirtana; to erect holy places; to teach "a simpler, more natural way of life"; and to publish and distribute books. Map each to a verse: 1 and 2 → BG 4.2 purport (the Gita as it is), 4 → SB 12.3.51, 5 → the temple, 6 → SB 1.17.38 (Kali's places avoided), 7 → SB 1.3.43. Say that this is a stated programme; its success is for the listener to examine.
5. **Connections.** Back to VL2-05 (the manual). Forward to VL2-07 (who can be trusted to transmit it) and VL3 (the practices).
6. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - **"Vedic culture was destroyed by outsiders."** Partly a historical claim with debated evidence, partly a deflection from BG 4.2's internal cause. Hold both honestly.
   - **"ISKCON is the only one re-establishing it."** Many organisations and individuals teach the Gita, run gurukulas and preserve ritual. Speak of them with respect. ISKCON's distinctive emphasis is the parampara text (BG 4.2 purport), the holy name (SB 12.3.51) and worldwide distribution (CC Adi 9.41 purport).
   - **"Relevant means scientifically proven."** The texts' relevance is argued from their diagnosis and their promised, checkable benefits, not from archaeology.
   - **"Re-establishing means going back."** BG 4.7 purport: the Lord speaks "as much about religion as can be understood by the particular people under their particular circumstances". The principles are old; the forms adapt.
7. **Pitch for VL2.** Define *parampara*, *Kali-yuga*, *sankirtana* once each. Say "the texts say", "historians debate", "ISKCON states", "you can check" and mean each one.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Welcome by name; say today is an honest look at a hard question: if this knowledge is valuable, why did we meet it so late?
2. Ask H.2 question 1; take three answers and thank each.
3. Notice aloud: late, accidental, from outside school.
4. Say: "The Gita predicted this (BG 4.2). We will look at that, at history said fairly, and at what one organisation does about it."
5. Promise the room that nobody will be blamed, and that every claim will be marked as the text's, a historian's, or ISKCON's own.

**H.2 Opening questions**
- Where and when did you first read or hear a verse of the Gita, or any scripture of your family's tradition? What was that like?
- Is there a skill or story in your family that an older person knows and nobody younger does?
- If you wanted to keep a tradition alive, what would you do first?

**Adapting for your audience:** the "lost family skill" works anywhere (a recipe, a song, a farming method, a craft); for professionals use knowledge that left a company when one expert retired. Use any tradition the room knows to show that transmission breaks everywhere.

**More material for the opening**

- **Opening question:** "When did you first read a verse of the Gita, and where? School? Home? YouTube?" Most answers will be late and accidental. "Why is a text this famous so rarely actually read? Today we take that question seriously, including the uncomfortable parts."
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** A student writes a college essay arguing that Indian philosophy is "mostly ritual and superstition". His grandfather, who knows the Gita, is hurt but cannot explain why the grandson never learnt otherwise. Who failed whom?
- **Transition:** "The Gita predicted this: 'in course of time the succession was broken' (BG 4.2). It also says what happens next."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** they are the generation deciding whether this transmission continues or ends with their grandparents.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Knowledge is transmitted, and transmission breaks.** BG 4.1–3. *Example:* a family recipe lost in two generations. *Transition:* the age makes it worse.
2. **The age works against hearing.** SB 12.2.1–2; SB 1.1.10 purport on education and faithlessness. *Example:* a timetable with no slot for "who am I?" *Transition:* history also happened.
3. **History, fairly.** Internal and external causes; scholars debate the weight of each; the texts stress the internal. *Transition:* so is it worth recovering?
4. **The benefits, in one line.** The texts promise a heart cleansed and steadied (SB 1.2.17–18) and protection from fear (BG 2.40); [DYS-3](dys-01-s3.md) and [VL2-05](vl2-05.md) already argued them, so only recall them. The measure used here is SB 1.17.24 and the map of where irreligion grows is SB 1.17.38. *Transition:* how is such a thing recovered?
5. **The Lord re-establishes, through representatives and example.** BG 4.7–8 purport; BG 3.21; the age's remedies (SB 1.3.43, 12.3.51–52). *Transition:* one attempt to do this.
6. **ISKCON's seven purposes, mapped to verses.** CC Adi 9.41 as mandate; "go and look" as the test. *Transition:* to P.

## J. Stories and illustrations

- **King Pariksit, the bull and Kali** (SB 1.17.24–25, 1.17.38–39). Tell the exchange as the text has it: the bull's broken legs, the king's diagnosis (pride, lust, intoxication), Kali's request and the five places granted. No added dialogue. *Connection:* the four regulative principles have a scriptural story behind them.
- **Historical (preacher's, with care):** if you mention Nalanda or Macaulay's Minute, give the date and say "historians discuss the causes" or "read the text before quoting it". No invented detail; no villains.
- **Contemporary (preacher's, verifiable):** a devotee the audience knows whose life changed through the practices, told with their permission. This is the CC Adi 9.41 purport's "practical effect", shown rather than claimed.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

> **Before you teach:** this topic can slide into blaming communities or praising ourselves. Both lose the sceptic and dishonour the texts. Keep to "the texts say", "historians debate", "ISKCON states", "you can check".

1. **"Was Vedic culture really 'systematically attacked'?"**
   - Some writers argue so, pointing to destroyed institutions, lost patronage and colonial policy. Mainstream historians describe a long process with many causes, internal as well as external. Say both, and add that the Gita's own explanation is internal (BG 4.2). Honest question; do not answer it with a slogan.
2. **"Isn't this an anti-Muslim or anti-Christian story?"**
   - It must not be. BG 4.7 purport says the Lord appears for all peoples and "not only on Indian soil". Speak of every faith with respect, and keep the historical section factual and brief. If you cannot, leave history out.
3. **"Hindus themselves kept the Vedas from most people. Why blame anyone else?"**
   - Fair objection. Hereditary caste restricted access; this is part of the honest history (F.1). CC Madhya 8.128 (taught in VL2-07) says anyone who knows the science of Krsna can teach it; ISKCON's practice of teaching everyone is a response to exactly this.
4. **"Why does ISKCON think it is the one to fix this?"**
   - It does not claim to be the only one; many teach the Gita. ISKCON states seven purposes and points to CC Adi 9.41. Its distinctive claims are an unaltered transmission (BG 4.2 purport) and the holy name as this age's means (SB 12.3.51). Judge it by its stated tests (VL2-07).
5. **"Is it 'doing this successfully'?"**
   - Give evidence the listener can check: people they can meet, books they can read, programmes they can attend. Prabhupada's claim in the CC Adi 9.41 purport about degraded people becoming devotees is a claim; let them meet someone. Do not quote distribution or membership numbers you have not sourced this year.
6. **"Chanting a name fixes civilisation? That seems too easy."**
   - The texts make the claim (SB 12.3.51–52, CC Adi 17.21) and the Gita says even a little progress protects from great fear (BG 2.40). The honest VL2 reply is "try it for a month and see" (SB 1.2.17–18 describe what to expect), not "trust me".
7. **"The four regulative principles are just rules."**
   - They are the inverse of Kali's four places (SB 1.17.38) and the counterparts of religion's four legs (1.17.24 purport). Present the map, not the rule.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The history debate.** At a college event Sameer says Vedic culture was "systematically destroyed by invaders and the British". A classmate replies that caste and ritualism did more damage from inside. The room splits.
- **Principles:** BG 4.2 (internal cause first); F.1 (fair history with uncertainty); BG 4.7 purport (the Lord appears for all).
- **Questions:** Which claims could Sameer support with sources? What does the Gita itself emphasise? How could he concede the classmate's point without abandoning his own?
- **Takeaway:** A preacher who holds both causes honestly is believed on the third claim: that recovery is possible.

**Case 2: The sceptical cousin.** Ananya's cousin says, "ISKCON is just one more sect claiming to save the world." Ananya wants to defend it without sounding like an advertisement.
- **Principles:** the seven purposes as stated aims (F.4); CC Adi 9.41 as mandate; BG 3.21 (example over claim); K.5 (checkable evidence).
- **Questions:** What can Ananya say that the cousin can verify? What should she not claim? Whom could she introduce the cousin to?
- **Takeaway:** State the purposes, point to the people, skip the superlatives.

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first)
1. What does BG 4.2 say happened to the science "in course of time"? *Expected:* the succession was broken.
2. Name religion's four legs (SB 1.17.24). *Expected:* austerity, cleanliness, mercy, truthfulness.
3. Is the lost-teaching story only about outsiders? *Expected:* No; the Gita's own explanation is internal, and historians name many causes.
4. Which two remedies does this age have (SB 1.3.43, 12.3.51)? *Expected:* the Bhagavatam and chanting the holy name.
5. Is ISKCON the only group working to revive this culture? *Expected:* No; many do. ISKCON states seven purposes and you can check them.

**M.2 Activities (engagement block, ~90 min; choose two)**
- **A. Match the seven purposes (35 min).** Groups of four receive the seven purposes (from O) and verse cards (BG 4.2, SB 1.17.24, 1.3.43, 12.3.51, CC Adi 9.41). The preacher marks what is ISKCON's stated purpose and what is the group's idea.
- **B. "Go and look" checklist (30 min).** Pairs write five things they would observe at any centre or gathering, of any tradition, to test whether its stated purposes are being lived (for example: is the teaching free of insult? are visitors welcomed? are accounts open?). Share and combine into one list for the room. This is a method for checking claims, not a verdict on anyone.
- **C. Transmission chain (25 min).** Each person draws the chain of people from whom they learned one skill and marks where it could break. Pairs share what would keep the chain intact, then link the ideas to BG 4.2 and 3.21.

**M.3 Reflection (private, optional).** Where did the transmission break in my own family, and where did it hold? Which of the seven purposes could I take one step in this month? Nobody needs to share.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

If the Vedic literature is so valuable, why did most of us meet it late and by accident? The Bhagavad-gita (an ancient Sanskrit scripture, here in Srila Prabhupada's translation) gives its own answer: knowledge is handed down from teacher to student, and "in course of time the succession was broken" (BG 4.2). The Srimad-Bhagavatam (a later scripture) adds that in this age, Kali-yuga, religion, truthfulness, memory and mercy all diminish (SB 12.2.1), and that religion stands on four legs, austerity, cleanliness, mercy and truthfulness, of which only truthfulness remains after pride, lust and intoxication broke the others (SB 1.17.24–25). When King Pariksit found Kali, the spirit of the age, he allowed him to stay only where there is gambling, drinking, prostitution and animal slaughter, and where there is gold (SB 1.17.38–39).

History played its part too. Scholars discuss lost centres of learning, lost royal support, colonial schooling and barriers of caste inside Indian society; the weight of each is debated, and no one group is to blame. Every tradition in the world has had its teachings thin out between generations.

But the Gita also says the Lord re-establishes religion whenever it declines (BG 4.7–8), and the texts name this age's remedies: the Bhagavatam (SB 1.3.43) and chanting the holy name (SB 12.3.51). ISKCON (the International Society for Krishna Consciousness) states seven purposes in its 1966 certificate of incorporation: to teach spiritual knowledge, to spread Krishna consciousness as in the Gita and Bhagavatam, to bring members closer to Krishna, to promote congregational chanting, to build holy places, to teach a simpler way of life, and to publish books. Many other groups also teach the Gita. You can look for yourself whether these purposes are being carried out, and test the remedy by trying it.

## S2. Key ideas

- Knowledge is transmitted; transmission breaks in time (BG 4.2).
- This age works against hearing: religion, truthfulness and memory diminish (SB 12.2.1).
- Religion's four legs: austerity, cleanliness, mercy, truthfulness (SB 1.17.24). Kali lives where there is gambling, drinking, prostitution, slaughter and gold (SB 1.17.38–39).
- The Lord re-establishes religion, sometimes through representatives (BG 4.7–8).
- This age's remedies: the Bhagavatam and the holy name (SB 1.3.43, 12.3.51–52).
- History has many causes; say what is known and what is debated.

## S3. Verses to remember

- **BG 4.2:** "In course of time the succession was broken, and therefore the science as it is appears to be lost." *For me:* losing the teaching is normal; recovering it is possible.
- **SB 1.17.24:** religion's legs are "austerity, cleanliness, mercy and truthfulness". *For me:* this is what a healthy culture would look like on my street.
- **SB 12.3.51:** "although Kali-yuga is an ocean of faults, there is still one good quality about this age: simply by chanting the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra, one can become free." *For me:* the remedy is available to me today.

## S4. Words to know

- **Parampara:** the chain of teachers through which knowledge is handed down (BG 4.2).
- **Kali-yuga:** the present age, described in SB 12.2.1–2 and SB 1.1.10.
- **Sankirtana:** congregational chanting of the holy name, ISKCON's fourth purpose (SB 12.3.51).
- **The four regulative principles:** no gambling, intoxication, illicit sex or meat-eating; the inverse of Kali's places (SB 1.17.38).

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Was Vedic culture "systematically attacked"?** Some writers say so; historians describe many causes, inside and outside. The Gita's own explanation is internal: the succession broke in time (BG 4.2).
2. **Is this a story against other religions?** No. The Lord appears for all peoples, "not only on Indian soil" (BG 4.7 purport). We speak of every faith with respect.
3. **Didn't caste keep the Vedas from most Indians?** Yes, that is part of the honest history. Teaching everyone is part of the remedy.
4. **Is ISKCON the only one re-establishing this?** No; many teach the Gita. ISKCON states seven purposes and emphasises the Gita as it is and the holy name.
5. **How do I know if it works?** Try the remedy (hearing and chanting) and watch what SB 1.2.17–18 describe; meet people whose lives changed.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. According to BG 4.2, why does the science "appear to be lost"?
2. Name religion's four legs (SB 1.17.24).
3. Where did King Pariksit permit Kali to live (SB 1.17.38–39)?
4. True or false: BG 4.7 purport says the Lord appears only in India.
5. What does SB 12.3.51 call the one good quality of this age?

**Answers:** 1. Because "in course of time the succession was broken" (BG 4.2). 2. Austerity, cleanliness, mercy and truthfulness (SB 1.17.24). 3. Where there is gambling, drinking, prostitution and animal slaughter, and where there is gold (SB 1.17.38–39). 4. False: "It is not a fact that the Lord appears only on Indian soil" (BG 4.7 purport). 5. That by chanting the Hare Krsna maha-mantra one can become free from material bondage (SB 12.3.51).
