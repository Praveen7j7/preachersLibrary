# 43. Overcoming Sexual Desire

> BSS · 120 min · drafted · SENSITIVE (sexuality and celibacy; adult men in brahmacari training; no private disclosure). Follows BIKC chapter 43. Builds on bss-21 (what the drive is), bss-22 (the suffering it causes), bss-23 (today's atmosphere), bss-24 (the senses) and bss-27 (discipline). Those pages explain the problem. This page gives the Krishna conscious way to deal with it.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level
The earlier pages of this group explain what sexual desire is, what it costs and how the modern world feeds it. A student who has heard all that has the right question: "What do I actually do when the desire comes?" BIKC chapter 43 answers: there is no quick trick, and the real answer is Krishna consciousness itself, supported by sensible habits. This page gives that answer from the Srimad-Bhagavatam, in everyday steps.

## C. Central message
Desire for sex cannot be removed by force alone. It fades as the heart is filled with the taste of serving Krishna through hearing, chanting and active service, and the Lord Himself protects the devotee who keeps trying. Until then, the devotee stays enthusiastic, avoids what provokes him, and talks honestly with a senior.

## D. Learning outcomes
1. Explain in plain words why forcing desire down does not remove it (SB 6.1.15 purport; BIKC).
2. Tell the story of Maharaja Ambarisa's senses in service (SB 9.4.18-20) and say what it teaches.
3. State what SB 11.20.27-29 tells a sincere devotee who is still troubled.
4. List the four steps of a personal plan: conviction, openness, safety and taste.
5. Say whom to approach for help.

## E. Scriptural foundation
Quotations are the BBT translations kept in `research/verification/bss-39.json`. Purport points were read in full on vedabase.io. SB Canto 11 is translated by Srila Prabhupada's disciples (BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples).

**SB 11.20.27-28** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/20/27-28/
> "Having awakened faith in the narrations of My glories, being disgusted with all material activities, knowing that all sense gratification leads to misery, but still being unable to renounce all sense enjoyment, My devotee should remain happy and worship Me with great faith and conviction. Even though he is sometimes engaged in sense enjoyment, My devotee knows that all sense gratification leads to a miserable result, and he sincerely repents such activities."
- Context: Lord Krishna's instruction to Uddhava.
- Purport point: the word *kaman* "basically refers to sex attraction and its by-products"; "even a sincere candidate in the loving service of the Lord may sometimes be disturbed by sex attraction". The Lord "encourages such a devotee not to be overly depressed or morose but to remain enthusiastic and to go on with his loving service". The devotee thinks that he has no power to stop the attachments himself, that "Only Lord Kṛṣṇa within my heart can remove such inauspicious contamination", and decides that he will never give up devotional service. The purport also warns that if a devotee becomes too discouraged "he may drift into an impersonal consciousness or give up his devotional service", and it says that "By faithful hearing of the mercy and glories of the Lord one will gradually be freed from all material desire".
- Use: in the talk. This is the main text of the page. BIKC reproduces it in full for this reason.

**SB 11.20.29** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/20/29/
> "When an intelligent person engages constantly in worshiping Me through loving devotional service as described by Me, his heart becomes firmly situated in Me. Thus all material desires within the heart are destroyed."
- Purport point: "Just as the rising sun gradually eliminates all trace of darkness, the Lord’s presence within the heart causes all material desires there to weaken and eventually disappear." A sincere devotee "should not be discouraged by the remnants of material desires within the heart. He should faithfully wait for the devotional process to naturally purify the heart of all contamination."
- Use: in the talk.

**SB 6.1.15** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/6/1/15/
> "Only a rare person who has adopted complete, unalloyed devotional service to Kṛṣṇa can uproot the weeds of sinful actions with no possibility that they will revive. He can do this simply by discharging devotional service, just as the sun can immediately dissipate fog by its rays."
- Purport point: the preceding verse compares the burning of dry leaves with the root still in the ground. In the same way, where "the root of sinful desire is not destroyed", desire "may reappear". "If one becomes attached to devotional service, however, his desires for material enjoyment are automatically vanquished without separate endeavor."
- Use: in the talk, for the picture of root and leaves (F.2).

**SB 9.4.18-20** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/9/4/18-20/
> "Mahārāja Ambarīṣa always engaged his mind in meditating upon the lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa, his words in describing the glories of the Lord, his hands in cleansing the Lord’s temple, and his ears in hearing the words spoken by Kṛṣṇa or about Kṛṣṇa."
> "…Indeed, Mahārāja Ambarīṣa never desired anything for his own sense gratification. He engaged all his senses in devotional service, in various engagements related to the Lord. This is the way to increase attachment for the Lord and be completely free from all material desires."
- Purport point: the first business of a devotee, according to Rupa Gosvami, is to accept a bona fide spiritual master "who can train him to engage his various senses in rendering transcendental service to the Lord"; "bhakti means to engage the senses in the service of the master of the senses, Kṛṣṇa".
- Use: in the talk (J.1).

- Purport point: "Lust for sexual intercourse is so strong" that even Brahma could not be dissuaded by his great sons, who then prayed to the Lord. "It is only by the grace of the Supreme Lord that one can be protected from the allurement of lusty material desires. The Lord gives protection to devotees who are always engaged in His transcendental loving service, and by His causeless mercy He forgives the accidental fall of a devotee." (BIKC quotes the first part of this.)
- Use: in the talk, for the Lord's protection. The sentence about forgiveness is used on bss-40.

- Purport point: "In the beginning of our spiritual life we must therefore worship Madana-mohana so that He may attract us and nullify our attachment for material sense gratification." He is called Madana-mohana because He "charms Cupid, the god of love". The purport says this relationship "is necessary for neophyte devotees".

BIKC uses this to explain why some people feel more agitation after beginning devotional life. The picture of a room that looks dirtier when first cleaned is BIKC's own, not the purport's.

## F. Philosophical explanation
**F.1 There is no quick remedy.** BIKC says plainly that "you can't just take a pill to stop sex desire", and that no mantra, charm or stone makes material desire vanish. This agrees with SB 11.20.27-28: the Lord does not say "become free at once". He says: remain happy, keep worshipping, keep faith, and repent sincerely.

**F.2 Root and leaves.** Force can cut the leaves of a plant. If the root remains, the plant grows again. This is the picture behind SB 6.1.15 and its purport. Desire is rooted in a wish to enjoy apart from Krishna. When a person takes up bhakti, the root is gradually dried, "without separate endeavor". BIKC adds a second picture, a bump in a carpet: if you press it down in one place, it rises in another. A student who is struggling with sex desire may find it appearing as anger, greed for food, or restlessness. The advice is to face the real problem with honesty and not to hide it even from oneself.

**F.3 What replaces the desire: service for all the senses.** Ambarisa engaged the mind, speech, hands, ears, eyes, touch, smell, taste, legs and head in the Lord's service (SB 9.4.18-20). The verse ends: "This is the way to increase attachment for the Lord and be completely free from all material desires." So the first answer to a restless sense is not "do not use it", but "give it a Krishna conscious engagement". For example, restless eyes can look at the Deity or read the Bhagavatam; restless hands can clean the temple or cook for Krishna; the restless tongue can chant and taste prasada. (Application (preacher's), following BIKC and the verses.)

**F.4 Be patient and keep going.** SB 11.20.27-29 and its purports are for a person who sincerely wants to be free and is still disturbed. The Lord tells him not to become depressed. The purport says that the devotee should "faithfully wait" while hearing and chanting purify the heart. The cause of discouragement is not the desire but the thought, "I am finished". The scriptures reject that thought.

**F.5 Four steps for the personal plan.** S.B. Keshava Swami, in *Desire Management* (https://tattva.keshavaswami.com/p/desire-management), says that overcoming temptation "will COST you" and lists four things. These are his words, summarised, and they match the sources above.
1. **Conviction.** Be convinced, by hearing and reading, that this discipline serves something greater (SB 11.20.27-28: "faith in the narrations of My glories").
2. **Openness.** Be honest with a friend or senior, whether you succeed or fail (NOI 4: "revealing one's mind in confidence"). BIKC says the same: a student who is excessively agitated "had better consult a senior devotee for help".
3. **Safety.** Avoid people, places and habits that provoke you (NOI 2: eating more than necessary, unnecessary mundane talk, worldly-minded company; bss-23 and bss-24 explain more).
4. **Taste.** Fill your day with a higher taste: kirtana, hearing the Bhagavatam, service, prasada, devotees' company (SB 9.4.18-20; SB 11.20.29).

**F.6 The Lord's protection.** Prabhupada's purport on SB 3.12.32 says it is only by the Lord's grace that anyone is protected from lust, and that He protects those engaged in His service. BIKC quotes a lecture of Srila Prabhupada (8 April 1975) in which he says that Krishna is Madana-mohana, the one who attracts even Cupid, so a devotee should pray, "Krishna, please save me", chant, tolerate a little and also control the body, for example by not overeating. The lecture was not found on vedabase.io during preparation, so use it only as BIKC's report (Requires source review). The same idea is in the purport of CC Adi 1.19.

**F.7 BIKC on women (ch. 43, in its own words).** "Ati-brahmacarya, 'extreme brahmacarya' may be manifested as artificial austerities or misogyny, but these are symptoms of the mode of passion." "It is a common misconception that lust can be conquered by being nasty to women, but traditional brahmacārī training is to learn to respect women." "One who feels reverence for women, considering them as mothers, cannot lust after them or want to exploit them." And: "A brahmacārī sees the body of a woman as an allurement of māyā, but also sees a soul within crying out for Kṛṣṇa." BIKC also says that "mechanical means, such as restricting association with women and dietary control, will help, the real magic formula is Kṛṣṇa consciousness, pure devotional service."

**F.8 BIKC on marriage.** BIKC (ch. 44) says: "if a brahmacārī persistently cannot control his mind, he had better get married before he falls down into illicit sex." The student makes that decision with a senior (bss-40 treats it).

**F.9 Health.** If the struggle leaves you unable to sleep, depressed, anxious, or thinking of harming yourself, a qualified counsellor or doctor is a necessary help and is fully compatible with spiritual life. In India, Tele-MANAS (14416) is a free helpline. Elsewhere, use your local crisis service. (Pastoral guidance.)

## H. Opening and engagement
**H.1 How to start.** (1) Say: "Today we will not repeat why desire is a problem. We already know that. Today we ask what to do about it." (2) Say: "Nobody has to say anything about himself. If you want to talk, I or any senior am available afterwards." (3) Read SB 11.20.27-28 slowly. Explain that "My devotee" is Krishna's own word for a person who is still struggling. (4) Ask H.2.

**H.2 Opening questions.**
1. Think of a strong habit you have changed, such as waking early. What helped you most? (Easy; any answer is fine.)
2. When a hungry person tries only to stop thinking of food, does it work? What works better?
3. In the verse we just read, what does Krishna ask the devotee to do?

## I. Core teaching points

2. **Stay enthusiastic.** SB 11.20.27-29: do not become depressed; keep hearing, chanting and serving. Transition: "What does that look like in a day?"
3. **Give every sense a Krishna conscious engagement.** SB 9.4.18-20. Transition: "What else helps?"
4. **Four steps: conviction, openness, safety, taste.** F.5. Transition: "Who is really protecting us?"

## J. Stories and illustrations
**J.1 Maharaja Ambarisa (SB 9.4.18-20).** Ambarisa was a great devotee and a king. The Bhagavatam describes how he engaged each part of himself in the Lord's service. His mind meditated on the lotus feet of Krishna. His words described the Lord's glories. His hands cleaned the Lord's temple. His ears listened to Krishna's words. His eyes looked at the Deity and at the places of Krishna's pastimes. His sense of touch was used to touch the bodies of the Lord's devotees. His nose smelled the tulasi offered to the Lord. His tongue tasted the Lord's prasada. His legs walked to holy places. His head bowed before the Lord. The text says: "Mahārāja Ambarīṣa never desired anything for his own sense gratification" (SB 9.4.20). **Devotional lesson:** he did not need to fight each desire one by one, because every sense already had a loving engagement. Prabhupada's purport says we need a spiritual master to train us in this. (Use no other detail from Ambarisa's life here; the later story of Durvasa is not needed.)

**J.2 Everyday image (Application (preacher's)).** A farmer who wants to keep the weeds out of a field does not only cut them. He plants a crop that covers the ground. If he only cuts, the weeds come back. The Krishna conscious point is that we plant a crop of hearing, chanting and service so that no empty ground is left for weeds.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections
- *If the Lord says "be happy", does this mean I should not worry about falling?* No. The same verse says the devotee "sincerely repents". He is happy and hopeful, and he also takes the problem seriously.
- *I feel more agitated since I joined. Am I going backwards?* The purport of SB 4.29.69 says that when we absorb ourselves in Krishna consciousness, the stock of old desires becomes visible, by the Lord's grace. BIKC compares this to a dusty room that looks dirtier when first cleaned. This is the preacher's reading of BIKC, not a quote from the purport. Keep chanting and talk to a senior.
- *Is it not enough to push the thought away?* BIKC warns that biting the teeth and straining eventually leads to collapse (F.2). Replace the thought with hearing, chanting or service.
- *I do not feel I can tell anyone.* This is very common. Start with one senior you trust and say only as much as you can. NOI 4 and BIKC both say it helps. You may also see a counsellor first; that is fully compatible with spiritual life.
- *What if I never feel free?* The Lord asks you to stay enthusiastic and to wait faithfully (SB 11.20.29 purport). If after real effort over time you are still unsettled, speak with a senior about whether family life with Krishna at the centre would suit you better (F.8). That is a decision, not a defeat.
- *Is the cure to avoid women completely?* BIKC says both things in its own words: "restricting association with women" is a mechanical means that "will help" but "the real magic formula is Kṛṣṇa consciousness" (ch. 43); and "For a brahmacārī, young women mean trouble" (ch. 35). It also says that "traditional brahmacārī training is to learn to respect women" (ch. 43). bss-31 and bss-32 give BIKC's rules of dealing in detail.
- *Do other methods such as exercise or cold showers help?* BIKC mentions that mechanical means "will help" but that "the real magic formula is Kṛṣṇa consciousness". This page makes no medical claim; ask a doctor about physical health.

## L. Case studies
**L.1 Invented case for discussion.** *Situation:* Rohit joined the ashram six months ago. After a long train journey for service he is troubled for several days by thoughts that he cannot shake off. He is tired and he skips two kirtanas. *Dilemma:* he thinks he is a hypocrite and wants to hide it. *Principles:* SB 11.20.27-28 (stay happy and keep worshipping), SB 6.1.15 (the root is dried by bhakti), NOI 4 (reveal the mind to a trusted senior). *Questions:* (a) What did Krishna say to a devotee in his position? (b) Which of the four steps (conviction, openness, safety, taste) would help him first? (c) What is one small action for this evening? *Analysis:* openness comes first: a short, calm talk with a senior. Safety next: rest, an early light dinner, and no idle time on the phone. Taste: return to the next kirtana, even if the mind is dull. *Takeaway:* the problem is not that Rohit has thoughts; the problem would be that he stops chanting and hides.

## M. Discussion and reflection
**M.1 Closing check questions.**
1. What does SB 11.20.27-28 tell a devotee who cannot yet give up sense enjoyment? *To remain happy and worship Krishna with faith, and to repent sincerely.*
2. What picture does SB 6.1.15's purport use? *Burnt leaves that grow again because the root remains.*
3. Name three of the senses Ambarisa engaged and how. *For example: mind on the lotus feet, ears hearing about Krishna, tongue tasting prasada (SB 9.4.18-20).*
4. What are the four steps in F.5? *Conviction, openness, safety, taste.*
5. According to the purport of SB 3.12.32, who can protect us from lust? *The Lord, by His grace; He protects those who are engaged in His service.*
6. Whom can you ask for help? *A trusted senior, a counsellor; Tele-MANAS 14416 in India or a local crisis service.*

**M.2 Activities.**
(a) *My plan (15 min):* each person writes four short lines, one for each step: one thing I will hear or read (conviction), one person I can talk to (openness), one thing I will avoid (safety), one service or kirtana I will do (taste). The pairs read them to each other. Nobody has to read to the whole room.
(b) *Give each sense a job (10 min):* in groups of four, take the list of Ambarisa's senses and write one modern engagement for each (for example: eyes, reading the Bhagavatam; hands, cooking for the Deity). One person from each group reads the list.
(c) *Anonymous question box (10 min):* the preacher answers written questions from E and F only.

**M.3 Reflection.** "What is my best 'job for the restless sense' this week?" Private.

## R. Adapting for another level

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary
Sexual desire is strong, and nobody removes it by force alone. The Bhagavatam gives a picture: if you cut the leaves of a plant but leave the root, it grows again (SB 6.1.15 purport). The root of desire is the wish to enjoy apart from Krishna. It dries up when we serve Krishna with hearing, chanting and service.

In SB 11.20.27-28 Krishna speaks to a devotee who has faith but cannot yet give up sense enjoyment. He says that the devotee should remain happy, worship Him with faith and sincerely repent. The purport says the devotee should not become depressed and should not stop his service, because hearing and chanting will slowly free him. SB 11.20.29 says that when the Lord is firmly in the heart, all material desires are destroyed, like darkness when the sun rises.

Maharaja Ambarisa engaged every sense in the Lord's service, so he never wanted anything for his own pleasure (SB 9.4.18-20). We can do the same in small ways. A student can follow four steps: be convinced by hearing and reading, be honest with a trusted senior, avoid what provokes him, and fill his day with Krishna conscious service. Behind all of it is the Lord's protection (SB 3.12.32). If the struggle is heavy, speak to a senior; if it affects your sleep or mood, see a qualified counsellor (Tele-MANAS 14416 in India, or your local crisis service).

## S2. Key ideas
- Desire is not removed by force alone. It fades as the heart is filled with the taste of serving Krishna.
- Krishna tells a struggling devotee to stay happy, keep his faith and repent sincerely.
- Every sense can be given a Krishna conscious service, as Ambarisa did.
- Four steps help: conviction, openness, safety and taste.
- The Lord protects the devotee who keeps trying.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **SB 11.20.27-28**: the devotee remains happy and worships with faith. *For me: I will not stop my service when I struggle.*
- **SB 9.4.18-20**: Ambarisa engaged all his senses in service. *For me: I will give each restless sense a Krishna conscious job.*
- **SB 11.20.29**: with the Lord in the heart, desires are destroyed. *For me: I will be patient while hearing and chanting do their work.*

## S4. Words to know
- **Lust (kama):** the desire for sense pleasure, especially sexual.
- **Anartha:** an unwanted habit in the heart.
- **Madana-mohana:** a name of Krishna, "He who charms even Cupid".
- **Repent:** to feel sincere regret and decide to do better.
- **Brahmacari:** a celibate student in devotional training.

## S5. Questions and answers
- *Why doesn't force work?* Because the root remains and the desire grows again.
- *What does Krishna ask of a devotee who still has desires?* To remain happy, worship with faith and repent sincerely (SB 11.20.27-28).
- *I feel worse since joining. Is that normal?* Many people feel this; the purport of SB 4.29.69 explains that old desires become visible. Keep chanting and talk to a senior.
- *Whom can I tell?* One senior you trust. A counsellor is also fine.
- *What if I do not improve?* Stay enthusiastic and talk to your senior about all options, including family life with Krishna at the centre.

## S6. Check your understanding
1. In SB 6.1.15's purport, what grows again if the root remains?
2. In SB 11.20.27-28, what does Krishna say a struggling devotee should do?
3. Which king engaged all his senses in service?
4. Name the four steps of the plan.
5. Who protects the devotee from lust, according to SB 3.12.32's purport?

**Answers:** 1. The plant (desire). 2. Remain happy, worship with faith and repent sincerely. 3. Maharaja Ambarisa. 4. Conviction, openness, safety, taste. 5. The Lord, by His grace.
