# Dharmah projjhita-kaitavah: religion without cheating

> **TRD · up to 180 min · review: drafted.** Group C (non-duplicity), first page. The foundation for trd-15, trd-16 and
> trd-17: a temple resident serves Krishna with no hidden motive, and knows the four ways religion is used to cheat.

## A. Topic overview

| | |
|---|---|
| Batch / level | TRD (temple residents: ashram brahmacaris and full-time preachers) |
| Suggested duration | Up to 3 hours; the main plan in G takes about 150 minutes |
| Preparation time | About 2½ hours the first time: SB 1.1.2 and CC Adi 1.90–92 with purports, SB 1.2.6–10 with purports |
| Difficulty | Advanced |
| Prerequisites | DPS-10 (service attitude), DPS-11 (humility); the resident already follows the four regulative principles and chants sixteen rounds |
| Audience | Men who have given their lives to the mission and now must examine *why* they do what they do |
| Teaching objective | Participants can explain what the Bhagavatam means by cheating religion (kaitava-dharma), locate the four cheating motives in their own service honestly, and know the one remedy: hearing the Bhagavatam submissively and serving without motive |

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

A new devotee is taught *what* to do: chant, follow the principles, serve. A temple resident has done that for
years. His danger is subtler: doing the right things for a wrong reason, and not noticing. The second verse of
Srimad-Bhagavatam names that danger (*kaitava*, cheating) and rejects it completely (*projjhita*). This page puts the
verse at the centre of a resident's self-examination.

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| VL1 | Not taught as a topic; the idea appears only as "serve Krishna without asking anything back" |
| VL2 | Religion is more than rituals for rewards; introduce the four goals (dharma, artha, kama, moksa) and why Krishna consciousness is above them |
| VL3 | SB 1.2.6 as the definition of real dharma (unmotivated, uninterrupted); regulated practice as the way there |
| SPS | SB 1.2.8–10 in a talk on "why my service feels like labour": motive checks for a preacher-in-training |
| DPS | Covered partly in DPS-10 (service attitude) and the stages of bhakti (DPS-12…18); this page is not repeated there |
| BSS | Builds on BIKC's sense-control chapters: the brahmacari's motive for austerity, not only its practice |
| **TRD (here)** | The full verse and purport, the four kinds of cheating including the desire for liberation, and the honest audit of a resident's own motives |

**Not done here:** the desire for fame and position (trd-15), truthfulness with authorities (trd-16), money (trd-17).
These three grow from this page.

## C. Central message

The Bhagavatam "completely rejects all religious activities which are materially motivated" (SB 1.1.2). A temple
resident's service is real religion only when its motive is Krishna's pleasure, not pious merit, money, comfort or
even liberation. Hearing the Bhagavatam submissively is the way to find and remove the hidden motive.

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Translate *dharmah projjhita-kaitavah* word by word and explain *projjhita* ("complete rejection") from the purport.
2. Name the four cheating goals (religiosity, economic development, sense gratification, liberation; CC Adi 1.90) and say why Prabhupada calls even liberation "the foremost process of cheating" (CC Adi 1.92).
3. State the definition of real dharma in SB 1.2.6 (unmotivated, uninterrupted) and the test in SB 1.2.8 (does my work produce attraction for Krishna's message?).
4. Examine their own service against SB 1.2.9–10 without despair, and know what to do with a motive they find (SB 2.3.10: keep serving, with intensity).
5. Explain why submissive hearing of the Bhagavatam, not analysis alone, removes the cheating (SB 1.1.2, SB 1.2.17).

## E. Scriptural foundation

**SB 1.1.2** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/1/2/
> "Completely rejecting all religious activities which are materially motivated, this Bhāgavata Purāṇa propounds the highest truth, which is understandable by those devotees who are fully pure in heart… As soon as one attentively and submissively hears the message of Bhāgavatam, by this culture of knowledge the Supreme Lord is established within his heart."
- **Context:** The second of the three opening verses of the Bhagavatam, spoken by Vyasadeva.
- **Teaching:** The Bhagavatam is for those who are "nonenvious and pure in heart" (*nirmatsaranam satam*); it rejects every religion that is a form of material motive.
- **Purport points:** Religion has four primary subjects: "pious activities, economic development, satisfaction of the senses, and finally liberation from material bondage." People "perform pious activities or religious functions in order to gain something material… religion is performed for some economic gains." Even salvation is included: "all these states are simply different types of sense gratification." The devotees "rise above such competitions… they are well-wishers to everyone." The method is given in the verse: "The desire to sincerely hear is the first qualification." The purport warns that "a challenging attitude cannot help one realize this transcendental message."
- **Application (preacher's):** A resident can be busy, respected and regulated while his motive is still one of the four. The verse does not ask him to stop serving; it asks him to let the Bhagavatam expose and remove the motive.
- **Use:** in the talk, as the central text. Put the Sanskrit and translation on the board.

**CC Adi 1.90–92** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/adi/1/91/
> 1.90: "The darkness of ignorance is called kaitava, the way of cheating, which begins with religiosity, economic development, sense gratification and liberation."
> 1.92: "The foremost process of cheating is to desire to achieve liberation by merging into the Supreme, for this causes the permanent disappearance of loving service to Kṛṣṇa."
- **Context:** Krishnadasa Kaviraja quotes SB 1.1.2 (as 1.91) and explains it.
- **Teaching:** The four goals are the four forms of cheating; liberation is the worst.
- **Purport point (1.91):** "A distinction between real religion and pretentious religion has been clearly made." Real religion "is his natural inborn quality"; pretentious religion "is a form of nescience that artificially covers a living entity's pure consciousness." Karma-kanda "even when decorated by religious ceremonies… is a cheating process"; the paths of knowledge and mystic power are "equally hazardous"; "Any process of religiosity based on sense gratification, gross or subtle, must be considered a pretentious religion." On *projjhita*: "Pra- means 'complete,' and ujjhita indicates rejection."
- **Purport point (1.92):** "The desire to merge into the impersonal Brahman is the subtlest type of atheism."
- **Use:** in the talk, immediately after SB 1.1.2.

**SB 1.2.6** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/6/
> "The supreme occupation [dharma] for all humanity is that by which men can attain to loving devotional service unto the transcendent Lord. Such devotional service must be unmotivated and uninterrupted to completely satisfy the self."
- **Purport point:** "Adoption of devotional service for material gain is certainly an obstruction to the progressive path of renunciation." Devotional service "must be pure in quality, i.e., without the least desire for material enjoyment." Dharma means "that which sustains one's existence," and the relation is "one of the servant and the served."
- **Use:** in the talk: the positive definition that SB 1.1.2's rejection protects.

**SB 1.2.8** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/8/
> "The occupational activities a man performs according to his own position are only so much useless labor if they do not provoke attraction for the message of the Personality of Godhead."
- **Use:** in the talk, as the practical test for a resident's many duties.

**SB 1.2.9–10** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/9/
> 1.2.9: "All occupational engagements are certainly meant for ultimate liberation. They should never be performed for material gain. Furthermore, according to sages, one who is engaged in the ultimate occupational service should never use material gain to cultivate sense gratification."
> 1.2.10: "Life's desires should never be directed toward sense gratification. One should desire only a healthy life, or self-preservation, since a human being is meant for inquiry about the Absolute Truth."
- **Purport points:** "The general tendency of any ordinary man in any part of the world is to gain some material profit in exchange for religious or any other occupational service" (1.2.9); "in religion or even in salvation, the very same tint of sense gratification is ever-increasingly predominant" (1.2.10).
- **Use:** in the talk (the audit section).

**BG 7.16** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/7/16/
> "Four kinds of pious men begin to render devotional service unto Me – the distressed, the desirer of wealth, the inquisitive, and he who is searching for knowledge of the Absolute."
- **Purport point:** "These are not pure devotees, because they have some aspiration to fulfill in exchange for devotional service. Pure devotional service is without aspiration and without desire for material profit."
- **Use:** in the talk: Krishna Himself says mixed motives are how most people begin, and that is not condemned.

**SB 2.3.10** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/2/3/10/
> "A person who has broader intelligence, whether he be full of all material desire, without any material desire, or desiring liberation, must by all means worship the supreme whole, the Personality of Godhead."
- **Purport point:** "Desireless means, therefore, not to be inert like the stone, but to be conscious of one's actual position and thus desire satisfaction only from the Supreme Lord." Prabhupada cites Jiva Gosvami's Sandarbha: *bhajaniya-parama-purusa-sukha-matra-sva-sukhatvam*, "one should feel happy only by experiencing the happiness of the Supreme Lord."
- **Use:** in the talk: the answer to "I found a motive; what now?"

**CC Madhya 19.167** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/19/167/
> "When first-class devotional service develops, one must be devoid of all material desires, knowledge obtained by monistic philosophy, and fruitive action. The devotee must constantly serve Kṛṣṇa favorably, as Kṛṣṇa desires."
- **Use:** in the talk: Rupa Gosvami's definition (BRS 1.1.11) as the positive standard.

**CC Antya 20.29** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/20/29/
> "O Lord of the universe, I do not desire material wealth, materialistic followers, a beautiful wife or fruitive activities described in flowery language. All I want, life after life, is unmotivated devotional service to You."
- **Use:** closing prayer of the session (Siksastaka 4; the full Siksastaka is trd-12).

**CC Madhya 19.159** — Verified. "Diplomatic behavior" (*kutinati*) is a weed beside the bhakti creeper; the purport: "If we treat people diplomatically or duplicitously, our spiritual advancement is obstructed." *Use:* the bridge to trd-15 and trd-16.

**BG 3.6** — Verified. One who restrains the senses outwardly "but whose mind dwells on sense objects… is called a pretender." *Use:* K (the inner side of cheating).

**SB 1.2.17** — Verified. Krishna is "the benefactor of the truthful devotee" and "cleanses desire for material enjoyment from the heart of the devotee who has developed the urge to hear His messages." *Use:* in the talk: the remedy is hearing.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** The word *dharma* has two uses in the Bhagavatam's opening. In SB 1.1.2, *dharma* with
   *kaitava* (cheating) is "religion" as people normally practise it: pious acts done to get something. In SB 1.2.6,
   *paro dharmah* is the real thing: loving service to Krishna that is "unmotivated and uninterrupted." The
   Bhagavatam throws out the first (*projjhita*, completely) to establish the second.
2. **The four cheatings.** Religiosity (pious merit), economic development, sense gratification and liberation
   (CC Adi 1.90). The first three are easy to see as material. The fourth is the subtle one: wanting to end my own
   suffering by merging into God is still about *me*. That is why Prabhupada calls it "the subtlest type of atheism"
   (CC Adi 1.92 purport), and why the Bhagavatam is for the *nirmatsara*, the nonenvious, those not competing even
   with God.
3. **Why it matters for a resident.** The same four motives can live inside temple service: "I have done so much,
   Krishna owes me" (merit); attachment to the facilities and position service brings (wealth); the comfort of being
   praised and fed (sense gratification); "I want relief from this struggle" (liberation, which SB 1.2.10's purport
   says carries "the very same tint of sense gratification"). Nothing outward changes; only the heart does. SB 1.2.8
   gives the test: does my work make me more attracted to hearing about Krishna, or less?
4. **Honesty without despair.** Krishna says most people begin with a motive (BG 7.16), and the Bhagavatam tells even
   the person "full of all material desire" to worship "by all means" (SB 2.3.10). Finding a motive is a reason to keep
   serving and hear more, not to stop or hide; SB 2.3.10's purport states the goal: happy only in Krishna's happiness.
5. **The remedy is hearing, not self-analysis alone.** SB 1.1.2 ends with the method: hear "attentively and
   submissively," and the Lord "is established within his heart." SB 1.2.17: Krishna Himself "cleanses desire for
   material enjoyment from the heart" of the one who wants to hear. A resident who stops hearing the morning class
   loses the very instrument that exposes his motives.
6. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *"Rejecting material motives means rejecting activity."* No: the Bhagavatam rejects the motive, not the work
     (SB 1.2.9: occupational engagements are meant for liberation, "never for material gain").
   - *"Wanting liberation is a good sign."* It is the "foremost process of cheating" when it means merging and losing
     service (CC Adi 1.92).
   - *"I should be ashamed of every motive I find."* The Lord's own words admit the mixed beginner (BG 7.16). Shame is
     not the method; hearing is.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet the residents and say: "Today's verse is the second verse of Srimad-Bhagavatam. We all know it. We will ask
   what it says about *us*, who already live in the temple."
2. Write on the board: *dharmah projjhita-kaitavo 'tra*, and under it: "completely rejecting all religious activities
   which are materially motivated."
3. Say: "Nobody in this room is being accused. The verse examines me first. We will do a private audit; nothing is
   shared unless you choose."
4. Ask H.2 question 1 for private writing (two minutes).
5. Ask question 2 aloud; take two or three answers, thank each by name; then begin the verse.

**H.2 Opening questions**
1. (Private.) Think of one service you did this week that nobody saw. Why did you do it? Write the honest reason.
2. When people outside the temple do religious acts, what do they usually hope to get? (Expected: health, wealth,
   good marriage, a good next life, peace.)
3. Is there any goal a devotee could want that would still be cheating? (Let them guess; the answer comes with
   CC Adi 1.92.)

## I. Core teaching points

1. **The Bhagavatam rejects cheating religion completely.** *Pra-ujjhita* means complete rejection (CC Adi 1.91
   purport). Religion as most people practise it is "performed for some economic gains" (SB 1.1.2 purport). *Example:*
   a man fasts for a promotion; if the promotion comes another way, "so-called religion is neglected" (purport).
   *Transition:* what exactly counts as cheating?
2. **Four cheatings, and the fourth is the worst.** Religiosity, economic development, sense gratification,
   liberation (CC Adi 1.90). Liberation by merging "causes the permanent disappearance of loving service to Kṛṣṇa"
   (CC Adi 1.92). *Example:* a tired resident's "I just want this to be over" is the fourth motive in everyday dress.
   *Transition:* then what is real dharma?
3. **Real dharma is unmotivated and uninterrupted service.** SB 1.2.6; Rupa Gosvami's definition (CC Madhya 19.167:
   free of material desire, monistic knowledge and fruitive action; serving Krishna "as Kṛṣṇa desires"). *Transition:*
   how do I test my own work?
4. **The test: does my work increase attraction to Krishna's message?** SB 1.2.8: otherwise it is "useless labor."
   SB 1.2.9–10: never for material gain, never using gain for sense gratification, wanting only a healthy life for
   inquiry. *Audit for a resident (Application, preacher's):* my preaching, my cooking, my management, my travel: after
   each, am I more eager to hear the Bhagavatam, or more eager for the next appreciation? *Transition:* and if I find a
   motive?
5. **Keep serving, with intensity, and hear more.** Krishna names the four kinds of mixed beginners without contempt
   (BG 7.16). The person "full of all material desire… must by all means worship" (SB 2.3.10). Krishna cleanses the
   heart of "the devotee who has developed the urge to hear" (SB 1.2.17). *Transition:* the first weed this exposes
   is diplomacy (CC Madhya 19.159), and the next pages (trd-15, trd-16) take it up.

## J. Stories and illustrations

**Narada corrects Vyasadeva (SB 1.5).** Vyasa had compiled all the Vedas and felt dissatisfied. Narada told him that
he had not sufficiently glorified the Lord: knowledge free of material affinity "does not look well if devoid of a
conception of the Infallible" (SB 1.5.12), and fruitive work is useless "if not utilized for the devotional service of
the Lord." The result was Srimad-Bhagavatam. *Connection:* even the greatest religious work, done without devotion
as its heart, left its author unsatisfied; the cure was to write about Krishna alone. *Use:* in the talk, five
minutes, as the origin of SB 1.1.2.

**Lord Caitanya's fourth prayer (CC Antya 20.29).** The Lord Himself, in the mood of a devotee, prays: no wealth, no
followers, no beautiful wife, no flowery fruitive activity; "all I want, life after life, is unmotivated devotional
service to You." *Connection:* this is *dharmah projjhita-kaitavah* spoken as a prayer. *Use:* to close.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"I joined the ashram sincerely. Why examine my motives now?"**
   - Because motives change quietly as service brings respect and facility. The Bhagavatam was spoken to experienced
     religious performers (J). SB 1.2.8 gives a test that takes one minute: is my attraction to hearing growing?
2. **"Isn't wanting liberation what every scripture recommends?"**
   - The Bhagavatam places liberation among the cheatings when it means merging and losing service (CC Adi 1.90, 1.92).
     A devotee wants service "life after life" (CC Antya 20.29), and liberation follows as a by-product.
3. **"If I find a material motive, should I stop that service until my heart is pure?"**
   - No. "Whether he be full of all material desire… must by all means worship" (SB 2.3.10). Keep the service, add
     hearing, and tell a senior devotee what you found (NOI 4 is the method; see trd-16).
4. **"How can I tell a pure motive from a mixed one, if I cannot see my own heart?"**
   - By its fruit over time (SB 1.2.8: attraction to Krishna's message) and by behaviour: straightforward dealings
     (CC Madhya 19.159 purport) and a mind not dwelling on what the hands gave up (BG 3.6). And by hearing: the Lord
     cleanses what we cannot see (SB 1.2.17).

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real people.*

**Case 1: The successful preacher.** Govinda has brought forty students to the Sunday programme in a year. He is
praised in meetings. Lately he notices he feels flat when another preacher's numbers are mentioned, and he has started
skipping the morning Bhagavatam class to prepare his sessions.
- **Dilemma:** His service is excellent. Is anything wrong?
- **Principles:** SB 1.2.8 (the test: attraction to the message), SB 1.1.2 purport (the devotee is nonenvious, above
  competition), SB 1.2.17 (hearing cleanses).
- **Questions:** Which of the four cheatings is closest to what Govinda feels? What has he stopped doing? What is one
  change for this week?
- **Analysis:** Competitive flatness points to adoration as the quiet motive (the third cheating in a subtle form).
  The lost hearing is both symptom and cause. The remedy is not to preach less but to return to the class and to tell
  his mentor what he noticed.
- **Takeaway:** Success is not the problem; the motive that grows beside success is, and hearing exposes it.

**Case 2: The resident who wants out.** Madhava, six years in the ashram, says privately: "I just want peace. I am
tired of people. I want to go to the dhama and chant alone until I leave this body."
- **Principles:** CC Adi 1.92 (the desire for release as the subtlest cheating), SB 2.3.10 purport (desireless means
  to want only the Lord's happiness, not to be inert), SB 1.2.10 (even salvation can carry the tint of sense
  gratification).
- **Questions and analysis:** Fatigue is real and needs care (trd-20), but "relief for me" is the fourth motive; a
  devotee's prayer is "service life after life" (CC Antya 20.29). He should reveal his mind to his authority (trd-16),
  rest if needed, and keep service at the centre. *Takeaway:* even the wish to escape is offered to Krishna and examined.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first; thank each answer)
1. What does *kaitava* mean? *Expected:* cheating.
2. What four goals does CC Adi 1.90 call cheating? *Expected:* religiosity, economic development, sense gratification, liberation.
3. Which of the four is the "foremost" cheating, and why? *Expected:* liberation by merging, because it ends loving service (CC Adi 1.92).
4. What two qualities must devotional service have according to SB 1.2.6? *Expected:* unmotivated and uninterrupted.
5. What is the one-line test in SB 1.2.8? *Expected:* does my work produce attraction for the Lord's message?
6. If I find a material motive in my service, what should I do? *Expected:* keep serving with intensity (SB 2.3.10), hear more (SB 1.1.2, 1.2.17), and tell a senior devotee.

**M.2 Activities**
- **Option 1: The private audit (15 min).** Each resident lists his three main services and writes beside each the
  honest answer to two questions: "After this service, do I want to hear about Krishna more or less?" and "Which of
  the four cheatings would most easily hide here?" Nothing is shared. Close by each writing one sentence of prayer in
  the mood of CC Antya 20.29.
- **Option 2: Four corners (20 min).** Four groups take one cheating each and prepare two invented examples of how it
  could appear inside temple life (about no one present); each reports in two minutes; the speaker names the verse that
  exposes each.

**M.3 Reflection**
- Why does the Bhagavatam say the remedy is *hearing* (SB 1.1.2, 1.2.17) rather than simply trying harder to be
  sincere? What does this say about where the power to purify comes from?

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

The second verse of Srimad-Bhagavatam says that this book "completely rejects all religious activities which are
materially motivated" (SB 1.1.2). The Sanskrit words are *dharmah projjhita-kaitavah*: *kaitava* means cheating, and
*projjhita* means completely thrown out. Srila Prabhupada's purport explains that ordinary religion has four subjects:
pious acts, economic development, sense gratification and liberation, and that most people practise religion "for some
economic gains." Caitanya-caritamrta repeats this: the four goals are "the way of cheating," and the worst of the four
is the wish for liberation by merging into God, because it ends loving service for ever (CC Adi 1.90, 1.92).

What is left when cheating is removed? SB 1.2.6 answers: the supreme dharma is loving devotional service to Krishna,
and it "must be unmotivated and uninterrupted." Rupa Gosvami says the same: pure devotion is free of material desire,
of the wish to merge, and of fruitive action, and serves Krishna "as Kṛṣṇa desires" (CC Madhya 19.167). SB 1.2.8
gives a simple test for anyone, including a temple resident: if my work does not increase my attraction to hearing
about Krishna, it is "useless labor." SB 1.2.9–10 add that duties should never be done for material gain, and that gain
should never be used for sense enjoyment.

Finding a mixed motive is not a disaster. Krishna says people with motives do begin devotional service (BG 7.16), and
the Bhagavatam tells even the person "full of all material desire" to worship Krishna "by all means" (SB 2.3.10). The
remedy is to keep serving and to hear the Bhagavatam "attentively and submissively" (SB 1.1.2), because Krishna
"cleanses desire for material enjoyment from the heart of the devotee who has developed the urge to hear" (SB 1.2.17).
The session ended with Lord Caitanya's prayer for "unmotivated devotional service… life after life" (CC Antya 20.29).

## S2. Key ideas

- *Kaitava-dharma*, cheating religion, means doing religious acts to get something: merit, money, enjoyment, or even liberation (SB 1.1.2; CC Adi 1.90).
- Liberation by merging is the foremost cheating because it ends loving service (CC Adi 1.92).
- Real dharma is service to Krishna that is unmotivated and uninterrupted (SB 1.2.6; CC Madhya 19.167).
- Test: does my service increase my attraction to hearing about Krishna? (SB 1.2.8)
- A motive found is a reason to serve harder and hear more, not to stop (SB 2.3.10; BG 7.16; SB 1.2.17).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **SB 1.1.2:** "Completely rejecting all religious activities which are materially motivated…" *For me:* my service is examined by its motive, not its size.
- **SB 1.2.6:** "Such devotional service must be unmotivated and uninterrupted to completely satisfy the self." *For me:* the goal is stated positively; it is possible.
- **SB 1.2.8:** work that does not "provoke attraction for the message of the Personality of Godhead" is "useless labor." *For me:* a one-minute daily test.
- **CC Antya 20.29:** "All I want, life after life, is unmotivated devotional service to You." *For me:* a prayer to say when I notice a motive.

## S4. Words to know

- **Dharma:** here, "occupation," that which sustains one's existence; real dharma is service to Krishna (SB 1.2.6 purport).
- **Kaitava:** cheating; religion practised for a material return (CC Adi 1.90).
- **Projjhita:** completely rejected; *pra* = complete, *ujjhita* = rejection (CC Adi 1.91 purport).
- **Ahaituki, apratihata:** unmotivated, uninterrupted (SB 1.2.6).

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Is wanting liberation wrong?** The Bhagavatam counts liberation by merging among the cheatings, because it ends service (CC Adi 1.90, 1.92). A devotee prays for service "life after life" (CC Antya 20.29).
2. **I found a selfish motive in my service. Should I stop?** No. "Whether he be full of all material desire… must by all means worship" (SB 2.3.10). Keep serving, hear more, and tell a senior devotee.
3. **Isn't pious merit good?** Better than sin, but still cheating when it is the goal (SB 1.1.2 purport; SB 1.2.9).
4. **How do I know my motive, when I cannot see my own heart?** By the fruit over time: more attraction to hearing (SB 1.2.8), and by Krishna's own cleansing when I hear eagerly (SB 1.2.17).

## S6. Check your understanding

1. What are the four goals that CC Adi 1.90 calls "the way of cheating"?
2. Which of them is called the "foremost" cheating, and why (CC Adi 1.92)?
3. Name the two qualities of real devotional service in SB 1.2.6.
4. True or false: SB 1.2.8 says that any occupational duty done carefully is spiritually valuable.
5. According to SB 2.3.10, what should a person "full of all material desire" do?

**Answers:** 1. Religiosity, economic development, sense gratification, liberation (CC Adi 1.90). 2. Liberation by
merging, because it "causes the permanent disappearance of loving service to Kṛṣṇa" (CC Adi 1.92). 3. Unmotivated and
uninterrupted (SB 1.2.6). 4. False: it is "useless labor" if it does not produce attraction for the Lord's message
(SB 1.2.8). 5. Worship the Supreme Personality of Godhead "by all means" (SB 2.3.10).
