## C. Central message

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura called the printing press the brhad-mrdanga, the "greater drum": a kirtana
party is heard a few streets away, but a printed book is heard across the world. He told the young Abhay Charan De in
1922 to preach Lord Caitanya's message throughout the world, and in 1936 that he could become "a very good English
preacher". ISKCON's printing, preaching and temples are those instructions carried out; a resident serving in any of
them is sounding the great drum.

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. State what Srila Prabhupada said his Guru Maharaja meant by "brhad-mrdanga", quoting one verified letter.
2. Tell the 1922 meeting and the 1936 letter as the *Lilamrta* gives them, without addition.
3. Explain from SB 1.5.11 and CC Antya 20.12 why transcendental literature and sankirtana change the world's atmosphere.
4. Name the three fields Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati worked in (printing, preaching, temples) with one dated fact each.
5. Place one of their own services inside his plan and say how to do it in his spirit.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**SB 1.5.11** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/5/11/
> "On the other hand, that literature which is full of descriptions of the transcendental glories of the name, fame, forms, pastimes, etc., of the unlimited Supreme Lord is a different creation, full of transcendental words directed toward bringing about a revolution in the impious lives of this world's misdirected civilization. Such transcendental literatures, even though imperfectly composed, are heard, sung and accepted by purified men who are thoroughly honest."
- **Context:** Narada Muni to Vyasadeva, after criticising literature that does not glorify the Lord (1.5.10).
- **Teaching:** Literature about the Lord is "a different creation", meant for "a revolution in the impious lives" of civilisation.
- **Purport point:** A saint "is always absorbed in glorifying the Supreme Lord because by broadcasting the holy name and fame of the Supreme Lord, the polluted atmosphere of the world will change, and as a result of propagating the transcendental literatures like Srimad-Bhagavatam, people will become sane in their transactions." Written during the 1962 China–India conflict, the purport says the quarrel comes from "the polluted atmosphere of this age" and that there is "a great need for disseminating the message of Srimad-Bhagavatam all over the world."
- **Application (preacher's):** This is the scriptural basis of the brhad-mrdanga: the book is a revolution in print.
- **Use:** in the talk; the central verse.

**CC Antya 20.12** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/20/12/
> "Let there be all victory for the chanting of the holy name of Lord Kṛṣṇa, which can cleanse the mirror of the heart and stop the miseries of the blazing fire of material existence…"
- **Context:** The first verse of Lord Caitanya's Siksastaka (the purport lists where the other seven are found).
- **Teaching:** *Param vijayate sri-krsna-sankirtanam*: the chanting is victorious. The mrdanga accompanies it; the "greater mrdanga" carries it further.
- **Use:** in the talk, read in Sanskrit and English before introducing the press.

**NOI 3** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/3/
- **Teaching:** Six favourable principles; the sixth is "following in the footsteps of the previous acaryas".
- **Purport point:** The purport describes how the movement began with no response and continued with patience (see dps-15).
- **Use:** in the talk: following Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati's methods is this sixth principle in practice.

**CC Adi 9.41** — Verified (treated in trd-01). The order Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati carried and passed on.

**SB 1.2.18** — Verified (treated in dps-15). Hearing the Bhagavatam and serving the devotee-Bhagavata establishes devotion; the two things the press and the preacher provide.

**Srila Prabhupada on his guru's "brhad-mrdanga" (letters, Verified on vedabase):**
- **To Kirtanananda and Hayagriva, Montreal, 23 August 1968** · https://vedabase.io/en/library/letters/letter-to-kīrtanānanda-hayagriva/
  > "my Guru Maharaja's opinion is that press is the brihat mrdanga, or the biggest, or the greater mrdanga. The sound of press goes long long distance, long distant places, so the organization of press and literature and public sales, should be our main business."
- **To Satsvarupa, Tittenhurst, 14 September 1969** · https://vedabase.io/en/library/letters/letter-to-satsvarupa-65/
  > "my Guru Maharaja drew a picture in which He gave great importance to the symbolic representation of the press next to the mrdanga. Press means publication of various types of books and literatures and the mrdanga means Sankirtana Party."
- **To Brahmananda, Los Angeles, 20 April 1970** · https://vedabase.io/en/library/letters/letter-to-brahmananda-137/
  > "This ISKCON Press insignia is very appropriately drawn. It bears the real meaning of mrdanga or press and mrdanga are two parallel lines."
- **To Tamal Krishna, 15 May 1970** (quoted in the Preaching Encyclopedia, "Inspirational Quotes on Book Distribution"; **Partially verified**: vedabase page not yet located): "My Guru Maharaja liked the publication of books very nice. He liked publications of books more than construction of Temples."
- **Use:** in the talk; the first three are read verbatim.

**Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati to Srila Prabhupada (BBT *Srila Prabhupada-lilamrta*, Verified text):**
- **1922, Calcutta (vol. 1 ch. 3)** · https://vedabase.io/en/library/spl/1/3/ : before any conversation had begun, he said to Abhay and his friend Naren: "You are educated young men. Why don't you preach Lord Caitanya Mahaprabhu's message throughout the whole world?"
- **December 1936, letter (vol. 1 ch. 4, epigraph)** · https://vedabase.io/en/library/spl/1/4/ : "I have every hope that you can turn yourself into a very good English preacher if you serve the mission to inculcate the novel impression of Lord Chaitanya's teachings to the people in general as well as philosophers and religionists."
- **His own words on Lord Caitanya's benefit (vol. 1 ch. 3, epigraph):** "There has not been, there will not be, such benefactors of the highest merit as [Chaitanya] Mahaprabhu and His devotees have been. The offer of other benefits is only a deception; it is rather a great harm, whereas the benefit done by Him and His followers is the truest and greatest eternal benefit."
- **The 1935 Radha-kunda instruction** (vol. 1 ch. 4): the *Lilamrta* records that at Radha-kunda in November 1935 he advised Abhay that if he ever got money he should print books. **Requires source review** for the exact wording; tell it in paraphrase.
- **Use:** in the talk and in J.

**Facts from *A Ray of Vishnu* (biography; events only, edition under review):** the Bhagavat Printing Press
(Sri Bhagavat-yantra) was established on 6 February 1913 at 4 Sanagar Lane, Kalighat, Calcutta; he called it the
"Brihat Mridanga", explaining that a kirtana party is heard a few blocks away but this drum far and wide; it printed
the Caitanya-caritamrta with Bhaktivinoda Thakura's Amrta-pravaha-bhasya and his own Anubhasya, the Bhagavatam and
other books, and was moved to Vrajapattana (1914) and Krishnanagar (1915). The *Lilamrta* (vol. 1 ch. 4) adds that
his mission printed a daily Bengali paper, *Nadiya Prakasa*, and the English *Harmonist*, and that he "had been
speaking regularly about sambandha, abhidheya, and prayojana" (see trd-23).

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **Why a drum?** In Lord Caitanya's sankirtana the mrdanga carries the holy name through a street (CC Antya 20.12). Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati saw that a book carries the same name across continents and years; so he called the press the greater drum and, as Srila Prabhupada records, drew the press beside the mrdanga (letter, 14 Sep 1969). The theology is SB 1.5.11: literature about the Lord is a "different creation" that works "a revolution" in the world's atmosphere.
2. **Three fields, one purpose.** He printed (the press from 1913; a daily spiritual newspaper), he preached (sending sannyasis to Europe by 1933–35, the *Lilamrta* notes that he "welcomed back the preachers he had sent to Europe" in 1935), and he founded temples and the Gaudiya Matha (research/iyf-materials/dps-44 biography, ch. 10). ISKCON repeats the pattern: BBT, travelling preachers, temples.
3. **Books above buildings.** "He liked publications of books more than construction of Temples" (letter 15 May 1970, verify wording). Residents should know this order of priority when temple projects compete with printing and distribution.
4. **The order to Srila Prabhupada.** 1922: preach throughout the whole world. 1936: become a very good English preacher. 1935: print books. Everything in trd-03 (the voyage) is the execution of these three sentences. The order came before the qualification, as the Jaladuta diary says.
5. **Following the previous acaryas (NOI 3).** Using his methods is not nostalgia; it is the sixth favourable principle. His standards for the Matha (early rising, cleanliness, study, strict dealings) are the temple standards residents keep.
6. **His own measure of benefit.** "There has not been, there will not be, such benefactors of the highest merit as Mahaprabhu and His devotees"; "the offer of other benefits is only a deception". This is CC Adi 9.41's purport in his voice (trd-01).
7. **Misunderstandings.** (a) "The press was a business": SB 1.5.11 says what the literature is for. (b) "He was a scholar, not a missionary": he sent preachers abroad and told a young stranger to preach to the world. (c) "His line is only Srila Prabhupada's": true for us as ISKCON's founder-acarya; the Gaudiya Matha's other branches are not discussed here (dps-43).

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Literature as revolution (SB 1.5.11).** Books about the Lord are a "different creation" meant to change "the polluted atmosphere of the world". Example: the purport was written during a war and names the cure as spreading the Bhagavatam. Transition: "Our param-guru gave this a name."
2. **The greater drum (letters 1968, 1969, 1970).** "The sound of press goes long long distance… the organization of press and literature and public sales, should be our main business." Example: a resident's two hours at a book table reach people who will never hear the kirtana. Transition: "He did not only say it."
3. **He built it (1913).** The Bhagavat Printing Press at Kalighat, named Brihat Mridanga, printing the Caitanya-caritamrta with Bhaktivinoda's commentary and his own. Example: our BBT catalogue is this press continued. Transition: "What else did he build?"
4. **Three fields (printing, preaching, temples).** A daily spiritual newspaper, preachers sent to Europe, the Gaudiya Matha's temples. Example: every resident's service falls in one of the three. Transition: "And what did he ask of our founder?"
5. **The order (1922, 1935, 1936).** "Why don't you preach Lord Caitanya Mahaprabhu's message throughout the whole world?"; print books; "a very good English preacher". Example: trd-03's voyage is these sentences obeyed thirty years later. Transition to J.

## J. Stories and illustrations

**The first meeting, Calcutta 1922 (*Lilamrta* vol. 1 ch. 3, told as it tells it).** Abhay's friend Narendranath Mullik
insisted that he meet a sadhu from Mayapur at Ultadanga Junction Road. Abhay refused at first, saying he knew "all these
sadhus", but was pulled along. On the roof of the building they found Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati sitting with a few
disciples. Before any conversation, he said: "You are educated young men. Why don't you preach Lord Caitanya
Mahaprabhu's message throughout the whole world?" Abhay "could hardly believe what he had just heard" and went on to
argue, as the chapter records, before being won over. Devotional lesson: the missionary acarya saw a preacher in a
stranger and gave the order first.

**The last letter, December 1936 (*Lilamrta* vol. 1 ch. 4).** Weeks before his departure he wrote to Abhay that he
had "every hope that you can turn yourself into a very good English preacher". Lesson: the order of 1922 was repeated
and made specific; the whole English BBT library follows from it.

**The Harmonist advertisement, 1932 (*Lilamrta* vol. 1 ch. 4).** His English journal invited "the co-operation of all
persons of every nationality, irrespective of caste, creed, colour, age, or sex" to the Vraja-mandala parikrama.
Lesson: the world mission was already in his own words a generation before ISKCON.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"Is 'brhad-mrdanga' Srila Prabhupada's phrase or his guru's?"** Srila Prabhupada attributes it to his Guru Maharaja in three letters (1968, 1969, 1970); the biography records that Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati named his 1913 press so.
2. **"Why books more than temples, when we live in a temple?"** Because the temple's purpose is to give Krishna to people (trd-01), and a book reaches further than a building. Temples remain necessary: he built them too. The order of priority is his.
3. **"What about the problems in the Gaudiya Matha after his departure?"** Real, and treated in dps-43 and trd-54. This page stays with what he built and what he said; the *Lilamrta* (vol. 1 ch. 5) records that both factions "deviated from the instructions of their spiritual master".
4. **"Can we read Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati's own essays?"** Yes, as background (research/desire-tree has a 2013 Mayapur lecture on his appearance day; his essays are being added to research/books/). Principles in the talk still come from Srila Prabhupada's books and verified statements.
5. **"I only clean the temple. Where am I in this?"** In the third field. He set temple standards so that the people the drum attracts find a clean, serious place. Cleaning for that purpose is the drum's work too.

## L. Case studies

**Case 1: The budget meeting (Invented case for discussion).** A temple council must choose between a new marble floor
for the hall and funding a year's book stock for the preaching centre. The floor wins because donors can see it.
- **Dilemma:** Both are service; which did our param-guru put first?
- **Principles:** letter of 15 May 1970 (books above temples; verify wording); SB 1.5.11.
- **Questions:** What did Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati build first (1913) and what did he build later? How would a resident present the books' case to donors?
- **Takeaway:** Make the drum's case in his words; a floor is good, the drum is "our main business".

**Case 2: The silent press (Invented case for discussion).** A resident runs the temple's small print room, producing
posters and prasadam menus; the pamphlet on chanting has not been reprinted in two years.
- **Dilemma:** Useful work, or a drum without sound?
- **Principles:** letter of 23 Aug 1968 ("press and literature and public sales"); CC Antya 20.12.
- **Takeaway:** The press is a mrdanga when it prints the holy name's glories; reprint the pamphlet first.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura was Srila Prabhupada's spiritual master and the acarya whose plan ISKCON follows.
In 1913 he founded a printing press in Calcutta and named it the brhad-mrdanga, the "greater drum". Srila Prabhupada
explained his guru's meaning in a letter of 23 August 1968: "press is the brihat mrdanga, or the biggest, or the
greater mrdanga. The sound of press goes long long distance… so the organization of press and literature and public
sales, should be our main business." In 1969 he recalled that his guru "drew a picture in which He gave great importance
to the symbolic representation of the press next to the mrdanga": the drum means the sankirtana party, the press means
books.

The scripture behind this is SB 1.5.11: literature describing the Lord is "a different creation, full of transcendental
words directed toward bringing about a revolution in the impious lives of this world's misdirected civilization." Srila
Prabhupada's purport says that by broadcasting the Lord's name and fame "the polluted atmosphere of the world will
change." Lord Caitanya's first Siksastaka verse declares victory for the chanting of the holy name (CC Antya 20.12);
the greater drum carries that chanting further.

Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati worked in three fields: printing (the press, a daily spiritual newspaper, the English
*Harmonist*), preaching (he sent sannyasis to Europe in the 1930s) and temples (the Gaudiya Matha). He gave Srila
Prabhupada three instructions: in 1922, at their first meeting, "You are educated young men. Why don't you preach Lord
Caitanya Mahaprabhu's message throughout the whole world?"; in 1935 at Radha-kunda, to print books if he ever got money;
and in December 1936, "I have every hope that you can turn yourself into a very good English preacher." Srila
Prabhupada's voyage to America (trd-03) and the BBT are those instructions obeyed. A resident who prints, distributes,
preaches or keeps the temple's standards is working in one of his three fields.

## S2. Key ideas

- The press is the greater drum: a book is heard far beyond a kirtana party (letters, 1968–1970).
- Transcendental literature is meant for a revolution in the world's atmosphere (SB 1.5.11).
- Three fields: printing, preaching, temples; he worked in all three from 1913 onward.
- Three instructions to Srila Prabhupada: preach to the world (1922), print books (1935), become an English preacher (1936).
- Following his methods is "following in the footsteps of the previous acaryas" (NOI 3).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **SB 1.5.11:** literature about the Lord is "directed toward bringing about a revolution in the impious lives of this world's misdirected civilization." *For me:* the book I hand someone is that revolution.
- **CC Antya 20.12:** "Let there be all victory for the chanting of the holy name of Lord Krsna." *For me:* the drum, and the greater drum, carry this.
- **Letter, 23 Aug 1968:** "The sound of press goes long long distance." *For me:* my hour at the book table reaches further than I can see.

## S4. Words to know

- **Brhad-mrdanga:** "the greater drum"; the printing press and the books it produces.
- **Mrdanga:** the clay drum played in kirtana.
- **Gaudiya Matha:** the mission and temples Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati founded.
- **Anubhasya:** his commentary on the Caitanya-caritamrta, printed on his press.
- **Param-guru:** the spiritual master of one's spiritual master.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Did Srila Prabhupada invent the "greater drum" idea?** No; in three letters he attributes it to his Guru Maharaja, who named his 1913 press so.
2. **Why books more than temples?** Because a book reaches further than a building, and the temple exists to give Krishna to people. He built temples too; the priority is his.
3. **What did he actually say to Srila Prabhupada?** 1922: preach Lord Caitanya's message throughout the whole world. 1936: become "a very good English preacher". At Radha-kunda in 1935 he advised printing books.
4. **I don't distribute books. Is this about me?** Preaching programmes and temple standards are his other two fields. All three serve the drum.
5. **Were there problems in his mission after he left?** Yes; they are discussed in dps-43 and trd-54. This session stays with what he built and said.
