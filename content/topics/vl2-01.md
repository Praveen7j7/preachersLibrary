# Reincarnation: fact or fiction?

> **VL2 · 180 min · review: sources-checked.** First VL2 topic: reasoned faith. The listener is interested but not convinced and
> wants reasons. IYF's notes for this topic are the "Essence of Bhagavad-gita" chapter *Reincarnation: Fact or Fiction?*
> (`research/iyf-materials/vl2-01/`), which argues from the Gita, from a survey of thinkers and religions, and from Ian
> Stevenson's past-life research, and closes with the story of King Bharata. This page keeps that shape but separates
> **what the Gita teaches** (E), **what research claims** (O, stated with its critics) and **what the preacher may conclude**.
> Someone in the room may be grieving; see K.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

**Builds on [dys-01-s4](dys-01-s4.md) (DYS 4, Science of the Soul):** that session argued that you are not the body and that consciousness is the soul's sign (BG 2.13, 2.17, 13.34, 2.20). This page does not repeat those arguments or the finger/lawyer examples; it recalls them in three minutes and takes up only what DYS leaves open: what happens at death, the evidence offered for rebirth, and how it changes the way we live.


VL1 raised the self (DYS 4, VL1-06) as something to reflect on. VL2 asks: why *accept* it? Reincarnation is the natural first
VL2 topic because it is where the Gita's teaching on the self becomes a claim about the world that can be argued, and because
it is the question listeners already have. The profile asks for respectful debate, no straw men and no anti-science tone: the
page therefore gives the sceptic's best case (K) and states the research honestly (O).

**IYF's VL2 frame** (IYF's own documents, `research/batch-criteria.md` §2 and §8 point 2): course name "Technical Science
for Spiritual Value", mood *questioning, reasoned*; motive "developing strong faith in practicing spirituality" and "removing all
misconceptions about ISKCON and spirituality"; KRAs "Start doing chanting", "Coming to regular classes", "Meeting with
facilitators". The 2015 VL2 lesson plan runs DYS 1–6 first and then the EBG study-sessions; this page mirrors the EBG session "Reincarnation: Fact or Fiction" (Encyclopedia transcript, O). Practice is
expected to *begin* during VL2 (by its end: 2–4 rounds, 15 min of Prabhupada's books and 15–30 min of kirtan hearing a day), so
this page may carry a first practical step without breaking IYF's level (P).

## C. Central message

The Gita teaches that the self is not the body and passes from body to body as a person changes clothes (BG 2.13, 2.22), carrying
its conceptions with it "as the air carries aromas" (BG 15.8), and that what one remembers at death shapes the next body (BG 8.6).
This is a coherent claim about who we are; some empirical research is consistent with it but does not prove it; and whether or
not one accepts it yet, it changes the question from "what will I get?" to "what am I becoming?"

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. State the Gita's model of transmigration in four verses: the self continues through bodily change (BG 2.13); it is never born
   and never dies (2.20); it changes bodies like clothes (2.22); it carries its conceptions to the next body (15.8).
2. Give the Gita's reasons for the self's distinctness from the body: the continuity of "I" through change (2.13), consciousness as
   the sign of the self (2.17, 13.34), and the difference between what endures and what does not (2.16).
3. Describe what past-life-memory research has claimed (Stevenson), what its critics say, and why it is evidence to weigh, not proof.
4. Explain BG 8.6 and its consequence: the quality of a life shapes its end, and the end shapes the next life.
5. Answer "why don't we remember?" and "isn't this just a Hindu belief?" with reasons rather than slogans.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** BG 2.13, 2.20, 2.22, 15.8 and 8.6 carry the talk; 2.17 and 13.34 answer the "brain" objection; the rest are for questions.

**BG 2.13**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/13/
> "As the embodied soul continuously passes, in this body, from boyhood to youth to old age, the soul similarly passes into another body at death. A sober person is not bewildered by such a change."
- **Context:** Krsna's first teaching to Arjuna.
- **Teaching:** The same self persists through the body's changes and continues into another body at death.
- **Purport point:** "each is changing his body every moment… Yet the same spirit soul is there and does not undergo any change."
  The purport also notes that the next body may be material or spiritual.
- **Application (preacher's):** The verse's argument is from analogy: the change we all witness (childhood to age) is of the same
  kind as the change at death. The IYF notes call the first "internal reincarnation". Present it as the Gita's inference.
- **Use:** in the talk; the key verse.

**BG 2.20**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/20/
> "For the soul there is neither birth nor death at any time… He is not slain when the body is slain."
- **Purport point:** The body has six changes (birth, duration, growth, by-products, dwindling, death); the soul has none. "The
  so-called old man, therefore, feels himself to be in the same spirit as in his childhood or youth."
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 2.22**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/22/
> "As a person puts on new garments, giving up old ones, the soul similarly accepts new material bodies, giving up the old and useless ones."
- **Purport point:** The transfer to another body "is made possible by the grace of the Supersoul", who fulfils the soul's desire
  as one friend fulfils another's; the two birds on one tree (Mundaka and Svetasvatara Upanisads).
- **Use:** in the talk; the IYF notes' opening verse.

**BG 15.8**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/15/8/
> "The living entity in the material world carries his different conceptions of life from one body to another, as the air carries aromas. Thus he takes one kind of body and again quits it to take another."
- **Purport point:** "At the time of death, the consciousness he has created will carry him on to the next type of body… the
  subtle body, which carries the conception of the next body, develops another body in the next life."
- **Application (preacher's):** This is the *mechanism* the Gita offers: not the brain's contents but the subtle body (mind,
  intelligence, false ego) carries the conception. It answers the objection "memories are in the brain, which dies" on the
  tradition's own terms: what carries over is conception and tendency, not brain-stored memory, which is also why ordinary
  recall of past lives is not expected (K).
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 8.6**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/8/6/
> "Whatever state of being one remembers when he quits his body, O son of Kuntī, that state he will attain without fail."
- **Purport point:** "One's thoughts during the course of one's life accumulate to influence one's thoughts at the moment of death,
  so this life creates one's next life." Bharata Maharaja, thinking of a deer, became a deer.
- **Application (preacher's):** The practical heart of the topic: the end of life is not a lottery; it is the sum of a life.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 2.17 and BG 13.34**: Verified. 2.17: what pervades the body is indestructible; purport: consciousness is felt all over the body, "and that is the
proof of the presence of the soul… consciousness is not due to any amount of material combination." 13.34: as the sun lights the universe, the
self lights the body with consciousness; purport: consciousness "is not a product of the combinations of matter." **Use:** for the
"brain produces mind" objection (K), as the tradition's position.

**BG 2.16, BG 2.12, BG 2.27**: Verified. 2.16: of the nonexistent (the body) no endurance; of the eternal (the soul) no change.
2.12: Krsna, Arjuna and the kings always existed and always will, as individuals (purport quotes the Katha Upanisad). 2.27: one
who is born is sure to die, and after death sure to be born again. **Use:** background; 2.27 states the cycle plainly.

**BG 13.22**: Verified. The living entity, associating with the modes of nature, "meets with good and evil among various species."
Purport: born "sometimes as a demigod, sometimes as a man, sometimes as a beast…"; the change is effected only by hearing from
authority. **Use:** bridge to VL2-02.

**SB 10.1.40**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/10/1/40/
> "Just as a person traveling on the road rests one foot on the ground and then lifts the other, or as a worm on a vegetable transfers itself to one leaf and then gives up the previous one, the conditioned soul takes shelter of another body and then gives up the one he had before."
- **Context:** Vasudeva reasoning with Kamsa about death.
- **Purport point:** "At the time of death, according to his mental condition, the living being is carried by the subtle body,
  consisting of mind, intelligence and ego, to another gross body." (The purport also contains sharp remarks about Western
  scientists; do not quote them at VL2.)
- **Use:** in the talk; the two images are good.

**BG 6.43**: Verified. Purport: King Bharata "took his third birth in the family of a good brāhmaṇa" as Jada Bharata; "transcendental
endeavors, or the practice of yoga, never go in vain." **Use:** anchors the Bharata story (J) in a Prabhupada source.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The claim.** A conscious self, distinct from the body, persists through the body's changes and, at death, takes another body
   suited to the conceptions and desires it carries (BG 2.13, 2.22, 15.8, 8.6). The body is "always changing" (2.20 purport); the
   IYF notes add a popular claim that "not a single molecule" of the body remains after seven years. **Do not use that figure**: it
   is a popularisation and is not accurate for every tissue (some molecules in the eye lens and some neurons persist for life). The
   Gita's point needs no such figure: cells, appearance and personality all change; "I" is the constant.
2. **The reasons the Gita gives.**
   - *Continuity of the "I"* through radical change (2.13, 2.20 purport): the old man "feels himself to be in the same spirit".
   - *Consciousness as the sign of the self* (2.17, 13.34): a body minus consciousness is a corpse; no rearrangement of matter
     restores it. This is the tradition's position in the mind–body debate; state it as such and name the alternative (K.1).
   - *What endures vs what does not* (2.16): the seers distinguish the changing from the unchanging by studying both.
   - *Analogy of the clothes* (2.22) and of the walking man and the caterpillar (SB 10.1.40): a change of vehicle, not an ending.
3. **The mechanism.** The subtle body (mind, intelligence, false ego) carries conception and tendency (15.8; SB 10.1.40 purport);
   the state of mind at death is decisive (8.6); the next body is "tailor-made" to the desires carried (the IYF notes' phrase). Under
   the supervision of higher authority, not by the soul's whim (SB 10.1.40 purport).
4. **Why we don't remember.** Two answers, both honest. (a) Forgetting is normal: hardly anyone recalls infancy, yet infancy
   happened (the IYF notes' lunch-a-week-ago example). (b) On the Gita's model what carries over is conception and tendency
   (15.8), not episodic memory stored in a brain that has died; recall would be the exception, which is what the research claims
   to find in some children (O). The IYF notes add a theological reason (memory of past pain would paralyse); offer it as the
   tradition's, not as a proof.
5. **The evidence (summary; details in O).** For: children's spontaneous past-life statements investigated by Ian Stevenson and
   successors; near-death experiences; the Gita's own arguments. Against: alternative explanations (cultural expectation, leading
   questions, memory distortion, chance matches, fraud), no controlled experiments, investigator belief. Honest conclusion: the
   research is *consistent with* reincarnation and unexplained in some cases; it is not proof, and most scientists do not accept it
   as evidence. The tradition's final ground is hearing (BG 13.22 purport: the change of conception "can be effected only by
   hearing from authoritative sources"), argued with reason and tested in life.
6. **The consequence.** If BG 8.6 is true, the question "what will I get?" becomes "what am I becoming?" A life spent cultivating
   a conception produces a death absorbed in it. This is the only part of the topic the listener can act on now, and the IYF notes
   rightly make it the climax ("relevance of reincarnation in our life").
7. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - "Reincarnation means I will be a human again." Not necessarily (13.22; 8.6 purport: Bharata became a deer).
   - "Memories prove it; no memories disprove it." Neither; see 4 and O.
   - "Science has disproved the soul." No experiment addresses it; the brain–mind question is open (K.1).
   - "It is only a Hindu belief." Many traditions and thinkers have held forms of it; but the Gita's case does not rest on a head-count.
8. **Pitch for VL2.** Key terms defined (*atma*, subtle body, *samsara*). Scripture as evidence: quote the verse, give its reasoning,
   admit what it does not settle. One reasoned conclusion to test (P).

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Welcome people by name; recall from DYS 4 (if they attended) one line: "I am not the body".
2. Say: "Tonight we ask what that implies at death. We will weigh reasons and evidence; no one is asked to believe."
3. Ask H.2 question 1; take three answers.
4. Ask the room to raise a hand if they have heard of reincarnation from family, films or books; note the mix.
5. Write on the board: "claim, reasons, evidence, consequence".

**Adapting for your audience:** the transplant question works anywhere hospitals exist; in a village or farming group use "a farmer's land passes to his son, but is the son the farmer?"; in a professional group use a company that keeps its name after every employee has been replaced.

**H.2 Opening questions**
- If you received a transplant, would you become partly someone else? Where does "you" stop?
- Has anyone ever told you "you have your grandfather's temper" or "you were born with that nature"? What did you think?
- What do you think happens to a person's character when the body stops?

**More on opening and engagement**


- **Opening question:** "If you received a kidney transplant, would you become partly someone else? A cornea? Where does 'you'
  stop?" (the IYF notes' opening). Collect answers.
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** A grandmother says the new baby "has her late husband's eyes and temper". A cousin laughs.
  Who is being unreasonable, and why?
- **Transition:** "Today we will look at a 5,000-year-old claim about this, the reasons it gives, the evidence people cite, and
  what follows if it is true."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** everyone will die; the view one holds about death shapes risk, ambition, grief and ethics.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Recall: the self persists through change, and consciousness is its sign.** Argued in [dys-01-s4](dys-01-s4.md) (BG 2.13, 2.17, 13.34); three minutes only. *Transition:* if the self is not the body, what happens to it when this body ends?
2. **The Gita's model of death.** BG 2.13 last line, 2.22: the change at death is the same kind of change as from boyhood to old age, only complete. *Transition:* how does the self move?
3. **The subtle body carries the conception.** BG 15.8; SB 10.1.40. *Example:* air and fragrance. *Transition:* is there any evidence?
4. **Evidence to weigh, not proof.** Stevenson's cases and their critics (O). *Transition:* what follows for me?
5. **This life creates the next.** BG 8.6; Bharata. *Transition:* to the test (P).

## J. Stories and illustrations

- **The three lives of King Bharata** (Srimad-Bhagavatam Canto 5, chapters 7–9; the IYF notes retell it with discussion
  questions). Confirmed in Prabhupada's purports to BG 8.6 (he thought of a deer and became a deer) and BG 6.43 (his third birth
  as Jada Bharata; "transcendental endeavors… never go in vain"). Tell it in your own words, keeping to the Bhagavatam's account;
  read Canto 5 chapters 7–9 before adding any detail. *Connection:* BG 8.6 and the hope in 6.43.
- **The walker and the caterpillar** (SB 10.1.40). Two images in one verse.
- **The two birds on one tree** (BG 2.22 purport, from the Upanisads). The soul eats the fruits; the Lord watches as a friend.
- **The Sukla Gupta case** (the IYF notes' "real case history"): a girl in West Bengal who from infancy spoke of a daughter "Minu"
  and a family in Bhatpara, and in 1959 led her family to the house and identified people there. **Partially verified:** the case is
  one of Stevenson's published Indian cases; the notes' retelling adds detail and emotion. If you tell it, tell it briefly, name the
  source (O) and say what critics would ask (how was the information recorded before the visit? who knew whom?).
- **Contemporary images (the preacher's):** changing clothes; a driver changing cars.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

> **Before you teach:** if someone has lost a loved one recently, "they are in another body now" can comfort or wound. Do not
> speculate about anyone's departed relative. Keep the teaching general and offer to talk afterwards.

1. **"Consciousness is produced by the brain. Damage the brain and the mind changes."**
   - The strongest objection; give it its due. The correlation is real and nobody denies it. The question is whether the brain
     *produces* consciousness or *mediates* it, as a radio mediates a broadcast: damage the radio and the music distorts, but
     the music is not made by the radio. The Gita takes the second view (BG 2.17, 13.34). The "hard problem" of consciousness
     (why physical processes are accompanied by experience at all) remains unsolved on any view. Say "open question", not "refuted".
2. **"If I lived before, why don't I remember?"**
   - F.4: forgetting is normal; what carries over is conception, not brain-stored memory (BG 15.8); the research claims rare
     exceptions.
3. **"Stevenson's cases: isn't this just suggestible children and hopeful parents?"**
   - That is exactly what critics say, and Stevenson tried to control for it (interviews with both families, written records where
     available, looking for prior contact). Critics reply that controls were weak and most cases came from cultures expecting
     rebirth. Both sides should be stated; the honest verdict is "unexplained in some cases; not proof" (O).
4. **"Isn't reincarnation unfair: punished for what I can't remember?"**
   - The Gita's picture is less a courtroom than a continuity: what I cultivate, I become (8.6, 15.8). The karma question proper is
     a separate topic; do not improvise it. Never apply it to anyone's present suffering (VL1-06).
5. **"Christianity and Islam reject it. Why should I accept a Hindu idea?"**
   - The Gita's case rests on reasons (F.2), not on which religions agree. The IYF notes list other traditions' views; several of its
     historical claims are disputed (O), so do not lean on them.
6. **"What about population growth? Where do new souls come from?"**
   - The tradition holds that souls move between species and worlds (BG 13.22), so human numbers alone say nothing. Say so, and
     admit that this is the tradition's answer, not an observation.
7. **"Does it matter, if I can't verify it?"**
   - It changes what you do with today (BG 8.6). That part is testable: cultivate one conception for a month and watch what your
     mind returns to.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The deathbed plan.** A businessman asks his doctor for "four more years to finish my plans" (an image from the IYF notes).
His son, who attends the Gita sessions, wonders what his father is carrying.
- **Principles:** BG 8.6; BG 15.8.
- **Questions:** What does a mind absorbed in unfinished plans carry? What could the son do that is kind and honest?
- **Takeaway:** The end of life gathers up what the life was about.

**Case 2: The sceptical friend.** Aisha, a medical student, says, "Show me one controlled experiment and I'll believe." Her friend
Varun has only Stevenson's cases.
- **Principles:** F.5; O.
- **Questions:** Is Aisha's standard reasonable? Can it be met for any claim about what happens after death? What can Varun
  honestly say the evidence shows?
- **Takeaway:** Overclaiming loses the honest sceptic; stating the status fairly keeps the conversation open.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first)
1. In BG 2.22, changing bodies is compared to what? *Expected:* changing garments.
2. What does BG 15.8 say carries over, and to what is it compared? *Expected:* conceptions of life, as air carries aromas.
3. Does the Gita say we remember past lives? *Expected:* No; forgetting is normal.
4. Is past-life research proof? *Expected:* No; it is suggestive to some, disputed by others.
5. What does BG 8.6 say decides the next state of being? *Expected:* what one remembers at death, which follows from how one lives.
6. Name one practical change that follows even if you are unsure about rebirth. *Expected:* watching what I dwell on. Praise honest answers.

**M.2 Activities (engagement block, about 90 min; choose two)**
- **A. Fact-or-fiction debate (40 min).** Two groups prepare 10 minutes using a one-page brief (the verses, the research summary, the critics); 5 minutes each; the room votes on "who used evidence fairly", not on who is right; the preacher sorts the arguments (10 min). Rule: restate the other side before replying.
- **B. Evidence cards (30 min).** Groups of four get cards: a Stevenson-type claim, a critic's objection, a verse, an everyday analogy. They rank them from strongest to weakest and justify (20 min); one group reports (10 min).
- **C. One-week mind log (25 min).** Individually design a simple night-time log of "what my mind kept returning to"; pairs compare formats (not content) and agree how to check in next week. Nothing is shared that a person wants private.

**M.3 Reflection (private, optional).** What conception of life would I carry if I died tonight? Does it match what I say I value? One habit of mind to cultivate this month, chosen with BG 8.6 in view.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

You are not your body, a point made in DYS 4: the body changes completely from childhood to old age, yet "you" remain (BG 2.13), and the sign of the self is consciousness (BG 2.17). The Bhagavad-gita (an ancient Sanskrit scripture) adds that the same self continues into another body at death, as a person changes clothes (BG 2.22). Whether the brain produces consciousness or only transmits it is an open question; the Gita sides with the second.

What carries over is not brain-stored memory but one's conceptions and tendencies, "as the air carries aromas" (BG 15.8). That is why forgetting past lives is normal; we do not remember being one year old either. The state of mind at death follows from the state of mind in life and shapes the next body (BG 8.6), so the next body is not necessarily human (BG 13.22).

Researchers have investigated children who seem to remember past lives. Some cases are hard to explain, but critics raise serious objections, there are no controlled experiments, and the research is suggestive, not proof. The tradition's claim is not that Hindus believe it; it gives reasons and invites you to weigh them.

What you can test now: what you dwell on, you become (BG 8.6). A week of noting what your mind returns to is a first step.

## S2. Key ideas

- The body changes; the self continues (BG 2.13, 2.20).
- Consciousness is the sign of the self; a corpse has the same matter and no consciousness (BG 2.17, 13.34).
- The subtle body (mind, intelligence, false ego) carries our conceptions to the next body (BG 15.8).
- Not remembering is normal; even this life's infancy is forgotten.
- Past-life research is suggestive, not conclusive; honest people disagree about it.
- The state of mind at death follows from the state of mind in life (BG 8.6).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **BG 2.13:** From boyhood to youth to old age, and then "into another body at death. A sober person is not bewildered." *For me:* I have already changed bodies many times.
- **BG 15.8:** The living entity carries "his different conceptions of life from one body to another, as the air carries aromas." *For me:* what I cultivate travels with me.
- **BG 8.6:** "Whatever state of being one remembers when he quits his body… that state he will attain without fail." *For me:* my daily mind is preparing my last thought.

## S4. Words to know

- **Atma:** the self or soul, distinct from the body.
- **Subtle body:** mind, intelligence and false ego; it carries the soul's conceptions between gross bodies.
- **Samsara:** the cycle of repeated birth and death (BG 2.27).
- **Transmigration:** the soul's passage from one body to another.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Doesn't the brain produce the mind?** Brain and mind are closely linked; whether the brain produces consciousness or transmits it is an open question. The Gita says consciousness is the sign of the soul (BG 2.17).
2. **Why don't I remember past lives?** Forgetting is normal (you don't remember being one year old). What carries over is tendency, not stored memory (BG 15.8).
3. **Is there scientific proof?** No. There is research on children's past-life memories that some find suggestive and others explain otherwise.
4. **Will I be human again?** Not necessarily. The next body follows the consciousness cultivated (BG 8.6; 13.22).
5. **Isn't this just Hindu belief?** The Gita gives reasons; weigh them. Many traditions have held some form of it, but the case does not rest on that.
6. **Why does it matter now?** Because what I dwell on, I become (BG 8.6). That is testable today.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. In BG 2.22, changing bodies is compared to: (a) a river flowing, (b) changing garments, (c) a lamp going out.
2. According to BG 15.8, what carries over from one body to the next, and to what is it compared?
3. True or false: the Gita says we always remember our past lives.
4. What does BG 8.6 say decides the next state of being?
5. Give one reason the Gita offers for thinking the self continues after the body ends.
6. In one sentence: why is past-life research "suggestive" rather than "proof"?

**Answers:** 1. (b) (BG 2.22). 2. One's conceptions of life, compared to air carrying aromas (BG 15.8). 3. False; no verse says so, and forgetting is
normal. 4. Whatever state of being one remembers when quitting the body (BG 8.6). 5. The "I" continues through complete bodily change (BG 2.13), so the next change, at death, is of the same kind (BG 2.22). 6. Because the cases can be explained in other ways and there are no controlled experiments; some remain unexplained.
