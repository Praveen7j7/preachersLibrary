# 20. Sädhana

> Status line: BSS (brahmacari training) · 120 min · drafted · not sensitive. Based on chapter 20 of Bhakti Vikasa Swami's *Brahmacarya in Krishna consciousness* (BIKC). Builds on bss-27 (discipline in general); this page is about *what the daily practice consists of and why it works*. The next page, bss-45, is about *holding it steady*.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level
BSS members are preparing to join the brahmacari ashram "maintaining strictly the initiation vows" (IYF's own description of the batch, research/batch-criteria.md, BSS section). A brahmacari's life is built around sadhana: BIKC says it is "traditionally centered" on chanting, studying scripture and worship. If sadhana is weak, every other subject in this batch (celibacy, austerity, service) turns into strain; if it is strong, they become natural.

## C. Central message
Sadhana is the practice of hearing, chanting and serving that wakes up the love for Krishna already in the heart (CC Madhya 22.105, 22.107). A brahmacari's day is arranged around it: the morning programme, sixteen rounds, reading and association. When the practice feels dry, the remedy is not to weaken it but to enter it more fully.

## D. Learning outcomes
By the end, participants can:
1. Define *sadhana-bhakti* from CC Madhya 22.105 and say what, according to CC Madhya 22.107, it produces.
2. List what Srila Prabhupada's standard day contains, using the NOI 3 purport and the BIKC summary.
3. Explain from SB 1.2.17-18 and Narada's life (SB 1.5.23-28) why regular hearing is the engine of sadhana.
4. Explain why the order to chant sixteen rounds is called the most essential (CC Madhya 22.113 purport) and why reading Prabhupada's books protects against doubt (CC Madhya 25.278 purport, BG 4.42).
5. Name one weak point of their own practice and one repair.

## E. Scriptural foundation
Use nine verses in the talk (BG 12.9, CC Madhya 22.105, 22.107, NOI 3, CC Madhya 22.113, SB 1.2.18, SB 1.5.25-26, CC Madhya 25.278). The others are for questions and the extension. "Verified" here means: the verse exists on vedabase.io (research/verification/bss-44.json), and I read the translation and the purport used (nothing is claimed from a purport I did not read).

**BG 12.9**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/12/9/
> "if you cannot fix your mind upon Me without deviation, then follow the regulative principles of bhakti-yoga. In this way develop a desire to attain Me."
- **Context:** Krishna has just described the highest worship (fixing the mind on Him); He now gives a ladder for those who cannot.
- **Teaching:** If the mind cannot rest on Krishna, there are regulative principles that develop the desire to attain Him.
- **Purport point:** Two processes are indicated: one for those who already have attachment to Krishna, and one for those who do not; for the second, "there are different prescribed rules and regulations". The purport describes them: rise early, bathe, enter the temple, offer prayers and chant Hare Krishna, collect flowers, cook for the Deity, take prasadam, "and so on", and "constantly hear Bhagavad-gita and Srimad-Bhagavatam from pure devotees", under an expert spiritual master. This practice "can help anyone rise to the level of love of God". It also says that love of God is "now in a dormant state in everyone's heart" and has to be revived.
- **Application (preacher's):** Few of us can fix the mind on Krishna by willing it. This verse gives permission to begin with the practice, not with the feeling.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**CC Madhya 22.105**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/22/105/
> "When transcendental devotional service, by which love for Kṛṣṇa is attained, is executed by the senses, it is called sādhana-bhakti, or the regulative discharge of devotional service."
- **Teaching:** Sadhana-bhakti is devotional service done with the senses, whose aim is to attain love of Krishna.
- **Purport point:** The verse is quoted from Rupa Gosvami's *Bhakti-rasamrta-sindhu* (1.2.2). Devotional service already exists dormant in every living entity; it "begins with sravanam kirtanam, hearing and chanting"; a sleeping man can be awakened by sound, so every conditioned soul should hear the Hare Krishna mantra from a pure Vaishnava; the mind gradually becomes purified and then the senses are used for the Lord instead of for sense gratification.
- **Use:** in the talk (definition).

**CC Madhya 22.107**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/22/107/
> "Pure love for Kṛṣṇa is eternally established in the hearts of the living entities. It is not something to be gained from another source. When the heart is purified by hearing and chanting, this love naturally awakens."
- **Teaching:** The goal of sadhana is not to acquire something new but to uncover love that is already there. Hearing and chanting purify the heart.
- **Use:** in the talk (the purpose).

**NOI 3**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/3/
> "(1) being enthusiastic, (2) endeavoring with confidence, (3) being patient, (4) acting according to regulative principles"
- **Teaching:** Six principles favourable to pure devotional service; the first four are quoted. (The rest: abandoning the association of nondevotees, and following the previous acaryas.)
- **Purport point (I read the whole purport):** Bhakti is "practical activity", not "sentimental speculation"; "Enthusiasm means action, but action for whom? ... for Kṛṣṇa"; neglect of the regulative principles "will destroy devotional service"; there are four prohibitions (illicit sex, meat-eating, gambling, intoxication) and positive principles "such as the daily chanting of sixteen rounds"; and "in this Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement we require everyone to rise early in the morning, by four A.M., and attend maṅgala-ārati, or morning worship, then read Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, perform kīrtana, and so forth", so that "continuous activities in devotional service twenty-four hours daily" are held. It calls this following the previous acaryas, "who expertly filled every moment of time with Kṛṣṇa conscious activities".
- **Application (preacher's):** The BIKC description of the day is Prabhupada's own purport put into a timetable.
- **Use:** in the talk (the shape of the day, and enthusiasm).

**CC Madhya 22.113**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/22/113/
> "He should always be remembered and never forgotten at any time. All the rules and prohibitions mentioned in the śāstras should be the servants of these two principles."
- **Context:** A verse from the *Padma Purana* about remembering Vishnu (Krishna).
- **Purport point:** The rules should serve the basic principle: always remember Krishna, never forget Him. "Of all the regulative principles, the spiritual master's order to chant at least sixteen rounds is most essential." Other services (selling books, enlisting life members) are "not ordinary duties"; they are "an impetus for remembering Kṛṣṇa".
- **Use:** in the talk (sixteen rounds; BIKC quotes this purport).

**SB 1.2.17**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/17/
> "cleanses desire for material enjoyment from the heart of the devotee who has developed the urge to hear His messages"
- **Purport point:** Krishna's messages are non-different from Him; He is present as transcendental sound; "there is no rigid fixture of time, and anyone can chant the holy name with attention and reverence at his convenience"; the taste for hearing is developed "through the medium of service to the pure devotee". Restlessness in the contact of women and wealth is "not an astonishment", because it takes time to recover from this "foreign nature", but by hearing the Lord's glories "gradually all disturbing elements are eliminated from his mind".
- **Use:** background and the extension.

**SB 1.2.18**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/18/
> "By regular attendance in classes on the Bhāgavatam and by rendering of service to the pure devotee, all that is troublesome to the heart is almost completely destroyed"
- **Purport point:** There are two Bhagavatas: the book and the devotee. Either can eliminate the obstacles; the combination helps the neophyte "make progress on and on". The purport cites Narada's own history (maidservant's son) as the proof.
- **Use:** in the talk (class attendance).

**SB 1.5.23-28 (Narada's previous life)**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/5/25/ (see also 1.5.23, 24, 26, 28). Told in J.1.
> "Once only, by their permission, I took the remnants of their food, and by so doing all my sins were at once eradicated." (1.5.25)
> "I could hear them describe the attractive activities of Lord Kṛṣṇa. And thus listening attentively, my taste for hearing of the Personality of Godhead increased at every step." (1.5.26)
- **Purport point (1.5.23):** Narada came from "the most insignificant parentage" and was "not properly educated"; "because his complete energy was engaged in the service of the Lord, he became an immortal sage".
- **Use:** in the talk (story).

**CC Madhya 25.278**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/25/278/
> "Men become strong and stout by eating sufficient grains, but the devotee who simply eats ordinary grains but does not taste the transcendental pastimes of Lord Caitanya Mahāprabhu and Kṛṣṇa gradually becomes weak and falls down from the transcendental position."
- **Purport point:** "All the devotees connected with the Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement must read all the books that have been translated ... otherwise, after some time, they will simply eat, sleep and fall down from their position."
- **Use:** in the talk (reading).

**BG 4.42**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/4/42/
> "the doubts which have arisen in your heart out of ignorance should be slashed by the weapon of knowledge"
- **Purport point:** "Ignorance can be removed by gradual acceptance of the principles of Kṛṣṇa consciousness"; one should study Bhagavad-gita "under a bona fide spiritual master, with service and surrender".
- **Use:** in the talk (doubt) and questions.
- **Note on a BIKC citation:** BIKC also cites BG 9.3 for "doubt is a disease which can culminate in spiritual death". BG 9.3 and its purport speak of *faith* and the faithless, not of doubt as a disease, so it is **dropped**; the point is made from BG 4.42 only.

**SB 10.1.4**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/10/1/4/ (BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples)
> "Descriptions of the Lord are the right medicine for the conditioned soul undergoing repeated birth and death."
- **Purport point:** Kṛṣṇa-kathā must be heard from persons free from material desire (nivṛtta-tarṣa); a "professional reciter" cannot help one to liberation. Prabhupada's movement is training qualified preachers to recite the Bhagavatam and Gita.
- **Use:** background (hearing from the right person).

**BG 9.14**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/9/14/
> "Always chanting My glories, endeavoring with great determination, bowing down before Me, these great souls perpetually worship Me with devotion."
- **Purport point:** In devotional service "there are certain activities which are called determined", such as fasting on Ekadasi (the eleventh day of the moon) and on the Lord's appearance days; these "rules and regulations are offered by the great ācāryas", and the great souls observe them strictly. Devotional service can be done "in a happy mood" and "in any position ... either as a householder or a sannyāsī or a brahmacārī".
- **Use:** extension, and the question "is sadhana joyless?".

**CC Madhya 23.14-15**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/23/14-15/. For R.1 only (not for this level's talk or handout).

**A BIKC citation not used.** BIKC says "Bhagavad-gita (16.1) states that *svadhyaya*, study of the scriptures, is especially meant for brahmacaris". BG 16.1-3 lists *svadhyaya* ("study of the Vedas") among the godly qualities, but neither the translation nor the purport says it is "especially meant for brahmacaris", so the claim is **not used**; reading is supported by CC Madhya 25.278 instead.

## F. Philosophical explanation
**F.1 What sadhana is.** The word *sadhana* means a practice that leads to a goal. In bhakti it means serving the Lord with the senses so that the hidden love of God (*prema*) wakes up (CC Madhya 22.105, 22.107). The whole teaching rests on one claim: the love is already in the heart. So sadhana is like cleaning a dusty mirror, not like earning a salary. We do not create Krishna or the love. We remove what covers the love, which is the dust on the mirror.

**F.2 Why the day is arranged this way.** Prabhupada gave a standard: a morning programme and an evening programme built from the same elements: worship (*mangala-arati*, the dawn worship of the Deity; *tulasi-arati*, offering to the sacred tulasi plant; *guru-puja*, honouring the spiritual master), hearing (a Bhagavatam class in the morning, a Gita class in the evening), kirtana (congregational singing), and sixteen rounds of japa (the Hare Krishna mantra on beads, a "round" being 108 mantras). In NOI 3's purport he says the aim is "continuous activities in devotional service twenty-four hours daily". BIKC's summary of the day: the programme above, attended from beginning to end, with most of one's rounds chanted during the morning programme, and rising by 4 a.m. at the latest. That specific timetable is BIKC's and ISKCON's practice; the principle behind it is NOI 3.

**F.3 Two engines: hearing and chanting.** CC Madhya 22.105's purport: devotional service "begins with" hearing and chanting. SB 1.2.17-18 says the Lord cleanses the heart of the one with urge to hear; regular class attendance and service to the devotee destroy what troubles the heart. Narada's life (SB 1.5.23-28) shows the same thing in a boy.

**F.4 Sixteen rounds are the root.** CC Madhya 22.113's purport says every other rule is a servant of remembering Krishna and that the sixteen rounds are "most essential" among the regulative principles. BIKC adds practical points: japa should be "clearly and properly heard" and prayerful, not slurred. Hence a brahmacari should not trade rounds for any other service, however attractive, without his guru's instruction.

**F.5 Reading and doubt.** Without conviction, no one volunteers for austerity (BIKC's reasoning). BG 4.42 prescribes knowledge as the weapon against doubts born of ignorance, and CC Madhya 25.278's purport says devotees who do not read "will simply eat, sleep and fall down". Reading Prabhupada's books is not an optional extra to sadhana; it is the third leg.

**F.6 Enthusiasm.** NOI 3 puts enthusiasm first. The purport defines it: action for Krishna, executed with intelligence. BIKC advises that when enthusiasm is low, act enthusiastically: for example, join the kirtana with full voice and dance. BIKC quotes Prabhupada as saying "Dance. Even if there is no ecstasy, dance and it will come" (from *ISKCON in the 1970s* by Satsvarupa dasa Gosvami, vol. 1, p. 40). **Requires source review**: I have not seen that source, so the quote is given here as BIKC's report.

**F.7 What sadhana is not.** (a) It is not a set of tasks to impress a senior. (b) It is not the goal: CC Madhya 22.113 makes all rules servants of remembering Krishna. (c) It is not rigid: the purport of SB 1.2.17 says the Name may be chanted "at his convenience"; there is flexibility for those with services that conflict with the programme (BIKC: pujaris, cooks and travelling preachers set time aside at another hour, and "best" is to rise very early and chant before mangala-arati).

**F.8 Misunderstandings.**
- "Sadhana is for beginners; the advanced do not need it." NOI 3's purport lists the practices as what the previous acaryas followed; BG 9.14's purport says the great souls observe the rules strictly.
- "If I feel nothing, it is useless." CC Madhya 22.107: the love awakens as the heart is purified; the purification is happening whether or not it is felt that day (the verse says the heart is purified "by hearing and chanting").
- "Service is more important than the programme." BIKC allows evening hours for preaching engagements, but makes up missed programmes at another time; CC Madhya 22.113's purport says the order to chant is the most essential regulative principle.

## H. Opening and engagement
**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes).**
1. Begin with a short prayer or one verse of kirtana; say the title once and explain that *sadhana* means "the practice".
2. Say: "Everyone here already has a sadhana, even if it is only the way you start your day. Today we look at how the devotees' sadhana works from the inside, and I will ask you to fix one thing in yours."
3. Read BG 12.9 slowly in English. Pause three seconds.
4. Say: "Notice that Krishna does not say 'if you do not feel love, go home'. He says, 'follow the practice, and the desire will grow'."

**H.2 Opening questions** (take three answers each; do not comment yet).
1. "When you wake up on a normal day, what is the first thing you do? What would your day look like if that first thing were the same every morning?"
2. "Name something you do every day without being forced. How did it become like that?"
3. "What part of your own sadhana do you find easiest, and what part do you find hardest?" (No need to answer aloud; write it down for M.3.)

## I. Core teaching points
**I.1 Sadhana awakens, it does not manufacture (BG 12.9; CC Madhya 22.105, 22.107).** Read BG 12.9 and the two CC verses. Explain *dormant love*: the purport says love of God is in everyone's heart in a dormant state. Illustration (preacher's, labelled): a farmer who ploughs and waters each day does not make the seed, he serves a power that is already in it. The Krishna conscious point is this: our daily hearing and chanting serve the seed of love that Krishna has already placed in the heart. Next question for the group: what does a full day of this practice contain?

**I.2 The shape of the day (NOI 3 purport; CC Madhya 22.113; BIKC).** Give Prabhupada's own list from the NOI 3 purport: rise early, mangala-arati, Bhagavatam reading, kirtana; add the evening programme and sixteen rounds from BIKC. Explain each term as you use it. Then the root: CC Madhya 22.113: the rules are servants of remembering Krishna; sixteen rounds is the most essential order. Example (preacher's): a village well has a rope, a pulley and a bucket, but the point is the water. All the programme items are the rope and bucket; the water is remembrance of Krishna. Next question for the group: where does the taste for this practice come from?

**I.3 Hearing and association (SB 1.2.17-18; Narada, SB 1.5.23-28).** Tell the story (J.1). Then SB 1.2.18: the combination of the book and the devotee. Stress "regular": class attendance every day, on time, from the start to the end (BIKC's picture of a good programme). Next question for the group: what protects our hearing when doubt arrives?

**I.4 Reading and the answer to doubt (CC Madhya 25.278; BG 4.42).** Read the translation of CC Madhya 25.278: physical food strengthens the body; nectar of the Lord's pastimes strengthens the devotee. Purport: read all the translated books. Then BG 4.42: doubt is cut by knowledge. Practical (BIKC): set a fixed daily reading time; read Prabhupada's books and other authorized literature; avoid the opposite kind of reading. Application (preacher's): the first step in a doubt is to ask a senior and read the answer in the book; do not carry it silently. Next question for the group: what should we do when we have the knowledge but not the energy?

**I.5 Enthusiasm (NOI 3).** The first favourable principle. The purport: enthusiasm is action for Krishna with intelligence. BIKC's advice: act enthusiastically and enthusiasm comes; join the kirtana; pray in the japa. Link to CC Madhya 22.107: it is the purification that matters; the feeling follows. Application (preacher's): if the morning program feels flat, add one change (stand for the kirtana, take a seat near the front, chant with the voice) rather than subtract anything.

**I.6 Adjustments and make-up (BIKC; SB 1.2.17 purport).** Pujaris (Deity worshippers), cooks and travelling preachers may miss part of the program. BIKC's rule: do not become lax; set time aside elsewhere; best is to rise earlier. The SB 1.2.17 purport shows the principle (no rigid fixture of time for chanting) but does not cancel the sixteen rounds. See bss-48 for travel.

## J. Stories and illustrations
**J.1 Narada's previous life (SB 1.5.23-28, key verse 1.5.25).** Retell in this order, and read the verses from the text: (1) Narada says he was the son of a maidservant who served brahmanas for four months of the rainy season (1.5.23); (2) as a boy he was self-controlled, did not play much and spoke only what was needed (1.5.24); (3) once, with their permission, he took the remnants of their food and his sins were eradicated (1.5.25); (4) he heard them describe Krishna's activities and his taste for hearing increased at every step (1.5.26); (5) over two seasons he heard them constantly chant the Lord's glories (1.5.28). **Devotional lesson:** he had no education and no qualification except service and attentive hearing, and through that regular hearing the covers of passion and ignorance went away. The sadhana of a brahmacari is the same structure: service, regular hearing, and the remnants of the Lord's food. Do not add any details beyond the verses.

**J.2 Invented illustration (labelled).** A young man cleans the courtyard of a village temple every morning. He is not asked to give a talk or lead the singing. After some months he notices that he knows the songs and hears himself humming the Name through the day. The point: SB 1.5.28 and CC Madhya 22.105: daily service plus hearing slowly changes what the heart does by itself.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections
1. *Is rising by 4 a.m. compulsory?* In the NOI 3 purport Prabhupada says "we require everyone to rise early in the morning, by four A.M." for this movement's centres. BIKC repeats it for the brahmacari ashram. Anyone with an illness or an unusual service should discuss adjustment with the guide; do not make medical claims.
2. *I chant but feel nothing. Is something wrong?* CC Madhya 22.107: the love awakens as the heart is purified by hearing and chanting. Keep the practice; add attentive hearing of the sound (BIKC); speak with a senior if it is a long-term dryness.
3. *Is reading as important as chanting?* CC Madhya 22.113 puts sixteen rounds first; CC Madhya 25.278 says devotees must also read; both are required, one does not replace the other.
4. *Can preaching replace the morning programme?* BIKC says the evening may be used for preaching engagements; for the morning, no. If a part of either programme is missed, make it up at another time.
5. *Why do we follow a fixed programme when love should be spontaneous?* Spontaneous love is the goal; the regulative principles are for those who have not reached it (BG 12.9 purport; see bss-45 for CC Madhya 22.109).
6. *My doubts keep returning.* BG 4.42: use knowledge. Put the doubt as a question to a senior, and read the matter in Prabhupada's books. Do not leave doubts to ferment alone.

## L. Case studies
**L.1 Invented case for discussion.** Arjun joined the ashram three months ago. He attends the morning program but sits at the back, chants most of his rounds after breakfast, and has begun to feel that the program is "a performance". *Dilemma:* whether to ask for permission to practise privately. *Principles:* BG 12.9 (follow the regulative principles even without the feeling); NOI 3 (enthusiasm and regulation); BIKC (chant most rounds during the program, be present from beginning to end). *Questions:* What small change could he make this week? Whom should he tell? *Analysis:* the answer is to enter the program more fully (CC Madhya 22.105: it is the senses that do the service), to speak with his guide, and to give it patience (NOI 3). *Takeaway:* when the practice feels dry, Arjun should give it more attention and talk to his guide, and he should not leave the community.

**L.2 Invented case for discussion (extension).** Ravi is a cook. He rises at 3:30, prepares the offering and misses most of the Bhagavatam class. *Principles:* BIKC (set time aside elsewhere; rise early and chant before mangala-arati); SB 1.2.18 (regular hearing). *Questions:* How can he arrange a daily hearing slot? Can a recorded class be used? *Takeaway:* Ravi can change the time and the way he hears, but he should still hear every day and chant all his rounds.

## M. Discussion and reflection
**M.1 Closing check questions (with expected answers).**
1. *What does CC Madhya 22.107 say love for Krishna is?* Eternally established in the heart; sadhana awakens it by purifying the heart through hearing and chanting.
2. *What does the NOI 3 purport say about rising time and the morning programme?* The movement requires everyone to rise by four a.m., attend mangala-arati, read the Bhagavatam, do kirtana: continuous devotional activity.
3. *What does CC Madhya 22.113's purport call the most essential regulative principle?* The spiritual master's order to chant at least sixteen rounds.
4. *What happened to Narada's heart as he served and heard the sages?* His sins were eradicated, his taste for hearing increased, and the covers of passion and ignorance disappeared (SB 1.5.25-28).
5. *What does CC Madhya 25.278's purport say happens to devotees who do not read?* They will simply eat, sleep and fall down from their position.
6. *According to BIKC, what should a devotee do when enthusiasm is low?* Act enthusiastically (for example in kirtana) and enthusiasm will come.

**M.2 Activities.**
(a) *My day on one page (15 min, individual then pairs).* Each draws their day with times for: rising, japa, class, reading, service, rest. They mark one weak point and show it to a partner who asks only: "What is the smallest change?"
(b) *Narada retold (10 min, groups of four).* Each person retells one verse (1.5.23, 24, 25, 26, 28) in their own words; the group names the one thing that Narada did every day.
(c) *Dry practice clinic (15 min, extension).* Two volunteers are given L.1 and asked to role-play a conversation with a guide; the group lists suggestions; the facilitator links each to a verse.
(d) *Enthusiasm test (5 min).* Everyone stands for a minute of loud kirtana. Ask who feels different afterwards, and link to BIKC's advice.

**M.3 Reflection (private; nothing shared).** "Which part of my sadhana do I treat as a duty, and which as a service?"

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary
*Sadhana* means the daily practice of devotional service: hearing, chanting, reading and serving. Srila Prabhupada's books say that love for Krishna is already in every heart, sleeping. When the heart is cleaned by hearing and chanting, this love wakes up (CC Madhya 22.107). So the aim of sadhana is not to earn something, but to uncover it. Krishna also says that if we cannot fix our mind on Him, we should follow the regulative principles and the desire will grow (BG 12.9).

The day of a brahmacari (a celibate student of spiritual life) is built from the same parts: rising early; the morning programme with worship, a class on the Bhagavatam and kirtana (group singing); sixteen rounds of the Hare Krishna mantra on beads; reading Prabhupada's books; and service. Prabhupada wrote that his movement asks everyone to rise early, attend the morning worship, read the Bhagavatam and sing, so that devotional activity continues all day (NOI 3). Of all rules, the order to chant sixteen rounds is the most essential, because all rules are servants of remembering Krishna (CC Madhya 22.113).

Hearing is an engine. Regular attendance at the class and service to devotees clear the troubles of the heart (SB 1.2.18). Narada was a poor boy who served saints for four months and heard them; his taste for the Lord increased "at every step" (SB 1.5.26). Reading is also needed: devotees who do not read can simply eat, sleep and fall down (CC Madhya 25.278), and doubts are cut by knowledge (BG 4.42). When enthusiasm is low, act enthusiastically: join the kirtana fully; enthusiasm is action for Krishna (NOI 3). If you must miss a programme, make it up at another time.

## S2. Key ideas
- Sadhana is the practice that wakes up the love for Krishna that is already in the heart.
- The basic parts of the day are the morning program, sixteen rounds of japa, reading and service.
- The order to chant sixteen rounds is the most essential rule, and the other rules help us remember Krishna.
- Regular hearing changes the heart, as the life of Narada shows.
- If our practice becomes weak, we repair it by taking part more fully, not by giving it up.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **CC Madhya 22.107** · "When the heart is purified by hearing and chanting, this love naturally awakens." · The love for Krishna is already in my heart, and my practice uncovers it.
- **BG 12.9** · If you cannot fix the mind on Krishna, follow the regulative principles of bhakti-yoga. · I can begin with the practice, and the desire for Krishna will grow.
- **SB 1.5.26** · Narada heard attentively and his taste "increased at every step". · If I hear regularly and attentively, my taste for Krishna grows.

## S4. Words to know
- **Sadhana:** the regulated daily practice of devotional service.
- **Japa / round:** quietly chanting the Hare Krishna mantra on beads; one round is 108 mantras; sixteen rounds are the standard daily practice.
- **Mangala-arati:** the dawn worship of the Deity.
- **Kirtana:** group singing of the holy names.
- **Brahmacari:** a celibate student who lives simply and serves the guru.
- **Dormant:** present but asleep.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *Is love for Krishna something I have to create?* No. CC Madhya 22.107 says it is already in the heart; sadhana awakens it.
2. *What is the most essential rule?* The order to chant at least sixteen rounds (CC Madhya 22.113).
3. *What if I feel nothing in my chanting?* Keep going and listen carefully to the sound; the heart is being purified (CC Madhya 22.107). Talk to a senior if it lasts.
4. *Why must I read?* Devotees who do not read can lose their position (CC Madhya 25.278); reading answers doubt (BG 4.42).
5. *What if my service makes me miss part of the program?* Make up the time at another hour and keep the sixteen rounds.
6. *What if I feel low in energy?* Act with enthusiasm (for example in kirtana); enthusiasm is action for Krishna (NOI 3).

## S6. Check your understanding
1. In CC Madhya 22.107, what does hearing and chanting do to the heart?
2. Which rule does Prabhupada call "most essential" (CC Madhya 22.113)?
3. What did Narada do for four months of the rainy season (SB 1.5.23-28)?
4. According to CC Madhya 25.278's purport, what happens to devotees who do not read the books?
5. What is the right response to low enthusiasm?

**Answers:** (1) It purifies the heart so that the love for Krishna awakens (CC Madhya 22.107). (2) The spiritual master's order to chant at least sixteen rounds (CC Madhya 22.113). (3) He served the sages and heard them chant and speak of the Lord (SB 1.5.23-28). (4) They will simply eat, sleep and fall down (CC Madhya 25.278). (5) Act enthusiastically: join the kirtana fully; enthusiasm is action for Krishna (NOI 3).
