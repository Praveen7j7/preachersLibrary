## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on sps-15 (where a new devotee learns not to shop for nectar), trd-13 (why taste grows by sadhana, not by shortcuts) and
trd-38 (the eligibility for raganuga, and Prabhupada's warning about siddha-pranali). Those pages treat the practitioner's own
appetite. This page treats the *movement* that institutionalises that appetite, the prakrta-sahajiyas, as Srila Prabhupada and
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta describe them; and it turns the description into a mirror for senior devotees.

The danger grows with seniority. A resident of fifteen years has heard the highest topics, leads kirtanas, and is called advanced by
juniors. Bhaktisiddhanta wrote a hundred warnings in 1916 because in Bengal the imitation of the Gosvamis' topics had become a
religion of its own. Prabhupada carried the same line into his purports, naming the prakrta-sahajiyas dozens of times. A senior
devotee who cannot recognise the pattern in others will not recognise it in himself.

## C. Central message

Real Vaisnavism is the soul's natural service of Visnu "out of pure love expecting nothing from Him in return" (Bhaktisiddhanta,
1926); imitation Vaisnavism, the prakrta-sahajiya way, displays the symptoms of love it does not have, claims advancement, neglects
sastra and the acharyas, and often hides forbidden habits under devotional dress. The acharyas said "no" to it a hundred times
because it steals the goal while wearing its clothes. Lord Caitanya rejected even His dear Chota Haridasa to protect the standard.

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Define prakrta-sahajiya from Prabhupada's purports (CC Antya 20.28; Madhya 22.153; SB 2.3.24) and list its marks.
2. State the real devotee's sign, "the devotee does not think himself a devotee" (CC Antya 20.28), and the pure devotee's test
   (SB 2.3.24 purport: ksanti, avyartha-kalatvam and the rest).
3. Give Bhaktisiddhanta's answer in his two works: the "no" of *Prakrta-rasa-sata-dusini* (texts 1, 25–30, 55–62) and the
   definition of real and apparent Vaisnavism in his 1926 essay.
4. Tell Chota Haridasa's story as the verses tell it (CC Antya 2.117–149), and say what Bhaktisiddhanta draws from it.
5. Apply the marks to themselves first, with the humility of CC Antya 20.21, and know how to deal with sahajiya association (NOI 5;
   CC Antya 6.278) without Vaisnava-aparadha.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**CC Antya 20.28 and purport** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/20/28/
> "Wherever there is a relationship of love of Godhead, its natural symptom is that the devotee does not think himself a devotee. Instead, he always thinks that he has not even a drop of love for Kṛṣṇa."
- **Context:** the Lord's own mood in the Siksastaka, explained by Krsnadasa Kaviraja.
- **Purport point:** Bhaktisiddhanta: those "who possess not even a drop of love of Godhead or pure devotional service falsely
  advertise themselves as great devotees". "A class of so-called devotees known as prākṛta-sahajiyās sometimes display devotional
  symptoms to exhibit their good fortune. They are pretending, however, because these devotional features are only external."
  "When one is actually advanced in ecstatic love of Kṛṣṇa, he does not try to advertise himself." The sahajiyas call pure devotees
  "philosophers, learned scholars, knowers of the truth, or minute observers, but not devotees", and "depict themselves as the most
  advanced, transcendentally blissful devotees ... Not actually knowing the transcendental nature of love of God, they accept their
  material emotions to be indicative of advancement."
- **Teaching:** the one mark that separates real from imitation: the real devotee does not advertise.
- **Use:** in the talk, first and last.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **What "sahajiya" means and why "prakrta".** *Sahaja* is "natural, easy"; the sahajiya claims the highest love is naturally
   available now, without purification. Prabhupada and Bhaktisiddhanta call them *prakrta*, "mundane", because they "accept their
   material emotions to be indicative of advancement" (CC Antya 20.28 purport). The error is not loving the highest topics; it is
   taking material feeling for spiritual love.
2. **The marks, from Prabhupada's purports.** (a) Display of symptoms and self-advertisement (Antya 20.28; SB 2.3.24). (b) Claim of
   advancement, of raganuga, of rasa (Antya 20.28). (c) Neglect of sastra, siddhanta and the acharyas; contempt for Vedanta and for
   "mixed devotees" (Adi 7.72, 7.102). (d) Forbidden habits under devotional dress: "illicit sex, intoxication and gambling, if not
   meat-eating" (Madhya 22.153); "attached to women, money and intoxication" (NOI 5). (e) Manufactured methods: siddha-pranali,
   "whimsical invention" (NOD 16; Madhya 8.83). (f) Trade and caste: the caste guru who sells mantras, the "professional business by
   means of chanting" (Madhya 8.83, 19.160). (g) The inner root: one's own enjoyment as the aim, even "by sharing the happiness of
   Kṛṣṇa" (Antya 20.52).
3. **The test that cannot be copied.** Symptoms can be imitated; the nine signs of bhava in conduct cannot: ksanti (cessation of
   material desire), avyartha-kalatvam (no wasted time), eagerness to glorify the Lord, and the rest (SB 2.3.24 purport). A devotee
   "addicted to smoking, drinking or illegitimate sex with women cannot have all the above-mentioned ecstatic symptoms."
4. **Why Bhaktisiddhanta said "never" a hundred times.** By 1916 the imitation had become a culture in Bengal with its own gurus and
   methods. His hundred couplets refuse each claim at its root: rasa is never produced by effort (1), never taught to the unqualified
   (55, 60), never present while anarthas remain (25, 29), never understood without siddhanta (26, 28), never claimed by the vaidha
   devotee (61), never reached by pulling unripe fruit (57). The genre is "no" because the disease is a false "yes".
5. **Real Vaisnavism, positively.** The soul's natural service of Visnu "out of pure love expecting nothing from Him in return"
   (1926); the jiva "identical ... qualitatively and different ... quantitatively"; every jiva a Vaisnava by nature, "false" only when
   the nature is not shown. The standard is simplicity (saralata, "the first qualification of a Vaiṣṇava", Antya 2.117 purport) and
   the refusal to advertise (Antya 20.28).
6. **Lord Caitanya's ruling.** Before the sahajiyas had a name, the Lord rejected Chota Haridasa for a small lapse "as an example to
   future sahajiyās who might adopt the dress of the renounced order ... but secretly have illicit connections with women" (Antya
   2.143 purport). The history is hard, and it is told as it is (J).
7. **The mirror.** Every mark can grow in a senior resident without his leaving the temple: the wish to be seen as advanced, a
   taste for the highest topics without the lower work, impatience with siddhanta, a habit kept secret, a method of one's own,
   service turned into a position. Bhaktivinoda's song was addressed to his own mind: "Thou art not a Vaishnava." Residents use this
   page on themselves first.
8. **Dealing with sahajiyas.** Respect within the mind, no intimate association (NOI 5; Antya 6.278), no blasphemy (trd-50), and
   firmness about the standard. The Lord's own method with Chota Haridasa was severity toward the lapse and mercy toward the person
   (Antya 2.148).
9. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *"Anyone who speaks of Radha-Krishna's pastimes is a sahajiya."* No: Prabhupada wrote the Krsna book and NOD. The mark is
     imitation and unqualified claim, not the subject (trd-33, trd-44).
   - *"Strictness is itself the goal."* No: the standard protects love; "Spontaneous service is not artificial" (Madhya 22.109
     purport) and comes by the regulative process.
   - *"Sahajiyas are only outside ISKCON."* The purports describe tendencies, not addresses; the mirror is for us.
   - *"Since the Lord was so severe, we may be severe with each other."* The Lord is the Lord; Vaisnava-aparadha is our danger
     (trd-50). The right use is on oneself and, with authorities, on standards.
10. **Pitch for TRD.** Give the purports as they are, the two works by number, the history as it is, and spend the last hour on the
    mirror and on humility (Antya 20.21).

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet the residents. Say: today's topic is uncomfortable and necessary. Our acharyas wrote whole books against a kind of
   devotion that looks like the real thing; we study it so that we can recognise it, first in ourselves.
2. Say plainly: no living person or group will be named today; the purports describe tendencies.
3. Ask H.2 question 1 for private writing. Then question 2; take three answers.
4. Read CC Antya 20.28 aloud, and say that this one sentence will be our measuring rod.

**H.2 Opening questions**
1. When did you last enjoy being thought more advanced than you are? What did you do with the feeling? (Private.)
2. Which "higher topic" have you been drawn to hear or discuss before doing the lower work it presupposes?
3. If a visiting devotee showed tears and trembling in kirtana, how would you tell real from imitation, from scripture?

## I. Core teaching points

1. **The real devotee does not think himself a devotee** (CC Antya 20.28). *Example:* Lord Caitanya's own prayers. *Transition:*
   and the imitator?
2. **The imitator displays and claims** (Antya 20.28 purport; SB 2.3.24 purport). *Transition:* what else goes with it?
3. **Neglect of siddhanta and the acharyas; forbidden habits under devotional dress; manufactured methods; trade** (Adi 7.72, 7.102;
   Madhya 22.153; NOD 16; Madhya 8.83, 19.160; NOI 5). *Transition:* can we test it?
4. **The nine signs of bhava in conduct cannot be copied** (SB 2.3.24 purport: ksanti, avyartha-kalatvam…). *Transition:* the acharya
   who fought this.
5. **Bhaktisiddhanta's hundred "nevers"** (PRSD 1, 25–29, 55–61) and his definition of real Vaisnavism (1926). *Transition:* the Lord's
   own ruling.
6. **Chota Haridasa** (CC Antya 2.117–149; 2.143 purport). *Transition:* the mirror.
7. **"Ye wicked mind! Thou art not a Vaishnava"**: the song to one's own mind; humility (Antya 20.21); dealing with sahajiyas
   (NOI 5; Antya 6.278).

## J. Stories and illustrations

**Chota (Junior) Haridasa** (CC Antya 2.101–169; told here from 2.117–120 and 2.143–156, exactly as the verses tell it). Junior
Haridasa, a dear devotee and singer, approached Madhavi-devi to beg rice to serve the Lord (2.143 purport). The Lord said He could
not tolerate "seeing the face of a person who has accepted the renounced order of life but who still talks intimately with a woman"
(2.117), that even "a wooden statue of a woman attracts the mind of even a great saintly person" (2.118), and that many "accept the
renounced order of life like monkeys" (2.120). He rejected Haridasa and would not relent to the devotees' pleas; "all the devotees
saw this example" and "stopped talking with women, even in dreams" (2.144). "In this way a complete year passed for Junior Haridāsa,
but still there was not a sign of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu's mercy toward him" (2.145). At the end of a night he left for Prayaga
"without saying anything to anyone" (2.146), "entered deep into the water at Triveṇī ... and in this way gave up his life" (2.147).
"Immediately after committing suicide in this way, he went in his spiritual body to Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu and received the mercy
of the Lord. However, he still remained invisible" (2.148), and sang for the Lord at night in a Gandharva-like body (2.149). When
the devotees lamented, the Lord "was mildly smiling" (2.152); hearing the invisible singer, they guessed Haridasa had become a
brahmana ghost (2.155–156), and the Lord later explained.
*Devotional lesson (Bhaktisiddhanta, 2.143 purport):* the chastisement established that a devotee "should not be a hypocrite", and was
"an example to future sahajiyās who might adopt the dress of the renounced order to imitate Rūpa Gosvāmī ... but secretly have illicit
connections with women." The lapse was small; the standard was for the future; the mercy to the person was complete (2.148).
*Note for the preacher:* tell the end as it is (CLAUDE.md "history as it is"). Lessons stay outside the narration. If anyone in the
room is in distress, the lesson is the Lord's mercy to Haridasa, and the resident speaks to a senior devotee; this history is never
used to shame a struggling devotee.

**Bhaktisiddhanta in 1916** (translator's preface to *Prakrta-rasa-sata-dusini*). He "regularly condemned the mood of the
prakrta-sahajiyãs" and composed "the 157 lines of Bengali poetry" published in *Sajjana-tosani* in 1916–17, "famous for the preaching
style called 'the chopping technique'", affirming "nă, nă, nă" to every false claim. *Devotional lesson:* the acharya in our line
spent his strength protecting Rupa Gosvami's standard; a resident inherits that duty, beginning with himself.

**Prabhupada's purports as the same voice** (Antya 20.28; Madhya 22.153; Adi 7.72). He names the prakrta-sahajiyas again and again
across the Caitanya-caritamrta, the Bhagavatam and NOD, in his guru's words. *Devotional lesson:* the line is unbroken; a disciple of
Prabhupada who softens it departs from him.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"Isn't it offensive to criticise devotees who chant and wear tilaka?"** We criticise no person; we read the acharyas' description
   of a tendency and apply it to ourselves. NOI 5: such persons "should be respected within one's mind, but their association should
   be avoided." Vaisnava-aparadha is our own danger (trd-50).
2. **"How do I tell real tears from imitation without judging?"** By conduct over time, not by the moment: the nine signs of bhava
   (SB 2.3.24 purport), and whether forbidden habits are present. And by Antya 20.28: the real devotee does not advertise. You need
   not pronounce; you need only not imitate, and not follow.
3. **"Bhaktisiddhanta says a vaidha devotee cannot understand raganuga (text 61). Then why do we read about it at all?"** Reading
   Prabhupada's books on the Vrajavasis is the hearing that awakens lobha (trd-38; CC Madhya 22.155). Text 61 forbids the vaidha
   devotee's *claim* to understand, and text 60 forbids teaching rasa to the weak in faith. Hear; do not claim.
4. **"Was the Lord not too severe with Chota Haridasa?"** The verse says He chastises His dear devotees "to reestablish the principles
   of religion or duty" (2.143), and the purport says the standard was set "for the future". Haridasa received the Lord's mercy at
   once in his spiritual body (2.148). We tell both facts.
5. **"Some sahajiya ideas sound very sweet. What harm if a devotee enjoys them privately?"** "Debauchees and paramahaṁsas are not on
   the same level" (Madhya 22.153 purport); "Such a manifestation of divine love is mere imitation and has no value" (NOD 16). The
   harm is that it replaces the real thing while feeling like it (Antya 20.28 purport).
6. **"A friend has left for a sahajiya group and invites me to visit. May I go?"** Prabhupada's instruction is to avoid their
   association (NOI 5; Antya 6.278), while respecting them within the mind. Visit your friend as a friend elsewhere; do not sit in
   their programme; tell your authorities.
7. **"Where is the line between taste and sahajiyaism in my own hearing?"** trd-13: taste grows by sadhana and service. Ask: do I
   do the lower work the topic presupposes (text 57); do I keep the standard (Madhya 22.153); do I advertise (Antya 20.28)? If the
   answers are yes, yes, no, hear on.
8. **"Why is this page sensitive?"** It describes illicit conduct under devotional dress and tells a hard history; the head reviews.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The kirtaniya's tears.** Madhu dasa, ten years a resident, leads kirtana with visible weeping that juniors admire; he has
begun to speak of his "mood" and to skip Bhagavatam class as "philosophy".
- **Dilemma:** gift, or the first marks?
- **Principles:** Antya 20.28 and purport (self-advertisement; pure devotees called "philosophers"); Adi 7.102 (acharyas were not
  "sentimentalists"); SB 2.3.24 purport (the nine signs); PRSD 26, 28.
- **Questions:** Which marks are present and which are not? What single thing would show real bhava here? Who should speak to him, and
  what should they not do (trd-50)?
- **Takeaway:** the test is conduct and humility; the remedy is hearing with the class, not stopping the kirtana.

**Case 2: The private method.** A visiting devotee tells a resident that he has received his "eternal identity" from a guru outside
the line and offers to arrange the same.
- **Principles:** NOD 16 (siddha-pranali: "manufactured their own way"; "imagine that they have become associates of the Lord simply
  by thinking of themselves like that"); Madhya 22.153 purport (following the six Gosvamis; svarupa-upalabdhi is realised); Madhya 8.83
  purport (whimsical invention; selling mantras); NOI 5 (respect within the mind, avoid association).
- **Questions:** What exactly is being offered, in Prabhupada's words? How does the resident decline without blasphemy? Whom does he
  inform?
- **Takeaway:** decline, respect, report; the inner identity is realised as the heart is purified, not assigned.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Lord Caitanya's own prayers show the natural sign of real love of God: "the devotee does not think himself a devotee. Instead, he
always thinks that he has not even a drop of love for Kṛṣṇa" (CC Antya 20.28). Srila Prabhupada's purport describes the opposite: a
class called prakrta-sahajiyas ("mundane easy-goers") who "display devotional symptoms to exhibit their good fortune", "depict
themselves as the most advanced, transcendentally blissful devotees", call real devotees mere philosophers, and "accept their
material emotions to be indicative of advancement". His purports give their other marks too: they neglect scripture and the
acharyas, calling the Gaudiya Vedanta commentary "untouchable philosophical speculation" and the acharyas "mixed devotees" (CC Adi
7.72); they keep forbidden habits under devotional dress, "illicit sex, intoxication and gambling, if not meat-eating" (CC Madhya
22.153 purport); they manufacture their own methods, such as the so-called siddha-pranali, imagining "that they have become
associates of the Lord simply by thinking of themselves like that" (NOD 16); some sell mantras or make a business of chanting (CC
Madhya 8.83, 19.160 purports); and at the root they seek their own enjoyment, even "by sharing the happiness of Kṛṣṇa" (CC Antya
20.52 purport). The symptoms of ecstasy can be copied, but not the signs of real bhava in conduct: the end of material desire, no
wasted time, constant eagerness to glorify the Lord (SB 2.3.24 purport).

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati fought this imitation all his life. In 1916 he wrote *Prakrta-rasa-sata-dusini*, a hundred Bengali
couplets each ending in "never": rasa is never produced by material effort (1); it never awakens while anarthas remain (25); without
siddhanta the heart is never attuned to Krishna (26); the followers of Rupa Gosvami never teach intimate mellows to one "engrossed in
material impediments" (55) or to one "who has weak, pliable faith" (60); one should "never climb into a tree, grasp the unripe fruits
and forcibly pull them off" (57). In his 1926 essay *Vaishnavism Real and Apparent* he defined the real Vaisnava as "one who worships
Vishnu out of pure love expecting nothing from Him in return", said every soul is a Vaisnava by nature and only "false" when that
nature is hidden, and turned Bhaktivinoda's song against his own mind: "Ye wicked mind! Thou art not a Vaishnava ... He is rich in
hypocrisy who sings the Lord's name to attain eminence."

Lord Caitanya Himself set the standard before the sahajiyas had a name. His dear singer Junior Haridasa begged rice from a woman
devotee to serve the Lord; the Lord rejected him, saying He could not tolerate "a person who has accepted the renounced order of
life but who still talks intimately with a woman" (CC Antya 2.117). A full year passed without mercy; Haridasa went to Prayaga and
"entered deep into the water at Triveṇī ... and in this way gave up his life" (2.147); "Immediately after committing suicide in this
way, he went in his spiritual body to Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu and received the mercy of the Lord" (2.148). Bhaktisiddhanta explains
that the Lord chastised him "to establish that one in the devotional line ... should not be a hypocrite", "as an example to future
sahajiyās who might adopt the dress of the renounced order" while secretly breaking it (2.143 purport). We study all this as a mirror
for ourselves first, with the humility of CC Antya 20.21; and we deal with those who imitate by respecting them "within one's mind"
while avoiding their association (NOI 5), never by blasphemy.

## S2. Key ideas

- The real devotee does not think himself a devotee; the imitator displays and claims (CC Antya 20.28 and purport).
- Prabhupada's marks of the prakrta-sahajiya: display, claim, neglect of sastra and acharyas, forbidden habits under devotional
  dress, manufactured methods, trade, self-enjoyment (Adi 7.72; Madhya 22.153; NOD 16; Madhya 8.83; Antya 20.52).
- The test that cannot be copied is conduct: the signs of bhava (SB 2.3.24 purport).
- Bhaktisiddhanta's hundred "nevers" refuse each false claim at its root; his 1926 essay defines real Vaisnavism as pure love
  expecting nothing.
- The Lord's chastisement of Chota Haridasa set the standard against hypocrisy in the dress of renunciation (Antya 2.143 purport).
- Use the page on yourself first; respect imitators within the mind and avoid their association (NOI 5; Antya 6.278).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **CC Antya 20.28:** "the devotee does not think himself a devotee. Instead, he always thinks that he has not even a drop of love for
  Kṛṣṇa." *For me:* the measuring rod.
- **CC Antya 20.21:** "One who thinks himself lower than the grass, who is more tolerant than a tree, and who does not expect personal
  honor but is always prepared to give all respect to others can very easily always chant the holy name of the Lord." *For me:* the
  protection.
- **CC Antya 2.143:** "When He chastises His dear devotees, He certainly does so to reestablish the principles of religion or duty."
  *For me:* the standard is mercy for the future.
- **SB 2.3.24:** "that heart is steel-framed which, in spite of one's chanting the holy name of the Lord with concentration, does not
  change". *For me:* the test is a changed heart, shown in conduct.

## S4. Words to know

- **Prakrta-sahajiya:** "mundane easy-goer"; one who takes material emotion for spiritual love and imitates the symptoms of the
  highest devotion without its qualification.
- **Anartha:** an unwanted thing in the heart; while anarthas remain, rasa never awakens (PRSD 25).
- **Siddhanta:** the established conclusions of scripture; the sahajiya neglects them (PRSD 26; CC Adi 7.72).
- **Siddha-pranali:** a so-called method of being assigned one's spiritual identity; Prabhupada calls it manufactured (NOD 16).
- **Saralata:** simplicity, "the first qualification of a Vaiṣṇava" (CC Antya 2.117 purport).
- **Ksanti, avyartha-kalatvam:** cessation of material desire; no time wasted: two of the nine signs of real bhava (SB 2.3.24 purport).

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Isn't it offensive to speak against devotees who chant and wear tilaka?** We speak of a tendency described by our acharyas and
   apply it to ourselves. Prabhupada's rule is to respect such persons "within one's mind" and avoid their association (NOI 5), never
   to blaspheme.
2. **How can I tell real ecstasy from imitation?** Not by the moment but by conduct over time: the signs of real bhava and the
   absence of forbidden habits (SB 2.3.24 purport), and whether the person advertises himself (Antya 20.28). You need not judge; you
   need only not imitate and not follow.
3. **Bhaktisiddhanta says a rule-following devotee cannot understand raganuga. Then why read about Vraja?** Hearing from Prabhupada's
   books is what awakens real attraction (trd-38). The "never" is against claiming to understand, and against teaching rasa to the
   weak in faith (PRSD 60–61). Hear; do not claim.
4. **Was the Lord not too harsh with Junior Haridasa?** The verse says He chastises to re-establish religious principles (2.143); the
   purport says the standard was set for the future; and Haridasa received the Lord's mercy at once in his spiritual body (2.148).
5. **A friend has joined such a group and invites me. May I visit?** Meet your friend elsewhere as a friend; do not sit in their
   programme (NOI 5; Antya 6.278); tell your authorities.
6. **Where is the line between loving the sweet topics and sahajiyaism in me?** Ask three questions: do I do the lower work the topic
   presupposes (PRSD 57)? do I keep every standard (Madhya 22.153)? do I advertise (Antya 20.28)? Yes, yes, no: hear on.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. What is the natural symptom of real love of God in CC Antya 20.28?
2. Name four marks of the prakrta-sahajiya from Prabhupada's purports.
3. What can be imitated, and what cannot, according to the SB 2.3.24 purport?
4. Give two of Bhaktisiddhanta's "nevers" with their numbers.
5. What did the Lord's chastisement of Junior Haridasa establish, according to Bhaktisiddhanta (2.143 purport)?
6. How does Prabhupada tell us to deal with sahajiyas (NOI 5)?

**Answers:** 1. The devotee does not think himself a devotee. 2. Any four of: display of symptoms; claim of advancement; neglect of
scripture and the acharyas; forbidden habits under devotional dress; manufactured methods such as siddha-pranali; trade in chanting;
self-enjoyment as the aim. 3. The eight symptoms of ecstasy can be imitated; the signs of bhava in conduct (ksanti, no wasted time,
eagerness to glorify) cannot. 4. Any two of: 1 (rasa never from material effort), 25 (never while anarthas remain), 26 (never
without siddhanta), 55/60 (never taught to the unqualified or weak in faith), 57 (never pull unripe fruit). 5. That a devotee should
not be a hypocrite; an example for future sahajiyas in the dress of renunciation. 6. Respect them within the mind, avoid their
association.
