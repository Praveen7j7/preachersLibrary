**About the sources.** The short account in *Srimad-Bhagavatam* (SB 9.23.7-10) is Srila Prabhupada's text. The full story is told in the *Mahabharata* (Vana-parva, Tirtha-yatra, sections CX-CXIII, translated by K.M. Ganguli) and in the *Ramayana* (Book I, translated by R.T.H. Griffith). These two are used for the narrative only. They are not Srila Prabhupada's books, and the page says each time which source is speaking. The principles come from Srila Prabhupada's books.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level
A brahmacari is often someone who has lived a protected life, in a family, a school or an ashram, and who believes that being away from temptation makes him pure. The story of Rsyasrnga is the classic example of a young man who was protected only by distance, and who was therefore not ready when the world came to him. This page gives the full story and the lessons for a brahmacari who wants to be ready.

**Builds on bss-31** (Dealings with Women Devotees), which gives a short retelling of SB 9.23.7-10 and a remark on it; that retelling is not repeated. **Builds on bss-12** (Haridasa Thakura and the prostitute), which shows the opposite case: a man who was not naive and who was protected by the holy name. **Builds on bss-07** (association and respect for all), bss-17 (the higher taste) and bss-16 and bss-55 (the brahmacari's rules), none of which is repeated here.

## C. Central message
A young man who has never met temptation has not yet conquered it. A brahmacari is protected by knowledge received from his guru, by good association, by a taste for the Lord's service and the holy name, and by a purpose for his purity. In the story, the pure sacrifice of Rsyasrnga brings rain to a country and sons to Maharaja Dasaratha, and the Lord appears as Rama.

## D. Learning outcomes
By the end, participants can:
1. Tell the story of Rsyasrnga in order and say which parts are from SB 9.23.7-10, which from the Mahabharata and which from the Ramayana.
2. Explain why distance from temptation is not the same as protection, and what BG 4.34 teaches about learning from a guru.
3. Explain why the sacrifice of Rsyasrnga is said to bring rain (BG 3.14) and what sacrifice is prescribed today.
4. Say what the story teaches about the purpose of a brahmacari's purity.
5. Name one person they can talk to when they feel pulled by attraction, and one practice that gives a higher taste.

## E. Scriptural foundation
Use SB 9.23.7-10, SB 9.10.2, BG 3.14 and BG 4.34 in the talk. SB 5.5.26 is for the closing.

**SB 9.23.7-10**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/9/23/7-10/
> "…Romapāda, however, was without issue, and therefore his friend Mahārāja Daśaratha gave him his own daughter, named Śāntā. Romapāda accepted her as his daughter, and thereafter she married Ṛṣyaśṛṅga."
> "When the demigods from the heavenly planets failed to shower rain, Ṛṣyaśṛṅga was appointed the priest for performing a sacrifice, after being brought from the forest by the allurement of prostitutes, who danced, staged theatrical performances accompanied by music, and embraced and worshiped him. After Ṛṣyaśṛṅga came, the rain fell. Thereafter, Ṛṣyaśṛṅga performed a son-giving sacrifice on behalf of Mahārāja Daśaratha, who had no issue, and then Mahārāja Daśaratha had sons."
- **Context:** Sukadeva Gosvami is listing the descendants of Anga in the line of kings (SB 9.23.1-10). Romapada is the king.
- **Teaching:** In a time when the demigods did not send rain, Rsyasrnga, who was in the forest, was brought "by the allurement of prostitutes, who danced, staged theatrical performances accompanied by music, and embraced and worshiped him"; he was made priest of a sacrifice, and rain fell. He later performed a sacrifice for Dasaratha, who then had sons. He married Santa, whom Romapada accepted as his daughter.
- **Purport point:** The verse has no purport on Vedabase, so the page claims nothing from a purport.
- **Application (preacher's):** The verse says what the prostitutes did; it does not say what he thought or felt. Those details come from the Mahabharata.
- **Use:** in the talk, as the base of the story.

**SB 9.10.2**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/9/10/2/
> "Being prayed for by the demigods, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, the Absolute Truth Himself, directly appeared with His expansion and expansions of the expansion. Their holy names were Rāma, Lakṣmaṇa, Bharata and Śatrughna."
- **Teaching:** The Lord appeared in four forms as the sons of Maharaja Dasaratha.
- **Purport point:** The purport says that Rama, Laksmana, Bharata and Satrughna are visnu-tattva, not jiva-tattva, and that they appeared in response to the prayers of the demigods.
- **Application (preacher's):** SB 9.23.7-10 and SB 9.10.2 both speak of the sons of Dasaratha. The Ramayana says that the sacrifice performed by Rsyasrnga was the sacrifice for these sons (Book I). The Bhagavatam does not state the link in words, so say "the Ramayana tells us".
- **Use:** in the talk, for the ending.

**BG 3.14**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/14/
> "All living bodies subsist on food grains, which are produced from rains. Rains are produced by performance of yajña [sacrifice], and yajña is born of prescribed duties."
- **Purport point:** The purport says that the demigods like Indra, Candra and Varuna are appointed officers who supply air, light and water, that the Lord is satisfied by sacrifices, and that one who cannot perform them will find himself in scarcity. It says that the sankirtana-yajna prescribed for this age must be performed to save us at least from scarcity of food supply.
- **Application (preacher's):** This is the principle behind the story: the people of Anga suffered when the demigods did not send rain, and a sacrifice by a pure brahmana brought it. Today we are told to perform sankirtana.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 4.34**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/4/34/
> "Just try to learn the truth by approaching a spiritual master. Inquire from him submissively and render service unto him."
- **Purport point:** The purport says that one has to approach a bona fide spiritual master to receive knowledge, and that "inquiries and submission constitute the proper combination for spiritual understanding". It also says that a bona fide spiritual master is by nature very kind to the disciple.
- **Application (preacher's):** Rsyasrnga's father did not explain the dangers until after the event, and then very briefly. The verse shows a better way: a student asks, and the guru answers.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 5.5.26**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/5/26/
> "My dear sons, you should not envy any living entity — be he moving or nonmoving. Knowing that I am situated in them, you should offer respect to all of them at every moment. In this way, you offer respect to Me."
- **Purport point:** The purport says all living entities are the abode of the Lord in His Paramatma feature, so "all living entities should similarly be offered respect"; "An advanced devotee will offer respects to everyone — even to cats and dogs." (Quoted in full in bss-07.)
- **Use:** for the closing.

## F. Philosophical explanation
**Principle.** Srila Prabhupada's books teach that the senses are strong and that the mind needs support (BG 2.59-2.60, covered in bss-17 and bss-03). The story adds one point to this: a person who has never been tested has not yet shown that he is protected. His innocence is a fact, not a strength.

**Why it matters.** Many brahmacaris come to the ashram young and sincere, from sheltered homes. They sometimes believe that sincerity is enough. The story shows a youth of great austerity who nonetheless leaves the forest with the first persons who come to him, because he does not know what they are. It says nothing against his austerity or his father's. It says that austerity without knowledge of the world and without a higher taste is not a full protection.

**Connections.** Association and guidance protect (SB 5.5.26, BG 4.34). A higher taste protects (BG 2.59, bss-17; Haridasa Thakura, bss-12). Purpose protects: the pure sacrifice of Rsyasrnga serves the people (BG 3.14), and today the sacrifice for us is sankirtana. Together, these answer the question "what keeps a brahmacari?"

**Consequences.** The sources show what each person did: the king made a plan that deceived a youth who had seen no one but his father; the women were sent by the king and did what SB 9.23.7-10 and the Mahabharata describe; the father warned his son only after the event; the youth did not ask. For the brahmacari the lesson the sources carry is BG 4.34: learn from the guru before the test comes.

**Common misunderstandings.**
1. "The story shows that innocence is dangerous." No. It shows that innocence alone is not enough. Training adds to it.
2. "The women acted on their own." The Mahabharata says the king's ministers sent them, that they feared the king's anger and the sage's curse, and that one older courtesan took the task on herself.
3. "Rsyasrnga fell." The sources do not say he fell. He performed sacrifices, married and returned to the forest as his father asked. The lesson is not about a fall.
4. "A brahmacari should never leave the ashram." The story does not say that. It says that a brahmacari should be trained and guided before and during his contact with the world.

**How to pitch it.** Tell it as the sources tell it, each person doing what they did, and say each time which source is speaking. Speak to men who may feel that they are like Rsyasrnga. Make clear that help is available, and give the next step (P).

## I. Core teaching points
**I.1 Innocence is not the same as protection (SB 9.23.7-10; Mahabharata, section CX-CXII).** SB 9.23.7-10 says Rsyasrnga was "brought from the forest by the allurement of prostitutes, who danced, staged theatrical performances accompanied by music, and embraced and worshiped him". The Mahabharata adds that he had never seen anyone but his father, and that the advice to the king was to send for a youth "knowing nothing of the female sex". When the courtesan came, she told him her religious observance was "that thou must be clasped in my arms", gave him food, drink and garlands, embraced him and left; he was "over-powered with love and lost his sense", and told his father that "a religious student" had come. He was sincere, but he had no way to know. **Illustration (preacher's, labelled):** a young swimmer who has trained only in a still pool has never felt a current. He is not weak, but he is not ready for a river. Ending: we need training for the river, not only the pool. Transition: who gives the training?

**I.2 Guidance protects (BG 4.34; Mahabharata, section CXII).** In the Mahabharata, Vibhandaka returned, found his son unhappy, and explained: "Those are, O son! Rakshasas. ... they assume lovely forms and try to allure by diverse means ... a pious man should never look at them"; the drinks were "spirituous liquors" and the garlands "not intended for saints". Then he went out to search for three days, and while he was out gathering fruits the courtesan returned and the youth said, "Let us go to thy hermitage before the return of my father." The father's explanation came after the event. BG 4.34 shows the better pattern: the student asks, the guru answers, and the student serves. The purport says a bona fide spiritual master is by nature very kind to his disciple. **Application (preacher's):** a senior in the ashram should explain, calmly and honestly, what dangers there are and how to meet them, before they arise. A junior should ask, and not hide what he feels. Both are protection. Transition: what does a trained person have that an innocent one does not?

**I.3 A higher taste protects (bss-12, bss-17).** Rsyasrnga's attraction was not weak. What he lacked was a stronger taste. Haridasa Thakura, in bss-12, was approached in the night by a woman sent to tempt him. He was not naive, and he was protected because he was chanting the holy name three hundred thousand times each day, and he kept chanting while he spoke to her. A brahmacari's real protection is the same: hearing and chanting, service and the company of devotees (see bss-07 and bss-17 for the verses). Transition: what is the purity for?

**I.4 Purity has a purpose (BG 3.14; SB 9.23.7-10; SB 9.10.2).** In SB 9.23.7-10, Rsyasrnga's sacrifice brings rain and later gives Dasaratha sons. BG 3.14 explains that rain comes from sacrifice and that the Lord is satisfied by sacrifice, and its purport says the sacrifice prescribed for this age is sankirtana. In the Ramayana, the sons are Rama and His brothers. SB 9.10.2 tells us they are the Lord Himself appearing. A brahmacari's purity is not an end in itself. It makes him fit to serve in the Lord's sankirtana. Ending: ask "What am I keeping my mind and heart clean for?" Transition: what do we take away?

## J. Stories and illustrations
**J.1 The story of Rsyasrnga (SB 9.23.7-10; Mahabharata, Vana-parva CX-CXIII).** Tell it in your own words and say "the Mahabharata says" for the details.
1. *Birth and boyhood.* Rsyasrnga is the son of the sage Vibhandaka. The Mahabharata says he was born of a deer and had a horn on his head. He grew up in the forest with his father, saw no other person, and lived in strict celibacy and austerity. (The Mahabharata gives an account of his unusual birth; it is not needed for this talk.)
2. *The drought.* King Lomapada (Romapada in the Bhagavatam) of Anga was without a son. SB 9.23.7-10 says that Maharaja Dasaratha gave him his daughter Santa and that he accepted her as his own. The Mahabharata says that the king had told a falsehood to a brahmana, that the brahmanas shunned him, and that Indra withheld rain. SB 9.23.7-10 says the demigods failed to send rain. Note that the sources differ on Santa's parentage. We follow the Bhagavatam.
3. *The advice.* Wise people told the king to bring Rsyasrnga, who would bring rain if he came into the country.
4. *The plan and the visit.* The king's ministers made a plan. Women were sent to the forest on a floating hermitage. The Mahabharata says they were afraid of the king's anger and of the sage's curse, and one older courtesan took the task on herself. SB 9.23.7-10 says the youth was brought "by the allurement of prostitutes, who danced, staged theatrical performances accompanied by music, and embraced and worshiped him". The Mahabharata tells the visit: she told him her religious observance was "that thou must be clasped in my arms", refused his fruits and gave him "unsuitable things for food", garlands, shining garments and "first-rate drinks", played with a ball, "touched his body with her own and repeatedly clasped Rishyasringa in her arms", and when she saw his heart was touched, went away saying she had to make offerings on the fire. He was "over-powered with love and lost his sense".
5. *The father's return and the departure.* Vibhandaka came back, found his son troubled, and heard him describe "a religious student" who "clasped my body ... and, mingling his mouth with my own, uttered a sound that was exceedingly pleasant". He said: "Those are, O son! Rakshasas ... a pious man should never look at them." He searched three days and returned; while he was out gathering fruits the courtesan came again, and the youth said, "Let us go to thy hermitage before the return of my father." The women took him into their boat to Anga, and the king kept him in the women's quarters of the palace.
6. *The rain.* In Anga, rain fell. The king gave Santa in marriage to Rsyasrnga (SB 9.23.7-10).
7. *The father is reconciled.* The Mahabharata says that Vibhandaka set out in anger and came to the king's cattle and fields, which the king had prepared in his son's name. His anger was pacified. He told his son to return to the forest when a son was born. Santa served her husband in the forest.
**Devotional lesson:** a young person's sincerity is precious, and it is honoured when elders train it. The story ends in service: a sacrifice that brings rain and sons.

**J.2 The sacrifice for Dasaratha (SB 9.23.7-10; Ramayana, Book I).** SB 9.23.7-10 says that Rsyasrnga performed a son-giving sacrifice on behalf of Maharaja Dasaratha, who then had sons. In the Ramayana, Sumantra tells Dasaratha that the sage Sanatkumara had foretold this long before: a young hermit, bred in the forest, would one day bring rain to Lomapad's land, marry the princess Santa and perform the sacrifice that would give Dasaratha sons. SB 9.10.2 tells us who these sons were: the Lord Himself, in four forms, in response to the prayers of the demigods.
**Devotional lesson:** the sacrifice of a pure brahmacari was used by the Lord's own arrangement. The Lord appeared as Rama in the house of the king for whom the sacrifice was done.

**J.3 Invented illustration (labelled).** A young man raised in a village temple joins an ashram in a big city. On his first day a friendly stranger invites him for tea and tells him it is a "special fast". He goes. Later, a senior devotee explains kindly that it was a group that collects money in the name of fasting. The young man says, "I did not know. I wish someone had told me." The senior says, "From now on, ask me first." The point: sincerity and ignorance can go together, and a guide who answers early protects what sincerity cannot (BG 4.34).

## K. Anticipated questions and objections
1. **"Is the Mahabharata version authoritative?"** It is one of the great histories and is respected by our tradition, but it is not Srila Prabhupada's text. We use it for the narrative. The principles are from Srila Prabhupada's books.
2. **"Why does the Bhagavatam say 'prostitutes'?"** Because that is what the verse says: he was brought "by the allurement of prostitutes, who danced, staged theatrical performances accompanied by music, and embraced and worshiped him". The Mahabharata calls them courtesans, says the king's ministers sent them and that they feared the sage's curse. Read the verse as it is.
3. **"Did Rsyasrnga do wrong?"** The sources do not say so. He did what the Mahabharata describes without knowing who the visitors were; he performed the sacrifice, married Santa and returned to the forest as his father asked.
4. **"Did the king do wrong?"** The Mahabharata shows him acting first from a fault of his own and then by a plan to bring about rain. The page does not judge the king beyond what the source says. As the preacher, say: "A good result does not make every means good. We are not told here that the plan was right."
5. **"I am a brahmacari and I feel pulled. What should I do?"** This is common and no cause for shame. Speak to a trusted senior devotee or your mentor; chant, hear and serve in the company of devotees; and if the struggle is heavy, or if you feel low, anxious or hopeless, see a qualified counsellor or doctor. In India, Tele-MANAS is 14416. In other countries, use the local crisis service.
6. **"Is this the same as the story of Vishvamitra?"** No. That is a different story. Both are about a sage and the mind, but they have different people and lessons. This page does not use the Vishvamitra story.
7. **"Why was his marriage to Santa not a fall?"** Marriage is a regulated stage of life. This page does not discuss it; the sources show him returning to the forest with his wife as his father asked.

## L. Case studies
**L.1 Invented case for discussion.** Amit, 19, has lived all his life with his family, who are devotees, and has had little contact with people outside. He joins a college hostel for his studies and a friend, who is friendly and not a devotee, invites him to a party where there will be alcohol. Amit thinks, "I am strong; nothing will happen."
- *Dilemma:* Should he go?
- *Principles:* the story of Rsyasrnga; BG 4.34 (ask the guru); SB 5.5.26 (respect for all).
- *Questions:* What does he know and not know about parties? Whom could he ask? What could he do instead that keeps the friendship?
- *Analysis:* He should ask his mentor before deciding, and say honestly what he does not know. He can invite the friend for prasadam and kirtana. He does not need to look down on the friend.
- *Takeaway:* "I am strong" is not the same as "I have been trained". Ask before you go.

**L.2 Invented case for discussion (extension).** A senior brahmacari notices that a new member is quiet and looks troubled after a visit to a market.
- *Principles:* BG 4.34 (kindness of the guru); H.1 (help-seeking).
- *Questions:* How should the senior speak? What should he not say? What help can he offer?
- *Analysis:* He should speak privately and kindly, listen first and not assume, and offer prayer, company and service. If the new member is low or distressed, he can suggest a counsellor and the helpline. He should not shame or make fun.
- *Takeaway:* the senior here acts as the father in the story could have acted: kindly and early.

## M. Discussion and reflection
**M.1 Closing check questions (with expected answers).**
1. *Where did Rsyasrnga grow up, and whom had he seen?* In the forest; he had seen only his father (Mahabharata).
2. *Why was he sent for?* The king's country had no rain, and he was said to be able to bring it (SB 9.23.7-10; Mahabharata).
3. *What does BG 3.14 say about rain?* Rain is produced by sacrifice, and sacrifice is born of prescribed duties.
4. *What does BG 4.34 say a student should do?* Approach a spiritual master, inquire submissively and render service.
5. *What did Rsyasrnga's sacrifice for Dasaratha give?* Sons, according to SB 9.23.7-10; the Ramayana says they were Rama and His brothers, and SB 9.10.2 says the Lord appeared as these sons.
6. *What is the difference between innocence and protection?* Innocence is not having met temptation. Protection is knowledge, good association, a higher taste and a purpose.

**M.2 Activities.**
a) *Four stops (15 min).* Tell the story in four parts. After each part ask the group one question and take three answers: (1) "What does he know?" (2) "What did the king want?" (3) "What did the father say, and when?" (4) "What did everyone gain?" No answer is wrong.
b) *A letter to a younger brahmacari (10 min).* In pairs, write four sentences that a senior would say to a new member about asking for guidance. Read one pair's letter aloud.
c) *Short drama (15 min, extension).* Roles: Vibhandaka, Rishyasringa, narrator. Script: a father returns and finds his son troubled. Perform it twice: first as in the Mahabharata (the father's warning), then with a full explanation given before the event (BG 4.34). Debrief: "What changed for the son?" The visitors' part is narrated by the narrator from the sources.
**M.3 Reflection.** "Who is my senior I can ask anything? When did I last ask?" Private; nothing is shared.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary
In *Srimad-Bhagavatam* (SB 9.23.7-10) we read that when the demigods did not send rain, a young sage named Rsyasrnga was brought out of the forest and made priest of a sacrifice, and that rain fell. He later performed a sacrifice for King Dasaratha, who then had sons. The Mahabharata tells more. Rsyasrnga grew up in the forest with his father and had never seen anyone else. A king whose land had no rain made a plan to bring him, and women were sent to the forest on the king's order. The Bhagavatam says he was brought "by the allurement of prostitutes, who danced, staged theatrical performances accompanied by music, and embraced and worshiped him". The Mahabharata says the courtesan told him that her religious observance was to be clasped in her arms, gave him food, drink and garlands, embraced him and left, and that he was "over-powered with love". He did not know who she was. His father told him afterwards that such visitors were Rakshasas who obstruct penance; while the father was out, she came again and the youth went with the women to Anga. The rain fell, the king gave him the princess Santa in marriage, and his father was calmed.
The lesson for a brahmacari is that innocence is not the same as protection. A student needs a guru who explains early and kindly (BG 4.34), good company, a higher taste in the holy name and service, and a purpose. The purpose in the story is a sacrifice: BG 3.14 teaches that rain comes from sacrifice, and the sacrifice for this age is sankirtana. In the Ramayana the sons of Dasaratha were Rama and His brothers, and SB 9.10.2 tells us that the Lord Himself appeared as these sons. If you feel pulled or low, speak to a trusted senior devotee or a counsellor. In India you can call Tele-MANAS on 14416.

## S2. Key ideas
- Innocence has not yet been tested. Protection comes from guidance, good association, a higher taste and a purpose.
- A student should ask his guru, and a guru should explain kindly (BG 4.34).
- "You should offer respect to all of them at every moment" (SB 5.5.26).
- Rain comes from sacrifice (BG 3.14). The sacrifice for this age is sankirtana.
- Help is always available. Speak to someone you trust.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **BG 4.34**: Approach a spiritual master, inquire submissively and serve him. *For me:* I will ask a senior what I do not know.
- **BG 3.14**: Rains are produced by sacrifice. *For me:* I take part in sankirtana.
- **SB 5.5.26**: "You should offer respect to all of them at every moment. In this way, you offer respect to Me." *For me:* I offer respect to every living being.

## S4. Words to know
- **Brahmacari:** a celibate student who lives simply and serves his guru.
- **Sacrifice (yajna):** an act of worship that satisfies the Lord; today, mainly sankirtana.
- **Sankirtana:** the congregational chanting of the holy names of the Lord.
- **Mahabharata and Ramayana:** two great histories of India. They are not Srila Prabhupada's books.
- **Counsellor:** a trained person who helps with feelings and problems.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *Is this story in Srila Prabhupada's books?* Only briefly (SB 9.23.7-10). The details are from the Mahabharata.
2. *What did the prostitutes do?* SB 9.23.7-10: they "danced, staged theatrical performances accompanied by music, and embraced and worshiped him", and so brought him from the forest. The Mahabharata says the king's ministers sent them.
3. *What should I do if I feel pulled?* Speak to a trusted senior devotee, chant, hear and serve in the company of devotees. If you feel low or the struggle is heavy, see a counsellor. In India call Tele-MANAS on 14416.
4. *Why does a sacrifice bring rain?* BG 3.14 says that rain is produced by sacrifice, and the purport says the Lord is satisfied by sacrifice.
5. *Who were the sons of Dasaratha?* Rama, Laksmana, Bharata and Satrughna, the Lord and His expansions (SB 9.10.2).
