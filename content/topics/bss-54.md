# SB Canto 5, Chapter 13 & 14

> Status line: BSS (brahmacari training) · 120 min · drafted · not sensitive. Study session: the verse-by-verse key to the "forest of material enjoyment". Builds on bss-10.

## A. Topic overview
| | |
|---|---|
| Batch / level | BSS, brahmacari training (Bhakti Siddhanta Sabha) |
| Suggested duration | 120 minutes (study session, with an "if time allows" extension) |
| Preparation time | About 2.5 hours: read SB 5.13 and 5.14 in full with purports once (about 70 verses), then this page |
| Difficulty | Intermediate |
| Prerequisites | bss-10 (the central equation of SB 5.14: merchant, forest, plunderers). Helpful: bss-07 |
| Audience | Young men living or preparing to live as brahmacaris; also any seeker. Brahmacari = a celibate student who lives simply, serves the guru and studies scripture |
| Teaching objective | The audience can decode the forest symbol by symbol as Srila Prabhupada's translations and purports decode it, recognise those symbols in their own day, and see that the only exit the chapters give is the mercy of a devotee, hearing and chanting, and service |

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level
**Builds on bss-10.** That page takes only the central equation of SB 5.14 (the merchant is the soul, the forest is the world,
the six plunderers are the senses, and the way out is association with devotees) and the confession of King Rahugana. This
page does not repeat it. It is the key to the rest of the two chapters: the other symbols of the allegory (mosquitoes, gold,
python, the house in the sky, the seeds in the field, the buzzards and crows, the wheel of time, the elephant), and the
passage on false and true refuge at the end of SB 5.14. A brahmacari who knows the key can read his own morning, his own
hostel and his own phone as part of the forest, and knows where the exit is.

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| VL1 | The story of the merchant in the forest as one picture of why happiness never settles (see bss-10 for the equation) |
| VL2 | The symbols that matter most to a student: gold, time, death |
| VL3 | Practice: hearing, chanting and association as the answer to each symbol |
| SPS | The two chapters as one argument, from Jada Bharata's teaching to Rahugana's change |
| DPS | The key in full, with the passage on false gurus and on the path of fruitive action |
| BSS | This page: the key for brahmacari life, ending in Bharata Maharaja's example of what service to Krsna does to a heart |

Not done here: the central equation and the six plunderers (bss-10); Rahugana's confession as a subject (bss-10); the family
life passages in detail (SB 5.14.14-19, 5.14.35-38), which concern householders more than brahmacaris; the Bharata life-story
itself (SB 5.7-5.9). Verses about the opposite sex are left to bss-21, bss-22 and bss-23.

## C. Central message
Every part of the forest is a way the Lord's energy keeps a soul who has turned away from Krsna busy, hungry and afraid; but
Jada Bharata and Sukadeva Gosvami give the exit in the same breath: shelter at the feet of a pure devotee, hearing and chanting
the holy name, and using all wealth and activity for the Lord. For a brahmacari, to know the key is to see where his service
and attention are being asked to go.

## D. Learning outcomes
By the end, participants can:
1. Explain why Sukadeva calls this teaching an allegory and what Prabhupada's purport says to do about it (SB 5.13.26).
2. Decode at least eight symbols of the forest (mosquitoes, gold, python, house in the sky, hill, shallow river, vultures and crows, elephant) from the verses.
3. State what the verses say happens to wealth (SB 5.14.24, 5.14.7) and what the purport says to do with it.
4. Explain why the seed of desire returns after it is cut (SB 5.14.4) and what the purport says burns it out.
5. Describe the path out given in SB 5.14.39-43, and say what Bharata Maharaja shows about a devotee who falls (SB 5.14.43).
6. Choose one concrete step: reduce one "python" of the day, or return to one source of association.

## E. Scriptural foundation
The study goes through eighteen verses. The two chapters have 26 and 46 verses; only the verses below carry the key. Where
a table in F.1 cites a verse only by its translation, the purport was not used for that line.

**SB 5.13.26** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/13/26/
> "…it is difficult to extricate the exact meaning from the allegory."
- **Context:** King Pariksit has heard Jada Bharata's story of the merchant told to King Rahugana. He asks Sukadeva Gosvami for the direct meaning.
- **Teaching:** The translation says that intelligent men can understand that the senses are like rogues and thieves and the wife and children like jackals and ferocious animals in that forest; for the unintelligent, the exact meaning is hard to extract from the allegory, so Pariksit asks for it plainly.
- **Purport point:** The purport says the Bhagavatam describes many stories and incidents figuratively, and unintelligent men may not understand them; so the student should approach a bona fide spiritual master for the direct explanation.
- **Application (preacher's):** The first lesson is about method. A brahmacari does not invent his own meaning for the forest. He asks, as Pariksit asks, and he receives the meaning from the line of devotees.
- **Use:** in the talk (opening).

**SB 5.13.3** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/13/3/
> "…cruelly biting mosquitoes [envious people]."
- **Teaching:** In the forest there are dense bowers of bushes, grass and creepers where the conditioned soul is always disturbed by biting mosquitoes (envious people). Sometimes he sees an imaginary palace, and sometimes a fleeting fiend or ghost "which appears like a meteor in the sky".
- **Purport point:** The purport says household material life is a hole of fruitive activity; in earning a livelihood one meets many undesirable people, whose behaviour is compared to the biting of mosquitoes. Even among disturbances one imagines building a grand house to live in permanently, although he knows he cannot. Gold is the quickly fleeting fiend: it shows itself for a moment and is gone; karmis are attracted to gold and money, which are compared to ghosts and witches.
- **Application (preacher's):** The purport says that in earning a livelihood one meets "many undesirable people" and that their behaviour is the mosquito bite. The palace and the fiend are explained in SB 5.14.5 and 5.14.7 below.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 5.14.5** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/14/5/
> "…he sees the material world as permanent, although it is temporary like a phantasmagoria, a house in the sky."
- **Teaching:** The soul in the forest is troubled by gadflies, mosquitoes, locusts, birds of prey and rats, yet goes on; out of ignorance he becomes lusty and engages in fruitive activity, and because his mind is absorbed in it he takes a temporary world to be permanent.
- **Purport point:** The purport quotes Narottama dasa Thakura (ahankare matta hana, nitai-pada pasariya, asatyere satya kari mani): forgetting the lotus feet of Lord Nityananda and puffed up by wealth, one takes the false, temporary world for fact. It says this is the material disease: the living entity is eternal and blissful, but thinks the world real.
- **Application (preacher's):** A "house in the sky" is any plan of the form "when I have this, I will finally be at peace", for example the perfect job, a high position or a good reputation. Ask the audience to name one such plan of their own.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 5.14.7** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/14/7/
> "Sometimes the living entity is interested in the yellow stool known as gold and runs after it."
- **Teaching:** Gold is the source of material opulence and envy and enables one to afford illicit sex, gambling, meat-eating and intoxication. Those in the mode of passion are attracted to its colour like a man suffering from cold in the forest who runs after a phosphorescent light in marshy land thinking it fire.
- **Purport point:** The purport recalls that Maharaja Pariksit told Kali-yuga to leave and reside in four places (brothels, liquor shops, slaughterhouses, gambling casinos); Kali asked for one place containing all four and was given where gold is stored. In its words: "Gold encompasses the four principles of sin, and therefore, according to spiritual life, gold should be avoided as far as possible. If there is gold, there is certainly illicit sex, meat-eating, gambling and intoxication. Because people in the Western world have a great deal of gold, they are victims of these four sins." And: "this gold is actually a type of stool. A person with a bad liver generally passes yellow stool. The color of this stool attracts a materialistic person, just as the will-o'-the-wisp attracts one who needs heat."
- **Application (preacher's):** Read the purport as it stands. SB 5.14.24 below says what the purport there says about money kept with Narayana.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 5.14.24** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/14/24/
> "Ultimately no one can enjoy the money, and it remains the property of the Supreme Personality of Godhead."
- **Teaching:** One person cheats another and keeps the money for a time; then a man named Devadatta cheats him, then Visnumitra steals it from Devadatta. The money passes from hand to hand.
- **Purport point:** The purport says riches come from Laksmi, who belongs to Narayana and cannot stay anywhere else (her name Cancala means restless); Ravana carried off Sita, the goddess of fortune belonging to Lord Rama, and lost family, opulence and kingdom. All wealth belongs to Krsna (it quotes BG 5.29). The best way to possess Laksmi is to keep her by the side of Narayana: money collected is used in the service of Radha and Krsna, and the devotee then lives in an opulent way automatically. Death, the representative of Krsna, finally takes everything.
- **Application (preacher's):** Even a brahmacari with little money can learn the right attitude. Whatever passes through his hands, such as a donation, a small allowance or a gift, belongs to Krsna. Money that is not used in His service will still be spent and lost.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 5.14.4** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/14/4/
> "Unless the desire to enjoy family life is completely burned out, it grows up again and again."
- **Teaching:** Every year the plowman uproots the weeds, yet seeds remain and come up again; family life is a field of fruitive activity. Even if camphor is removed from a pot, the aroma remains. As long as the seeds of desire are not destroyed, fruitive activities are not destroyed.
- **Purport (Srila Prabhupada's words, in full):** "Unless one's desires are completely transferred to the service of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, the desire for family life continues, even after one has taken sannyāsa. Sometimes in our society, ISKCON, a person out of sentiment may take sannyāsa, but because his desires are not burned completely, he again takes to family life, even at the risk of losing his prestige and disgracing his good name. These strong desires can be burned out completely when one fully engages in the service of the Lord in devotional service."
- **Application (preacher's):** Read the purport as it stands. It names the cause (sannyasa taken out of sentiment, desires not burned completely), the result (he again takes to family life, "even at the risk of losing his prestige and disgracing his good name") and the one remedy (full engagement in the service of the Lord).
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 5.13.9** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/13/9/
> "…left lying in the forest like a dead person, devoid of consciousness and knowledge."
- **Teaching:** The conditioned soul is sometimes swallowed by a python or crushed and left unconscious; poisonous snakes bite him; blind to his consciousness, he falls into a dark well of hellish life with no hope of rescue.
- **Purport point:** The purport says the unconscious condition is like deep sleep: the soul is sleeping on the lap of the illusory energy. It quotes Bhaktivinoda Thakura (kota nidra yao maya-pisacira kole) and Lord Caitanya's words, "I have brought medicine to awaken every living being from perpetual sleep. Please receive the holy name of the Lord, the Hare Krsna maha-mantra, and awaken", and the Katha Upanisad call to arise and awake. It says the sleeping condition called kala-sarpa (the time factor) keeps the soul in ignorance, so that pure consciousness is lost.
- **Application (preacher's):** Explain to the audience that this sleep does not mean ordinary rest. It means forgetting what life is for. The purport names the medicine for this forgetfulness: the holy name.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 5.14.20** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/14/20/
> "…sleep is exactly like a python."
- **Teaching:** Those who wander in the forest are always devoured by the python of sleep and remain in darkness, like dead bodies thrown in a distant forest.
- **Purport (Srila Prabhupada's words):** "Material life means being fully absorbed in eating, sleeping, mating and defending. Out of these, sleep is taken very seriously. While asleep, one completely forgets the object of life and what to do. For spiritual realization, one should try to avoid sleep as much as possible. The Gosvāmīs of Vṛndāvana practically did not sleep at all. Of course, they slept some, for the body requires sleep, but they slept only about two hours, and sometimes not even that. They always engaged in spiritual cultivation. … Following in the footsteps of the Gosvāmīs, we should try to reduce sleeping, eating, mating and defending."
- **Application (preacher's):** Teach the purport's measure: "try to avoid sleep as much as possible" and "try to reduce sleeping". A practical step is a fixed bedtime so that you attend mangala-arati (the early morning worship) awake.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 5.14.13** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/14/13/
> "…receives cheap blessings from atheists."
- **Teaching:** To lessen distress, the soul takes cheap blessings from atheists and loses intelligence in their company, like jumping in a shallow river and breaking one's head; he also approaches so-called sadhus and svamis who preach against the Vedas, and gains nothing now or later.
- **Purport (Srila Prabhupada's words):** "Cheaters are always there to manufacture their own way of spiritual realization. To get some material benefit, the conditioned soul approaches these pseudo sannyāsīs and yogīs for cheap blessings, but he does not receive any benefit from them, either spiritual or material. In this age there are many cheaters who show some jugglery and magic. They even create gold to amaze their followers, and their followers accept them as God." It quotes Visvanatha Cakravarti's description of the real guru (samsara-davanala-lidha-loka), then: "People want to be cheated, and therefore they go to yogīs and svāmīs who play tricks, but tricks do not mitigate the miseries of material life. If being able to manufacture gold is a criterion for becoming God, then why not accept Kṛṣṇa, the proprietor of the entire universe, wherein there are countless tons of gold? … one should not be allured by gold-manufacturing gurus but should sincerely approach a devotee like Jaḍa Bharata." A guru is to be accepted as advised in SB 11.3.21: "Such a guru does not manufacture gold or juggle words. He is well versed in the conclusions of Vedic knowledge … He is freed from all material contamination and is fully engaged in Kṛṣṇa's service. If one is able to obtain the dust of the lotus feet of such a guru, his life becomes successful. Otherwise he is baffled both in this life and in the next."
- **Application (preacher's):** Give the purport's test as it stands, and its direction: "sincerely approach a devotee like Jaḍa Bharata."
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 5.14.29** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/14/29/
> "This cakra is the wheel of time."
- **Teaching:** Krsna's disc, the hari-cakra, is the wheel of time, expanding from atoms to the death of Brahma and spending the lives of all beings from Brahma to a blade of grass. It cannot be checked. Fearing death, the soul takes shelter of man-made gods described in unauthorized scriptures, "like buzzards, vultures, herons and crows"; imminent death is like a lion's attack and these cannot save one from it.
- **Purport (Srila Prabhupada's words):** "It is stated: hariṁ vinā mṛtiṁ na taranti. No one can save himself from the cruel hands of death without being favored by Hari, the Supreme Personality of Godhead. In Bhagavad-gītā it is stated, mām eva ye prapadyante māyām etāṁ taranti te: whoever fully surrenders unto Kṛṣṇa can be saved from the cruel hands of material nature. The conditioned soul, however, sometimes wants to take shelter of a demigod, man-made god, pseudo incarnation or bogus svāmī or yogī. All these cheaters claim to follow religious principles, and all this has become very popular in this Age of Kali. There are many pāṣaṇḍīs who, without referring to the śāstras, pose themselves as incarnations, and foolish people follow them. Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, has left behind Him Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam and Bhagavad-gītā. Not referring to these authorized scriptures, rascals take shelter of man-made scriptures and try to compete with Lord Kṛṣṇa. That is the greatest difficulty one encounters when trying to promote spiritual consciousness in human society. … In any case, we cannot accept the unauthorized ways of so-called incarnations, gods, cheaters and bluffers, who are described here as crows, vultures, buzzards and herons."
- **Application (preacher's):** Time teaches us that the opportunity is short (compare bss-26 and bss-27). Read the purport as it stands; it names the kinds (demigod, man-made god, pseudo incarnation, bogus svami or yogi, pasandis) and the one shelter: Hari, His Bhagavatam and Bhagavad-gita.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 5.14.33** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/14/33/
> "…out of fear of the elephant of death, he falls into the darkness found in a mountain cave."
- **Teaching:** When the soul forgets his relationship with the Supreme Lord and has no Krsna consciousness, he engages in mischievous and sinful activities and suffers the threefold miseries; out of fear of the elephant of death he falls into the darkness of a mountain cave.
- **Purport point:** The purport says everyone fears death; however strong a materialist is, disease and old age bring death's notice, and the fear is compared to entering a dark mountain cave with death as a great elephant.
- **Use:** background; the last symbol of the table in F.1.

**SB 5.13.15** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/13/15/
> "…they fight one another and lay down their lives in battle."
- **Teaching:** Many political and social heroes have conquered enemies of equal power, but believing that the land is theirs, they fight one another and die; they cannot take up the renounced spiritual path.
- **Purport point:** The purport says big leaders can conquer powerful enemies but cannot subdue their strong senses, the enemies that always accompany them; not conquering those near enemies, they try to conquer others and die in the struggle for existence. It adds that because they spoil their lives with the illusion of "this is my land and my family", they cannot progress spiritually.
- **Application (preacher's):** For a brahmacari the first battle is with his own senses and mind (see bss-27). Winning a debate or gaining a higher position over others is not what these chapters measure.
- **Use:** in the talk or as extension.

**SB 5.14.23** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/14/23/
> "Learned scholars and transcendentalists therefore condemn the materialistic path of fruitive activity…"
- **Teaching:** The verse says that fruitive activity is the original source and breeding ground of miseries in this life and the next.
- **Purport point:** The purport says people do not know the value of life; the Vedas enjoin awakening to spiritual consciousness and using all activities to gain the Lord's favour; the Lord says in BG 9.27 that whatever one does, eats, offers, gives or performs in austerity should be done as an offering to Him. The results of activities are to be used for the mission of the Lord, not for sense gratification, and one who cultivates knowledge for many births finally surrenders to Him.
- **Application (preacher's):** This is the positive half of the table: activity is not the enemy; its aim is.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 5.14.41** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/14/41/
> "In this way he perpetually goes up and comes down."
- **Teaching:** One who takes shelter of the creeper of fruitive activity may be raised by piety to higher planets, but cannot remain there; after the results are used he returns to lower planets.
- **Purport point:** The purport quotes Lord Caitanya (brahmanda bhramite kona bhagyavan jiva, guru-krsna-prasade paya bhakti-lata-bija): wandering for millions of years one is not free unless one takes shelter at the lotus feet of a pure devotee. It compares the soul to a monkey on a banyan branch thinking he is enjoying. By Krsna's grace one finds the guru, learns devotional service, and gets a clue how to get out; so one must approach a spiritual master (it quotes Mundaka Upanisad 1.2.12, BG 4.34, SB 11.3.21 and Visvanatha Cakravarti's yasya prasadad bhagavat-prasadah).
- **Application (preacher's):** Even good results, such as comfort, reputation or a pleasant post, rise and fall like this if they are not used in service. So even a talented brahmacari can still be in the forest.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 5.14.39** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/14/39/
> "Saintly persons, who are friends to all living entities, have a peaceful consciousness."
- **Teaching:** Such persons have controlled senses and mind and easily attain the path of liberation; the unfortunate materialist, attached to miserable conditions, cannot associate with them.
- **Purport point:** The purport says Jada Bharata described both the miserable condition and the way out; the only way out is association with devotees, and this association is very easy. Though unfortunate people also get the opportunity, they cannot take shelter of pure devotees and suffer. The movement insists everyone chant the Hare Krsna maha-mantra, and preachers go from door to door; by Krsna's and guru's mercy one gets the seed of devotional service.
- **Application (preacher's):** Give the purport's own two sentences together: "this association is very easy", and "Although unfortunate people also get this opportunity, due to their great misfortune they cannot take shelter of pure devotees, and consequently they continuously suffer." Then its close: "If one is a little intelligent he can cultivate Kṛṣṇa consciousness and be freed from the miserable conditions of material life."
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 5.14.40** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/14/40/
> "…those great kings could not even conquer the false consciousness…"
- **Teaching:** Great saintly kings, expert in sacrifices and conquest, could not attain loving service to the Lord because they could not conquer the false identification with the body and property; they created enmity with rival kings and died without discharging life's mission.
- **Purport point:** The purport says the real mission is to reestablish the forgotten relationship with the Supreme Lord, and one need not give up one's occupation: in any position one can develop Krsna consciousness by associating with devotees who teach this science. It says material advancement ends in defeat because nothing belongs to the person, not even the body, and quotes SB 1.2.13 (the perfection of duty is to satisfy Hari).
- **Application (preacher's):** For those who will take up a career or a family later and for those who will not: the purport frees both from the thought that only one outer form is spiritual.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 5.14.42** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/14/42/
> "…ordinary kings are just like flies. Flies cannot follow the path of Garuḍa…"
- **Teaching:** Jada Bharata's path of devotional service is like Garuda's path; none of the great kings and victors could follow it even mentally.
- **Purport point:** The purport quotes BG 7.3 (among thousands, hardly one endeavours for perfection) and says the path is difficult even for conquerors, but very easy if the candidate wants to follow the path of the mahajana; in this age it is the path of Lord Caitanya, so simple that everyone can take to it by chanting the holy name (it quotes harer nama harer nama).
- **Application (preacher's):** The verse does not say that we are Garuda. It says the path belongs to the Lord's own carrier, and we follow it by walking behind those who already walk it, and by chanting the holy name.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 5.14.43** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/14/43/
> "Such was the greatness of His Majesty."
- **Teaching:** In the prime of life Maharaja Bharata gave up his wife, children, friends and a vast empire because he was fond of serving Uttamasloka, the Lord. (bss-26 uses this verse for detachment; here it serves the question: what if one falls?)
- **Purport point:** The purport says Krsna is so attractive that a pure devotee can give up everything for Him. Somehow the king became affectionate to a deer and took a deer's body; by Krsna's great mercy he did not forget and knew how he had fallen, so in the next life as Jada Bharata he was careful not to spoil his energy and presented himself as deaf and dumb. It says: learn from Bharata to be cautious; a little inattention retards service for a time, but service is never lost (BG 2.40), and quotes SB 1.5.17: even if one falls from immaturity or bad association, his devotional assets are never lost; examples are Ajamila and Maharaja Bharata.
- **Application (preacher's):** Give both halves of the purport as it gives them: learn caution from Bharata, and the service done is never lost (SB 1.5.17, BG 2.40, quoted there).
- **Use:** in the talk (closing).

## F. Philosophical explanation
**Principle.** The two chapters draw one picture: a soul who has come to the material world "for profit" wanders in a forest made by the external energy under Lord Visnu's control (SB 5.14.1, treated in bss-10); each hardship is a symbol the Bhagavatam later decodes, and each decoding points to the same cure.
**F.1 The key at a glance.** Every row is from the verse named; where only the translation was used, the verse is marked (tr.).

| Symbol | Meaning in the verses | Verse |
|---|---|---|
| Biting mosquitoes | Envious people met in earning a livelihood | 5.13.3 |
| Imaginary palace / house in the sky | The material world taken as permanent and real | 5.13.3, 5.14.5 |
| Fleeting fiend, meteor / yellow stool, will-o'-the-wisp | Gold and the pursuit of money | 5.13.3, 5.14.7 |
| Money passing between Devadatta and Visnumitra | Wealth is Krsna's; it cannot be kept | 5.14.24 |
| Plowman's field and returning seeds | Family-life desire that returns unless burned out by service | 5.14.4 |
| Owls, crickets, serpents | Harsh words and the tricks of enemies (tr.) | 5.14.11, 5.14.21 |
| Impure trees, creepers, poisonous wells | People with wealth who are "living dead" (tr.) | 5.14.12 |
| Shallow river | Cheap blessings from atheists and false svamis | 5.14.13 |
| Forest fire | The burning of household life (tr.) | 5.14.15 |
| The hill with thorns | The many ceremonies and duties of fruitive life (tr.) | 5.14.18 |
| Python | Sleep, the time factor that wipes out knowledge | 5.13.9, 5.14.20 |
| Buzzards, vultures, herons, crows | Man-made gods and unauthorised scriptures | 5.14.29 |
| The lion (death), the wheel of time | The disc of Hari, which cannot be checked | 5.14.29 |
| Mountain cave, elephant | Fear of death (tr. and purport) | 5.14.33 |
| Creeper of fruitive action | The path that lifts and drops the soul again | 5.14.41 |
| Garuda's path | Devotional service under the mahajanas | 5.14.42 |

Family members, the six senses, the merchant, the forest and the monkeys are in bss-10 and bss-21 to bss-23; they are not repeated.
**Why it matters.** Each symbol answers a question that a brahmacari will ask. "Why is it never enough?" is answered by gold and the palace. "Why does the desire come back?" is answered by the seeds. "Why am I drowsy and dull?" is answered by the python. "Whom can I trust?" is answered by the shallow river and the crows. "What about death?" is answered by the wheel of time and the elephant.
**Connections.** The seeds of SB 5.14.4 are the same attachment as the knot of the heart (SB 5.5.8, bss-07); sleep and time link to bss-26 and bss-27; false refuge links to the discussion of association (bss-07). The ending verses link to Bharata's own life (bss-05 treats his deer story as a fall).
**Consequences.** Without this key, austerities such as rising early and eating simple food can feel like losing the world's pleasures. With the key, they are a way to avoid the specific dangers the forest holds. If the key is understood only as a reason for fear, one becomes grim. The chapter itself ends with Bharata's service and the promise of SB 5.14.43.
**Common misunderstandings.**
1. "All work is the forest." The verse and purport condemn the fruitive aim (SB 5.14.23); the purport directs all work to be offered to the Lord (BG 9.27 quoted there), and SB 5.14.40 says one need not give up one's occupation.
2. "Sleep is sinful." The purport says the body requires sleep; the aim is to reduce, following the Gosvamis, while keeping life steady.
3. "Money is evil." The purport (5.14.24) says that money in the service of Radha and Krsna brings opulence automatically; the fault is in stealing Laksmi from Narayana.
4. "Every teacher who is not in our line is a cheat." The verses speak of those who preach against the Vedas, who show magic and make gold, and of man-made gods not mentioned in scripture. The purport of 5.14.29 names them in its own words: "demigod, man-made god, pseudo incarnation or bogus svāmī or yogī", "pāṣaṇḍīs who, without referring to the śāstras, pose themselves as incarnations". Read it as it stands.
**How to pitch it.** Speak to brahmacaris in a study tone and read the translations and purports as they stand. After each symbol, say at once what the exit is, as the chapters do.

3. Read SB 5.13.26's request aloud (in English), then ask nobody anything. Pause for three seconds.

## I. Core teaching points
**I.1 An allegory needs a key (SB 5.13.26, 5.13.3), about 12 minutes.** An allegory is a story in which each part stands for something else. King Pariksit asks for the direct meaning, and the purport tells a student to approach a bona fide spiritual master for it. Say this plainly: we do not guess what the forest means; we receive the meaning from the Bhagavatam and from devotees. Then give the first two symbols. The biting mosquitoes are the envious people we meet while earning a living. The fleeting fiend that shines like a meteor and disappears is gold. Ask the group where they meet small irritations that take their attention away from Krsna. End by saying that the meaning comes by hearing from the line of devotees. Next question: what is the main thing the soul runs after?
**I.2 Gold and the house in the sky (SB 5.14.5, 5.14.7, 5.14.24), about 18 minutes.** The soul takes the world to be permanent, like a house built in the sky. Gold attracts him as a false light attracts a cold traveller in the forest, who thinks it is a fire. The purport of 5.14.24 gives the cure: wealth belongs to Laksmi, and Laksmi stays with Narayana, so money used in the service of Radha and Krsna brings what we need, and money kept for sense enjoyment is lost. Example (preacher's, labelled): a donation box is opened and the money goes through many hands before it is spent. What matters is whom it finally serves. Ask the group: "What do I handle that really belongs to Krsna?" Next question: if the world is false, why does desire for it keep coming back?
**I.3 The returning seeds (SB 5.14.4), about 12 minutes.** A farmer pulls out the weeds every year, but seeds stay in the soil and new weeds grow. The smell of camphor stays in a pot after the camphor is gone. In the same way, desire remains after we stop acting on it. Read the purport in Srila Prabhupada's words: "Sometimes in our society, ISKCON, a person out of sentiment may take sannyāsa, but because his desires are not burned completely, he again takes to family life, even at the risk of losing his prestige and disgracing his good name. These strong desires can be burned out completely when one fully engages in the service of the Lord in devotional service." Application (preacher's): keep hands and mind fully engaged in the Lord's service, as the purport says. Next question: why do we feel dull and drowsy while the seeds are still there?
**I.4 The python of sleep (SB 5.13.9, 5.14.20), about 15 minutes.** The verse compares sleep to a python that swallows a person so that he lies like a dead body. The purport explains that sleep makes us forget what life is for. It names the medicine that Lord Caitanya brought to wake every sleeping soul: the holy name, the Hare Krsna maha-mantra. It also says that we should reduce sleep, eating, mating and defending by following the Gosvamis of Vrndavana, who slept about two hours a day. The purport itself says that the body needs some sleep. So the practical step is this: go to bed at a fixed, reasonable hour so that you can attend the morning programme awake, and chant attentively. Next question: where do we run for help when we are afraid?
**I.5 False shelters and time (SB 5.14.13, 5.14.29, 5.14.33), about 18 minutes.** The verses say that cheap blessings from atheists are like jumping into a shallow river: you only hurt your head. Man-made gods are like crows and vultures, which cannot save anyone from a lion, and death is that lion. The wheel of time is Krsna's own disc, and it cannot be stopped. The purport says that no one can cross death without the favour of Hari. Fear of death is compared to an elephant at the back of a dark cave. The purport of 5.14.29 names who the crows and vultures are: "a demigod, man-made god, pseudo incarnation or bogus svāmī or yogī. All these cheaters claim to follow religious principles"; "pāṣaṇḍīs who, without referring to the śāstras, pose themselves as incarnations, and foolish people follow them"; "rascals take shelter of man-made scriptures and try to compete with Lord Kṛṣṇa." The purport of 5.14.13 gives the test for a true guru from SB 11.3.21: "Such a guru does not manufacture gold or juggle words. He is well versed in the conclusions of Vedic knowledge … He is freed from all material contamination and is fully engaged in Kṛṣṇa's service." The answer to fear is the one the purport gives: "No one can save himself from the cruel hands of death without being favored by Hari." Next question: what then is the way out?
**I.6 The way out (SB 5.14.23, 5.14.41, 5.14.39, 5.14.42, 5.14.43), about 15 minutes.** The chapters give the way out in five steps. First, offer all work to Krsna, as in BG 9.27 quoted in the purport of 5.14.23. Second, take shelter of a pure devotee, because without it the soul keeps going up and down between higher and lower planets (SB 5.14.41). Third, keep company with saintly persons; the purport says this is very easy (SB 5.14.39). Fourth, follow the path of the mahajanas, the great devotees, by chanting the holy name; this is the simple path of Lord Caitanya (SB 5.14.42). Fifth, remember Bharata Maharaja: even a devotee who falls does not lose the service he has done (SB 5.14.43). Ask each person to choose one of these steps for this week.

## J. Stories and illustrations
**J.1 Jada Bharata and the merchant, decoded (SB 5.13.1-26 and 5.14).** Retell in short and quickly, since bss-10 told it: the merchant meets mosquitoes, a fiend, a palace, dust, a python, a well; Pariksit asks the meaning, and Sukadeva repeats Jada Bharata's key. Devotional lesson: the one who told the story, Jada Bharata, also lived the exit, and Rahugana changed by one moment of his association (SB 5.13.22, in bss-10).
**J.2 Bharata Maharaja's deer and his return (SB 5.14.43-45; story in SB 5.7-5.9).** Use only what SB 5.14.43's purport says: he gave up an empire for Uttamasloka; he fell through affection for a deer; Krsna's mercy preserved his memory; as Jada Bharata he guarded his energy. Devotional lesson: an offering to Krsna is never lost. Do not add details beyond the text.
**J.3 Invented illustration (labelled):** a student who starts each term with a clean timetable, but at 2 a.m. watches short videos "for just a minute" and then wakes up late. He does not think of himself as lost. This shows the python of sleep and the seed of desire. The answer is not to scold himself. He can build a habit of waking early that is supported by chanting the Lord's name, and he can ask a friend to rise with him.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections
1. **"Isn't this a pessimistic view of life?"** The chapters diagnose in order to cure; the end of SB 5.14 is Bharata's service and the promise that service is never lost (SB 5.14.43, SB 1.5.17).
2. **"Do I have to give up all money?"** The purport (SB 5.14.24) says keep Laksmi with Narayana: engage wealth in the Lord's service.
3. **"Is it a sin to sleep?"** No; the purport asks us to reduce sleep following the Gosvamis (who still slept some).
4. **"How do I know a real guru from a false one?"** The purport of 5.14.13 uses SB 11.3.21: well versed in the conclusions of scripture, able to convince others, free from contamination, engaged in Krsna's service. The standard is in the book; if unsure, ask a senior devotee.
5. **"What if I fall again?"** SB 5.14.43's purport: any service is a permanent asset; learn caution from Bharata and do not give up.
6. **"Why compare people to animals in these verses?"** Answer from the purport of 5.14.29: "we cannot accept the unauthorized ways of so-called incarnations, gods, cheaters and bluffers, who are described here as crows, vultures, buzzards and herons." The verse itself gives the reason: "Imminent death is like the attack of a lion, and neither vultures, buzzards, crows nor herons can save one from such an attack."

## L. Case studies
**L.1 Invented case for discussion.** A brahmacari is asked to manage a small fund for a festival. A friend suggests that he keep a small part of it "for convenience". The dilemma is whether such a small amount matters. The principles are in SB 5.14.24 and 5.14.7: money belongs to Krsna and cannot really be kept for oneself. Questions for the group: What does SB 5.14.24 say happens to money taken by cheating? How can he speak openly to his leader about the offer? The takeaway: handle everything as Krsna's property, and report any doubt to your leader.
**L.2 Invented case for discussion (extension).** A young man gets a very good result in his studies and feels he can now relax his sadhana (daily spiritual practice). The principles are in SB 5.14.41 (results of fruitive action rise and fall) and SB 5.14.40 (outer victories do not remove the false identification with the body). Questions for the group: What do these verses say about good worldly results? How could he turn this result into an offering to Krsna? The takeaway: use every result in Krsna's service, and keep up the daily practice.

## M. Discussion and reflection
**M.1 Closing check questions (with expected answers).**
1. *Who explains the allegory, and what does the purport say a student should do?* Sukadeva at Pariksit's request; approach a bona fide spiritual master (SB 5.13.26).
2. *What are the "mosquitoes" and the "fleeting fiend"?* Envious people met in earning; gold (SB 5.13.3).
3. *What does the python stand for?* Sleep (SB 5.14.20).
4. *Why does the desire for enjoyment come back after being cut?* The seeds remain until desire is fully engaged in the Lord's service (SB 5.14.4).
5. *What cannot save one from death?* Man-made gods; only Hari's favour (SB 5.14.29).
6. *Does a devotee who falls lose his service?* No: devotional assets are never lost (SB 5.14.43, SB 1.5.17).
**M.2 Activities.**
a) *Decode the forest (15 min).* In groups of three, each gets the F.1 table with the Meaning column covered. They guess a meaning, then check against the verse and say how it appears today (e.g., "house in the sky" = a future plan). Each group shares one answer.
b) *Python log (10 min, private).* On a card, write the times of day when "sleep" takes my time. Mark one to change this week. No one reads their card.
c) *Short drama (15 min, extension).* Roles: Pariksit, Sukadeva, narrator. Script: Pariksit asks for the plain meaning (SB 5.13.26); Sukadeva replies with three symbols from the table (labelled dramatisation, no new claims).
**M.3 Reflection.** "Which symbol from the key do I meet most often, and where is my exit?" Private.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary
Jada Bharata told King Rahugana a story of a merchant lost in a forest. King Pariksit asked Sukadeva Gosvami for its plain meaning, because it is hard to extract from an allegory (SB 5.13.26). The purport says a student should approach a bona fide spiritual master to learn the direct meaning.
The key says the forest is the material world, and its troubles are signs. Mosquitoes are envious people met while earning (SB 5.13.3). The "house in the sky" is the world taken as permanent (SB 5.14.5). Gold is a fleeting fiend that leads on and vanishes (SB 5.14.7); money passes from one cheat to another and in the end belongs to the Lord (SB 5.14.24), so wealth is to be kept with Narayana, in His service. Desire is like seeds in a field that sprout again unless burned out by full engagement in service (SB 5.14.4); the purport says that one who takes sannyasa out of sentiment, with desires not burned completely, "again takes to family life, even at the risk of losing his prestige and disgracing his good name." Sleep is a python which makes us lose knowledge of life's aim (SB 5.13.9, 5.14.20); the purport says, "For spiritual realization, one should try to avoid sleep as much as possible." Cheap blessings and man-made gods do not help: the purport of 5.14.29 calls them "demigod, man-made god, pseudo incarnation or bogus svāmī or yogī … cheaters", and says, "No one can save himself from the cruel hands of death without being favored by Hari" (SB 5.14.13, 5.14.29, 5.14.33).
The way out is in the same chapters: do everything as an offering to the Lord (SB 5.14.23), take shelter of a pure devotee (SB 5.14.41), associate with saintly persons (SB 5.14.39), and follow the simple path of the holy name (SB 5.14.42). Bharata Maharaja shows that even one who falls does not lose what he has given to Krsna (SB 5.14.43).

## S2. Key ideas
- The forest's hardships are symbols; the Bhagavatam gives their meaning.
- Gold, sleep and the fear of death keep the soul busy and unaware of the Lord.
- Desire returns like seeds until the heart is filled with service.
- The exit is a devotee's shelter, the holy name, and work offered to Krsna.
- Service done for Krsna is never lost.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **SB 5.14.4**: Unless desire is burned out, it grows again and again; the purport: "These strong desires can be burned out completely when one fully engages in the service of the Lord in devotional service." *For me:* fill the day with service.
- **SB 5.14.20**: Sleep is like a python. *For me:* wake up for the name.
- **SB 5.14.43**: Bharata gave up everything for Uttamasloka (and service is never lost, with SB 1.5.17). *For me:* no offering is wasted.

## S4. Words to know
- **Allegory:** a story whose parts stand for something else.
- **Brahmacari:** a celibate student who lives simply and serves his guru.
- **Laksmi / Narayana:** the goddess of fortune and the Lord she serves.
- **Guru:** a spiritual teacher who knows the conclusions of scripture and serves Krsna.
- **Karma, fruitive activity:** work done for its results.
- **Maha-mantra:** the Hare Krsna chant.
- **Mahajana:** a great devotee whose path is followed.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *Why are there symbols instead of a direct teaching?* The Bhagavatam uses allegory; one learns the meaning from a guru (SB 5.13.26 purport).
2. *Does this mean money is bad?* No: money is Krsna's and is kept well in His service (SB 5.14.24 purport).
3. *Do I have to sleep less?* The purport asks us to follow the Gosvamis in reducing sleep, but the body needs some (SB 5.14.20 purport).
4. *Why does desire come back?* Seeds remain unless desire is burned out by service (SB 5.14.4).
5. *If I fall, is everything lost?* No: devotional assets are never lost (SB 5.14.43 purport).

## S6. Check your understanding
1. What do the mosquitoes of SB 5.13.3 stand for?
2. What is the "python" in SB 5.14.20?
3. Whose property does money finally remain, according to SB 5.14.24?
4. What does SB 5.14.4 say about desire for family life that is not burned out?
5. Name two things the verses give as the way out.

**Answers:** 1. Envious people (SB 5.13.3). 2. Sleep (SB 5.14.20). 3. The Supreme Lord's (SB 5.14.24). 4. It grows up again and again (SB 5.14.4). 5. For example: the shelter of a pure devotee (SB 5.14.41) and association with saintly persons (SB 5.14.39); or all work offered to Krsna (SB 5.14.23).
