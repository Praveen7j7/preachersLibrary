# Deity worship as a relationship, not a duty

> **TRD · up to 180 min · review: drafted (30 Oct 2026).** For pujaris, and for every resident who dresses, cooks for,
> cleans for or simply greets the Deity daily. The philosophy of Deity worship (why the Deity is not an idol) is SPS-12;
> the sixty-four limbs are DPS-27…29; *Hari-bhakti-vilasa* and the rules are TRD-34. This page asks: after years before
> the altar, is the Deity still a person to me?

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on SPS-12 (the Deity is not an idol: arca-avatara) and DPS-27 (Deity worship among the sixty-four items): this page does not re-argue that the Deity is the Lord; it asks what follows for the one who has believed it for years.

A newcomer must be convinced that the Deity is Krishna. A resident is convinced, and that is his danger: the altar becomes his workplace. He knows the sequence, the timings, the dress for each day, and can do all of it while thinking of the afternoon's programme. Srila Prabhupada's books speak about this worshipper directly, and Madhavendra Puri shows the other kind.

## C. Central message

Krishna says: "whatever is offered to Me with faith by My devotee — even if only a little water — is most dear to Me" (SB 11.27.16–17), and "even very opulent presentations do not satisfy Me if they are offered by nondevotees" (11.27.18). The Deity is a person who accepts love, not a schedule that accepts items. He sees the worshipper's heart and his treatment of others (SB 3.29.21–24), He answers (SB 3.25.35 purport), and He tests and rewards love, as He did with Madhavendra Puri (CC Madhya 4).

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Quote what Krishna Himself says He wants from the worshipper (SB 11.27.15–18; BG 9.26) and what He rejects.
2. State the two conditions in SB 3.29.21–25 under which temple worship becomes "imitation" and "oblations into ashes".
3. Retell the Madhavendra Puri narrative from CC Madhya 4 as the text gives it and draw its lesson on reciprocation.
4. Explain, from Prabhupada's purports (SB 3.25.35; CC Madhya 20.217; BG 12.5), why the Deity is visible to material eyes and what that mercy asks of us.
5. Choose one change that makes their own Deity service personal again, and know its limits (K.4: no imitation of Madhavendra Puri's visions).

## E. Scriptural foundation

**SB 11.27.9, 11.27.12, 11.27.15, 11.27.16–17, 11.27.18**: Verified · *BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples* · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/27/18/
> 11.27.16–17: "Actually, whatever is offered to Me with faith by My devotee — even if only a little water — is most dear to Me."
> 11.27.18: "Even very opulent presentations do not satisfy Me if they are offered by nondevotees. But I am pleased by any insignificant offering made by My loving devotees, and I am certainly most pleased when nice presentations of fragrant oil, incense, flowers and palatable foods are offered with love."
- **Context:** Krishna answers Uddhava's questions on Deity worship. One should worship "without duplicity" (11.27.9). The Deity "is said to appear in eight varieties — stone, wood, metal, earth, paint, sand, the mind or jewels" (11.27.12). One should offer "the most excellent paraphernalia", but a devotee free of material desire "may worship Me with whatever he is able to obtain" (11.27.15).
- **Purport point (11.27.15):** "the neophyte must offer opulent paraphernalia to the Deity so that he may constantly remember that the Deity is the supreme enjoyer and that he, the neophyte, is simply the worshiper... The pure devotee offers his unalloyed love to the Personality of Godhead along with whatever paraphernalia is easily obtained."
- **Purport point (11.27.18):** "all offenses against the Deity are based upon irreverence and disregard for the Lord's position as master, and thus upon disobedience to His orders. Since one must worship the Deity with reverence, one should offer opulent presentations to the Deity with love, for such presentations both enhance the respectfulness of the worshiper and help him avoid" offence.
- **Use:** in the talk; the central block. Krishna's own words on what He wants.

**BG 9.26; BG 9.34; BG 9.11; BG 12.5**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/9/26/
> 9.26: "If one offers Me with love and devotion a leaf, a flower, a fruit or water, I will accept it."
- **Purport point (9.26):** "Kṛṣṇa wants only loving service and nothing more. Kṛṣṇa accepts even a little flower from His pure devotee. He does not want any kind of offering from a nondevotee. He is not in need of anything from anyone."
- **Purport point (12.5):** "the bhakti-yogī accepts the Deity of Kṛṣṇa as worshipable because there is some bodily conception fixed in the mind, which can thus be applied... worship of the Supreme Personality of Godhead in His form within the temple is not idol worship." The mailbox example: "God has an authorized representation in the Deity form, which is called arcā-vigraha. This arcā-vigraha is an incarnation of the Supreme Lord. God will accept service through that form."
- **Teaching (9.34, 9.11):** "worship Me" is Krishna's own instruction; "fools deride Me when I descend in the human form" warns against treating any form of the Lord as ordinary.
- **Use:** in the talk (9.26, 12.5); background (9.34, 9.11; the idol argument is SPS-12).

**SB 3.29.21–22, 3.29.24–25**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/29/21/
> 3.29.21: "I am present in every living entity as the Supersoul. If someone neglects or disregards that Supersoul everywhere and engages himself in the worship of the Deity in the temple, that is simply imitation."
> 3.29.24: "My dear mother, even if he worships with proper rituals and paraphernalia, a person who is ignorant of My presence in all living entities never pleases Me by the worship of My Deities in the temple."
- **Context:** Lord Kapila to Devahuti. 3.29.22: such worship "is compared to one who offers oblations into ashes". 3.29.25: worship the Deity "until one realizes My presence in his own heart and in the hearts of other living entities as well."
- **Purport point (3.29.21):** "One who worships the Deity in the temple and does not show respect to others is a devotee on the material platform, in the lowest stage of devotional service."
- **Purport point (3.29.24):** "The real purpose is to exhibit one's loving devotion to the Lord; the offerings themselves are secondary... We cannot bribe the Personality of Godhead... As such, temple worship necessarily includes distribution of prasāda."
- **Application (preacher's):** the pujari's relationship with the Deity is tested in the corridor, with the devotee who arrived late or the guest who did not know the rules.
- **Use:** in the talk; the hard half of the message, given as it is.

**SB 3.25.35; SB 4.30.27; SB 6.3.25; SB 3.9.11; CC Madhya 20.217**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/25/35/
> 3.25.35: "O My mother, My devotees always see the smiling face of My form, with eyes like the rising morning sun. They like to see My various transcendental forms, which are all benevolent, and they also talk favorably with Me."
- **Purport point (3.25.35):** "Arcā refers to the form which we can worship in our present condition... In order to be seen by our material senses, the Supreme Personality of Godhead accepts a favorable form, which is called arcā-vigraha... These forms are not material." "The devotees express their minds before the Deity, and in many instances the Deity also gives answers. But one must be a very elevated devotee in order to be able to speak with the Supreme Lord. Sometimes the Lord informs the devotee through dreams. These exchanges of feelings between the Deity and the devotee are not understandable by atheists, but actually the devotee enjoys them."
- **Purport point (4.30.27):** the arca-vigraha "facility is offered to neophyte devotees so that they can see the real form of the Lord face to face".
- **Purport point (6.3.25):** "Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura therefore recommended that one follow both processes simultaneously. We strictly follow his principle of performing Deity worship and saṅkīrtana along parallel lines."
- **Purport point (CC Madhya 20.217):** "Although the arcā-mūrti... appears to be made of material elements, it is as good as the spiritual forms found in the spiritual Vaikuṇṭhalokas. The Deity in the temple, however, is visible to the material eyes of the devotee... To bestow causeless mercy upon us, the Lord appears as the arcā-mūrti so that we can see Him."
- **Teaching (3.9.11):** devotees "see You through the ears by the process of bona fide hearing", and the Lord manifests "the particular eternal form of transcendence in which they always think of You".
- **Use:** in the talk (3.25.35, 20.217); background (4.30.27, 6.3.25, 3.9.11).

**CC Madhya 4.39–40, 4.124, 4.127, 4.133, 4.160, 4.173, 4.186, 4.190**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/4/40/
> 4.39–40 (Gopala, in a dream): "For many days I have been observing you, and I have been wondering, 'When will Mādhavendra Purī come here to serve Me?' I have accepted your service due to your ecstatic love for Me."
> 4.186 (Lord Caitanya): "This is the natural result of intense love of Godhead. The devotee does not consider personal inconveniences or impediments. In all circumstances he wants to serve the Supreme Personality of Godhead."
- **Teaching:** The Deity watches, waits, calls, steals for His devotee, feels the heat of one body in another, and tests love (4.189: "To test the intense love of Mādhavendra Purī, Gopāla... ordered him to bring sandalwood"). 4.190: "Such behavior exhibited in loving service between the devotee and the devotee's lovable object, Śrī Kṛṣṇa, is transcendental. It is not possible for a common man to understand."
- **Use:** in the talk (story in J).

**CC Madhya 15.106 purport**: Verified. "When the novice engages in devotional service — especially in Deity worship — and follows the order of a bona fide spiritual master, he is a pure devotee." *Use:* one line, for the resident who thinks Deity service is "only kanistha".

**NOD ch. 9, "Further Consideration of Devotional Principles"**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/nod/9/. "Arcanā means worship of the Deity in the temple. By executing this process one confirms himself to be not the body but spirit soul." The chapter lists the limbs around the Deity: dancing before the Deity, bowing down, standing up to receive the Lord, following the Deity, circumambulating, singing, offering prayers, partaking of prasada, smelling the incense and flowers offered. *Use:* in the talk, as the picture of a relationship lived with the whole body (DPS-27…29 give the full lists).

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The Deity is the Lord's mercy on our eyes.** We cannot see spirit; so the Lord "accepts a favorable form" (SB 3.25.35 purport), "visible to the material eyes of the devotee" (CC Madhya 20.217 purport), "so that they can see the real form of the Lord face to face" (SB 4.30.27 purport). A gift of this kind asks for a response, not an attendance record.
2. **What He wants is stated by Himself.** SB 11.27.15–18 and BG 9.26 agree: faith and love make a little water "most dear"; their absence makes opulence useless. The purport to 11.27.15 explains why opulence is still prescribed for us: so that the worshipper "may constantly remember that the Deity is the supreme enjoyer" and he "simply the worshiper". Opulence is for our remembrance; love is for His pleasure.
3. **What makes worship useless.** Lord Kapila names it twice: neglecting the Lord in other living beings makes temple worship "simply imitation" and "oblations into ashes" (SB 3.29.21–22, 3.29.24). Prabhupada's purport places such a worshipper "in the lowest stage of devotional service". The resident's relationship with the Deity is therefore inseparable from his dealings with the devotee beside him and the guest at the door.
4. **He reciprocates.** The purport to SB 3.25.35 says it plainly: devotees "express their minds before the Deity, and in many instances the Deity also gives answers"; "sometimes the Lord informs the devotee through dreams." CC Madhya 4 shows it: Gopala waits for Madhavendra Puri, Gopinatha steals sweet rice for him, Gopala says "there is no difference between My body and Gopīnātha's body" (4.160). Lord Caitanya adds that this is "not possible for a common man to understand" (4.190): we hear it with faith, we do not demand it.
5. **Two things at once.** Deity worship and sankirtana go "along parallel lines" (SB 6.3.25 purport). Chanting feeds the worship; the worship gives chanting a face.
6. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *"Deity worship is for kanisthas; residents should preach."* CC Madhya 15.106 purport: the novice who worships the Deity under the guru's order "is a pure devotee". Prabhupada built temples and installed Deities everywhere he preached.
   - *"If the Deity is the Lord, my mood does not matter; the items do."* SB 11.27.18 and 3.29.24 say the opposite.
   - *"A personal relationship means I may improvise."* SB 11.27.18 purport: offences "are all based upon irreverence and disregard for the Lord's position as master". Love shows as obedience to the standards (TRD-34), not freedom from them.
   - *"Madhavendra Puri had visions; so should I."* CC Madhya 4.190. The paramahamsa's exchanges are to be heard, never imitated or expected (K.4).
7. **Pitch for TRD.** Residents have heard "the Deity is not an idol" a hundred times. Do not say it again. Read CC Madhya 4 slowly and let Gopala's own words do the work.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet everyone. Say: "This session is about the person on the altar. Nobody will be asked to describe his own worship aloud."
2. Read SB 11.27.18 once, slowly.
3. Ask H.2 question 1 for private writing (two minutes).
4. Ask H.2 question 2; take three answers; thank each by name.
5. Say: "Krishna told Uddhava exactly what pleases Him on the altar. We will begin with His words, and end with a devotee whom He served."

**H.2 Opening questions**
1. (Private) At this morning's arati, what was the Deity doing, in your mind? What were you doing?
2. If the Deity could only receive one thing from you today, an item or a mood, which would you give?
3. Why do you think Srila Prabhupada insisted on opulent Deity worship when Krishna says a little water is enough?

## I. Core teaching points

1. **He made Himself visible for us.** SB 3.25.35 and CC Madhya 20.217 purports. *Example:* "face to face" (SB 4.30.27 purport). *Transition:* what does a person who has done this want?
2. **He wants love; items carry it.** SB 11.27.15–18; BG 9.26. *Example:* a little water with faith is "most dear"; opulence without devotion does not satisfy. Opulence is prescribed for our remembrance (11.27.15 purport). *Transition:* can love be present at the altar and absent at the door?
3. **Worship without respect for others is imitation.** SB 3.29.21–24 and purports. *Example:* "oblations into ashes"; the lowest stage. Prasada distribution belongs to worship (3.29.24 purport). *Transition:* and when love is real?
4. **He answers.** SB 3.25.35 purport; CC Madhya 4: Gopala waiting, Gopinatha stealing, the sandalwood. *Example:* "When will Mādhavendra Purī come here to serve Me?" (4.39). *Transition:* heard with faith, not demanded (4.190).
5. **The relationship is lived with the whole body.** NOD ch. 9: dancing, bowing, following, singing, prasada. Deity worship and sankirtana in parallel (SB 6.3.25 purport). *Transition:* P.

## J. Stories and illustrations

**Madhavendra Puri and Gopala** (CC Madhya 4.21–50; stories list "madhavendra-gopala"). At Govardhana, "almost mad in his ecstasy", Madhavendra Puri sits under a tree. A cowherd boy brings milk and says, "Why don't you beg some food to eat?" (4.25); the women at the well saw him and sent the milk (4.30); the boy leaves to milk the cows and does not return (4.31–33). That night the same boy appears in a dream, takes him to a bush and says: "I reside in this bush, and because of this I suffer very much from severe cold, rain showers, winds and scorching heat" (4.36); "For many days I have been observing you, and I have been wondering, 'When will Mādhavendra Purī come here to serve Me?' I have accepted your service due to your ecstatic love for Me" (4.39–40); "My name is Gopāla. I am the lifter of Govardhana Hill. I was installed by Vajra" (4.41); hidden in the bush by His priest when the Muslims attacked (4.42). Madhavendra wakes and laments: "I saw Lord Kṛṣṇa directly, but I could not recognize Him!" (4.45), then assembles the village and they cut a path to the Deity (4.47–50). *Lesson:* the Deity is a person who suffers neglect, watches for His servant and accepts service "due to your ecstatic love".

**Ksira-cora Gopinatha** (CC Madhya 4.112–146). At Remuna, Madhavendra Puri asks what food is offered to Gopinatha, hearing of the sweet rice called amrta-keli (4.117), and thinks: "If, without my asking, a little sweet rice is given to me, I can then taste it and make a similar preparation to offer my Lord Gopāla" (4.120). He "became greatly ashamed when he desired to taste the sweet rice" and left (4.121–122); the text says he "considered that he had committed an offense by desiring to eat what was being offered to the Deity" (4.124). That night Gopinatha tells the sleeping priest: "I have kept one pot of sweet rice for the sannyāsī Mādhavendra Purī. This pot of sweet rice is just behind My cloth curtain. You did not see it because of My tricks" (4.127–128). The priest searches the marketplace calling, "Gopīnātha has stolen this pot of sweet rice for you!" (4.133). Madhavendra eats it in ecstasy, breaks the pot, keeps the pieces and eats one each day (4.138–140), and leaves before dawn fearing the crowds (4.141–142). *Lesson:* the Deity notices a hidden desire of His devotee and fulfils it Himself, and the devotee's response to being favoured is to flee from fame.

**The sandalwood** (CC Madhya 4.108–110, 4.148–169). Gopala orders sandalwood in a dream; Madhavendra Puri walks to Puri, collects it, carries "one maund [about eighty-two pounds] of sandalwood and twenty tolās of camphor" (4.182) back through Muslim provinces with toll papers, and at Remuna Gopala appears again: "There is no difference between My body and Gopīnātha's body... if you smear the sandalwood pulp on the body of Gopīnātha, you will naturally also smear it on My body" (4.160). Lord Caitanya, telling this, says Gopala ordered it "to test the intense love of Mādhavendra Purī", and "when Mādhavendra Purī passed this examination, the Lord became very merciful to him" (4.189). *Lesson:* the Deity tests love with trouble, and accepts the trouble as the offering.

**Lord Caitanya's judgment** (CC Madhya 4.170–190). The Lord asks Nityananda Prabhu to judge "whether there was anyone within the world as fortunate as Mādhavendra Purī" (4.171); "Kṛṣṇa Himself appeared as the Gopāla Deity, and, accepting his service, He liberated the whole world" (4.173). *Lesson:* this is the standard the Lord Himself praises; we hear it with faith and follow the mood, never the visions (4.190).

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"I am a pujari. My worship is correct, but I feel nothing. Is that a problem for the Deity?"**
   - Krishna says He is pleased by offerings "with love" and not by opulence from the loveless (SB 11.27.18). Correctness is the reverence He asks for (11.27.18 purport); love is what He wants. Feel nothing today and still offer; and treat the dryness by TRD-09's remedy: hearing about Him. Taste for the Deity grows from hearing about the person.
2. **"Is strict standard worship compatible with a personal relationship?"**
   - The purport to SB 11.27.18 says offences come from "irreverence and disregard for the Lord's position as master". A servant who loves his master keeps his master's rules more carefully, not less (TRD-34).
3. **"The Deity is on the altar; the devotees are my colleagues. Why mix them?"**
   - Lord Kapila mixes them: worship without regard for the Lord in other beings is "simply imitation" and "oblations into ashes" (SB 3.29.21–22). The purport puts such a worshipper "in the lowest stage of devotional service".
4. **"Should I expect dreams and answers like Madhavendra Puri?"**
   - The SB 3.25.35 purport says the Deity "in many instances" answers, "but one must be a very elevated devotee". Lord Caitanya says of Madhavendra Puri's exchanges, "It is not possible for a common man to understand" (CC Madhya 4.190). Hear with faith; do not expect, imitate or report visions. A resident who believes he receives orders from the Deity tells his authority and follows the authority.
5. **"Prabhupada said a little water is enough. Why so much opulence?"**
   - Because we are not yet "completely freed from material desire" (SB 11.27.15); opulence trains the worshipper to remember who is the enjoyer (purport). And the Lord is "most pleased" by nice presentations "offered with love" (11.27.18).
6. **"Is Deity worship not for beginners while preachers do higher work?"**
   - CC Madhya 15.106 purport: the novice engaged "especially in Deity worship" under the guru's order "is a pure devotee"; SB 6.3.25 purport: Deity worship and sankirtana go "along parallel lines". Prabhupada installed Deities wherever he preached.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The exact pujari.** Vamana dasa has done the morning dressing for five years without a mistake. A new devotee came late to help and was told, sharply, not to come again. The Deities were dressed perfectly that morning.
- **Dilemma:** Was the morning's worship complete? What did Lord Kapila say about it?
- **Principles:** SB 3.29.21–24 and purports; SB 11.27.18 (reverence with love).
- **Questions:** What did Vamana offer, and what did he withhold? How does he keep the standard and keep the newcomer?
- **Takeaway:** the standard stays; the newcomer is trained, not dismissed. The Deity received the dress and watched the door.

**Case 2: The resident who wants a sign.** Nrsimha dasa has begun telling others that the Deity "told him" in a dream to change his service and leave for another temple. He cites Madhavendra Puri.
- **Principles:** SB 3.25.35 purport ("one must be a very elevated devotee"); CC Madhya 4.190; SB 11.27.18 purport (obedience to the master's orders, which come through authorities).
- **Questions:** What is the difference between hearing Madhavendra Puri's story with faith and claiming his position? Who decides his service?
- **Takeaway:** he tells his authority privately and follows the authority's decision; dreams are not orders for a sadhaka.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first; thank every answer)
1. What does Krishna say about a little water offered with faith? *Expected:* it is "most dear to Me" (SB 11.27.16–17).
2. What does He say about opulent offerings from nondevotees? *Expected:* they do not satisfy Him (SB 11.27.18).
3. What does Lord Kapila call temple worship by one who neglects the Lord in other beings? *Expected:* imitation; oblations into ashes (SB 3.29.21–22).
4. Why does the Lord appear as the arca-vigraha? *Expected:* so that we can see Him with material eyes; causeless mercy (CC Madhya 20.217 purport).
5. What did Gopala say He had been wondering? *Expected:* "When will Mādhavendra Purī come here to serve Me?" (CC Madhya 4.39).
6. Why did Gopala order the sandalwood? *Expected:* to test Madhavendra Puri's love (CC Madhya 4.189).

**M.2 Activities**
- **Option 1: One service, read as a relationship (20 min).** Pairs take one daily Deity service they do (cooking, dressing, cleaning the altar room, arati, offering) and write two lines: "What does He receive here?" and "Where can love be shown that items cannot show?" Pairs read out; the preacher connects each to SB 11.27.15–18.
- **Option 2: CC Madhya 4 read aloud (25 min).** Four readers take 4.21–50, 4.112–146, 4.148–169 and 4.170–190; the room hears without notes. Then each person writes one sentence: "What did the Deity do here that I had not noticed before?"

**M.3 Reflection**
- Why does Krishna want love rather than items, when He owns everything? What does that tell me about who He is?
- (Private) Which person in the temple do I treat in a way that would make my worship "oblations into ashes"?

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

After years before the altar, the arati bell can become a timetable. This session asks whether the Deity is still a person to us, and answers from Krishna's own words. The Lord appears as the arca-vigraha, the worshippable Deity form, because "in our present state it is not possible to see God in His spiritual form"; "to be seen by our material senses, the Supreme Personality of Godhead accepts a favorable form" (SB 3.25.35 purport). "To bestow causeless mercy upon us, the Lord appears as the arcā-mūrti so that we can see Him" (CC Madhya 20.217 purport).

What does He want from us there? He told Uddhava: "whatever is offered to Me with faith by My devotee — even if only a little water — is most dear to Me" (SB 11.27.16–17); "even very opulent presentations do not satisfy Me if they are offered by nondevotees. But I am pleased by any insignificant offering made by My loving devotees, and I am certainly most pleased when nice presentations... are offered with love" (SB 11.27.18). The purport explains why we still offer opulently: so that the worshipper remembers "that the Deity is the supreme enjoyer and that he... is simply the worshiper". Lord Kapila adds the hard condition: one who worships the Deity in the temple but "neglects or disregards" the Lord in other living beings is doing "simply imitation" (SB 3.29.21), "compared to one who offers oblations into ashes" (3.29.22); such a worshipper "never pleases Me" (3.29.24). Our relationship with the Deity is tested at the door, with the devotee beside us.

And the Deity reciprocates. "The devotees express their minds before the Deity, and in many instances the Deity also gives answers... Sometimes the Lord informs the devotee through dreams" (SB 3.25.35 purport). Caitanya-caritamrta Madhya 4 tells how Gopala, hidden in a bush at Govardhana, came to Madhavendra Puri with milk, then said in a dream: "For many days I have been observing you, and I have been wondering, 'When will Mādhavendra Purī come here to serve Me?'" (4.39); how Gopinatha at Remuna stole a pot of sweet rice for him (4.127–133); and how Gopala sent him for sandalwood "to test the intense love of Mādhavendra Purī" (4.189). Lord Caitanya says such exchanges are "not possible for a common man to understand" (4.190): we hear them with faith and follow the mood, never expecting or imitating the visions.

## S2. Key ideas

- The Deity is the Lord made visible to our material eyes, out of mercy (SB 3.25.35 and CC Madhya 20.217 purports).
- He wants faith and love; items carry them (SB 11.27.15–18; BG 9.26). Opulence is for our remembrance of who enjoys.
- Worship while disregarding the Lord in other beings is "imitation" and "oblations into ashes" (SB 3.29.21–22).
- The Deity accepts, answers, steals for and tests His devotee (SB 3.25.35 purport; CC Madhya 4).
- Deity worship and sankirtana run "along parallel lines" (SB 6.3.25 purport).
- Love shows as careful obedience to the standards, not freedom from them (SB 11.27.18 purport).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **SB 11.27.16–17:** "whatever is offered to Me with faith by My devotee — even if only a little water — is most dear to Me." *For me:* what He receives from me is my faith and love.
- **SB 3.29.21:** "If someone neglects or disregards that Supersoul everywhere and engages himself in the worship of the Deity in the temple, that is simply imitation." *For me:* my worship is tested in how I treat devotees.
- **CC Madhya 4.39:** "For many days I have been observing you, and I have been wondering, 'When will Mādhavendra Purī come here to serve Me?'" *For me:* He is watching and waiting for my service.
- **BG 9.26:** "If one offers Me with love and devotion a leaf, a flower, a fruit or water, I will accept it." *For me:* nothing I have is too small if love is in it.

## S4. Words to know

- **Arca-vigraha / arca-avatara / arca-murti:** the Lord's worshippable Deity form, His incarnation accepted so that we can see and serve Him.
- **Arcana:** worship of the Deity in the temple (NOD ch. 9).
- **Ksira-cora:** "the thief who stole the sweet rice", Gopinatha's name after CC Madhya 4.
- **Paramahamsa:** a fully liberated devotee, such as Madhavendra Puri (CC Madhya 4.124).

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **My worship is correct but I feel nothing. Does it matter to the Deity?** He is pleased by offerings made "with love" (SB 11.27.18). Keep the standard, offer anyway, and treat the dryness by hearing about Him (TRD-09); love for the person grows from hearing about the person.
2. **Why so much opulence if a little water is enough?** Because we are not yet free of material desire; opulence reminds the worshipper that the Deity is the enjoyer and he the servant (SB 11.27.15 purport). And the Lord is "most pleased" by nice offerings made with love (11.27.18).
3. **Should I expect the Deity to speak to me as He did to Madhavendra Puri?** No. "One must be a very elevated devotee" (SB 3.25.35 purport); such exchanges are "not possible for a common man to understand" (CC Madhya 4.190). Hear with faith; follow your authority.
4. **Why does how I treat devotees affect my worship?** Lord Kapila says worship with disregard for the Lord in others is imitation and oblations into ashes (SB 3.29.21–22); the purport places such a worshipper in the lowest stage.
5. **Is Deity worship only for beginners?** No. The novice who worships the Deity under the guru's order "is a pure devotee" (CC Madhya 15.106 purport); worship and sankirtana go together (SB 6.3.25 purport).

## S6. Check your understanding

1. According to SB 11.27.18, what kind of offering does not satisfy Krishna?
2. What did Lord Kapila compare temple worship by one who neglects the Lord in other beings to?
3. Why does the Lord appear as the arca-murti (CC Madhya 20.217 purport)?
4. What did Gopala say He suffered in the bush (CC Madhya 4.36)?
5. Why did Gopala order Madhavendra Puri to fetch sandalwood (CC Madhya 4.189)?
6. True or false: a devotee should expect dreams and answers from the Deity as proof of his love.

**Answers:** 1. "Very opulent presentations... offered by nondevotees" (SB 11.27.18). 2. Offering oblations into ashes (SB 3.29.22). 3. To bestow causeless mercy, so that we can see Him with material eyes. 4. "Severe cold, rain showers, winds and scorching heat" (CC Madhya 4.36). 5. "To test the intense love of Mādhavendra Purī" (4.189). 6. False: such exchanges need "a very elevated devotee" (SB 3.25.35 purport) and are "not possible for a common man to understand" (CC Madhya 4.190).
