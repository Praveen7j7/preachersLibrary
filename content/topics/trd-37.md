## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on trd-14 (serving Krishna with no hidden motive), trd-16 (honesty with authorities and oneself) and dps-02 (opening the
mind to a confidant). Those pages treat sincerity between the devotee and others. This page treats the conversation a mature
devotee has *with his own mind*, as the acharyas modelled it: Raghunatha dasa Gosvami's twelve verses addressed to his mind,
Narottama dasa Thakura's songs in which he tells his mind what he will do, and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati's instruction to
"make their wicked mind hear" a song every morning.

The lower batches teach mind control as a skill for students (vl1-03, vl1-16, vl1-30, vl1-40). TRD residents have lived in the
ashram for years and know the technique. What they need now is what the Gosvamis knew: that the mind of an advanced practitioner
is not conquered once but addressed daily, and that its later enemies are subtler than lust: deceit, fault-finding, the hunger
for honour, even the wish for liberation. The texts residents already sing are the acharyas' own method of self-correction.

## C. Central message

The mind is the friend or the enemy (BG 6.5–6), and it is "the mind alone that causes happiness and distress" (SB 11.23.42). The
acharyas did not only control the mind; they *instructed* it, every day, in writing and song: give up pride, surrender to guru
and the Vaisnavas, drop worldly talk, the desire for liberation and the hunger for honour, and serve Radha and Krishna in
Vraja under Rupa Gosvami. A mature devotee makes this address his own morning practice.

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. State the scriptural ground: the mind as friend or enemy (BG 6.5–6), controlled "by suitable practice and by detachment"
   (BG 6.35), and the Avanti brahmana's conclusion that the mind alone is the cause and that fixing it on Krishna's service is
   the way across (SB 11.23.42–57).
2. Name the twelve instructions of *Manah-siksa* in order and the enemy each targets (pride; karma and mukti; the desire for
   liberation and worldly talk; lust's gang; deceit and fault-finding; the hunger for honour), with the verse number.
3. Explain the method Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati gives: make the mind hear a song every morning, broom-stick it with a warning,
   find fault with oneself rather than others.
4. Sing *Prema-bhakti-candrika* song 1 texts 1–2 and 10 and song 2 text 12 knowing what each asks of the mind.
5. Write their own one-line instruction to the mind for the week, from these texts, and keep it beside their beads.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**NOI 1 and purport** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/1/
- **Teaching:** a sober person who tolerates the urges of speech, the mind, anger, the tongue, the belly and the genitals "is
  qualified to make disciples all over the world."
- **Purport point:** Prabhupada opens with Pariksit Maharaja's question (SB 6.1.9–10): why do people atone if they cannot control
  their senses? and the elephant's bath: "The elephant may take a very nice bath in the river, but as soon as it comes onto the
  bank, it throws dirt all over its body." The purport's answer, over its length, is that only devotional service controls the
  urges at the root.
- **Use:** in the talk, as the frame: the acharyas' instructions to the mind are the sober person's daily work.

**BG 6.5–6, 6.26, 6.34–35** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/5/
> 6.5: "One must deliver himself with the help of his mind, and not degrade himself. The mind is the friend of the conditioned soul, and his enemy as well."
> 6.6: "For him who has conquered the mind, the mind is the best of friends; but for one who has failed to do so, his mind will remain the greatest enemy."
> 6.26: "From wherever the mind wanders due to its flickering and unsteady nature, one must certainly withdraw it and bring it back under the control of the Self."
> 6.34: "The mind is restless, turbulent, obstinate and very strong, O Kṛṣṇa, and to subdue it, I think, is more difficult than controlling the wind."
> 6.35: "Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa said: O mighty-armed son of Kuntī, it is undoubtedly very difficult to curb the restless mind, but it is possible by suitable practice and by detachment."
- **Purport point (6.6):** "The purpose of practicing eightfold yoga is to control the mind in order to make it a friend in
  discharging the human mission." (6.26): "a self-realized yogī has to control the mind; the mind should not control him."
- **Teaching:** the mind is addressed because it can be made a friend; the means are practice and detachment, which in the
  acharyas' texts become daily instruction and service.
- **Use:** in the talk; 6.34 opens the problem, 6.35 the hope.

**SB 11.23.42–47, 11.23.57 (the Avanti brahmana)** — Verified · *BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples* ·
https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/23/42/
> 11.23.42: "These people are not the cause of my happiness and distress. Neither are the demigods, my own body, the planets, my past work, or time. Rather, it is the mind alone that causes happiness and distress and perpetuates the rotation of material life."
> 11.23.45: "Charity, prescribed duties, observance of major and minor regulative principles, hearing from scripture, pious works and purifying vows all have as their final aim the subduing of the mind. Indeed, concentration of the mind on the Supreme is the highest yoga."
> 11.23.47: "All the senses have been under the control of the mind since time immemorial, and the mind himself never comes under the sway of any other. He is stronger than the strongest, and his godlike power is fearsome. Therefore, anyone who can bring the mind under control becomes the master of all the senses."
> 11.23.57: "I shall cross over the insurmountable ocean of nescience by being firmly fixed in the service of the lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa. This was approved by the previous ācāryas, who were fixed in firm devotion to the Lord, Paramātmā, the Supreme Personality of Godhead."
- **Context:** Krishna tells Uddhava of a brahmana of Avanti who, ruined and insulted, sang this song instead of blaming anyone.
  11.23.43–44: the mind drives the modes; the soul "embraced this mind, which is the mirror reflecting the image of the material
  world." 11.23.46: if the mind is fixed, what need of rituals; if not, what use are they?
- **Teaching:** the Bhagavatam's own "instruction to the mind", ending where the Gosvamis' texts end: fixed service to Krishna's
  feet, following the previous acharyas.
- **Use:** in the talk, as the scriptural model for the whole genre.

**CC Antya 6.236–237; CC Antya 20.21** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/6/237/
> 6.236: "Do not talk like people in general or hear what they say. You should not eat very palatable food, nor should you dress very nicely."
> 6.237: "Do not expect honor, but offer all respect to others. Always chant the holy name of Lord Kṛṣṇa, and within your mind render service to Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa in Vṛndāvana."
- **Context:** the Lord's instruction to Raghunatha dasa Gosvami, the author of *Manah-siksa*, at Puri. Bhaktisiddhanta quotes
  exactly these lines in his 1936 lecture as "our line of conduct in holding bhajan". CC Antya 20.21 (trnad api sunicena) is the
  humility that makes the chanting possible; he quotes it too.
- **Purport point (6.237):** Bhaktivinoda: talk of family life is gramya-katha; the renounced "should show all respect to others,
  but should not expect respect for himself."
- **Teaching:** *Manah-siksa* is Raghunatha's life-long reply to this instruction: verse 4 drops worldly talk, verse 7 the hunger
  for honour, verses 8–11 serve Radha and Krishna in Vraja within the mind.
- **Use:** in the talk, as the link between the Lord's words and the Gosvami's verses.

**CC Antya 20.28; CC Madhya 22.153; CC Antya 4.71** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/20/28/
- **20.28:** where there is love of God, "the devotee does not think himself a devotee"; the purport (Bhaktisiddhanta) describes

  cannot be a bona fide spontaneous lover of Kṛṣṇa", quoting Narottama's *rūpa-raghunātha-pade haibe ākuti*. **4.71:** chant

- **Use:** 20.28 for verse 7 (honour) and for Bhaktisiddhanta's warning not to "proclaim thyself" a devotee; 22.153 for verses

***Sri Manah-siksa* (Srila Raghunatha dasa Gosvami), Desire Tree edition (OCR), with Bhaktivinoda Thakura's *Bhajana-darpana*;
translator not named** — read against the file, 2 Nov 2026. Twelve verses; the twelfth gives the fruit of singing the eleven.
- **Verse 1:** "O MY UNCOMPREHENDING INNER SELF, my dear brother mind, I humbly prostrate myself before you. Taking hold of your
  feet, I beseech you, please give up all pride and surrender fully to Sri Guru, to the spiritual abode of Vraja- dhama, to the
  residents of Vraja, to all the Vaisnava devotees of the Lord, to the sattvika-brahmanas, to the holy name of the Supreme Lord,
  and to the ever-fresh and youthful Divine Couple". *Target:* pride (dambha); the eight objects of attachment.
- **Verse 2:** "O my dear mind, please do not perform the religious activities described in the Vedas, or the sinful activities also
  designated in the scriptures." Meditate on Lord Caitanya as non-different from Krishna, and "on Sri Guru, who is the dearmost
  devotee of Lord Mukunda". *Target:* karma, pious or sinful, as a goal.
- **Verse 3:** desire "to remember and worship birth after birth the lotus feet of Sri Svarupa Damodara, Srila Rupa Gosvami, Sri
  Sanatana Gosvami and all the other associates of Sri Caitanya". *Target:* independence from the Lord's associates.
- **Verse 4:** "you must categorically relinquish all frivolous and mundane talk, which is like a prostitute who steals all your
  intelligence and resolve. The desire for liberation is equally evil, for it is like a terrifying tiger, devouring your very being."
  *Target:* gramya-katha and mukti-kamana.
- **Verse 5:** "you are attacked by lust, which is like a gang of thugs"; "you must cry out piteously and invoke the mercy of the
  unalloyed devotees of Sri Krsna". *Target:* lust and its five companions; the remedy is the devotees' mercy.
- **Verse 6:** "you foolishly think that you are cleansing yourself by bathing in the pretentiously fierce qualities of deceit and
  fault-finding, which are compared to impure donkey urine." *Target:* kutinati (deceit) and fault-finding in the dress of
  renunciation. This is the verse for residents.
- **Verse 7:** "the despicable desire for material honor and distinction is compared to a shameless and low-born prostitute who eats
  dog meat - yet she is flagrantly dancing in my heart." Only the Lord's intimate devotees "can drive out this prostitute".
  *Target:* pratistha (see trd-15, trd-49).
- **Verse 8:** "O MIND, PLEASE RESIDE IN VRAJA and earnestly worship Lord Krsna, the lifter of Govardhana Hill"; He will cleanse "a
  despicably deceitful soul like me". *Target:* the mind's final direction, after the enemies are named.
- **Verses 9–11:** absorption in Krishna as Radharani's beloved, in Her associates, Radha-kunda and Govardhana (9); worship of
  Radharani (10); shelter of Rupa Gosvami and his five services, "worshipping, glorification, meditation, hearing, and offering
  obeisaces" (11). *Use:* read as the author gives them, with Bhaktisiddhanta's note (lecture) that Vraja-seva in the mind is for
  one "whose self-realisation is complete"; no expansion of the confidential names (advanced-books.md §2).
- **Verse 12:** whoever, "having taken shelter of Srila Rupa Gosvami and ills [his] direct followers", sings these eleven verses
  "will certainly obtain the priceless gem of devotional service to Sri Radha and Sri Krsna."

- **Song 1, text 9:** "I shall eternally engage in the nine-fold process of devotional service, which is the essential message of

**Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura, "Instructions to the Mind on the Process of Bhajan" (Gaudiya Math, Calcutta, 12 July

against the file, 2 Nov 2026.
- On the daily rule: people "excel only in giving advice to others. (Their damned mind is, no doubt, responsible for this.) So,
  every morning, on rising from bed they should make their wicked mind hear the following immortal song of Thakur Bhaktivinoda:
  bhaja re bhaja re amara mana ati manda".
- On what to tell it: "This wicked mind, which is never to be trusted, should be broom-sticked every morning with such warning as,
  'Be not anxious to find fault with others, or to proclaim thyself as a true, sincere, bonafide bhakta, which certainly thou art
  not.'"
- On the Lord's instruction to Raghunatha (CC Antya 6.236–237) as "our line of conduct in holding bhajan", with his own rendering:
  "Have Krşna-nama always, humbly, on your tongue, Serve Radha-Krsna in the closet of the mind, all day long."
- On fault-finding: "I should rather find fault with my own damned mind"; "a man who is habituated to criticise others' conduct will
  never prosper."
- On the object of bhajan: Radha-Krsna in Vraja, not Dvaraka or Vaikuntha, served "with the help of all the limbs from head to foot".
- **Use:** in the talk, as the acharya's own application of the two texts; quote him as Bhaktisiddhanta, not as scripture.

**Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, *Vaishnavism Real and Apparent* (1926), section "The characteristic defects of the mind"** (Desire
Tree edition, OCR): "The mind and the soul are hostile to each other." Four defects: "(1) mistaking the mirage for water, the rope

horse can not hold its own reins, so the mind can not guide itself". He then renders Bhaktivinoda's song to the mind ("Ye wicked
mind! Thou art not a Vaishnava"). **Use:** in F, for why the mind must be *addressed* from outside itself; trd-39 uses the same
essay for its own topic.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **Why address the mind at all?** Because it "can not guide itself" (Bhaktisiddhanta): it mistakes, is intoxicated, perceives
   imperfectly and deceives. The soul must instruct it from the intelligence, with scripture's words. The Gita's two verses
   (6.5–6) make the stakes plain: friend or enemy. The Avanti brahmana (SB 11.23.42) locates the whole of material life in it.
2. **Instruction, not only suppression.** Yoga restrains the mind; the acharyas *tell it what to love*. Verse 1 of *Manah-siksa*
   does not say "be still"; it says surrender to guru, Vraja, the Vaisnavas, the holy name and Radha-Krishna, and develop
   attachment. This is BG 6.35's "practice and detachment" in devotional form: practice is daily service and hearing, detachment is
   the named enemies dropped one by one.
3. **The enemies of a mature devotee.** Raghunatha's list is not a beginner's: pride (1), religiosity as a goal (2), independence
   from the Lord's associates (3), worldly talk and the wish for liberation (4), lust's gang (5), deceit and fault-finding dressed
   as renunciation (6), the hunger for honour (7). Verses 6 and 7 name what grows in a temple after ten years. Bhaktisiddhanta's
   morning warning targets exactly these: fault-finding and self-proclamation.
4. **The method has a time and a form.** "Every morning, on rising from bed" (Bhaktisiddhanta); a song the mind must *hear*; a
   warning in the second person; and a positive object: Radha-Krishna's service in the mind, as the Lord told Raghunatha (CC
   Antya 6.237). Narottama's songs are the same method set to melody: "I shall control my mind, and always remember Lord
   Krşnacandra" (song 2, text 12).
5. **Where it leads.** Verses 8–12 turn the corrected mind toward Vraja under Rupa Gosvami. This is raganuga territory (trd-38),
   and Bhaktisiddhanta's caution stands: the inner service belongs to one whose realisation is complete; the rest of us sing the
   verses as prayer and keep the external practice (CC Madhya 22.156–157, in trd-38). The fruit is promised to the one who sings
   under Rupa's shelter (verse 12).
6. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *"Calling my mind 'wicked' is negative thinking."* It is the acharyas' honesty; the address is to the mind, not to the soul,
     and it ends in a positive object. Bhaktisiddhanta: find fault with "my own damned mind", not with others.
   - *"Verse 4 rejects Narayana; is that offensive?"* Raghunatha speaks as a Vraja devotee preferring Radha-Krishna's service to
     Vaikuntha's; Bhaktisiddhanta explains the gradation of rasas in the same lecture. It is a statement of taste in the Gaudiya
     line, read as the author gives it.
   - *"These songs are for kirtana, not for study."* Narottama wrote vows; sung without knowing them they become sound only.
   - *"I have controlled my mind; I do not need this daily."* Bhaktisiddhanta's rule is for every morning, for everyone; the mind
     "is never to be trusted".
7. **Pitch for TRD.** Residents sing these texts already. Spend the time making each verse a mirror, and leave with one line each
   has written to his own mind.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Sing *sri-guru-carana-padma* texts 1–2 with the residents (two minutes).
2. Say: we have sung these words a thousand mornings; today we find out what they ask of our mind, and we learn how two Gosvamis
   and our Prabhupada's guru spoke to their own minds every day.
3. Ask H.2 question 1 for private writing; then question 2; take three answers.
4. Read BG 6.5 aloud and say that the Gita gives the soul a job: deliver yourself with the help of your mind.

**H.2 Opening questions**
1. When your mind last pulled you somewhere you did not want to go this month, what did you say to it, in words? (Private.)
2. Which enemy has grown quieter in your ashram years, and which has grown louder: lust, pride, fault-finding, the wish for
   honour, worldly talk?
3. What do you think about while singing *sri-guru-carana-padma*?

## I. Core teaching points

1. **The mind is friend or enemy, and it alone drives material life** (BG 6.5–6; SB 11.23.42). *Example:* the Avanti brahmana,
   robbed and insulted, blaming no one. *Transition:* can it be controlled?
2. **Yes, by practice and detachment** (BG 6.34–35), bringing it back each time (6.26). *Transition:* the acharyas added a method.
3. **Address the mind, every morning** (Bhaktisiddhanta 1936): make it hear a song, warn it, give it its object. *Transition:*
   the first text of that address.
4. ***Manah-siksa* names the mature devotee's enemies**: pride, karma, independence, worldly talk, the wish for liberation, lust,
   deceit and fault-finding, the hunger for honour (verses 1–7). *Transition:* and then sends the mind somewhere.
5. **The mind is sent to Vraja under Rupa Gosvami** (verses 8–12; CC Antya 6.237), as prayer for most of us, as inner service for
   the realised (Bhaktisiddhanta's caution; CC Madhya 22.153). *Transition:* the songs we sing.
6. ***Prema-bhakti-candrika* is a set of vows**: guru's words one with my heart (text 2), sadhu-sastra-guru one with my heart (text
   10), the nine-fold process and no demigods (text 9), control the mind and remember Krsnacandra (song 2, text 12), pure bhajana
   following the Vraja devotees (text 18). *Transition:* the practice.
7. **One line to my mind, beside my beads** (P).

## J. Stories and illustrations

**The Avanti brahmana** (SB 11.23, told by Krishna to Uddhava). A rich, miserly brahmana loses everything, is abandoned by family
and insulted by passers-by, takes the dress of a renunciant, and, instead of blaming them, sings: these people are not the cause of
my happiness and distress, nor the demigods, my body, the planets, my past work or time; "it is the mind alone" (11.23.42); the
mind drives the modes (11.23.43); the soul "embraced this mind, which is the mirror reflecting the image of the material world"
(11.23.44); all pious works aim at subduing it (11.23.45); whoever controls it masters the senses (11.23.47); and he resolves: "I
shall cross over the insurmountable ocean of nescience by being firmly fixed in the service of the lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa" (11.23.57).
*Devotional lesson:* the Bhagavatam's own instruction to the mind ends in fixed service to Krishna, following the previous
acharyas; it is the model the Gosvamis followed. Keep to the verses; the full chapter is for SB class.

**The Lord's instruction to Raghunatha dasa** (CC Antya 6.236–237). At Puri the Lord tells him in two verses: no worldly talk or
hearing it, no fine food or dress, no expecting honour, all respect to others, always chant, and "within your mind render service to
Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa in Vṛndāvana." *Devotional lesson:* *Manah-siksa* is the Gosvami's lifelong reply; verses 4, 7 and 8–11 follow the
instruction line by line. Bhaktisiddhanta called these two verses "our line of conduct in holding bhajan".

**Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati in the Bagh Bazaar temple, 12 July 1936** (his lecture, Desire Tree edition). Before distinguished
guests he says people "excel only in giving advice to others" and prescribes that "every morning, on rising from bed they should
make their wicked mind hear" Bhaktivinoda's *bhaja re bhaja re amara mana ati manda*; the mind "should be broom-sticked every
morning"; and he quotes Narottama's *karmi jnani micha-bhakta* and the Lord's words to Raghunatha. *Devotional lesson:* the
founder-acharya of our line made the Gosvamis' address to the mind a daily rule for his own followers.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"Isn't talking to my own mind a kind of self-help trick?"** The Gita commands it: deliver yourself with the help of your mind
   (BG 6.5). The acharyas did it in writing. The difference from self-help is the object: the mind is sent to guru, the Vaisnavas,
   the holy name and Radha-Krishna's service, not to self-confidence.
2. **"Verse 4 says to give up even attachment to Lord Narayana. How do I preach that?"** You do not preach it to beginners; it is a
   Vraja devotee's statement of taste, read as the author gives it. Bhaktisiddhanta explains the gradation of rasas in the same
   lecture. For every audience below TRD, verse 4's first half (worldly talk, the wish for liberation) is the teaching.
3. **"Verses 9–11 name Radharani's associates. May I expand on them in class?"** Read them as the author gives them. The intimate
   lila texts are the preacher's background (research/advanced-books.md §2); Bhaktisiddhanta's caution in the lecture is that
   Vraja-seva in the mind is for one "whose self-realisation is complete".
4. **"I sing these songs daily and feel nothing. Is that hypocrisy?"** It is the state the songs are written for: Narottama calls
   himself fallen in text 1. Sing knowing the words, and let the vow in text 2 ("I will make the words from my spiritual master's
   lotus mouth one with my heart") be the prayer. Feeling follows practice (BG 6.35).
5. **"Fault-finding is sometimes a duty: I am a leader."** Correcting a dependent in service is duty (dps-02, trd-18); the habit of
   seeing others' faults is what verse 6 calls donkey urine and Bhaktisiddhanta calls the mind's disease. The test is whether you
   speak to the person for his good or about him for your relief.
6. **"The Avanti brahmana says the mind alone causes distress. Then are others never to blame?"** Others act; the mind decides what
   their acts do to me. The brahmana does not say the robbers were right; he says his happiness and distress come from his mind
   (11.23.42), and he fixes it on Krishna (11.23.57).
7. **"How long before the mind becomes a friend?"** Krishna says it is "undoubtedly very difficult" and possible by practice and
   detachment (BG 6.35); Bhaktisiddhanta's rule is "every morning", which means for life. The mind "is never to be trusted".

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The disciplined critic.** Madhava Prabhu, fifteen years a resident, keeps every rule and is respected. In his notebook he
has begun to list the faults of three other seniors "to protect the standard".
- **Dilemma:** is this vigilance or verse 6?
- **Principles:** *Manah-siksa* 6 (deceit and fault-finding "compared to impure donkey urine"); Bhaktisiddhanta's morning warning
  ("Be not anxious to find fault with others"); CC Antya 20.28 (the devotee does not think himself a devotee); NOI 1 (the sober
  person).
- **Questions:** What is the notebook doing to his own mind? What would the acharyas have him write each morning instead? When is
  raising a concern with authority right (trd-16), and how does that differ from the list?
- **Takeaway:** verse 6 is written for exactly this; the remedy is the morning address and service to the devotees he is listing.

**Case 2: The sweet singer.** Gaura dasa leads *sri-guru-carana-padma* beautifully every morning and is praised for it; lately he
chooses the songs by which ones he sings best.
- **Principles:** *Manah-siksa* 7 (the desire for honour "flagrantly dancing in my heart"); *Prema-bhakti-candrika* text 2 (no other
  desire to stay in my heart); CC Antya 6.237 (do not expect honour); CC Antya 20.21.
- **Questions:** What has the song become for him? Which text of the song itself answers him? What small change in how he leads
  kirtana would make the words true again?
- **Takeaway:** the texts are vows; singing them for honour turns them against the singer, and the same texts show the way back.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Krishna says the mind is the friend of the soul and its enemy as well, and that we must deliver ourselves with its help (BG 6.5–6).
Arjuna says it is "restless, turbulent, obstinate and very strong"; Krishna agrees that curbing it is "very difficult, but it is
possible by suitable practice and by detachment" (BG 6.34–35), bringing it back each time it wanders (BG 6.26). The Bhagavatam
tells of a brahmana of Avanti who lost everything and was insulted, and who sang: these people, the gods, my body, my past work
and time are not the cause of my happiness and distress; "it is the mind alone" (SB 11.23.42); and he resolved to cross the ocean
of ignorance "by being firmly fixed in the service of the lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa" (11.23.57).

The Gaudiya acharyas took this further: they *spoke to their minds* in writing and song. Raghunatha dasa Gosvami's *Manah-siksa*
("instructions to the mind") has twelve verses. The first seven tell the mind what to give up: pride, and in its place surrender
to guru, Vraja, the Vaisnavas, the holy name and Radha-Krishna (1); religious and sinful works as goals (2); independence from
the Lord's associates (3); worldly talk, "like a prostitute who steals all your intelligence and resolve", and the desire for
liberation, "like a terrifying tiger" (4); lust, "like a gang of thugs", against which one must cry to the devotees (5); deceit
and fault-finding, which the Gosvami compares to donkey urine (6); and the desire for honour, a prostitute "flagrantly dancing in
my heart" (7). Verses 8–12 send the corrected mind to Vraja, to the worship of Krishna and Radharani under the shelter of Rupa
Gosvami, and promise the service of Radha-Krishna to whoever sings these verses under that shelter. This follows Lord Caitanya's
own instruction to Raghunatha: no worldly talk, no fine food or dress, no expecting honour, all respect to others, always chant,
and "within your mind render service to Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa in Vṛndāvana" (CC Antya 6.236–237).

Narottama dasa Thakura's *Prema-bhakti-candrika*, which we sing every morning, is a set of vows: "I will make the words from my
spiritual master's lotus mouth one with my heart" (song 1, text 2); make the words of guru, sadhu and sastra one with my heart
(text 10); practise the nine-fold devotional service and worship no demigods (text 9); "I shall control my mind, and always
remember Lord Krşnacandra" (song 2, text 12); and, "O mind, please engage in pure devotional service" following the devotees of
Vraja (text 18). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati made this a daily rule in 1936: every morning, on rising, make the mind hear
Bhaktivinoda's song *bhaja re bhaja re amara mana ati manda*, warn it not to find fault with others or to proclaim itself a
devotee, and find fault with "my own damned mind" instead. A mature devotee does the same: one line to his own mind each morning,
ending in Krishna's service.

## S2. Key ideas

- The mind is friend or enemy; it is controlled by practice and detachment (BG 6.5–6, 6.35) and is the one cause of our happiness
  and distress (SB 11.23.42).
- The acharyas instructed their minds daily in writing and song; this is the mature devotee's method of self-correction.
- *Manah-siksa* verses 1–7 name the enemies: pride, karma as a goal, independence, worldly talk, the wish for liberation, lust,
  deceit and fault-finding, the hunger for honour.
- Verses 8–12 send the mind to Radha-Krishna's service in Vraja under Rupa Gosvami, following the Lord's words to Raghunatha
  (CC Antya 6.236–237).
- *Prema-bhakti-candrika* is a set of vows about guru, sadhu and sastra, the nine-fold service and the controlled mind.
- Bhaktisiddhanta's rule: every morning, make the mind hear a song and warn it; find fault with yourself, not others.

## S3. Verses to remember

- **BG 6.5:** "One must deliver himself with the help of his mind, and not degrade himself. The mind is the friend of the conditioned
  soul, and his enemy as well." *For me:* the job is mine, and the tool is my mind.
- **BG 6.35:** "it is possible by suitable practice and by detachment." *For me:* difficulty is not impossibility.
- **SB 11.23.57:** "I shall cross over the insurmountable ocean of nescience by being firmly fixed in the service of the lotus feet of
  Kṛṣṇa." *For me:* every instruction to the mind ends in service.
- **CC Antya 6.237:** "Do not expect honor, but offer all respect to others. Always chant the holy name of Lord Kṛṣṇa, and within your
  mind render service to Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa in Vṛndāvana." *For me:* the Lord's own instruction to the author of *Manah-siksa*.

## S4. Words to know

- **Manah-siksa:** "instructions to the mind", twelve Sanskrit verses by Raghunatha dasa Gosvami addressed to his own mind.
- **Prema-bhakti-candrika:** "the moonbeam of loving devotion", Narottama dasa Thakura's Bengali songs of vows; *sri-guru-carana-padma*
  is its first song.
- **Dambha:** pride, the first thing verse 1 asks the mind to give up.
- **Gramya-katha:** worldly talk, which the Lord told Raghunatha to neither speak nor hear.
- **Kutinati:** deceit, named in verse 6 together with fault-finding.
- **Pratistha:** the desire for honour and position, named in verse 7.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Isn't talking to my own mind just a psychological trick?** The Gita commands it (BG 6.5) and the acharyas did it in writing.
   The difference is the object: the mind is sent to guru, the devotees, the holy name and Radha-Krishna's service, not to
   self-confidence.
2. **I sing these songs every day and feel nothing. Am I a hypocrite?** Narottama calls himself fallen in his first text; the songs
   are written for exactly this state. Sing knowing the words, and let text 2 be your prayer. Feeling follows practice (BG 6.35).
3. **Verse 4 says to give up attachment even to Lord Narayana. Is that not offensive?** It is a Vraja devotee's statement of taste,
   read as the author gives it; for most audiences the teaching of verse 4 is its first half: give up worldly talk and the wish for
   liberation.
4. **The Avanti brahmana says the mind alone causes distress. So others are never to blame?** Others act; the mind decides what their
   acts do to me (SB 11.23.42). The brahmana does not excuse the robbers; he fixes his mind on Krishna (11.23.57).
5. **Is noticing others' faults always wrong? Sometimes I must correct people.** Correcting someone in your care, to his face and for
   his good, is service. The habit of seeing and listing faults is what verse 6 condemns and Bhaktisiddhanta warned against every
   morning.
6. **How long until the mind is a friend?** Krishna says it is very difficult and possible by practice and detachment (BG 6.35).
   Bhaktisiddhanta's rule is "every morning", for life: the mind "is never to be trusted".

mind hear Bhaktivinoda's song *bhaja re bhaja re amara mana ati manda* and warn it not to find fault with others or to proclaim
itself a devotee.
