# Srila Prabhupada's mood of compassion: the Jaladuta prayers

> **TRD · up to 180 min · review: drafted (30 Oct 2026).** Group A. The two poems are quoted from the BBT text on
> vedabase (*Srila Prabhupada-lilamrta*, vol. 1, appendix pages "Prayer to the Lotus Feet of Krsna" and "Markine
> Bhagavata-dharma"); the diary entries from *Lilamrta* vol. 1 ch. 12. Verses checked with `tools/verify-refs.mjs`.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

dps-41 tells the life and asks for attachment to the founder-acarya. Residents have that attachment; what they need is
his *mood* when he had nothing: sixty-nine years old, two heart attacks at sea, forty rupees, and a trunk of books.
The two poems he wrote on the Jaladuta say exactly what he felt. They are read here in full, as his own words.

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| VL1 | One sentence: an old man crossed the sea alone to give Krishna to the world |
| VL2 | The journey as evidence of conviction; "make me dance" as surrender, not showmanship |
| VL3 | The diary line "I have left Bharatabhumi just to execute the order" and the practice of following an order |
| SPS | SB 1.2.17-21 as the poem quotes them: hearing purifies; why the preacher begins with the Bhagavatam |
| DPS | Builds on dps-41: the founder's mood toward his guru (Prayer, verses 2 and 5) and toward the people (Markine, verse 3) |
| BSS | A brahmacari's dependence: "I have no qualification, but have taken up the risk" |
| **TRD (here)** | Both poems **in full**, the diary, and the mood as the model for the resident's own preaching |

**Not done here:** the purpose of preaching (trd-01); Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati's role (trd-04); the biography (dps-41).

## C. Central message

On the ship Srila Prabhupada wrote that he had no qualification, that the people he was going to were covered by
passion and ignorance, and that only Krishna's causeless mercy could make them understand; then he asked Krishna to make
him dance as He liked. That mood, dependence without excuse and compassion without contempt, is the missionary spirit a
resident is asked to carry.

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Tell the voyage as the *Lilamrta* tells it (dates, the heart attacks, the dream, the arrival) without addition.
2. Quote the key lines of both poems and say what each reveals about Srila Prabhupada's mood.
3. Explain why the Markine poem quotes SB 1.2.17-21 and what that says about his method.
4. Explain from SB 7.9.28, quoted in the Prayer, why he saw his mission as service to his guru.
5. Name one place in their own service where the poem's mood is missing, and what to do about it.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**SB 1.2.17, 1.2.18, 1.2.19, 1.2.20, 1.2.21** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/17/ (and following)
> 1.2.17: "Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the Personality of Godhead, who is the Paramātmā [Supersoul] in everyone's heart and the benefactor of the truthful devotee, cleanses desire for material enjoyment from the heart of the devotee who has developed the urge to hear His messages, which are in themselves virtuous when properly heard and chanted."
- **Context:** Suta Gosvami to the sages at Naimisaranya: how hearing about Krishna purifies the heart in stages (cleansing; fixed devotion; passion and ignorance subside; knowledge; the knot of the heart is cut).
- **Teaching:** Srila Prabhupada put these five verses into the middle of the Markine poem, in Sanskrit, with his own translation. His plan for America was this sequence: make people hear the Bhagavatam and Krishna Himself will do the cleansing.
- **Purport point (1.2.17):** "Messages of the Personality of Godhead, Sri Krsna, are nondifferent from Him. Whenever, therefore, offenseless hearing and glorification of God are undertaken, it is to be understood that Lord Krsna is present there in the form of transcendental sound." This is why the poem says "the words of Srimad-Bhagavatam are Your incarnation."
- **Use:** in the talk. The stages themselves are taught in dps-15; here only the point that the founder's method was hearing.

**SB 7.9.28** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/9/28/
> "My dear Lord, O Supreme Personality of Godhead, because of my association with material desires, one after another, I was gradually falling into a blind well full of snakes, following the general populace. But Your servant Nārada Muni kindly accepted me as his disciple and instructed me how to achieve this transcendental position. Therefore, my first duty is to serve him. How could I leave his service?"
- **Context:** Prahlada Maharaja to Lord Nrsimhadeva. Srila Prabhupada quotes it inside the "Prayer to the Lotus Feet of Krsna" (13 September 1965) after the verse about his Guru Maharaja's order.
- **Teaching:** The devotee's first duty is to serve the guru who rescued him; Srila Prabhupada read his own voyage as that service.
- **Use:** in the talk, read where the poem places it.

**CC Adi 9.41** — Verified (treated in trd-01). The diary says he left India "to execute the order of Sri Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati, in pursuance of Lord Chaitanya's order"; this is that order.

**SB 7.9.44** — Verified (treated in trd-01). Prahlada's refusal to be liberated alone is the scriptural form of the poem's third verse ("I do not know how they will be able to understand it") followed by the request for mercy on them.

**"Prayer to the Lotus Feet of Krsna"** (on board the Jaladuta, 13 September 1965) — Verified text · https://vedabase.io/en/library/spl/1/prayer-lotus-feet-krsna/ (BBT, *Srila Prabhupada-lilamrta* vol. 1). Key lines, in the BBT translation:
> Refrain: "I emphatically say to you, O brothers, you will obtain your good fortune from the Supreme Lord Krsna only when Srimati Radharani becomes pleased with you."
> Verse 2: "Sri Srimad Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura, who is very dear to Lord Gauranga, the son of mother Saci, is unparalleled in his service to the Supreme Lord Sri Krsna. He is that great, saintly spiritual master who bestows intense devotion to Krsna at different places throughout the world."
> Verse 3: "By his strong desire, the holy name of Lord Gauranga will spread throughout all the countries of the Western world. In all the cities, towns, and villages on the earth, from all the oceans, seas, rivers, and streams, everyone will chant the holy name of Krsna."
> Verse 5: "Although my Guru Maharaja ordered me to accomplish this mission, I am not worthy or fit to do it. I am very fallen and insignificant. Therefore, O Lord, now I am begging for Your mercy so that I may become worthy, for You are the wisest and most experienced of all."
> Verse 8: "O dear friend, in Your company I will experience great joy once again. In the early morning I will wander about the cowherd pastures and fields. Running and frolicking in the many forests of Vraja, I will roll on the ground in spiritual ecstasy. Oh, when will that day be mine?"
> Verse 9: "I am Your eternal servant, and therefore I desire Your association so much. O Lord Krsna, except for You there is no other means of success."
- **Use:** in the talk, read in full (about five minutes). Verse 8 is quoted as he wrote it; the preacher adds no commentary on its inner meaning beyond what the words say (the head's rule on rasa topics; see trd-13).

**"Markine Bhagavata-dharma"** (Boston Harbor, 18 September 1965) — Verified text · https://vedabase.io/en/library/spl/1/markine-bhagavata-dharma/ (BBT). Key lines:
> Verse 1: "My dear Lord Krsna, You are so kind upon this useless soul, but I do not know why You have brought me here. Now You can do whatever You like with me."
> Verse 3: "Most of the population here is covered by the material modes of ignorance and passion. Absorbed in material life, they think themselves very happy and satisfied, and therefore they have no taste for the transcendental message of Vasudeva. I do not know how they will be able to understand it."
> Verse 4: "But I know Your causeless mercy can make everything possible, because You are the most expert mystic."
> Verse 8: "The words of Srimad-Bhagavatam are Your incarnation, and if a sober person repeatedly receives it with submissive aural reception, then he will be able to understand Your message."
> Verse 10: "How will I make them understand this message of Krsna consciousness? I am very unfortunate, unqualified, and the most fallen. Therefore I am seeking Your benediction so that I can convince them, for I am powerless to do so on my own."
> Verse 12: "O spiritual master of all the worlds! I can simply repeat Your message, so if You like You can make my power of speaking suitable for their understanding."
> Verse 14: "O Lord, I am just like a puppet in Your hands. So if You have brought me here to dance, then make me dance, make me dance, O Lord, make me dance as You like."
> Verse 15: "I have no devotion, nor do I have any knowledge, but I have strong faith in the holy name of Krsna. I have been designated as Bhaktivedanta, and now, if You like, You can fulfill the real purport of Bhaktivedanta."
> Signed: "the most unfortunate, insignificant beggar A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami, on board the ship Jaladuta, Commonwealth Pier, Boston, Massachusetts, U.S.A. dated 18th of September, 1965".
- **Use:** in the talk, read in full (about eight minutes with the Sanskrit verses).

**Jaladuta diary** (quoted in *Srila Prabhupada-lilamrta* vol. 1 ch. 12) — Verified text · https://vedabase.io/en/library/spl/1/12/
> 10 September 1965: "I have left Bharatabhumi just to execute the order of Sri Bhaktisiddhanta Saraswati, in pursuance of Lord Chaitanya's order. I have no qualification, but have taken up the risk just to carry out the order of His Divine Grace. I depend fully on Their mercy, so far away from Vrindaban."
> 9 September: "I am struggling and the nectarine of life is Sri Chaitanya Charitamrita, the source of all my vitality."
- **Use:** in the talk and in J.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **An order, not an ambition.** The diary gives the reason for the voyage in one sentence: to execute his guru's order, in pursuance of Lord Caitanya's order (CC Adi 9.41). The Prayer quotes SB 7.9.28: "my first duty is to serve him. How could I leave his service?" The missionary spirit begins as obedience.
2. **No claim of qualification.** "I have no qualification" (diary); "I am not worthy or fit" (Prayer 5); "I am very unfortunate, unqualified, and the most fallen" (Markine 10). He did not wait to feel qualified; he asked Krishna to make him so. A resident who delays preaching until he feels ready has reversed the founder's order of events.
3. **Compassion without contempt.** Markine 3 states plainly that the people are covered by passion and ignorance and have no taste for Krishna; the next line asks for mercy on them, not for a better audience. This is Prahlada's mood (SB 7.9.44): the diagnosis is honest and the response is to stay and ask for their deliverance.
4. **The method is hearing.** Five Bhagavatam verses sit in the middle of the Markine poem. He did not plan to adjust the message to America; he planned to make America hear the Bhagavatam, trusting Krishna in the sound (SB 1.2.17 purport). His books were already in the trunk.
5. **"I can simply repeat Your message"** (Markine 12). This is CC Madhya 7.128 (trd-02) in the first person: the power of speech is Krishna's to give; the preacher repeats.
6. **Make me dance** (Markine 14). The puppet image is his own. It is not helplessness; it is the request that his effort be moved entirely by Krishna. The resident's version: "use me as You like, here, with these people."
7. **Dependence as strength.** "The nectarine of life is Sri Chaitanya Charitamrita, the source of all my vitality" (diary, 9 September). Between two heart attacks he was reading the Caitanya-caritamrta. The missionary's inner life is the source of his outer work, not a competitor to it.
8. **Misunderstanding.** "He was great, so this mood is not for us." The poems do not claim greatness; they claim nothing. The mood is available precisely because it rests on Krishna's mercy, not on the devotee's merit.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes).** Bring a printed copy of both poems for every resident. Say: "On 13 September
1965, between two heart attacks, a sixty-nine-year-old sannyasi wrote a Bengali poem in his cabin. Five days later, when
the ship reached Boston, he wrote another. Tonight we read both, all the way through, as his own words. First, the
diary line that explains why he was on the ship at all." Read the 10 September entry aloud.

**H.2 Opening questions**
1. "If you were sent alone tomorrow to a country whose language you barely speak, with forty rupees and a box of books, what would you write in your diary the first night?"
2. "What do you expect a great preacher to feel on arrival: confidence, or something else?"
3. "Have you ever started a service feeling completely unqualified? What happened?" (No one has to answer aloud.)

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Why he went (diary; SB 7.9.28).** To execute an order, as service to the guru who rescued him. Example: a resident asked to run a programme he did not choose can read the diary entry as the founder's own answer to "why me?" Transition: "What did he think of himself?"
2. **No qualification claimed (Prayer 5; Markine 10, 15).** "I have no devotion, nor do I have any knowledge, but I have strong faith in the holy name." Example: a brahmacari who postpones speaking "until I know enough" is asked what Srila Prabhupada knew in 1965 compared with what he felt he had. Transition: "What did he think of the people?"
3. **Honest diagnosis, then mercy (Markine 3–4).** Covered by passion and ignorance, no taste; "but I know Your causeless mercy can make everything possible." Example: a resident's private contempt for "these students who only want jobs" fails the second half of the verse. Transition: "What was his method?"
4. **Hearing (Markine 8; SB 1.2.17-21).** The Bhagavatam is Krishna's incarnation in sound; hearing cleanses. Example: a programme planned around entertainment first and a short reading last has inverted the founder's plan. Transition: "What did he ask for himself?"
5. **To be made to speak and to dance (Markine 12, 14).** "I can simply repeat Your message"; "make me dance as You like." Example: before a class, the resident's prayer can be these two lines. Transition: "What did he hold on to?"
6. **The source of vitality (diary, 9 September).** The Caitanya-caritamrta between heart attacks. Example: residents who drop their reading when service gets heavy have cut the line he held. Transition to J.

## J. Stories and illustrations

**The voyage (as *Srila Prabhupada-lilamrta* vol. 1 ch. 12 tells it; Verified text).** The Jaladuta, a cargo ship of the
Scindia Steam Navigation Company, left Calcutta at 9:00 a.m. on Friday 13 August 1965 with one passenger, "Sri Abhoy
Charanaravinda Bhaktivedanta Swami", aged sixty-nine, on "a complimentary ticket with food" arranged by Sumati Morarji.
He suffered seasickness in the Bay of Bengal. On Janmastami, 20 August, he spoke to the crew about Krishna and
distributed prasadam he had cooked. His trunks of Bhagavatam volumes were loaded at Cochin on 21 August, his birthday.
In the Red Sea he had "rain, seasickness, dizziness, headache, no appetite, vomiting", and in two days two heart
attacks; he thought a third would kill him. That night he dreamed that Lord Krishna, in His many forms, was rowing a
boat and told him not to fear but to come along; the attacks did not recur. He recovered slowly, cooking his own
kichari and puris, and wrote on 9 September that the Caitanya-caritamrta was "the source of all my vitality". On
13 September he wrote the Prayer; on 18 September, in Boston Harbor, the Markine poem; on 19 September the ship docked
in New York. Devotional lesson: every stage of the journey was met with hearing, prasadam, prayer and the order; nothing
else was available, and nothing else was needed.

**No addition.** The *Lilamrta* records the dream and the heart attacks; tell them as it does, without embellishing the
dream or adding dialogue.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"Isn't it discouraging to read that he felt unqualified? We look to him for confidence."** His confidence was in Krishna's mercy, stated in the next line each time (Markine 4, 10, 12). The poems show where real confidence sits.
2. **"Verse 3 of the Markine poem sounds harsh about Americans."** It describes the condition of people absorbed in material life, as the Bhagavatam does, and immediately asks for their deliverance. We read it as it is; it is the diagnosis that makes the compassion real.
3. **"Verse 8 of the Prayer speaks of wandering in Vraja's forests. Should we discuss that?"** Read it as he wrote it. It shows that the preacher who crossed the sea had an inner life in Vrindavana. We do not add our own interpretation of its details; the rasa topics are treated under the head's review in group G (trd-41 onward).
4. **"Is 'make me dance' a licence to do anything in the name of surrender?"** No. The dancer in the poem is "a puppet in Your hands", moved by the order of his guru and of Lord Caitanya (diary). Surrender here means following that order exactly, not acting on one's own impulse.
5. **"Why did he quote five Sanskrit verses in a Bengali poem?"** Because they were his plan: hearing the Bhagavatam cleanses the heart. He arrived with the verses and with the books that contain them.

## L. Case studies

**Case 1: The new centre (Invented case for discussion).** Ravi, four years in the ashram, is sent to open a programme
in a town where no one knows the movement. After three weeks of empty halls he writes to his authority that the town
"has no taste" and asks to come back.
- **Dilemma:** Honest report, or the first half of Markine verse 3 without the second half?
- **Principles:** Markine 3–4; the diary of 10 September (the order, the risk, dependence); NOI 3 purport's patience (see dps-15).
- **Questions:** What did Srila Prabhupada do in his first months in New York, according to what Ravi knows from dps-41? What is Ravi's equivalent of the Bhagavatam trunk? What is the next sentence after "no taste"?
- **Analysis:** The diagnosis may be accurate; the poem's response is to ask for mercy on them and to keep making them hear. The request to return is premature by the founder's own measure.
- **Takeaway:** Report the condition honestly and stay; the poem gives the prayer to pray.

**Case 2: The confident speaker (Invented case for discussion).** Arjun gives excellent classes and knows it; he
prepares little, rarely reads the purport, and tells juniors that "feeling" matters more than text.
- **Dilemma:** Gift, or the opposite of Markine 12 and 15?
- **Principles:** Markine 12 ("I can simply repeat Your message"); Markine 15 ("I have no devotion, nor do I have any knowledge"); diary (Caitanya-caritamrta as vitality).
- **Questions:** Whose power of speaking did the founder think he had? What was his source of vitality during the voyage? What is Arjun's?
- **Takeaway:** The founder's mood was to repeat and to read; skill is welcome, but it is not the source.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions**
1. According to his diary, why did Srila Prabhupada leave India? (To execute Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati's order, in pursuance of Lord Caitanya's order.)
2. Which Bhagavatam verses did he put in the Markine poem, and why? (SB 1.2.17-21: hearing the Bhagavatam cleanses the heart; that was his method.)
3. What did he say about his own qualification? ("I have no qualification"; "very fallen and insignificant"; "I have no devotion, nor do I have any knowledge, but I have strong faith in the holy name.")
4. What did he say about the people he was going to, and what did he ask next? (Covered by passion and ignorance, no taste; then: Your causeless mercy can make everything possible.)
5. What did he ask Krishna to do with him? (To make him dance as He likes; to make his speaking suitable for their understanding.)
6. What was his "source of all vitality" on the ship? (Sri Caitanya-caritamrta.)

**M.2 Activity (30 min): "My own Markine."** Each resident writes, privately, four lines in plain language addressed to
Krishna about his present service, following the poem's order: (1) I do not know why You have put me here; (2) the
people I serve are like this (honestly); (3) only Your mercy can make them understand; (4) make me ___ as You like.
Nobody reads it aloud. Then, in pairs, each says one thing the exercise showed him, without reading the text.

**M.3 Reflection (private).** "Where in my service do I still wait to feel qualified? What would Srila Prabhupada's
diary line say to that?"

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

In August 1965 Srila Prabhupada, aged sixty-nine, sailed alone from Calcutta to New York on a cargo ship, the Jaladuta,
with a free ticket, a few rupees and trunks of his Srimad-Bhagavatam. In the Red Sea he suffered two heart attacks in
two days; that night he dreamed that Krishna, in many forms, was rowing a boat and told him not to fear but to come along.
In his diary he wrote why he had gone: "I have left Bharatabhumi just to execute the order of Sri Bhaktisiddhanta
Saraswati, in pursuance of Lord Chaitanya's order. I have no qualification, but have taken up the risk just to carry out
the order of His Divine Grace."

On 13 September he wrote a Bengali poem, "Prayer to the Lotus Feet of Krsna". In it he praises his guru, who "bestows
intense devotion to Krsna at different places throughout the world", says that by his guru's desire the holy name will
spread through the Western world, and admits, "I am not worthy or fit to do it… Therefore, O Lord, now I am begging for
Your mercy." He quotes Prahlada Maharaja: "my first duty is to serve him. How could I leave his service?" (SB 7.9.28).

On 18 September, in Boston Harbor, he wrote "Markine Bhagavata-dharma". He says he does not know why Krishna has brought
him to this "terrible place", that the people are covered by passion and ignorance and have no taste for Krishna, "but I
know Your causeless mercy can make everything possible." He puts five Bhagavatam verses in the middle (SB 1.2.17-21):
hearing about Krishna cleanses the heart; that was his plan. He calls himself "very unfortunate, unqualified, and the
most fallen", says "I can simply repeat Your message", and ends: "make me dance, make me dance, O Lord, make me dance as
You like… I have no devotion, nor do I have any knowledge, but I have strong faith in the holy name of Krsna."

That is the missionary mood: obedience to the order, no claim of qualification, honesty about people together with
compassion for them, and complete dependence on Krishna.

## S2. Key ideas

- He went to execute his guru's order, which carried Lord Caitanya's order (diary; CC Adi 9.41).
- He claimed no qualification and asked Krishna to supply it (Prayer 5; Markine 10, 15).
- He described the people honestly and then asked for mercy on them (Markine 3–4).
- His method was hearing the Bhagavatam (Markine 8; SB 1.2.17-21).
- He asked to be made to speak and to dance as Krishna liked (Markine 12, 14).
- His strength on the ship was the Caitanya-caritamrta (diary, 9 September).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **SB 1.2.17:** Krishna "cleanses desire for material enjoyment from the heart of the devotee who has developed the urge to hear His messages." *For me:* making people hear is the method.
- **SB 7.9.28:** "my first duty is to serve him. How could I leave his service?" *For me:* my service is service to the one who rescued me.
- **Markine Bhagavata-dharma, verse 14:** "make me dance, O Lord, make me dance as You like." *For me:* my prayer before any service.

## S4. Words to know

- **Jaladuta:** the Scindia cargo ship that carried Srila Prabhupada to America in 1965.
- **Markine Bhagavata-dharma:** "Krishna consciousness in America", the poem written in Boston Harbor.
- **Bharatabhumi:** the land of India.
- **Bhaktivedanta:** his title; the poem asks Krishna to "fulfill the real purport" of it.
- **Causeless mercy (ahaituki krpa):** Krishna's mercy given without a reason on our side.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Why did he feel so unqualified if he was a pure devotee?** The poems show where he placed his confidence: in Krishna's mercy, not in himself. That is why they are a model, not a discouragement.
2. **Was he being negative about Americans?** He described their condition honestly and asked for their deliverance in the next line. Both halves belong together.
3. **What was his plan for preaching?** To make people hear the Bhagavatam; the five verses in the poem say that hearing cleanses the heart.
4. **Does "make me dance" mean doing whatever one feels?** No. He calls himself a puppet in Krishna's hands, moved by his guru's order and Lord Caitanya's. Surrender means following that order.
5. **What kept him going on the ship?** Reading the Caitanya-caritamrta, cooking prasadam, prayer, and the order he carried.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. Why, according to his diary, did Srila Prabhupada leave India?
2. Where and when did he write "Markine Bhagavata-dharma"?
3. Which Bhagavatam verses did he quote in it, and what do they teach?
4. Complete: "I have no devotion, nor do I have any knowledge, but I have ___."
5. True or false: in the Prayer he says he is fully qualified for the mission his guru gave.
6. What did he say he could do with Krishna's message?

**Answers:** 1. To execute Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati's order, in pursuance of Lord Caitanya's order. 2. Boston
Harbor, 18 September 1965. 3. SB 1.2.17-21: hearing about Krishna cleanses the heart and establishes devotion.
4. "strong faith in the holy name of Krsna." 5. False: "I am not worthy or fit to do it." 6. "I can simply repeat Your
message."
