# PEACE Formula

> **VL3 · 180 min · review: sources-checked.** The "peace formula" is Srila Prabhupada's name for Bhagavad-gita 5.29 (his purport: "The greatest
> peace formula is simply this"). IYF's material for this row is the *Essence of Bhagavad-gita* chapter 11 outline (`research/iyf-materials/vl3-03/`)
> and the Preaching Encyclopedia chapter *Peace Formula* (VOICE; `research/encyclopedia/IYF Books/0416`). Their flow (why there is no
> peace; is wanting peace wrong; the three-point formula; prosperity follows pleasing the Lord) is kept; two of their stories need source
> review and one point of the formula is restored from the purport (B, J). No special sensitivity.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

At VL1 "peace" would be a relevance talk about stress. At VL3 the participants have a practice, and the formula explains *why* the
practice steadies them and *what* in their day still cancels the steadiness. The Gita's own placement helps: 2.66 ("without which there
is no possibility of peace") comes right after the "regulative principles of freedom" (2.64–65) that VL3-02 taught; 5.29 ends the
chapter on action in Krsna consciousness; 5.12 and 2.47 give the practice of working without claiming the fruit. So the page moves from
VL3-02's "what I regulate" to "what I know while I work, eat and chant".

Builds on VL3-02: only its bridge (BG 2.64–66, regulation then peace) is repeated; the higher-taste argument and the fall chain are not re-taught here.

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| VL1 | Stress as an everyday problem; peace as something people want, without the formula |
| VL2 | Happiness talks; the self and its needs; "peace" as a felt problem |
| **VL3-03** (here) | BG 5.29 as a formula with three points (enjoyer, proprietor, friend); why desire-chasing fails (2.70–71); the formula as daily practice: offer (9.27; 3.13), quota (ISO 1), fruit (2.47; 5.12), friend (5.29); where chanting fits |
| SPS | Peace under difficulty and loss |
| DPS | *Yukta-vairāgya* (detachment that uses things in Krsna's service) in depth (DPS 27–31); the formula at the level of the heart's motives |
| BSS | The formula inside a brahmacari (celibate student) day; see R.6 |

**Not done here:**
- A survey of world peace, politics or "no God, no peace" debates. The formula is applied to the participant's own week.
- The Encyclopedia's long "evolution"/*avidyā* section and "religion without philosophy" discussion: one line each at most.
- The parable of the prodigal son (Encyclopedia): a parable from another faith's scripture; not used here.
- Stories whose source is not established: the crow and the necklace, the magic food bowl (J).
- Promising that chanting removes anxiety or depression. It gives many people steadiness; it is not a treatment (K.7).

## C. Central message

Peace does not come from getting what we want but from knowing three things and living by them: Krsna is the enjoyer of all our
sacrifice and work, the owner of everything, and the friend of every living being (BG 5.29). Without this connection there is no peace,
and without peace no happiness (BG 2.66); with it, desires can keep flowing in like rivers into a full ocean and leave us still (BG 2.70).

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. State the three points of the peace formula from BG 5.29 and its purport (enjoyer, proprietor, friend) and say why the third point
   matters.
2. Explain from BG 2.66 and 2.70–71 why satisfying desires cannot produce peace and what does.
3. Translate each point into a daily practice: offer the result (9.27; 5.12), take one's quota (ISO 1), act without claim on the fruit
   (2.47), approach the Lord as a friend in japa (5.29).
4. Diagnose one recurring disturbance of their own week by asking which of the three points was forgotten.
5. Answer "isn’t wanting peace selfish?" and "I chant and I’m still anxious" honestly.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** BG 5.29 (with purport), 2.66, 2.70–71, 5.12, 2.47, 9.27, ISO 1 carry the talk. BG 2.64–65, 6.7, 12.12, 18.62, 4.39, 4.31, 3.9,
3.12–13, 5.25 and the ISO Invocation answer questions.

**BG 5.29**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/5/29/
> "A person in full consciousness of Me, knowing Me to be the ultimate beneficiary of all sacrifices and austerities, the Supreme Lord of all planets and demigods, and the benefactor and well-wisher of all living entities, attains peace from the pangs of material miseries."
- **Context:** The last verse of the fifth chapter, on action in Krsna consciousness (karma-yoga).
- **Purport point:** "The greatest peace formula is simply this: Lord Kṛṣṇa is the beneficiary in all human activities. Men should offer
  everything to the transcendental service of the Lord because He is the proprietor of all planets"; living entities "are trying to be
  lords of all they survey, but actually they are dominated by the material energy of the Lord"; "unless one understands these bare
  facts, it is not possible to achieve peace in the world either individually or collectively".
- **Teaching:** Three facts (He enjoys, He owns, He is everyone's friend) and one result (peace).
- **Application (preacher's):** IYF's outline gives the three points as "controller, proprietor, enjoyer". The verse and the 2.66
  purport give "enjoyer, proprietor and friend". Keep *friend* (*suhṛdaṁ sarva-bhūtānām*): it is the point that turns a formula into a
  relationship, and the point a VL3 participant meets in japa.
- **Use:** in the talk; the formula itself.

**BG 2.66**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/66/
> "One who is not connected with the Supreme [in Kṛṣṇa consciousness] can have neither transcendental intelligence nor a steady mind, without which there is no possibility of peace. And how can there be any happiness without peace?"
- **Purport point:** The purport points forward to 5.29: when one understands that Krsna is "the enjoyer, proprietor and friend of
  everyone and everything, then one can, with a steady mind, bring about peace"; "disturbance is due to want of an ultimate goal".
- **Teaching:** No connection, no steady mind; no steady mind, no peace; no peace, no happiness.
- **Use:** in the talk; the "why".

**BG 2.70**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/70/
> "A person who is not disturbed by the incessant flow of desires – that enter like rivers into the ocean, which is ever being filled but is always still – can alone achieve peace, and not the man who strives to satisfy such desires."
- **Purport point:** "As long as one has the material body, the demands of the body for sense gratification will continue. The
  devotee, however, is not disturbed by such desires, because of his fullness"; the fruitive workers, salvationists and mystic yogis
  "are all unhappy because of unfulfilled desires".
- **Application (preacher's):** The verse does not say desires stop. It says the full ocean is not disturbed by rivers. For VL3 this is
  realistic: the practice is not the end of wanting but a fullness that wanting cannot shake.
- **Use:** in the talk; the image.

**BG 2.71**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/71/
> "A person who has given up all desires for sense gratification, who lives free from desires, who has given up all sense of proprietorship and is devoid of false ego – he alone can attain real peace."
- **Teaching:** Giving up "all sense of proprietorship" and false ego is named as a condition of real peace.
- **Application (preacher's):** This is the second point of the formula in negative form.
- **Use:** in the talk, with 2.70.

**BG 2.64–65**: Verified (VL3-02). 2.64: sense control "through regulative principles of freedom" obtains the Lord's mercy; 2.65: for
one so satisfied "the threefold miseries of material existence exist no longer". **Use:** recap; the bridge from VL3-02 to 2.66.

**BG 5.12**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/5/12/
> "The steadily devoted soul attains unadulterated peace because he offers the result of all activities to Me; whereas a person who is not in union with the Divine, who is greedy for the fruits of his labor, becomes entangled."
- **Purport point:** The devotee "has no anxiety over the results of his work"; "one who is entangled in profit calculation for sense
  gratification cannot have that peace"; the platform of peace is the realisation "that there is no existence besides Kṛṣṇa".
- **Teaching:** Peace is tied to *offering the result* of all activities; greed for the fruit entangles.
- **Application (preacher's):** Peace is a practice, not a feeling.
- **Use:** in the talk; the first point as practice.

**BG 2.47**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/47/
> "You have a right to perform your prescribed duty, but you are not entitled to the fruits of action. Never consider yourself the cause of the results of your activities, and never be attached to not doing your duty."
- **Use:** in the talk: the working form of the first point (exam, placement, project).

**BG 9.27**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/9/27/
> "Whatever you do, whatever you eat, whatever you offer or give away, and whatever austerities you perform – do that, O son of Kuntī, as an offering to Me."
- **Use:** in the talk: how the first point is practised at the plate and the desk.

**BG 3.9, 3.12, 3.13**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/9/ (and following)
> 3.9: "Work done as a sacrifice for Viṣṇu has to be performed; otherwise work causes bondage in this material world…"
> 3.12: "…But he who enjoys such gifts without offering them to the demigods in return is certainly a thief."
- **Teaching:** Unoffered enjoyment is theft (3.12); offered eating releases from sin (3.13); work as sacrifice frees (3.9).
- **Application (preacher's):** The "proprietor" point in the Gita's own economics: using what is His without offering is theft, and
  a thief is never at peace. Prasadam (VL3-02) is the formula at the table.
- **Use:** in the talk, briefly; K.4.

**BG 4.31**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/4/31/
> "O best of the Kuru dynasty, without sacrifice one can never live happily on this planet or in this life: what then of the next?"
- **Purport point:** The path of sacrifice "automatically solves our economic problems"; "the highest kind of happiness" is "to be
  promoted to the spiritual planets by practice of Kṛṣṇa consciousness".
- **Use:** background for IYF's "pleasing the Lord leads to prosperity" section (F.6).

**ISO Invocation**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/iso/invocation/
> "The Personality of Godhead is perfect and complete, and because He is completely perfect, all emanations from Him, such as this phenomenal world, are perfectly equipped as complete wholes…"
- **Use:** background: the world is complete; the shortage is in our claim on it (F.3).

**ISO 1**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/iso/1/
> "Everything animate or inanimate that is within the universe is controlled and owned by the Lord. One should therefore accept only those things necessary for himself, which are set aside as his quota, and one should not accept other things, knowing well to whom they belong."
- **Purport point:** The Lord's proprietorship "is confirmed in the Seventh Chapter of the Bhagavad-gītā (7.4–5)"; everything, including
  mind, intelligence and ego, belongs to His energies.
- **Teaching:** Owner, controller, and the practical consequence: take your quota.
- **Application (preacher's):** The second point as a practice: a quota is not a vow of poverty; it is a settled "enough", which is
  where peace about money, marks and possessions begins.
- **Use:** in the talk; the second point as practice.

**BG 6.7**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/7/
> "For one who has conquered the mind, the Supersoul is already reached, for he has attained tranquillity. To such a man happiness and distress, heat and cold, honor and dishonor are all the same."
- **Use:** K.6 (peace and the pairs of opposites); link to VL3-01.

**BG 12.12**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/12/12/
> "If you cannot take to this practice, then engage yourself in the cultivation of knowledge. Better than knowledge, however, is meditation, and better than meditation is renunciation of the fruits of action, for by such renunciation one can attain peace of mind."
- **Use:** K.3: the Gita names "renunciation of the fruits of action" as the direct route to peace of mind.

**BG 18.62**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/62/
> "O scion of Bharata, surrender unto Him utterly. By His grace you will attain transcendental peace and the supreme and eternal abode."
- **Use:** the closing verse.

**BG 4.39**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/4/39/
> "A faithful man who is dedicated to transcendental knowledge and who subdues his senses is eligible to achieve such knowledge, and having achieved it he quickly attains the supreme spiritual peace."
- **Purport point:** This faith "is attained by the discharge of devotional service and by chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa… which cleanses one's
  heart of all material dirt".
- **Application (preacher's):** The purport places chanting inside the path to peace. For VL3, japa is where the three points are
  remembered each morning before the day tests them.
- **Use:** in the talk; the bridge to VL3-04.

**BG 5.25**: Verified. Those "always busy working for the welfare of all living beings" and free from sin "achieve liberation in the
Supreme". **Use:** K.2: the formula's third point (friend of all) makes the devotee a friend of all, not an escapist.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **Why no peace.** BG 2.66 gives the chain: no connection with the Supreme, no steady mind; no steady mind, no peace; no peace, no
   happiness. The purport names the root: "disturbance is due to want of an ultimate goal". IYF's outline opens with the same
   diagnosis ("root cause of our suffering": the soul trying to squeeze happiness from matter). Say it as the Gita's claim about *us*,
   not as a comment on society.
2. **Why desire-chasing cannot work.** BG 2.70: the ocean is full and still while rivers pour in; the man who "strives to satisfy such
   desires" is never still, because the body’s demands "will continue" (purport). Peace is therefore a *fullness*, not an absence of
   wanting. At VL3 this is a relief: no one is asked to stop wanting by Friday.
3. **The formula.** BG 5.29: Krsna is (a) the enjoyer of all sacrifice and austerity, (b) the Lord of all worlds, (c) the friend of all
   beings. The purport calls this "the greatest peace formula". Each point removes one cause of disturbance: (a) the anxiety of the
   doer who must enjoy the result; (b) the anxiety of the owner who must protect and increase; (c) the anxiety of the one who is alone.
   The Isopanisad adds the ground for (b): the world is complete (Invocation) and the shortage is in our claim, so the remedy is to take
   one's quota (ISO 1).
4. **The formula as practice.** This is the VL3 turn: each point has a daily action.
   - *Enjoyer* → **offer.** Whatever you do, eat, give or undergo, do it as an offering (9.27); offer the result and anxiety over
     results goes (5.12); work without claim on the fruit (2.47). At the desk: "I will do this well; the result is His."
   - *Proprietor* → **quota.** Take what is set aside for you and do not grasp the rest (ISO 1); unoffered enjoyment is theft and a
     thief has no peace (3.12). At the plate: prasadam (3.13; VL3-02). With money and marks: a settled "enough".
   - *Friend* → **approach.** The maha-mantra is an address to a person; the morning rounds are where the third point is renewed
     (4.39 purport places chanting on the path to peace). Through the day: "He is the well-wisher of all, including the person
     irritating me" (5.25: the devotee becomes a friend of all).
5. **Why chanting belongs here.** The 4.39 purport says the faith that leads to peace "is attained by the discharge of devotional
   service and by chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa". A VL3 participant already has the instrument; this topic tells them what the instrument
   is tuning: the three facts of 5.29, remembered before the day begins. (Method: VL3-04.)
6. **Prosperity.** IYF's outline ends with "pleasing the Lord leads to prosperity". The Gita's version is 4.31 and its purport:
   sacrifice "automatically solves our economic problems", and "the highest kind of happiness" is promotion to the spiritual planets by Krsna consciousness. Teach it
   as the texts put it (needs met, not wealth promised), and never as a bargain.
7. **Is wanting peace wrong?** IYF's outline asks this and answers with *yukta-vairāgya* versus *phalgu-vairāgya*. At VL3 one sentence:
   the Gita does not condemn wanting peace; it says where peace is (5.29) and that renouncing the *fruit*, not the work, is the route
   (12.12: "by such renunciation one can attain peace of mind"). The full treatment of yukta-vairagya is DPS.
8. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - "Peace means no desires." 2.70 says undisturbed by desires, not without them.
   - "Peace means withdrawing from work." 2.47 and 5.12 keep the work and release the claim; 5.25 keeps the devotee busy for others'
     welfare.
   - "Peace is a mood I should feel after japa." It is knowledge lived (2.66: "transcendental intelligence"), which steadies the mind
     over time; feelings follow unevenly.
   - "If I’m anxious, my chanting is failing." Anxiety has many causes, including medical ones; the formula is not a treatment (K.7).
   - "The three points are controller, proprietor, enjoyer." Two of those overlap; the verse's third point is *friend*. Keep it.
9. **Pitch for VL3.** Practical, encouraging. One Sanskrit phrase (*suhṛdaṁ sarva-bhūtānām*), defined. Every participant leaves with a
   three-line formula they can say under pressure and one disturbance of their own week diagnosed against it.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet and say the session is practical: we will use one verse as a tool, not as a promise of a trouble-free life.
2. Ask H.2 question 1; write three one-word answers on the board without comment.
3. Thank each person by name; do not ask anyone to explain.
4. Tell them that by the end each word will go under one of three headings.
5. Give the transition line and begin with the chain of BG 2.66.

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the group's own lives)
1. Name one thing that took your peace this week, in one word.
2. When you got something you had worked for, how long did the calm last?
3. Who is the person whose presence makes you feel steadier, and what do they do?

- **Opening question:** "Name one thing that took your peace this week. One word." Write three on the board without comment. "By the

## I. Core teaching points

1. **No connection, no peace.** BG 2.66. *Example:* a phone with no network keeps searching and drains itself. *Transition:* so get
   more of what we want?
2. **Full, not emptied.** BG 2.70–71. *Example:* the ocean during the monsoon. *Transition:* what fills it?
3. **He enjoys.** BG 5.29 (first point); 9.27; 5.12; 2.47. *Example:* the project done well and handed over: the worker sleeps.
   *Transition:* and what about what I own?
4. **He owns.** BG 5.29 (second point); ISO Invocation and 1; 3.12. *Example:* a guest in a host's house uses what is offered and does
   not count the silverware. *Transition:* a formula with two points is cold.
5. **He is the friend of all.** BG 5.29 (third point); 5.25. *Example:* the maha-mantra is addressed to someone; the morning rounds are
   where the friendship is renewed. *Transition:* so the practice and the formula meet.
6. **Chanting carries the formula.** BG 4.39 purport. *Transition:* to the diagnosis (G) and the practice (P).

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"Isn’t wanting peace for myself selfish?"**
   - The Gita assumes you want it and tells you where it is (5.29; 2.66). The third point makes the peaceful person a friend of all,
     "always busy working for the welfare of all living beings" (5.25). Peace sought this way is not private.
2. **"Why do I need God for peace? Meditation apps give me calm."**
   - Calm and peace differ in the Gita: peace needs "transcendental intelligence" and "a steady mind" (2.66), that is, knowledge of
     who enjoys, who owns and who is my friend. Calm without that knowledge returns to disturbance when the next river comes (2.70).
     Say it as the Gita's claim and let them test it.
3. **"Renunciation of the fruits of action: does that mean I shouldn’t aim for a good result?"**
   - No. 2.47 says you have a right to the work; 5.12 says offer the result. Aim high, work fully, release the claim. 12.12 names
     exactly this as the route to "peace of mind".
4. **"How is eating involved in peace?"**
   - 3.12–13: enjoying without offering is theft, and the eater of unoffered food "eats only sin"; offered food releases. A thief is
     never at peace. Prasadam (VL3-02) is the formula at the table.
5. **"The outline says 'controller, proprietor, enjoyer'. You say 'friend'. Which is right?"**
   - The verse says *bhoktāram* (enjoyer), *sarva-loka-maheśvaram* (Lord of all worlds) and *suhṛdaṁ sarva-bhūtānām* (friend of all
     beings); Prabhupada's 2.66 purport summarises "enjoyer, proprietor and friend". Controller and proprietor are one point. Keep
     friend; it is the warm one.
6. **"What about things I can’t control: illness, a death, a failed exam?"**
   - 6.7: for the one whose mind is conquered, "happiness and distress, heat and cold, honor and dishonor are all the same". That is
     the goal, reached by practice (VL3-01), not demanded today. Meanwhile the third point: the friend of all beings is present in the
     hard thing too. *Application (preacher's)*; do not promise outcomes.
7. **"I chant and I’m still anxious. Is something wrong with me?"**
   - Nothing is wrong with you. 2.70 says desires and demands continue while the body does; steadiness grows. Anxiety can also have
     medical causes; professional help is compatible with spiritual life, and the free national helpline Tele-MANAS is 14416. Keep
     chanting, and tell your facilitator. (Pastoral guidance, not scripture.)
8. **"Does pleasing the Lord really make me prosperous?"**
   - 4.31 and its purport: sacrifice solves the economic problem (needs are met), and the highest happiness is promotion to
     the spiritual world by Krsna consciousness. The Gita does not promise wealth; it promises provision and peace. Never teach it as a bargain.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: Results night.** Ananya has done everything for her exam and now cannot sleep. She has chanted four rounds daily for six
months and is disappointed that the practice "isn’t working".
- **Principles:** 2.47 (right to the work, not the fruit); 5.12 (offer the result; "no anxiety over the results"); 5.29 (enjoyer).
- **Questions:** Which point of the formula is she holding on to? What would "offering the result" look like tonight in words? Is her
  practice failing, or is it not yet applied here?
- **Takeaway:** The rounds built the steadiness; the formula tells her what to do with the exam.

**Case 2: The sibling's success.** Rahul's younger brother got the placement Rahul wanted. Rahul is happy for him and also cannot
stop comparing; his japa this week has been full of it.
- **Principles:** ISO 1 (quota; "knowing well to whom they belong"); 2.71 ("sense of proprietorship"); 5.29 (friend of all, including
  the brother).
- **Questions:** Which point is disturbed: enjoyer, proprietor or friend? What is Rahul's quota this year? How does BG 6.26 (VL3-01)
  apply to the comparing thoughts during japa?
- **Takeaway:** Comparison is a proprietorship problem; the quota and the friend settle it.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Srila Prabhupada called one verse of the Gita "the greatest peace formula": Krsna is the enjoyer of all our sacrifice and work, the owner of all worlds, and the friend of every living being (BG 5.29). The Gita says that without this connection the mind cannot be steady, without a steady mind there is no peace, and without peace no happiness (BG 2.66). Peace does not come from getting what we want: the person who keeps chasing desires never arrives, while the one who is full is like an ocean that stays still as rivers pour in (BG 2.70).

The formula becomes practice in three ways. Offer your work and its result: you have a right to the work but not to its fruits (BG 2.47; 9.27; 5.12), and renouncing the fruit, rather than the work, gives peace of mind and is better than meditation (BG 12.12). Take your quota and no more: ISO 1 says to accept only what is necessary, set aside as your quota, and eating without offering is theft while offered food releases (BG 3.12–13). Approach Krsna as a friend each morning in your chanting rounds (BG 4.39 purport).

When something disturbs you, ask which of the three points you forgot. Calm from an app is not the Gita's peace; steadiness grows with practice, and anxiety that persists can also be helped by a doctor or counsellor alongside spiritual life.

## S2. Key ideas

- Three points: He enjoys, He owns, He is everyone's friend (BG 5.29).
- No connection, no steady mind; no steady mind, no peace; no peace, no happiness (BG 2.66).
- Peace is fullness, not the end of wanting: the ocean and the rivers (BG 2.70).
- Offer the result and anxiety over results goes (BG 5.12; 2.47).
- Take your quota; unoffered enjoyment is theft (ISO 1; BG 3.12).
- Chanting is where the friendship is renewed each day (BG 4.39 purport).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **BG 5.29:** knowing Krsna as "the ultimate beneficiary of all sacrifices and austerities, the Supreme Lord of all planets and demigods, and the benefactor and well-wisher of all living entities", one "attains peace". *For me:* three facts to remember under pressure.
- **BG 2.66:** "…without which there is no possibility of peace. And how can there be any happiness without peace?" *For me:* peace first, then happiness.
- **BG 2.70:** desires "enter like rivers into the ocean, which is ever being filled but is always still". *For me:* I don't have to stop wanting to be still.
- **BG 2.47:** "You have a right to perform your prescribed duty, but you are not entitled to the fruits of action." *For me:* work fully, release the claim.

## S4. Words to know

- **Peace formula:** Srila Prabhupada's name for BG 5.29 (purport).
- **Bhoktā:** the enjoyer; in 5.29, Krsna as the enjoyer of all sacrifice.
- **Suhṛd:** a well-wishing friend; *suhṛdaṁ sarva-bhūtānām*, the friend of all living beings.
- **Quota:** what is set aside for me; taking only that is the Isopanisad's instruction (ISO 1).
- **Yajña (sacrifice):** work and offering done for the Lord, not for oneself (BG 3.9).

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Isn't wanting peace selfish?** The Gita tells you where it is (BG 5.29) and makes the peaceful person a friend of all, busy for others' welfare (BG 5.25).
2. **Why do I need God for peace? Apps calm me.** Calm is not the Gita's peace, which needs a steady mind founded on knowledge (BG 2.66). Calm without knowledge is disturbed by the next river (BG 2.70). Test it.
3. **Should I stop aiming for good results?** No. Do the work fully; offer the result (BG 2.47; 5.12). Renouncing the *fruit*, not the work, gives peace of mind (BG 12.12).
4. **What does food have to do with peace?** Enjoying without offering is theft (BG 3.12); offered food releases (BG 3.13). A thief is never at peace. Prasadam is the formula at the table.
5. **I chant and I'm still anxious. Is something wrong with me?** No. Desires and demands continue while the body does (BG 2.70); steadiness grows with practice. Anxiety can also have medical causes; professional help goes well with spiritual life (Tele-MANAS: 14416). Keep chanting and tell your facilitator.
6. **Does pleasing the Lord make me rich?** The Gita promises provision and peace through sacrifice (BG 4.31), not wealth. Never treat it as a bargain.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. The three points of BG 5.29 are that Krsna is: (a) creator, judge, king; (b) enjoyer, proprietor, friend; (c) teacher, father, guide.
2. Complete the chain of BG 2.66: no connection → no steady mind → no ___ → no ___.
3. In BG 2.70, what happens to the ocean when rivers flow in?
4. Which verse says you have a right to your work but not to its fruits?
5. What does ISO 1 tell you to accept?
6. True or false: BG 12.12 says meditation is the best route to peace of mind.

**Answers:** 1. (b) (BG 5.29). 2. No peace → no happiness (BG 2.66). 3. It is "ever being filled but is always still" (BG 2.70). 4. BG 2.47. 5. Only
what is necessary, "set aside as his quota" (ISO 1). 6. False: "better than meditation is renunciation of the fruits of action, for by such renunciation one
can attain peace of mind" (BG 12.12).
