# Dhruva's determination

> **BSS · 2 hours · review: drafted.** Builds on bss-01 (renunciation is the fruit of attachment). This page is about the *engine*: determination, and how Krishna purifies its motive.

## A. Topic overview

| | |
|---|---|
| Batch / level | BSS |
| Suggested duration | 120 minutes |
| Preparation time | About 2 hours |
| Difficulty | Intermediate |
| Prerequisites | bss-01 |
| Audience | Aspiring and practising brahmacaris |
| Teaching objective | Participants see how a determined start, even with a mixed motive, becomes pure when anchored in the Lord's service, and how to build determination that survives their own moods |

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

A brahmacari's vow is only as strong as his determination on a flat, ordinary day. Dhruva is the scriptural example of resolve in a child and of
a mixed motive being corrected by the Lord Himself, which is the real situation of most of us.

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| VL1 | Skipped or told as a short story of "a child who found God" |
| VL2 | Why a resolute search ends in the Lord (reasoning) |
| VL3 | Determination in sadhana (NOI 3) |
| SPS | Perseverance in daily standards |
| DPS | Determination while carrying responsibilities |
| BSS | This page: motive, guidance, shelter, and the end of the wish |

**Not done here:** another telling of renunciation as a life-pattern (bss-01), obstacles from women and the senses (bss-03 onwards), or Prahlada's preaching (bss-06).

## C. Central message

Dhruva began with a wish for a throne and ended by asking for nothing. Determination did not carry him alone: it
was guided by his mother and by Narada, and finished by the Lord's presence. We ask for the same: a firm start and guidance that makes the motive pure.

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants will be able to:
1. Tell Dhruva's story in order and identify the guides who helped him.
2. Say, from BG 18.33-35, what makes determination sattvic, rajasic or tamasic, and where Dhruva began and ended.
3. Explain SB 4.9.35 and why honesty about our own motives is part of devotion.
4. Name the six principles of NOI 3 and apply two to a brahmacari week.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**SB 4.8.12** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/8/12/
> "My dear child, you are unaware that you were born not of my womb but of another woman."
- Context: Queen Suruci's words to the boy Dhruva, who wanted to sit on his father's lap.
- Teaching: The wound that begins his journey. The text names no blame of Suruci as a person; it presents the event.
- Use: in the talk (briefly; avoid dwelling on her)

**SB 4.8.22** — Verified
> "My dear boy, you also should take shelter of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, who is very kind to His devotees."
- Context: Dhruva's mother Suniti answers, pointing him to the Lord.
- Purport point: the instruction was given with the full intention of making the child take shelter (the translation of 4.8.24 confirms the intent).
- Application (preacher's): A mother's one sentence set a life's direction; our words to younger devotees count.
- Use: in the talk

**SB 4.8.24** — Verified
> "after deliberate consideration and with intelligence and fixed determination, he left his father’s house."
- Teaching: His resolve is described as deliberate, not impulsive.
- Use: in the talk

**SB 4.8.32-33, 36** — Verified
> "For this reason, my dear boy, you should not endeavor for this; it will not be successful."
- Context: Narada tests the child, advising him to go home and wait. The advice continues: "One should try to keep himself satisfied in any condition of life" (4.8.33).
> "My stepmother, Suruci, has pierced my heart with her harsh words."
- Teaching: Dhruva is honest that his motive is hurt (4.8.36). Narada does not reject him; he trains him.
- Application (preacher's): A guide receives honest motives, and does not require a perfect motive at the start.
- Use: in the talk

**SB 4.9.11** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/9/11/
> "kindly bless me so that I may associate with great devotees who engage in Your transcendental loving service constantly"
- Context: Dhruva's first request after seeing the Lord; also quoted in the BIKC (Bhakti Vikasa Swami) on the need for association.
- Teaching: He asks for association, not for the kingdom.
- Use: in the talk

**SB 4.9.17** — Verified
> "worshiping Your lotus feet is better than becoming king and lording it over a kingdom."
- Teaching: The result is more than the wish: devotion for its own sake.
- Use: in the talk

**SB 4.9.29** — Verified
> "Dhruva Mahārāja’s heart, which was pierced by the arrows of the harsh words of his stepmother, was greatly aggrieved"
- Context: Maitreya states Dhruva did not forget the wound, even at the Lord's appearance, and that he was ashamed of the material wish.
- Use: background for the preacher

**SB 4.9.35** — Verified
> "although the Lord offered me His personal service, I wanted material name, fame and prosperity."
- Teaching: Dhruva himself calls the first wish a lapse, like asking an emperor for a few broken grains of rice.
- Application (preacher's): The right response to a mixed motive is neither pretence nor shame, but turning it to the Lord.
- Use: in the talk

**BG 18.33-35** — Verified
> "O son of Pṛthā, that determination which is unbreakable, which is sustained with steadfastness by yoga practice, and which thus controls the activities of the mind, life and senses is determination in the mode of goodness."
> "But that determination by which one holds fast to fruitive results in religion, economic development and sense gratification is of the nature of passion, O Arjuna."
- Teaching: Determination has a mode: the goal decides. Dhruva began near the mode of passion (a throne) and was lifted to goodness and beyond by the Lord's company. (BG 18.35 describes the dark kind: dreaming, fear, lamentation.)
- Use: in the talk

**NOI 3** — Verified
> "There are six principles favorable to the execution of pure devotional service: (1) being enthusiastic, (2) endeavoring with confidence, (3) being patient"
- Teaching: Determination is not one burst; the list continues with regulation, avoiding nondevotees' association, and following the acaryas.
- Use: in the talk (last part)

## F. Philosophical explanation

**Principle.** The goal and the shelter decide the quality of determination (BG 18.33-34); the Lord corrects a mixed motive as the devotee proceeds (SB 4.9.29, 4.9.35).

**Why it matters.** A brahmacari often waits for pure motives before committing. Dhruva shows that the commitment, with guidance and shelter, purifies.

**Consequences.** (1) Honest speech to guides about what we actually want. (2) Daily fixed practices (NOI 3) rather than an emotional peak. (3) Seeking association (SB 4.9.11) as the first demand.

**Misunderstandings.**
1. "A material wish spoils the path." It does not cancel it; the Lord's presence lifts the motive (4.9.17).
2. "Resolve is willpower." In the text it is first fed by hurt, then guided by Narada, then answered by the Lord: it is a relationship.
3. "The five-year-old did it alone." Suniti and Narada both appear (4.8.22; 4.8.32).

**Pitch.** Speak to hurt feelings and ambition without approval or judgment; show the Lord answering what the boy really needed.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start.** Say: "Think of a resolution you made and kept, and one you made and lost. Do not share it, just hold it." Pause. Then: "Today a five-year-old teaches us how a resolution ends in the Lord's own presence." Write on the board: *What kept it? What broke it?*

**H.2 Opening questions.**
1. What is the smallest daily promise you have kept for more than a month?
2. When you decided something at a low moment, did the decision survive when you felt better?
3. Who helped you keep a promise?

## I. Core teaching points

1. **A wound can start a quest** (SB 4.8.12, 4.8.36). Dhruva did not pretend. Transition: "Where did the wound lead him?"
2. **Guidance before technique** (SB 4.8.22; Narada's test, 4.8.32). Suniti and Narada first; then the mantra and the forest.
3. **Determination has a mode** (BG 18.33-35). The goal decides.
4. **Persistence in a fixed place and routine** (NOI 3). The text gives a structured regimen (not quoted here; see SB 4.8.42 onward in Prabhupada's translation; *Requires source review* for details).
5. **The Lord answers more than the wish** (SB 4.9.17, 4.9.35). The final prayer is for association (4.9.11).

## J. Stories and illustrations

*Source: SB 4.8-9 and the story index (data/stories.json), key verse SB 4.9.17.*

**J.1 The insult.** SB 4.8.12 (above): Dhruva's father's queen speaks harshly to him.
**J.2 The mother's answer.** SB 4.8.22-24: Suniti points to the Lord; Dhruva leaves with deliberate resolve.
**J.3 Narada's test.** Narada appears and advises patience (4.8.32-33). Dhruva answers honestly (4.8.36). Narada then initiates him (the mantra is in SB 4.8; not quoted here).
**J.4 Austerity in the forest.** The story index summarises severe austerity and the Lord's appearance (SB 4.9). *Requires source review* for the sequence of months.
**J.5 The boon.** "Free of desire, he is fully satisfied": SB 4.9.17 and the key lines 4.9.29-35.
**Devotional lesson.** The Lord does not wait for pure motives; He gives Himself to the sincere child and then raises the desire. Our task is to begin and stay near the Lord's devotees.
**Illustration (preacher's).** A student who reaches a hard exam through fixed morning study shows what Dhruva's routine was meant to do; the point is the object of determination: here, chanting at a fixed hour, with a senior to answer to.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"Dhruva wanted a kingdom. Is his example pure?"** He himself says it was a lapse (4.9.35); the Lord forgave and perfected him. Chaitanya Charan has a talk on exactly this question (O).
2. **"I made vows and broke them."** The text invites a fresh start in the Lord's company; talk to your guide and increase chanting and hearing. If your low mood is heavy and lasting, also speak to a health professional (Tele-MANAS 14416 in India, or your local service).
3. **"Can a child be so determined?"** The text presents it as a special case; the lesson is the process.
4. **"Is determination just willpower?"** See F.2.
5. **"What if my guide says wait, like Narada?"** Dhruva was tested and then accepted; guidance includes the test.

## L. Case studies

**Invented case for discussion.** Kunal, 20, begins strongly: 16 rounds, early rising, no entertainment. After six weeks a harsh comment from a senior hurts him; he stops chanting for three days and feels it proves he is unfit.
- Principles: SB 4.8.36 (honest hurt), SB 4.8.22 (shelter), NOI 3 (patience and enthusiasm).
- Questions: Who is Kunal's Suniti? What would Narada say? What is the first action?
- Analysis: He returns to chanting (even if few rounds first), tells the senior or his guide about the hurt, and asks for association.
- Takeaway: hurt is not disqualification; take it to the name and to devotees.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check (answers).**
1. What did Suniti advise? *Take shelter of the Lord (4.8.22).*
2. How did Narada respond to Dhruva's resolve? *Tested it (4.8.32), then trained him.*
3. What are the three kinds of determination? *Goodness, passion, ignorance (BG 18.33-35).*
4. What did Dhruva ask in SB 4.9.11? *Association with devotees.*
5. What did he say about his earlier wish (4.9.35)? *It was foolish, like asking only broken grains.*
6. Name two principles of NOI 3. *E.g., enthusiasm, patience.*

**M.2 Activities.**
- **My six-line plan (15 min, pairs).** Choose three of NOI 3's six principles; write one concrete action each for this week (e.g., enthusiasm: join the morning kirtan five days; patience: a missed round is made up the same day, not abandoned). Partner asks: "Does this lead you to the Lord?"
- **Role-play: Narada and Dhruva (10 min, threes).** Roles: Dhruva, Narada, observer. Dhruva states a real wish honestly; Narada guides him toward the Lord, using the verses in E. Observer notes one respectful line. No private disclosure; use an invented wish.

**M.3 Reflection.** "What am I really asking for from Krishna? Could I ask for association?"

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Dhruva was a small boy who was hurt by his stepmother's harsh words. His mother, Suniti, advised him to take shelter of the Lord (SB 4.8.22). He left home with deliberate resolve (SB 4.8.24). The sage Narada tested him by advising him to go home and wait (SB 4.8.32), but Dhruva honestly said his heart was pierced and the advice did not stay (SB 4.8.36). Narada then trained him in worship.

When the Lord appeared, Dhruva asked for the association of devotees (SB 4.9.11) and said that worshipping the Lord's feet is better than a kingdom (SB 4.9.17). He later felt ashamed that he first wanted name, fame and prosperity (SB 4.9.35). The Lord had answered more than his wish.

For us: be honest about what we want, accept guidance, keep a steady practice (NOI 3), and ask the Lord for devotee association.

## S2. Key ideas
- A hurt can start a search; the Lord can raise the motive.
- Determination is of three kinds, set by the goal (BG 18.33-35).
- Guidance and shelter come before technique.
- Steady practice (NOI 3) keeps determination alive.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **SB 4.8.22** — take shelter of the Lord. For me: I start by going to Him.
- **SB 4.9.17** — worship of His feet is better than a kingdom. For me: devotion is the real prize.
- **NOI 3** — enthusiasm, confidence, patience, regulation, avoiding bad association, following the acaryas. For me: choose two this week.

## S4. Words to know
- *Sattva, rajas, tamas*: goodness, passion, ignorance.
- *Determination (dhrti)*: firm resolve.
- *Narada Muni*: the Lord's sage who guides devotees.
- *Regulative principles*: the fixed practices of devotional life.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. **Was Dhruva's first wish wrong?** He himself called it foolish (4.9.35); the Lord raised it.
2. **I broke my vows. Is it over?** No: restart with chanting, honesty and association.
3. **Why did Narada say go home?** He tested and trained (4.8.32).
4. **What is the best determination?** One in goodness, fixed on the Lord (BG 18.33).

## S6. Check your understanding
1. Who advised Dhruva to take shelter of the Lord?
2. What did Dhruva ask the Lord in SB 4.9.11?
3. Which mode of determination holds to fruitive results?
4. What did Dhruva say about his first wish?
5. Name one principle of NOI 3.

**Answers:** 1. Suniti (4.8.22). 2. Association with devotees. 3. Passion (BG 18.34). 4. It was like asking an emperor for broken grains (4.9.35). 5. E.g., enthusiasm.

Choose one fixed practice (for example, japa at a fixed hour) and tell a senior. Private reflection: "What do I most want from Krishna?"
