# Mind your mind

> **VL1 · 45–60 min · review: sources checked, awaiting senior review.** Scripture light but exact. IYF's slides (a "Mind your mind" seminar for
> NIT Rourkela students, VOICE) carry two formulas: ESCAPE and the ABC of "internal body-building". The
> ESCAPE tips overlap VL1-11 "Eight practical tips for positive thinking" and are only pointed to here; this
> talk takes the nature of the mind and the ABC.

## A. Topic overview

| | |
|---|---|
| Batch / level | VL1 (Vedic Level 1), a stand-alone talk |
| Suggested duration | 45–60 min (G gives a 30-minute cut) |
| Preparation time | About 90 min, the first time |
| Difficulty | Introductory |
| Prerequisites | None (pairs with VL1-11; VL1-17 and VL1-18 build on it) |
| Audience | Students and young professionals who already know their mind sabotages them (procrastination, scrolling, overthinking) and want a working model, not a sermon |
| Teaching objective | Give the Gita's model of the mind (friend or enemy; reins between intelligence and senses), show why willpower alone fails, and offer three concrete supports (association, books, chanting) the listener can try |

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on vl1-15: "higher taste" (BG 2.59) is there the reason lesser habits fade; here it is applied to the mind and its restlessness. Do not retell the ledger.

Everyone in the room has lost an argument with their own mind this week. The subject needs no selling; what
it needs is a model that explains the experience and a method that is not "just try harder". The Gita's sixth
chapter is one of the oldest, clearest treatments of exactly this. The talk stays experiential and practical.

How the subject grows at other levels:

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **VL1** (here) | The mind as friend or enemy; the chariot model; why suppression fails; ABC supports |
| VL1-11 | The ESCAPE-type tips (expectations, comparison, gratitude, principles, endurance) |
| VL3 | The method in depth: chanting as the practice that engages the mind (VL3-04) |
| VL2 | The mind, senses and self argued philosophically: what the mind is and why the self is not the mind |
| SPS | Tolerating the urges of mind and speech (NOI 1) as a devotee's discipline; the six urges |
| DPS | Helping others steady their minds without pressure |
| BSS | Daily routine as the training of the mind and senses |

**Not done here:**
- A full theory of the subtle body (mind, intelligence, false ego) beyond one simple diagram.
- Claims about brainwaves, EEG and meditation (on IYF's slides; unsourced, left out).
- Testimonials from named executives about chanting (on IYF's slides; unverified, left out).

## C. Central message

Your mind can be your best friend or your worst enemy, and the difference is not willpower alone: it is
whether the intelligence holds the reins and whether the mind is given something better to hold on to.

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. Explain the Gita's claim that the mind is both friend and enemy (BG 6.5–6) with one example from their
   own week.
2. Describe the chariot model (SB 7.15.41): senses as horses, mind as reins, intelligence as driver, self as
   passenger, and say what goes wrong when the reins are loose.
3. Explain why suppression alone fails and what replaces it (BG 2.59, 6.35).
4. Name the three supports (association, books, chanting) and choose one to try.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** BG 6.5–6, 6.34–35 and SB 7.15.41 carry the talk. The rest prepare you for questions.

**BG 6.5**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/5/
> "One must deliver himself with the help of his mind, and not degrade himself. The mind is the friend of the conditioned soul, and his enemy as well."
- **Context:** Chapter 6, the opening of Krsna's teaching on controlling the mind.
- **Teaching:** The same mind can lift or sink us.
- **Purport point:** The mind "should be trained so that it will not be attracted by the glitter of material
  nature"; Prabhupada cites the Amrta-bindu Upanisad: the mind is the cause of bondage and of liberation.
- **Application (preacher's):** Nobody needs a different mind; they need the one they have trained and
  engaged.
- **Use:** in the talk (frame).

**BG 6.6**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/6/
> "For him who has conquered the mind, the mind is the best of friends; but for one who has failed to do so, his mind will remain the greatest enemy."
- **Purport point:** An unconquered mind means one "has to serve the dictations of lust, anger, avarice,
  illusion, etc."
- **Application (preacher's):** Ask: "Who is in charge in your head at 11 p.m. before a deadline?"
- **Use:** in the talk (frame).

**BG 6.34**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/34/
> "The mind is restless, turbulent, obstinate and very strong, O Kṛṣṇa, and to subdue it, I think, is more difficult than controlling the wind."
- **Context:** Arjuna's honest objection after hearing the method of meditation.
- **Teaching:** Even a great man finds the mind nearly impossible to control.
- **Purport point:** "The mind is so strong and obstinate that it sometimes overcomes the intelligence." The
  purport gives the Katha Upanisad image: body as chariot, intelligence as driver, mind as reins, senses as
  horses. The remedy recommended is to absorb the mind in Krsna, especially by chanting the holy name.
- **Application (preacher's):** Arjuna's complaint is the room's complaint. Scripture does not pretend the
  mind is easy.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 6.35**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/35/
> "…it is undoubtedly very difficult to curb the restless mind, but it is possible by suitable practice and by detachment."
- **Teaching:** Difficult, yes; impossible, no. Two means: practice and detachment.
- **Purport point:** "The first and foremost of such devotional engagements is hearing about Kṛṣṇa." "The
  more one hears about Kṛṣṇa, the more one becomes enlightened and detached." Hearing is compared to expert
  treatment for the "mad mind" and prasadam to the proper diet.
- **Application (preacher's):** "Practice" here is not gritting your teeth; it is giving the mind something
  to hear and hold.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 7.15.41**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/15/41/
> "…compare the body… to a chariot. The senses are like the horses; the mind, the master of the senses, is like the reins; the objects of the senses are the destinations; intelligence is the chariot driver…"
- **Context:** Narada Muni instructs Maharaja Yudhisthira.
- **Teaching:** The chariot model of the person: horses (senses), reins (mind), driver (intelligence),
  destinations (sense objects).
- **Application (preacher's):** The model explains the week: strong horses, slack reins, a sleepy driver. The
  fix is to wake the driver and firm the reins, not to shoot the horses.
- **Use:** in the talk (with N).

**BG 3.42**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/42/
> "The working senses are superior to dull matter; mind is higher than the senses; intelligence is still higher than the mind; and he [the soul] is even higher than the intelligence."
- **Teaching:** The hierarchy: matter < senses < mind < intelligence < self.
- **Use:** in the talk, one line, to label the diagram.

**BG 2.62–63**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/62/ · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/63/
> 2.62: "While contemplating the objects of the senses, a person develops attachment for them, and from such attachment lust develops, and from lust anger arises."
- **Teaching:** The chain: contemplation → attachment → desire → anger → delusion → loss of memory → loss of
  intelligence → fall.
- **Purport point (2.62):** The senses "will certainly seek engagement in the service of materialism" if not
  engaged otherwise; one who represses artificially finds "the slightest thought of sense pleasure will agitate
  him". (2.63): in Krsna consciousness "everything becomes spiritualized, and there is no danger of a downfall."
- **Application (preacher's):** The chain starts with *contemplation*, which is where the mind is. Mind
  management is upstream of behaviour management.
- **Use:** in the talk. (VL1-17 "Overcoming anger" takes the same chain further.)

**BG 2.67**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/67/
> "As a strong wind sweeps away a boat on the water, even one of the roaming senses on which the mind focuses can carry away a man's intelligence."
- **Teaching:** One unguarded sense, with the mind on it, can carry off the intelligence.
- **Use:** in the talk (the boat image; one line).

**BG 6.26**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/26/
> "From wherever the mind wanders due to its flickering and unsteady nature, one must certainly withdraw it and bring it back under the control of the Self."
- **Teaching:** The practice is bringing the mind back, again and again.
- **Application (preacher's):** Every wander-and-return is one repetition of the exercise, not a failure.
- **Use:** in the talk and P.

**BG 2.59**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/59/
> "Though the embodied soul may be restricted from sense enjoyment, the taste for sense objects remains. But, ceasing such engagements by experiencing a higher taste, he is fixed in consciousness."
- **Teaching:** Restriction alone leaves the taste; a higher taste changes it.
- **Use:** in the talk (why suppression fails).

**NOI 1**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/1/
> "A sober person who can tolerate the urge to speak, the mind's demands, the actions of anger and the urges of the tongue, belly and genitals is qualified to make disciples all over the world."
- **Purport point:** "The restlessness or fickleness of the mind (mano-vega) is controlled when one can fix
  his mind on the lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa."
- **Use:** background; the six urges are SPS/DPS material. At VL1 mention only that the tradition lists
  "the mind's demands" among the urges a sober person learns to tolerate.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** The mind is an instrument, not the self (BG 3.42). Trained and engaged, it is the
   best friend; left to itself, the worst enemy (BG 6.5–6). Its control is hard (6.34) but possible by
   practice and detachment (6.35). Detachment comes by higher taste (2.59), not by suppression; practice means
   giving the mind something to hear and hold (6.35 purport) and bringing it back each time it strays (6.26).
2. **Why it matters.** Most "self-improvement" aims at behaviour. The Gita goes upstream: the chain to
   downfall begins with *contemplation* (2.62). Change what the mind dwells on and behaviour follows.
3. **Connections.** The chain of 2.62–63 is the engine of VL1-17 (anger). The chariot (SB 7.15.41) is the
   model for VL1-18 (fear) and VL3's chanting. The ESCAPE tips of IYF's seminar are VL1-11.
4. **Consequences.** A person who sees the mind as reins, not as self, can notice "my mind wants X" without
   obeying; can treat each wander-and-return as practice; and stops blaming themselves for having a restless
   mind, since Arjuna had one too.
5. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - **"Control the mind = suppress everything."** BG 2.59 and the 2.62 purport say suppression without a
     higher taste fails and backfires.
   - **"I am my mind."** The self is higher than the intelligence, which is higher than the mind (3.42).
     Thoughts are not commands.
   - **"A restless mind means I'm spiritually hopeless."** Arjuna said the same (6.34); Krsna agreed it is
     hard and then said it is possible (6.35).
   - **"Chanting works because of brainwaves."** IYF's slides make EEG claims; they are unsourced. Offer
     chanting as a practice to test, not as neuroscience.
6. **Pitch for VL1.** Use "mind", "intelligence", "senses" in plain English; the one Sanskrit term worth
   using is *mantra* ("that which delivers the mind", IYF's gloss, as a traditional etymology). No Sanskrit
   verse recitation is needed.

**Alternative structure (workshop):** give each small group one everyday scenario (exam night or a deadline, a
notification mid-study, a comment that stings) and ask them to map it onto the chariot: which horse bolted,
where were the reins, what was the driver doing? Debrief with BG 6.5–6 and 6.35, then the ABC.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet, say your name, and say today's topic is something everyone already struggles with.
2. Ask the "hands up" question below and thank the room for honesty; laugh with them, not at them.
3. Ask H.2 question 1; take two or three answers.
4. Say what "ABC" will mean later: three supports for the mind (association, books, chanting). Do not explain yet.
5. Give the transition and begin with friend or enemy.

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the room's own life)
1. What was the last thing your mind pulled you towards when you meant to do something else?
2. What helps you settle when your mind is restless (music, a walk, a friend)?
3. Which would you rather have: a stronger will or a calmer mind? Why?

- **Opening question:** "Hands up if, this week, you decided to do one thing and your mind made you do
  another." Pause. "Then the question isn't whether your mind needs managing. It's how."
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** Aditi sits down to study at 9 p.m. with a plan. At 9:04 a notification
  arrives. At 10:30 she has watched eleven videos and feels terrible. Who decided?
- **Transition:** "Arjuna told Krsna the mind was harder to control than the wind. Krsna did not disagree.
  Then he said it was possible, and how."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** Grades, careers and relationships are decided less by what we know than
  by whether we can make ourselves do what we have decided. (Adapt the example: Aditi's study night can be any task and any distraction: a work deadline, a game, social media.)

## I. Core teaching points

1. **The mind is both friend and enemy.** The same instrument (BG 6.5–6). *Example:* a knife in a surgeon's
   hand and in a criminal's (IYF's analogy). *Transition:* so what decides which it is?
2. **Who holds the reins.** Senses are horses, mind the reins, intelligence the driver, the self the
   passenger (SB 7.15.41; BG 3.42). *Example:* Aditi's evening: the eyes bolted, the reins were slack, the
   driver dozed. *Transition:* is it realistic to hold those reins?
3. **It is hard; scripture says so.** Arjuna: harder than the wind (BG 6.34). Krsna: hard, but possible by
   practice and detachment (6.35). *Transition:* but not the practice you think.
4. **Suppression alone fails.** The taste remains (BG 2.59); artificial repression is agitated by the
   slightest thought (2.62 purport); one unguarded sense can carry off the intelligence (2.67). *Transition:*
   so give the mind something better.
5. **Three supports: association, books, chanting.** Who you spend time with shapes what the mind dwells on
   (2.62: contemplation is the first link); good books feed the driver; chanting gives the mind a sound to
   return to, and "hearing about Krsna" is the first of the practices the purport recommends (6.35). Each
   return is the exercise (6.26). *Transition:* to the one practice.

## J. Stories and illustrations

- **Arjuna's complaint** (BG 6.33–35). Keep to the text: Arjuna says the mind is restless, turbulent, obstinate,
  strong, harder than the wind; Krsna agrees it is difficult and names practice and detachment. *Connection:*
  honesty about difficulty, then a method.
- **The chariot** (SB 7.15.41; also in the BG 6.34 purport from the Katha Upanisad). Draw it; it is the talk's
  spine.
- **The knife** (IYF's slides): the same blade heals or harms depending on the hand. *Contemporary analogy,
  not scripture.*
- **The monkey and the restless child** (IYF's slides): traditional comparisons for the mind, used by many
  teachers. Label them as comparisons, not citations.
- **The husband who says "hmm"** (Preaching Encyclopedia, Holy Name, "Understanding the nature of mind", VOICE; the
  speaker credits the example to HH Sacinandana Swami): a man comes home, opens the newspaper and answers his wife's
  account of the day with "hmm… hmm"; later he cannot repeat a word of it. The passage uses it for chanting with the
  mind elsewhere: the mouth chants while the mind says "hmm" and attends to what it has decided is more important. At
  VL1 use it for the mind's habit of switching off from whatever it has filed as unimportant; everyone recognises it.
  A teaching illustration, not scripture.
- **Left out from IYF's slides:** brainwave/EEG frequency claims about mantra meditation; testimonials
  attributed to named business executives and a professor; WHO and war-death statistics; an Einstein
  quotation ("problems cannot be solved at the same level…", commonly misattributed); an Emerson quotation
  on the Gita (real but not needed; **requires source review** if used). An Italian proverb on friends and a
  "Tragedy of life" poem are harmless but unnecessary.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"Isn't controlling the mind just repression? Psychologists say that backfires."**
   - The Gita agrees that repression alone backfires: the taste remains (BG 2.59) and "the slightest thought
     of sense pleasure will agitate him" (2.62 purport). Its method is engagement and higher taste, not
     clamping down.
2. **"My mind is unusually restless. Maybe this isn't for me."**
   - Arjuna said exactly that (BG 6.34) and Krsna agreed it is difficult (6.35) before saying it is possible.
     Restlessness is the normal starting condition, not a disqualification.
3. **"Why chanting? Why not any meditation or music?"**
   - At VL1: "Try it and compare." The purport to 6.34 recommends chanting as the practical way to absorb the
     mind for people in our condition; 6.35 puts hearing about Krsna first. We present it as a practice to test,
     not as proven neuroscience.
4. **"Isn't the mind *me*? If I control it, who is controlling whom?"**
   - BG 3.42: the self is higher than intelligence, which is higher than the mind. You are the passenger;
     the mind is the reins. That you can *observe* your mind wanting something is evidence you are not it.
5. **"I don't have time for association, books and chanting."**
   - Start with one, small. BG 2.40 (VL1-15) says even a little is kept. The point is to change what the mind
     dwells on, since that is where the chain of 2.62 begins.
6. **Someone discloses intrusive thoughts, compulsions or severe anxiety.**
   - *Pastoral guidance:* this talk is about the ordinary restless mind. If someone describes a mind that is
     tormenting them, acknowledge them, talk afterwards, and say plainly that professional help is fully
     compatible with spiritual life. Do not diagnose.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The 9 p.m. plan.** Aditi plans to study; one notification leads to ninety minutes of videos and
self-blame.
- **Dilemma:** Where did the evening go wrong, and what would have changed it?
- **Principles:** SB 7.15.41 (which horse, which reins, which driver); BG 2.62 (contemplation is the first
  link); BG 6.26 (bring it back).
- **Questions:** At which minute was the reins' moment? What could "bringing the mind back" have looked like
  at 9:05? What would an ABC support be for her?
- **Takeaway:** Manage the first link (what the mind dwells on), not the ninetieth minute.

**Case 2: The willpower diet.** Karan decides to quit a habit by pure willpower and lasts four days, then
binges and concludes he is weak.
- **Principles:** BG 2.59 (taste remains under restriction); 2.62 purport (artificial repression); 6.35
  (practice and detachment together).
- **Questions:** What did Karan try to do with the horses? What did he give the mind instead? Is "weak" the
  right diagnosis?
- **Takeaway:** Replace, don't only restrain; the Gita predicted Karan's week.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first; praise every try)
1. Can the mind be both friend and enemy? *Yes, the same mind (BG 6.5-6).*
2. In the chariot, what are the reins, and who is the driver? *The mind is the reins; the intelligence is the driver (SB 7.15.41).*
3. Did Krishna say controlling the mind is easy? *No: difficult, but possible (BG 6.35).*
4. What two means does BG 6.35 name? *Practice and detachment.*
5. Why does "just try harder" fail? *Restriction leaves the taste; a higher taste changes what we want (BG 2.59).*

**M.2 Activity (about 8 minutes): "Map my week on the chariot"**
1. In pairs, 2 min: each recalls one moment this week the mind bolted.
2. 3 min: draw the chariot (N) and mark: which horse bolted, where were the reins, what was the driver doing?
3. 2 min: each picks one support to add: association, a book, or a few minutes of chanting (the "ABC").
4. 1 min: volunteers share the support chosen (not the story). Thank each.

**M.3 Reflection**
- **Reflection:** When this week was my mind a friend? When an enemy? What was different?
- **Application (private):** Which one support (association, a book, a few minutes of chanting) could I add
  this week, and when in the day? *(Nobody needs to share.)*

## P. Practical takeaway

- **Main lesson:** My mind is the reins, not me; it becomes a friend when the driver is awake and it has
  something better to hold.
- **One action (VL1):** For seven days, five minutes a day, sit quietly and softly chant (any name of God you
  are comfortable with, or the Hare Krsna mantra). Each time the mind wanders, bring it back without blame
  (BG 6.26). Count returns as repetitions, not failures.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Everyone has lost an argument with their own mind: you planned to study or work and ended up scrolling. The Bhagavad-gita says the mind can be "the best of friends" or "the greatest enemy", and the same mind does both (BG 6.5-6). An old picture explains why: the body is a chariot, the senses are horses, the mind is the reins, the intelligence is the driver and you are the passenger (SB 7.15.41; BG 3.42). When the driver dozes and the reins go slack, the horses go wherever they like.

Arjuna told Krishna the mind is harder to control than the wind (BG 6.34). Krishna agreed it is difficult, then said it is possible "by suitable practice and by detachment" (BG 6.35). Just trying harder usually fails: restriction leaves the taste in place, and the thought stirs it again (BG 2.59, 2.62 purport).

So the method is to give the mind something better to hold. The talk offers three supports, called "ABC" at IYF (the youth movement running this course): association (good company), books (hearing and reading) and chanting (a sound to bring the mind back to, BG 6.26). Start with one, small, and keep it.

## S2. Key ideas

- The mind is friend and enemy; the difference is training and engagement (BG 6.5–6).
- The chariot: senses = horses, mind = reins, intelligence = driver, you = passenger (SB 7.15.41).
- A restless mind is normal. Arjuna had one (BG 6.34). Control is hard but possible (BG 6.35).
- Suppression alone fails; a higher taste works (BG 2.59).
- Trouble starts with what the mind dwells on (BG 2.62). Change that, and behaviour follows.
- Three supports: association, books, chanting. Each time the mind wanders, bring it back (BG 6.26).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **BG 6.6:** "For him who has conquered the mind, the mind is the best of friends; but for one who has failed
  to do so, his mind will remain the greatest enemy." *For me:* I don't need a different mind; I need this one
  trained.
- **BG 6.35:** It is "very difficult to curb the restless mind, but it is possible by suitable practice and by
  detachment." *For me:* difficult is not impossible.
- **BG 6.26:** Wherever the mind wanders, "one must certainly withdraw it and bring it back." *For me:* every
  return is one repetition of the exercise.

## S4. Words to know

- **Mind:** the inner instrument that accepts and rejects, dwells and wanders. The "reins", not the self.
- **Intelligence:** the faculty that judges and decides. The "driver" (BG 3.42).
- **Mantra:** a sound for the mind to hold and return to. Traditional gloss: that which delivers the mind.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Isn't controlling the mind just repression?**
   The Gita says repression alone backfires: the taste remains (BG 2.59) and the slightest thought stirs it
   again (BG 2.62 purport). Its method is to engage the mind in something better.
2. **My mind is unusually restless. Is this hopeless?**
   Arjuna said the same (BG 6.34). Krsna agreed it is hard and then said it is possible (BG 6.35). Restless is
   the normal starting point.
3. **Why chanting and not just music or any meditation?**
   Try it and compare. The purport calls chanting Hare Krsna "the easiest way to control the mind" (BG 6.34
   purport), and hearing about Krsna is put first among the practices (BG 6.35 purport). It is offered as something to test.
4. **If I control my mind, who is controlling whom?**
   You are the passenger; the mind is the reins (SB 7.15.41; BG 3.42). The fact that you can notice "my mind
   wants this" shows you are not your mind.
5. **I don't have time for all three supports.**
   Start with one, small. Even a little is kept (BG 2.40). The aim is to change what the mind dwells on.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. In the chariot picture (SB 7.15.41), the mind is: (a) the horses, (b) the reins, (c) the driver.
2. True or false: Krsna tells Arjuna that controlling the mind is easy.
3. According to BG 2.62, what is the first link in the chain that ends in downfall?
4. What two means does BG 6.35 name for curbing the mind?
5. In your own words: why does "just try harder" usually fail (BG 2.59)?

**Answers:** 1. (b). 2. False: He agrees it is "very difficult" but says it is possible (BG 6.35). 3.
Contemplating the objects of the senses (BG 2.62). 4. Suitable practice and detachment (BG 6.35). 5. Because
restriction leaves the taste intact; only a higher taste changes what we want (BG 2.59).
