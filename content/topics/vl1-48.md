# Stay High Forever

> **VL1 · 45–60 min · review: sources checked, awaiting senior review · SENSITIVE (intoxication).** The title is a phrase from Srila Prabhupada's first year in New York
> (1966), where young people asked his disciples "Do you get high from this?" and one of them answered that
> chanting is a high you never come down from. The page uses that story (source: *Srila Prabhupada-lilamrta*,
> vol. 1, ch. 9, via a Desire Tree extract; see J) and the Gita's account of two kinds of happiness. IYF holds
> one recording on the site for this row (HH Prabodhananda Sarasvati Swami, catalogued as "Prabhodhanad
> Saraswati Swami", Viplava 2021) and no notes; the
> page follows the Gita, not the recording. Sensitive because the subject touches intoxication and
> addiction: **read the note in K** (no shame language; when to refer).

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on vl1-21 (Pleasure hunt) and vl1-05: those talks show that sense pleasure has a beginning and an end (BG 5.22) and that desire refills (BG 2.70); here BG 5.22 and 2.70 are used only briefly, for the shape of the "come-down", and the talk's own angle is the second curve, the high that does not end, plus the New York story and the experiment.

Young people are not wrong to want to feel more alive; they are wrong about where the feeling lasts. The Gita
does not scold the wish. It describes the two curves exactly: the happiness that is "nectar at first but poison
at the end" (BG 18.38) and the one that is "poison" at first but "nectar" at the end (18.37), and it says the
second is "boundless" and leaves one "never shaken" (6.20–23). At VL1 the talk is an honest comparison of the
two curves, one story from New York, and a ten-minute experiment at the end of the programme.

How the subject grows at other levels:

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **VL1** (here) | The high and the come-down; two curves; the New York story; "try it" |
| VL1-21 | The sibling talk: pleasure, its sources, the "higher taste" (BG 2.59) |
| VL2 | The soul as the seat of happiness; the modes of nature and the three kinds of happiness (BG 18.36–39) |
| VL3 | The fourth principle (no intoxication) as a choice for a higher taste, not a rule; chanting as daily practice |
| SPS | Steadiness when the taste is absent; *ruci* (taste) and *asakti* (attachment to the practice) |
| DPS | The jaundiced tongue (NOI 7) in depth, and leading others through the dry stretch |
| BSS | Sense-control as a trained higher taste in the student household |

**Not done here:**
- A lecture against drugs or drinking. The talk compares two highs; the audience draws the conclusion.
- The modes of nature as a system (VL2).
- Rules. The fourth regulative principle is not announced; a first-time listener is invited to test, not to vow.
- Clinical advice on addiction (K gives referral guidance only).

## C. Central message

The wish to be high is the soul's wish to feel fully alive, and nothing material can keep that promise: every
sense-high is "nectar at first but poison at the end", with the come-down built in. The Gita describes a
happiness that runs the other way: hard at first, nectar at the end, "boundless", and "never shaken". That is a
high you do not come down from, and it can be tasted, not just believed.

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. Draw the two curves of BG 18.37–38 and place three of their own highs on the first curve.
2. Explain, with BG 5.22 and 2.70, why the come-down is structural ("a beginning and an end"; desire refilled
   like a river into the ocean) and not a personal failure.
3. Describe the happiness of BG 6.20–23 in three words from the verse (boundless, never departs, never shaken).
4. Retell the New York story accurately and say what "stay high forever" meant in it.
5. Choose one experiment for the week and know where to get help if a habit has become a problem.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** BG 18.37–38, 6.20–23 and the New York story carry the talk; BG 5.22 and 2.70 explain the come-down;
NOI 7 explains why the higher taste is slow to arrive. The rest prepare you for questions.

**BG 18.38**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/38/
> "That happiness which is derived from contact of the senses with their objects and which appears like nectar at first but poison at the end is said to be of the nature of passion."
- **Context:** Chapter 18, the three kinds of happiness by the modes.
- **Teaching:** Sense-happiness is nectar first, poison later.
- **Purport point:** The purport's example is a young man and woman drawn together by the senses: "In the
  beginning this may be very pleasing to the senses, but at the end, or after some time, it becomes just like
  poison."
- **Application (preacher's):** This is the come-down in one line. Draw it as a curve that rises fast and falls
  below where it started. Every listener has lived on it.
- **Use:** in the talk (curve 1).

**BG 18.37**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/37/
> "That which in the beginning may be just like poison but at the end is just like nectar and which awakens one to self-realization is said to be happiness in the mode of goodness."
- **Teaching:** The happiness that awakens self-realisation is hard first, nectar later.
- **Purport point:** "All these procedures are very difficult, bitter like poison, but if one is successful in
  following the regulations and comes to the transcendental position, he begins to drink real nectar, and he
  enjoys life."
- **Application (preacher's):** Be honest that the second curve starts below zero: early mornings, sitting still,
  a mantra that feels dry. That honesty is what makes the New York story believable.
- **Use:** in the talk (curve 2).

**BG 5.22**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/5/22/
> "An intelligent person does not take part in the sources of misery, which are due to contact with the material senses. O son of Kuntī, such pleasures have a beginning and an end, and so the wise man does not delight in them."
- **Teaching:** Sense pleasures have a beginning and an end; the wise do not build on them.
- **Purport point:** "The more one is addicted to material pleasures, the more he is entrapped by material
  miseries." The purport quotes SB 5.5.1 (below).
- **Application (preacher's):** "A beginning and an end" is the technical reason every high has a come-down:
  it is a contact, and contacts end. Say this gently; the verse calls the person who sees it "intelligent", not
  "sinful".
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 2.70**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/70/
> "A person who is not disturbed by the incessant flow of desires – that enter like rivers into the ocean, which is ever being filled but is always still – can alone achieve peace, and not the man who strives to satisfy such desires."
- **Teaching:** Peace belongs to the one who is full like the ocean, not to the one who chases each desire.
- **Purport point:** "As long as one has the material body, the demands of the body for sense gratification will
  continue. The devotee, however, is not disturbed by such desires, because of his fullness."
- **Application (preacher's):** The come-down is not only that the high ends; it is that the river refills. "One
  more" is the structure of desire, not a character flaw. The alternative is fullness, not a bigger river.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 6.20–23**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/20-23/
> "…In that joyous state, one is situated in boundless transcendental happiness, realized through transcendental senses. Established thus, one never departs from the truth, and upon gaining this he thinks there is no greater gain. Being situated in such a position, one is never shaken, even in the midst of greatest difficulty."
- **Context:** Chapter 6, the yogi in *samadhi*.
- **Teaching:** The happiness of the self is boundless; one who reaches it never leaves it and is never shaken.
- **Purport point:** The purport insists that "transcendental pleasure – realized through transcendental senses
  – is accepted" here, and that after material cessation "there is the manifestation of spiritual activities, or
  devotional service".
- **Application (preacher's):** Three words from the verse answer the title: "boundless", "never departs",
  "never shaken". This is the "forever". Present it as the text's description of a state most of us have not
  reached, and the story as a hint that it is reachable.
- **Use:** in the talk (the "forever").

**BG 2.59**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/59/
> "Though the embodied soul may be restricted from sense enjoyment, the taste for sense objects remains. But, ceasing such engagements by experiencing a higher taste, he is fixed in consciousness."
- **Teaching:** Restriction leaves the taste; a higher taste replaces it.
- **Purport point:** Restriction by rules "is something like restricting a diseased person from certain types of
  eatables. The patient, however, neither likes such restrictions nor loses his taste for eatables."
- **Application (preacher's):** This is why the talk is not an anti-drug lecture: "just stop" fails by the Gita's
  own account. The method is a *replacement* taste. VL1-21 builds the whole talk on this verse; here it is one
  line.
- **Use:** in the talk, one line.

**SB 7.5.30**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/5/30/
> "Because of their uncontrolled senses, persons too addicted to materialistic life make progress toward hellish conditions and repeatedly chew that which has already been chewed."
- **Context:** Prahlada answers his father.
- **Teaching:** Material enjoyment is "chewing the chewed": the same thing again, with less in it each time.
- **Purport point:** "a materialistic person tries to enjoy sense gratification in different bodily forms, life
  after life, but is still unsatisfied."
- **Application (preacher's):** The perfect image for tolerance: the same dose gives less, so the dose goes up.
  Soften "hellish conditions" at VL1 to "downhill".
- **Use:** in the talk (the image).

**SB 5.5.1**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/5/1/
> "…One should engage in penance and austerity to attain the divine position of devotional service. By such activity, one's heart is purified, and when one attains this position, he attains eternal, blissful life, which is transcendental to material happiness and which continues forever."
- **Context:** Rsabhadeva to His sons.
- **Teaching:** The human form is not for labouring after sense pleasure "available even for dogs and hogs";
  austerity purifies the heart and leads to a bliss that "continues forever".
- **Application (preacher's):** The verse's last two words are the talk's title. Quote the second half; the
  first half ("dogs and hogs") is true but not for a VL1 opening.
- **Use:** in the talk (the title verse).

**NOI 7**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/7/
> "The holy name, character, pastimes and activities of Kṛṣṇa are all transcendentally sweet like sugar candy. Although the tongue of one afflicted by the jaundice of avidyā [ignorance] cannot taste anything sweet, it is wonderful that simply by carefully chanting these sweet names every day, a natural relish awakens within his tongue, and his disease is gradually destroyed at the root."
- **Teaching:** The higher taste is there, but a sick tongue cannot taste it at first; daily chanting cures the
  tongue.
- **Purport point:** "a person with jaundice considers something sweet to taste very bitter… if one with great
  care and attention takes to Kṛṣṇa consciousness, chanting the holy name… his ignorance will be destroyed and his
  tongue enabled to taste the sweetness".
- **Application (preacher's):** This answers the honest objection "I tried chanting and felt nothing": the Gita
  said curve 2 begins like poison; NOI 7 says why, and says the taste comes with regularity. Sugar candy and
  jaundice is the one metaphor every listener will remember.
- **Use:** in the talk (why the taste is slow).

**CC Antya 20.12**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/20/12/
> "…The chanting of the holy name of Kṛṣṇa expands the blissful ocean of transcendental life. It gives a cooling effect to everyone and enables one to taste full nectar at every step."
- **Context:** The first verse of Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu's *Siksastaka*.
- **Teaching:** Chanting expands an ocean of bliss and gives nectar "at every step".
- **Application (preacher's):** "At every step" against "at the end" (BG 18.38): the high that does not wait for a
  come-down. Use as the closing line before the experiment.
- **Use:** in the talk, one line.

**SB 1.2.6**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/6/
> "…Such devotional service must be unmotivated and uninterrupted to completely satisfy the self."
- **Teaching:** The self is completely satisfied by unmotivated, uninterrupted devotional service.
- **Purport point:** "temporary cessation of misery is falsely called happiness."
- **Application (preacher's):** The purport sentence is a sharp description of a high: relief mistaken for joy.
  One line in F; the verse is the spine of VL1-46.
- **Use:** background; one line.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** Happiness from "contact of the senses with their objects" is nectar first and poison
   later (BG 18.38) because every contact has "a beginning and an end" (5.22), and because desire refills like
   rivers into an ocean (2.70), so the same dose gives less ("chewing the chewed", SB 7.5.30). The happiness
   that "awakens one to self-realization" runs the other way (18.37): hard first, then "boundless… never
   shaken" (6.20–23), "which continues forever" (SB 5.5.1). It is slow to taste because the tongue is sick, and
   daily chanting is the cure (NOI 7); when it comes, it is nectar "at every step" (CC Antya 20.12).
2. **Why it matters.** The audience is not wrong to want a high; the Gita agrees the self is meant for
   "boundless" happiness. They are wrong about the curve. Most come-downs, hangovers and "one more episode"
   nights are curve 1 behaving exactly as described; seeing the curve removes the self-blame and points to the
   alternative.
3. **Connections.** Sibling of VL1-21 (pleasure and its sources; BG 2.59 in depth). Draws on VL1-04/26 (habits)
   and VL1-32 (bad habits) for the mechanics of change. Leads to VL3-04 (chanting) and, much later, to the
   stages of taste (DPS). SB 1.2.6 purport names the trap: "temporary cessation of misery is falsely called
   happiness".
4. **Consequences.** Someone who sees the two curves stops asking "why do I always come down?" and starts
   asking "what have I tried that starts below zero?" They run one experiment: ten minutes of chanting daily for
   a week, judged by NOI 7's promise, not by the first day.
5. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - **"So pleasure is bad."** No; the verse calls it a *kind* of happiness (18.38) and the Gita's goal is
     "boundless" happiness (6.21). The claim is about the curve, not about guilt.
   - **"Spiritual life means no highs, just calm."** 6.20–23 says "boundless transcendental happiness" and
     "rejoice". The tradition's word for its state is bliss, not sedation.
   - **"Just stop the habit."** BG 2.59 purport: restriction alone leaves the taste; a higher taste replaces it.
     The talk offers a replacement, not a prohibition.
   - **"I chanted once and felt nothing, so it doesn't work."** 18.37 said it starts like poison; NOI 7 says the
     tongue is sick and the cure is daily and gradual.
   - **"This is anti-science; drugs are just chemistry."** Agreed that they are chemistry; the Gita's point is
     about what chemistry can and cannot do: a contact ends (5.22). Research on adaptation (O) says the same about
     every external gain.
6. **Pitch for VL1.** Use "high" and "come-down" as the audience does. Say *samadhi* once if at all; define
   *avidya* as "not knowing who I am". No rules announced, no fourth principle named; the experiment is the
   ask. Keep the New York story short and exact.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet, say your name, and say the talk blames no one: wanting to feel alive is normal.
2. Say that "high" here means any strong lift of feeling: a win, music, a party, a purchase, a substance.
3. Ask H.2 question 1 as a private list; nobody shares.
4. Ask for a show of hands: "who has felt the come-down after a high?" and say "that is nearly everyone".
5. Say the transition line below and go to the first curve.

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the room's own life)
1. What are your three favourite ways to feel most alive, and how did you feel twelve hours later?
2. Think of something you worked for over weeks (a skill, a crop, a certificate, a trade). Did the satisfaction build or fade?
3. When the lift fades, what do you reach for next?

- **Opening question:** "Privately, list your three favourite highs: the things that make you feel most alive.
  A win, a party, a person, a screen, a substance, a purchase. Don't share. Now, next to each, write how you felt
  twelve hours later." Pause. "That second column is what this talk is about."
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** Arjun's week: Friday night out (great, then Saturday wasted); a new phone
  (great for three days); a series binge till 4 a.m. (great, then exam). Each high was real. Each had a bill.
  He assumes the problem is him.
- **Transition:** "The Gita says the problem is not Arjun. It is the curve, and there are two of them."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** They are the most entertained generation in history and report being the
  most anxious. Those two facts are the same curve.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **The high is real and so is the bill.** BG 18.38: "nectar at first but poison at the end". *Example:*
   Arjun's three highs. *Transition:* why does it always end?
2. **Because a contact ends and the river refills.** BG 5.22 ("a beginning and an end"); BG 2.70 (desires like
   rivers into the ocean); SB 7.5.30 (chewing the chewed: the same dose gives less). *Example:* "one more
   episode" is the structure of desire, not a character flaw. *Transition:* so is there anything that does not
   end?
3. **In New York in 1966, someone who had tried both said yes.** The story (J): "Do you get high from this?";
   Hayagriva's rocket that must land; "You can stay high forever." *Transition:* what was he describing?
4. **The other curve.** BG 18.37: poison first, nectar later; BG 6.20–23: "boundless", "never departs", "never
   shaken"; SB 5.5.1: "which continues forever". *Example:* anyone who has kept a hard practice for a month knows
   the shape. *Transition:* then why did I feel nothing the first time?
5. **The tongue is sick, and the cure is daily.** NOI 7 (deeper verse; the talk uses only its plain meaning): jaundice makes sugar taste bitter; careful daily
   chanting "awakens a natural relish". BG 2.59: replacement, not prohibition. CC Antya 20.12: nectar "at every
   step". *Transition:* to the experiment.

## J. Stories and illustrations

- **"Stay high forever", New York, 1966.** *Source:* *Srila Prabhupada-lilamrta*, vol. 1 (*Planting the
  Seed*), ch. 9 "Stay High Forever", by Satsvarupa dasa Goswami; read here from a Desire Tree extract
  (research/desire-tree/…/Srimati_Mataji_-_Stay_High_Forever.txt). *Status: Partially verified* (secondary
  extract; check against the printed edition before quoting at length). The account: Srila Prabhupada, aged
  seventy-one, lectured in a storefront on the Lower East Side amid a drug culture. Visitors regularly asked the
  disciples "Do you get high from this?" and were told they could get high just by chanting. A group from an LSD
  commune came to test the chanting; after the lecture their leader asked about drugs, and Prabhupada replied that
  drugs were not necessary for spiritual life, could not produce spiritual consciousness, and that
  "drug-induced religious visions were simply hallucinations". Challenged ("But have you ever taken LSD?"), he
  answered that he had not, "not even cigarettes or tea", but that his disciples had taken all these things and
  given them up, and asked Hayagriva to speak. Hayagriva compared LSD to a rocket that must eventually land: "On
  LSD, we experience going up, but we always have to come down again. That's not spiritual consciousness. When
  you actually attain spiritual or Kṛṣṇa consciousness, you stay high. Because you go to Kṛṣṇa, you don't have
  to come down. You can stay high forever." *Connection:* the two curves, said by someone who had been on both.
  Tell it from the source; do not add dialogue.
- **The newspaper line** (same chapter, quoting *The East Village Other*, Oct 1966): the chanting described as
  "sweeter than acid, cheaper than pot" and the swami's method as "Turn Off, Sing Out, and Fall In". *Use:*
  optional, for colour; attribute to the newspaper via the *Lilamrta*.
- **Prahlada and his father** (SB 7.5; verified verse 7.5.30): the boy's one-line diagnosis of material life,
  "chewing the chewed". *Connection:* tolerance; the dose goes up because the taste goes down. One line; the
  story itself is for higher levels.
- **Contemporary illustration (invented, labelled):** Arjun's week (H).
- **Left out:** the Encyclopedia's "Plain living, high thinking" conversation (1976) is about simple living and
  technology, not about the high; useful background for a preacher, not used here. The Viplava 2021 recording
  on the site is "hear next"; its content was not available to this page.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

> **Before you teach:** someone in the room may be dependent on alcohol, nicotine, cannabis or something
> stronger, or may be living with someone who is. This talk is not an intervention and the preacher is not a
> clinician. Use no shame language; the Gita's word for the person who sees curve 1 is "intelligent" (BG 5.22),
> and the come-down is described as structural, not as moral failure. If someone discloses a problem, talk
> afterwards, take it seriously, and say plainly that medical and counselling help is fully compatible with
> spiritual life. In India, Tele-MANAS (14416) or your local crisis service is a free, 24-hour mental-health helpline that also
> handles substance-use concerns; a doctor or a de-addiction centre is the right next step for dependence.
> Chanting is offered alongside such help, never instead of it. *(Pastoral guidance, not a scriptural claim.)*

1. **"Isn't this just an anti-drug lecture dressed up?"**
   - It is a comparison of two highs, and it includes the ones nobody bans: the phone, the binge, the win. The
     Gita's own position is that prohibition alone fails (BG 2.59 purport); what it offers is a replacement taste.
2. **"Drugs are chemistry. Chanting is just psychology. Why is one 'higher'?"**
   - Both are experiences; the question is the curve. Every external contact has "a beginning and an end"
     (BG 5.22), and research on adaptation (O) finds that even large external gains return people to baseline.
     The tradition's claim is that the self's own happiness does not adapt away (6.20–23). That is a claim to test,
     and the test is cheap.
3. **"I tried chanting and felt nothing."**
   - That is what the Gita predicts for the first stretch: curve 2 begins "like poison" (18.37). NOI 7 explains
     why (a jaundiced tongue cannot taste sugar) and gives the method (carefully, every day). Judge after a week of
     ten minutes, not after one try.
4. **"Prabhupada never took LSD; how could he judge it?"**
   - The same challenge was put to him in 1966 (J). His answer was to let those who had taken it speak. The
     disciples' testimony, not his, carried the room; the talk does the same, by inviting the audience's own
     experiment.
5. **"My friend can't stop. What do I do?"**
   - *Pastoral:* take it seriously; this is beyond a talk. Encourage him to see a doctor or a de-addiction
     service; Tele-MANAS (14416) or your local crisis service is free and confidential. Stay his friend; good company is itself part of the
     cure (VL1-44). Chanting with him is good; it is not a substitute for treatment.
6. **"Isn't 'boundless happiness' an exaggeration? Devotees look pretty ordinary."**
   - The verse describes a perfected state (6.20–23), not the first year. Most of us are on the "poison" stretch
     of curve 2 and honest about it. The claim is about where the curve goes, and the story is of people who went
     some distance along it.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: Arjun's week.** Friday out, a new phone, a 4 a.m. binge; three real highs, three bills; he blames
his willpower.
- **Dilemma:** Is Arjun weak, or is he on curve 1 behaving exactly as described?
- **Principles:** BG 18.38 (nectar, then poison); BG 5.22 (a beginning and an end); BG 2.70 (the river refills);
  SB 7.5.30 (the same dose gives less).
- **Questions:** Plot each high on N. What would an experiment on curve 2 look like for Arjun? What does he lose by
  trying it for a week?
- **Takeaway:** Change the curve before blaming the will.

**Case 2: The friend who can't stop.** Neha's flatmate drinks every night now and laughs it off. Neha has come

## O. Research and supplementary resources

Two studies support the *general* claim that external gains do not raise happiness for long (hedonic
adaptation). They say nothing about spiritual happiness and the talk must not pretend they do.

- Brickman, P., Coates, D. and Janoff-Bulman, R. (1978). "Lottery winners and accident victims: Is happiness
  relative?" *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 36(8): 917–927. doi:10.1037/0022-3514.36.8.917.
  Interviews with lottery winners, accident victims and controls; winners were not significantly happier than
  controls and took less pleasure in ordinary events. *Limit:* small samples (22 winners), cross-sectional,
  1970s Illinois. *Use:* one line, "even the lottery adapts away".
- Diener, E., Lucas, R. E. and Scollon, C. N. (2006). "Beyond the hedonic treadmill: Revising the adaptation
  theory of well-being." *American Psychologist* 61(4): 305–314. doi:10.1037/0003-066X.61.4.305. A review
  arguing that adaptation to many life events is real but not total, and that set points differ between people.
  *Limit:* a review, not a trial; it also records exceptions to adaptation. *Use:* "psychology calls curve 1
  the hedonic treadmill, and notes that it is not the whole story", which is exactly the opening the Gita needs.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Everyone wants to feel more alive, and the Gita does not scold that wish. It says the problem is the curve.
Happiness that comes from "contact of the senses with their objects… appears like nectar at first but poison at
the end" (BG 18.38), because every contact has "a beginning and an end" (BG 5.22) and because desire refills
like rivers into the ocean (BG 2.70): the same dose gives less, "chewing the chewed" (SB 7.5.30). There is a
second curve: happiness that is "like poison" at first "but at the end is just like nectar" (BG 18.37), and at its
height is "boundless", so that one "never departs" and "is never shaken" (BG 6.20–23); it "continues forever"
(SB 5.5.1). In New York in 1966, young people asked Srila Prabhupada's students "Do you get high from this?",
and one of them, who had tried everything else, said that with chanting "you can stay high forever". The taste is
slow to come because, as the *Nectar of Instruction* says, a tongue with jaundice cannot taste sugar until it is
cured, and the cure is careful daily chanting (NOI 7). The invitation is an experiment, not a rule.

## S2. Key ideas

- The high is real; so is the bill: "nectar at first but poison at the end" (BG 18.38).
- The come-down is built in: a contact has "a beginning and an end" (BG 5.22) and the river of desire refills
  (BG 2.70).
- The same dose gives less: "chewing the chewed" (SB 7.5.30).
- The other curve: "poison" first, "nectar" at the end, and it "awakens one to self-realization" (BG 18.37).
- At its height: "boundless transcendental happiness… never shaken" (BG 6.20–23); it "continues forever"
  (SB 5.5.1).
- Why it tastes of nothing at first: a jaundiced tongue; careful daily chanting cures it (NOI 7; deeper verse, the talk uses only its plain meaning).
- Prohibition alone leaves the taste; a higher taste replaces it (BG 2.59).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **BG 18.38:** sense-happiness "appears like nectar at first but poison at the end". *For me:* the come-down
  is the design, not my failure.
- **BG 6.20–23:** "boundless transcendental happiness… one is never shaken, even in the midst of greatest
  difficulty." *For me:* that is the high worth wanting.
- **NOI 7:** "simply by carefully chanting these sweet names every day, a natural relish awakens within his
  tongue." *For me:* judge the experiment after a week, not after a day.

## S4. Words to know

- **Higher taste:** an experience that replaces a lower one instead of forbidding it (BG 2.59).
- **Avidya:** "not knowing who I am"; NOI 7 calls it the jaundice that stops the tongue tasting sweetness.
- **Samadhi:** the fixed, joyful state described in BG 6.20–23.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Is this an anti-drug lecture?**
   No. It compares two highs, including the ones nobody bans: the phone, the binge, the win. The Gita itself says
   prohibition alone fails (BG 2.59 purport); it offers a replacement taste.
2. **Drugs are chemistry; chanting is psychology. Why call one higher?**
   Both are experiences; the question is the curve. Every external contact ends (BG 5.22) and even big gains adapt
   away. The tradition claims the self's own happiness does not (BG 6.20–23). Test it.
3. **I tried chanting and felt nothing.**
   The Gita predicts that: curve 2 starts "like poison" (BG 18.37). NOI 7 explains the sick tongue and gives
   the cure: carefully, every day. Judge after seven days of ten minutes.
4. **Prabhupada never took LSD. How could he judge it?**
   He was asked exactly that in 1966 and let his students, who had taken it and given it up, answer. This talk
   does the same: your own experiment is the evidence.
5. **My friend can't stop. What do I do?**
   Take it seriously; this is beyond a talk. Encourage a doctor or a de-addiction service; Tele-MANAS (14416) or your local crisis service is
   free and confidential. Stay his friend. Chanting with him is good; it is not a substitute for treatment.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. According to BG 18.38, happiness from the senses is: (a) poison first, nectar later, (b) nectar first,
   poison later, (c) always nectar.
2. True or false: BG 5.22 says sense pleasures "have a beginning and an end".
3. What image does SB 7.5.30 use for repeating the same material enjoyment?
4. Name two things BG 6.20–23 says about the happiness of the self.
5. In your own words: why does NOI 7 say the higher taste is slow to come, and what is the cure?

**Answers:** 1. (b) (BG 18.38). 2. True (BG 5.22). 3. Chewing what has already been chewed (SB 7.5.30). 4. Any
two of: boundless; realised through transcendental senses; one never departs from it; one thinks there is no
greater gain; one is never shaken (BG 6.20–23). 5. Ignorance is like jaundice, which makes sweet things taste
bitter; carefully chanting the holy names every day awakens the taste and cures the disease at the root (NOI 7).
