# 34. Attitudes Towards Gåhastha Men

> BSS (brahmacari training) · 120 min · drafted · not sensitive. Follows BIKC chapter 34. Builds on bss-18 (an ashram label is a designation; devotion decides closeness to the Lord) and bss-07 (SB 5.5.3, detachment is an attitude, not an address).

## A. Topic overview
| | |
|---|---|
| Batch / level | BSS, brahmacari training (Bhakti Siddhanta Sabha). Brahmacari = a celibate student; grhastha (also spelled gåhastha) = a married householder who follows spiritual principles |
| Suggested duration | 120 minutes (lecture, with an "if time allows" extension) |
| Preparation time | About 2 hours: read BIKC ch. 34 (about two pages), SB 7.14.2 and 7.14.5 with purports, CC Madhya 3.168 with purport, then this page |
| Difficulty | Intermediate |
| Prerequisites | None. bss-18 and bss-07 help |
| Audience | Young men living or preparing to live as brahmacaris; also anyone who lives alongside householders in a temple community |
| Teaching objective | The audience can hold two things together: honest respect and service towards householder devotees, and prudent distance from the comforts of home life that could disturb a brahmacari's vow |

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level
A brahmacari's strictness can slide into criticism of those who live differently. BIKC names the risk (a "superiority complex")
and also gives the opposite risk (becoming attracted to the affection and comforts of a home). IYF's BSS motive is "following the principles of brahmachari life" and "serving
more dedicatedly" (research/batch-criteria.md, section 6): both need the right attitude to the householders who support the mission.

| VL2 | Why a householder can be a devotee; the varnasrama picture in a sentence |
| VL3 | Household life offered to Krishna; association with householder devotees |
| SPS | Duties of the ashrams and mutual service |
| DPS | Serving as a resource for householders in leadership and counselling |

## C. Central message
A householder who offers the fruit of his work to Krishna is a devotee and a teacher, and a brahmacari who is proud of his own strictness has lost the very thing the strictness is for. A brahmacari respects and serves householder devotees with humility, and protects his own heart by keeping a modest distance from the comforts of their homes.

## D. Learning outcomes
By the end, participants can:
1. State Srila Prabhupada's purport on householder life: work for livelihood and offer the result to Krishna, learn this from great devotees (SB 7.14.2).
2. Give two scriptural examples of householders who were exemplary or taught (Kardama Muni with Devahuti; Sandipani Muni, Krishna's guru).
3. Explain why "I am a brahmacari, so I am dearer to Krishna" is not supported (BG 12.13-14; BIKC).
4. Describe BIKC's two cautions: avoid the homes of householders who live like karmis, and limit time in even good homes.
5. Choose one concrete way to serve a householder devotee this month and one limit to keep.

## E. Scriptural foundation
Use SB 7.14.2, SB 7.14.5, SB 10.45.32 and 10.45.37, BG 12.13-14 and BG 18.61 in the talk. The rest are for questions. All verses and purports were read on Vedabase (Srila Prabhupada's translation and purport). SB 10.45 is after SB 10.14, so the translation is the BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples; say so if you read it aloud.

**SB 7.14.2**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/14/2/
> "My dear King, those who stay at home as householders must act to earn their livelihood, and instead of trying to enjoy the results of their work themselves, they should offer these results to Kṛṣṇa, Vāsudeva."
- **Context:** Yudhisthira asks Narada how those who stay at home can attain liberation (SB 7.14.1). Narada has already described the brahmacari, vanaprastha and sannyasi; now he describes the householder.
- **Teaching:** The householder earns, offers the result to Vasudeva, and learns how to do so from great devotees.
- **Purport point:** The purport says the format of householder life is dedication to the Supreme Lord, citing BG 6.1, and that whether one acts as brahmacari, grhastha, vanaprastha or sannyasi, one must act only to satisfy Vasudeva. It adds that one cannot learn this without association with devotees, and recalls that in Bengal villages people heard Srimad-Bhagavatam daily.
- **Application (preacher's):** The purport puts the four ashrams on one footing: the same aim, different practices. The brahmacari does not stand above the householder by aim, only by practice.
- **Use:** in the talk (I.1).

**SB 7.14.5**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/14/5/
> "…should live in human society unattached to family affairs, although externally appearing very much attached."
- **Purport point:** The purport calls this "the picture of ideal family life". It says Ramananda Raya explained to Lord Caitanya that one may stay in one's own position, brahmana, sudra, sannyasi or whatever, but must inquire about the goal of life. Earning a livelihood is not the problem; the problem is freedom from birth and death. A sincere devotee invents some way to earn his living and Krishna helps him.
- **Use:** in the talk (I.1); the ideal is internal detachment, which a householder can have.

**SB 3.22.19**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/22/19/
> "…I shall accept the life of devotional service accepted by the most perfect human beings."
- **Context:** Svayambhuva Manu offers his daughter Devahuti to the celibate sage Kardama. Kardama accepts on a condition: after she bears his child, he will take up devotional service in the renounced life.
- **Purport point:** The purport says Kardama was a brahmacari in his hermitage who did not want to be a householder for his whole life; he wished for a child who would be a ray of the Supreme Lord (Kapila, as later chapters show). It adds that one should not beget children like cats and dogs, and that human life is meant for complete devotion to the Lord. It also gives Bhaktivinoda Thakura's prayer for a preaching son as an example.
- **Application (preacher's):** Even the sanctioned householder life, as Kardama enters it, is a service with a purpose and an end date; it is not a lower rung.
- **Use:** in the talk (J.1).

**SB 3.24.41, 3.24.42, 3.24.47**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/24/41/ (and /42/, /47/)
> "Without association, he traveled over the surface of the globe as a sannyāsī" (3.24.42)
> "…ultimately attained the path back to Godhead." (3.24.47)
- **Teaching:** After Lord Kapila's instruction, Kardama leaves home for the forest (3.24.41), takes a vow of silence and wanders as a sannyasi (3.24.42), and attains the path back to Godhead (3.24.47).
- **Purport point (3.24.41):** Going to the forest means taking exclusive shelter of the Lord and giving up family relationships for Krishna consciousness; one need not literally go to the forest. The purport says family life without Krishna consciousness is like a blind well.
- **Use:** in the talk, for the full arc of Kardama's life (J.1).

**SB 10.45.32 and 10.45.37**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/10/45/32/ (and /37/)
> "By serving him as devotedly as one would serve the Supreme Lord Himself, They showed others an irreproachable example of how to worship the spiritual master." (10.45.32)
> "…after consulting with his wife, he chose as his remuneration the return of his young son" (10.45.37)
- **Teaching:** Krishna and Balarama studied under Sandipani Muni in Avanti (10.45.30-31). 10.45.37 shows that Sandipani consulted his wife and had a son who had died at Prabhasa; he asked the Lords to return him as his guru-dakshina.
- **Application (preacher's):** The householder Sandipani is the guru whom the Lord Himself serves "as devotedly as one would serve the Supreme Lord". The text itself says the Lords served him as an example. This answers the thought that a guru must be in a particular ashram; for the qualification of a guru, builds on bss-18 (CC Madhya 8.128).
- **Use:** in the talk (J.2).

**CC Madhya 3.168**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/3/168/
> "…the other devotees, headed by Śrīvāsa Ṭhākura, also desired to give Him alms and invite Him for lunch."
- **Purport point:** The purport says it is the duty of householders to invite a sannyasi who is in the neighbourhood; as long as he stays he enlightens the inhabitants, and he has no housing or food problem.
- **Application (preacher's):** The relationship runs both ways: householders feed and house; renunciants teach. Gratitude is the brahmacari's side.
- **Use:** in the talk (I.2); background.

**BG 12.13-14**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/12/13-14/
> "…engaged in devotional service with determination, his mind and intelligence fixed on Me – such a devotee of Mine is very dear to Me."
- **Purport point:** The purport lists the qualities of a pure devotee: not envious, kind to all, tolerant, satisfied, not swayed by false arguments. It says a devotee becomes situated in that stage by following the regulative principles of devotional service.
- **Application (preacher's):** The Lord's description of the devotee dear to Him names qualities, not an ashram. This is the basis for dropping comparisons.
- **Use:** in the talk (I.3).

**BG 18.61**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/61/
> "…directing the wanderings of all living entities, who are seated as on a machine, made of the material energy."
- **Purport point:** The purport says the individual is not independent; he works under the Supersoul's direction and the spell of his bodily situation; the duty is to surrender (next verse).
- **Application (preacher's):** BIKC's "we are like toys in the hands of Maya" is this verse in everyday words: whatever strictness we show is sustained by the Lord's protection.
- **Use:** in the talk (I.3).

**CC Antya 20.21**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/20/21/
> "One who thinks himself lower than the grass, who is more tolerant than a tree"
- **Use:** closing; the Siksastaka verse is the daily check on pride. No purport is printed on Vedabase for this verse; use the verse and translation only.

**SB 6.3.20-21**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/6/3/20-21/
> "Lord Brahmā, Bhagavān Nārada, Lord Śiva, the four Kumāras, Lord Kapila [the son of Devahūti], Svāyambhuva Manu, Prahlāda Mahārāja, Janaka Mahārāja, Grandfather Bhīṣma, Bali Mahārāja, Śukadeva Gosvāmī and I myself know the real religious principle."
- **Context and purport point:** Yamaraja names the twelve mahajanas who know bhagavata-dharma. The purport says it is understood by following the disciplic succession of Brahma, Siva, Laksmi and the Kumaras.
- **Application (preacher's):** BIKC says seven of the twelve are or were householders. The verse gives only the names. Svayambhuva Manu is a householder in SB 3.21-22 (he gives his daughter Devahuti to Kardama). The status of the others as householders was not checked for this page: *Requires source review* before quoting "seven of twelve".
- **Use:** background; question K.2.

## F. Philosophical explanation
**Principle.** The ashrams are different arrangements of one service. The householder works and offers the result to Krishna (SB 7.14.2), lives simply and is unattached inside (7.14.5); the brahmacari trains under a guru with full time for service. What makes a person dear to the Lord is the devotee's qualities (BG 12.13-14), not the label.
**BIKC's two cautions, in balance.**
1. *Against pride.* BIKC says it is healthy for brahmacaris to speak among themselves of the glories of brahmacari life and the dangers of household life, but to become unnecessarily critical or develop a superiority complex is against pure devotion and damages progress. It is not necessarily true that a staunch brahmacari is dearer to Krishna than an apparently entangled householder; we are like toys in the hands of Maya, and if Krishna withdraws protection no vow can be kept. It points out that traditional gurukula teachers were often householders (Sandipani), and that many great personalities were householders. It quotes Narottama dasa Thakura's song that it does not matter whether a devotee is a grhastha or sannyasi if he chants "Gauranga": "I want his association." (Narottama's song: *Requires source review* for the exact text; BIKC is the source here.)
2. *Against entanglement.* BIKC advises brahmacaris to be eager to serve devotee householders humbly, but to take only good association, to be cautious with those whose talk is only family and money or who do not follow the regulative principles. BIKC (ch. 34) says: "if a householder regularly watches TV and in other ways lives like a karmī, avoid his home—it is not a gṛhastha-āśrama, but a blind well." It adds that "Brahmacārīs are not meant for socializing and hanging around gṛhastha's homes"; even among good householders "it is better that brahmacārīs not associate much with them in their homes or with their families", and when doing so "a brahmacārī must maintain a strong internal resolve, otherwise he may become attracted to the affection and comforts of home life and think, 'I could also be enjoying this.' Once a brahmacārī starts thinking like that, the seed of his downfall is planted." BIKC also notes that householders often look to brahmacaris and sannyasis for inspiration, and that humble, blissful brahmacaris are appreciated everywhere. (Summary; see O.)
**Connections.** bss-07 (SB 5.5.3: unattached though a householder; SB 5.5.8, the knot of attraction); bss-18 (designations); bss-13 (leaving home); bss-28 (determination and early limits).
**Consequences.** Pride makes the brahmacari useless as a servant; unguarded comfort makes him unable to remain one. The remedy for both is service: humble service to devotees with a lively internal remembrance of one's own aim.
**Common misunderstandings.**
1. "Householders are lower." SB 7.14.2 and 3.24.47 (Kardama attained the Lord) say no.
2. "Therefore I should spend time in their homes." BIKC says limit it.
3. "Limiting visits means looking down." BIKC gives both in the same chapter: "brahmacārīs should be eager to humbly serve devotee gṛhasthas and respect the service they render", and the limit on visits, whose reason is the seed of the brahmacari's own downfall (F, caution 2).
4. "Brahmacaris should never speak of the dangers of household life." BIKC says such talk among brahmacaris is healthy; the problem is contempt.
**How to pitch it.** Speak to men who are proud of what they are learning. Praise the courage of their vow and the service of the householders in the same breath.

## H. Opening and engagement
**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes).**
1. One round of kirtana or a short prayer; say the title.
2. Say: "Everyone in this room has eaten prasadam cooked or paid for by a householder. Today we think about how we see them, and how they see us."
3. Read SB 7.14.2 slowly. Say: "This is Narada Muni, a lifelong brahmacari, describing the householder's life. Listen for what he asks of him."
**H.2 Opening questions.**
1. "Name one householder devotee who has helped you in your spiritual life, and say in one sentence how." (Take three answers; thank each by name.)
2. "What would you say if a friend asked: why do you live as a brahmacari when so many great devotees are married?" (Gather answers without comment.)
3. "What do you think householders think of brahmacaris?"

## I. Core teaching points
**I.1 Householder life is also service (SB 7.14.2, 7.14.5; SB 3.22.19, 3.24.41-47).** The householder works for his livelihood, offers the result to Krishna and learns to do so from devotees; he is unattached inside though he appears attached outside. Kardama's arc: he accepts marriage on a condition and with a purpose, serves the Lord as a householder, receives Kapila as his son, then leaves for sannyasa and attains the path to Godhead. Transition: "If this is so, what is a householder's place in the mission?"
**I.2 Householders in the mission (SB 10.45.32, 10.45.37; CC Madhya 3.168).** Krishna and Balarama served Sandipani, a householder, as they would serve the Lord. Householders in Navadvipa invited Lord Caitanya for lunch; the purport says this is their duty towards a sannyasi. A brahmacari receives food, shelter and support from householders' work; the right response is gratitude and service. Transition: "So where does pride come from?"
**I.3 The measure is the devotee, not the label (BG 12.13-14, 18.61; CC Antya 20.21).** The Lord says the devotee dear to Him is the one with qualities such as non-envy, kindness, tolerance and determined devotional service. BG 18.61: the Lord directs every living being as on a machine; no vow is held by our own power. BIKC's phrase "toys in the hands of Maya" is this fact. CC Antya 20.21 is the daily practice: lower than grass, more tolerant than a tree, offering respect to others. Transition: "But there is also wisdom in distance."
**I.4 The prudent limit (BIKC).** (a) Take good association; avoid homes where karmi life prevails. (b) Even in good homes, do not hang around: BIKC says that a brahmacari must hold strong internal resolve or he may think "I could also be enjoying this". (c) Serve with purpose: a defined service, then return to the temple. (d) When preaching must take you into worldly homes, be extra careful; BIKC reports Srila Prabhupada's remark that he prayed not to fall whenever he entered a rich man's house (BIKC's report; *Requires source review* for the original). Close with L.1.

## J. Stories and illustrations
**J.1 Kardama Muni and Devahuti (SB 3.21-3.24; key verse 3.23.1).** Svayambhuva Manu, with his queen and daughter Devahuti, comes to Kardama's hermitage (SB 3.21-22). Kardama, a celibate sage, accepts Devahuti on a condition: he will stay with her until she bears a child, then take up devotional service in the renounced order (SB 3.22.19). She serves him with great love, as Bhavani serves Lord Siva (3.23.1, and the purport says the reciprocation of service and love between husband and wife is the ideal of a householder's life). Their son is Lord Kapila (bss-14). After Kapila instructs him, Kardama leaves home, takes silence as a vow, wanders as a sannyasi and attains the path back to Godhead (3.24.41-47). **Devotional lesson:** the householder stage, entered for a purpose and with the Lord's service in mind, can end in the Lord Himself appearing as a son and in the householder's own perfection.
**J.2 Sandipani Muni (SB 10.45.30-40).** Krishna and Balarama went to the school of Sandipani, a native of Kasi living in Avanti (10.45.30-31). They served him as devotedly as one would serve the Supreme Lord (10.45.32), learned all knowledge in sixty-four days and nights (10.45.35-36) and, as guru-dakshina, brought back his son who had died in the ocean at Prabhasa (10.45.37 onward; read to 10.45.40 before telling). **Devotional lesson:** the Lord Himself honours the householder guru, to teach everyone the attitude of a student.
**Illustration (preacher's, labelled).** A mountain trekker who relies on the village at the foot of the mountain for supplies does not look down on the villagers because he climbs. His climb depends on their work. The point for us: the brahmacari trains full time, the householder supplies much of what he needs, and both are serving Krishna, so the brahmacari should be grateful and not proud.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections
1. **"Isn't it true that a brahmacari is on a higher path?"** BIKC says it is not necessarily true that the strict brahmacari is dearer to Krishna than an apparently entangled householder. The aim is the same (SB 7.14.2 purport), and BG 12.13-14 describes the dear devotee by qualities.
2. **"Are the great devotees really householders?"** Kardama attained the Lord (SB 3.24.47); Svayambhuva Manu is among the twelve mahajanas (SB 6.3.20-21; SB 3.21-22). BIKC says seven of the twelve are or were householders: *Requires source review* for the count.
3. **"Why then limit visits to good householders' homes?"** BIKC's reason, in its words: "he may become attracted to the affection and comforts of home life and think, 'I could also be enjoying this.' Once a brahmacārī starts thinking like that, the seed of his downfall is planted."
4. **"What if a householder devotee criticises brahmacaris?"** Respond with service and Siksastaka humility (CC Antya 20.21); speak to a senior if there is a real problem.
5. **"What if I start doubting my ashram?"** Speak to your mentor. Doubts are discussed with guidance; see bss-28 for how a decision is renewed. If you feel distressed, speak to a trusted senior or a qualified counsellor (Tele-MANAS 14416 in India).

## L. Case studies
**L.1 Invented case for discussion.** A brahmacari is invited every Sunday to lunch at the home of a devoted couple who are generous and Krishna conscious. After some months he notices he wishes to spend more of Sunday there, and has begun to think of their home life as ideal. *Dilemma:* he does not want to seem ungrateful. *Principles:* SB 7.14.2 (the home is also service), BIKC (limit time, internal resolve), SB 5.5.8 (the knot, bss-07). *Questions:* How can he keep gratitude and also a limit? Whom can he speak to? How can he serve them in another way? *Analysis:* thank them, keep a modest schedule, serve in a defined way (kirtana, reading, helping a programme), tell his mentor. *Takeaway:* gratitude and limit go together.
**L.2 Invented case for discussion (extension).** Two brahmacaris joke about a householder devotee who wears expensive clothes. The householder is also the temple's major supporter. *Principles:* BG 12.13-14, CC Antya 20.21. *Questions:* What is the harm of the joke? What do they know of his service and his heart? *Takeaway:* speak of what you can praise.

## M. Discussion and reflection
**M.1 Closing check questions (with expected answers).**
1. *What should a householder do with the result of his work (SB 7.14.2)?* Offer it to Krishna, Vasudeva.
2. *What did Kardama do after Kapila instructed him?* Left home, took silence and wandered as a sannyasi; he attained the path back to Godhead (SB 3.24.41-47).
3. *Who was Sandipani, and how did Krishna and Balarama treat him?* Krishna's guru, who consulted his wife and had a son; They served him as devotedly as the Lord (SB 10.45.32, 10.45.37).
4. *Whom does BG 12.13-14 call dear to the Lord?* The devotee with qualities such as non-envy, kindness, tolerance, determination; no ashram is named.
5. *What are BIKC's two cautions?* Do not look down on householders; do not hang around their homes, and keep internal resolve.
6. *What is the daily check on pride?* CC Antya 20.21: lower than grass, more tolerant than a tree.
**M.2 Activities.**
a) *Thank-you list (10 min, pairs).* Each lists three householders who help the mission and one way to thank each. Share one.
b) *Two sentences (10 min).* In pairs, write one sentence of respect for householders and one sentence of the limit a brahmacari keeps, so that both can be said to a friend kindly.
c) *Short drama (15 min, extension).* Roles: young brahmacari, senior brahmacari, narrator. Script: the young man has been mocking householders; the senior retells Kardama's arc in five lines. Label as dramatisation.
**M.3 Reflection.** "Where have I, even in a joke, valued myself above someone who serves?" Private.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary
A brahmacari lives simply and trains full time. A householder (grhastha) lives with family and works. Srila Prabhupada explains, in his purport to SB 7.14.2, that the householder earns his livelihood, offers the result to Krishna and learns how from great devotees. The aim of all four ashrams is the same: to satisfy Krishna. SB 7.14.5 says the ideal is to live in society unattached inside, even if one appears attached outside.
The scriptures show householders who were exemplary or who taught. Kardama Muni accepted marriage on the condition that he would take up devotional service after a child was born; his son was Lord Kapila, and at the end he attained the path back to Godhead. Krishna and Balarama served Sandipani Muni, who had a wife and a son, as devotedly as they would serve the Lord. Householders in Navadvipa invited Lord Caitanya for lunch.
The book *Brahmacarya in Krishna consciousness* gives two cautions. Do not become critical or proud: it is not necessarily true that a strict brahmacari is dearer to Krishna than a householder, and we are like toys in the hands of Maya. And do not hang around householders' homes: even good homes can awaken the thought "I could also be enjoying this", so keep a modest limit and strong internal resolve. Serve devotees humbly, take good association, and keep your heart on your own service.

## S2. Key ideas
- All ashrams aim to satisfy Krishna.
- A householder who offers his work to Krishna is a devotee and often a teacher.
- The Lord's dear devotee is known by qualities, not by an ashram.
- A brahmacari serves householders with gratitude and keeps a modest limit.
- Pride is checked by "lower than the grass".

## S3. Verses to remember
- **SB 7.14.2**: householders "must act to earn their livelihood" and "offer these results to Kṛṣṇa". *For me:* all of us work for the same Lord.
- **BG 12.13-14**: "such a devotee of Mine is very dear to Me." *For me:* the Lord looks at qualities.
- **CC Antya 20.21**: "One who thinks himself lower than the grass, who is more tolerant than a tree" *For me:* humility is the daily check.

## S4. Words to know
- **Brahmacari:** a celibate student living simply and serving his guru.
- **Grhastha:** a householder who lives with family and follows spiritual principles.
- **Sannyasi:** a person in the renounced order.
- **Guru-dakshina:** a gift to the teacher after study.
- **Karmi:** a person living for the fruits of his work.
- **Mahajana:** a great authority on spiritual life.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *Is a brahmacari higher than a householder?* The aim is the same (SB 7.14.2 purport); the Lord calls dear the devotee with qualities (BG 12.13-14).
2. *Were great devotees householders?* Yes: for example Kardama Muni attained the Lord (SB 3.24.47), and Sandipani Muni, Krishna's teacher, had a wife and son (SB 10.45.37).
3. *Why avoid hanging around homes?* To protect your own heart; the book says the thought "I could also be enjoying this" is the seed of a fall.
4. *How do I thank householders?* By humble service and by respect.
