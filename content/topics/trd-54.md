## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on dps-43, which teaches the controversies (jiva-fall, gopi-bhava, apasampradayas, the poison issue) on their
merits; on trd-50, where criticism of devotees is the mad elephant; and on dps-41, which establishes attachment to the
founder-acharya. This page takes what those leave: the moment when a resident's own faith is shaken by what he saw or
heard, and he must decide where his faith was resting.

## C. Central message

Faith is "confident, firm faith that by rendering transcendental loving service to Krsna one automatically performs all
subsidiary activities" (CC Madhya 22.62). Its objects are Krishna, His words, the spiritual master in disciplic
succession and the devotees' association (BG 4.34; SB 1.2.18), not any person's conduct. When a leader falls, scripture
has already said what to think: "My devotee never perishes" (BG 9.31), "there is no danger of his being unsuccessful"
(SB 1.5.17), and "one who is not a great controller should never imitate the behavior of ruling personalities" (SB
10.33.30). The doubt is taken upward, to guru and sastra, "and chant Hare Krishna regularly without any offense"
(Prabhupada, letter of 16 January 1970).

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Define sraddha from CC Madhya 22.62 and name its proper objects from BG 4.34 and SB 1.2.18.
2. State what Krishna says about a devotee who commits "the most abominable action" (BG 9.30-31) and how Prabhupada's
   purport guards the verse against misuse.
3. Explain SB 10.33.29-31: why the conduct of controllers is not the follower's standard, and why their instructions
   are.
4. Give Prabhupada's answer to a disciple's doubt (letter to Madhusudana, 1970) and to second-hand information (letter
   to Satsvarupa, 1970), and his instruction when leaders had fallen (letter to Balabhadra, 1973).
5. Describe the senior's duty to juniors after a scandal: truth without blasphemy, the standard without the person
   (BG 3.21), and referral where distress is severe.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**CC Madhya 22.62**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/22/62/
> "Śraddhā is confident, firm faith that by rendering transcendental loving service to Kṛṣṇa one automatically performs all subsidiary activities. Such faith is favorable to the discharge of devotional service."
- **Purport point:** "Firm faith and confidence are called śraddhā. When one engages in the Lord's devotional service, he
  is to be understood to have performed all his responsibilities in the material world."
- **Use:** in the talk: faith is defined by its object (service to Krishna), not by trust in an institution's members.

**CC Madhya 22.54**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/22/54/
> "The verdict of all revealed scriptures is that by even a moment's association with a pure devotee, one can attain all success."
- **Use:** in the talk: the devotees' association remains the means after any scandal; one does not leave the devotees
  because one devotee fell.

**BG 4.34**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/4/34/
- **Purport point:** "No one can be a bona fide spiritual master without following this principle of disciplic
  succession… No one can be spiritually realized by manufacturing his own process, as is the fashion of the foolish
  pretenders… mental speculation or dry arguments cannot help lead one to the right path. Nor by independent study of
  books of knowledge can one progress in spiritual life."
- **Use:** in the talk: the object of faith is the spiritual master *in disciplic succession*, measured by sastra; doubt
  is answered by inquiry and submission, not by private speculation.

**BG 4.40**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/4/40/
> "But ignorant and faithless persons who doubt the revealed scriptures do not attain God consciousness; they fall down. For the doubting soul there is happiness neither in this world nor in the next."
- **Purport point:** "those who have no faith and are always doubtful make no progress at all… One should therefore
  follow the principles of revealed scriptures with faith and thereby be raised to the platform of knowledge… doubtful
  persons have no status whatsoever in spiritual emancipation."
- **Use:** in the talk, as it is: a doubt entertained permanently is itself a fall; the verse is about doubting the
  scriptures, and it is read for that.

**BG 17.3**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/17/3/
> "O son of Bharata, according to one's existence under the various modes of nature, one evolves a particular kind of faith. The living being is said to be of a particular faith according to the modes he has acquired."
- **Purport point:** "according to his particular type of faith, one associates with certain persons… one has to become
  cleansed of the material contamination that he has acquired, in order to regain his relationship with the Supreme
  Lord."
- **Use:** in the talk: faith placed in persons according to one's own modes is material faith and can be shaken;
  faith cleansed by hearing rests on Krishna.

**BG 9.30, 9.31**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/9/30/ (and /31/)
> 9.30: "Even if one commits the most abominable action, if he is engaged in devotional service he is to be considered saintly because he is properly situated in his determination."
> 9.31: "He quickly becomes righteous and attains lasting peace. O son of Kuntī, declare it boldly that My devotee never perishes."
- **Purport points:** on 9.30, "As far as possible, a devotee is very cautious so that he does not do anything that
  could disrupt his wholesome condition"; on 9.31, "This should not be misunderstood… one who is engaged in mischievous
  activities cannot become a devotee of the Lord… a devotee who is engaged in the nine kinds of devotional activities is
  engaged in the process of cleansing all material contamination from the heart… here there is no such condition,
  because the purifying process is already there in the heart of the devotee" (the purport continues with the same
  guard; read it whole).
- **Use:** in the talk: how scripture itself regards a devotee who has fallen, with the purport's guard against using the
  verse as a licence. dps-15 used these verses against one's own despair; here the point is how to see another.

**SB 1.5.17**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/5/17/
> "One who has forsaken his material occupations to engage in the devotional service of the Lord may sometimes fall down while in an immature stage, yet there is no danger of his being unsuccessful…"
- **Purport point:** "There are so many instances of this in the histories. Bharata Mahārāja was obliged to take his
  birth as a stag due to his intimate attachment to a stag… Similarly, Citraketu also fell down due to his offenses at
  the feet of Śiva. But in spite of all this, the stress is given here to surrendering unto the lotus feet of the Lord,
  even if there is a chance of falling down."
- **Use:** in the talk: the Bhagavatam records falls of great devotees and still stresses surrender; a fall is "in the
  histories", not a refutation.

**SB 1.2.18, 1.2.19**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/18/ (and /19/)
> 1.2.18: "By regular attendance in classes on the Bhāgavatam and by rendering of service to the pure devotee, all that is troublesome to the heart is almost completely destroyed, and loving service unto the Personality of Godhead… is established as an irrevocable fact."
> 1.2.19: "As soon as irrevocable loving service is established in the heart, the effects of nature's modes of passion and ignorance… disappear from the heart. Then the devotee is established in goodness, and he becomes completely happy."
- **Purport point (1.2.18):** "Here is the remedy for eliminating all inauspicious things within the heart, which are
  considered to be obstacles in the path of self-realization. The remedy is the association of the Bhāgavatas."
- **Use:** in the talk: the remedy for a troubled heart after a scandal is the same as always: hearing the Bhagavatam
  and serving the devotee-Bhagavata; and "irrevocable" is the kind of faith a senior should have reached. dps-15 and
  trd-51 use these verses for nistha and its obstacles; here the point is faith's repair.

**BG 3.21**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/21/
> "Whatever action a great man performs, common men follow. And whatever standards he sets by exemplary acts, all the world pursues."
- **Purport point:** "A leader cannot teach the public to stop smoking if he himself smokes. Lord Caitanya said that a
  teacher should behave properly before he begins teaching… The teacher cannot manufacture rules against the principles
  of revealed scriptures… One who desires to improve himself must follow the standard rules as they are practiced by the
  great teachers."
- **Use:** in the talk: the verse explains why a leader's fall harms many, and the purport gives the follower's rule:
  follow the standard of sastra as the great teachers practised it, not a person who departed from it.

**SB 10.33.29, 10.33.30, 10.33.31**: Verified · BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples ·
https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/10/33/29/ (and /30/, /31/)
> 10.33.29: "The status of powerful controllers is not harmed by any apparently audacious transgression of morality we may see in them, for they are just like fire, which devours everything fed into it and remains unpolluted."
> 10.33.30: "One who is not a great controller should never imitate the behavior of ruling personalities, even mentally. If out of foolishness an ordinary person does imitate such behavior, he will simply destroy himself, just as a person who is not Rudra would destroy himself if he tried to drink an ocean of poison."
> 10.33.31: "The statements of the Lord's empowered servants are always true, and the acts they perform are exemplary when consistent with those statements. Therefore one who is intelligent should carry out their instructions."
- **Context:** Pariksit asks how Krishna, who descended to establish religion, could dance with others' wives; Sukadeva
  answers. The verses are about the Lord and the great controllers (Brahma, Indra, Soma, Visvamitra are the examples
  the purport gives from Sridhara Svami).
- **Purport points (disciples'):** on 10.33.30, "we should not imitate Lord Kṛṣṇa's activities with the gopīs… We should
  not try to imitate Kṛṣṇa, for we will suffer severely"; on 10.33.31, the word *isvara* and the follower's rule: carry
  out the instructions.
- **Application (preacher's), labelled:** the verses are about the Lord and the great controllers, and 10.33.29 is not
  to be applied to an ordinary leader who falls; such a person is not a "powerful controller", and 10.33.30 says plainly
  that an ordinary person who imitates transgression "will simply destroy himself". What the passage gives the
  resident is the rule of 10.33.31: the instructions of the Lord's empowered servants are the standard, and acts are
  exemplary only "when consistent with those statements". A leader's departure from the instructions does not change
  the instructions.
- **Use:** in the talk, with the application labelled and the context stated.

**BG 6.40**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/40/
> "The Supreme Personality of Godhead said: Son of Pṛthā, a transcendentalist engaged in auspicious activities does not meet with destruction either in this world or in the spiritual world; one who does good, My friend, is never overcome by evil."
- **Purport point:** opens by quoting SB 1.5.17 (Narada to Vyasadeva).
- **Use:** in the talk: Krishna's own assurance about the one who fell from yoga; read with 6.41 (dps-15) if time
  allows.

**NOI 6**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/6/
- **Purport point:** "Unless one is actually a devotee, he cannot see another devotee perfectly… The kaniṣṭha-adhikārī
  cannot distinguish between a devotee and nondevotee." And on offence: see trd-50.
- **Use:** in the talk: after a scandal the resident must still not see devotees "from a materialistic point of view";

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **Where faith rests.** Sraddha is faith "that by rendering transcendental loving service to Krsna one automatically
   performs all subsidiary activities" (CC Madhya 22.62). Its objects are Krishna and His word, the spiritual master in
   disciplic succession measured by sastra (BG 4.34), and the devotees' association (SB 1.2.18; CC Madhya 22.54). A
   resident whose faith rested on a particular leader's conduct had placed it, as BG 17.3 says, "according to the modes
   he has acquired"; it was material faith, and it was going to be shaken by something.
2. **What scripture already says about a devotee who falls.** BG 9.30-31: he is "to be considered saintly because he is
   properly situated in his determination", and "My devotee never perishes"; the purport guards the verse: it is no
   licence, and "one who is engaged in mischievous activities cannot become a devotee". SB 1.5.17: a devotee "may
   sometimes fall down while in an immature stage, yet there is no danger of his being unsuccessful"; the purport lists
   Bharata and Citraketu. The Bhagavatam is not embarrassed by falls; it records them and still stresses surrender. So
   a resident who reads of a fall has not learnt something scripture did not know.
3. **The controller and the follower.** SB 10.33.29-31 answers Pariksit about the Lord's own conduct. For the resident
   the usable verse is 10.33.31: the empowered servants' statements are true, their acts exemplary "when consistent with
   those statements", and the intelligent person carries out the instructions. When a leader's act is not consistent
   with the instructions, the instructions stand and the act does not become a standard (BG 3.21 purport: "The teacher
   cannot manufacture rules against the principles of revealed scriptures"). 10.33.29 is about the Lord and the great
   controllers and is not a cover for anyone; 10.33.30 says the imitator "will simply destroy himself".
4. **The doubt itself.** BG 4.40 is read as it is: the person who doubts the revealed scriptures falls and has no
   happiness. The resident's doubt after a scandal is usually not about scripture but about people; the danger is that
   it migrates. Prabhupada's letter to Madhusudana treats a disciple's returning doubt bluntly: after four years, "you
   are feeling doubts means certainly you are being attacked by Maya", and the remedy is "chant Hare Krishna regularly
   without any offense." The letter to Satsvarupa adds the rule for rumour: "do not be disturbed by second-hand
   information." The letter to Balabhadra gives the rule when leaders fall: "Do not join them. Stay in the association
   of our Temple and work according to established programs."
5. **Upward, not outward.** BG 4.34's purport: inquiry and submission to the spiritual master, not "mental speculation
   or dry arguments" nor "independent study". trd-50's purport on CC Madhya 19.157: "if there is any doubt, one should
   consult the spiritual master." The doubt is spoken, to guru and senior Vaisnavas (dps-02), and answered from sastra.
   It is not spoken to juniors, not posted, not resolved by leaving.
6. **The senior's duty to juniors.** BG 3.21: they follow what he does. After a scandal he must neither lie ("nothing
   happened") nor blaspheme (trd-50). He tells the truth that is his to tell, points to the standard of sastra and the
   acharyas' practice (3.21 purport), and shows by his own steadiness where faith rests. Hearing the Bhagavatam together
   is the repair (SB 1.2.18).
7. **The medical line.** A scandal involving someone close, or a doubt that becomes sleeplessness and dread, can be
   more than a spiritual question. If distress is severe or lasting, the resident sees a doctor as well; in India
   Tele-MANAS 14416 is free and confidential. This is not a failure of faith.
8. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *If he could fall, the process does not work.* SB 1.5.17 and BG 9.30-31 are the process's own account of falls.
     The process is hearing and service (SB 1.2.18); a person's departure from it does not test it.
   - *BG 9.30 means his conduct does not matter.* The purport forbids this reading.
   - *SB 10.33.29 means leaders are above morality.* It is about the Lord and the great controllers; 10.33.30 condemns
     the ordinary imitator to destruction. The application to a fallen leader is the opposite of a cover.
   - *A senior should have no doubts.* Arjuna had them before Krishna (BG 2.7, trd-52); the rule is where they are
     taken.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes).** Say: "This is the last of six sessions on the blockers senior devotees meet.
It is the one no one wants: a leader falls, a scandal is read, an old doubt returns. We will not discuss any case; dps-43
covers the controversies on their merits, and the mad-elephant session covers why we do not blaspheme. Tonight is only
about our own faith: where it was resting when it was shaken, and where scripture says it should rest." Read CC Madhya
22.62. Then: "Faith, by Rupa Gosvami's definition, is faith in service to Krishna. We will see what Krishna and the
Bhagavatam already said about devotees who fall, and what Prabhupada told disciples who doubted."

**H.2 Opening questions:**
1. "When your faith was last shaken, what exactly was it resting on at that moment: a verse, a person, a result?"
2. "To whom did you speak first: a junior, a friend, or your spiritual master?"
3. "What did you tell the juniors who asked you about it?"

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Faith is defined by its object: service to Krishna.** CC Madhya 22.62; BG 4.34; CC Madhya 22.54. Example
   (preacher's application): two residents hear the same news; one had faith in a man, one in the process; only one is
   shaken. Transition: material faith.
2. **Faith placed by one's modes is material and will be shaken.** BG 17.3 and purport. Transition: what scripture says
   of falls.
3. **Scripture already recorded falls and still stresses surrender.** SB 1.5.17 (Bharata, Citraketu); BG 9.30-31 with
   the purport's guard; BG 6.40. Example: a resident who reads SB 5 for the first time after a scandal and finds Bharata
   there. Transition: leaders.
4. **The instructions are the standard; acts are exemplary only when consistent with them.** SB 10.33.31 (context
   stated); BG 3.21 purport. Example: a leader taught the four principles and broke one; the principles did not change.
   Transition: the doubt.
5. **A doubt kept is a fall; a doubt taken upward is inquiry.** BG 4.40; letter to Madhusudana; BG 4.34 purport.
   Transition: rumour.
6. **"Do not be disturbed by second-hand information"; "Do not join them. Stay."** Letters to Satsvarupa and
   Balabhadra. Transition: the juniors.
7. **Truth without blasphemy; the standard without the person; hear the Bhagavatam together.** BG 3.21; SB 1.2.18;
   the medical line. Transition to M.

## J. Stories and illustrations

- **Bharata Maharaja and Citraketu, as SB 1.5.17's purport cites them.** Bharata "was obliged to take his birth as a
  stag due to his intimate attachment to a stag"; Citraketu "fell down due to his offenses at the feet of Siva". The
  purport's lesson, stated in its own words: "in spite of all this, the stress is given here to surrendering unto the
  lotus feet of the Lord, even if there is a chance of falling down." The full histories are SB 5.7-8 and SB 6.17; read
  them before telling more than the purport tells.
- **Pariksit's question about the rasa dance (SB 10.33.26-31, context).** The king asks how the Lord who descended to
  establish religion could act as He did; Sukadeva answers that the great controllers are not harmed and that no one
  else may imitate them, and that the intelligent carry out the empowered servants' instructions. Devotional lesson for
  this page: the Bhagavatam itself raises the hardest question about conduct and answers it by pointing to the
  instructions; the resident does the same. The verses are told as they are; the application to an ordinary leader is
  labelled the preacher's (E).
- **Arjuna's doubt (BG 2.7, taught in trd-52).** Not retold; pointed to: the model for where a doubt is taken.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. *"A senior I followed for twenty years has fallen. What was the point of following him?"* What you followed, if you
   followed rightly, was the instruction he carried (SB 10.33.31; BG 4.34 purport: the spiritual master "can convey the
   message of the Lord as it is"). The message was not his and did not fall. Your service, by BG 2.40 (trd-52), is not
   lost. Grieve, speak to your spiritual master or senior Vaisnavas, and keep the programme (letter to Balabhadra).
2. *"Does BG 9.30 mean we excuse him?"* No; the purport forbids that reading, and institutional consequences are the
   authorities' duty. BG 9.30-31 tells you how Krishna regards the fallen devotee's future, so that you neither
   blaspheme him (trd-50) nor conclude the process failed.
3. *"Is it blasphemy to acknowledge that he fell?"* No. Stating a fact that is yours to state, without relish and
   without broadcast, is not ninda; the mad elephant is contempt, enjoyment of the fault, and spreading it (trd-50).
   SB 1.5.17's purport itself records the falls of great devotees.
4. *"What do I tell the juniors?"* The truth that is yours to tell, briefly; the standard of sastra and the acharyas'
   practice (BG 3.21 purport); where faith rests (CC Madhya 22.62); and then hear the Bhagavatam with them (SB 1.2.18).
   Never "nothing happened", never the details, never a verdict on the person's destination.
5. *"My doubt is now about the philosophy itself."* Then it is the doubt BG 4.40 names, and it is urgent. Take it to
   your spiritual master and to sastra with "inquiries and submission" (BG 4.34 purport); dps-43 answers the common
   ones; and chant without offence (letter to Madhusudana). Do not resolve it alone or by leaving.

## L. Case studies

**Case 1 (Invented case for discussion).** A resident of fourteen years learns that a preacher whose classes brought
him to the temple has left and broken his vows. He feels that his own first years were "built on a lie" and stops
reading. *Principles:* BG 17.3 (faith placed in a person by one's modes); SB 10.33.31 (the instructions are the
standard); SB 1.5.17; letter to Balabhadra. *Questions:* What did he receive in those classes: the preacher, or
Prabhupada's message? Where is his faith resting now? *Analysis:* the message that moved him was true then and is true

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Sooner or later every devotee sees a senior fall, reads a scandal, or feels an old doubt return. Rupa Gosvami defines
faith: "Sraddha is confident, firm faith that by rendering transcendental loving service to Krsna one automatically
performs all subsidiary activities" (CC Madhya 22.62). Faith is in service to Krishna, and it rests on Krishna's word,
on the spiritual master in disciplic succession measured by scripture (BG 4.34), and on the association of devotees
(SB 1.2.18; CC Madhya 22.54). Faith that rested on a particular person's conduct was placed, as the Gita says,
"according to the modes" one has acquired (BG 17.3); it was material, and something was going to shake it.

Scripture already knew that devotees fall. Krishna says, "Even if one commits the most abominable action, if he is
engaged in devotional service he is to be considered saintly… declare it boldly that My devotee never perishes"
(BG 9.30-31), and Srila Prabhupada's purport warns that this is no licence: "one who is engaged in mischievous
activities cannot become a devotee." Narada tells Vyasadeva that a devotee "may sometimes fall down while in an
immature stage, yet there is no danger of his being unsuccessful" (SB 1.5.17); the purport names Bharata Maharaja and
Citraketu and says that "in spite of all this, the stress is given here to surrendering." When Pariksit asked how the
Lord Himself could act as He did in the rasa dance, Sukadeva answered that the great controllers are not harmed, that no
ordinary person may imitate them "even mentally" without destroying himself, and that "the statements of the Lord's
empowered servants are always true, and the acts they perform are exemplary when consistent with those statements.
Therefore one who is intelligent should carry out their instructions" (SB 10.33.29-31). For us the rule is the last
verse: a leader's act that departs from the instructions does not change the instructions. "The teacher cannot
manufacture rules against the principles of revealed scriptures" (BG 3.21 purport).

A doubt about the scriptures, kept forever, is itself a fall: "For the doubting soul there is happiness neither in this
world nor in the next" (BG 4.40). So the doubt is taken upward, to the spiritual master and scripture, with "inquiries
and submission" (BG 4.34 purport), not outward to juniors or the internet, and not resolved by leaving. Prabhupada wrote
to a doubting disciple of four years: "now you are feeling doubts means certainly you are being attacked by Maya. So
please take care and chant Hare Krishna regularly without any offense" (16 January 1970). To another: "Please do not be
disturbed by second-hand information" (19 February 1970). And when two leaders had "almost fallen down": "Do not join
them. Stay in the association of our Temple and work according to established programs" (13 December 1973). A senior
asked by juniors tells the truth that is his to tell, without blasphemy (trd-50), points to the standard of scripture,
and hears the Bhagavatam with them, because "by regular attendance in classes on the Bhagavatam and by rendering of
service to the pure devotee, all that is troublesome to the heart is almost completely destroyed" (SB 1.2.18). If the
distress is severe or lasting, he also sees a doctor; in India Tele-MANAS 14416 is free and confidential.

## S2. Key ideas
- Faith is faith in service to Krishna; it rests on His word, guru in succession and the devotees, not on any
  person's conduct (CC Madhya 22.62; BG 4.34; SB 1.2.18).
- Scripture records falls of great devotees and still stresses surrender (SB 1.5.17; BG 9.30-31, with the purport's
  guard).
- The instructions are the standard; a leader's act is exemplary only when consistent with them (SB 10.33.31).
- A doubt is taken upward, to guru and scripture, and chanting continues without offence (BG 4.34; Prabhupada, 1970).
- Juniors are given truth without blasphemy, the standard without the person, and Bhagavatam hearing together.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **CC Madhya 22.62**: "confident, firm faith that by rendering transcendental loving service to Krsna…" For me: my
  faith is in service, not in a person.
- **SB 10.33.31**: "the acts they perform are exemplary when consistent with those statements." For me: I follow the
  instruction; I do not copy the departure.
- **BG 9.31**: "declare it boldly that My devotee never perishes." For me: I do not write off a fallen devotee, and I
  do not take the verse as a licence.

## S4. Words to know
- **Sraddha:** faith; firm confidence that serving Krishna fulfils all duties.
- **Parampara:** the chain of disciplic succession through which the message comes unchanged.
- **Isvara:** controller; in SB 10.33 the Lord and the great controllers, not ordinary leaders.
- **Ninda:** blasphemy, criticism with contempt (trd-50).
- **Tele-MANAS 14416:** India's free, confidential mental-health helpline.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *A senior I followed for years has fallen. What was the point?* What you followed, if rightly, was the message he
   carried; it did not fall (SB 10.33.31; BG 4.34 purport). Your service is not lost (BG 2.40). Speak to your spiritual
   master and keep the programme.
2. *Does BG 9.30 mean we excuse him?* No; the purport forbids that. It tells us how Krishna regards his future, so we
   neither blaspheme nor conclude the process failed.
3. *Is acknowledging the fall blasphemy?* Stating a fact that is yours to state, without relish and without spreading
   it, is not. Contempt and broadcast are (trd-50).
4. *What do I tell juniors?* The truth that is yours to tell, briefly; the standard of scripture; where faith rests;
   then hear the Bhagavatam with them. Never "nothing happened".
5. *My doubt is about the philosophy itself.* That is the urgent kind (BG 4.40). Take it to your spiritual master and
   scripture with inquiry and submission (BG 4.34), and chant without offence.

## S6. Check your understanding
1. On what does faith rest, according to BG 4.34 and SB 1.2.18?
2. Which two fallen devotees does the SB 1.5.17 purport name, and what does it still stress?
3. According to SB 10.33.31, when are the acts of the Lord's empowered servants exemplary?
4. True or false: BG 9.30 means a devotee's conduct does not matter.
5. What did Prabhupada tell a disciple disturbed by rumours?

**Answers:** 1. The spiritual master in disciplic succession, measured by scripture, and the association of devotees
(BG 4.34; SB 1.2.18). 2. Bharata Maharaja and Citraketu; surrender to the Lord "even if there is a chance of falling
down" (SB 1.5.17 purport). 3. When consistent with their statements (SB 10.33.31). 4. False: the purport says one
engaged in mischievous activities cannot become a devotee (BG 9.31 purport). 5. "Please do not be disturbed by
second-hand information" (letter to Satsvarupa, 19 February 1970).
