# Dharma ki adalat

> **VL1 · 45–60 min · review: sources checked, awaiting senior review · sensitive: true.** The Hindi title ("the court of dharma") is kept as
> given. The talk puts *dharma* on trial: four common definitions are cross-examined and the Bhagavatam's own
> definition is the verdict. **Sensitive** because the word *dharma* is used in public life for political and
> communal claims. This page makes no political claim, characterises no community, and treats dharma as the
> Bhagavatam does: "for all humanity" (SB 1.2.6). Read K before teaching. IYF holds no notes for this topic; one
> IYF recording (HG Sundar Gopal Prabhu, "SB 1.2.6, Yuga Dharma", 25 Nov 2017) and one Preaching Encyclopedia
> entry ("Sankirtan yagna is dharma for Kali Yuga") inform the last segment.

|---|---|

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

The word is everywhere and means nothing definite to most young people; many have decided it means "the old
rules" or "the politics of religion" and have stopped listening. A courtroom gives them permission to doubt
out loud. Each popular definition is a "witness"; the audience cross-examines it; the scripture gives the
verdict. At VL1 the verdict is deliberately simple: dharma is what sustains your real self, it is "for all
humanity", and in this age it is as simple as hearing and chanting.

How the subject grows at other levels:

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **VL1** (here) | Four popular definitions tested; the Bhagavatam's definition; the four legs; the dharma of the age |
| VL2 | *Dharma* and *kaitava-dharma* (SB 1.1.2); varnasrama as social dharma; "If God is one, why so many religions?" |
| VL3 | Living the yuga-dharma: chanting as practice (VL3-04); the four principles as the four legs (SB 1.17.24) |
| SPS | *Sarva-dharman parityajya* (BG 18.66) in depth; *bhagavata-dharma* (the dharma taught in the Bhagavatam) |
| DPS | The Yamaduta–Visnuduta debate (SB 6.1–3): how dharma is judged in the next life |
| BSS | Dharma as the student household's daily duty, tested by SB 1.2.8 |

**Not done here:**
- Any comment on the word's use by parties, governments or movements. The talk is about what the *Bhagavatam*
  means by dharma; what anyone else means by it is their business.
- Comparison of religions. SB 1.2.6 says "for all humanity"; the talk stays there.
- Varnasrama and social duty in detail (VL2).
- The Yamaduta–Visnuduta debate: only its one-line conclusion (SB 6.3.19) is used.

## C. Central message

Dharma is not a costume, a club or a cage. In the Bhagavatam it is "that which sustains one's existence": the
soul's living connection with God, open to all humanity. Any practice that awakens that connection is dharma;
any that does not is "useless labor", however religious it looks. In this age the simplest form of it is to
hear and chant the Lord's names.

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. State the Bhagavatam's definition of dharma (SB 1.2.6 and purport) in their own words.
2. Apply its test (SB 1.2.8: does the practice awaken attraction for God?) to a religious act, a duty and a
   ritual.
3. Explain why dharma is said to be "enacted by the Supreme Personality of Godhead" (SB 6.3.19) rather than
   invented by communities, and why this makes it universal, not sectarian.
4. Describe the four legs of dharma (SB 1.17.24) and name which one is left in this age.
5. Say what the dharma of this age is (SB 12.3.51–52; 11.5.32) and try its simplest form once.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** SB 1.2.6, 1.2.8 and 1.17.24–25 carry the trial; SB 12.3.51–52 give the verdict for this age. The rest
prepare you for questions.

**SB 1.2.6**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/6/
> "The supreme occupation [dharma] for all humanity is that by which men can attain to loving devotional service unto the transcendent Lord. Such devotional service must be unmotivated and uninterrupted to completely satisfy the self."
- **Context:** Suta Gosvami answers the sages of Naimisaranya, who asked for the essence of all scripture.
- **Teaching:** The highest dharma, for all humanity, is that by which one attains loving service to God;
  it must be unmotivated and uninterrupted, and it satisfies the self.
- **Purport point:** "We have purposely denoted dharma as occupation because the root meaning of the word
  dharma is 'that which sustains one's existence.' A living being's sustenance of existence is to coordinate
  his activities with his eternal relation with the Supreme Lord Kṛṣṇa."
- **Application (preacher's):** Three words in the translation decide the trial: "for all humanity" (dharma is
  not a community's property), "unmotivated" (not a bargain with God), and "satisfy the self" (the test is
  inner, not social).
- **Use:** in the talk (the verdict).

**SB 1.2.8**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/8/
> "The occupational activities a man performs according to his own position are only so much useless labor if they do not provoke attraction for the message of the Personality of Godhead."
- **Teaching:** Duties well performed are wasted labour if they do not awaken attraction for God's message.
- **Purport point:** "Simply by cleansing the cage of the bird, one does not satisfy the bird. One must actually
  know the needs of the bird himself." And: "any occupational activity which does not help one to achieve
  attachment for hearing and chanting the transcendental message of Godhead is said herein to be simply a waste
  of time."
- **Application (preacher's):** This is the court's single test question, put to every witness: "Does it awaken
  attraction for God, or only polish the cage?"
- **Use:** in the talk (the test).

**SB 1.1.2**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/1/2/
> "Completely rejecting all religious activities which are materially motivated, this Bhāgavata Purāṇa propounds the highest truth…"
- **Teaching:** The Bhagavatam sets aside religion practised for material gain (*kaitava-dharma*).
- **Purport point:** "Religion includes four primary subjects, namely pious activities, economic development,
  satisfaction of the senses, and finally liberation from material bondage." The purport observes that when
  material gains come by other means, "so-called religion is neglected", which "practically proves that religion
  is performed for some economic gains".
- **Application (preacher's):** The witness "dharma = a deal with God" is dismissed by the Bhagavatam's own
  opening verse. At VL1 say "religion as a bargain" and leave *kaitava* for VL2.
- **Use:** in the talk, one line.

**SB 6.3.19**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/6/3/19/
> "Real religious principles are enacted by the Supreme Personality of Godhead. Although fully situated in the mode of goodness, even the great ṛṣis who occupy the topmost planets cannot ascertain the real religious principles…"
- **Context:** Yamaraja instructs his servants after their defeat in the Ajamila episode.
- **Teaching:** Real dharma is given by God; not even the greatest sages manufacture it.
- **Purport point:** "Herein it is stated that the real religious principle is that which is given by the
  Supreme Personality of Godhead. That principle is stated in Bhagavad-gītā. Sarva-dharmān parityajya mām
  ekaṁ śaraṇaṁ vraja: one should give up all other duties and surrender unto the lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa."
- **Application (preacher's):** This is why dharma is not any community's property: nobody invented it, so
  nobody owns it. Say exactly that, and nothing more pointed.
- **Use:** in the talk (why dharma is universal).

**BG 4.7**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/4/7/
> "Whenever and wherever there is a decline in religious practice, O descendant of Bharata, and a predominant rise of irreligion – at that time I descend Myself."
- **Teaching:** The Lord Himself descends to restore dharma.
- **Purport point:** "Only the Lord can manufacture a system of religion." And, important for this page: "It is
  not a fact that the Lord appears only on Indian soil. He can manifest Himself anywhere and everywhere, and
  whenever He desires to appear."
- **Application (preacher's):** The second purport sentence is the page's safeguard against any nationalist
  reading: dharma's author is not tied to a country.
- **Use:** in the talk (one line) and K.

**BG 4.8**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/4/8/
> "To deliver the pious and to annihilate the miscreants, as well as to reestablish the principles of religion, I Myself appear, millennium after millennium."
- **Teaching:** The purpose of the Lord's appearances includes re-establishing dharma.
- **Application (preacher's):** Together with 4.7, this grounds the last segment: dharma takes a form fit for
  each age, and in this age that form was established by Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu (SB 11.5.32).
- **Use:** background.

**BG 18.66**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/66/
> "Abandon all varieties of religion and just surrender unto Me. I shall deliver you from all sinful reactions. Do not fear."
- **Teaching:** The Gita's conclusion: give up all varieties of dharma and surrender to the Lord.
- **Purport point:** "According to the devotional process, one should simply accept such religious principles
  that will lead ultimately to the devotional service of the Lord. One may perform a particular occupational duty
  according to his position in the social order, but if by executing his duty one does not come to the point of
  Kṛṣṇa consciousness, all his activities are in vain."
- **Application (preacher's):** At VL1 this verse is the closing line of the verdict, not a segment. Do not let
  it sound like "abandon your religion"; the purport reads it as "keep what leads to God, drop the rest".
- **Use:** in the talk, one line.

**SB 1.17.24**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/17/24/
> "In the age of Satya [truthfulness] your four legs were established by the four principles of austerity, cleanliness, mercy and truthfulness. But it appears that three of your legs are broken due to rampant irreligion in the form of pride, lust for women, and intoxication."
- **Context:** King Pariksit addresses Dharma, who has appeared as a bull with three legs broken.
- **Teaching:** Dharma stands on four legs: austerity, cleanliness, mercy, truthfulness; three are broken by
  pride, lust and intoxication.
- **Purport point:** "Man cannot check all these evils simply by statutory acts and police vigilance, but he can
  cure the disease of the mind by the proper medicine, namely advocating the principles of brahminical culture
  or the principles of austerity, cleanliness, mercy and truthfulness."
- **Application (preacher's):** Here is a definition of dharma that no community can object to and every
  listener can measure himself by. Soften "lust for women" to "lust" for a mixed room (a
  wording choice for the room; the verse's own words are "lust for women").
- **Use:** in the talk (the evidence).

**SB 1.17.25**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/17/25/
> "You are now standing on one leg only, which is your truthfulness, and you are somehow or other hobbling along. But quarrel personified [Kali], flourishing by deceit, is also trying to destroy that leg."
- **Teaching:** In this age only truthfulness remains, and deceit attacks even that.
- **Purport point:** "The principles of religion do not stand on some dogmas or man-made formulas, but they
  stand on four primary regulative observances…" and "by too much lying propaganda, truthfulness is spoiled."
- **Application (preacher's):** The purport's first sentence is a gift for this talk: dharma is "not dogmas or
  man-made formulas". The young sceptic in the room is half right.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 12.3.51** *(BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples)*: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/12/3/51/
> "My dear King, although Kali-yuga is an ocean of faults, there is still one good quality about this age: Simply by chanting the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra, one can become free from material bondage and be promoted to the transcendental kingdom."
- **Teaching:** The one good quality of this age: chanting frees one.
- **Purport point:** "Just as one powerful king can kill innumerable thieves, one brilliant spiritual quality
  can destroy all the contamination of this age."
- **Use:** in the talk (the verdict for this age).

**SB 12.3.52** *(BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples)*: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/12/3/52/
> "Whatever result was obtained in Satya-yuga by meditating on Viṣṇu, in Tretā-yuga by performing sacrifices, and in Dvāpara-yuga by serving the Lord's lotus feet can be obtained in Kali-yuga simply by chanting the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra."
- **Teaching:** Each age has its form of dharma; in this age it is chanting.
- **Purport point:** The purport quotes parallel verses from the Visnu, Padma and Brhan-naradiya Puranas and
  concludes that "massive propaganda should be made all over the world to induce people to chant".
- **Application (preacher's):** The word "all over the world" keeps the ending universal.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 11.5.32** *(BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples)*: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/5/32/
> "In the Age of Kali, intelligent persons perform congregational chanting to worship the incarnation of Godhead who constantly sings the names of Kṛṣṇa. Although His complexion is not blackish, He is Kṛṣṇa Himself…"
- **Teaching:** The incarnation for this age is worshipped by congregational chanting.
- **Purport point:** The purport reproduces Srila Prabhupada's commentary on the same verse at CC Adi 3.52,
  identifying this incarnation as Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu, "who is worshiped by intelligent men in this age".
- **Application (preacher's):** One sentence at VL1: the one who established this age's dharma did it by
  chanting in the streets with everyone. Who He is becomes a topic at VL3 (proposed) and SPS.
- **Use:** in the talk, one line.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** Dharma is "that which sustains one's existence" (SB 1.2.6 purport): the soul's eternal
   relation with God, lived. It is "for all humanity" (1.2.6), given by God rather than devised by sages or
   communities (6.3.19; BG 4.7 purport), and it stands on four observable legs: austerity, cleanliness, mercy,
   truthfulness (SB 1.17.24). Its test is simple: does a practice awaken attraction for God's message? If not,
   it is "useless labor" (1.2.8). In this age its simplest and recommended form is hearing and chanting the
   Lord's names (SB 12.3.51–52; 11.5.32).
2. **Why it matters.** A young person who thinks dharma means "my community's rules" either obeys resentfully
   or rejects the whole subject. The Bhagavatam's definition rescues the word: it is about what sustains *you*,
   and it has a test you can apply yourself.
3. **Connections.** Follows VL1-45: the "centre" a group needs is this. Leads to VL1-47 "India vs Bharat":
   Bharata-varsa is valued in scripture only as a place where this dharma is easy to practise. At VL2 the four
   witnesses return as philosophy (*kaitava-dharma*, varnasrama, "why so many religions?"); at VL3 the fourth
   leg and the yuga-dharma become daily practice.
4. **Consequences.** Someone who holds this definition stops arguing about whose dharma is right and asks
   whether *his own practice* passes the test of 1.2.8. He measures himself by four legs, not by labels, and
   tries the age's prescribed practice to see whether it does what the verse claims.
5. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - **"Dharma = Hinduism" (or any one religion).** SB 1.2.6: "for all humanity". The Lord "can manifest
     Himself anywhere" (BG 4.7 purport). The Bhagavatam's dharma is older and wider than any label on a form.
   - **"Dharma = the rules of my family or community."** "The principles of religion do not stand on some
     dogmas or man-made formulas" (SB 1.17.25 purport). Where a custom passes the four-leg test and the 1.2.8
     test, keep it; where it does not, it is custom, not dharma.
   - **"Dharma = a deal: I do the ritual, God gives the marks."** SB 1.1.2 sets aside religion for material
     gain; SB 1.2.6 requires "unmotivated".
   - **"Dharma = whatever duty life hands me."** Duty is honoured (1.2.8 speaks of "occupational activities")
     but judged by its result: does it awaken attraction for God?
   - **"Sarva-dharman parityajya means abandon religion."** The purport reads it as: accept what leads to
     devotion, drop what does not (BG 18.66 purport).
6. **Pitch for VL1.** Keep the courtroom light and a little theatrical; let the audience be the jury. Use
   *dharma* (defined), *Kali-yuga* (defined as "this age"), and no other Sanskrit. No current affairs, no
   community named, no comparison of faiths. The verdict verses do the work.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet, say your name, and say that "dharma" is a Sanskrit word often translated as religion, duty or law; today we test what it means.
2. Set up the room as a court: the word is "accused", the audience is the jury.
3. Ask H.2 question 1; take two answers and thank each.
4. Write the answers on the board as "first definitions".
5. Say the opening in role below and call the first witness.

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the room's own life)
1. When you hear the word "dharma", what is the first thing you picture or think?
2. Who first taught you what is the right thing to do: a parent, a village elder, a teacher, a book?
3. Is there something you do every week out of habit, not because you chose it? Does it make you feel more alive or just busy?

- **Opening (in role):** "This court is now in session. The accused is a word: *dharma*. The charge: that it
  means everything and therefore nothing. Religion, duty, ritual, morality, politics. You are the jury. Four
  witnesses will testify. At the end you decide what the word means, and whether you want anything to do with it."
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** Ankit's grandmother says dharma means doing the morning puja. His
  professor says dharma means doing your job well. A news anchor says dharma means standing up for the community.
  His friend says dharma is "all of that, and I want none of it". Who is right?
- **Transition:** "The Bhagavatam was asked exactly this question, by sages who wanted the essence of all
  scripture. Its answer is one verse."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** They will hear this word in every family argument and every election for
  the rest of their lives. Knowing what it means in the text itself is a kind of freedom.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **The test.** "…useless labor if they do not provoke attraction for the message of the Personality of Godhead"
   (SB 1.2.8); "cleansing the cage… does not satisfy the bird" (purport). *Example:* a perfectly performed
   ritual with the mind on the exam. *Transition:* let the witnesses face this test.
2. **Dharma is for all humanity and nobody invented it.** SB 1.2.6; 6.3.19; BG 4.7 purport ("not… only on
   Indian soil"). *Example:* gravity is not Newton's property. *Transition:* so it is not a club.
3. **Dharma is not a bargain.** SB 1.1.2; "unmotivated" (1.2.6). *Example:* the vow kept only until the result
   arrives. *Transition:* nor is it a cage.
4. **Dharma has four legs you can see.** SB 1.17.24–25: austerity, cleanliness, mercy, truthfulness; broken by
   pride, lust, intoxication; deceit attacks the last. "Not… dogmas or man-made formulas" (1.17.25 purport).
   *Example:* ask the jury which leg their own week stands on. *Transition:* so what sustains it in an age where
   three legs are gone?
5. **Dharma is what sustains your existence, and in this age it is simple.** SB 1.2.6 purport; SB 12.3.51–52 (deeper verse; the talk uses only its plain meaning);
   SB 11.5.32 (one line); BG 18.66 purport (keep what leads to God). *Example:* ten minutes of chanting at the
   end of this programme is the age's dharma in its simplest form. *Transition:* to the sentence (P).

## J. Stories and illustrations

- **The bull Dharma and King Pariksit** (SB 1.16–17; verified verses 1.17.24–25). Dharma appears as a bull with
  three legs broken, beside the weeping earth; Kali is beating him. Pariksit asks who has hurt him; the bull
  will not blame anyone, and the king identifies the four legs and what broke them. *Connection:* a definition
  of dharma by its visible legs, and a diagnosis of the age. Tell from the text; do not add dialogue.
- **The sages' question at Naimisaranya** (SB 1.1–2; verified verses 1.2.6, 1.2.8). Sages ask Suta Gosvami to
  give the essence of all scripture for people of this age; his first answer is the definition of dharma.
  *Connection:* the verdict was itself the answer to a court-like inquiry.
- **Ajamila and the two sets of messengers** (SB 6.1–3; verified verse 6.3.19): tell only the conclusion.
  Yamaraja's servants claimed to know dharma from the Vedas and were corrected: real dharma is given by God and
  not even great sages ascertain it. *Connection:* W2 (dharma as a community's property) dismissed. The full
  story is SPS/DPS.
- **Contemporary illustration (invented, labelled):** Ankit's four definitions (H).
- **Left out:** any "witness" drawn from a named community, party or public figure; the Encyclopedia's detail
  about Prakasananda Sarasvati (CC Adi 7) is useful background for the preacher but not told at VL1.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

> **Before you teach:** this word is contested in public life. Someone may try to pull the talk toward a party,
> a government or another community, for or against. The page's answer is the Bhagavatam's own: dharma is "for
> all humanity" (SB 1.2.6), nobody invented it (SB 6.3.19), and the Lord is not tied to any soil (BG 4.7
> purport). Do not take a side, do not characterise any group, and if pressed say "that is a question about
> politics; this talk is about a word in a text". *(Pastoral guidance, not a scriptural claim.)*

1. **"Isn't 'dharma' just the Hindu word for religion?"**
   - The Bhagavatam's word is wider than any label: "for all humanity" (SB 1.2.6), and its root meaning is
     "that which sustains one's existence" (purport). The Lord who gives it "can manifest Himself anywhere and
     everywhere" (BG 4.7 purport). The talk uses the word as the text does, not as a form asks for it.
2. **"My religion says something different about God. Is my dharma false then?"**
   - This talk does not compare religions. It gives one test from one text: does a practice awaken love for God
     and sustain the self (SB 1.2.6, 1.2.8)? Apply it to your own practice; you are the jury of that. Comparisons
     belong, if anywhere, to VL2.
3. **"You say nobody invented dharma, but every scripture was written by someone."**
   - SB 6.3.19 says real dharma is enacted by God and that even great sages "cannot ascertain" it; the
     texts claim to transmit it, not to invent it. Whether that claim is true is a fair VL2 question
     (VL1-08 "Are Vedas mythology", VL1-14 "Faithology"). Here the point is narrower: a dharma nobody invented
     is a dharma nobody owns.
4. **"So rituals are useless?"**
   - No. The test is in the result: a ritual that awakens attraction for God passes; one performed with the mind
     elsewhere is "cleansing the cage" (SB 1.2.8 purport). The same act can be dharma for one person and labour
     for another.
5. **"'Abandon all varieties of religion' (BG 18.66) sounds like abandoning dharma itself."**
   - The purport reads it as keeping what leads to devotion and dropping what does not; "if by executing his duty
     one does not come to the point of Kṛṣṇa consciousness, all his activities are in vain". It is the court's
     closing line, not an attack on anyone's practice.
6. **"The four legs are just morality. Why bring God into it?"**
   - Fair observation, and SB 1.17.24–25 is one of the most "secular" definitions of dharma anywhere. The text
     adds that these legs are broken in this age and that the remedy is not law and police but the "medicine" of
     reviving those principles (1.17.24 purport), which it ties to glorifying the Lord (1.17.25 purport:
     "cleanliness of the mind… is effected by glorifying the Supreme Lord"). Offer the connection as the text's
     claim, to be tested.
7. **"Chanting is the dharma of the age? That is a big claim."**
   - It is the text's claim (SB 12.3.51–52), and it is testable: the programme ends with ten minutes of it.
     Nobody is asked to believe first.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The grandmother's puja.** Ankit does the morning puja because his grandmother insists, with his mind
on the exam. His grandmother does the same puja with tears in her eyes.
- **Dilemma:** Same act; is it dharma for both?
- **Principles:** SB 1.2.8 (the test: attraction for God, or cleaning the cage); SB 1.2.6 ("satisfy the self").
- **Questions:** Which of the two is practising dharma by the Bhagavatam's test? What would make Ankit's act
  pass? Is the problem the ritual or the mind?
- **Takeaway:** Dharma is judged by what it awakens, not by what it looks like.

**Case 2: The argument on the train.** Two passengers argue loudly: one says dharma means defending "our"
community; the other says dharma is a word for oppression and should be dropped. Rhea, listening, is asked to
take a side.
- **Principles:** SB 1.2.6 ("for all humanity"); SB 6.3.19 (nobody invented it); SB 1.17.24–25 (four legs:
  which is each passenger standing on?); BG 4.7 purport.
- **Questions:** Does either passenger pass the four-leg test in the argument itself (mercy? truthfulness)? What
  can Rhea say that takes neither side and keeps the text's meaning? When is silence the dharma?
- **Takeaway:** The word belongs to nobody; the legs belong to everybody.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first; praise every try)
1. For whom is the supreme dharma, according to SB 1.2.6? *For all humanity, not one community.*
2. What does the word's root meaning say dharma is? *That which sustains one's existence.*
3. What is the one test in SB 1.2.8? *Does the duty or ritual awaken attraction for the message of God? If not, it is useless labour.*
4. Name the four legs of dharma (SB 1.17.24). *Austerity, cleanliness, mercy, truthfulness.*
5. Who invented dharma, according to SB 6.3.19? *Nobody: it is enacted by God.*
6. What is the simplest dharma for this age? *Hearing and chanting the Lord's names (SB 12.3.51–52).*

**M.2 Activity (about 10 minutes): "Jury cards"**
1. 2 min: give each small group one card with a popular definition (ritual, my community's religion, a deal with God, my duty).
2. 4 min: the group argues for the card for two minutes, against it for two.
3. 3 min: each group reads SB 1.2.8 aloud and decides whether its card passes the test; the group may say "partly".
4. 1 min: thank every group; do not mark anyone's own tradition wrong.
Adapt: for a mixed or non-religious room, use the cards as "what people mean by duty" and skip the God-test until the verdict.

**M.3 Reflection**
- **Comprehension:** What does "for all humanity" (SB 1.2.6) rule out?
- **Reflection:** Which of the four witnesses was *my* definition before today? Which leg does my week stand on
  most, and which least?
- **Application (private):** One practice I do by habit that I will do this week with attention, and notice
  whether it awakens anything. *(Nobody needs to share.)*

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

The word *dharma* is accused of meaning everything and therefore nothing: religion, duty, ritual, politics. The
Bhagavatam was asked for the essence of all scripture and answered with a definition: the supreme dharma "for
all humanity is that by which men can attain to loving devotional service unto the transcendent Lord"
(SB 1.2.6); the word's root meaning is "that which sustains one's existence". It gives one test: any duty or
ritual that does not "provoke attraction for the message of the Personality of Godhead" is "useless labor"
(SB 1.2.8). It says nobody invented dharma, so nobody owns it (SB 6.3.19). It describes dharma as a bull with
four legs, austerity, cleanliness, mercy and truthfulness, three of them broken in this age by pride, lust and
intoxication (SB 1.17.24–25). And it names the simplest form of dharma for this age: chanting the Lord's names
(SB 12.3.51–52). You are the jury.

Two further points help the jury. Dharma done as a bargain, religion for a material result, is dropped when the result comes another way (SB 1.1.2), whereas dharma as "that which sustains" is unmotivated and satisfies the self (SB 1.2.6). And since real dharma is enacted by God, even great sages cannot ascertain it by themselves (SB 6.3.19). The talk uses the courtroom to test four popular meanings (ritual, my community's religion, a deal with God, my duty) against these statements and invites you to try the simplest dharma, ten minutes of chanting, once.

## S2. Key ideas

- Dharma is "that which sustains one's existence": the soul's living connection with God (SB 1.2.6 purport).
- It is "for all humanity" (SB 1.2.6), and "real religious principles are enacted by the Supreme Personality of
  Godhead" (SB 6.3.19): nobody invented it, nobody owns it.
- The test: does a practice awaken attraction for God, or only "cleanse the cage" (SB 1.2.8 and purport)?
- Dharma is "not… dogmas or man-made formulas" but four legs: austerity, cleanliness, mercy, truthfulness
  (SB 1.17.24–25).
- Religion as a bargain for material gain is set aside by the Bhagavatam's own first words (SB 1.1.2).
- In this age, the simplest dharma is to chant the Lord's names (SB 12.3.51–52; deeper verse, the talk uses only its plain meaning).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **SB 1.2.6:** "The supreme occupation [dharma] for all humanity is that by which men can attain to loving
  devotional service unto the transcendent Lord." *For me:* dharma is about my connection, not my label.
- **SB 1.2.8:** duties are "useless labor if they do not provoke attraction for the message of the Personality
  of Godhead". *For me:* the test of any practice is what it awakens.
- **SB 1.17.24:** "your four legs were established by the four principles of austerity, cleanliness, mercy and
  truthfulness." *For me:* four things I can measure my week by.

## S4. Words to know

- **Dharma:** root meaning "that which sustains one's existence" (SB 1.2.6 purport); the soul's connection with
  God, lived.
- **Kali-yuga:** the present age, in which three of dharma's four legs are broken (SB 1.17.24–25).
- **Yuga-dharma:** the form of dharma recommended for an age; for this age, chanting (SB 12.3.52).

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Isn't dharma just the Hindu word for religion?**
   The Bhagavatam uses it for "all humanity" (SB 1.2.6) and gives it the root meaning "that which sustains
   one's existence". It is wider than any label.
2. **My religion is different. Is my dharma false?**
   This session compares nothing. It offers one test from one text: does your practice awaken love for God and
   sustain you (SB 1.2.6, 1.2.8)? You are the jury of your own practice.
3. **So rituals are useless?**
   No. A ritual that awakens attraction for God passes the test; one done with the mind elsewhere is "cleansing
   the cage" (SB 1.2.8 purport). Same act, different result.
4. **"Abandon all varieties of religion" (BG 18.66): does that mean abandon dharma?**
   Its purport reads it as: keep what leads to devotion, drop what does not.
5. **Chanting as the dharma of the age sounds like a big claim.**
   It is the text's claim (SB 12.3.51–52), and the programme ends with ten minutes of it, so you can test it
   before believing it.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. According to SB 1.2.6, the supreme dharma is for: (a) one country, (b) one community, (c) all humanity.
2. What single test does SB 1.2.8 give for whether a duty or ritual is worthwhile?
3. Name the four legs of dharma (SB 1.17.24) and the three things that broke three of them.
4. True or false: SB 6.3.19 says great sages ascertain real dharma by themselves.
5. In your own words: what is the difference between dharma as a bargain and dharma as "that which sustains"?

**Answers:** 1. (c) (SB 1.2.6). 2. Whether it provokes attraction for the message of the Personality of Godhead
(SB 1.2.8). 3. Austerity, cleanliness, mercy, truthfulness; broken by pride, lust and intoxication (SB 1.17.24).
4. False: real dharma is enacted by God, and even great sages cannot ascertain it (SB 6.3.19). 5. A
bargain does religion for a material result and drops it when the result comes another way (SB 1.1.2 purport);
dharma as "that which sustains" is unmotivated and satisfies the self (SB 1.2.6).
