# Morality Vs Transcendental morality

> **VL3 · 180 min · review: sources-checked.** IYF has no notes or deck for this topic; the site lists three videos and one
> lecture by HG Sundar Gopal Prabhu ("Leading a responsible life based on transcendental wisdom"; O). The Preaching
> Encyclopedia's "The Foundation of Morality" entry is a title only (133 characters; no text). The page is therefore
> built from scripture: the Gita's own drama (Arjuna's moral arguments against Krsna's instruction), the Bhagavatam's
> definition of dharma (SB 1.2.6–13), and the Asvatthama episode (SB 1.7), where Krsna reconciles conflicting codes.
> The VL3 danger this topic must prevent is the opposite of what its title suggests: not that students will be too
> moral, but that "transcendental" will become an excuse for small dishonesties. F.7 and K are built against that.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on vl3-02 (the four principles as freedoms) and vl3-09 (the "above ethics" misconception): this page asks what morality is for, not what the rules are.

At VL2 the student accepted the soul and God by argument. At VL3 he keeps four regulative principles (VL3-02) and
meets two pressures: friends who say morality needs no God, and his own mind, which has discovered that "Krsna
consciousness is above rules" can be made to mean "rules don't apply to me". This topic gives the Gita's own answer to
both. It is placed after VL3-09 because the "transcendental morality" question is also one of the misconceptions
*about* ISKCON ("they think they're above ordinary ethics").

|---|---|

| **VL3** (here) | What morality is for; what makes a devotee's morality "transcendental"; the three safeguards; daily honesty |

**Not done here:**
- Metaethics (whether morality can be objective without God). Chaitanya Charan's articles on it are listed in O for
  the preacher's own reading; the talk does not argue it.
- Justifying any specific "exception" for the students themselves. The Asvatthama episode is used to show how
  *Krsna* reconciles codes, not to license the student to.
- Krsna's pastimes with the gopis beyond SB 10.33.29–30, which are cited only for the rule "do not imitate".

## C. Central message

Ordinary morality is the set of duties by which society and the self are kept in order; scripture says its real purpose
is to please the Lord, and without that it is "useless labor" (SB 1.2.8, 1.2.13). Transcendental morality is acting
on the Lord's desire (BG 18.66), which fulfils what morality was for and produces every good quality (SB 5.18.12),
and which is never a licence to lower one's standards (BG 9.30–31 and purports).

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. Define ordinary morality (dharma as duty) and state its purpose from SB 1.2.8–13.
2. Explain from the Gita's own setting (BG 1.36; 2.31; 18.66) why Arjuna's moral arguments were not the last word,
   and what "transcendental" means there.
3. Describe how Krsna reconciled conflicting codes in the Asvatthama case (SB 1.7.35–36, 53–54, 57) and what that
   shows about the Lord's relation to rules.
4. State the three safeguards against misusing "transcendental morality" (the Lord's order, not mine; great souls
   are not to be imitated, SB 10.33.30; a devotee's qualities rise, SB 5.18.12 and BG 16.1–3).
5. Identify one area of their own conduct (exams, money, speech, online) where devotion should raise the standard.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** SB 1.2.8 and 1.2.13 define; BG 1.36 → 18.66 dramatise; SB 1.7.53–54 illustrates; SB 5.18.12, BG 9.30–31 and
SB 10.33.30 safeguard.

**SB 1.2.8** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/8/
> "The occupational activities a man performs according to his own position are only so much useless labor if they do not provoke attraction for the message of the Personality of Godhead."
- **Context:** Suta Gosvami's answer to the sages (1.2.6–13): what dharma is for.
- **Teaching:** Duty is judged by whether it leads to attraction for Krsna.
- **Purport point:** Selfishness "concentrated" (the body) and "extended" (family, nation, world); "without knowing the
  need of the dormant soul, one cannot be happy simply with" bodily and mental duties.
- **Application (preacher's):** This is the hinge: morality is not rejected; it is given its purpose. The moral
  atheist's goodness is real labour; the verse says what it lacks, not that it is worthless in itself.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 1.2.9** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/9/
> "All occupational engagements are certainly meant for ultimate liberation. They should never be performed for material gain…"
- **Use:** in the talk, one line: dharma is not for profit.

**SB 1.2.13** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/13/
> "O best among the twice-born, it is therefore concluded that the highest perfection one can achieve by discharging the duties prescribed for one's own occupation according to caste divisions and orders of life is to please the Personality of Godhead."
- **Purport point:** The divisions are "in terms of one's work and qualification and not by birth"; "in each and every
  one of the above-mentioned divisions of life, the aim must be to please the supreme authority of the Personality of
  Godhead."
- **Application (preacher's):** The definition of transcendental morality in one verse: duty done to please the Lord.
- **Use:** in the talk; the verse to remember.

**BG 1.36** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/1/36/
> "Sin will overcome us if we slay such aggressors. Therefore it is not proper for us to kill the sons of Dhṛtarāṣṭra and our friends…"
- **Context:** Arjuna's chapter of moral arguments (1.28–46): compassion, family, sin, the ruin of dynasties.
- **Teaching:** Arjuna argues from ordinary morality, and argues well.
- **Purport point:** Six kinds of aggressors may be killed without sin, but "Arjuna was not an ordinary person. He was
  saintly by character"; "saintly persons are advised to forgive. Such injunctions for saintly persons are more
  important than any political emergency."
- **Application (preacher's):** Do not caricature Arjuna. The purport credits his saintliness. The Gita's point is that
  even good moral reasoning is not the final authority; the Lord's desire is.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 2.31** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/31/
> "Considering your specific duty as a kṣatriya, you should know that there is no better engagement for you than fighting on religious principles; and so there is no need for hesitation."
- **Purport point:** "religious violence is sometimes a necessary factor"; "Nonviolence in politics may be a diplomacy,
  but it is never a factor or principle."
- **Application (preacher's):** Krsna's first answer is itself a moral one: *your* duty. Transcendental morality
  begins by taking ordinary duty more seriously, not less.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 3.35** and **BG 18.47** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/35/
> 3.35: "It is far better to discharge one's prescribed duties, even though faultily, than another's duties perfectly…"
> 18.47: "…Duties prescribed according to one's nature are never affected by sinful reactions."
- **Purport point (3.35):** "when one transcends the modes of material nature and is fully situated in Kṛṣṇa
  consciousness, he can perform anything and everything under the direction of a bona fide spiritual master."
- **Purport point (18.47):** "no work is abominable, if performed in the service of the Supreme Lord"; a ksatriya
  "sometimes a kṣatriya has to tell lies for the sake of diplomacy"; "One should act to satisfy the Supreme Lord."
- **Application (preacher's):** Note the condition in 3.35's purport: "under the direction of a bona fide spiritual
  master". Transcendence of the modes is not self-certified. For VL3 this is safeguard one.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 18.66** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/66/
> "Abandon all varieties of religion and just surrender unto Me. I shall deliver you from all sinful reactions. Do not fear."
- **Purport point:** "one should simply accept such religious principles that will lead ultimately to the devotional
  service of the Lord. One may perform a particular occupational duty according to his position in the social order,
  but if by executing his duty one does not come to the point of Kṛṣṇa consciousness, all his activities are in vain."
- **Application (preacher's):** The verse that names the "transcendental": all *varieties* of dharma are left behind
  for their *goal*. The purport does not say "ignore duty"; it says duty that does not reach Krsna is in vain (the
  same point as SB 1.2.8).
- **Use:** in the talk; the second verse to remember.

**BG 18.78** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/78/
> "Wherever there is Kṛṣṇa, the master of all mystics, and wherever there is Arjuna, the supreme archer, there will also certainly be opulence, victory, extraordinary power, and morality. That is my opinion."
- **Purport point:** Yudhisthira "was also a strict moralist. He never spoke a lie during his life."
- **Application (preacher's):** The Gita's last word includes *nīti*, morality, among what follows Krsna. Transcendence
  does not leave morality behind; it brings it.
- **Use:** in the talk; the close.

**BG 9.30** and **BG 9.31** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/9/30/
> 9.30: "Even if one commits the most abominable action, if he is engaged in devotional service he is to be considered saintly because he is properly situated in his determination."
> 9.31: "He quickly becomes righteous and attains lasting peace. O son of Kuntī, declare it boldly that My devotee never perishes."
- **Purport point (9.30):** "this verse only refers to an accident due to the strong power of material connections…
  No one should take advantage of this verse and commit nonsense and think that he is still a devotee. If he does not
  improve in his character by devotional service, then it is to be understood that he is not a high devotee."
- **Purport point (9.31):** "This should not be misunderstood… one who is engaged in mischievous activities cannot
  become a devotee of the Lord."
- **Application (preacher's):** The verses most misused to excuse immorality; their purports forbid the misuse in so
  many words. Read the purport sentences aloud. Safeguard three.
- **Use:** in the talk and K.

**BG 16.1-3** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/16/1-3/
> "Fearlessness; purification of one's existence; cultivation of spiritual knowledge; charity; self-control; performance of sacrifice; study of the Vedas; austerity; simplicity; nonviolence; truthfulness; freedom from anger; renunciation; tranquillity; aversion to faultfinding; compassion for all living entities; freedom from covetousness; gentleness; modesty; steady determination; vigor; forgiveness; fortitude; cleanliness; and freedom from envy and from the passion for honor – these transcendental qualities, O son of Bharata, belong to godly men endowed with divine nature."
- **Application (preacher's):** Krsna calls these moral qualities *transcendental* (*daivī*). Truthfulness,
  nonviolence and modesty are on His list. A "transcendental" life with fewer of them is misnamed.
- **Use:** in the talk; the checklist for M.

**SB 5.18.12** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/18/12/
> "All the demigods and their exalted qualities, such as religion, knowledge and renunciation, become manifest in the body of one who has developed unalloyed devotion for the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Vāsudeva. On the other hand, a person devoid of devotional service and engaged in material activities has no good qualities…"
- **Purport point:** Devotees' qualities listed from CC Adi 8 (Pandita Haridasa: "he spoke very sweetly… he respected
  everyone, he always worked for everyone's benefit, his mind was free of duplicity").
- **Application (preacher's):** The positive statement of safeguard three: devotion *produces* good qualities. The
  second half ("no good qualities") must be handled with care at VL3 (K.1): it means no *lasting* or *complete*
  qualities, not that the moral atheist's honesty is fake. Say what the verse says and label the reading.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 11.5.41** (deeper verse; the talk uses only its plain meaning) — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/5/41/ · *BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples.*
> "O King, one who has given up all material duties and has taken full shelter of the lotus feet of Mukunda, who offers shelter to all, is not indebted to the demigods, great sages, ordinary living beings, relatives, friends, mankind or even one's forefathers who have passed away…"
- **Purport point:** "Āpta means one's own family members, to whom one is certainly obligated according to normal
  morality"; "Until one becomes a devotee of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, one is certainly a product of his
  society."
- **Application (preacher's):** The verse that sounds most like "the devotee is above all obligations". Its purport
  spends its length on how real those obligations are. A VL3 student has not "given up all material duties"; he has
  exams and parents. Teach the verse as the horizon, the purport as the ground.
- **Use:** background; K.4.

**SB 1.7.35**, **SB 1.7.36**, **SB 1.7.53-54**, **SB 1.7.57** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/7/35/
> 1.7.35: "Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa said: O Arjuna, you should not show mercy by releasing this relative of a brāhmaṇa [brahma-bandhu], for he has killed innocent boys in their sleep."
> 1.7.36: "A person who knows the principles of religion does not kill an enemy who is careless, intoxicated, insane, asleep, afraid or devoid of his chariot. Nor does he kill a boy, a woman, a foolish creature or a surrendered soul."
> 1.7.53–54: "A friend of a brāhmaṇa is not to be killed, but if he is an aggressor he must be killed. All these rulings are in the scriptures, and you should act accordingly. You have to fulfill your promise to your wife, and you must also act to the satisfaction of Bhīmasena and Me."
> 1.7.57: "Cutting the hair from his head, depriving him of his wealth and driving him from his residence are the prescribed punishments for the relative of a brāhmaṇa. There is no injunction for killing the body."
- **Context:** Asvatthama has murdered the sleeping sons of Draupadi; Arjuna captures him; Draupadi pleads for him;
  Bhima and Krsna want him killed; the codes conflict.
- **Purport point (1.7.53–54):** "Arjuna was perplexed because Aśvatthāmā was to be killed as well as spared according
  to different scriptures… This dilemma was present before Arjuna, and the solution was awarded by Kṛṣṇa."
- **Application (preacher's):** Krsna does not cancel the codes; He honours all of them at once (the aggressor is
  punished; the brahmana's relative is not killed; Draupadi's promise and Bhima's demand are both satisfied). This
  is what transcendental morality looks like when the Lord does it: more exact, not less. And note who resolves it:
  Krsna, not Arjuna's preference.
- **Use:** in the talk; the central story.

**SB 10.33.29** and **SB 10.33.30** (deeper verse; the talk uses only its plain meaning) — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/10/33/29/ · *BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples.*
> 10.33.30: "One who is not a great controller should never imitate the behavior of ruling personalities, even mentally. If out of foolishness an ordinary person does imitate such behavior, he will simply destroy himself, just as a person who is not Rudra would destroy himself if he tried to drink an ocean of poison."
- **Purport point (10.33.29):** "if we try to imitate the great personalities ruling the universe, the result will be
  catastrophic."
- **Application (preacher's):** Safeguard two. Whatever is "above the rules" is so for the Lord and His empowered
  servants, not for us. Cited only for this; the context (the gopis) is not opened at VL3.
- **Use:** in the talk, one line.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **Two words, one root.** *Dharma* in ordinary use means duty and right conduct: honesty, non-violence,
   obligations to parents, teachers, society (SB 11.5.41 purport: "normal morality"). The Bhagavatam does not discard
   this; it asks what it is *for*, and answers: to please the Lord (SB 1.2.13), to awaken attraction for Him (SB
   1.2.8), never for profit (SB 1.2.9). "Transcendental morality" is this same dharma with its purpose restored:
   conduct governed by the Lord's desire rather than by convention, reward or fear.
2. **The Gita's own drama.** Chapter One is Arjuna's moral case against fighting, and the purport to 1.36 says he
   argued as a saint. Krsna's reply moves in two steps. First (2.31), *your* duty: ordinary morality taken seriously.
   Last (18.66), *abandon all varieties of dharma and surrender*: the goal to which every duty points. Between them,
   3.35 and 18.47: do your own duty even faultily; no work is abominable in the Lord's service. The arc is not
   "morality, then no morality"; it is "morality, then its purpose".
3. **The Lord reconciles; He does not cancel.** SB 1.7: four codes collide over Asvatthama (punish the aggressor;
   spare the brahmana's relative; keep Draupadi's promise; satisfy Bhima). Krsna's solution (1.7.53–57) satisfies
   every one. Transcendental morality, when the Lord exercises it, is *more* scrupulous than any single rule. The
   student should notice who resolves it: "the solution was awarded by Kṛṣṇa" (1.7.53–54 purport), and Arjuna
   "could understand the motive of the Lord" and carried it out (1.7.55). Arjuna does not invent an exception.
4. **What makes a devotee's morality "transcendental".** Three marks, each from the texts. (a) *Source:* the
   standard is the Lord's instruction through scripture and guide, not my feeling (3.35 purport: "under the direction
   of a bona fide spiritual master"). (b) *Motive:* to please Him, not to earn, impress or avoid shame (SB 1.2.9, 1.2.13).
   (c) *Fruit:* the divine qualities appear (SB 5.18.12; BG 16.1–3), and *nīti*, morality, is listed among what follows
   Krsna (BG 18.78). A VL3 student can apply all three to one honest act this week.
5. **Why the moral atheist's objection is half right.** "I'm a good person without God." SB 5.18.12 and BG 16.1–3
   answer carefully: his honesty is real and is a divine quality wherever it appears; what it lacks, says SB 1.2.8, is
   its purpose, and so it does not satisfy the self or last beyond this body. Say this with respect; the student's
   father may be exactly this person. Never "atheists have no good qualities" as a slogan; the verse's purport is
   about the *source* and *completeness* of qualities.
6. **Why "above the rules" is not for us.** SB 10.33.29–30: great controllers are not harmed by apparent
   transgressions; "one who is not a great controller should never imitate… even mentally." BG 3.35 purport's
   condition. SB 11.5.41's "not indebted" belongs to one who "has given up all material duties and has taken full
   shelter"; its purport reminds everyone else of normal morality. For VL3 the conclusion is simple: *you* are not the
   exception; the exceptions are the Lord's to make.
7. **The misuse, named.** Students hear and sometimes say: "A devotee is beyond karma, so a small lie / a copied
   assignment / a pirated file / a broken promise to parents doesn't count." BG 9.30–31 are quoted. Their purports:
   "No one should take advantage of this verse and commit nonsense and think that he is still a devotee"; "one who is
   engaged in mischievous activities cannot become a devotee." Krsna's own list of transcendental qualities (16.1–3)
   includes truthfulness, simplicity and modesty. Transcendental morality that lowers honesty is a contradiction in
   terms. Read these purport lines aloud in the class; they do the work.
8. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - "Devotees think they're above ethics." The opposite: the standard rises (BG 18.78; 16.1–3).
   - "Krsna told Arjuna to kill, so religion justifies violence." BG 2.31 purport: *religious* fighting by a ksatriya
     under the Lord's order; SB 1.7.36: the codes of war are strict. Not a general licence, and not the student's call.
   - "Rules don't matter, only love matters." Love is shown by following the beloved's wishes (BG 18.66 purport: accept
     the principles that lead to devotional service).
   - "Good people without God are fakes." F.5.
9. **Pitch for VL3.** Practical and encouraging. One principle, three marks, three safeguards, one act. Terms kept
   to *dharma*, *transcendental*, *nīti*.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet, say your name, and say today is about why we do right, not only what is right.
2. Write on the board: "Is it ever right to break a rule?"
3. Ask H.2 question 1; collect three cases and park them without judging.
4. Ask H.2 question 2 and note the answers.
5. Say that you will return to the cases at the end, then use the transition below.

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the room's own life)
1. When was the last time honesty cost you something small, such as a mark, a sale or a favour?
2. Who do you trust to be fair when nobody is watching, and why?
3. Does a person need God to be good? What do you think?

- **Opening question:** "Is it ever right to break a rule? Give me a case." Collect three. Then: "Arjuna had a
  stronger case than any of these, and Krsna said no. Let's see why, and what He said yes to."
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** Nikhil, a devotee, is asked by a classmate to sign the attendance sheet for
  him. Nikhil thinks: "I'm above these small karmas now; and he's a friend." His own facilitator, told of it later,
  asks one question: "Whose desire did you serve?"
- **Transition:** "That question is the whole topic."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** their peers judge Krsna consciousness by their honesty in small things, and
  their own mind is looking for exemptions.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Morality has a purpose: to please the Lord.** SB 1.2.8, 1.2.13. *Example:* the same honesty, done for marks or
   for Krsna. *Transition:* then why did Krsna overrule Arjuna's morality?
2. **The Gita's arc: duty, then its goal.** BG 1.36 (saintly argument); 2.31 (your duty); 18.66 (its goal). *Example:*
   Arjuna. *Transition:* what does the Lord do when codes collide?
3. **The Lord reconciles codes.** SB 1.7.53–54, 57. *Example:* Asvatthama's hair and jewel. *Transition:* can I do
   that?
4. **Not mine to decide; not to imitate.** BG 3.35 purport; SB 10.33.30; SB 11.5.41 purport. *Example:* Rudra's ocean of
   poison. *Transition:* then how do I know my morality is becoming transcendental?
5. **The qualities rise.** SB 5.18.12; BG 16.1–3; BG 9.30–31 purports; BG 18.78. *Example:* Yudhisthira "never spoke a
   lie". *Transition:* to P.

## J. Stories and illustrations

- **Arjuna's arguments and Krsna's answer** (BG 1.28–2.38): tell it as a real moral debate in which the better
  arguer is overruled by the One who knows the purpose. Read 1.36's purport first; do not belittle Arjuna.
- **Asvatthama** (SB 1.7.13–58): the murder of the sleeping boys; the capture; Draupadi's plea (1.7.42–48); Bhima's
  demand; Krsna's ruling (1.7.53–54); Arjuna's solution (1.7.55–57: the hair and the jewel). Read the chapter; tell
  only what it says.
- **Yudhisthira who never spoke a lie** (BG 18.78 purport): one line. *(The Mahabharata's account of his half-truth
  about Asvatthama's death during the war is often raised; it is not in the Gita or Bhagavatam text used here. If
  asked, say so and refer it: Requires source review.)*
- **Pandita Haridasa's qualities** (SB 5.18.12 purport, from CC Adi 8): "he respected everyone… his mind was free of
  duplicity." One line, as the picture of transcendental morality.
- **Contemporary image (the preacher's):** traffic rules and an ambulance. The ambulance is allowed through a red
  light because it serves life, which is what the lights are for; the rest of us are not ambulances, and a driver who
  decides he is one causes the accident.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

> **Before you teach:** read the BG 9.30 and 9.31 purports in full and mark the sentences you will read aloud. This
> topic is safe only if those sentences are heard.

1. **"My father is an atheist and the most honest man I know. Does the Bhagavatam say he has no good qualities?"**
   - Honour your father; his honesty is a divine quality wherever it appears (BG 16.1–3). SB 5.18.12 is about the
     *source* and *completeness* of qualities: without connection to the Lord they do not satisfy the self or last
     beyond this body (SB 1.2.8). Say it that way, and say it with respect.
2. **"Krsna told Arjuna to fight. So religion justifies violence?"**
   - BG 2.31 purport: a ksatriya's *religious* duty under the Lord's direct order, with strict codes (SB 1.7.36:
     no killing of the sleeping, the unarmed, the surrendered). Not a general licence, and no one in this room is
     Arjuna receiving Krsna's order.
3. **"If a devotee is beyond karma, why does a small lie matter?"**
   - BG 9.30 purport: "No one should take advantage of this verse and commit nonsense and think that he is still a
     devotee." BG 16.1–3 lists truthfulness as transcendental. The lie matters more, not less, because the standard
     is Krsna's pleasure, not getting away with it.
4. **"SB 11.5.41 says the surrendered soul owes nothing to parents or society."**
   - Read its purport: family are those "to whom one is certainly obligated according to normal morality", and
     "until one becomes a devotee… one is certainly a product of his society." The verse describes one who has given
     up *all* material duties; you have exams on Monday. It is the horizon, not your exemption.
5. **"Krsna broke rules Himself (the gopis, the promise to Bhisma). Why can't devotees?"**
   - SB 10.33.30: "One who is not a great controller should never imitate the behavior of ruling personalities, even
     mentally." The purport to 10.33.29: imitation is "catastrophic". The Lord's freedom is the Lord's. The gopi question itself
     goes to seniors (vl3-09, K.9).
6. **"So is a devotee just a very moral person?"**
   - More than that, and never less. The difference is the source (the Lord's desire), the motive (His pleasure) and
     the fruit (BG 18.78: *nīti* follows Krsna). Yudhisthira "never spoke a lie"; Pandita Haridasa was "free of
     duplicity".
7. **"What if following Krsna conflicts with my parents' wishes?"**
   - At VL3 almost nothing does: study, honesty, kindness and chanting are all compatible (BG 3.8, from vl3-09). Where a
     real conflict seems to arise, that is a one-to-one question (vl3-08; vl3-13), not a decision to make alone under
     the banner of "transcendental".

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The copied assignment.** Aditya submits a friend's assignment with his name on it. Asked, he says: "Marks
are material; I'm not attached to them, so it's not really dishonesty."
- **Principles:** BG 16.1–3 (truthfulness is transcendental); BG 9.30 purport ("take advantage… commit nonsense");
  SB 1.2.13 (duty done to please the Lord).
- **Questions:** Whose desire did he serve? What does detachment from marks actually look like (doing the work, not
  caring about the grade)? What would Krsna's pleasure have required?
- **Takeaway:** Detachment from results is not detachment from honesty.

**Case 2: The honest atheist.** Priya's colleague says: "I don't need God to be good. Look at me." He is, in fact,
good.
- **Principles:** BG 16.1–3; SB 5.18.12 (handled as F.5); SB 1.2.8.
- **Questions:** What should Priya grant him? What is the one thing scripture says his goodness lacks, and how does
  she say it without insult? What would be gained by arguing, and what lost?
- **Takeaway:** Respect first; purpose second; no slogan.

**Case 3: The ambulance driver.** Sameer reads SB 1.7 and concludes that, like Krsna, he may "balance" rules: he
breaks a promise to his mother to attend a kirtana, reasoning that the higher purpose justifies it.
- **Principles:** F.3 (Krsna decides, Arjuna obeys); SB 10.33.30; BG 3.35 purport (under direction); BG 17.14 (respect
  for parents, from vl3-13).
- **Questions:** Who reconciled the codes in SB 1.7? Who is reconciling them here? What should Sameer have done
  before deciding (ask)? Is a kirtana ever served by a broken word?
- **Takeaway:** The exceptions are the Lord's to make; ours is to ask and to keep our word.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Ordinary morality is duty and right conduct: honesty, non-violence, respect for parents and teachers, obligations to society. Scripture does not throw it away; it asks what it is for, and answers: to please the Lord (SB 1.2.13). Duty that never leads to attraction for Krsna is "useless labor" (SB 1.2.8). Transcendental morality is the same conduct with its purpose restored: done on the Lord's instruction, for His pleasure, and bearing the divine qualities Krsna lists in BG 16.1-3, which include truthfulness, simplicity and modesty.

In the Gita, Arjuna's moral arguments against fighting were sincere. Krsna overruled them in two steps: first do your own duty as a warrior (BG 2.31), and last abandon all varieties of dharma and surrender to Him (BG 18.66). The Lord knows the purpose of every rule; in the Asvatthama case He satisfied four conflicting codes at once, and Arjuna understood His motive and carried it out (SB 1.7.53-57).

But that freedom is His, not ours. There are three safeguards: it is not for me to decide when a rule may be set aside (BG 3.35 purport); "one who is not a great controller should never imitate the behavior of ruling personalities, even mentally" (SB 10.33.30); and the purport to BG 9.30 says plainly that no one should take advantage of the verse "and commit nonsense and think that he is still a devotee." A devotee's standard rises. Where Krsna is, "there will also certainly be... morality" (BG 18.78). This week, choose one standard to raise, such as no copying or no convenient lies, and keep it for Krsna's pleasure.

## S2. Key ideas

- Morality is for pleasing the Lord (SB 1.2.13); without that purpose it is useless labour (SB 1.2.8).
- Transcendental morality = the same conduct, from the Lord's instruction, for His pleasure, bearing His qualities.
- Arjuna argued well and was still overruled: the Lord knows the purpose of rules (BG 1.36; 2.31; 18.66).
- The Lord reconciles codes; He does not cancel them (SB 1.7.53–57).
- Never imitate the great controllers, "even mentally" (SB 10.33.30).
- BG 9.30 is not a licence: "No one should take advantage of this verse" (purport).
- Where Krsna is, morality follows (BG 18.78).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **SB 1.2.13:** "…the highest perfection one can achieve by discharging the duties prescribed for one's own occupation… is to please the Personality of Godhead." *For me:* my honesty has an address.
- **BG 16.1–3 (part):** "…truthfulness; freedom from anger; renunciation; tranquillity; aversion to faultfinding; compassion for all living entities… these transcendental qualities… belong to godly men…" *For me:* truthfulness is on Krsna's own list of transcendental qualities.
- **SB 10.33.30:** "One who is not a great controller should never imitate the behavior of ruling personalities, even mentally…" *For me:* the exceptions are the Lord's, not mine.

## S4. Words to know

- **Dharma:** duty and right conduct; in the Bhagavatam, whatever leads to loving service of the Lord.
- **Transcendental:** beyond the modes of material nature; here, conduct governed by the Lord's desire rather than by convention, reward or fear.
- **Niti:** morality, ethics (BG 18.78).
- **Daivi prakrti:** the divine nature whose qualities BG 16.1–3 lists.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **My father doesn't believe in God and is very honest. Does scripture say he has no good qualities?** His honesty is a divine quality wherever it appears (BG 16.1–3). SB 5.18.12 is about where qualities come from and whether they are complete and lasting; without connection to the Lord they do not satisfy the self (SB 1.2.8). Honour him.
2. **Krsna told Arjuna to fight. Does religion justify violence?** A ksatriya's religious duty under the Lord's direct order, with strict codes (SB 1.7.36). Not a general licence; nobody here is Arjuna.
3. **If a devotee is beyond karma, why does a small lie matter?** BG 9.30 purport: no one should take advantage of the verse "and commit nonsense". Truthfulness is transcendental (BG 16.1–3). It matters more, not less.
4. **Krsna broke rules Himself. Why can't we?** SB 10.33.30: never imitate the great controllers, even mentally. His freedom is His.
5. **So is a devotee just a very moral person?** Never less than that, and more: the source, motive and fruit differ. Yudhisthira "never spoke a lie" (BG 18.78 purport).

## S6. Check your understanding

1. According to SB 1.2.13, what is the highest perfection of doing one's duty?
2. What does SB 1.2.8 call duty that does not awaken attraction for Krsna?
3. Give the two steps of Krsna's answer to Arjuna (BG 2.31; BG 18.66).
4. In SB 1.7.53–57, how many conflicting codes did Krsna's solution satisfy, and who decided?
5. What does the purport to BG 9.30 say about using the verse as an excuse?
6. True or false: BG 16.1–3 lists truthfulness among the transcendental qualities.

**Answers:** 1. To please the Personality of Godhead (SB 1.2.13). 2. "Useless labor" (SB 1.2.8). 3. First, your own duty as a ksatriya (BG 2.31); last, abandon all varieties of dharma and surrender to Him (BG 18.66). 4. All of them (aggressor punished; brahmana's relative not killed; Draupadi's promise kept; Bhima and Krsna satisfied); Krsna gave the solution and Arjuna understood His motive and carried it out (SB 1.7.53–54 purport; 1.7.55). 5. "No one should take advantage of this verse and commit nonsense and think that he is still a devotee." 6. True.
