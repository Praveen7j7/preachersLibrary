# The happiness of service: "ananda-cinmaya-rasa"

> **TRD · up to 180 min · review: drafted (30 Oct 2026).** Group E (happiness in devotional life), first of two.
> Written for temple residents: brahmacaris and full-time preachers who have already given their lives. Every verse
> was checked on vedabase (research/verification/trd-21.json). Not on the IYF catalogue; the id is the library's own.

## A. Topic overview

| Audience | Residents who have served for years. They know the philosophy of happiness; the question now is whether they themselves are happy, and why a renunciant should be the happiest person in the room |
| Teaching objective | Residents can say, from scripture, what the happiness of devotional service is, where it comes from, why it does not depend on circumstances, and how to recognise and nourish it in their own day |

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on vl1-level happiness talks and on dps-15/dps-16 (the stages). It does not re-argue that material pleasure
fails; the residents left that behind. It asks a sharper question: Krishna's own realm is "permeated and vitalized
by His ever-blissful spiritual rasa" (*ananda-cinmaya-rasa*, Bs 5.37). A servant of that Lord, living in His temple,
should therefore be visibly happy. When he is not, something in the understanding or the practice needs to be found.

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| VL1 | "Happiness" as the search of every person, and why sense pleasure cannot satisfy (the vl1 pages) |
| VL2 | Reasoning: the self is not the body, so bodily pleasure cannot fill it |
| VL3 | Practice: the taste that comes from regular chanting and hearing |
| SPS | Happiness in service and association; dealing with dry periods |
| DPS | Happiness as a stage: SB 1.2.19, "he becomes completely happy", placed after nistha (dps-15, dps-16) |
| BSS | The brahmacari's happiness: austerity that tastes "like poison" at first and "like nectar" at the end (BG 18.37) |
| **TRD** (here) | The nature of transcendental happiness itself (BG 6.20-23; Bs 5.37), why it belongs to the servant, and the honest audit of one's own joy after years in the ashram |

**Not done here:** rasa-tattva (trd-33, trd-41 to trd-48); festivals and kirtana as nourishment (trd-22); dryness and
the plateau (trd-09); burnout (trd-52).

## C. Central message

Krishna is "ever-blissful" and so is His service (Bs 5.37). The devotee's happiness is not a reward added to service;
it is the nature of service itself, "boundless transcendental happiness, realized through transcendental senses"
(BG 6.21). It is established when devotion becomes irrevocable (SB 1.2.19), it depends on nothing outside, and it is
the first thing a newcomer should see in a resident.

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Explain, from Bs 5.37 and BG 6.20-23, what "ananda-cinmaya-rasa" and "transcendental happiness realized through
   transcendental senses" mean, in plain words.
2. State from SB 1.2.6 and SB 1.2.19 why devotional service alone "completely satisfies the self", and at which point
   the devotee "becomes completely happy".
3. Distinguish the three kinds of happiness in BG 18.37 and the Bhagavatam's warning against mixing motives (SB 1.2.6
   purport).
4. Recognise, in their own day, the signs of this happiness (BG 10.9: satisfaction and bliss in talking about Krishna)
   and its absence.
5. Name one practice that nourishes it (hearing, chanting, serving with devotees) and one habit that steals it.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Bs 5.37**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bs/5/37/
> "I worship Govinda, the primeval Lord, residing in His own realm, Goloka, with Rādhā, resembling His own spiritual figure, the embodiment of the ecstatic potency possessed of the sixty-four artistic activities, in the company of Her confidantes [sakhīs], embodiments of the extensions of Her bodily form, permeated and vitalized by His ever-blissful spiritual rasa."
- **Context:** Lord Brahma's prayer (Brahma-samhita), with Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura's commentary as
  published by the BBT. The Sanskrit phrase for "ever-blissful spiritual rasa" is *ananda-cinmaya-rasa*.
- **Teaching:** Krishna's own realm, His pleasure potency (Radha) and Her expansions are "permeated and vitalized" by
  ever-blissful spiritual rasa. Bliss is not something Krishna gets from outside; it is His nature and the nature of
  His realm.
- **Purport point:** the commentary explains that the Lord and His potency "are one and the self-same existence,
  still They exist eternally as separate entities, as Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa", and that the rasa they share "is
  inconceivable". It then lists the sixty-four arts of Radharani's company.
- **Application (preacher's):** the servant enters the Lord's own happiness by service, never by trying to enjoy
  separately. We quote this verse for its statement about Krishna's realm; the details of Radha-Krishna's rasa belong
  to the sensitive group G topics, not here.
- **Use:** in the talk, read once with the translation on a slide; the rasa detail stays in the preacher's background.

**CC Adi 4.72** and **CC Madhya 8.163**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/adi/4/72/ ·
https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/8/163/
- **Teaching:** Krsnadasa Kaviraja quotes Bs 5.37 twice: in Adi 4, explaining the Lord's own pleasure potency, and in
  Madhya 8, in Ramananda Raya's answers to Lord Caitanya. Both pages carry only the note "This is a quotation from the
  Brahma-saṁhitā (5.37)". *Use:* background: the Gaudiya acharyas take this verse as authority.

**BG 6.20-23**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/20-23/
> "In that joyous state, one is situated in boundless transcendental happiness, realized through transcendental senses. Established thus, one never departs from the truth, and upon gaining this he thinks there is no greater gain. Being situated in such a position, one is never shaken, even in the midst of greatest difficulty. This indeed is actual freedom from all miseries arising from material contact."
- **Context:** Krishna describes samadhi, the perfection of yoga, to Arjuna.
- **Teaching:** real happiness is (a) boundless, (b) transcendental, (c) realised through *transcendental* senses,
  not through the material senses, (d) so complete that one "thinks there is no greater gain", and (e) steady even in
  the greatest difficulty.
- **Purport point:** Prabhupada writes that "in this verse transcendental pleasure – realized through transcendental
  senses – is accepted", against the monists who "do not accept this transcendental pleasure, out of fear of
  jeopardizing the theory of oneness". He connects the "clearance of the impure mirror of the mind"
  (*ceto-darpana-marjanam*, Lord Caitanya's Siksastaka 1) to this state, and says that "after nirvāṇa, or material
  cessation, there is the manifestation of spiritual activities, or devotional service to the Lord".
- **Application (preacher's):** the resident's happiness is tested by (e): is it shaken by a difficult temple
  president, a transfer, an illness? If so, it is still partly material.
- **Use:** in the talk, the central definition.

**SB 1.2.6**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/6/
> "The supreme occupation [dharma] for all humanity is that by which men can attain to loving devotional service unto the transcendent Lord. Such devotional service must be unmotivated and uninterrupted to completely satisfy the self."
- **Teaching:** devotional service completely satisfies the self only when it is *ahaituki* (unmotivated) and
  *apratihata* (uninterrupted).
- **Purport point:** "Material existence is temporary, illusory and full of miseries. There is no happiness at all.
  There is just the futile attempt to get rid of the miseries, and temporary cessation of misery is falsely called
  happiness." And: "adoption of devotional service for material gain is certainly an obstruction to the progressive
  path of renunciation."
- **Application (preacher's):** a resident who is unhappy in service should first ask whether a hidden motive
  (position, comfort, recognition) has entered; the purport says mixed service cannot give full satisfaction.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 1.2.19**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/19/
> "As soon as irrevocable loving service is established in the heart, the effects of nature's modes of passion and ignorance, such as lust, desire and hankering, disappear from the heart. Then the devotee is established in goodness, and he becomes completely happy."
- **Teaching:** complete happiness follows the establishment of irrevocable service (*naisthiki bhakti*).
- **Purport point:** "A living being in his normal constitutional position is fully satisfied in spiritual bliss…
  This self-satisfaction is not like the satisfaction of the inactive fool… Devotional service is not inactivity, but
  the unalloyed activity of the soul."
- **Use:** in the talk; dps-15 used this verse to place nistha, here the point is the happiness that follows it.

**BG 18.37**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/37/
> "That which in the beginning may be just like poison but at the end is just like nectar and which awakens one to self-realization is said to be happiness in the mode of goodness."
- **Purport point:** rules and regulations to control the mind and senses "are very difficult, bitter like poison,
  but if one is successful in following the regulations and comes to the transcendental position, he begins to drink
  real nectar, and he enjoys life."
- **Use:** in the talk, for the honest admission that the beginning of ashram life tasted bitter.

**BG 5.21**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/5/21/
> "Such a liberated person is not attracted to material sense pleasure but is always in trance, enjoying the pleasure within. In this way the self-realized person enjoys unlimited happiness, for he concentrates on the Supreme."
- **Purport point:** Prabhupada quotes Sri Yamunacarya: "Since I have been engaged in the transcendental loving service
  of Kṛṣṇa, realizing ever-new pleasure in Him, whenever I think of sex pleasure I spit at the thought, and my lips
  curl with distaste."
- **Use:** in the talk (J): the testimony of a devotee who tasted both.

**SB 5.5.1**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/5/1/
> "…One should engage in penance and austerity to attain the divine position of devotional service. By such activity, one's heart is purified, and when one attains this position, he attains eternal, blissful life, which is transcendental to material happiness and which continues forever."
- **Teaching:** Lord Rsabhadeva to His sons: human life is not for the sense gratification "available even for dogs
  and hogs"; austerity (*tapo divyam*) purifies the heart and leads to "eternal, blissful life". bss-07 teaches the
  whole instruction; here only this verse is used, for its promise of bliss.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 2.66**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/66/
> "One who is not connected with the Supreme [in Kṛṣṇa consciousness] can have neither transcendental intelligence nor a steady mind, without which there is no possibility of peace. And how can there be any happiness without peace?"
- **Use:** in the talk: the order is connection, steady mind, peace, happiness.

**BG 9.2**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/9/2/
> "…It is everlasting, and it is joyfully performed."
- **Teaching:** devotional service (the knowledge of chapter 9) is *su-sukham kartum*, joyfully performed. *Use:* in
  the talk, one line.

**BG 18.54**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/54/
> "One who is thus transcendentally situated at once realizes the Supreme Brahman and becomes fully joyful. He never laments or desires to have anything. He is equally disposed toward every living entity. In that state he attains pure devotional service unto Me."
- **Purport point:** for the devotee, the joyful *brahma-bhuta* stage is not the end: "one has to go still further, to
  become engaged in pure devotional service." *Use:* in the talk, to show that joy is the ground of service, and
  service the fullness of joy.

**BG 10.9**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/10/9/
> "The thoughts of My pure devotees dwell in Me, their lives are fully devoted to My service, and they derive great satisfaction and bliss from always enlightening one another and conversing about Me."
- **Purport point:** "Devotees of the Supreme Lord are twenty-four hours daily engaged in glorifying the qualities and
  pastimes of the Supreme Lord. Their hearts and souls are constantly submerged…"
- **Use:** in the talk: the visible sign of this happiness is what the devotees talk about when no one is watching.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** Krishna is *ananda-cinmaya-rasa*: His realm, His potency and His associates are "permeated and
   vitalized by His ever-blissful spiritual rasa" (Bs 5.37). The living being is a part of that whole. When he is
   connected in service, he shares the bliss; when he tries to enjoy separately, he gets only "temporary cessation of
   misery… falsely called happiness" (SB 1.2.6 purport).
2. **Why the servant, and not the enjoyer, is happy.** BG 6.21 says the happiness is "realized through transcendental
   senses". The material senses are instruments for enjoying matter; they cannot touch Krishna. Service engages the
   spiritual senses (hearing His name, speaking about Him, cooking for Him), and through them the bliss is actually
   tasted. This is why Prabhupada insists "devotional service is not inactivity, but the unalloyed activity of the
   soul" (SB 1.2.19 purport): the soul's happiness is an activity, service, not a feeling we wait for.
3. **Why it is stable.** "Being situated in such a position, one is never shaken, even in the midst of greatest
   difficulty" (BG 6.22). Material happiness depends on the senses and their objects, which always change; the
   happiness of service depends on Krishna, who does not.
4. **Why it comes in stages.** BG 18.37: goodness-happiness is poison first and nectar later. SB 1.2.19: complete
   happiness is established when devotion becomes irrevocable. So a resident in his first years who still finds
   mangala-arati hard is not failing; he is in the "poison" phase the Gita names. But a resident of ten years who
   finds no nectar should ask what the purports ask: has a motive crept in (SB 1.2.6), or has the hearing stopped
   (SB 1.2.18, taught in dps-15)?
5. **Connections.** The happiness in question is the fruit of nistha and ruci (dps-15, dps-16). It is nourished by
   kirtana, festivals and the dhama (trd-22). It is stolen by the weeds of profit, adoration and position (trd-49).
   Its loss over long years is burnout (trd-52).
6. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *A serious devotee should look grave.* BG 18.54: he "becomes fully joyful". BG 10.9: devotees "derive great
     satisfaction and bliss" from talking about Krishna. Gravity about one's vows and joy in service go together.
   - *Happiness is the goal.* No: Krishna's pleasure is the goal; our happiness is the natural result of His
     (SB 1.2.6: service must be unmotivated). Seeking the happiness itself turns service into another enjoyment.
   - *If I were happy, I would never feel austerity as hard.* BG 18.37 says the opposite: the path to this happiness
     "in the beginning may be just like poison".

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes).** Greet the residents and say: "Tonight's topic is not whether material life
can make a person happy. You have settled that. It is whether *we* are happy, and what scripture says our happiness
should look like after years in the temple." Read Bs 5.37 aloud in English. Then say: "Lord Brahma says Krishna's own
realm is permeated by ever-blissful spiritual rasa. We serve that Lord. So the question for tonight is simple: a
servant of the ever-blissful Lord should be the happiest person in any room he enters. Is that true of us? If not,
why not, and what do the acharyas say to do?" Ask everyone to write one private line before any discussion.

**H.2 Opening questions (ask, do not collect answers publicly unless offered):**
1. "In the last week, when were you most genuinely happy? Was it during service, or during a break from service?"
2. "When a guest meets you at the temple gate, what does your face tell him about Krishna consciousness?"
3. "The Gita says real happiness is 'never shaken, even in the midst of greatest difficulty' (BG 6.22). What shook
   yours most recently?"

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Krishna is ever-blissful, and so is His service.** Bs 5.37. Plain explanation: bliss is Krishna's nature, not
   His possession; the servant shares it by being connected. Transition: how is it tasted?
2. **It is tasted through transcendental senses, in activity.** BG 6.20-23; SB 1.2.19 purport ("unalloyed activity
   of the soul"). Example: the pleasure of hearing a Bhagavatam class that one did not want to attend, and leaving
   lighter. Transition: why does it sometimes fail?
3. **It fails when service is mixed.** SB 1.2.6 and purport. Example (preacher's application): a resident given a
   responsible post who is happy while praised and heavy when corrected: the happiness was partly in the post.
4. **It grows in stages.** BG 18.37; SB 1.2.19. Example: the first months of 4 a.m. rising versus the tenth year.
5. **It is stable because its object is stable.** BG 6.22; BG 2.66. Example: the same difficulty (a transfer to
   another temple) that crushes one resident and leaves another peaceful.
6. **Its visible sign is what we talk about.** BG 10.9; BG 18.54. Example: a prasadam table where the conversation
   is Krishna-katha, and one where it is complaints. Transition to P: choose which table to be.

## J. Stories and illustrations

- **Sri Yamunacarya's testimony (BG 5.21 purport).** Prabhupada quotes the acharya's own verse: since he engaged in
  Krishna's service, "realizing ever-new pleasure in Him", the thought of sex pleasure makes him spit and his lips curl
  with distaste. Devotional lesson: higher taste is not suppression; it is the natural result of tasting the higher
  pleasure (the purport's own point).
- **Lord Rsabhadeva's instruction (SB 5.5.1).** The Lord in the form of a king tells His hundred sons that human life
  is not for the sense gratification of dogs and hogs; austerity for devotional service purifies the heart and gives
  "eternal, blissful life". Devotional lesson: austerity is the door to bliss, not its opposite. (bss-07 tells the
  whole instruction; here only this verse.)
- **The Panca-tattva "danced, cried, laughed and chanted like madmen" (CC Adi 7.22, taught in trd-22).** Lord
  Caitanya's associates distributed love of God by their own joy. Devotional lesson: joy in kirtana is itself
  preaching. Point the residents to trd-22 for the whole passage.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. *"I serve sincerely and I am not happy. Am I failing?"* Not necessarily. BG 18.37: goodness-happiness is poison
   first. SB 1.2.19 places complete happiness after irrevocable devotion. Ask the honest diagnostic questions: is
   hearing regular (SB 1.2.18, dps-15)? has a motive entered (SB 1.2.6)? is there an unresolved offence (trd-50)? If
   low mood continues for weeks with sleep and appetite affected, also see a doctor; the spiritual diagnosis and plain
   medical care do not compete.
2. *"Isn't seeking happiness selfish for a devotee?"* Seeking it for oneself is (SB 1.2.6: service must be
   unmotivated). But Krishna Himself promises it (BG 6.21, BG 9.2 "joyfully performed"), and a joyless preacher
   preaches a joyless Krishna, which is false (Bs 5.37).
3. *"Grave devotees are the serious ones; the joyful ones are sentimental."* BG 18.54 and BG 10.9 describe the
   advanced devotee as "fully joyful" and deriving "great satisfaction and bliss" from Krishna-katha. Sentimentality is
   imitation of symptoms (trd-13); joy in hearing and chanting is the real thing.
4. *"Why does the Gita's definition come from the yoga chapter? We are not yogis."* Prabhupada's purport says the
   state described is reached by Lord Caitanya's *ceto-darpana-marjanam*, the cleansing of the heart by chanting, and
   that devotional service follows it. The yoga chapter describes the state; bhakti gives it.

## L. Case studies

**Case 1 (Invented case for discussion).** A brahmacari of eight years was the temple's best book distributor and
was known for it. He has been moved to kitchen service after a change of leadership. He does the service well but says
he has "lost his taste". *Dilemma:* is this loss of service happiness, or loss of position happiness? *Principles:*
SB 1.2.6 (unmotivated service); BG 6.22 (never shaken). *Questions:* What exactly did he lose? What stayed? *Analysis:*
the kitchen service is also service to the ever-blissful Lord; if the bliss left with the post, part of the bliss was in
the post. *Takeaway:* name the mixed motive with kindness, and ask him to hear more, not to serve less.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions (expected answers in brackets):**
1. What does Bs 5.37 say about Krishna's realm? [It is permeated and vitalized by His ever-blissful spiritual rasa.]
2. Through which senses is real happiness realised, according to BG 6.21? [Transcendental senses.]
3. What two qualities must service have to completely satisfy the self (SB 1.2.6)? [Unmotivated and uninterrupted.]
4. When does the devotee "become completely happy" (SB 1.2.19)? [When irrevocable loving service is established.]
5. What does happiness in the mode of goodness taste like at first (BG 18.37)? [Like poison; later like nectar.]
6. What is the visible sign of devotees' happiness in BG 10.9? [Satisfaction and bliss in conversing about Krishna.]

**M.2 Activity (20 min): the private audit.** Each resident writes, for himself only: (a) three moments of real
happiness in the last month and what he was doing; (b) the last time he was "shaken" and what was threatened; (c) one
motive that may have mixed into his service. Then pairs discuss only the general pattern ("most of my happy moments
were in kirtana"), never the private details. Close with each pair naming one practice to protect.

**M.3 Reflection.** "If a guest watched me for one day, would he conclude that Krishna is ever-blissful?"

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Lord Brahma prays to Govinda, who lives in His own realm with Radha and Her companions, "permeated and vitalized by
His ever-blissful spiritual rasa" (Bs 5.37). Krishna is ever-blissful by nature. We are small parts of Him, and when we
serve Him we share His happiness. That is why a servant of Krishna, living in His temple, should be the happiest person
in any room.

The Bhagavad-gita describes what real happiness is like: it is "boundless transcendental happiness, realized through
transcendental senses"; a person who has it "thinks there is no greater gain" and "is never shaken, even in the midst
of greatest difficulty" (BG 6.20-23). Our ordinary senses can only touch matter, so they cannot touch this happiness.
Service engages our spiritual senses, by hearing Krishna's name, speaking about Him and working for Him, and through
them the happiness is actually tasted. Srila Prabhupada says devotional service "is not inactivity, but the unalloyed
activity of the soul" (SB 1.2.19 purport).

The Bhagavatam adds two conditions. Service completely satisfies the self only when it is "unmotivated and
uninterrupted" (SB 1.2.6); a hidden motive such as position or praise spoils the taste. And the devotee "becomes
completely happy" when his devotion has become irrevocable (SB 1.2.19), so this happiness grows in stages. The Gita
is honest about the beginning: happiness in goodness "in the beginning may be just like poison but at the end is just
like nectar" (BG 18.37). Lord Rsabhadeva taught His sons that austerity for devotional service purifies the heart and
gives "eternal, blissful life" (SB 5.5.1). The visible sign of this happiness is simple: devotees "derive great
satisfaction and bliss from always enlightening one another and conversing about Me" (BG 10.9).

## S2. Key ideas
- Krishna and His realm are ever-blissful (*ananda-cinmaya-rasa*, Bs 5.37); His servant shares that bliss.
- Real happiness is boundless, tasted through transcendental senses, and never shaken (BG 6.20-23).
- Service satisfies the self fully only when it is unmotivated and uninterrupted (SB 1.2.6).
- It grows in stages: poison first, nectar later (BG 18.37); complete happiness follows irrevocable devotion (SB 1.2.19).
- Its sign is what we talk about with each other (BG 10.9).

## S3. Verses to remember
- **Bs 5.37**: Krishna's realm is "permeated and vitalized by His ever-blissful spiritual rasa". For me: the Lord I
  serve is bliss itself.
- **BG 6.21-22**: "boundless transcendental happiness, realized through transcendental senses… never shaken". For me:
  if I am shaken, I check what I was depending on.
- **SB 1.2.6**: service "must be unmotivated and uninterrupted to completely satisfy the self". For me: I look for
  the hidden motive when the taste goes.

## S4. Words to know
- **Ananda-cinmaya-rasa:** ever-blissful spiritual rasa (taste, relationship); the nature of Krishna's realm.
- **Ahaituki, apratihata:** unmotivated, uninterrupted (SB 1.2.6).
- **Naisthiki bhakti:** irrevocable, fixed devotional service (SB 1.2.19).
- **Brahma-bhuta:** the joyful, liberated stage from which pure devotional service begins (BG 18.54).

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *I serve sincerely but I am not happy. Am I failing?* Not necessarily. The Gita says the path tastes like poison
   first (BG 18.37), and the Bhagavatam places complete happiness after devotion becomes irrevocable (SB 1.2.19). Check
   your hearing, your motives and your relationships with devotees. If low mood lasts for weeks and affects sleep and
   appetite, see a doctor as well.
2. *Isn't wanting happiness selfish?* Wanting it for myself spoils service (SB 1.2.6). But Krishna promises it
   (BG 6.21), and a joyless devotee misrepresents an ever-blissful Lord (Bs 5.37).
3. *Are serious devotees supposed to look grave?* BG 18.54 calls the advanced devotee "fully joyful"; BG 10.9 says
   devotees are satisfied and blissful in Krishna-katha.
4. *Why quote the yoga chapter?* Prabhupada's purport on BG 6.20-23 says this state comes by cleansing the heart
   through chanting (Lord Caitanya's *ceto-darpana-marjanam*) and leads to devotional service.

## S6. Check your understanding
1. According to BG 6.21, through which senses is real happiness realised?
2. Name the two qualities service must have to completely satisfy the self (SB 1.2.6).
3. True or false: BG 18.37 says happiness in goodness is sweet from the very beginning.
4. What does BG 10.9 say devotees derive "great satisfaction and bliss" from?
5. According to SB 1.2.19, what happens to lust, desire and hankering when irrevocable service is established?

**Answers:** 1. Transcendental senses (BG 6.21). 2. Unmotivated and uninterrupted (SB 1.2.6). 3. False: it is "just like
poison" in the beginning and nectar at the end (BG 18.37). 4. Enlightening one another and conversing about Krishna
(BG 10.9). 5. They disappear from the heart, and the devotee becomes completely happy (SB 1.2.19).
