# God and Google

> **VL1 · 45–60 min · review: sources checked, awaiting senior review.** IYF's deck (*God and Google*, subtitled "Human relationships in the
> Internet age") sets two columns side by side: Google (ephemeral, in flux, public, impersonal, "no strings
> attached", information) and God (eternal, stable, private, personal, moral responsibility, transformation);
> argues that "The Purpose of Knowledge is not just Information, but Transformation"; defines God as
> sat-cit-ananda (stable, factual, beneficial); notes that our top searches are for people ("we are searching for
> relationship"); and closes with head–heart–hands and the six loving exchanges (NOI 4). This page keeps that
> structure, which is good, supports it from the books, and drops the dated search statistics and an unverified
> Einstein quotation (B1). The tone is playful: nobody is told to stop using Google.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

The title does the work: it puts the audience's daily reflex next to the biggest word there is, and lets them
laugh before they think. At VL1 that is exactly right. The talk needs no faith to begin: everyone knows the
difference between looking something up and being taught by someone who cares, and everyone has felt that the
feed knows everything and nobody. The Gita speaks to both: it insists that knowledge worth having passes
through a chain of persons who have seen the truth (4.2, 4.34), that the crisis Arjuna faced was solved by
asking someone (2.7), and that the final source of remembrance and knowledge is a Person within (15.15,
10.10–11) who, unlike any index, knows "all things that are yet to come" (7.26).

**Builds on vl1-14 and DYS-1:** [vl1-14](vl1-14.md) shows how trust in a source is built and tested (BG 4.34 as the method of inquiry); DYS-1 argues the case for God and science. Here BG 4.34 gets one line and the argument for God is not repeated: the angle is information versus relationship.

**Not done here:**
- Telling anyone to stop using Google, AI assistants or social media. The talk is about what to use them for.
- Proving God's existence (VL2-08). The talk compares two kinds of knowing and lets the comparison invite.
- Fear of technology, or claims about its effects on the brain beyond what O cites.

## C. Central message

Google gives information instantly, from no one in particular, with no interest in what it does to you. The Gita
says the knowledge that changes a life comes through a person who has seen the truth and cares what you do with
it, and finally from the One who knows past, present and future and sits in your own heart. We are not really
searching for information; we are searching for someone who knows us. That search has an answer.

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. State the difference between information and transformation in their own words (BG 4.38, 9.2) and give an
   example of each from their week.
2. Explain why the Gita insists on a *source* for knowledge (BG 4.2, 4.34) and what a "source-free" feed lacks.
3. Describe what BG 7.26 and 15.15 say about God's knowledge, and how that differs from an index.
4. Name the six exchanges that build a relationship (NOI 4) and say which one a search engine cannot do.
5. Choose one question this week to take to a person instead of a search bar.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** BG 4.2, 4.34 and 2.7 carry the "source" argument; 7.26, 15.15 and 10.10–11 the "Person within"
argument; 4.38, 9.2, ISO 9 and 11 the "what knowledge is for" argument; Bs 5.1 defines; NOI 4 closes.

**BG 7.26**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/7/26/
> "O Arjuna, as the Supreme Personality of Godhead, I know everything that has happened in the past, all that is happening in the present, and all things that are yet to come. I also know all living entities; but Me no one knows."
- **Context:** Chapter 7, Krsna describing Himself after explaining why people do not know Him.
- **Teaching:** God knows past, present and future, and every living being; He is not known by anyone in return.
- **Purport point:** "Anyone with a material body cannot remember his past life, nor can he foretell his future
  life"; Krsna "knows every living entity because He is situated in every living being's heart as the
  Supersoul."
- **Application (preacher's):** The hook of the talk. Google indexes the past and guesses the present; the verse
  claims knowledge of what "is yet to come" and of every person. The last clause is the joke and the point:
  everyone knows Google; "Me no one knows" is the invitation to find out.
- **Use:** in the talk (opening verse).

**BG 15.15**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/15/15/
> "I am seated in everyone's heart, and from Me come remembrance, knowledge and forgetfulness. By all the Vedas, I am to be known. Indeed, I am the compiler of Vedānta, and I am the knower of the Vedas."
- **Teaching:** The Lord is in the heart and is the source of remembrance, knowledge and forgetting.
- **Purport point:** "Required knowledge is supplied to him, and remembrance is given to him, and he forgets,
  also"; the Lord is "the giver of knowledge in the form of the Vedas".
- **Application (preacher's):** A search engine is outside, in a data centre; this knower is inside, "seated in
  everyone's heart". Remembrance and forgetfulness both come from Him: the thing you cannot recall and the thing
  that suddenly comes to you. Say it lightly; it lands.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 4.2**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/4/2/
> "This supreme science was thus received through the chain of disciplic succession, and the saintly kings understood it in that way. But in course of time the succession was broken, and therefore the science as it is appears to be lost."
- **Teaching:** This science was received through a chain of persons; when the succession was broken, the
  science as it is appeared to be lost.
- **Purport point:** "there are so many editions of the Gītā (especially in English), but almost all of them are
  not according to authorized disciplic succession."
- **Application (preacher's):** The deck's best observation is that the internet is "source-free": anyone can
  post anything without responsibility. The Gita's idea of knowledge is the opposite: a chain of named persons,
  each answerable to the last. Ten translations of the same verse online do not make a tradition.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 4.34**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/4/34/
> "Just try to learn the truth by approaching a spiritual master. Inquire from him submissively and render service unto him. The self-realized souls can impart knowledge unto you because they have seen the truth."
- **Teaching:** Truth is learned by approaching someone who has seen it, with inquiry and service.
- **Purport point:** "Nor by independent study of books of knowledge can one progress in spiritual life. One has
  to approach a bona fide spiritual master"; "both blind following and absurd inquiries are condemned."
- **Application (preacher's):** Three things a search cannot do: it has not *seen* anything; it cannot receive
  your service; and it does not care whether you understood. The purport's balance ("blind following and absurd
  inquiries" both condemned) also answers the fear that a teacher means switching off the mind.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 2.7**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/7/
> "…In this condition I am asking You to tell me for certain what is best for me. Now I am Your disciple, and a soul surrendered unto You. Please instruct me."
- **Teaching:** At his crisis, Arjuna asks a person he trusts.
- **Purport point:** The remedy for life's perplexities is to "approach a bona fide spiritual master".
- **Application (preacher's):** Arjuna had all the information: he knew the armies, the law, the stakes. What he
  lacked was someone who knew *him*. The whole Gita exists because he asked a person, not a reference.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 4.38**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/4/38/
> "In this world, there is nothing so sublime and pure as transcendental knowledge. Such knowledge is the mature fruit of all mysticism. And one who has become accomplished in the practice of devotional service enjoys this knowledge within himself in due course of time."
- **Purport point:** "Ignorance is the cause of our bondage, and knowledge is the cause of our liberation";
  one situated in it "need not search for peace elsewhere, for he enjoys peace within himself."
- **Application (preacher's):** The deck's "information vs transformation" in the text's words: this knowledge is
  "enjoyed within", and it ends the search ("need not search for peace elsewhere"). Information leaves you
  searching; this does not.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 9.2**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/9/2/
> "This knowledge is the king of education, the most secret of all secrets. It is the purest knowledge, and because it gives direct perception of the self by realization, it is the perfection of religion. It is everlasting, and it is joyfully performed."
- **Purport point:** "There are so many departments of knowledge all over the world and many huge universities,
  but there is, unfortunately, no university or educational institution where the science of the spirit soul is
  instructed."
- **Application (preacher's):** Every subject is searchable; the one the Gita calls the king of education is not
  on the syllabus. Not a complaint about universities; an observation about what the search bar does not index.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**ISO 9**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/iso/9/
> "Those who engage in the culture of nescient activities shall enter into the darkest region of ignorance. Worse still are those engaged in the culture of so-called knowledge."
- **Teaching:** Ignorance is dangerous; misdirected knowledge is more so.
- **Purport point:** "Modern civilization has advanced considerably in the field of mass education, but the
  result is that people are more unhappy than ever before because of the stress placed on material advancement
  to the exclusion of the most important part of life, the spiritual aspect."
- **Application (preacher's):** The deck's point that science tells "how things are, not how they should be" and
  its list of consequences (weapons, climate) is this mantra's. Say it as the text does, about *direction*, not
  against scientists.
- **Use:** in the talk, with care.

**ISO 11**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/iso/11/
> "Only one who can learn the process of nescience and that of transcendental knowledge side by side can transcend the influence of repeated birth and death and enjoy the full blessings of immortality."
- **Purport point:** "Material science can discover the nuclear bomb to accelerate the process of death, but it
  cannot discover anything that can protect man from the cruel hands of old age, disease and death."
- **Application (preacher's):** "Side by side" is the talk's balance: keep Google, add the other knowledge. The
  mantra does not ask anyone to delete an app.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 10.10**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/10/10/
> "To those who are constantly devoted to serving Me with love, I give the understanding by which they can come to Me."
- **Purport point:** "People do not know this; therefore the association of devotees and a bona fide spiritual
  master are important."
- **Use:** in the talk, with 10.11.

**BG 10.11**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/10/11/
> "To show them special mercy, I, dwelling in their hearts, destroy with the shining lamp of knowledge the darkness born of ignorance."
- **Purport point:** "even if a devotee does not take advantage of their literatures or of his spiritual master,
  if he is sincere in his devotional service he is helped by Kṛṣṇa Himself within his heart."
- **Application (preacher's):** The final source is personal and inside: a lamp lit by someone who dwells in the
  heart. Contrast gently with a result returned by a server.
- **Use:** in the talk (climax of the "Person within" thread).

**NOI 4**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/4/ (deeper verse; the talk uses only its plain meaning: six everyday exchanges)
> "Offering gifts in charity, accepting charitable gifts, revealing one's mind in confidence, inquiring confidentially, accepting prasāda and offering prasāda are the six symptoms of love shared by one devotee and another."
- **Teaching:** Six exchanges make love between persons: giving, receiving, confiding, inquiring confidentially,
  sharing food both ways.
- **Purport point:** "Even in ordinary social activities, these six types of dealings between two loving friends
  are absolutely necessary." The purport says ISKCON "has been established to facilitate these six kinds of
  loving exchanges".
- **Application (preacher's):** The deck's conclusion: we are searching for relationship. Here is what a
  relationship is made of, and the search bar can do none of the six. "revealing one's mind in confidence" is
  the one the audience most lacks and most wants. The invitation is to a room where these six happen.
- **Use:** in the talk (close).

**Bs 5.1**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bs/5/1/
> "Kṛṣṇa who is known as Govinda is the Supreme Godhead. He has an eternal blissful spiritual body. He is the origin of all. He has no other origin and He is the prime cause of all causes."
- **Teaching:** God has an eternal, blissful, spiritual form and is the cause of all causes.
- **Application (preacher's):** The deck's "Vedantic definition" sat-cit-ananda (eternal, full of knowledge,
  full of bliss) is in this verse's *sac-cid-ananda-vigrahah*. Use it for the God column: eternal (not
  ephemeral), knowing (not in flux), blissful (beneficial, not "no strings").
- **Use:** in the talk (the definition).

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** Two kinds of knowing. Information is retrieved from a source-free index; it is ephemeral,
   impersonal, and indifferent to what it does to you. Transformative knowledge, the Gita says, is received
   through persons who have seen the truth (4.34) in an answerable chain (4.2); it is "enjoyed within" and ends
   the search (4.38); it is "the king of education", taught (the purport says) in no institution (9.2); and its final source
   is a Person who knows past, present and future (7.26), sits in the heart as the giver of remembrance and
   forgetfulness (15.15), and lights a lamp there for the sincere (10.10–11). God is eternal, knowing and
   blissful (Bs 5.1); the feed is none of the three. Both kinds of knowledge are needed, "side by side"
   (ISO 11); misdirected knowledge is worse than none (ISO 9). And what we are really searching for is a
   relationship, which is made of six exchanges no engine performs (NOI 4).
2. **Why it matters.** The audience's instinct that "everything is online" quietly includes the answer to their
   life. The talk does not scold that instinct; it shows what the search bar structurally cannot return: a
   person who has seen, who knows them, who cares what they do next. Once that is seen, the invitation to a
   teacher and a community is not religious pressure; it is the obvious next search.
3. **Connections.** How trust in a source is built (VL1-14); technology used well (VL1-39); the argued case
   (VL2-08). At VL3 hearing from a teacher becomes a practice; NOI 4 becomes the texture of community life.
4. **Consequences.** A listener who hears this well will (a) keep using Google and know what for, (b) take at
   least one real question to a person this week, (c) notice when a feed is source-free and treat it
   accordingly, and (d) look for a room where the six exchanges happen.
5. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - **"You're against technology."** No. ISO 11 says "side by side". The talk is about direction and source.
   - **"A guru means switching off my mind."** The 4.34 purport condemns "blind following" as much as "absurd
     inquiries". Inquiry is required.
   - **"God is just a bigger database."** 7.26 and 15.15 describe a Person who knows *you* and sits in your
     heart; Bs 5.1 describes a form, eternal and blissful. Not an index.
   - **"Information is bad."** Information is useful and the texts never say otherwise; the danger is knowledge
     with the wrong direction (ISO 9) or without a source (4.2).
   - **"AI assistants are different; they talk back."** They are more fluent and still source-free and unseeing;
     they have not "seen the truth" (4.34) and cannot receive your service or share your food (NOI 4). Use them
     for what they are.
6. **Pitch for VL1.** Sanskrit: *sat-cit-ananda* once, glossed as the deck does; *parampara* (the chain) once.
   Tone: playful about our search habits, warm about the alternative, never nostalgic or anti-tech.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet, give your name, and say the talk compares two ways of knowing, with no demand to believe.
2. Ask H.2 question 1 by show of hands; welcome laughter.
3. Ask question 2; collect three answers on the board.
4. Read BG 7.26 (below), pausing before "but Me no one knows."
5. Give the transition line and go to segment 2.

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the room's own life)
1. Who searched for something online today? Who asked a real life question (a job, a relationship, a worry)?
2. When did a person, a grandparent, a teacher, a village elder, a mentor, help you more than any search result?
3. What could that person do that a search engine cannot?

- **Opening:** "What did you search for today? Don't tell me the embarrassing ones." Laughter. "Now: who has
  asked a search engine, or an AI, a real life question this week? Should I take this job. Why am I like this.
  Does she like me." Hands, grinning. "The Gita has a verse about someone who knows the answer to all three.
  Here it is." Read BG 7.26, pausing before "but Me no one knows."
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** At one a.m., after a fight with his father, Aarav types "how to know if I'm
  making the right decision about my career" and reads eleven articles. He feels briefly better and no different.
  His grandmother, who has watched him for twenty years, is asleep in the next room.
- **Transition:** "What did the eleven articles not have, that the next room did?"
- **Why it matters to this audience:** They have more information than any generation and report feeling less
  known. The two facts are related.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Two columns.** Google: ephemeral, in flux, impersonal, source-free, information. God: eternal, knowing,
   blissful (Bs 5.1), knowing "all things that are yet to come" and every living being (7.26). *Example:* the
   deck's table. *Transition:* so what is knowledge for?
2. **Information vs transformation.** Transcendental knowledge is "enjoyed within" and ends the search (4.38);
   it is "the king of education", taught, the purport says, in no university (9.2). Keep both kinds "side by side" (ISO 11); direction
   matters more than quantity (ISO 9). *Example:* Aarav's eleven articles. *Transition:* what did the articles lack?
3. **A source.** The internet is source-free; the Gita's knowledge comes through an answerable chain (4.2), from
   someone who has "seen the truth", by inquiry and service, neither blindly nor absurdly (4.34). Arjuna had all
   the data and asked a person (2.7). *Example:* the grandmother in the next room. *Transition:* and the final
   source?
4. **A Person within.** "I am seated in everyone's heart"; remembrance and forgetting come from Him (15.15); for the
   sincere He lights "the shining lamp of knowledge" from inside (10.10–11). *Example:* the answer that "came to
   you" in the shower. *Transition:* what are we searching for, then?
5. **Relationship.** Our searches are mostly people. A relationship is six exchanges: giving, receiving, confiding,
   inquiring confidentially, sharing food both ways (NOI 4). The engine does none; the room after this talk does
   all six. *Transition:* to one question for a person.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

> **Before you teach:** the room includes engineers who build these tools and people whose livelihoods depend on
> them. Never mock the internet, AI or those who work on them. The talk is about what knowledge is for and where
> it comes from, not against any technology. If someone brings a genuine life crisis to the "ask a person"
> point, that is the moment to offer a conversation afterwards, not a verse.

1. **"Are you telling us to stop using Google?"**
   - No. The Isopanisad says to learn both kinds of knowledge "side by side" (ISO 11). Use it for what it is
     good at. Just don't ask it who you are.
2. **"AI assistants aren't source-free. They cite sources and even give advice."**
   - They are more fluent, and they have still not "seen the truth" (4.34), do not know you (7.26 is about a
     Person who does), and cannot receive your service or share your food (NOI 4). Good tools; not teachers in
     the Gita's sense.
3. **"Why do I need a guru? Isn't that just outsourcing my thinking?"**
   - The 4.34 purport condemns "blind following" in the same sentence as "absurd inquiries". A teacher in this
     tradition is someone you question, serve and test over time (VL1-14). Arjuna argued through the end of chapter 1
     and into chapter 2 before he asked (2.7).
4. **"God as 'the one who knows everything' sounds like a bigger search engine."**
   - 7.26 says He knows every *living entity*, and 15.15 that He sits in your heart giving remembrance and
     forgetting. An index does not know you or live in you. Bs 5.1 adds that He has an eternal, blissful form:
     a Person, not a server.
5. **"Science tells us how things are. Isn't that enough?"**
   - It is a great deal, and the texts do not belittle it. ISO 9 and 11 say direction matters as much as
     content, and that no amount of "how things are" tells you what to do about death. The deck's line that
     science says "how things are, not how they should be" is fair; the "should" needs a different source.
6. **"I don't have a grandmother in the next room. Whom do I ask?"**
   - Start with the six exchanges (NOI 4): find people who will give, receive, confide, inquire and share food
     with you. The facilitation after this talk is designed to be that. A teacher comes through such a community
     (10.10 purport: "the association of devotees and a bona fide spiritual master are important").
7. **"Isn't 'revealing your mind in confidence' risky online and offline?"**
   - Online, the deck's "public" column is the risk: nothing is confidential. Offline, confidence is built slowly
     with people who have earned it; NOI 4 lists it third, after giving and receiving. Take your time.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: Aarav at one a.m.** After a fight with his father, Aarav reads eleven articles on career decisions
and feels no different. His grandmother is asleep next door.
- **Dilemma:** Why did eleven good articles not help?
- **Principles:** BG 4.34 (someone who has seen; inquiry and service); 2.7 (Arjuna asked a person); 4.38
  (knowledge "enjoyed within" ends the search); NOI 4 (confiding).
- **Questions:** What did the articles have, and lack? What does his grandmother know that no article does? What
  would "inquiring confidentially" look like tomorrow morning?
- **Suggested analysis:** The articles were information from no one; Aarav needed someone who knows him and
  cares what he does next. One conversation at breakfast is the Gita's method in miniature.
- **Takeaway:** Take real questions to real people; use the search bar for the rest.

**Case 2: Nisha's group chat.** Nisha has four hundred contacts, three group chats that never sleep, and nobody
she would tell about the diagnosis she received on Tuesday.
- **Principles:** NOI 4 (six exchanges; "revealing one's mind in confidence"); the deck's "public" column;
  BG 15.15 (she is not unknown: "seated in everyone's heart").
- **Questions:** Which of the six exchanges does Nisha's network provide? Which does she lack? Where might the
  missing ones be found?
- **Takeaway:** Connection is not relationship; look for the six exchanges, and for a room that practises them.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

We look everything up. The talk asks what the search bar cannot return. Google gives information instantly, from no one in particular, with no interest in what it does to us. The Bhagavad-gita (an ancient Indian text) describes a different kind of knowing. It says that the knowledge which changes a life passes through persons who have "seen the truth"
(BG 4.34), in a chain where each is answerable to the last (BG 4.2); Arjuna had every fact about the battle and
still turned to the person beside him (BG 2.7). This knowledge is "enjoyed within" and ends the search (BG 4.38);
it is "the king of education", and the purport says no university teaches it (BG 9.2).

Its final source is a Person who knows
"everything that has happened in the past, all that is happening in the present, and all things that are yet to
come" (BG 7.26), who is "seated in everyone's heart" giving remembrance and forgetfulness (BG 15.15), and who
lights "the shining lamp of knowledge" within the sincere (BG 10.11).

Keep Google; the Isopanisad says to learn
both kinds of knowledge "side by side" (ISO 11). And notice that most of what we search for is people. A
relationship is made of six exchanges: giving, receiving, confiding, inquiring in confidence, and sharing food
both ways (NOI 4). No engine does any of them. The room you are in does.

## S2. Key ideas

- Information comes from no one; transforming knowledge comes through someone who has seen (BG 4.34, 4.2).
- Arjuna had all the facts and asked a person (BG 2.7).
- This knowledge is "enjoyed within" and ends the search (BG 4.38); the purport to BG 9.2 says no university teaches it.
- God knows past, present, future and every being (BG 7.26); He sits in the heart (BG 15.15) and lights a lamp
  there (BG 10.10–11). He is eternal, knowing and blissful (Bs 5.1).
- Keep both kinds of knowledge "side by side" (ISO 11); direction matters (ISO 9).
- We are searching for relationship; a relationship is six exchanges (NOI 4).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **BG 7.26:** "I know everything that has happened in the past, all that is happening in the present, and all
  things that are yet to come. I also know all living entities; but Me no one knows." *For me:* someone knows
  the answer to my real questions, and knows me.
- **BG 4.34:** "The self-realized souls can impart knowledge unto you because they have seen the truth." *For
  me:* ask someone who has seen, not something that has indexed.
- **NOI 4:** "…revealing one's mind in confidence, inquiring confidentially…" *For me:* this is what I am actually
  searching for.

## S4. Words to know

- **Sat-cit-ananda:** eternal, full of knowledge, full of bliss: the nature of God's form (Bs 5.1).
- **Parampara:** the chain of teachers through which knowledge is received, each answerable to the last (BG 4.2).
- **Information vs transformation:** facts retrieved, versus knowledge that changes how you live (BG 4.38).
- **The six exchanges:** giving, receiving, confiding, inquiring confidentially, accepting and offering prasada
  (NOI 4).

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Are you telling me to stop using Google?**
   No. Learn both kinds of knowledge "side by side" (ISO 11). Use it for what it is good at; don't ask it who
   you are.
2. **AI assistants give advice and cite sources. Aren't they teachers?**
   They are fluent tools. They have not "seen the truth" (BG 4.34), do not know you, and cannot share a meal with
   you (NOI 4).
3. **Why do I need a teacher? Isn't that outsourcing my thinking?**
   The Gita condemns blind following as much as silly questioning (BG 4.34 purport). A teacher is someone you
   question, serve and test over time.
4. **Isn't "God knows everything" just a bigger database?**
   He knows every living being (BG 7.26) and sits in your heart (BG 15.15). A database does not know you or live
   in you.
5. **Science tells us how things are. Isn't that enough?**
   It is a great deal. The Isopanisad says direction matters as much as content, and that "how things are" does
   not tell you what to do about death (ISO 9, 11).
6. **Whom do I ask, if I have no one?**
   Start with the six exchanges (NOI 4): find people who will give, receive, confide, ask and share food with you.
   The conversation after this talk is meant to be that.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. According to BG 7.26, Krsna knows: (a) the past only, (b) past, present and future, and every living being,
   (c) whatever has been written down.
2. Why, according to BG 4.34, can self-realised souls impart knowledge?
3. True or false: ISO 11 says we should give up material knowledge entirely.
4. Name three of the six exchanges in NOI 4.
5. In your own words: what did Arjuna have in BG 2.7, and what did he lack?

**Answers:** 1. (b) (BG 7.26). 2. Because "they have seen the truth" (BG 4.34). 3. False: it says to learn
nescience and transcendental knowledge "side by side" (ISO 11). 4. Any three of: offering gifts, accepting gifts,
revealing one's mind in confidence, inquiring confidentially, accepting prasada, offering prasada (NOI 4). 5. He
had all the information about the battle; he lacked someone who knew him and could tell him what was best, so
he asked (BG 2.7).
