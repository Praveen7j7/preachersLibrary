# The seven secondary rasas, and rasabhasa (mixed and incompatible mellows)

> **TRD · up to 180 min · review: drafted · `sensitive: true` (rasa; the head reviews).** Group G, page 2. trd-33 named the seven
> indirect rasas and said that rasabhasa exists; trd-41 took the five direct rasas one by one. This page completes the map: the seven
> secondary mellows (laughter, astonishment, chivalry, compassion, anger, dread, ghastliness) as Srila Prabhupada summarises the fourth
> division of the Bhakti-rasamrta-sindhu (NOD 45–48), then the science of mixing: which mellows combine palatably, which do not, the
> rule of "whole and part", and the three kinds of perverted mellow, uparasa, anurasa and aparasa (NOD 49–51). The page ends where
> Lord Caitanya's own practice ends: nothing was recited before Him until Svarupa Damodara had checked it for rasabhasa (CC Antya
> 5.95–98). **Source note:** the BRS file in `research/books/` (OCR) holds only the Eastern Division; the Northern Division (secondary
> rasas and rasabhasa) is read through Prabhupada's summary study, which is the primary source in any case.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on trd-33 and trd-41. Those pages gave the twelve names and the five direct relationships; this page gives the seven that remain and
the science that follows from having a complete set: once relationships have exact ingredients, some combinations nourish taste and others
spoil it. Prabhupada calls this "a scientific analysis of the compatibility and incompatibility of the mixtures of these various rasas" (NOD
49). It is the part of rasa-tattva a resident uses most often without knowing its name: every time he chooses a bhajana for a programme,
writes a skit for a youth festival, or hears a junior describe Krishna's pastimes in a way that makes the room uneasy, he is judging
rasabhasa.

Lord Caitanya took this seriously enough to make a rule of it: "If there were a hint that transcendental mellows overlapped in a manner
contrary to the principles of the bhakti cult, Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu would not tolerate it and would become very angry" (CC Antya 5.97),
and so nothing was recited before Him until Svarupa Damodara had heard it first (5.98). A resident who leads others needs the same ear.
Lower batches hear only that Krishna's pastimes have humour, wonder and heroism in them (Krsna book); no lower batch learns the seven as
mellows or studies mixing.

## C. Central message

Beside the five permanent relationships there are seven mellows that "appear suddenly under certain conditions and appear more powerful"
(CC Madhya 19.188): laughter, astonishment, chivalry, compassion, anger, dread and ghastliness. In each of them "Kṛṣṇa or matters pertaining
to Kṛṣṇa are the cause" (NOD 45). Because every rasa is exact, mixtures are either compatible or incompatible; the prominent mellow is the
"whole", the subordinate the "part" (NOD 49), and an incompatible mixture is rasabhasa, "a perverted presentation of mellows" (NOD 45), of
three kinds: uparasa, anurasa and aparasa (NOD 51). Lord Caitanya would not hear a composition that had even a hint of it until Svarupa
Damodara had checked it (CC Antya 5.95–98). A resident who sings, writes or speaks of Krishna keeps that same check.

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Name the seven secondary rasas in the English words Srila Prabhupada uses in his translation and give one Nectar of Devotion example for each (NOD 45–48).
2. Explain from CC Madhya 19.187–188 and NOD 49 why they are called indirect: occasional, dependent on conditions, "eternally manifesting and unmanifesting in Gokula Vṛndāvana".
3. State the whole-and-part rule and read two lines of the compatibility table (NOD 49) correctly for a given mellow.
4. Distinguish an incompatible mixture (NOD 50's impersonalist, the gopi who wants to "smell Your head") from an acceptable one (Nanda Maharaja's chivalry inside parental love), and define uparasa, anurasa and aparasa with an example (NOD 51).
5. Apply Lord Caitanya's rule (CC Antya 5.95–98, 102–108) to the temple's own songs, dramas and classes: who checks, for what, and in what spirit (5.130–132).

## E. Scriptural foundation

**NOD 45 ("Laughing Ecstasy")** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/nod/45/
- **Teaching:** "In the fourth division of Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu, Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī has described seven kinds of indirect ecstasies of devotional service, known as laughing, astonishment, chivalry, compassion, anger, dread and ghastliness." "When one kind of ecstatic devotional service overlaps with another in a conflicting way, this state of affairs is called rasābhāsa, or a perverted presentation of mellows." Laughter "is generally found among youngsters or in the combination of old persons and young children"; "In such a laughing ecstasy of love, Kṛṣṇa or matters pertaining to Kṛṣṇa are the cause of the laughter." Six degrees of smiling: "smita, hasita, vihasita, avahasita, apahasita and atihasita".
- **Application (preacher's):** devotional laughter has Krishna as its object or occasion; the test for a joke in a class or drama is the same.
- **Use:** in the talk; the first secondary rasa.

**NOD 46 ("Astonishment and Chivalry")** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/nod/46/
- **Teaching:** "The ecstasy of astonishment in devotional service is perceived in two ways: directly, by the experience of one's own eyes, and indirectly, by hearing from others." Narada seeing Krishna in every palace at Dvaraka; Pariksit hearing of Narakasura's defeat. Chivalry: "When on account of love and devotional service for the Lord there is special valorous enthusiasm, the resultant activities are called chivalrous." Four kinds: yuddha-vira (fighting), dana-vira (charity), daya-vira (mercy), dharma-vira (religious rites); "In all such different chivalrous activities, Kṛṣṇa is the object." Yudhisthira is "The best example of a dharma-vīra".
- **Use:** in the talk; the second and third secondary rasas.

**NOD 47 ("Compassion and Anger")** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/nod/47/
- **Teaching:** "When the ecstasy of devotional service produces some kind of lamentation in connection with Kṛṣṇa, it is called devotional service in compassion." The cowherd boys falling to the ground when Kaliya coiled around Krishna (SB 10.16.10). Anger: Rohini rebuking Yasoda when the arjuna trees fell is "an example of ecstatic love in anger caused by Kṛṣṇa"; "In all these humors of ecstatic love, the feeling of anger is accepted as the steady factor."
- **Use:** in the talk; the fourth and fifth.

**NOD 48 ("Dread and Ghastliness")** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/nod/48/
- **Teaching:** "In ecstatic love for Kṛṣṇa in dread, there are two causes of fear: either Kṛṣṇa Himself or some dreadful situation for Kṛṣṇa." Kaliya's prayer; Yasoda's anxiety about Kesi. Ghastliness: "When a devotee, lamenting for his past abominable activities, shows special symptoms on his body, his feeling is called ecstasy in devotional service in ghastliness. This is caused by the awakening of his Kṛṣṇa consciousness." "The person experiencing such ecstatic love for Kṛṣṇa is almost always in the neutral stage of devotional service, or śānta-rasa."
- **Application (preacher's):** ghastliness is the one secondary rasa many residents have felt: disgust at one's past life on awakening to Krishna. NOD 48 names it a mellow and places it on the road to santa.
- **Use:** in the talk; the sixth and seventh.

**NOD 49 ("Mixing of Rasas")** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/nod/49/
- **Teaching:** "The five direct rasas are eternally manifested in the Vaikuṇṭha world, the spiritual kingdom, whereas the seven indirect rasas are eternally manifesting and unmanifesting in Gokula Vṛndāvana". "The following is a scientific analysis of the compatibility and incompatibility of the mixtures of these various rasas, or loving moods." Sample lines: "With the ecstasy of fraternal love a mixture of conjugal love, laughter or chivalry is highly compatible. With the same fraternal love, a mixture of dread or parental love is most incompatible." "With the ecstasy of parental love a mixture of conjugal love, chivalry or anger is incompatible." The rule: "the prominent feeling is called the whole, and the subordinate feeling is called the part." And: "when different mellows overlap one another, the mellow that is the whole, or the prominent humor, is called the permanent ecstasy."
- **Purport point (the practice):** "When Lord Caitanya Mahāprabhu was residing in Jagannātha Purī, many poets and devotees used to come to Him and offer their different kinds of poetry, but the regulation was that Lord Caitanya's secretary, Svarūpa Dāmodara, first examined all of these writings scrutinizingly, and if he would find that there were no incompatibilities in the rasas, or transcendental mellows, he would then allow the poet to approach Lord Caitanya and recite his poetry."
- **Use:** in the talk; the heart of the page (N).

**NOD 50 ("Further Analysis of Mixed Rasas")** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/nod/50/
- **Teaching:** "When one is eating sweet rice and something salty or sour is mixed in, the mixture is not very tasteful and it is called incompatible." Incompatible examples: the impersonalist lamenting his wasted trance; the one who "may immediately and artificially try to attain to the platform of conjugal love" from a slight neutral attitude and "is found to fall from the standard of devotional service"; the gopi who says "I shall first smell Your head" (servitude inside conjugal love). Acceptable mixtures: Nanda Maharaja's chivalry and dread inside parental love when Krishna goes to kill Kesi, "Both of them, however, improve the position of parental love, and therefore there is no incompatibility"; Rukmini's description of a material husband "shows correct discrimination between matter and spirit."
- **Use:** in the talk; the examples that teach the ear.

**NOD 51 ("Perverted Expression of Mellows")** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/nod/51/
- **Teaching:** "Rasābhāsa, or incompatible mixtures of mellows, may be classified as uparasa (false expression), anurasa (imitation) and aparasa (perverted or misrepresented mellows)." Uparasa: the impersonalist who sees Krishna and feels "the same bliss" as trance (santa-uparasa); Kamsa's "parental" reassurance to Devaki; the yajnika-brahmanas' wives feeding Krishna with the gopis' attraction. Anurasa: Narada astonished at the parrot couple debating Vedanta. Aparasa: Jarasandha laughing proudly as Krishna fled. Closing: "Everything in connection with Kṛṣṇa is called ecstatic devotional love, although it may be exhibited in different ways: sometimes in the right order and sometimes as a perverted reflection."
- **Use:** in the talk; the three names, with examples.

**NOD 33 ("Indirect Expressions of Ecstatic Love")** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/nod/33/
- **Teaching:** short instances of each of the seven: Radharani's friends laughing at Krishna's yogurt oath; Brahma's "What am I seeing here?"; Sridama tightening his belt to fight Krishna.
- **Use:** background; a second example where a group needs one.

**SB 10.43.17** — Verified · *BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples* · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/10/43/17/
> "The wrestlers saw Kṛṣṇa as a lightning bolt, the men of Mathurā as the best of males, the women as Cupid in person, the cowherd men as their relative, the impious rulers as a chastiser, His parents as their child, the King of the Bhojas as death, the unintelligent as the Supreme Lord's universal form, the yogīs as the Absolute Truth and the Vṛṣṇis as their supreme worshipable Deity."
- **Purport point:** Sridhara Svami's verse lists the ten moods: "fury [perceived by the wrestlers], wonder [by the men], conjugal attraction [the women], laughter [the cowherds], chivalry [the kings], mercy [His parents], terror [Kaṁsa], ghastliness [the unintelligent], peaceful neutrality [the yogīs] and loving devotion [the Vṛṣṇis]."
- **Application (preacher's):** one Krishna, one moment, and the hearts in the arena taste Him in different mellows, including anger, dread and ghastliness in those who oppose Him. The verse is the Bhagavatam's own picture of the secondary rasas at work.
- **Use:** in the talk; J.2.

**CC Madhya 23.46** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/23/46/
- **Purport point (BRS 2.5.1):** the permanent moods "bring under control the favorable ecstasies (such as laughing) and unfavorable ecstasies (such as anger)."
- **Also in:** trd-41. Here for the exact relation: the five are kings; the seven are their subjects.
- **Use:** one line; the bridge from trd-41.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **Kings and subjects.** The five direct rasas are sthayi-bhavas, permanent moods that rule "like kings" over favourable emotions such as
   laughing and unfavourable ones such as anger (CC Madhya 23.46 purport). The seven secondary rasas are those subjects raised, for a time,
   to the status of a mellow: they "appear suddenly under certain conditions and appear more powerful" (CC Madhya 19.188). Prabhupada's
   summary: the five "are eternally manifested in the Vaikuṇṭha world", the seven "eternally manifesting and unmanifesting in Gokula Vṛndāvana"
   (NOD 49): they need the pastimes of Vraja, where there are demons to dread, hills to lift and butter to steal.
2. **The seven, each with Krishna as cause.** Laughter at Krishna's play (NOD 45); astonishment at His acts, seen or heard (NOD 46); chivalry
   in fighting, giving, mercy or religious rites "with Kṛṣṇa as the object" (NOD 46); compassion when something seems to threaten Him (NOD 47);
   anger at whatever opposes Him or neglects Him (NOD 47); dread of Him when one has offended, or for Him when He is in danger (NOD 48);
   ghastliness at one's own past when Krishna consciousness awakens (NOD 48). Remove Krishna from any of these and it is an ordinary emotion;
   that is what "indirect" means.
3. **Why mixing is a science.** Because each rasa has exact ingredients (trd-43), two rasas in one expression either reinforce or contradict.
   Prabhupada's table (NOD 49) gives the pairs; its logic is visible: fraternity welcomes laughter and chivalry (friends joke and wrestle) and
   rejects parental love and dread (a friend neither maintains nor fears); parental love welcomes compassion and dread (a mother worries) and
   rejects conjugal love, chivalry and anger toward Krishna.
4. **Whole and part.** In any mixture "the prominent feeling is called the whole, and the subordinate feeling is called the part", and the whole
   is the sthayi-bhava (NOD 49). A compatible part "merges quickly back into the whole", like "a small blade of grass" in a palatable dish (NOD 49).
   Nanda Maharaja's vow to kill Kesi himself is chivalry and dread inside parental love, and both "improve the position of parental love" (NOD 50).
5. **Rasabhasa.** When the part contradicts the whole, taste is spoiled: "sweet rice and something salty or sour" (NOD 50). The gopi who wants
   first to smell Krishna's head has put servitude inside conjugal love; the impersonalist who laments his wasted trance has put conjugal longing
   inside neutrality (NOD 50). Three kinds (NOD 51): uparasa, a false expression (Kamsa's "parental" words); anurasa, imitation (parrots debating
   Vedanta; Narada's wonder is at an imitation); aparasa, a perverted mellow (Jarasandha's proud laughter at Krishna's flight).
6. **The danger is practical, not literary.** NOD 50 records that a person with "a slight devotional attitude of neutral love" who "may immediately
   and artificially try to attain to the platform of conjugal love" is "found to fall from the standard of devotional service." Rasabhasa in the
   heart precedes rasabhasa on the page. This is why trd-13 and trd-39 exist.
7. **Lord Caitanya's rule.** Nothing composed about Him was recited before Him until Svarupa Damodara had checked it, because a hint of overlapping
   mellows against bhakti's principles made the Lord angry (CC Antya 5.95–98). Svarupa Damodara's reasons: such poetry displeases those who know
   (5.102), its author "cannot cross the ocean of the conclusions of devotional service" (5.103), and man-woman poetry from one without transcendental
   knowledge "simply causes unhappiness" (5.107). The standard is Rupa Gosvami's dramas (5.108).
8. **How a correction ends.** The poet was "ashamed, fearful and astonished" (5.129); Svarupa Damodara, "naturally very kindhearted", told him to
   associate with the Lord's devotees (5.130, 5.132); the poet stayed in Puri (5.158). Strict about the text, kind to the person.
9. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *Sincerity makes any devotional song fit for the temple room.* The poet was sincere and praised by all (5.94); the Lord still would not hear it unchecked (5.98).
   - *Anger, fear and disgust cannot be devotion.* They are mellows when Krishna is their cause (NOD 47–48); Kamsa's arena shows them (SB 10.43.17).
   - *Rasabhasa is about Sanskrit poetry, not us.* It is about every kirtana, drama and class; Svarupa Damodara checked songs and verses, not only dramas (5.95).
   - *Checking a junior's composition is pride.* It is the Lord's "regulative principle" (5.98), done "scrutinizingly" and then kindly (NOD 49; CC Antya 5.130–132).
10. **Pitch for TRD.** Teach the seven briskly with one example each, spend the middle of the session on NOD 49–51 with the table on the board, and
    end in Puri with the poet, so the room leaves with an ear and a procedure, not a list.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet the devotees and recall trd-41: five permanent relationships, each a king. Say that today we meet the king's subjects, seven emotions that for a time become mellows, and learn why some mixtures of mellows spoil the taste.
2. Say: "Lord Caitanya made a rule in Puri: no song or poem about Him was recited before Him until Svarupa Damodara had checked it. Today we learn what Svarupa Damodara was listening for."
3. Ask H.2 question 1; take three answers without comment.
4. Ask H.2 question 2; let two devotees answer.
5. Read CC Madhya 19.188 aloud and begin.

**H.2 Opening questions**
1. Think of a bhajana or a drama you have seen in a temple that left the room uneasy, though everyone was sincere. Without naming anyone, what was wrong?
2. Can fear or anger be devotion to Krishna? Give one example from the Bhagavatam if you can.
3. Who checks what is sung and performed in our temple room, and against what standard?

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Kings and subjects.** The five rule the emotions (CC Madhya 23.46 purport); seven of those emotions become mellows "under certain conditions" (19.188). *Transition:* where?
2. **Manifesting and unmanifesting in Gokula.** The seven need Vraja's pastimes; the five are eternal in Vaikuntha (NOD 49). *Transition:* the seven, one by one.
3. **Laughter, astonishment, chivalry.** Krishna is the cause of the laughter (NOD 45); wonder seen or heard; the four viras with Krishna as object (NOD 46). *Transition:* the harder four.
4. **Compassion, anger, dread, ghastliness.** Lamentation in connection with Krishna; anger caused by Krishna; fear of Him or for Him; disgust at one's past on awakening (NOD 47–48). *Transition:* all ten in one arena.
5. **Kamsa's arena.** SB 10.43.17: ten moods toward one Krishna. *Transition:* so what happens when moods mix?
6. **Compatible and incompatible; whole and part.** NOD 49's table and rule; the blade of grass. *Transition:* hear the difference.
7. **Sweet rice and salt.** NOD 50: the impersonalist, the gopi who would smell His head, the artificial leap to conjugal love; against them Nanda Maharaja's vow and Rukmini's discrimination. *Transition:* the three names.
8. **Uparasa, anurasa, aparasa.** NOD 51 with Kamsa, the parrots, Jarasandha. *Transition:* what the Lord did about it.
9. **Svarupa Damodara first.** CC Antya 5.95–98, 102–108; the correction and its kindness (5.129–132, 158). *Transition:* our temple room.

## J. Stories and illustrations

**1. Rohini's anger (NOD 47).** When the two arjuna trees fell with a roar and the neighbourhood ran to the spot, Rohini-devi rebuked Yasoda: "You may
be very expert in giving lessons to your son by binding Him with rope, but don't you look to see if your son is in a dangerous spot? The trees are
falling on the ground, and He is simply loitering there!" Prabhupada calls this "an example of ecstatic love in anger caused by Kṛṣṇa." *Lesson:*
anger is a mellow when its root is love for Krishna's safety; its object is not Krishna but whatever endangers Him.

**2. Kamsa's arena (SB 10.43.17 and purport; BBT translation completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples).** When Krishna entered the wrestling arena with
Balarama, "The wrestlers saw Kṛṣṇa as a lightning bolt, the men of Mathurā as the best of males, the women as Cupid in person, the cowherd men as their
relative, the impious rulers as a chastiser, His parents as their child, the King of the Bhojas as death, the unintelligent as the Supreme Lord's universal
form, the yogīs as the Absolute Truth and the Vṛṣṇis as their supreme worshipable Deity." The purport gives Sridhara Svami's ten moods: fury, wonder,
conjugal attraction, laughter, chivalry, mercy, terror, ghastliness, neutrality, loving devotion. *Lesson:* one Krishna, many hearts, many mellows; in the
opposed hearts the mellows are reflections (Kamsa's terror is aparasa, trd-42's own word for it), in the devotees' hearts they are rasa.

**3. The poet from Bengal (CC Antya 5.91–158).** A brahmana from Bengal wrote a drama about Lord Caitanya and brought it to Puri. Bhagavan Acarya and the
devotees heard it and said, "Very good, very good" (5.94). But the Lord's rule stood: nothing was recited before Him until Svarupa Damodara had heard it,
because a hint of overlapping mellows made the Lord angry (5.95–98). Svarupa Damodara at first declined: so-called poets usually overlap mellows, and "no
one likes to hear such poetry" (5.102); the standard is Rupa Gosvami's dramas (5.108). Pressed for days, he heard the opening verse and asked the poet to
explain it; the explanation treated Lord Jagannatha as a body and Lord Caitanya as its owner, and Svarupa Damodara "began to speak in great anger" (5.116),
telling the poet he had committed an offence and "must be condemned" (5.120). The assembly agreed (5.128); the poet was "ashamed, fearful and astonished"
(5.129). Then Svarupa Damodara, "naturally very kindhearted", advised him: "Associate regularly with the devotees of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, for then only
will you understand the waves of the ocean of devotional service" (5.130, 5.132). The poet fell at the devotees' feet, gave up everything and stayed in Puri
(5.156, 5.158). *Lesson:* the fault found that day was siddhanta-virodha (Mayavada), but the rule that caught it was made for rasabhasa (5.97); the two go
together, and the procedure, check first, correct plainly, then care for the person, is ours.

**Illustration (writer's, not scriptural):** a cook knows that cardamom belongs in sweet rice and chilli does not, though both are good spices. Prabhupada's
own image is stronger: "sweet rice and something salty or sour" (NOD 50). The point it serves: taste in Krishna's service has rules, and the one who knows
them protects everyone's relish.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"If a devotee feels anger or disgust, has he fallen from love?"** Not when Krishna is the cause. Rohini's anger (NOD 47), Yasoda's dread for Krishna
   (NOD 48) and the awakened devotee's disgust at his past (NOD 48) are mellows. Anger at a godbrother for one's own sake is not.
2. **"Why should a sincere bhajana be 'checked'? Is that not pride?"** It is Lord Caitanya's own "regulative principle" (CC Antya 5.98). Sincerity was not
   in question with the Bengali poet; the text was. The check is done "scrutinizingly" (NOD 49) and the person is cared for (5.130–132).
3. **"Who in our temple is qualified to judge rasabhasa?"** One who knows the twelve rasas and their mixtures from Prabhupada's books and has heard the
   Caitanya-caritamrta from senior devotees (5.132). Where no one is sure, the standard is Rupa Gosvami's own compositions (5.108) and the songs of the
   acharyas; the doubtful piece waits.
4. **"Is a drama in which Krishna and the gopis are played romantically only 'a little' rasabhasa?"** Svarupa Damodara says poetry about man and woman from
   one without transcendental knowledge "simply causes unhappiness" (5.107); NOD 50 says the artificial leap to conjugal love ends in falling. It is not little.
5. **"Can the seven indirect rasas be a devotee's permanent relationship?"** No. They appear "under certain conditions" (CC Madhya 19.188) and are
   "eternally manifesting and unmanifesting" (NOD 49); the whole is always one of the five (NOD 49).
6. **"Is Kamsa's fear of Krishna devotion, since Krishna is its object?"** The Bhagavatam records it as a way of seeing Krishna (SB 10.43.17); NOD 51 calls
   the enemy's mellows perverted reflections (aparasa, as with Jarasandha). Krishna is the object, but love is absent, so it is rasabhasa, not rasa.

## L. Case studies

**Case 1 (Invented case for discussion).** For a youth festival, a brahmacari writes a skit in which Krishna, as a child, is scolded by Yasoda while the
cowherd boys fight demons beside Him and a gopi sings a love song in the same scene "to show all the rasas". *Dilemma:* rich or spoiled? *Principles:*
NOD 49 (parental love with conjugal love or chivalry is incompatible; fraternity with chivalry and laughter is compatible); whole and part; CC Antya 5.97.
*Questions:* Which mellow is the whole of this scene? Which parts contradict it? *Analysis:* the scene has no whole; parental love is contradicted by
the conjugal song, and the fighting belongs to the boys' scene, not to Yasoda's. Keep Yasoda's scene with laughter and dread (compatible); give the
boys their own scene. *Takeaway:* "all the rasas" in one frame is rasabhasa, not fullness.

**Case 2 (Invented case for discussion).** A new devotee writes a bhajana in which he, the singer, addresses Krishna as his beloved and in the next line
as his master, and asks the kirtana leader to use it tonight. The leader does not want to hurt him. *Dilemma:* sing it, refuse it, or what? *Principles:*
NOD 50 (servitude inside conjugal love is incompatible; the artificial leap to conjugal love ends in falling); CC Antya 5.95–98 (check first); 5.130–132
(kindness after). *Questions:* What did Svarupa Damodara do when praised poetry came to him? How did he treat the poet afterwards? *Analysis:* the song
is not sung tonight; the leader explains the mixture plainly from NOD 50, and then gives the devotee the acharyas' songs to learn and the devotees'
association (5.132). *Takeaway:* strict about the text, kind to the person, every time.

6. What was Lord Caitanya's rule in Puri, and why? (nothing was recited before Him until Svarupa Damodara had heard it, because a hint of overlapping mellows against bhakti made Him angry: CC Antya 5.95–98)

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Beside the five permanent relationships with Krishna (trd-41), Rupa Gosvami describes "seven kinds of indirect ecstasies of devotional service,
known as laughing, astonishment, chivalry, compassion, anger, dread and ghastliness" (NOD 45). Lord Caitanya told Rupa Gosvami that the five direct
mellows are permanently in the devotee's heart, "whereas the seven indirect emotions appear suddenly under certain conditions and appear more
powerful" (CC Madhya 19.188). Each of the seven has Krishna as its cause: devotees laugh at Krishna's play, wonder at His acts, fight, give or
perform rites for Him, lament when He seems in danger, grow angry at whatever threatens Him, fear for Him, and feel disgust at their own past when
Krishna consciousness awakens (NOD 45–48). In Kamsa's arena one Krishna was seen ten ways: as a lightning bolt by the wrestlers, as Cupid by the
women, as a child by His parents, as death by Kamsa (SB 10.43.17).

Because every mellow is exact, mixtures matter. Prabhupada gives "a scientific analysis of the compatibility and incompatibility of the mixtures of
these various rasas" (NOD 49). In any mixture "the prominent feeling is called the whole, and the subordinate feeling is called the part" (NOD 49).
Laughter, compassion and dread sit well inside parental love; conjugal love, chivalry and anger do not. A bad mixture is like "sweet rice and
something salty or sour" (NOD 50) and is called rasabhasa, "a perverted presentation of mellows" (NOD 45). It has three kinds: "uparasa (false
expression), anurasa (imitation) and aparasa (perverted or misrepresented mellows)" (NOD 51). NOD 50 warns that one who artificially leaps from a
slight neutral attitude to conjugal love "is found to fall from the standard of devotional service."

Lord Caitanya took this so seriously that in Puri "anyone who composed a song, verse, literary composition or poem about Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu
first had to bring it to Svarūpa Dāmodara Gosvāmī to be heard" (CC Antya 5.95), because a hint of mellows overlapping against bhakti's principles
made the Lord angry (5.97); "The Lord made this etiquette a regulative principle" (5.98). When a poet from Bengal brought a praised drama, Svarupa
Damodara found a fault, corrected it in strong words, and then kindly told the poet, "Associate regularly with the devotees of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu,
for then only will you understand the waves of the ocean of devotional service" (5.132). The poet stayed with the devotees (5.158). Our temple
rooms keep the same check and the same kindness.

## S2. Key ideas
- Seven secondary mellows, occasional and strong, each with Krishna as cause (NOD 45–48; CC Madhya 19.188).
- The prominent mellow is the whole, the subordinate the part; compatible parts merge back, incompatible parts spoil (NOD 49).
- Rasabhasa has three kinds: uparasa, anurasa, aparasa (NOD 51).
- An artificial leap to conjugal love ends in falling (NOD 50).
- Lord Caitanya's rule: Svarupa Damodara heard everything first; strict about the text, kind to the person (CC Antya 5.95–98, 130–132).

## S3. Verses to remember
- **CC Madhya 19.188:** the seven indirect emotions "appear suddenly under certain conditions and appear more powerful." For me: my strong feelings are not my relationship; the whole is.
- **CC Antya 5.98:** "The Lord made this etiquette a regulative principle." For me: what I sing and stage is checked first.
- **CC Antya 5.132:** "Associate regularly with the devotees of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu". For me: the ear for rasa is learned in the devotees' company.

## S4. Words to know
- **Secondary (indirect) rasas:** laughter, astonishment, chivalry, compassion, anger, dread, ghastliness (NOD 45).
- **Whole and part:** the prominent and the subordinate mellow in a mixture (NOD 49).
- **Rasabhasa:** an incompatible mixture of mellows; a perverted presentation (NOD 45, 51).
- **Uparasa, anurasa, aparasa:** false expression, imitation, perverted mellow (NOD 51).
- **Vira:** chivalry; yuddha-, dana-, daya- and dharma-vira are its four kinds (NOD 46).

## S5. Questions and answers
1. **Can anger or fear be devotion?** Yes, when Krishna is the cause: Rohini's anger when the trees fell (NOD 47), Yasoda's fear for Krishna (NOD 48). Anger for my own sake is not.
2. **Why should a sincere song be checked?** Because Lord Caitanya made it a rule (CC Antya 5.98); the poet from Bengal was sincere and praised, and his drama still had a fault (5.94, 5.116–120).
3. **What makes a mixture incompatible?** A part that contradicts the whole, like salt in sweet rice: servitude inside conjugal love, conjugal love inside parental love (NOD 49–50).
4. **Is a romantic Krishna drama only a small problem?** No. Poetry about man and woman from one without transcendental knowledge "simply causes unhappiness" (CC Antya 5.107), and the artificial leap to conjugal love ends in falling (NOD 50).
5. **How should a correction be given?** As Svarupa Damodara gave it: plainly about the fault, then kindly about the person, pointing him to the devotees' association (CC Antya 5.116–132).
