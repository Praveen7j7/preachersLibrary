# Our life on two wheels

> **VL1 · 45–60 min · review: sources checked, awaiting senior review.** HG Sundar Gopal Prabhu's seminar deck gives the image: life is a chariot on
> two wheels, material knowledge and spiritual knowledge; most of us ride on one. The deck's four themes are kept
> (understanding life; are we achieving our goals; activating the second wheel; curing the root of problems). Its
> long run of statistics (alcohol, tobacco, prisons, pornography, suicide) and its slides naming public figures
> who died by suicide are **not used**: the figures are not checked, and the suicide material can harm listeners
> (K). Marked sensitive (it touches suicide; §2.5): read the note in K before teaching.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

The image does the work. Everyone in the room has spent fifteen to twenty years on one wheel, and many feel the
wobble: anxiety, meaninglessness, the sense that success is not delivering. The talk does not ask them to doubt
their education; it asks them to notice what it never covered. At VL1 the aim is **attraction through a missing
piece**, not an argument against the piece they have.

The deck's pairings (lifestyle versus life; quantity versus quality; going through life versus growing through
life; information versus transformation) are IYF's own and are kept. Its verse is BG 6.6.

Builds on vl1-05 (the gap between having and feeling fulfilled; BG 3.37 and 16.21 as the gates of trouble) and vl1-22 (BG 13.8–12 as the hierarchy and "SQ"): here BG 3.37 and 16.21 get one sentence as the root, and BG 13.8–12 is used only as the second wheel's curriculum, not re-argued.

**Not done here:**
- Claiming that education, science or careers are bad. The first wheel is necessary (SB 1.2.10 asks for "a healthy
  life").
- Alarming the room with statistics.
- Any discussion of suicide beyond the pastoral note in K.

## C. Central message

We have spent years fitting one wheel, how to make a living, and almost none on the other, how to live. A life on
one wheel wobbles however fast it goes. The second wheel is spiritual knowledge: who I am, what this world is,
what lasts. It can be fitted at any age, and a small start is not lost.

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. Explain the two-wheel image and name the deck's five contrasts (lifestyle/life, quantity/quality, go/grow,
   information/transformation, material/spiritual knowledge).
2. Say what the Gita counts as knowledge (five items from BG 13.8–12) and why none of it is in a syllabus.
3. Describe the one-wheel mindset in the Gita's words (BG 16.13–15) without mocking anyone.
4. Name the root the Gita gives for the problems we blame on circumstances (BG 3.37, 16.21).
5. Choose one small way to start the second wheel this week (BG 2.40).

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** BG 6.6, SB 1.2.10, BG 13.8–12, BG 16.13–15, BG 3.37 and SB 1.2.17–18 carry the talk. The rest prepare you
for questions.

**BG 6.6**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/6/
> "For him who has conquered the mind, the mind is the best of friends; but for one who has failed to do so, his mind will remain the greatest enemy."
- **Context:** Chapter 6, the opening on the mind; the deck's chosen verse.
- **Purport point:** "As long as one's mind remains an unconquered enemy, one has to serve the dictations of lust,
  anger, avarice, illusion, etc."
- **Application (preacher's):** The deck ends on this verse because the second wheel is, in practice, a trained
  mind. Twenty years of education train the intellect; nothing trains the mind.
- **Use:** in the talk (the deck's close).

**SB 1.2.10**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/10/
> "Life's desires should never be directed toward sense gratification. One should desire only a healthy life, or self-preservation, since a human being is meant for inquiry about the Absolute Truth. Nothing else should be the goal of one's works."
- **Teaching:** Two things are named: a healthy life, or self-preservation, and inquiry about the Absolute Truth,
  which is what a human being is meant for.
- **Purport point:** "no one is serious about the problems of life"; "One should satisfy the senses only insomuch
  as required for self-preservation."
- **Application (preacher's):** The verse is the two wheels in one sentence. It does not abolish the first wheel; it
  sizes it.
- **Use:** in the talk (the definition).

**BG 16.13–15**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/16/13-15/
> "The demoniac person thinks: “So much wealth do I have today, and I will gain more according to my schemes. So much is mine now, and it will increase in the future, more and more… I am the lord of everything. I am the enjoyer. I am perfect, powerful and happy… thus I shall rejoice.” In this way, such persons are deluded by ignorance."
- **Context:** Chapter 16, the description of the ungodly nature.
- **Teaching:** A mind organised entirely around acquisition and self-enjoyment is called deluded.
- **Application (preacher's):** This is the one-wheel mindset in its own voice. Read it aloud as an inner
  monologue, not as an accusation; most of us recognise a milder version. Avoid the word "demoniac" at VL1; say
  "the Gita calls this deluded".
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 5.22**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/5/22/
> "An intelligent person does not take part in the sources of misery, which are due to contact with the material senses. O son of Kuntī, such pleasures have a beginning and an end, and so the wise man does not delight in them."
- **Purport point:** "The more one is addicted to material pleasures, the more he is entrapped by material
  miseries."
- **Application (preacher's):** The deck's "quantity versus quality" rests here: one-wheel pleasures have a start
  and a stop. Not forbidden; just not enough.
- **Use:** in the talk, one line.

**BG 13.8–12**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/13/8-12/
> "Humility; pridelessness; nonviolence; tolerance; simplicity; approaching a bona fide spiritual master; cleanliness; steadiness; self-control…; the perception of the evil of birth, death, old age and disease; detachment…; even-mindedness amid pleasant and unpleasant events…; accepting the importance of self-realization; and philosophical search for the Absolute Truth – all these I declare to be knowledge, and besides this whatever there may be is ignorance."
- **Teaching:** The Gita's definition of knowledge is a list of qualities and perceptions, not facts.
- **Purport point:** "Humility means that one should not be anxious to have the satisfaction of being honored by
  others." "Tolerance means that one should be practiced to bear insult and dishonor from others." The list is given
  as a test: "one should have a test to see how far he is progressing. He can judge by these items."
- **Application (preacher's):** Here is the second wheel's syllabus. Read five items and ask which course taught
  them. (VL1-22 uses the same verses as "SQ"; here they define the wheel.)
- **Use:** in the talk (the content of the second wheel).

**BG 4.38**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/4/38/
> "In this world, there is nothing so sublime and pure as transcendental knowledge. Such knowledge is the mature fruit of all mysticism. And one who has become accomplished in the practice of devotional service enjoys this knowledge within himself in due course of time."
- **Purport point:** "Ignorance is the cause of our bondage, and knowledge is the cause of our liberation."
- **Application (preacher's):** The Gita ranks this knowledge above everything else in the world. Say it as the
  text's claim and invite the room to test it, not to accept it.
- **Use:** in the talk, one line.

**BG 3.37**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/37/
> "It is lust only, Arjuna, which is born of contact with the material mode of passion and later transformed into wrath, and which is the all-devouring sinful enemy of this world."
- **Context:** Arjuna has asked (3.36) what forces a person to act wrongly against his will.
- **Purport point:** Love of God "becomes transformed into lust, as milk in contact with sour tamarind is
  transformed into yogurt"; "when lust is unsatisfied, it turns into wrath".
- **Application (preacher's):** The deck's "infected hard drive" has a scriptural name. Lust here means
  self-centred desire in general, not only sexual desire; say so.
- **Use:** in the talk (the root).

**BG 16.21**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/16/21/
> "There are three gates leading to this hell – lust, anger and greed. Every sane man should give these up, for they lead to the degradation of the soul."
- **Purport point:** "One tries to satisfy his lust, and when he cannot, anger and greed arise."
- **Application (preacher's):** The deck lists six "viruses" (lust, greed, anger, envy, pride, illusion), a
  traditional list of six enemies; the Gita names three gates here, and the BG 6.6 purport adds illusion. At VL1
  soften "hell" to "a hellish state", which is also what the person feels.
- **Use:** in the talk, one line.

**SB 1.2.17**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/17/
> "Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the Personality of Godhead, who is the Paramātmā [Supersoul] in everyone's heart and the benefactor of the truthful devotee, cleanses desire for material enjoyment from the heart of the devotee who has developed the urge to hear His messages…"
- **Teaching:** Krsna, as Paramatma, cleanses the desire for material enjoyment from the heart of the devotee who wants to
  hear His messages.
- **Purport point:** "it takes time to recover from this foreign nature. But if one is engaged in hearing the glories
  of the Lord, gradually he realizes his real position."
- **Application (preacher's):** The deck's "antivirus" has a text. Hearing is the first turn of the second wheel.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 1.2.18**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/18/
> "By regular attendance in classes on the Bhāgavatam and by rendering of service to the pure devotee, all that is troublesome to the heart is almost completely destroyed…"
- **Purport point:** Two "Bhāgavatas", the book and the devotee; "both of them or either of them can be good enough
  to eliminate the obstacles."
- **Application (preacher's):** The deck's ABC formula (association, books, chanting) rests on this verse and the
  one before it. "Regular attendance in classes" describes the Sunday programme itself.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 2.40**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/40/
> "In this endeavor there is no loss or diminution, and a little advancement on this path can protect one from the most dangerous type of fear."
- **Purport point:** "Even a small beginning of such activity finds no impediment, nor can that small beginning be
  lost at any stage."
- **Application (preacher's):** The second wheel can be fitted at any age and with a small turn. This answers the
  objection "it's too late / too big for me".
- **Use:** in the talk (the close).

**BG 3.16**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/16/
> "My dear Arjuna, one who does not follow in human life the cycle of sacrifice thus established by the Vedas certainly leads a life full of sin. Living only for the satisfaction of the senses, such a person lives in vain."
- **Purport point:** "The mammonist philosophy of 'work very hard and enjoy sense gratification' is condemned
  herein."
- **Use:** background. The verse's own word is *cakra*, wheel; the "cycle of sacrifice" is the Gita's own wheel
  image. Mention only if asked; the full teaching on yajna is VL2-05.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** A human being has two needs that the Bhagavatam names in one verse: a healthy life, and
   inquiry about the Absolute Truth (SB 1.2.10). Education serves the first; nothing in it serves the second. The
   Gita defines the missing knowledge not as information but as qualities and perceptions, humility, tolerance,
   the perception of the evil of birth, death, old age and disease, even-mindedness (BG 13.8–12), and ranks it above
   everything else (BG 4.38).
2. **Why it matters.** A life on one wheel organises itself around acquisition (BG 16.13–15) and pleasures that
   begin and end (BG 5.22). It can be very fast and very successful and still wobble. The wobble is what this
   audience feels and cannot name.
3. **Connections.** To the necessity of spirituality (VL1-05), to the hierarchy and "SQ" (VL1-22), to the art of
   living (VL1-13), and later to hearing and chanting as the daily turning of the wheel (VL3).
4. **Consequences.** A person who fits the second wheel does not abandon the first; the Bhagavatam asks for a
   healthy life. But the first wheel stops being the purpose and becomes the vehicle. Information becomes
   transformation (the deck's phrase) because the knowledge in BG 13.8–12 is measured by character, not marks.
5. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - **"The first wheel is bad."** SB 1.2.10 asks for health and self-preservation, and the Gita's teaching that
     one cannot maintain the body without work is taught in VL1-29. The talk sizes the first wheel; it does not
     remove it.
   - **"The second wheel is more information."** BG 13.8–12 lists qualities; "information versus transformation" is
     the deck's correct reading.
   - **"The root of the world's problems is other people."** The Gita places the root in desire (BG 3.37) and its
     gates (BG 16.21), in everyone. No group is blamed.
   - **"It's too late for me."** BG 2.40: a small beginning is never lost.
6. **Pitch for VL1.** Words: "two wheels", "wobble", "how to live", "root". No Sanskrit needed; *kama* (desire)
   once with BG 3.37 if you like. The deck's computer analogy (virus, antivirus, installation manual) is fine for the
   room; label it as an analogy.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet, give your name, and say the talk is about something school never covered, not a criticism of school.
2. Ask H.2 question 1; let the room add up its years of education.
3. Ask H.2 question 2 and wait through the silence.
4. Thank the room; say you will return to the answers at minute 20.
5. Give the transition line below and draw the chariot.

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the room's own life)
1. How many years have you spent learning how to make a living?
2. How many hours in those years were about how to handle a loss, an insult or your own mind?
3. Who taught you the most about living, and what did they do?

- **Opening question:** "How many years have you spent in school and college? Add them up." Pause. "In those years,
  how many hours were about how to *live*: how to handle a loss, an insult, a craving, your own mind?" Silence.
  "That is the second wheel."
- **Relatable scenario (invented; adapt to a skilled tradesperson, a farmer's child who moved to a city, or a senior executive):** Neha is a topper. First-class degree, good job, good salary. At 26 she has
  everything she planned and lies awake wondering why it feels like nothing. She can solve a differential equation
  and cannot sit alone for ten minutes.
- **Transition:** "The deck we use for this topic has an image: a chariot on two wheels. Material knowledge is one.
  Spiritual knowledge is the other. Most of us have been given a very good single wheel."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** The wobble arrives around placements, first jobs and first failures. Knowing
  its cause before it arrives is the gift of this talk.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Two needs, one verse.** A healthy life, for inquiry about the Absolute Truth (SB 1.2.10). *Example:* Neha has
   the first and never heard of the second. *Transition:* what does one wheel sound like from inside?
2. **The one-wheel monologue.** "So much is mine now, and it will increase… I am the enjoyer" (BG 16.13–15).
   Pleasures that begin and end (BG 5.22). The galloping horse that cannot eat grass (traditional saying, from the
   deck). *Example:* read the verse as an inner voice; most will recognise a quiet version. *Transition:* so what is
   the second wheel made of?
3. **Knowledge is a list of qualities.** Humility, tolerance, simplicity, steadiness, self-control, the perception
   of the evil of birth, death, old age and disease, even-mindedness, the search for the Absolute (BG 13.8–12). The
   Gita ranks it first (BG 4.38). *Example:* which course examines tolerance? *Transition:* and what problem does
   it solve?
4. **The root is inside, in everyone.** Desire that turns to anger (BG 3.37); three gates (BG 16.21); the mind as
   enemy (BG 6.6 purport). The deck's infected hard drive. *Example:* corruption, quarrels and addictions share one
   root. *Transition:* what is the antivirus?
5. **Hearing and association cleanse.** The Lord cleanses the heart of one who hears (SB 1.2.17); regular classes and
   good company remove what troubles the heart (SB 1.2.18). The ABC formula. *Example:* the programme they are
   sitting in is SB 1.2.18 in practice. *Transition:* how big must the start be?
6. **A small turn is never lost.** BG 2.40. *Transition:* to the takeaway.

## J. Stories and illustrations

- **Milk and tamarind** (BG 3.37 purport). Love of God turns to lust "as milk in contact with sour tamarind is
  transformed into yogurt". *Connection:* the root is a corruption of something good, not an alien thing.
- **Narada's previous life** (SB 1.2.18 purport). The son of a maidservant, by associating with sages and taking
  their remnants, became Narada. Keep to the purport's words. *Connection:* association turns the wheel.
- **The galloping horse** (deck): a horse at full gallop cannot bend to eat. A traditional image; label it as such.
  *Connection:* speed on one wheel starves the rider.
- **Contemporary (invented):** Neha at 26 (H); the "How to Live" syllabus exercise (G).

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

> **Before you teach (pastoral guidance):** the deck's slides on suicide are not used. If the subject arises, speak briefly and
> carefully: thoughts of ending one's life are a medical emergency, not a philosophical position; help works;
> professional help is compatible with spiritual life; and
> **Tele-MANAS, 14416, is a free 24-hour national mental-health helpline**. Offer to talk afterwards (outside India, name your local crisis line or emergency number). Never list
> names of people who died, and never present suicide as evidence for a thesis.

1. **"Are you saying my degree was a waste?"**
   - No. SB 1.2.10 asks for "a healthy life"; the first wheel is needed. The talk says only that the second was never
     fitted. Both wheels, one chariot.
2. **"Isn't spiritual knowledge just more theory?"**
   - The Gita's list (BG 13.8–12) contains almost no theory: humility, tolerance, steadiness, even-mindedness. It is
     tested in character, which is why the deck says "transformation, not information".
3. **"Successful people seem fine without the second wheel."**
   - Some are, for a time; BG 5.22 says such pleasures "have a beginning and an end". The checked study in O found
     that valuing possessions and status is associated with lower well-being across many samples. Don't name
     individuals; speak of the pattern.
4. **"Why blame lust for everything? Isn't that simplistic?"**
   - BG 3.37 uses "lust" for self-centred desire in general, which turns to anger when blocked (16.21 purport). The
     claim is not that every problem is sexual but that acquisition-and-enjoyment, frustrated, drives quarrel, greed
     and addiction. Test it against one problem you know well.
5. **"How is chanting an 'antivirus'? Sounds like a slogan."**
   - It is the deck's analogy; the texts behind it are SB 1.2.17–18: hearing the Lord's messages cleanses the heart's
     desire, and regular classes plus good company remove what troubles the heart. Offer the ten-minute meditation as
     a test, not a creed.
6. **"I'm 20. Isn't this for later?"**
   - BG 2.40: a small beginning is never lost. The second wheel is easiest to fit before the chariot is heavy.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The topper.** Neha (H) tells a friend she feels "like a very well-built car with no destination". The
friend says she should get a hobby.
- **Dilemma:** Is a hobby the second wheel?
- **Principles:** SB 1.2.10 (inquiry as the purpose); BG 13.8–12 (what knowledge is); BG 5.22 (pleasures that begin
  and end).
- **Questions:**
  - What is Neha missing that a hobby would not supply?
  - Which three items from BG 13.8–12 would change her evenings?
  - What is the smallest first turn of the second wheel for her?
- **Suggested analysis:** A hobby is more first wheel. The second wheel begins with the question SB 1.2.10 names, and
  with one of the two "Bhāgavatas" (a book, or a person who lives it, SB 1.2.18 purport).
- **Takeaway:** Destination before speed.

**Case 2: The root.** Three friends argue about why their city has so much corruption. One blames politicians, one
blames poverty, one blames culture.
- **Principles:** BG 3.37 (desire as the root); BG 16.21 (three gates); BG 16.13–15 (the acquisitive monologue).
- **Questions:**
  - What do the three explanations have in common? What do they leave out?
  - Where does the Gita locate the root, and who is exempt?
  - What follows for the three friends personally?
- **Takeaway:** The root is in every heart, including mine; so is the remedy.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first; praise every try)
1. What are the two wheels? *Material knowledge (how to make a living) and spiritual knowledge (how to live).*
2. What two things does SB 1.2.10 ask us to desire? *A healthy life, and inquiry about the Absolute Truth.*
3. Name two items the Gita calls knowledge (BG 13.8-12). *E.g. humility, tolerance, simplicity, steadiness.*
4. What does BG 5.22 say about sense pleasures? *They have a beginning and an end.*
5. Why start small (BG 2.40)? *A little progress on this path is not lost.*

**M.2 Activity (about 8 minutes): "Syllabus for How to Live"**
1. Groups of three, 4 min: write five topics for a course called *How to Live*.
2. 2 min: compare with BG 13.8–12 on the screen; mark matches.
3. 2 min: volunteers read one topic; thank each. No private stories needed.

**M.3 Reflection**
- **Reflection:** Which of the five contrasts (lifestyle/life, quantity/quality, go/grow, information/transformation,
  material/spiritual) describes your last year best?
- **Application (private, optional):** Write a five-topic syllabus for "How to Live". Compare it with BG 13.8–12.
  *(Nobody needs to share.)*

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

We spend fifteen to twenty years learning how to make a living and almost no hours learning how to live. Life is like a chariot on two wheels: material knowledge and spiritual knowledge. Most of us ride on one, and however fast it goes, it wobbles. The Bhagavatam names both needs in one verse: "a healthy life", and "inquiry about the Absolute Truth" (SB 1.2.10).

The Gita describes the one-wheel mind from inside ("So much is mine now, and it will increase…", BG 16.13–15) and says such pleasures "have a beginning and an end" (BG 5.22). The second wheel is not more information; it is a list of qualities the Gita calls knowledge: humility, tolerance, simplicity, steadiness, seeing the problems of birth, death, old age and disease, and even-mindedness (BG 13.8–12). Facts alone do not supply these.

The Gita names the root of our problems as self-centred desire, which turns into anger (BG 3.37). Hearing spiritual knowledge and good company clean it (SB 1.2.17–18). You do not need to change your life overnight: a small start is never lost (BG 2.40).

## S2. Key ideas

- Two wheels: how to make a living, and how to live. Both are needed (SB 1.2.10); only one was taught.
- The one-wheel mind: "so much is mine, and it will increase" (BG 16.13–15). Its pleasures begin and end (BG 5.22).
- The second wheel is character, not facts (BG 13.8–12). Transformation, not information.
- The root of our problems is inside, in everyone: desire that turns to anger (BG 3.37; 16.21).
- The cleaning method is hearing and association (SB 1.2.17–18): the ABC formula.
- Start small; nothing is lost (BG 2.40).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **SB 1.2.10:** "One should desire only a healthy life, or self-preservation, since a human being is meant for
  inquiry about the Absolute Truth." *For me:* keep the first wheel; fit the second.
- **BG 13.8–12:** "Humility; pridelessness; nonviolence; tolerance; simplicity… all these I declare to be
  knowledge." *For me:* a syllabus I was never given.
- **BG 2.40:** "…a little advancement on this path can protect one from the most dangerous type of fear." *For me:*
  I can start with fifteen minutes.

## S4. Words to know

- **Two wheels:** material knowledge (how to make a living) and spiritual knowledge (how to live); the image is from
  IYF's seminar.
- **Knowledge (jnana), in the Gita's sense:** qualities and perceptions such as humility and tolerance (BG 13.8–12),
  not information.
- **ABC:** Association, Books, Chanting: the practices behind SB 1.2.17–18.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Is my degree a waste?**
   No. The Bhagavatam asks for "a healthy life" (SB 1.2.10). The first wheel is needed; only the second is missing.
2. **Isn't spiritual knowledge just more theory?**
   The Gita's list is almost all character: humility, tolerance, steadiness (BG 13.8–12). It is tested in how you
   live.
3. **Successful people seem fine on one wheel.**
   For a time; such pleasures "have a beginning and an end" (BG 5.22). Research across many samples finds that
   valuing possessions and status above other things goes with lower well-being. It's a pattern, not a verdict on
   anyone.
4. **Why is chanting called an "antivirus"?**
   It's an analogy. The texts behind it say hearing the Lord's messages cleanses the heart (SB 1.2.17) and regular
   classes with good company remove what troubles it (SB 1.2.18). Try it and see.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. SB 1.2.10 asks us to desire: (a) wealth and health, (b) a healthy life, for inquiry about the Absolute Truth,
   (c) nothing at all.
2. True or false: BG 13.8–12 defines knowledge mainly as facts about the universe.
3. Which verse gives the one-wheel mind's inner voice ("So much is mine now, and it will increase…")?
4. What does the Gita name as the root that turns into anger (BG 3.37)?
5. In your own words: why can a fast, successful life still wobble?

**Answers:** 1. (b) (SB 1.2.10). 2. False: humility, tolerance, simplicity, steadiness and similar qualities
(BG 13.8–12). 3. BG 16.13–15. 4. Lust, self-centred desire (BG 3.37). 5. Because it runs on one wheel: pleasures that
begin and end (BG 5.22) with no answer to who I am or what lasts (SB 1.2.10).
