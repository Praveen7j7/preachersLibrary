# 22. Engagement

> Status line: BSS (brahmacari training) · 120 min · drafted · not sensitive. Based on chapter 22 of BIKC. Builds on bss-44 and bss-45 (the practice and its regulation) and on bss-24 (sense control; BG 2.67 is used here for a different point: engaging all the senses). "Engagement" here means *service assigned to a devotee*, not a marriage engagement, though BIKC makes that contrast.

## A. Topic overview
| | |
|---|---|
| Batch / level | BSS, brahmacari training |
| Suggested duration | 120 minutes (lecture, with an "if time allows" extension) |
| Preparation time | About 2 hours: read SB 9.4.18-28 and 9.4.68, BG 3.5 and 18.45-47 with purports, then this page |
| Difficulty | Intermediate |
| Prerequisites | None |
| Audience | Young men living or preparing to live as brahmacaris (celibate students of spiritual life), and anyone who serves in a spiritual community or workplace |
| Teaching objective | The audience understands why a soul must always be engaged, how a devotee accepts any service for Krishna, how service is matched to a person's nature without pride or complaint, and why all the senses should be engaged in Krishna's service |

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level
IYF's BSS KRA is "Giving more time for service and maintaining high standard of sadhana" (research/batch-criteria.md). A brahmacari who is not absorbed in a devotional engagement will, BIKC warns, soon find another engagement that keeps him busy for years, namely a marriage engagement. Service is therefore not an extra to the celibate life; it is what the energy is for.

## C. Central message
The soul cannot stop acting (BG 3.5); the only question is whether the action serves illusion or Krishna. A brahmacari is trained to accept any service gladly, and best of all to engage every sense in the Lord's service, as Maharaja Ambarisa did (SB 9.4.18-20).

## D. Learning outcomes
1. Explain from BG 3.5 and its purport why idleness is not an option for the soul.
2. Recount how Ambarisa engaged each sense and what the Lord gave him (SB 9.4.18-28).
3. Explain, from BG 18.46, how an ordinary occupation becomes worship.
4. State BIKC's three cautions: do not feel superior to those in humbler service; do not choose business or dealings with women as a brahmacari's engagement; do not be idle.
5. Name one service and one way to do it with all the senses.

## E. Scriptural foundation
Use six verses in the talk (BG 3.5, BG 18.46, SB 9.4.18-20, SB 9.4.28, SB 7.12.5, CC Madhya 22.113). "Verified" means the verse exists (research/verification/bss-46.json) and I read the translation and the purport I use.

**BG 3.5**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/5/
> "no one can refrain from doing something, not even for a moment."
- **Purport point (read in full):** It is "the nature of the soul to be always active". The body is "a dead vehicle" moved by the soul; so "the spirit soul has to be engaged in the good work of Kṛṣṇa consciousness, otherwise it will be engaged in occupations dictated by the illusory energy." If the soul is engaged in Krishna consciousness, "whatever he is able to do is good for him" (the purport quotes SB 1.5.17).
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 18.45-46**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/45/ and /46/
> "By worship of the Lord, who is the source of all beings and who is all-pervading, a man can attain perfection through performing his own work." (18.46)
- **Purport point (18.46, read in full):** "Everyone should think that he is engaged in a particular type of occupation by Hṛṣīkeśa, the master of the senses. And by the result of the work in which one is engaged, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Śrī Kṛṣṇa, should be worshiped." "In whatever occupation one may be engaged, if he serves the Supreme Lord he will achieve the highest perfection." (18.45 has no purport on vedabase.)
- **Use:** in the talk (service by one's nature).

**SB 9.4.18-20**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/9/4/18-20/
> "Mahārāja Ambarīṣa always engaged his mind in meditating upon the lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa, his words in describing the glories of the Lord, his hands in cleansing the Lord's temple, and his ears in hearing the words spoken by Kṛṣṇa or about Kṛṣṇa."
- **Teaching (the rest of the translation):** eyes on the Deity and the holy places, touch on the bodies of the Lord's devotees, smell on tulasi offered to the Lord, tongue on the Lord's *prasada* (food offered to Him), legs walking to temples, head bowing, "all his desires in serving the Lord, twenty-four hours a day". "This is the way to increase attachment for the Lord and be completely free from all material desires."
- **Purport point (read in full):** the first business of a devotee is to accept a bona fide spiritual master who can train him to engage the senses in service; "*hṛṣīkeṇa hṛṣīkeśa-sevanaṁ bhaktir ucyate*: bhakti means to engage the senses in the service of the master of the senses, Kṛṣṇa".
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 9.4.28**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/9/4/28/
> "Being very pleased by the unalloyed devotion of Mahārāja Ambarīṣa, the Supreme Personality of Godhead gave the King His disc, which is fearful to enemies and which always protects the devotee from enemies and adversities."
- **Purport point (what I read):** a devotee "may not be expert in self-defense", but because he depends on the Lord's lotus feet "he is always sure of protection".
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 9.4.68**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/9/4/68/
> "The pure devotee is always within the core of My heart, and I am always in the heart of the pure devotee."
- **Purport point (what I read):** Durvasa's wish to punish Ambarisa was a wish to give pain to the Lord's heart; offences at a devotee's feet are serious.
- **Use:** extension (the Lord's response to service).

**BG 2.67**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/67/
> "even one of the roaming senses on which the mind focuses can carry away a man's intelligence."
- **Purport point (read in full):** "Unless all of the senses are engaged in the service of the Lord, even one of them engaged in sense gratification can deviate the devotee"; and "as mentioned in the life of Mahārāja Ambarīṣa, all of the senses must be engaged in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, for that is the correct technique for controlling the mind."
- **Use:** in the talk, linking Ambarisa to sense control.

**SB 7.12.5**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/12/5/ (translation; no purport on vedabase)
> "The brahmacārī should go out morning and evening to collect alms, and he should offer all that he collects to the spiritual master."
- **Use:** in the talk (the traditional service of begging for the guru).

**SB 7.12.6**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/12/6/
> "He must always be active and expert, fully believing in the instructions of the spiritual master and the śāstra."
- **Purport point:** A brahmacari should be very careful not to mix with women or with men addicted to women; on begging rounds the talk should be only about alms.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**CC Madhya 22.113**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/22/113/
> "All the rules and prohibitions mentioned in the śāstras should be the servants of these two principles."
- **Purport point:** "One may sell books or enlist life members or render some other service, but these duties are not ordinary duties. These duties serve as an impetus for remembering Kṛṣṇa." Going out with a saṅkīrtana party or selling books, "he naturally remembers that he is going to sell Kṛṣṇa's books."
- **Use:** in the talk (service as remembrance).

**BG 9.27**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/9/27/
> "Whatever you do, whatever you eat, whatever you offer or give away, and whatever austerities you perform – do that, O son of Kuntī, as an offering to Me."
- **Purport point:** "It is the duty of everyone to mold his life in such a way that he will not forget Kṛṣṇa in any circumstance"; if anyone meditates on Krishna by chanting on beads twenty-four hours a day, "he is surely the greatest meditator and the greatest yogī".
- **Use:** extension.

**BG 18.47, CC Madhya 19.170**: Verified for R.1 only (see R); not for this level's talk or handout.

**Quotations in BIKC chapter 22 not used.** BIKC cites Prabhupada's lecture of 5 April 1974 (begging trains a brahmacari to become a sannyasi), his letter of 6 May 1974 ("doing more than any sannyasi" about a book distributor), his letter of 12 January 1974 ("Brahmana brahmacaris are very nice for Deity worship") and a statement of Indradyumna Swami. **Requires source review:** I have not located these on vedabase; they appear only as BIKC reports them and are paraphrased, not quoted.

## F. Philosophical explanation
**F.1 The soul acts.** BG 3.5 and its purport: action is the soul's nature; the real choice is the object of action. Idleness is therefore not a neutral state. BIKC: "an idle mind is the devil's workshop", a popular saying, not a verse, which I give as BIKC's. If there is no assigned service, find one: help another devotee, read, chant, learn a verse (BIKC).

**F.2 Any service is dear.** BIKC: a pure devotee is satisfied to do any service for Krishna, however menial it appears; the brahmacari is trained to do whatever is asked, quickly, efficiently and cheerfully; a highly qualified man should be ready for humble work too. BIKC adds (as a reported tradition, source not given) that Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura had new men wash pots, even if highly educated, to test sincerity. **Requires source review** for that story.

**F.3 Service by one's nature.** BIKC: in the gurukula tradition the guru observes a boy's propensities and engages him accordingly; if a young man is never suitably engaged according to his psychophysical propensities, he may become dissatisfied. BG 18.45-46 supports the principle: the Lord is worshipped by the work one is naturally fitted for; BG 18.47 (R.1) adds that one's own duty is better than another's performed well. The result is not a ranking. BIKC: "In the absolute sense, all services are equal. Kṛṣṇa accepts the service attitude of the devotee, not exactly the service externally performed." A brahmacari out distributing books is not necessarily more advanced than one cleaning the floor.

**F.4 Engaging all the senses.** Ambarisa's list (SB 9.4.18-20) is a map of a full day's service: mind on the feet, words in glorification, hands in cleaning, ears in hearing, eyes on the Deity, touch on devotees, smell on tulasi, tongue on prasadam, legs to holy places, head in bowing, desires in service. BG 2.67's purport turns this into a method: the senses are controlled not by neglect but by engagement.

**F.5 Begging and book distribution.** SB 7.12.5 describes the brahmacari's begging and offering to the guru. BIKC explains the value: humility, offering everything to the guru, practising detachment ("nothing belongs to me"). Fund-raising is not the same as traditional begging, BIKC says, but Prabhupada engaged brahmacaris in collecting. Book distribution is, in BIKC's words, the way alms collecting is "dovetailed with preaching", and CC Madhya 22.113's purport supports the principle: such duties serve as an impetus to remember Krishna. The training aspects (facing opposing arguments, detachment through seeing the world's suffering) are covered in bss-47.

**F.6 Cautions (BIKC).** (a) Not every brahmacari will go out daily; those who cannot are not disqualified; there is Deity worship and much else. (b) Pride comes before a fall. (c) Business is "not at all a suitable engagement" for brahmacaris; let grhasthas do it, BIKC says. (d) As much as possible, engage brahmacaris so as to have little dealing with women; outside India temple management often compromises brahmacari principles, according to BIKC. SB 7.12.6's purport is the scriptural basis for the limit on dealings with women. These are BIKC's judgments from experience, offered as guidance, not as verses.

**F.7 How leaders engage.** BIKC: "Ours is a pushing movement, so pressure must be there. But the best leaders inspire enthusiasm in their followers. Force will not always stand. It is better to command respect rather than demand it." Junior devotees need encouragement and guidance. Hence the leaders who read this page should note the second half.

**F.8 Misunderstandings.** "Service is a price I pay for membership" (it is the purpose: BG 9.27). "I will serve when I get the position I deserve" (F.3). "Menial service is for lesser people" (F.2). "A busy person is a pure devotee" (the Lord accepts the attitude, F.3).

## H. Opening and engagement
**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes).**
1. Prayer or short kirtana; give the title and say "engagement" means a service that occupies one's time.
2. Ask everyone to sit still and do nothing for thirty seconds. Then ask "Who found it easy?" Laugh with them.
3. Say: "BG 3.5 says nobody can do nothing, not even for a moment. So today's question is not whether to be busy but what to be busy with."
4. Read the translation of BG 3.5.

**H.2 Opening questions.**
1. "What is the best job or task you ever had, and what made it good?" (Take three answers.)
2. "When you are free for an hour with no plan, what does your mind do?"
3. "What service do you do now? Which part do you like and which do you avoid?"

## I. Core teaching points
**I.1 The soul cannot stop (BG 3.5).** Read the translation and the purport. The body is a vehicle; the driver is the soul. Illustration (preacher's, labelled): a bus engine left running in a garage still burns fuel, but the bus goes nowhere. In the same way, the soul's energy is always being used, and the only question is where it is going. The Krishna conscious point is that the soul's energy goes either to Krishna's service or to illusion. Next question for the group: what does a fully engaged life look like?

**I.2 Ambarisa engages everything (SB 9.4.18-20, 9.4.28, BG 2.67).** Tell J.1 and read the verses. Ask the group to count the senses and assign one to each of the following: cleaning, kirtana, hearing class, seeing the Deity, prasadam, tulasi, walking to a temple. Then BG 2.67's purport: one sense out of service is enough to deviate the devotee. Next question for the group: what should we do when we are not given the service we would choose?

**I.3 Any service, and service by nature (BG 18.45-46; BIKC).** Read BG 18.46's translation. A person is perfected "through performing his own work" when it is offered to the Lord. BIKC's two sides: be ready for humble work; and, after a time, a man is better satisfied if the guide engages him according to his nature. Application (preacher's): when asking for a different service, do it through the guide, with the request framed as "where can I serve best?", not "I do not like this".

**I.4 Begging, books and remembrance (SB 7.12.5; CC Madhya 22.113).** The brahmacari collects for the guru and offers it all. BIKC: this teaches humility and detachment. In ISKCON, book distribution is the form, and CC Madhya 22.113's purport shows the point: such duties are "an impetus for remembering Kṛṣṇa". Application (preacher's): ask not "how many?" but "was Krishna remembered?"

**I.5 Cautions (BIKC; SB 7.12.6).** (a) Do not rank services. (b) Business is for grhasthas (BIKC). (c) Limit dealings with women to what is needed (SB 7.12.6 purport; BIKC). (d) Leaders: inspire, do not merely demand.

## J. Stories and illustrations
**J.1 Maharaja Ambarisa (SB 9.4.18-28; 9.4.68).** Retell using only the verses: the king always engaged his mind, words, hands, ears, eyes, touch, smell, tongue, legs, head and desires in the Lord's service (9.4.18-20); the Lord, pleased, gave him His disc (9.4.28), which protects the devotee. In the following episode, the sage Durvasa, offended, sent a creature against the king; the king stood unmoved (9.4.46-47, translation and the purport's remark that a pure devotee of Narayana is never afraid); the Lord's disc turned on Durvasa, who finally fell at the king's feet (9.5.1); the Lord says that His pure devotee is in His heart and He is in the devotee's (9.4.68). **Devotional lesson:** a life fully engaged in service becomes the Lord's own responsibility; the devotee is protected not by his skill but by his dependence. Do not add details beyond these verses.

**J.2 Invented illustration (labelled).** In a village kitchen, a new boy is asked to wash the pots for a month. He does it carefully, although other boys joke about it. At the end of the month the cook says to him, "You are the only one who knows where everything is." The Krishna conscious point is from BG 18.46: when he offered the work he was given, it made him fit for more service. This also matches BIKC's idea that humble service tests sincerity.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections
1. *What if I dislike my service?* Speak with the guide. BIKC says that after some time a man should be engaged according to his propensities (F.3), and that all services are equal in the absolute sense.
2. *Is book distribution compulsory?* BIKC says some devotees cannot adjust to it, and that is not a disqualification. It also says that, if a brahmacari can preach, that is best.
3. *Is preaching better than other services?* BIKC: preaching gives a taste, but it is not necessarily true that the one out distributing is more advanced than the one cleaning the floor.
4. *Why not business for brahmacaris?* BIKC's view: it breeds a profit-and-loss mentality and materialistic dealings. This is BIKC's judgment, not a verse.
5. *What about tasks which involve women?* SB 7.12.6's purport: keep the talk to the business and brief; see bss-31 onward for the full treatment.
6. *Is rest a lack of service?* No; BG 6.16's purport sets the measure of sleep and BG 3.5 does not forbid rest, but idleness without service is the danger BIKC names.

## L. Case studies
**L.1 Invented case for discussion.** Mohan, a graduate, is asked to clean the temple floor each morning. He says "I could do something more useful." *Principles:* BG 18.46 (his work, offered), SB 9.4.18-20 (hands in cleansing the Lord's temple), BIKC F.2. *Questions:* What did Ambarisa use his hands for? How could Mohan ask for a change without complaint? *Analysis:* he can serve in cleaning with full attention and ask his guide where else his abilities would help. *Takeaway:* the attitude with which we serve matters more than which service we are given.

**L.2 Invented case for discussion (extension).** Dev distributes many books and starts to look down on those who stay in the kitchen. *Principles:* BIKC (pride before a fall; Krishna accepts the attitude), CC Madhya 22.113 (service as impetus for remembering). *Questions:* What has changed in his remembrance? *Takeaway:* when Dev starts comparing himself with others, he stops remembering Krishna in his service.

## M. Discussion and reflection
**M.1 Closing check questions (with expected answers).**
1. *What does BG 3.5 say about idleness?* No one can refrain from action, even for a moment.
2. *Name five senses Ambarisa engaged and how.* E.g., ears in hearing, hands in cleaning the temple, eyes on the Deity, tongue in prasadam, legs to holy places.
3. *What did the Lord give Ambarisa (SB 9.4.28)?* His disc, which protects the devotee.
4. *How does BG 18.46 make ordinary work worship?* By offering the result to the Lord who is the source of all.
5. *What does BIKC say about ranking services?* In the absolute sense all are equal; the Lord accepts the attitude.
6. *According to BIKC, what happens to a brahmacari with no absorbing engagement?* He is likely to end up in a marriage engagement.

**M.2 Activities.**
(a) *The ten senses (10 min, pairs).* Write the senses on a card. Beside each, write one specific action today that serves Krishna; compare with Ambarisa's list.
(b) *Service swap (15 min, groups of four).* Each says one service they are now doing and one thing they learned from it; the others name the verse that applies.
(c) *Role-play (10 min, extension).* One plays a brahmacari given a service he dislikes, the other the guide. Use L.1. The group observes whether the request is made as a complaint or as a question.

**M.3 Reflection (private).** "Is there a sense of mine that I have never engaged in Krishna's service?"

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary
Every soul acts; nobody can stay without doing anything, even for a moment (BG 3.5). So the real question is what we are busy with: illusion or Krishna. Srila Prabhupada explains that if the soul is not engaged in Krishna consciousness, it will be engaged in what the illusory energy dictates.

Maharaja Ambarisa shows the full answer. He engaged his mind on Krishna's feet, his words in glorifying the Lord, his hands in cleaning the temple, his ears in hearing about Krishna, his eyes on the Deity, his touch on devotees, his smell on tulasi, his tongue on prasadam, his legs in going to holy places, his head in bowing, and all his desires in serving the Lord (SB 9.4.18-20). Because he depended on the Lord, the Lord gave him protection (SB 9.4.28). Prabhupada adds that all the senses must be engaged; one sense left for enjoyment can lead a devotee astray (BG 2.67).

A brahmacari is trained to take any service, quickly and cheerfully, even humble service. After some time a guide will, ideally, give service that suits his nature. By offering the result of one's own work to the Lord, a person becomes perfect (BG 18.46). Do not compare services: the Lord accepts the attitude. The traditional service of a brahmacari is to collect alms for the guru and offer everything to him (SB 7.12.5); in ISKCON, book distribution and preaching do the same work and help one remember Krishna (CC Madhya 22.113). Business is for householders, and dealings with women should be limited to what is needed (BIKC; SB 7.12.6).

## S2. Key ideas
- The soul always acts, so we must choose whom to act for.
- Ambarisa engaged all his senses in the Lord's service.
- Any service is good when done for Krishna, and we should not compare services.
- When we offer our own work to the Lord, we can reach perfection.
- Service is given to help us remember Krishna.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **BG 3.5** · No one can refrain from doing something, not even for a moment. · I will not waste my energy, because I cannot stop acting.
- **SB 9.4.18-20** · Ambarisa engaged all his senses in service. · I can also engage all my senses in service.
- **BG 18.46** · By worship of the Lord through his own work, a man attains perfection. · I can offer my own work to Krishna.

## S4. Words to know
- **Engagement:** a service that occupies one's time.
- **Brahmacari:** a celibate student of spiritual life.
- **Prasadam:** food offered to Krishna.
- **Tulasi:** the sacred plant offered to Krishna.
- **Hrsikesa:** a name of Krishna meaning master of the senses.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *Can I do nothing and be peaceful?* The soul cannot stop acting (BG 3.5).
2. *What if my service is humble?* All services can be equal; the Lord accepts the attitude.
3. *What if I dislike my service?* Speak with your guide and ask where you can serve best.
4. *Is a book distributor better than a cook?* Not necessarily; pride comes before a fall.
5. *How can I engage all my senses?* Follow Ambarisa: hear, see the Deity, take prasadam, clean the temple, walk to holy places.

## S6. Check your understanding
1. What does BG 3.5 say about doing nothing?
2. Name three ways Ambarisa engaged his senses.
3. What did the Lord give Ambarisa?
4. What does BG 18.46 say about one's own work?
5. Why does BIKC say service should not be compared?

**Answers:** (1) No one can refrain from action even for a moment. (2) E.g., hands cleaning the temple, ears hearing about Krishna, tongue tasting prasadam. (3) His disc for protection (SB 9.4.28). (4) By worshipping the Lord through it a man attains perfection. (5) Because the Lord accepts the attitude of the devotee, not the outward service.
