# Spirituality and technology

> **VL1 · 45–60 min · review: sources checked, awaiting senior review.** Scripture light but exact. IYF holds three decks ("Technology: blessing or
> curse", "Science & Spirituality", "Absolute necessity of spirituality"); this page follows the first deck's
> flow (what technology gave → what went wrong → the knife → the synthesis) and its five-level blessing/curse
> table, and drops its unsourced statistics (WHO, divorce, war deaths) and its "science discovers God" slides,
> which belong to VL2-08. Not sensitive, but see K on comparisons with other faiths and on screen addiction. Mood:
> light, curious, not anti-technology: the room is full of engineers.

## A. Topic overview

| | |
|---|---|
| Batch / level | VL1 (Vedic Level 1), a stand-alone talk; also useful on campuses (IITs, DU) |
| Suggested duration | 45–60 min (G gives a 30-minute cut) |
| Preparation time | About 90 min, the first time |
| Difficulty | Introductory |
| Prerequisites | None |
| Audience | Students and young professionals, many in engineering or IT; proud of technology, uneasy about their own phone use; allergic to "technology is evil" talks |
| Teaching objective | Show that technology is a tool that multiplies whatever its user is, that the user is where spirituality works, and that the Gita's own tests (attention, moderation, direction) let a young person use technology without being used by it |

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

The question is live in every pocket in the room. A VL1 audience will reject a talk that condemns technology
and will not need a talk that praises it. What they lack is a frame: where exactly does the problem sit, in the
tool or in the hand? The Gita places matter and the living being on two sides (BG 7.4–5), and its practical
verses on attention, moderation and direction (BG 2.67, 6.17, NOI 2, 9.27) apply to screens without
modification. The talk is relevance, not philosophy.

Builds on vl1-37 (information versus knowledge) and vl1-16 (the mind as equipment): this page keeps only its own angle,
the device as a tool and the user as the one to train; link those pages for the rest. NOI 2 below is a deeper verse (the
talk uses only its plain meaning: do not over-collect, over-work, or talk idly).

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **VL1** (here) | Tool vs user; the knife; the five-level balance sheet; three tests; one experiment |
| VL2 | God and science: the evidence question, argued (VL2-08) |
| VL3 | The debate "Technology: boon or curse" as a training exercise (VL3-11) |
| SPS | Using technology in service, with accountability |
| DPS | *Yukta-vairagya*, using things in the service of the Lord without clinging (DPS-29) |
| BSS | Simple living in training: fewer devices, steadier mind |

**Not done here:**
- "Science proves God" (intelligent design, consciousness studies): VL2-08, with sources.
- Statistics on war, suicide, divorce, pollution (on IYF's slides, unsourced): left out.
- Prabhupada's remarks on machines and unemployment (Encyclopedia 0235): an economic argument, not for VL1.
- Comparisons with other religions' views of technology.

## C. Central message

Technology is a knife: it cuts vegetables or throats depending on the hand. Spirituality works on the hand. The
problem of our age is not that we have powerful tools but that we have stopped asking who the user is and what
he is for; the Gita's tests of attention, moderation and direction let us keep the tools and recover the user.

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. Explain the Gita's two-sided picture, matter (including mind and intelligence) and the living being who
   uses it (BG 7.4–5), and say why that places the problem in the user, not the tool.
2. Retell Srila Prabhupada's knife analogy accurately and name its source (a 1975 morning-walk conversation).
3. Apply three tests from the Gita to their own device use: attention (BG 2.67), moderation (BG 6.17, NOI 2)
   and direction (BG 9.27, 18.46).
4. Fill in IYF's five-level balance sheet (physical, psychological, economic, ecological, medical) for one
   technology they use daily.
5. Choose one small change for the week.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** BG 7.4–5 and 2.67 carry the frame; NOI 2, BG 6.17 and 9.27 carry the tests. The Prabhupada
conversation (secondary) gives the knife. SB 5.5.1 and BG 18.46 prepare you for questions.

**BG 7.4**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/7/4/
> "Earth, water, fire, air, ether, mind, intelligence and false ego – all together these eight constitute My separated material energies."
- **Context:** Chapter 7, Krsna describes His energies.
- **Teaching:** The material elements, gross and subtle, are the Lord's separated energy. Mind and intelligence
  are on the list.
- **Purport point:** The purport names the eight elements and states that "the ingredients of the material
  manifestation are separated energies of the Lord"; it adds that the assumption that living entities are "the
  causes, controllers and enjoyers of the material energy" is, according to the Gita, false.
- **Application (preacher's):** Every technology is an arrangement of these elements. Including the mind: the
  mind is itself a piece of equipment, which is why a phone can hijack it. At VL1 say "the stuff" and "the
  user"; do not lecture on *prakrti*.
- **Use:** in the talk (frame).

**BG 7.5**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/7/5/
> "Besides these, O mighty-armed Arjuna, there is another, superior energy of Mine, which comprises the living entities who are exploiting the resources of this material, inferior nature."
- **Teaching:** The living beings are a superior energy; they use ("exploit") the inferior, material one.
- **Purport point:** "The cosmic manifestation has no power to act unless it is moved by the superior energy,
  the living entity." "While exploiting the gross and subtle inferior energy (matter), the superior energy (the
  living entity) forgets his real spiritual mind and intelligence."
- **Application (preacher's):** The user is superior to the tool; the danger named in the purport is that in
  using the tool the user forgets himself. That is the whole talk in one sentence.
- **Use:** in the talk (frame).

**BG 2.67**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/67/
> "As a strong wind sweeps away a boat on the water, even one of the roaming senses on which the mind focuses can carry away a man's intelligence."
- **Teaching:** A single sense the mind fixes on can carry off the intelligence.
- **Purport point:** "even one of them engaged in sense gratification can deviate the devotee from the path";
  the correct technique is to engage all the senses, as Maharaja Ambarisa did.
- **Application (preacher's):** One notification is "one of the roaming senses". The verse describes the
  attention economy exactly; let the audience make the connection.
- **Use:** in the talk (the attention test).

**NOI 2**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/2/
> "One's devotional service is spoiled when he becomes too entangled in the following six activities: (1) eating more than necessary or collecting more funds than required; (2) over-endeavoring for mundane things that are very difficult to obtain; (3) talking unnecessarily about mundane subject matters…"
- **Context:** Rupa Gosvami's second instruction; the six things that spoil spiritual life.
- **Teaching:** Over-collecting (*atyahara*), over-endeavour (*prayasa*) and idle talk (*prajalpa*) are three of
  the six spoilers.
- **Purport point:** "Human life is meant for plain living and high thinking."
- **Application (preacher's):** Three of the six map directly onto device habits: collecting (tabs, downloads,
  feeds), over-endeavour (the next upgrade), idle talk (scrolling and commenting). At VL1 present them as a
  checklist, not as "devotional service is spoiled"; the room has no devotional service yet.
- **Use:** in the talk (the moderation test).

**BG 9.27**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/9/27/
> "Whatever you do, whatever you eat, whatever you offer or give away, and whatever austerities you perform – do that, O son of Kuntī, as an offering to Me."
- **Teaching:** Every activity can be offered.
- **Purport point:** "it is the duty of everyone to mold his life in such a way that he will not forget Krsna in
  any circumstance."
- **Application (preacher's):** The direction test: the same screen can be given a direction. At VL1 say
  "offered to something higher than yourself" and let the devotional meaning wait.
- **Use:** in the talk (the direction test).

**BG 18.46**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/46/
> "By worship of the Lord, who is the source of all beings and who is all-pervading, a man can attain perfection through performing his own work."
- **Purport point:** "In whatever occupation one may be engaged, if he serves the Supreme Lord he will achieve
  the highest perfection."
- **Application (preacher's):** For the engineer who hears "spirituality" as "leave tech": no. Same work,
  new direction.
- **Use:** in the talk, one line; background for K.

**SB 5.5.1**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/5/1/
> "…one who has been awarded this human form should not work hard day and night simply for sense gratification, which is available even for dogs and hogs…"
- **Teaching:** Human life is not for hard labour aimed only at sense gratification.
- **Purport point:** Srila Prabhupada describes the commuter's day: the local train, "nine to five", the bus
  home, "after eating, he has sex and goes to sleep. For all this hardship, his only happiness is a little sex."
  "Human life is meant for tapasya".
- **Application (preacher's):** The deepest form of the question "what is the technology for?": if the
  convenience it buys is spent on the same four animal activities, nothing has advanced. Use gently; the purport
  is blunt.
- **Use:** background; one line in the talk if the room is ready.

**BG 6.17**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/17/
> "He who is regulated in his habits of eating, sleeping, recreation and work can mitigate all material pains by practicing the yoga system."
- **Teaching:** Regulation of eating, sleeping, recreation and work removes material pains.
- **Purport point:** "Extravagance in the matter of eating, sleeping, defending and mating … can block
  advancement in the practice of yoga."
- **Application (preacher's):** "Recreation" (*vihara*) is where screens live. The verse does not ban it; it
  regulates it.
- **Use:** in the talk (moderation test).

**Srila Prabhupada, morning-walk conversation, Chicago, July 1975**: Partially verified (secondary report:
the *Back to Godhead* "Srila Prabhupada Speaks Out" column, transcribed in the Preaching Encyclopedia,
`research/encyclopedia/Srila Prabhupada Speaks Out/0243 Seeing Technology in a Spiritual Light.txt`; check
against the vedabase transcript before quoting on a slide).
> "with a knife you can cut vegetables and fruit, but if you use it for cutting your throat, that is bad. So now people are using the knife of technology for cutting their own throat, for forgetting all about self-realization".
- The same conversation: "If there is a nice car, why should I condemn it? Utilize it for Krsna: then it is all
  right. We don't say, 'Give it up.'" And the lame man and the blind man: "If the Western world, the blind man,
  takes India, the lame man, on its shoulders, then the lame man can point the way spiritually and the blind
  man can sustain them materially".
- **Application (preacher's):** The knife is the talk's image; the car line protects the talk from being
  anti-technology; the lame/blind pairing is IYF's "synthesis" slide with its proper source.
- **Use:** in the talk.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** Two energies: matter, which includes the subtle equipment of mind and intelligence
   (BG 7.4), and the living being who uses it (BG 7.5). Technology is matter arranged by intelligence; it has no
   purpose of its own. The purport's warning is that in using matter the user "forgets his real spiritual mind
   and intelligence". Spirituality is the recovery of the user.
2. **Why it matters.** A tool that multiplies the user's power multiplies his forgetfulness too. The deck says
   "technology covers, doesn't cure"; the scriptural version is that convenience spent on the four animal
   activities has advanced nothing (SB 5.5.1). The Gita's tests are therefore not about the tool but about the
   hand: attention (BG 2.67: one sense can carry away the intelligence), moderation (BG 6.17; NOI 2's
   over-collecting, over-endeavour, idle talk), direction (BG 9.27, 18.46).
3. **Connections.** VL1-16 (the mind as equipment), VL1-37 (information vs knowledge), VL1-42 (the same
   synthesis at the scale of society), VL2-08 (science and God), VL3-11 (the debate).
4. **Consequences.** Someone who accepts this stops the two lazy answers ("technology is progress", "technology
   is the problem") and asks three questions of each device: does it hold my attention or scatter it; is my use
   regulated or extravagant; what is it for? They keep the tool and change the hand.
5. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - **"Spiritual people are anti-technology."** "If there is a nice car, why should I condemn it?" (Prabhupada,
     1975). Perfection through one's own work (BG 18.46).
   - **"Technology is neutral, so there's nothing to discuss."** The knife is neutral; the hand is not. And the
     tool is designed to pull one sense (BG 2.67); neutrality does not mean harmlessness.
   - **"Spirituality is a painkiller too, just an older one."** Fair challenge; answer with the tests. If a
     practice sharpens attention and regulates habits (BG 6.17), it is doing the opposite of a painkiller.
   - **"Science disproved the soul."** Not this talk; VL2-08. Say so and move on.
6. **Pitch for VL1.** No Sanskrit except *prajalpa* ("idle talk"), which the room will enjoy. Never shame
   phone use; everyone in the room, including the speaker, has lost an hour to a screen this week. Say so.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet, give your name, and say plainly: nobody will be asked to give up their phone.
2. Ask for a show of hands for the opening question (private, nobody is named).
3. Ask H.2 question 1 and take two or three answers, thanking each.
4. Say you will return to those answers when the "knife" image comes.
5. Give the transition line below and begin "What technology gave".

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the room's own life)
1. What is one thing technology lets you do that your grandparents could not?
2. When did a device last make you lose time you meant to use for something else?
3. If you could change one habit with your phone, what would it be? (Think it; sharing is optional.)

- **Opening question:** "Hands up if the first thing you touched this morning was a person. Now, if it was a
  phone." Laughter. "Nobody here is going to be told to throw away the phone. The question is who picked it up."
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** Aman sits down to study for an hour. Forty-eight minutes later he has
  answered three messages, watched a reel about studying, compared two laptops he will not buy and read one
  paragraph. His tools worked perfectly. What didn't?
- **Transition:** "The Gita has a two-line answer about where the problem sits, and an old teacher gave a
  one-line image for it, on a morning walk in Chicago in 1975."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** They will build the next decade's technology. The question of the user
  is theirs to answer, for themselves first.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Technology is a gift; honour it.** Light, medicine, flight, the global village (IYF's list). *Example:*
   this talk reaches you on a screen later. *Transition:* so why does it also feel like a curse?
2. **The balance sheet has two columns at every level.** Physical, psychological, economic, ecological, medical
   (IYF's table, N). *Example:* communication vs loneliness; diagnostics vs expensive medicine. *Transition:*
   where does the minus column come from?
3. **Stuff and user.** Matter, including mind and intelligence, is one energy (BG 7.4); the living being who
   uses it is another (BG 7.5), and in using it he forgets himself (purport). *Example:* the knife (Prabhupada
   1975). *Transition:* so the work is on the user. How?
4. **Test 1: attention.** "Even one of the roaming senses … can carry away a man's intelligence" (BG 2.67).
   *Example:* one notification, forty-eight minutes. *Transition:* second test.
5. **Test 2: moderation.** Regulated recreation (BG 6.17); not over-collecting, over-endeavouring or idle
   talking (NOI 2). *Example:* 40 open tabs, the upgrade cycle, the comment thread. *Transition:* third test.
6. **Test 3: direction.** "Whatever you do … do that … as an offering" (BG 9.27); perfection through one's own
   work (BG 18.46). *Example:* the same coding skill, pointed at something higher than the next salary.
   *Transition:* and together?
7. **The synthesis.** The lame man who sees and the blind man who walks (Prabhupada 1975): technology carries,
   spirituality points. *Transition:* to one experiment.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

> **Before you teach:** two cautions. (1) Do not compare religions' attitudes to technology; keep to the Gita.
> (2) Someone in the room may have a real compulsion (gaming, pornography, gambling apps). This talk is about
> ordinary distraction. If a question points to a compulsion, do not diagnose publicly; speak afterwards, and
> say plainly that counselling is compatible with spiritual life. *(Pastoral guidance, not a scriptural claim.)*

1. **"Isn't this just an old person's complaint about phones?"**
   - The frame is older than phones and does not mention them: a single sense the mind fixes on can carry away
     the intelligence (BG 2.67). The complaint is yours, not ours: who in this room is happy with their screen
     time?
2. **"Technology lifted billions out of poverty. Spirituality didn't."**
   - True, and the tradition says use the tool: "If there is a nice car, why should I condemn it?" (Prabhupada
     1975). The question is what the lifted life is for (SB 5.5.1). Both are needed: the lame man and the blind
     man.
3. **"Religion is also a painkiller; it just doesn't have an app."**
   - Test it. A painkiller dulls attention and regulation; the Gita's practice sharpens them (BG 6.17, 2.67).
     Judge by the effect, in your own week.
4. **"Can I use technology for spirituality?"**
   - Yes: "Whatever you do … do that … as an offering" (BG 9.27). Scripture apps, recorded lectures, this
     programme's own reach. The test is the same: attention, moderation, direction.
5. **"I'm an engineer. Are you telling me to feel guilty about my work?"**
   - No. Perfection "through performing his own work" (BG 18.46). Build well; ask whom it serves.
6. **"I can't put the phone down. Is that a spiritual failure?"**
   - It is a human condition (BG 2.67 is about everyone). Start with one test, one hour a day. If it feels like
     a compulsion you cannot shift, talking to a counsellor is sensible and fully compatible with spiritual
     life. *(Pastoral.)*

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The forty-eight minutes.** Aman, 20, sits down to study for an hour and reads one paragraph. Every
tool he used worked exactly as designed.
- **Dilemma:** Whose failure was it?
- **Principles:** BG 7.5 (the user forgets himself while using); BG 2.67 (one sense carries off the
  intelligence); BG 6.17 (regulated recreation).
- **Questions:** Which single sense did the design target? What one rule for the next study hour follows from
  BG 2.67? Is the rule about the phone or about Aman?
- **Takeaway:** The tool did its job; the hand did not know its own.

**Case 2: The upgrade.** Neha, 25, earns well and replaces her phone every year, keeps 60 tabs open "for
later" and spends her commute in comment threads about people she will never meet.
- **Principles:** NOI 2 (over-collecting, over-endeavour, idle talk); BG 9.27 (direction).
- **Questions:** Match each habit to one of Rupa Gosvami's three. Which costs her most? What would the same
  commute look like with a direction?
- **Takeaway:** "Plain living and high thinking" (NOI 2 purport) is a technology policy.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first; praise every try)
1. Does the talk say technology is bad? *No: it is a gift; the work is on the user.*
2. In the knife image, what decides whether it cuts vegetables or throats? *The hand.*
3. Which verse says one sense can carry away the intelligence? *BG 2.67.*
4. Name the three tests. *Attention, moderation, direction.*
5. In BG 7.4–5, which side are mind and intelligence on, and which is the user? *Mind and intelligence are matter; the user is the living being.*

**M.2 Activity (about 8 minutes): "One device rule"**
1. 2 min: each person thinks of one moment in the day when the phone takes more than they meant to give.
2. 3 min: in pairs, each says which test (attention, moderation, direction) it fails, and writes one small rule (for example: phone out of reach for the first hour of study).
3. 2 min: volunteers share; sharing is optional, with no judgment. Thank each.
4. 1 min: link to the experiment in P.

**M.3 Reflection**
- **Reflection:** Which of the three tests (attention, moderation, direction) do I fail most often? Which of
  Rupa Gosvami's three (collecting, over-endeavour, idle talk) is my habit?
- **Application (private):** One device rule for one hour a day this week. *(Nobody needs to share.)*

## O. Research and supplementary resources

None required; the talk cites no study and makes no empirical claim about screens. Two attributions to get right:
- Einstein's sentence, from his 1941 paper "Science, Philosophy and Religion", reads "Science without religion
  is lame, religion without science is blind." IYF's slide reverses the halves; use the correct order.
- "Guided missiles and misguided men" is attributed to Martin Luther King Jr. (*Strength to Love*, 1963);
  attributed, verify before use.

harmonizes with our spirituality?" (https://www.thespiritualscientist.com/how-can-we-use-technology-in-a-way-that-harmonizes-with-our-spirituality/)

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Nobody here will be told to throw away their phone. The Bhagavad-gita puts the world in two columns: matter,
which includes the subtle equipment of mind and intelligence (BG 7.4), and the living being who uses it
(BG 7.5). Technology is matter arranged by intelligence; it has no purpose of its own. Srila Prabhupada compared
it to a knife, which "can cut vegetables and fruit" or cut your own throat, depending on the hand. The problem of
our age is not the tool but the user, who, the Gita says, forgets himself while using it. So the work is on the
hand, and the Gita gives three tests: attention (one sense the mind fixes on can carry off the intelligence,
BG 2.67), moderation (regulated recreation, BG 6.17; not over-collecting, over-endeavouring or idly talking,
NOI 2), and direction (whatever you do, offer it to something higher, BG 9.27). Keep the tool; recover the user.

In a village with one shared phone or a city office with three screens, the question is the same: who is holding the knife? Think of a busy
study hour that disappeared into messages and short videos: the tools worked perfectly, and the attention went elsewhere. The talk offers one small
experiment: pick one device rule for one hour a day this week, and notice what changes. It does not ask you to give up your devices or to
believe anything first. This is an invitation to test, and nobody needs to share their results. If screen use feels out of your control, talk to a
trusted person; support from others, and professional help where needed, is a good thing.

## S2. Key ideas

- Two columns: the stuff (matter, including mind and intelligence, BG 7.4) and the user (BG 7.5).
- The user "forgets his real spiritual mind and intelligence" while using matter (BG 7.5 purport). That is
  the problem, not the tool.
- The knife cuts vegetables or throats; the hand decides (Prabhupada, 1975).
- Test 1, attention: "even one of the roaming senses … can carry away a man's intelligence" (BG 2.67).
- Test 2, moderation: regulated recreation (BG 6.17); not over-collecting, over-endeavouring, idle talk (NOI 2).
- Test 3, direction: "Whatever you do … do that … as an offering" (BG 9.27); perfection through your own
  work (BG 18.46).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **BG 2.67:** "As a strong wind sweeps away a boat on the water, even one of the roaming senses on which the
  mind focuses can carry away a man's intelligence." *For me:* one notification is enough; guard the first one.
- **BG 7.5:** The living entities are a "superior energy … who are exploiting the resources of this material,
  inferior nature." *For me:* I am the user, not the used.
- **BG 9.27:** "Whatever you do, whatever you eat, whatever you offer or give away … do that … as an offering
  to Me." *For me:* the same screen can be given a direction.

## S4. Words to know

- **Prajalpa:** idle talk about mundane things (NOI 2); the comment thread has a Sanskrit name.
- **Atyahara:** collecting more than needed (NOI 2); sixty open tabs.
- **Prayasa:** over-endeavour for things hard to get (NOI 2); the upgrade cycle.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Isn't this an old person's complaint about phones?**
   The frame is older than phones and never mentions them: one sense the mind fixes on can carry away the
   intelligence (BG 2.67). Ask yourself whether you are happy with your own screen time.
2. **Technology lifted billions out of poverty. What did spirituality do?**
   Use the tool: "If there is a nice car, why should I condemn it?" (Prabhupada, 1975). Then ask what the
   lifted life is for. Both are needed, like a lame man who can see carried by a blind man who can walk.
3. **Can I use technology for spirituality?**
   Yes (BG 9.27): lectures, scripture apps, this programme. The three tests still apply.
4. **I'm an engineer. Should I feel guilty?**
   No. Perfection "through performing his own work" (BG 18.46). Build well; ask whom it serves.
5. **I can't put the phone down. Is that a spiritual failure?**
   It is a human condition (BG 2.67 is about everyone). Start with one test for one hour a day. If it feels like
   a compulsion you cannot shift, a counsellor can help, and that is fully compatible with spiritual life.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. In BG 7.4, are mind and intelligence listed with matter or with the living being?
2. True or false: Srila Prabhupada said technology should be given up.
3. Which verse says that one sense can carry away the intelligence like a wind sweeping a boat?
4. Name the three habits from NOI 2 that this talk applied to devices.
5. In your own words: why does the talk say the work is "on the hand, not the tool"?

**Answers:** 1. With matter: they are among the eight "separated material energies" (BG 7.4). 2. False: "If
there is a nice car, why should I condemn it? Utilize it for Krsna" (Prabhupada, 1975). 3. BG 2.67. 4. Collecting
more than needed, over-endeavouring, idle talk (NOI 2). 5. Because the tool has no purpose of its own (BG 7.4)
and the user is the one who forgets himself while using it (BG 7.5 purport); the knife cuts whatever the hand
chooses.
