## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on trd-25 (the three features of the one Absolute Truth), trd-26 (acintya-bhedabheda: the jiva is one with and different
from the Lord) and sps-03 (the personal God, for beginners). Those pages establish the positive siddhanta. This page is the
*defence*: it takes the Mayavada claims one by one and answers each with the argument the acharyas actually used.

TRD residents preach to the educated. In India, Mayavada (the doctrine that only impersonal Brahman is real, that the
individual soul *is* Brahman, and that the world and the Lord's form are illusion) is the default "Hindu philosophy" of
colleges, yoga studios and many families. A resident who cannot answer it clearly will either avoid the subject or argue by
emotion. Lord Caitanya Himself debated it twice, with Prakasananda Sarasvati in Benares (CC Adi 7) and with Sarvabhauma
Bhattacarya in Puri (CC Madhya 6), and both opponents became devotees. The acharyas then wrote the systematic answer:
Jiva Gosvami in the Sandarbhas, Baladeva Vidyabhusana in *Prameya-ratnavali*, *Govinda-bhasya* and *Gita-bhusana*.

## C. Central message

The Vedas are to be understood as they are, in their direct meaning; read that way, they say the Absolute Truth is the
Supreme Person with a spiritual form, the soul is His tiny energy, and the world is a real transformation of His energy, not
of Him. Sankara covered this on the Lord's own order to bring atheists part of the way; those who stop at his covering lose
everything (CC Adi 7.108–114). A preacher answers Mayavada with scripture and reason, respectfully, and ends with bhakti.

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. State the five Mayavada claims and the one method behind them (indirect interpretation, CC Adi 7.108–109).
2. Answer each claim from Lord Caitanya's two debates: the Lord's form is spiritual (Adi 7.112–115; Madhya 6.166–167); the
   jiva is energy, not the energetic (Adi 7.116–120); the world is parinama (transformation of energy), not vivarta (illusion)
   (Adi 7.121–127; Madhya 6.173); omkara, not *tat tvam asi*, is the maha-vakya (Adi 7.128–130; Madhya 6.175); liberation is
   not the end, bhakti is (Madhya 6.184).
3. Quote Baladeva's *Prameya-ratnavali* for the world's reality, the jiva's difference and servanthood, and liberation as
   association with Krishna (prameyas 3, 4, 5, 7), naming the edition.
4. Explain why Lord Caitanya called Sankara faultless and his followers doomed (Adi 7.110, 7.114; Madhya 6.180–182), and
   keep that tone.
5. Turn a debate into an invitation to chant and hear, as the Lord did with both opponents.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**CC Adi 7.108–109** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/adi/7/108/
> 7.108: "The Absolute Truth is described in the Upaniṣads and Brahma-sūtra, but one must understand the verses as they are. That is the supreme glory in understanding."
> 7.109: "Śrīpāda Śaṅkarācārya has described all the Vedic literatures in terms of indirect meanings. One who hears such explanations is ruined."
- **Context:** Lord Caitanya, at the house of a brahmana in Benares, answers the Mayavadi sannyasis led by Prakasananda
  Sarasvati, who have asked why He avoids the Vedanta-sutra (7.101).
- **Teaching:** the whole dispute turns on *method*: direct meaning (mukhya-vrtti) versus indirect meaning (laksana-vrtti).
- **Purport point (7.108):** "It has become fashionable since the time of Śaṅkarācārya to explain everything regarding the
  śāstras in an indirect way." The 7.110 purport gives the Lord's rule for teaching:
  "To teach the Vedic literature according to its direct meaning, without false commentary, is glorious."
- **Use:** in the talk, as the first point.

**CC Adi 7.110, 7.114; CC Madhya 6.180–182** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/adi/7/110/
> 7.110: "Śaṅkarācārya is not at fault, for it is under the order of the Supreme Personality of Godhead that he has covered the real purport of the Vedas."
> 7.114: "Śaṅkarācārya, who is an incarnation of Lord Śiva, is faultless because he is a servant carrying out the orders of the Lord. But those who follow his Māyāvādī philosophy are doomed. They will lose all their advancement in spiritual knowledge."
- **Teaching:** Sankara acted under order (the Padma Purana verses the Lord quotes in Madhya 6.181–182: Lord Siva is told to
  "make the general populace averse to Me by imagining your own interpretation of the Vedas", and tells Durga, "In the Age of
  Kali I take the form of a brāhmaṇa and explain the Vedas through false scriptures in an atheistic way, similar to Buddhist
  philosophy"). The fault lies with those who stop there.
- **Purport point (7.110):** Sankara "compromised between atheism and theism in order to cheat the atheists and bring them to
  theism". "One should not, therefore, attribute very much importance to the Śārīraka-bhāṣya. In order to understand Vedānta
  philosophy, one must study Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam".
- **Application (preacher's):** this is the tone of the whole page: no contempt for Sankara, no compromise with his
  conclusion.
- **Use:** in the talk, and as the answer to "why do you attack a great acharya?" (K).

**CC Adi 7.111–115; CC Madhya 6.166–167** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/adi/7/111/
> 7.111: "According to direct understanding, the Absolute Truth is the Supreme Personality of Godhead, who has all spiritual opulences. No one can be equal to or greater than Him."
> 7.112: "Everything about the Supreme Personality of Godhead is spiritual, including His body, opulence and paraphernalia. Māyāvāda philosophy, however, covering His spiritual opulence, advocates the theory of impersonalism."
> 7.115: "One who considers the transcendental body of Lord Viṣṇu to be made of material nature is the greatest offender at the lotus feet of the Lord. There is no greater blasphemy against the Supreme Personality of Godhead."
> Madhya 6.167: "One who does not accept the transcendental form of the Lord is certainly an agnostic. Such a person should be neither seen nor touched. Indeed, he is subject to be punished by Yamarāja."
- **Teaching:** answer to claim 1 ("God has no form; the form is a product of goodness"): the Lord's form, name and entourage
  are spiritual potency, not the mode of goodness (7.113; Madhya 6.166).
- **Purport point (7.111):** Prabhupada confirms it with SB 1.2.11 (the three features; see trd-25).
- **Use:** in the talk. Read 7.115 and Madhya 6.167 exactly as they are; they are the Lord's words.

**CC Adi 7.116–120; BG 7.4–5** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/adi/7/116/
> 7.116: "The Lord is like a great blazing fire, and the living entities are like small sparks of that fire."
> 7.117: "The living entities are energies, not the energetic. The energetic is Kṛṣṇa. This is very vividly described in the Bhagavad-gītā, the Viṣṇu Purāṇa and other Vedic literatures."
> 7.120: "The Māyāvāda philosophy is so degraded that it has taken the insignificant living entities to be the Lord, the Supreme Truth, thus covering the glory and supremacy of the Absolute Truth with monism."
- **Teaching:** answer to claim 2 ("the soul is God"): the Lord quotes BG 7.5 (7.118) and the Visnu Purana's three potencies
  (7.119). The soul is the superior *energy*; the energetic is one.
- **Purport point (7.120):** Bhaktivinoda Thakura: in all Vedic scriptures the jiva-tattva "is mentioned to be one of the
  energies of the Lord"; to equate it with the Supreme means "his entire philosophy is based on a misunderstanding".
- **Use:** in the talk. The full acintya-bhedabheda teaching is trd-26; here only the refutation.

**CC Adi 7.121–127; CC Madhya 6.173** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/adi/7/121/
> 7.121: "In his Vedānta-sūtra Śrīla Vyāsadeva has described that everything is but a transformation of the energy of the Lord. Śaṅkarācārya, however, has misled the world by commenting that Vyāsadeva was mistaken. Thus he has raised great opposition to theism throughout the entire world."
> 7.123: "Transformation of energy is a proven fact. It is the false bodily conception of the self that is an illusion."
> 7.127: "If there is such inconceivable potency in material objects, why should we not believe in the inconceivable potency of the Supreme Personality of Godhead?"
> Madhya 6.173: "The theory of illusion can be applied only when the living entity identifies himself with the body. As far as the cosmic manifestation is concerned, it cannot be called false, although it is certainly temporary."
- **Teaching:** answer to claim 3 ("the world is an illusion"): parinama-vada (the energy is transformed, the Lord remains the
  same, like a touchstone turning iron to gold, 7.125) against vivarta-vada (illusion). The only real illusion is the soul's
  thinking it is the body (7.123).
- **Purport point (7.121):** Bhaktivinoda: Sankara, "not accepting the energy of the Lord, thinks that it is the Lord who is
  transformed", and so "has accused Śrīla Vyāsadeva of being mistaken". Prabhupada names the sutra Sankara twisted
  (Brahma-sutra 2.1.14, *tad-ananyatvam ārambhaṇa-śabdādibhyaḥ*) and gives the plain example: a businessman's energy builds
  many factories, "yet he remains a person although his energy has been transformed".
- **Use:** in the talk; the touchstone and the businessman are the two examples to keep.

**CC Adi 7.128–131; CC Madhya 6.175** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/adi/7/128/
> 7.129: "It is the purpose of the Supreme Personality of Godhead to present praṇava [oṁkāra] as the reservoir of all Vedic knowledge. The words 'tat tvam asi' are only a partial explanation of the Vedic knowledge."
> 7.130: "Praṇava [oṁkāra] is the mahā-vākya [mahā-mantra] in the Vedas. Śaṅkarācārya's followers cover this to stress without authority the mantra tat tvam asi."
> 7.131: "In all the Vedic sūtras and literatures, it is Lord Kṛṣṇa who is to be understood, but the followers of Śaṅkarācārya have covered the real meaning of the Vedas with indirect explanations."
- **Teaching:** answer to claim 4 ("the Vedas say *tat tvam asi*, you are That"): the principal Vedic sound is omkara, the
  sound form of the Lord (Madhya 6.174); *tat tvam asi* is a subsidiary statement about the soul's spiritual nature.
- **Purport point (Madhya 6.175):** "Tat tvam asi is a warning to the living entity not to mistake the body for the self".
- **Use:** in the talk, briefly; the full argument is for K.

**CC Madhya 6.168, 6.176–177, 6.179, 6.184** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/6/168/
> 6.168: "The Buddhists do not recognize the authority of the Vedas; therefore they are considered agnostics. However, those who have taken shelter of the Vedic scriptures yet preach agnosticism in accordance with the Māyāvāda philosophy are certainly more dangerous than the Buddhists."
> 6.179: "If one tries to explain the Vedic literature in a different way, he is indulging in imagination. Any interpretation of the self-evident Vedic version is simply imaginary."

- **Context:** the debate with Sarvabhauma Bhattacarya in Puri. Sarvabhauma "presented various types of false arguments with

- **Purport point (6.168):** "Vedāśraya nāstikya-vāda means 'agnosticism under the shelter of Vedic culture'". Prabhupada cites

- **Teaching:** answer to claim 5 ("liberation, merging, is the goal"): the Lord ends every refutation with bhakti as the
  highest perfection, and quotes the self-satisfied sages who still serve Krishna (6.185–186).
- **Use:** in the talk; 6.168 is read as it is.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The five claims and the one method.** Mayavada says: (1) Brahman has no form; the Lord's form is a product of material
   goodness. (2) The soul is Brahman; "you are God". (3) The world is an illusion (vivarta). (4) The great Vedic saying is *tat
   tvam asi*, "you are That". (5) The goal is liberation, merging into Brahman. All five rest on one method: reading the
   Vedas indirectly (CC Adi 7.108–109). Take the method away and the claims fall.
2. **Why direct meaning is the rule.** The Vedas and the Vedanta-sutra are the Lord's own words, free of the four human
   defects (Adi 7.106–107). Words of such a source are read as they stand; to "interpret" them is to call the author unclear.
   Lord Caitanya: "Any interpretation of the self-evident Vedic version is simply imaginary" (Madhya 6.179).
3. **The five answers.**
   - *Form:* everything about the Lord is spiritual; to call His body material is the greatest offence (Adi 7.112–115).
   - *Soul:* energy, not energetic; spark, not fire (Adi 7.116–120; BG 7.5). Baladeva, prameyas 4–5.
   - *World:* real transformation of energy, temporary but not false; the only illusion is "I am this body" (Adi 7.121–127;
     Madhya 6.173). Baladeva, prameya 3.
   - *Maha-vakya:* omkara, the sound form of the Lord, is principal; *tat tvam asi* warns the soul it is not the body
     (Adi 7.128–130; Madhya 6.174–175).
   - *Goal:* bhakti is "the highest perfection of human activity" (Madhya 6.184); even the self-satisfied serve Krishna
     (6.185–186). Baladeva, prameya 7.
4. **Why the acharyas were so strong.** Lord Caitanya called Mayavada "more dangerous than the Buddhists" (Madhya 6.168) because
   it quotes the Vedas while removing the Lord from them. Prabhupada's purport: Mayavadis "concoct some idea of a transcendental
   position and call themselves Nārāyaṇa, or God." Residents must not soften this; they must also not add anger to it.
5. **Why Sankara is not blamed.** He is Lord Siva, acting under order to draw atheists toward the Vedas (Adi 7.110, 7.114;
   Madhya 6.180–182). The preacher honours him and refuses his conclusion, exactly as the Lord did.
6. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *"Refuting Mayavada means hating impersonalists."* No: Prakasananda and Sarvabhauma became the Lord's devotees. The aim of
     the debate is the opponent's bhakti.
   - *"Winning the argument is the point."* No: the Lord ended each debate with bhakti (Madhya 6.184) and chanting (Madhya
     25.154). An argument that does not end in Krishna's service is incomplete.
   - *"The world is false, so I need not care for it."* The world is real and temporary (Madhya 6.173); the devotee uses it in
     Krishna's service.
   - *"The Sandarbhas and Baladeva are only for scholars."* They are the systematic form of the very arguments Lord Caitanya
     used in public; a preacher learns four prameyas by number and has sruti for every point.
7. **Pitch for TRD.** Residents already believe the siddhanta. The work here is precision: exact verses, exact prameyas, the
   right tone, and the habit of ending in bhakti.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet the residents. Say: today we learn to answer, with the acharyas' own arguments, the philosophy we meet most often
   from educated people: "God is formless, you are God, the world is an illusion."
2. Say plainly what Mayavada is (one sentence from F.1) and that Lord Caitanya debated it twice and won both opponents as
   devotees.
3. Ask H.2 question 1; take three answers; write the exact phrases on the board.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **One method under five claims.** Indirect interpretation is the root (CC Adi 7.108–109). *Example:* a court does not let a
   witness "interpret" the law into its opposite. *Transition:* so what do the Vedas say directly?
2. **The Lord's form is spiritual.** His body, opulence and entourage are spiritual potency; to call them material is the
   greatest offence (Adi 7.112–115; Madhya 6.166–167). *Transition:* then what is the soul?
3. **The soul is energy, the Lord is the energetic.** Spark and fire (Adi 7.116–117); BG 7.5; Baladeva's two birds (prameya 4)
   and the servant (prameya 5). *Transition:* then is this world nothing?
4. **The world is a real transformation of energy.** Touchstone (Adi 7.125–127), businessman (7.121 purport); the only illusion
   is "I am this body" (7.123; Madhya 6.173); Baladeva, prameya 3. *Transition:* what about *tat tvam asi*?
5. **Omkara is the maha-vakya.** *Tat tvam asi* is a partial statement (Adi 7.129–130; Madhya 6.175). *Transition:* and the goal?
6. **Bhakti, not merging.** The Lord ends with bhakti as the highest perfection (Madhya 6.184); Baladeva, prameya 7; Krishna's
   own words, BG 7.24 and 9.11. *Transition:* how did the Lord treat the opponents?
7. **Respect for Sankara, no compromise with the conclusion.** Adi 7.110, 7.114; Madhya 6.180–182. *Transition:* the stories.

## J. Stories and illustrations

**Prakasananda Sarasvati in Benares** (CC Adi 7.101–148). The Mayavadi sannyasis ask the Lord why He avoids Vedanta (7.101). He
answers with their permission (7.102, 7.105); they are pleased by His words and His bodily features (7.104). He explains the
Vedanta-sutra as Narayana's own words (7.106), refutes indirect interpretation point by point (7.108–133), and the sannyasis'
hearts change. *Devotional lesson:* the Lord debated only after they asked and only to bring them to Krishna. Years later a
Maharastriya brahmana tells Prakasananda himself that Sankara's interpretations are accepted by scholars "but they do not appeal
to the heart" (Madhya 25.27), and Prakasananda comes to the Lord (Madhya 25).

**Sarvabhauma Bhattacarya in Puri** (CC Madhya 6). The greatest Vedanta teacher of his day teaches the Lord Vedanta for seven
days while the Lord listens silently (6.121–129), then the Lord says that the explanations "have simply agitated My mind" (6.130)
and refutes the Sariraka-bhasya, pointing out "hundreds of faults" (6.176). Sarvabhauma argues with "pseudo logic" (6.177);
the Lord quotes the Padma Purana on Lord Siva's order (6.181–182); Sarvabhauma is stunned (6.183); the Lord at
once turns to bhakti (6.184). *Devotional lesson:* the Lord did not leave a defeated scholar; He gave him the highest truth and
made him a devotee.

**A contemporary illustration (labelled):** Srila Prabhupada's purport to Adi 7.110 is itself an illustration: the Gita had been
taught for years with commentary and "no one came to the conclusion of Kṛṣṇa consciousness"; presented as it is, "within four or
five short years thousands of people all over the world have become Kṛṣṇa conscious." *Devotional lesson:* direct meaning is not
only correct, it works.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"Why attack Sankaracarya? He is a great acharya."** We do not attack him. Lord Caitanya called him faultless, Lord Siva
   acting under the Lord's order (Adi 7.110, 7.114; Madhya 6.180–182). We refuse his *conclusion*, as he himself was
   ordered to cover the truth for a purpose that is now finished.
2. **"The Upanisads do say the Absolute is without qualities (nirguna)."** They say the Lord has no *material* qualities; that is
   what impersonal descriptions establish (Madhya 6.141–142). Baladeva, prameya 3, cites Svetasvatara 4.1: the Absolute "who
   possesses no material attributes" creates by His potencies.
3. **"If the world is temporary, is it not as good as false?"** No. Temporary and false are different words (Madhya 6.173). The
   Vedas call it *asat*, "temporary, not unreal" (Baladeva, prameya 3). A dream is false; a season is temporary.
4. **"Tat tvam asi: the Veda says I am That."** It says the soul is spiritual, not the body (Madhya 6.175 purport). The principal
   Vedic sound is omkara, the Lord (Adi 7.128–130). A partial statement is not the whole Veda.
5. **"The soul is Brahman in quality; is that not oneness?"** Yes, in quality; not in quantity or in position. Spark and fire
   (Adi 7.116); energy and energetic (7.117). The full doctrine is trd-26.
6. **"Isn't merging into Brahman at least a real liberation?"** It is reached with great trouble and lost again (SB 10.2.32,
   quoted in the Madhya 6.168 purport), because the Lord's feet are neglected. Bhakti is the highest perfection (Madhya 6.184);
   liberation means the Lord's association (Baladeva, prameya 7).
7. **"My uncle will not read the Caitanya-caritamrta. What do I say in two minutes?"** Give BG 7.24 and 9.11 in Krishna's own
   words, one example (touchstone or businessman), and invite him to hear the Gita as it is. End with the holy name, as the Lord
   did with Prakasananda (Madhya 25.154).
8. **"Can I quote the Gita-bhusana?"** Only where Srila Prabhupada quotes it (BG 2.61, 3.14 purports) until a checked edition is
   in research/books/. Say "Requires source review" for anything else.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Many educated people say: God has no form; the soul is God; the world is an illusion; the Veda says "you are That" (*tat tvam
asi*); the goal is to merge into the One. This is Mayavada, the philosophy of Sankaracarya's school. Lord Caitanya met it twice,
with Prakasananda Sarasvati in Benares (CC Adi 7) and with Sarvabhauma Bhattacarya in Puri (CC Madhya 6), and He answered it so
completely that both scholars became His devotees.

The Lord first showed the root: Sankara explained the Vedas by *indirect* meanings, and "one who hears such explanations is
ruined" (Adi 7.109). The Vedas and the Vedanta-sutra are the Lord's own words and must be read as they are (Adi 7.108). Read
directly, they say: the Absolute Truth is the Supreme Person with a spiritual body, opulence and associates; to call His body
material is the greatest offence (Adi 7.111–115). The soul is the Lord's energy, a spark of the fire, never the fire itself
(Adi 7.116–120; BG 7.5). The world is a real transformation of the Lord's energy, temporary but not false, like gold made by a
touchstone that itself stays the same; the only real illusion is the soul's thinking "I am this body" (Adi 7.121–127; Madhya
6.173). The principal Vedic sound is omkara, the Lord Himself in sound; "*tat tvam asi*" only reminds the soul that it is
spiritual (Adi 7.128–130; Madhya 6.175). And the goal is not merging but devotional service, "the highest perfection of human
activity" (Madhya 6.184). Krishna says the same in the Gita: those who think He was impersonal before and became a person are
"unintelligent men" (BG 7.24); those who deride His human-like form are fools (BG 9.11).

The Lord did not blame Sankara: he is Lord Siva, who covered the truth on the Lord's own order to draw atheists toward the Vedas
(Adi 7.110, 7.114; Madhya 6.180–182). Those who stop at his conclusion lose everything. Baladeva Vidyabhusana later wrote the
same answers systematically in *Prameya-ratnavali*: the world is real (prameya 3), the souls are different from Visnu (4) and
are His servants (5), and liberation means the Lord's association, not merging (7). A devotee answers Mayavada with scripture
and reason, with respect, and ends by inviting the other person to hear and chant, as the Lord did.

## S2. Key ideas

- One method, indirect interpretation, underlies all five Mayavada claims (CC Adi 7.108–109).
- The Lord's form is spiritual; calling it material is the greatest offence (Adi 7.112–115).
- The soul is energy, the Lord is the energetic: spark and fire (Adi 7.116–117; BG 7.5).
- The world is a real transformation of energy, temporary, not false; the only illusion is "I am this body" (Adi 7.121–127;
  Madhya 6.173).
- Omkara is the principal Vedic sound; *tat tvam asi* is partial (Adi 7.129–130).
- Bhakti, not merging, is the perfection (Madhya 6.184); Sankara is honoured, his conclusion refused (Adi 7.110, 7.114).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **CC Adi 7.108:** "one must understand the verses as they are. That is the supreme glory in understanding." *For me:* I read
  scripture as it is and teach it as it is.
- **CC Adi 7.123:** "Transformation of energy is a proven fact. It is the false bodily conception of the self that is an illusion."
  *For me:* the one illusion to fight is in me, not in the world.
- **BG 7.24:** "Unintelligent men, who do not know Me perfectly, think that I, the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Kṛṣṇa, was
  impersonal before and have now assumed this personality." *For me:* Krishna Himself answers impersonalism.
- **CC Madhya 6.184:** "devotional service unto the Supreme Personality of Godhead is the highest perfection of human activity."
  *For me:* every argument must end here.

## S4. Words to know

- **Mayavada:** the doctrine that only impersonal Brahman is real and the Lord's form, the soul's individuality and the world
  are illusion (maya).
- **Mukhya-vrtti / laksana-vrtti:** direct meaning / indirect (interpreted) meaning of scripture.
- **Parinama-vada / vivarta-vada:** the world as a transformation of the Lord's energy / the world as an illusion.
- **Omkara (pranava):** the sound form of the Lord, the principal Vedic sound.
- **Prameya:** a truth to be established; Baladeva's *Prameya-ratnavali* lists nine.
- **Sariraka-bhasya:** Sankara's commentary on the Vedanta-sutra.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Why do devotees criticise Sankaracarya if he is Lord Siva?** We do not criticise him; Lord Caitanya called him faultless,
   acting under the Lord's order (Adi 7.110, 7.114). We refuse the *conclusion*, because those who stop there "will lose all
   their advancement in spiritual knowledge" (Adi 7.114).
2. **The Upanisads say Brahman has no qualities. Is that not impersonal?** They mean no *material* qualities; impersonal
   descriptions establish that everything about the Lord is transcendental (Madhya 6.141).
3. **If the world is temporary, why not call it false?** Temporary and false are different (Madhya 6.173). Baladeva: the Vedic
   word *asat* means "temporary, not unreal" (prameya 3).
4. **What does *tat tvam asi* really mean?** That the soul is spiritual, not the body (Madhya 6.175 purport); it is a partial
   statement, and omkara is the principal one (Adi 7.129–130).
5. **Is merging into Brahman not a real liberation?** It is reached with great trouble and lost, because the Lord's feet are
   neglected (SB 10.2.32, in the Madhya 6.168 purport). Real liberation is the Lord's association (Baladeva, prameya 7), and
   bhakti is the perfection (Madhya 6.184).
6. **How do I answer my uncle in two minutes?** One verse in Krishna's own words (BG 7.24 or 9.11), one example (the touchstone),
   and an invitation to hear the Gita as it is and to chant.

5. Name two prameyas of Baladeva's *Prameya-ratnavali* that answer Mayavada.

**Answers:** 1. Indirect interpretation of the Vedas (CC Adi 7.108–109). 2. True (Adi 7.116). 3. The soul's false bodily

3 (the world is real), 4 (jivas differ from Visnu), 5 (jivas are His servants), 7 (liberation is Krishna's association).
6. He called Sankara faultless, acting under the Lord's order, and said his followers are doomed (Adi 7.110, 7.114).
