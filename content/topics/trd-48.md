# The goal: Goloka, the spiritual world and eternal service

> **TRD · up to 180 min · review: drafted · `sensitive: true` (the eternal associates and their service; the head reviews).** Group G, page 8, the
> last of the series on the mellows and the goal of devotion. A resident says "back to Godhead" many times a day. This page asks what the words
> mean, positively, in Srila Prabhupada's books: where the devotee goes (Bhagavad-gita 8 and 15; Brahma-samhita 5.29, 5.43, 5.56; CC Adi 5.14–22;
> CC Madhya 21), what it is like there (SB 2.9.10; SB 3.15.14–25; Bs 5.56), what he does there (eternal service in his own rasa: CC Madhya 8.89,
> 8.229–230; SB 3.15.14; CC Antya 20.60), and how he reaches it ("only by surrender and by no other means", BG 15.6 purport). trd-30 gave the
> gradation of realms through Gopa-kumara's search; this page gives Goloka itself and the life of service there, and ends in the resident's own
> honesty: do I want this, and is my service today the beginning of it? **Source note:** Brahma-samhita is quoted from the BBT edition with
> Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati's purports; Brhad-bhagavatamrta 2.5 from the Desire Tree edition (OCR) in `research/books/`, in one short line.
> Gopala-campu and Govinda-lilamrta, which the head allows as references for the spiritual world, are named in O for further study and not quoted
> here. The spiritual world is not described from imagination; every feature on this page has its verse.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on trd-30, which climbed with Gopa-kumara from the earth through the heavens, Brahmaloka and Vaikuntha to Goloka and taught the gradation of
realms (Bs 5.43; CC Adi 5.14–17); this page does not repeat the climb. It stays at the top and asks what is there. Builds on trd-41 and trd-44: the five
rasas and the gopis' service are what the devotee does in Goloka (CC Madhya 8.229–230). Builds on trd-47: the Lord's own conclusion that service is
sweeter than union (CC Antya 20.60) tells us what the goal is made of.

A resident needs this page for two reasons. First, precision: when he tells a student that the soul can "go back to Godhead", he must be able to say,
from Prabhupada's books and no further, what that place is, what the soul does there, and how it is reached. Vagueness here breeds either mythology or
embarrassment. Second, honesty, the TRD aim of non-duplicity: the Gita says the devotees "only want Kṛṣṇa and Kṛṣṇa's association, and nothing else"
(BG 8.15 purport). A resident who has given his life can ask himself whether that sentence is true of him, and what in his day is already that
association and what is a substitute for it. Lower batches hear that the spiritual world exists and is eternal (VL1–VL3) and learn the gradation
(SPS; trd-30); no lower batch studies the life of service in Goloka as the goal of rasa.

| BSS | "Back to Godhead" as the brahmacari's reason for everything; the dhama on earth as Goloka seen with the eyes of love (CC Adi 5.19–21) |

**Not done here:** the gradation of realms and Gopa-kumara's search (trd-30); the five rasas (trd-41); Radha-tattva (trd-45); the stages of prema (trd-46);
descriptions of Goloka's pastimes from the confidential lila texts (named in O as references, not lecture content; research/advanced-books.md §2).

## C. Central message

"After attaining Me, the great souls, who are yogīs in devotion, never return to this temporary world, which is full of miseries, because they have
attained the highest perfection" (BG 8.15); that abode "is not illumined by the sun or moon, nor by fire or electricity" (15.6); it is Krishna's own
realm, Goloka Vrindavana, "the supermost planet in the spiritual kingdom" (8.21 purport). There the land is touchstone, the trees yield all desires, the
cows give oceans of milk, "every word is a song, every gait is a dance" (Bs 5.29, 5.56), and the residents "engage in devotional service to the Lord
without desires for sense gratification" (SB 3.15.14). The goal is not a place of rest but eternal service in one's own relationship with Krishna
(CC Madhya 8.89, 8.229–230), and "Service to My lover is the home of happiness and is more sweet than direct union with Him" (CC Antya 20.60). It
"can be achieved only by surrender and by no other means" (BG 15.6 purport), and it begins now, in the service a resident does today.

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. State from BG 8.15–16, 8.21 and 15.6 with purports where the devotee goes, what it is called, and why there is no return.
2. Describe the spiritual world and Goloka only in the terms the sources give: SB 2.9.10 (no modes, no time, no maya), SB 3.15.14–19 (the residents, their singing, tulasi), Bs 5.29 and 5.56 (touchstone, desire trees, surabhi cows, song and dance, eternal present), CC Adi 5.14–22 (Vaikuntha; Krsnaloka's three divisions; Gokula identical with the dhama on earth).
3. Explain that the goal is eternal service in one's rasa, in the siddha-deha, and why the acharyas call service sweeter than union (CC Madhya 8.89, 8.229–230; SB 3.15.14; CC Antya 20.60).
4. Name the means and its proof texts (BG 18.55, 18.65–66; 15.6 purport; SB 3.15.25; SB 1.2.6).
5. Examine, privately, whether "They only want Kṛṣṇa and Kṛṣṇa's association, and nothing else" (BG 8.15 purport) is true of them, and connect today's service to the goal.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **There is a place, and it has a name.** Beyond material nature is the spiritual sky, paravyoma (CC Adi 5.14), compared to a lotus whose petals are the
   Vaikunthas and whose whorl is Krishna's abode (CC Madhya 21.7). Its highest region is Krsnaloka with three divisions, Dvaraka, Mathura and Gokula, and
   "Śrī Gokula, the highest of all, is also called Vraja, Goloka, Śvetadvīpa and Vṛndāvana" (CC Adi 5.16–17). Prabhupada names it in the Gita purports:
   "Kṛṣṇaloka, Goloka Vṛndāvana" (BG 8.15, 15.6 purports). Narada calls it "the crown of all Vaikuntha planets" (Brhad-bhagavatamrta 2.5.78).
2. **What is absent there.** No passion, no ignorance, no influence of goodness, no predominance of time, no maya (SB 2.9.10); no sun, moon, fire or
   electricity, because everything is self-luminous (BG 15.6 and purport); no birth and death, no return (BG 8.15–16, 8.21, 15.6). Even time is present only
   as an eternal now, "without past or future" (Bs 5.56).
3. **What is present there.** Land of touchstone, desire trees, surabhi cows, palaces of gems, hundreds of thousands of goddesses of fortune serving Govinda
   (Bs 5.29; BG 8.21 purport); "every word is a song, every gait is a dance, the flute is the favorite attendant" (Bs 5.56). Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati reads
   the features as forms of love: the trees bear "checkered divine love", the cows pour "the fountain of love" (Bs 5.29 purport). The residents have forms like
   the Lord's and "engage in devotional service to the Lord without desires for sense gratification" (SB 3.15.14); they sing His glories and prefer the singing
   to the fragrant breeze (3.15.17); even the flowers honour tulasi (3.15.19).
4. **What one does there.** Serves. "Those living beings who render service unto Me are eligible to be transferred to the spiritual world and attain eternal life"
   (CC Madhya 8.89); "there is no occupation but the service of the Lord, and this service is not rendered with a purpose" (SB 3.15.14 purport). In Goloka the
   service is in one's own rasa, in the siddha-deha, "to serve Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa as one of the gopīs" for those who follow the gopis (8.229–230), or as friend,
   parent or servant (trd-41). "The devotees attain Goloka ... according to the quality of rasa of the respective services" (Bs 5.56 purport). And the Lord
   Himself says the service is sweeter than union (CC Antya 20.60): the goal is not to arrive but to serve forever.
5. **Goloka and the dhama on earth.** Gokula "is manifested within the material world by the will of Lord Kṛṣṇa. It is identical to that original Gokula"
   (CC Adi 5.19); "Material eyes see it as an ordinary place" (5.20); "with the eyes of love of Godhead one can see its real identity" (5.21). Prabhupada: Vrindavana
   "ninety miles southeast of Delhi, is a replica of that supreme Goloka Vṛndāvana" (BG 8.21 purport). The resident is not far from Goloka in space; he is far in vision.
6. **Why Goloka above Vaikuntha.** Vraja, "a confidential part of Goloka Vṛndāvana", is where the pastimes of parakiya love are eternal (CC Adi 4.50 purport;
   trd-44); the opulence of Vaikuntha gives way to the sweetness of Vraja, and Brahma marvels at the fortune of its cowherds, whose friend the Supreme Brahman
   has become (SB 10.14.32). trd-30 climbed this; here it is the last word on the goal: the top is not the most powerful place but the most loving.
7. **How it is reached.** "That supreme abode can be achieved only by surrender and by no other means" (BG 15.6 purport); "Always think of Me, become My devotee,
   worship Me and offer your homage unto Me. Thus you will come to Me without fail" (18.65); "Abandon all varieties of religion and just surrender unto Me" (18.66).
   Devotional service alone gives knowledge of Krishna and entry into His kingdom (18.55), and it gives everything the other paths promise besides (8.28). The
   Bhagavatam's sign of the one who will go: "Persons whose bodily features change in ecstasy and who breathe heavily and perspire due to hearing the glories of
   the Lord are promoted to the kingdom of God, even though they do not care for meditation and other austerities" (SB 3.15.25); the service that takes one there
   is "unmotivated and uninterrupted" (SB 1.2.6).
8. **The goal is already the practice.** The residents of Vaikuntha sing the Lord's glories, serve tulasi, and want nothing for themselves (SB 3.15.14–19). A temple
   resident sings the same glories and waters the same tulasi. What differs is the motive and the vision, and both are what sadhana purifies. Hence Prabhupada: "One
   should be captivated by this information. He should desire to transfer himself to that eternal world" (BG 15.6 purport), and the devotees "only want Kṛṣṇa and
   Kṛṣṇa's association, and nothing else" (8.15 purport). That sentence is the resident's test of non-duplicity.
9. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *"Back to Godhead" means merging into light.* "Becoming Brahman does not mean that one loses his identity. Devotional service is there" (BG 18.55 purport); the brahmajyoti is the effulgence of Goloka (15.6 purport).
   - *The spiritual world is a reward for a hard life, a place to rest.* "there is no occupation but the service of the Lord" (SB 3.15.14 purport); service is sweeter than union (CC Antya 20.60).
   - *Goloka is a far planet to be reached after death.* Gokula is manifested here and is identical (CC Adi 5.19); the distance is the eyes (5.20–21). Death only removes the material covering from one who is already serving.
   - *I may describe Goloka's pastimes freely from the lila texts.* The verses on this page are the lecture content; the confidential texts are references for the preacher's own study (research/advanced-books.md §2); nothing is added from imagination.
10. **Pitch for TRD.** Read the verses; let Bs 5.56 be read whole once; keep the sequence place, absence, presence, activity, means, and end with the two
    purport sentences from BG 8.15 and 15.6 as the resident's private examination.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **The place named.** Paravyoma, the lotus, Krsnaloka's three divisions, Gokula's five names (CC Adi 5.14–18; CC Madhya 21.7); "Kṛṣṇaloka, Goloka Vṛndāvana" (BG 8.15 purport). *Transition:* what is not there.
2. **Three absences.** No modes, no time, no maya (SB 2.9.10); no sun or moon (BG 15.6); no return (8.15–16, 8.21). *Transition:* what is there.
3. **Touchstone, desire trees, surabhi cows, song and dance.** Bs 5.29, 5.56; BG 8.21 purport; every feature a form of love (Bs 5.29 purport). *Transition:* who lives there and what they do.
4. **Service without a second motive.** SB 3.15.14–19; "no occupation but the service of the Lord" (purport). *Transition:* in what body, in what relationship.
5. **One's own rasa, in the siddha-deha.** CC Madhya 8.89, 8.229–230; Bs 5.56 purport; service sweeter than union (CC Antya 20.60). *Transition:* how far away is it?
6. **Goloka is here, unseen.** CC Adi 5.19–21; BG 8.21 purport. *Transition:* why Goloka, not Vaikuntha.
7. **The most loving place, not the most powerful.** CC Adi 4.50 purport; SB 10.14.32. *Transition:* how it is reached.
8. **Only by surrender.** BG 15.6 purport; 18.55, 18.65–66, 8.28; SB 3.15.25; SB 1.2.6. *Transition:* then what about today?
9. **The goal is already the practice.** Same glories, same tulasi, different motive; "nothing else" (BG 8.15 purport); be captivated (15.6 purport).

## J. Stories and illustrations

**1. Brahma's prayer for Vraja (SB 10.14.32; BBT translation completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples, using Prabhupada's translation from CC Madhya 6.149).**
After Krishna showed Brahma the calves and boys as four-armed Visnus and then as themselves, Brahma prayed: "How greatly fortunate are Nanda Mahārāja, the
cowherd men and all the other inhabitants of Vrajabhūmi! There is no limit to their good fortune, because the Absolute Truth, the source of transcendental
bliss, the eternal Supreme Brahman, has become their friend." *Lesson:* the creator of the universe, looking at cowherds, calls them the most fortunate; the
goal is not Brahma's post but Nanda's friendship with Krishna.

**2. Gopa-kumara hears of Goloka (Brhad-bhagavatamrta 2.5.78; Desire Tree edition, OCR; the whole search is trd-30).** Having travelled to the highest realms,
Gopa-kumara is told by Narada: "very far from here a beautiful place named Goloka, the crown of all Vaikuntha planets, is splendidly manifest." *Lesson:* the
acharya's own name for the goal; everything below it, however glorious, is not the end of the search.

**3. Krishna's own words to the gopis (SB 10.82.44, as given in CC Madhya 8.89 and purport).** At Kuruksetra Krishna told the gopis: "The means of attaining My
favor is loving service unto Me, and fortunately you are all thus engaged. Those living beings who render service unto Me are eligible to be transferred to the
spiritual world and attain eternal life with knowledge and bliss." Prabhupada: "There are two important words in this verse: bhakti (devotional service) and
amṛtatva (eternal life)." *Lesson:* the Lord names the means and the end in one sentence, and the end is the means continued forever.

**Illustration (writer's, not scriptural):** a man rehearsing a song for years is not waiting to begin singing; he is singing, and the day of the performance only
changes the hall. The verses that give the point: the residents of Vaikuntha sing the Lord's glories and serve tulasi (SB 3.15.17, 3.15.19), which is what the
resident does this morning; "no occupation but the service of the Lord" there (3.15.14 purport), and the same here, when the motive is purified.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"Isn't a spiritual world with cows and trees just a projection of a farmer's heaven?"** The features are from scripture (Bs 5.29, 5.56; BG 8.21 purport), and
   Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati reads each as a form of love (Bs 5.29 purport). The material world is the reflection of the spiritual, not the other way round (BG 15.6
   purport, the "false reflection of reality"); a cow here reminds of surabhi, not surabhi of a cow.
2. **"If Goloka is here, identical with Vrindavana (CC Adi 5.19), why go anywhere?"** We do not go; we see. "Material eyes see it as an ordinary place" (5.20); "with the
   eyes of love of Godhead one can see its real identity" (5.21). The journey is the purification of vision by service, which is also the journey "after death" (BG 8.15).
3. **"What will I actually do there? Service sounds like more work."** The residents' service "is not rendered with a purpose" (SB 3.15.14 purport); it is love's own
   activity, and the Lord says it is "more sweet than direct union with Him" (CC Antya 20.60). Work is service with a motive; the goal is service without one.
4. **"Can I choose my rasa or my place in Goloka?"** "The devotees attain Goloka ... according to the quality of rasa of the respective services" (Bs 5.56 purport);
   the rasa is eternal and revealed (trd-41), and the service of the gopis is reached only by following them (CC Madhya 8.229–230; trd-44). The resident's part is
   surrender (BG 15.6 purport), not selection.
5. **"Is it not presumptuous to aim at Goloka? Should I not be content with any service anywhere?"** Prabhupada says "One should be captivated by this information. He
   should desire to transfer himself to that eternal world" (BG 15.6 purport). Humility is not aiming low; it is aiming at Krishna "and nothing else" (8.15 purport)
   and knowing that only His mercy takes one there.
6. **"May I use the Gopala-campu or Govinda-lilamrta to describe Goloka in class?"** The head allows them as references for the spiritual world at TRD (research/advanced-books.md §2);
   the lecture content stays Prabhupada's verses on this page, and anything taken from those books is cited by book, chapter and verse with the edition, in short quotes,
   after the preacher's own study. Imagination is never a source.

## L. Case studies

**Case 1 (Invented case for discussion).** A resident preaching at a college is asked, "What happens after you die, if you are a devotee?" He answers, "We go to a
spiritual planet and live happily forever." The student replies, "That's what every religion says." *Dilemma:* was the answer wrong, or thin? *Principles:* BG 8.15–16
(no return; "nothing else"); SB 3.15.14 (service without sense gratification); CC Madhya 8.89 (bhakti and amrtatva); CC Antya 20.60 (service sweeter than union).
*Questions:* What did "live happily" leave out? What makes the Gita's goal different from a reward-heaven? *Analysis:* the answer omitted the content of the goal,
eternal loving service to a person, and so sounded like every heaven. The better answer: "We go to Krishna's own abode and serve Him in love forever, wanting
nothing else; that service, not rest, is the happiness" (8.15 purport; 3.15.14). *Takeaway:* preach the goal as service to Krishna, or it is not our goal that is preached.

**Case 2 (Invented case for discussion).** A senior resident reads BG 8.15 purport, "They only want Kṛṣṇa and Kṛṣṇa's association, and nothing else", and realises he
wants his position, his projects and his comfort more than Krishna's association; he is shaken and considers leaving the ashram as a hypocrite. *Dilemma:* what does
honesty require? *Principles:* BG 15.6 purport ("if he takes to Kṛṣṇa consciousness there is a chance of gradually becoming detached"; association of devotees); SB 1.2.6
(unmotivated and uninterrupted as the standard, approached gradually); SB 3.15.14 (the same service there, purified of motive). *Questions:* Is the discovery a reason to
stop or to begin? What does Prabhupada prescribe for exactly this attachment? *Analysis:* the discovery is the beginning of non-duplicity, and the purport prescribes the
cure: stay in the devotees' association and keep serving, so that the motive is purified while the activity continues. Leaving would keep the attachment and lose the
cure. *Takeaway:* the test of 8.15 is passed by continuing to serve honestly, not by pretending to have passed it.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions**
1. Where does the devotee go, by what names, and does he return? (to Krishna's abode, Krsnaloka, Goloka Vrindavana, also called Gokula, Vraja, Svetadvipa; never returns: BG 8.15–16, 15.6; CC Adi 5.16–17)
2. Name three things absent in the spiritual world. (the modes of passion and ignorance and the influence of goodness; the predominance of time; maya; sun and moon: SB 2.9.10; BG 15.6)
3. Name four features of Goloka from the Brahma-samhita. (touchstone land, desire trees, surabhi cows, goddesses of fortune serving; every word a song, every step a dance, the flute, eternal present: Bs 5.29, 5.56)
4. What do the residents do there? (serve the Lord without desire for sense gratification, sing His glories, honour tulasi: SB 3.15.14–19)
5. How is it reached? (only by surrender; devotional service; the Lord's promise: BG 15.6 purport, 18.55, 18.65–66)
6. Where is Goloka in relation to Vrindavana on earth? (identical, manifested here by Krishna's will; seen as ordinary by material eyes and as itself by the eyes of love: CC Adi 5.19–21)

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Krishna says in the Bhagavad-gita: "After attaining Me, the great souls, who are yogīs in devotion, never return to this temporary world, which is full of miseries,
because they have attained the highest perfection" (BG 8.15); "one who attains to My abode, O son of Kuntī, never takes birth again" (8.16); "That supreme abode of
Mine is not illumined by the sun or moon, nor by fire or electricity" (15.6). Srila Prabhupada names that abode: Krsnaloka, Goloka Vrindavana, "the supermost
planet in the spiritual kingdom" (8.21 purport). Lord Caitanya described the spiritual sky to Sanatana Gosvami as a lotus whose petals are the Vaikuntha planets
and whose whorl is Krishna's abode (CC Madhya 21.7); in its highest region is Krsnaloka with three divisions, Dvaraka, Mathura and Gokula, and "Śrī Gokula, the
highest of all, is also called Vraja, Goloka, Śvetadvīpa and Vṛndāvana" (CC Adi 5.16–17).

What is there? Nothing of the modes, of time or of maya (SB 2.9.10). The land is touchstone, the trees yield all desires, the surabhi cows give limitless milk,
hundreds of thousands of goddesses of fortune serve Govinda (Bs 5.29; BG 8.21 purport); "every word is a song, every gait is a dance, the flute is the favorite
attendant", and time is "ever present and without past or future" (Bs 5.56). Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati explains that these features are forms of love (Bs 5.29
purport). The residents have forms like the Lord's and "engage in devotional service to the Lord without desires for sense gratification" (SB 3.15.14); they sing
His glories and prefer that to the sweetest breeze (3.15.17), and even the flowers honour tulasi (3.15.19). Prabhupada: "there is no occupation but the service of
the Lord, and this service is not rendered with a purpose" (3.15.14 purport). Those who follow the gopis serve Radha and Krishna in the perfected spiritual body
(CC Madhya 8.229), others as friends, parents or servants, each "according to the quality of rasa of the respective services" (Bs 5.56 purport). And the Lord
Himself said: "Service to My lover is the home of happiness and is more sweet than direct union with Him" (CC Antya 20.60).

Goloka is not far in space. "That abode is manifested within the material world by the will of Lord Kṛṣṇa. It is identical to that original Gokula" (CC Adi 5.19);
"Material eyes see it as an ordinary place" (5.20), but the eyes of love see it as it is (5.21). It is reached in one way: "That supreme abode can be achieved only
by surrender and by no other means" (BG 15.6 purport); "Always think of Me, become My devotee, worship Me and offer your homage unto Me. Thus you will come to Me
without fail" (18.65). Brahma himself called the cowherds of Vraja the most fortunate of all, because the Supreme Brahman "has become their friend" (SB 10.14.32).
Prabhupada writes that the great souls "only want Kṛṣṇa and Kṛṣṇa's association, and nothing else" (8.15 purport), and that "One should be captivated by this
information. He should desire to transfer himself to that eternal world" (15.6 purport). The service we do today, when it is done without a second motive, is already
the life of Goloka begun.

## S2. Key ideas
- The devotee goes to Krishna's own abode, Goloka Vrindavana, and never returns (BG 8.15–16, 15.6; CC Adi 5.16–17).
- There is no time, no maya, no sun or moon there; every feature is a form of love (SB 2.9.10; Bs 5.29, 5.56).
- The residents serve without any desire for sense gratification, each in his own rasa; service is sweeter than union (SB 3.15.14; CC Madhya 8.229; CC Antya 20.60).
- Goloka is manifested here as Vrindavana; the distance is the eyes (CC Adi 5.19–21).
- It is reached only by surrender; the great souls want Krishna "and nothing else" (BG 15.6 and 8.15 purports).

## S3. Verses to remember
- **BG 8.15:** "After attaining Me, the great souls ... never return to this temporary world". For me: there is an end to this, and it is Krishna.
- **SB 3.15.14:** "They all engage in devotional service to the Lord without desires for sense gratification." For me: what I will do there, and can begin now.
- **CC Adi 5.20:** "The land there is touchstone [cintāmaṇi], and the forests abound with desire trees. Material eyes see it as an ordinary place." For me: Vrindavana is Goloka; my eyes are the distance.

## S4. Words to know
- **Paravyoma:** the spiritual sky beyond material nature (CC Adi 5.14).
- **Vaikuntha:** the spiritual planets, the petals of the lotus (CC Madhya 21.7).
- **Krsnaloka, Goloka Vrindavana, Gokula, Vraja, Svetadvipa:** names of Krishna's own abode, the whorl (CC Adi 5.16–17).
- **Cintamani, kalpa-vrksa, surabhi:** touchstone, desire trees, wish-fulfilling cows of Goloka (Bs 5.29).
- **Siddha-deha:** the perfected spiritual body in which one serves there (CC Madhya 8.229 purport).
- **Tri-pad-bhuta:** the spiritual world, three fourths of the Lord's energy (CC Madhya 21.56).

## S5. Questions and answers
1. **Is the spiritual world just a nicer version of this one?** No. The modes, time and maya are absent (SB 2.9.10), there is no birth or death (BG 8.16), and this world is its reflection, not the reverse (BG 15.6 purport). Its features are forms of love (Bs 5.29 purport).
2. **What will I do there, forever?** Serve Krishna in your own relationship with Him, "without desires for sense gratification" (SB 3.15.14); the Lord says that service is "more sweet than direct union with Him" (CC Antya 20.60).
3. **Is "going back to Godhead" merging into light?** No. "Becoming Brahman does not mean that one loses his identity. Devotional service is there" (BG 18.55 purport); the light is the effulgence of Goloka (15.6 purport).
4. **How do I get there?** "only by surrender and by no other means" (BG 15.6 purport); Krishna's promise is to the one who thinks of Him, worships Him and becomes His devotee (18.65).
5. **Is Goloka far away?** It is manifested here, in Vrindavana, identical with the original (CC Adi 5.19); "Material eyes see it as an ordinary place" (5.20). The journey is the purification of vision by service.
