## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on dps-15, which places nistha after anartha-nivrtti and lists the five obstacles whose "absence… is symptomatic
of nistha". That page could give them one paragraph. A senior devotee needs more: a resident of ten years who falls
asleep in japa, or completes his rounds while planning the day, is not a beginner with a beginner's problems; he has a
precisely named obstacle, and the acharya tells him its cause and its sign.

## C. Central message

Even one who wants to practise daily cannot make his bhajana steady while five obstacles remain: "laya (drowsiness),
viksepa (distraction), apratipatti (incompetence), kasaya (bitterness) and rasasvada (taste for mundane pleasure)"
(*Madhurya-kadambini*, Fourth Shower, text 2a). The remedy is not a new technique. Krishna in the heart cleanses the
devotee "who has developed the urge to hear His messages" (SB 1.2.17, taught in dps-15), and "by regular attendance in
classes on the Bhagavatam and by rendering of service to the pure devotee, all that is troublesome to the heart is
almost completely destroyed" (SB 1.2.18).

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Define the five obstacles in the words of *Madhurya-kadambini* (Fourth Shower 2b) and say which of hearing, chanting
   and remembering each attacks most.
2. Assign each obstacle to its mode of nature as the commentary does (laya and apratipatti to ignorance; viksepa,
   kasaya and rasasvada to passion) and connect this to BG 14.8 and BG 3.37.
3. Explain why the acharya says the absence of the five is "symptomatic of nistha", and why their presence is not a
   reason to stop practising (SB 1.2.19 with MK 2c).
4. State Prabhupada's remedies: regulated sleep and eating (BG 6.16-17), hearing about Krishna as the method of
   controlling the mind (BG 6.35 purport), the six favourable and six unfavourable items (NOI 2-3).
5. Diagnose their own japa and hearing with the five, privately, and choose one remedy.

## E. Scriptural foundation

***Madhurya-kadambini* (Srila Visvanatha Cakravarti Thakura), Fourth Shower of Nectar, texts 1-2c**: acharya's book,
approved by the head (28 Oct 2026). Edition cited: *Madhurya-kadambini*, with the *Bhavanuvada* and
*Piyusa-varsini-vrtti* commentary of Bhaktivedanta Narayana Gosvami Maharaja, English, first edition, Gaudiya Vedanta
Publications, August 2018 (ISBN 978-1-63316-169-6); text in `research/books/`. Verified against the edition text
(30 Oct 2026). Supporting source for the structure; the principle rests on SB 1.2.17-19 and the Gita.
- **Text 2a (the five named).** "Even when someone desires to daily perform the limbs (angas) of bhakti, such as
  sravana and kirtana, if he is still in the stage of being bound by anarthas, then he will be unable to make his
  performance of bhakti steady on account of the following five almost irrepressible obstacles: laya (drowsiness),
  viksepa (distraction), apratipatti (incompetence), kasaya (bitterness) and rasasvada (taste for mundane pleasure)."
  "…we should understand that the absence of these five obstacles is symptomatic of nistha." The commentary quotes
  SB 4.24.59 as the picture of a sadhaka free of all five.
- **Text 2b (definitions, the author's own).** "(1) Increasingly oppressive drowsiness while chanting, more while
  hearing, and even more while remembering, is called laya. (2) Reminiscing about various topics related to mundane
  life at the time of hearing, chanting or remembering is called viksepa. (3) Even in the absence of laya and viksepa,
  sometimes we may still find that we are simply unable to properly hear, chant or remember. This obstacle is known as
  apratipatti. (4) The various impressions of anger, greed and false ego that manifest while performing sadhana of the
  limbs of bhakti, such as hearing, chanting or remembering, is called kasaya. (5) The inability to absorb the mind in
  the angas of bhakti such as kirtana when the opportunity for material sense pleasure arises is known as rasasvada."
- **Text 2b, commentary (modes and causes).** Laya: "Such sleepiness manifests from the mode of ignorance… In fact, this
  obstacle arises because relish of the names, forms, qualities and pastimes of Bhagavan is absent." Viksepa: "the
  sadhaka may hold his japa-mala in his hand and continue chanting his fixed number of rounds, and at the same time
  engage in gossiping with others about all kinds of mundane things… Since the sadhaka's daily goal is merely the
  completion of his fixed number of rounds, he remains deprived of truly tasting the nectar of the holy name… Such
  wandering is the work of the mode of passion." Apratipatti: "a lack of enthusiasm appears within the sadhaka's mind,
  creating lethargy in bhajana. In fact, enthusiasm (utsaha) has been called the life of sraddha. This apratipatti is
  born from the mode of ignorance." Kasaya: "anger, even at the slightest provocation; a desire to relish sweet,
  tasteful preparations; greed for wealth; and false ego and vanity in regard to one's body and possessions."
  Rasasvada: "The mind cannot experience material pleasure and simultaneously be absorbed in hearing, chanting and
  remembering… both kasaya and rasasvada are the work of the mode of passion." "It is by these symptoms that the
  sadhakas can ascertain for themselves whether or not they have entered nisthita-bhakti."
- **Text 2c.** The author reads SB 1.2.19 to answer whether remaining anarthas obstruct bhakti after nistha: the
  "effects of passion and ignorance" may persist up to bhava "without obstructing bhakti" (Bhavanuvada).
- **Application (preacher's):** the five are a mirror for the sadhaka "for themselves", as the text says; never a
  grading of others.

**SB 1.2.18**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/18/
> "By regular attendance in classes on the Bhāgavatam and by rendering of service to the pure devotee, all that is troublesome to the heart is almost completely destroyed, and loving service unto the Personality of Godhead… is established as an irrevocable fact."
- **Purport point:** "Here is the remedy for eliminating all inauspicious things within the heart, which are considered
  to be obstacles in the path of self-realization. The remedy is the association of the Bhāgavatas." Both the book and
  the devotee Bhagavata "can be good enough to eliminate the obstacles."
- **Use:** in the talk: Prabhupada calls the heart's troubles "obstacles" and names the remedy. dps-15 used the verse
  for the cause of nistha; here the point is the remedy for the five.

**SB 1.2.19**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/19/
> "As soon as irrevocable loving service is established in the heart, the effects of nature's modes of passion and ignorance, such as lust, desire and hankering, disappear from the heart…"
- **Purport point:** "the diseased activities are expressed in the form of lust, desire, hankering, inactivity,
  foolishness and sleep. The effect of devotional service becomes manifest by complete elimination of these effects of
  passion and ignorance."
- **Use:** in the talk: Prabhupada's own list, "inactivity, foolishness and sleep" with "lust, desire, hankering",
  matches the five: ignorance (laya, apratipatti) and passion (viksepa, kasaya, rasasvada).

**SB 4.24.59**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/24/59/
> "The devotee whose heart has been completely cleansed by the process of devotional service and who is favored by Bhaktidevī does not become bewildered by the external energy, which is just like a dark well. Being completely cleansed of all material contamination in this way, a devotee is able to understand very happily Your name, fame, form, activities, etc."
- **Context:** Lord Siva's prayer to Sankarsana; quoted by MK's commentary as the devotee free of the five.
- **Purport point:** opens with SB 3.25.25, *satam prasangan mama virya-samvido*: by association with devotees, hearing
  becomes pleasing to heart and ear, and faith, attraction and devotion follow. *Use:* in the talk.

**BG 14.8**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/14/8/
> "O son of Bharata, know that the mode of darkness, born of ignorance, is the delusion of all embodied living entities. The results of this mode are madness, indolence and sleep, which bind the conditioned soul."
- **Use:** in the talk: the Gita's own account of the mode from which laya and apratipatti come.

**BG 3.37**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/37/
> "It is lust only, Arjuna, which is born of contact with the material mode of passion and later transformed into wrath…"
- **Purport point:** "when lust is unsatisfied, it turns into wrath"; "if… the mode of passion, instead of being
  degraded into the mode of ignorance, is elevated to the mode of goodness by the prescribed method of living and
  acting", one is saved. *Use:* in the talk: kasaya (anger, greed, pride) and rasasvada are passion; the Gita gives
  their root.

**BG 2.44**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/44/
> "In the minds of those who are too attached to sense enjoyment and material opulence… the resolute determination for devotional service to the Supreme Lord does not take place."
- **Use:** in the talk: rasasvada stated by Krishna: attachment to enjoyment and resolute devotion exclude each other.

**BG 6.33, 6.34, 6.35**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/33/ (and /34/, /35/)
> 6.34: "The mind is restless, turbulent, obstinate and very strong, O Kṛṣṇa, and to subdue it, I think, is more difficult than controlling the wind."
> 6.35: "…it is undoubtedly very difficult to curb the restless mind, but it is possible by suitable practice and by detachment."
- **Purport point (6.35):** "By the practice of Kṛṣṇa consciousness, however, one engages in nine types of devotional
  service to the Lord. The first and foremost of such devotional engagements is hearing about Kṛṣṇa. This is a very
  powerful transcendental method for purging the mind of all misgivings. The more one hears about Kṛṣṇa, the more one
  becomes enlightened and detached from everything that draws the mind away from Kṛṣṇa."
- **Use:** in the talk: Arjuna's complaint is viksepa; Krishna's answer is practice and detachment; Prabhupada names the
  practice: hearing.

**BG 6.19**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/19/
> "As a lamp in a windless place does not waver, so the transcendentalist, whose mind is controlled, remains always steady in his meditation on the transcendent Self."
- **Use:** in the talk: the picture of nistha, the state without the five.

**BG 6.16, 6.17**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/16/ (and /17/)
> 6.16: "There is no possibility of one's becoming a yogī, O Arjuna, if one eats too much or eats too little, sleeps too much or does not sleep enough."
> 6.17: "He who is regulated in his habits of eating, sleeping, recreation and work can mitigate all material pains by practicing the yoga system."
- **Purport point (6.16):** "Regulation of diet and sleep is recommended herein for the yogīs. Too much eating means
  eating more than is required to keep the body and soul together." Read the whole purport, which gives the measure of
  sleep; trd-20 teaches it in full.
- **Use:** in the talk, for laya: the Gita's measure, not research, is the standard (head, 30 Oct 2026).

**NOI 2**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/2/
> "One's devotional service is spoiled when he becomes too entangled in the following six activities: (1) eating more than necessary or collecting more funds than required; (2) over-endeavoring for mundane things…; (3) talking unnecessarily about mundane subject matters; (4) practicing the scriptural rules and regulations only for the sake of following them…; (5) associating with worldly-minded persons…; (6) being greedy for mundane achievements."
- **Purport point:** "Another impediment is prajalpa, unnecessary talking. When we mix with a few friends, we immediately
  begin unnecessary talking, sounding just like croaking toads."
- **Use:** in the talk: *atyahara* feeds laya; *prajalpa* feeds viksepa; *niyamagraha* (rules for their own sake) is
  the mood of apratipatti; *laulya* is rasasvada.

**NOI 3**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/3/
> "There are six principles favorable to the execution of pure devotional service: (1) being enthusiastic, (2) endeavoring with confidence, (3) being patient, (4) acting according to regulative principles…, (5) abandoning the association of nondevotees, and (6) following in the footsteps of the previous ācāryas."
- **Purport point:** "Without enthusiasm, one cannot be successful… Endeavor executed with intelligence in Kṛṣṇa
  consciousness is called utsāha, or enthusiasm."
- **Use:** in the talk: the direct answer to apratipatti, whose sign MK gives as "a lack of enthusiasm".

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** Unsteady practice is not one problem but five, and they are different in kind. Two come from
   ignorance (laya, apratipatti), three from passion (viksepa, kasaya, rasasvada). Prabhupada's purport on SB 1.2.19
   gives the same two groups: "inactivity, foolishness and sleep" and "lust, desire, hankering". A remedy for the wrong
   one does nothing: more sleep cures nothing in a man whose problem is gossip on the japa mat.
2. **Laya.** Drowsiness that grows from chanting to hearing to remembering, because the subtler the limb, the less the
   sleepy mind can hold it. The commentary names the real cause: "relish… of Bhagavan is absent". Remedies in the
   purports: the measure of sleep and food in BG 6.16-17 (trd-20 gives it), and the restoration of relish by hearing
   (SB 1.2.18).
3. **Viksepa.** The mind on mundane topics during practice. The commentary's example is exact and uncomfortable: beads
   in hand, rounds counted, gossip continuing; "the sadhaka's daily goal is merely the completion of his fixed number of
   rounds". NOI 2's *prajalpa* is its food. Krishna's remedy: practice and detachment (BG 6.35); Prabhupada names the
   practice as hearing about Krishna.
4. **Apratipatti.** Neither asleep nor distracted, yet "simply unable". Its sign is lost enthusiasm, and "enthusiasm has
   been called the life of sraddha". NOI 3's *utsaha* is the remedy; Prabhupada defines it as "endeavor executed with
   intelligence in Krsna consciousness", that is, a positive act, not waiting for feeling. trd-52 treats its long form,
   burnout.
5. **Kasaya.** Old impressions surfacing in practice: "anger, even at the slightest provocation; a desire to relish
   sweet, tasteful preparations; greed for wealth; and false ego and vanity". BG 3.37 gives their root in lust born of
   passion. The senior meets kasaya in japa as the memory of an insult, or pride in his own standing.
6. **Rasasvada.** When sense pleasure is available, the mind will not stay in kirtana. BG 2.44: in minds attached to
   enjoyment "the resolute determination for devotional service… does not take place". NOI 2's *laulya* (greed for
   mundane achievements) is the same disease.
7. **Why their presence is not despair.** MK text 2c reads SB 1.2.19: effects of passion and ignorance may remain even
   after nistha "without obstructing bhakti". The five are the measure of unsteadiness, not of failure. SB 1.2.18 says
   the heart's troubles are "almost completely destroyed" by hearing and service: *almost*.
8. **Connections.** dps-15 placed the five on the stage map. trd-20 gives BG 6.16-17 in full. trd-52 takes apratipatti's
   long form. trd-49's weeds (profit, adoration, importance) are kasaya's content in a senior. trd-08 and trd-36 treat
   the holy name's quality, which is what viksepa and laya rob.
9. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *I need a new japa technique.* The acharya and the purports prescribe hearing, service, regulation and enthusiasm,
     not a method. Techniques (posture, time, place) serve these; they do not replace them.
   - *My sleepiness is only physical.* BG 6.16-17 and BG 14.8 agree that it has a physical side and a modal one; MK adds
     the devotional one: absence of relish. Regulate the body; also hear.
   - *Completing sixteen rounds is the goal.* The commentary says this very attitude is viksepa's sign.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes).** Say: "Everyone here chants sixteen rounds and hears the morning class. So let
us ask the question a beginner cannot ask: why, after all these years, does the practice still stall? Visvanatha
Cakravarti Thakura says there are five reasons, and he names them. Tonight we learn his five names so precisely that
each of us can diagnose his own japa tomorrow morning." Read MK text 2a (the five named) from the edition. Then: "The
remedy, we will see, is the one Prabhupada gives everywhere: hearing, service, regulation, enthusiasm. But the
diagnosis comes first."

**H.2 Opening questions:**
1. "In this morning's japa, which happened to you: sleep, wandering thoughts, a dull inability, a surge of anger or
   pride, or the pull of something you were looking forward to?"
2. "When you hear the Bhagavatam class, at what minute do you usually lose it, and to what?"
3. "What is the goal of your japa: the number, or the name?"

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Five obstacles, two modes.** MK 2a-2b; SB 1.2.19 purport. Plain explanation: sleep and dullness are ignorance;
   wandering, anger-greed-pride and the pull of pleasure are passion. Example: two residents late to mangala-arati, one
   from sleep, one from a conversation; different obstacles. Transition: each in turn.
2. **Laya: relish is absent.** MK 2b commentary; BG 14.8; BG 6.16-17. Example (preacher's application): the resident
   who sleeps in class but not in the committee meeting. Transition: dullness without sleep.
3. **Apratipatti: enthusiasm, "the life of sraddha", is low.** MK 2b; NOI 3. Example: rounds done, class attended,
   nothing received. Transition: the passionate three.
4. **Viksepa: the beads in hand, the mind elsewhere.** MK 2b (the gossip example); BG 6.33-35; NOI 2 (prajalpa).
   Example: planning the festival during japa. Transition: when the mind brings not plans but feelings.
5. **Kasaya: old impressions surface.** MK 2b; BG 3.37. Example: an insult of last week replayed through four rounds.
   Transition: and when pleasure is near.
6. **Rasasvada: the mind cannot be in two pleasures.** MK 2b; BG 2.44; NOI 2 (laulya). Example: the phone within
   reach. Transition: one remedy.
7. **The common remedy: hear, serve, regulate, be enthusiastic.** SB 1.2.18 purport; BG 6.35 purport; SB 4.24.59;
   BG 6.19. Transition to M.

## J. Stories and illustrations

- **Arjuna's complaint and Krishna's answer (BG 6.33-35).** Arjuna, "a practical man", tells Krishna the yoga system
  described "appears impractical and unendurable to me, for the mind is restless and unsteady", harder to control than
  the wind. Krishna agrees it is difficult and says it is possible "by suitable practice and by detachment".
  Devotional lesson (the purport's own): the practice is Krishna consciousness, and "the first and foremost… is hearing
  about Krsna".
- **Lord Siva's prayer (SB 4.24.59).** Lord Siva, instructing the Pracetas, prays to the Lord describing the devotee
  whose heart is cleansed and who "does not become bewildered by the external energy, which is just like a dark well".
  Devotional lesson: the commentary of MK takes this devotee as the one free of all five obstacles; the state is real
  and described.
- **The japa-mala and the gossip (MK 2b commentary).** Not a story but the acharya's own illustration, given in his
  words (E). Devotional lesson: the number is not the name.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. *"I sleep in japa however much I rest. What then?"* Follow the Gita's measure of sleep and food first (BG 6.16-17;
   trd-20) and keep it for a month; if drowsiness remains only in practice and not in other work, the commentary's
   diagnosis applies: relish is absent, and the remedy is hearing (SB 1.2.18). If you are always unwell, see a doctor.
2. *"Isn't counting rounds what Prabhupada asked?"* Yes, sixteen rounds is the vow. The commentary does not criticise
   the count; it criticises making the count the goal while gossiping. Both the number and the attention are the vow.
3. *"Anger in japa: should I stop and deal with the person?"* Kasaya is an impression surfacing; note it, return to
   the name (the practice of BG 6.35), and deal with the relationship later in the Vaisnava way (trd-19). Do not leave
   the mat to argue.
4. *"My apratipatti has lasted months. Is this burnout?"* Possibly; trd-52 treats it. Begin with NOI 3's *utsaha* as
   an act of intelligence, not a feeling, and with regular hearing. If low mood affects sleep, appetite and work for
   weeks, also speak to a doctor; Tele-MANAS 14416 is available in India.
5. *"Can I use the five to help a junior?"* Teach him the five so he can "ascertain for himself", as the text says.
   Do not diagnose him aloud; that becomes fault-finding (dps-07) and, with a senior, worse (trd-50).

## L. Case studies

**Case 1 (Invented case for discussion).** A resident of nine years chants his rounds while walking the temple
grounds, answering greetings, noting repairs. He finishes early and feels efficient. *Principles:* MK 2b (viksepa, the
gossip example); NOI 2 (prajalpa); BG 6.35. *Questions:* Is his vow kept? What is his goal? *Analysis:* the number is
kept; the name is not heard; the commentary says he "remains deprived of truly tasting the nectar". *Takeaway:* one
hour, one place, beads only; and an extra half hour of Bhagavatam reading, since hearing is the practice Prabhupada
names.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions (expected answers in brackets):**
1. Name the five obstacles. [Laya, viksepa, apratipatti, kasaya, rasasvada.]
2. Which two come from the mode of ignorance, according to the commentary? [Laya and apratipatti.]
3. What does the commentary say is the real cause of laya? [Absence of relish of the Lord's names, forms, qualities
   and pastimes.]
4. What has enthusiasm "been called", in the passage on apratipatti? [The life of sraddha.]
5. What practice does Prabhupada name first for controlling the restless mind (BG 6.35 purport)? [Hearing about Krishna.]
6. According to SB 1.2.18, what is the remedy for all that is troublesome to the heart? [Regular hearing of the
   Bhagavatam and service to the pure devotee.]

**M.2 Activity (25 min): the private diagnosis.** Hand out the five definitions (MK 2b, in the edition's words). Each
resident reviews his last seven days of japa and hearing and writes, privately, which obstacle appeared most, at what
time of day, and in which limb (chanting, hearing, remembering). Then he writes one remedy from the page (regulated
sleep; a fixed japa place; a hearing habit; an act of enthusiasm; removing the phone). Pairs discuss remedies only.

**M.3 Reflection.** "Is the goal of my japa the number or the name?"

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Many devotees chant their rounds and hear the class every day and still find their practice unsteady. Srila Visvanatha
Cakravarti Thakura, in *Madhurya-kadambini*, says that even one who wants to practise daily cannot be steady while five
obstacles remain: "laya (drowsiness), viksepa (distraction), apratipatti (incompetence), kasaya (bitterness) and
rasasvada (taste for mundane pleasure)". He defines them exactly. Laya is drowsiness that grows "while chanting, more
while hearing, and even more while remembering". Viksepa is thinking of mundane topics during practice; his example is
a devotee holding his beads and finishing his rounds while gossiping, for whom "the daily goal is merely the completion
of his fixed number of rounds". Apratipatti is being "simply unable" to hear, chant or remember even without sleep or
distraction; its sign is lost enthusiasm, and "enthusiasm (utsaha) has been called the life of sraddha". Kasaya is old
impressions surfacing in practice: "anger, even at the slightest provocation", greed, pride. Rasasvada is the mind's
inability to stay in kirtana "when the opportunity for material sense pleasure arises".

The commentary groups them by the modes of nature: laya and apratipatti come from ignorance, which the Gita says
produces "madness, indolence and sleep" (BG 14.8); viksepa, kasaya and rasasvada come from passion, whose root is lust
turning to anger (BG 3.37). Srila Prabhupada's purport on SB 1.2.19 gives the same two groups: "inactivity, foolishness
and sleep" with "lust, desire, hankering". Arjuna himself complained that the mind is harder to control than the wind,
and Krishna answered that it is possible "by suitable practice and by detachment" (BG 6.34-35); Prabhupada says the
first practice is hearing about Krishna.

The remedy for all five is the one the Bhagavatam gives: "By regular attendance in classes on the Bhagavatam and by
rendering of service to the pure devotee, all that is troublesome to the heart is almost completely destroyed"
(SB 1.2.18). Regulated sleep and eating (BG 6.16-17), enthusiasm as an act of intelligence (NOI 3) and avoiding idle
talk and greed (NOI 2) support it. The acharya says the absence of the five is the sign of steadiness, nistha, and that
sadhakas can use them "to ascertain for themselves" where they stand. They are a mirror for oneself, never a label for
others.

## S2. Key ideas
- Unsteady practice has five names: laya, viksepa, apratipatti, kasaya, rasasvada (*Madhurya-kadambini*, Fourth Shower).
- Two come from ignorance (sleep, dullness), three from passion (wandering, anger-greed-pride, pull of pleasure).
- The count is not the goal; making it the goal is itself the sign of viksepa.
- The remedy is hearing the Bhagavatam and serving devotees (SB 1.2.18), with regulation (BG 6.16-17) and enthusiasm
  (NOI 3).
- Their absence is the sign of nistha; they are diagnosed privately, for oneself.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **SB 1.2.18**: hearing and service destroy "all that is troublesome to the heart". For me: when practice stalls, I
  hear more.
- **BG 6.35**: the mind is controlled "by suitable practice and by detachment". For me: returning the mind is the
  practice, not a failure.
- **BG 6.16**: no yoga for one who "sleeps too much or does not sleep enough". For me: I keep the Gita's measure.

## S4. Words to know
- **Laya:** drowsiness in practice.
- **Viksepa:** distraction to mundane topics.
- **Apratipatti:** inability or dullness; lost enthusiasm.
- **Kasaya:** old impressions of anger, greed and pride surfacing.
- **Rasasvada:** the pull of available sense pleasure.
- **Nistha:** steadiness; the stage at which the five are almost gone.
- **Utsaha:** enthusiasm; "the life of sraddha".

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *I sleep in japa however much I rest.* Keep the Gita's measure of sleep and food for a month (BG 6.16-17). If
   drowsiness comes only in practice, the cause is absent relish; hear more (SB 1.2.18). If you are always unwell, see a
   doctor.
2. *Isn't counting rounds what Prabhupada asked?* Yes; the vow is sixteen rounds. The acharya criticises making the
   count the goal while the mind gossips, not the count itself.
3. *Anger comes up in japa.* Note it, return to the name (BG 6.35), and settle the relationship later in the Vaisnava
   way. Do not leave the mat to argue.
4. *My dullness has lasted months.* Begin with enthusiasm as an act (NOI 3) and regular hearing; trd-52 treats long
   loss of enthusiasm. If low mood affects sleep, appetite and work for weeks, also speak to a doctor (Tele-MANAS 14416).

## S6. Check your understanding
1. Name the five obstacles.
2. Which two come from the mode of ignorance?
3. What does the commentary say is the real cause of laya?
4. What does the acharya say about a devotee who finishes his rounds while gossiping?
5. What does SB 1.2.18 give as the remedy for all that troubles the heart?

**Answers:** 1. Laya, viksepa, apratipatti, kasaya, rasasvada. 2. Laya and apratipatti. 3. Absence of relish of the
Lord's names, forms, qualities and pastimes. 4. His goal has become the number; he "remains deprived of truly tasting the
nectar of the holy name" (viksepa). 5. Regular hearing of the Bhagavatam and service to the pure devotee (SB 1.2.18).
