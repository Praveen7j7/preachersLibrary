## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on trd-23: this is the abhidheya, the second of the three subjects, and on trd-24 to trd-27, which established whom
we serve. Builds on dps-05, dps-27 to dps-29 (the sixty-four limbs in practice), dps-30 (spontaneous service), dps-12 to
dps-18 (the stages) and sps-09 (the nine processes): those pages teach *how* to practise; this page gives the siddhanta of
*what* bhakti is and *why* it is the only means, which no lower batch takes as a subject. Builds on trd-22, where SB 7.5.23–24
was used for the limbs; here the same passage is read, in *Bhakti-sandarbha*, for the definition of pure service.

Residents live inside devotional service. The danger is not that they do not practise but that practice becomes mixed, with
desire for position, with pride of knowledge, with the mood of "my work", while the words stay devotional. The TRD aim of
non-duplicity needs the exact definition: "devoid of all material desires, knowledge obtained by monistic philosophy, and fruitive action" (CC Madhya 19.167). And students ask daily whether good work, meditation or knowledge is enough; the Lord's own answer is here.

## C. Central message

Pure devotional service means serving Krishna favourably, as He desires, "devoid of all material desires, knowledge obtained by monistic philosophy, and fruitive action" (CC Madhya 19.167). It is the only means that attains Him: "Only by practicing unalloyed devotional service with full faith in Me can one obtain Me" (SB 11.14.21); "One can understand Me as I am, as the Supreme Personality of Godhead, only by devotional service" (BG 18.55). Knowledge, work and yoga depend on it (CC Madhya 22.17). It comes by the mercy of a devotee and begins with surrender.

## D. Learning outcomes

Residents should be able to:
1. Give Rupa Gosvami's definition as Lord Caitanya quotes and explains it (CC Madhya 19.167–169), naming the three things
   excluded and the one thing required (favourable service as Krishna desires).
2. Give the Bhagavatam's definitions: unmotivated and uninterrupted (SB 1.2.6); flowing like the Ganges, without reason and
   without interruption, refusing even liberation (SB 3.29.11–13).
3. Explain Krishna's words to Uddhava: three paths, who is fit for which, and why bhakti alone brings Him under control and
   attains Him (SB 11.20.6–8; 11.14.20–21); and Lord Caitanya's conclusion that the other paths depend on bhakti
   (CC Madhya 22.17, 22.21).
4. Show where bhakti comes from (the mercy of a devotee, CC Madhya 22.45–54) and how it begins (the six divisions of
   surrender, CC Madhya 22.100–101; BG 18.66).
5. Say how *Bhakti-sandarbha* orders this (anucchedas 1, 170, 235–237) and apply the definition to their own service
   honestly.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**CC Madhya 19.167–169**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/19/167/
> 19.167: "When first-class devotional service develops, one must be devoid of all material desires, knowledge obtained by monistic philosophy, and fruitive action. The devotee must constantly serve Kṛṣṇa favorably, as Kṛṣṇa desires."
> 19.168: "A pure devotee must not cherish any desire other than to serve Kṛṣṇa. He should not offer worship to the demigods or to mundane personalities. He should not cultivate artificial knowledge, which is devoid of Kṛṣṇa consciousness, and he should not engage himself in anything other than Kṛṣṇa conscious activities. One must engage all one's purified senses in the service of the Lord. This is the favorable execution of Kṛṣṇa conscious activities."
> 19.169: "These activities are called śuddha-bhakti, pure devotional service. If one renders such pure devotional service, he develops his original love for Kṛṣṇa in due course of time."
- **Context:** Lord Caitanya teaching Rupa Gosvami at Prayaga; the verse is BRS 1.1.11 (*anyabhilasita-sunyam*).
- **Purport point (19.167):** "The criterion is that a devotee must know what Kṛṣṇa wants him to do." Krishna wants everyone to think of Him, become His devotee, worship Him and offer obeisances (BG 9.34, 18.65); "Kṛṣṇa wants everyone to surrender unto Him, and devotional service means preaching this gospel all over the world." "one who spreads this philosophy of Kṛṣṇa consciousness for the benefit of all conditioned souls in the universe is perfect in pure devotional service."
- **Purport point (19.169):** bhakti develops "under the directions of a pure devotee, the spiritual master, and in accordance with the Vedic directions given in the Pañcarātra and Bhāgavata systems."
- **Use:** in the talk. The definition; have 19.167 on a slide.

**SB 1.2.6–8**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/6/
> 1.2.6: "The supreme occupation [dharma] for all humanity is that by which men can attain to loving devotional service unto the transcendent Lord. Such devotional service must be unmotivated and uninterrupted to completely satisfy the self."
> 1.2.7: "By rendering devotional service unto the Personality of Godhead, Śrī Kṛṣṇa, one immediately acquires causeless knowledge and detachment from the world."
> 1.2.8: "The occupational activities a man performs according to his own position are only so much useless labor if they do not provoke attraction for the message of the Personality of Godhead."
- **Purport point (1.2.6):** two paths, enjoyment and renunciation; devotional service is "the superior quality of occupation. This is sometimes polluted when mixed with the inferior quality. For example, adoption of devotional service for material gain is certainly an obstruction to the progressive path of renunciation."
- **Purport point (1.2.7):** "The bhakti cult is the topmost of all transcendental activities, and therefore it is simultaneously sublime and easy." It is "open for all living beings, including the śūdras, vaiśyas, women and even those lower than the lowborn śūdras". Knowledge and detachment "are all corollary factors following the pure and scientific bhakti cult."
- **Use:** in the talk. The Bhagavatam's definition and its two fruits.

**SB 3.29.11–13**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/29/11-12/
> 3.29.11–12: "The manifestation of unadulterated devotional service is exhibited when one's mind is at once attracted to hearing the transcendental name and qualities of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, who is residing in everyone's heart. Just as the water of the Ganges flows naturally down towards the ocean, such devotional ecstasy, uninterrupted by any material condition, flows towards the Supreme Lord."
> 3.29.13: "A pure devotee does not accept any kind of liberation — sālokya, sārṣṭi, sāmīpya, sārūpya or ekatva — even though they are offered by the Supreme Personality of Godhead."
- **Context:** Lord Kapila to Devahuti.
- **Purport point (3.29.11–12):** "The word ahaitukī means 'without reason.' A pure devotee does not render loving service to the Personality of Godhead for any cause or for any benefit, material or spiritual. This is the first symptom of unalloyed devotion. Anyābhilāṣitā-śūnyam: he has no desire to fulfill by rendering devotional service."
- **Purport point (3.29.13):** Lord Caitanya's Siksastaka prayer is quoted: "All I want from You is that in life after life I may remain a pure devotee at Your lotus feet." Merging "is beyond the dream of a pure devotee"; the happiness of kaivalya "is considered by the pure devotee to be hellish."
- **Use:** in the talk. Prabhupada links the Bhagavatam's definition to Rupa Gosvami's words.

**SB 11.14.20–21**: Verified · BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/14/20/
> 11.14.20: "My dear Uddhava, the unalloyed devotional service rendered to Me by My devotees brings Me under their control. I cannot be thus controlled by those engaged in mystic yoga, Sāṅkhya philosophy, pious work, Vedic study, austerity or renunciation."
> 11.14.21: "Only by practicing unalloyed devotional service with full faith in Me can one obtain Me, the Supreme Personality of Godhead. I am naturally dear to My devotees, who take Me as the only goal of their loving service. By engaging in such pure devotional service, even the dog-eaters can purify themselves from the contamination of their low birth."
- **Purport point (11.14.20):** "a devotee should simply depend on Kṛṣṇa and should not unnecessarily complicate his loving service with tendencies toward fruitive work or mental speculation." The cowherd boys entered Aghasura's mouth "completely confident in their friendship with Lord Kṛṣṇa".
- **Purport point (11.14.21):** such service "must have Lord Kṛṣṇa as the only goal ( man-niṣṭhā ), must be rendered with full faith ( śraddhayā ), and must be unalloyed, or without any selfish motivation ( ekayā )."
- **Use:** in the talk. Krishna's own statement that bhakti alone attains Him.

**SB 11.20.6–8**: Verified · BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/20/6/
> 11.20.6: "My dear Uddhava, because I desire that human beings may achieve perfection, I have presented three paths of advancement — the path of knowledge, the path of work and the path of devotion. Besides these three there is absolutely no other means of elevation."
> 11.20.7: "Among these three paths, jñāna-yoga, the path of philosophical speculation, is recommended for those who are disgusted with material life and are thus detached from ordinary, fruitive activities. Those who are not disgusted with material life, having many desires yet to fulfill, should seek perfection through the path of karma-yoga."
> 11.20.8: "If somehow or other by good fortune one develops faith in hearing and chanting My glories, such a person, being neither disgusted with nor very much attached to material life, should achieve perfection through the path of loving devotion to Me."
- **Purport point (11.20.6):** "philosophical speculation and regulated pious work can never be considered equal to pure love of Godhead"; "One cannot achieve the same result by mere speculation or piety that one achieves by directly surrendering to the Personality of Godhead".
- **Purport point (11.20.8):** "If somehow or other one gets the association of pure devotees of the Lord and hears from them the transcendental message of Lord Kṛṣṇa, then one has the chance to become a devotee of the Lord."
- **Use:** in the talk. Jiva Gosvami quotes 11.20.6 at the head of his proof that bhakti is the best path (anuccheda 170).

**BG 18.55 and 18.66**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/55/
> 18.55: "One can understand Me as I am, as the Supreme Personality of Godhead, only by devotional service. And when one is in full consciousness of Me by such devotion, he can enter into the kingdom of God."
> 18.66: "Abandon all varieties of religion and just surrender unto Me. I shall deliver you from all sinful reactions. Do not fear."
- **Purport point (18.55):** "No one can understand God simply by erudite scholarship or mental speculation. Only one who is actually engaged in Kṛṣṇa consciousness and devotional service can understand what Kṛṣṇa is." - **Purport point (18.66):** the Lord "should give up all the processes that have been explained to him; he should simply surrender to Kṛṣṇa." > 22.5: "A human being's activities should be centered only on devotional service to Lord Kṛṣṇa. That is the verdict of all Vedic literatures, and all saintly people have firmly concluded this."
> 22.17: "Devotional service to Kṛṣṇa is the chief function of the living entity. There are different methods for the liberation of the conditioned soul — karma, jñāna, yoga and bhakti — but all the others are dependent on bhakti."
> 22.21: "Speculative knowledge alone, without devotional service, is not able to give liberation. On the other hand, even without knowledge one can obtain liberation if one engages in the Lord's devotional service."
> 22.33: "One is immediately freed from the clutches of māyā if he seriously and sincerely says, 'My dear Lord Kṛṣṇa, although I have forgotten You for so many long years in the material world, today I am surrendering unto You. - **Purport point (22.21):** "one falls down again onto the material platform because considering oneself the Supreme Person, the Supreme Absolute Truth, is offensive." Bilvamangala's prayer is quoted: liberation "stands before the devotee with folded hands waiting to render service."
- **Purport point (22.25):** "every living entity is eternally a servant of Kṛṣṇa"; if "he gets in touch with a bona fide spiritual master, abides by his order and serves him, engaging other conditioned souls in the Lord's service, he then attains liberation and Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa's shelter."

**CC Madhya 22.45, 22.51, 22.54**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/22/51/

- **Purport point (22.45):** Bhaktivinoda Thakura's three kinds of pious activity; only *bhakty-unmukhi sukrti*, pious acts that awaken Krishna consciousness, is real fortune, and it "is attainable only when one comes in contact with a devotee."

- **Anuccheda 1, the turn to abhidheya.** After summarising the four sambandha books, Jiva Gosvami asks what is to be done and
  what is the goal, and answers that, since the soul's disease is aversion to the Lord, "that relationship is one of turning one's face of the Lord and it is characterised by service to the Lord. From that service knowledge of the Lord becomes manifested." He quotes SB 1.1.2 for how this is received by hearing, and SB 11.2.37 for the diagnosis: fear comes from absorption in what is "separate from Krsna", and "one who is actually learned and advanced worships the Supreme Personality of Godhead as his spiritual master, worshipful Deity, and source of life."
- **Anuccheda 47.** He quotes SB 3.24.44: "Therefore persons whose minds are fixed on the Lord engage in the intensive practice of devotional service. That is the only means for attainment of the final perfection of life."

On the nine processes: "It is not necessary to perform all nine kinds of devotional service. By performing any one of them, any combination of them, or all of them with faith and love one will attain the desired result."

By performing pure devotional service one becomes free of his aversion to the Supreme Lord and instead comes to love Him." He names three ways of approach, knowledge, devotion and offering the fruits of work, and says: "These three are the only ways. There are no others." Then he quotes SB 11.20.6.
- **Anuccheda 235, two kinds of bhakti.** "Devotional service is of two main kinds: 1. vaidhi, and 2. raganuga. In vaidhi-bhakti one performs devotional service by following the rules spoken in the scriptures." Vaidhi is itself of two kinds: one concerned with what should and should not be done (SB 1.2.14 quoted), one concerned "with worship, vows, and similar activities" (SB 11.27.53 quoted).
- **Anucchedas 236–237, taking shelter first.** Observances, "Surrendering to and taking shelter of the Lord, service to the spiritual master and the Vaisnavas, and hearing and chanting the Lord's glories" are all vaidhi-bhakti; "All of these activities together, or only one of them, or two or three of them, lead the devotee to advanced devotional service." Taking shelter "puts an end to the fearful condition of repeated birth and death"; "Thus one should take shelter of the Lord and no one else." He quotes BG 18.66. Anuccheda 237: "Without first taking shelter of the Lord one cannot attain perfection. If one takes shelter of the Lord, then one attains all perfection."
- **Application (preacher's):** Jiva Gosvami's order is Lord Caitanya's order in CC Madhya 22: the definition, the source,
  surrender, the limbs. Teach the book as the systematic form of the chapter, and name the edition when quoting.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle: what bhakti is.** Pure devotional service is favourable service to Krishna as He desires, free of three
   things: material desire, knowledge aimed at oneness, and fruitive work (CC Madhya 19.167–168). The Bhagavatam says the
   same: unmotivated and uninterrupted (SB 1.2.6); flowing to the Lord like the Ganges, "without reason", refusing even
   liberation (SB 3.29.11–13 and purport, where Prabhupada writes *anyabhilasita-sunyam* over Kapila's verse). The Upanisad
   Jiva Gosvami quotes: free from desire for profit "either in this life or in the next" (Gopala-tapani 1.18).
2. **Why "only means".** Krishna Himself: "Only by practicing unalloyed devotional service with full faith in Me can one obtain Me" (SB 11.14.21); He is not controlled by yoga, Sankhya, pious work, Vedic study, austerity or renunciation (11.14.20); "One can understand Me as I am... only by devotional service" (BG 18.55). Lord Caitanya: the other paths "are dependent on bhakti" (CC Madhya 22.17); knowledge alone cannot liberate, bhakti liberates even without knowledge (22.21). Jiva Gosvami: service "is the only means for attainment of the final perfection of life" (SB 3.24.44 in anuccheda 47).
3. **The three paths rightly placed.** Krishna gives three paths and no fourth (SB 11.20.6): knowledge for the disgusted,
   work for the attached, devotion for one who "develops faith in hearing and chanting My glories" (11.20.7–8). The paths
   are not equal: "philosophical speculation and regulated pious work can never be considered equal to pure love of Godhead" (11.20.6 purport). Jiva Gosvami opens his proof that bhakti is best with this very verse (anuccheda 170). Knowledge and detachment are not rivals of bhakti but its fruits (SB 1.2.7).
4. **Where bhakti comes from.** Not from pious works, "not by giving charity, opening big hospitals and schools or working philanthropically", but "only by the mercy of a pure devotee" (CC Madhya 22.51 purport); a moment's association gives all success (22.54); the one fortune that counts is the pious act that brings one to a devotee (22.45 purport). Love is already in the heart; hearing and chanting purify the heart and it awakens (22.107).
5. **How it begins and what it is made of.** It begins with surrender in six divisions (CC Madhya 22.100; BG 18.66 and
   purport); Jiva Gosvami: "Without first taking shelter of the Lord one cannot attain perfection" (anuccheda 237). Its
   natural characteristic is hearing, chanting and remembering; its marginal characteristic is that it awakens love
   (22.106). Any one of the nine processes, with faith and love, gives the result (the passage before anuccheda 170). All
   rules serve one rule: remember Krishna, never forget Him (22.113).
6. **Two kinds.** Vaidhi, by the rules of scripture, and raganuga, following the spontaneous love of the Lord's eternal
   associates (anuccheda 235; CC Madhya 22 continues with this; dps-30 and trd-38 treat raganuga). This page stays with the
   definition common to both.
7. **Connections.** trd-23: this is the abhidheya. trd-27: knowing who Krishna is makes "favourable, as Krishna desires"
   definite. trd-29: the fruit, prema, is the next page. dps-12 to dps-18: the stages are the growth of this one thing.
   trd-22: the limbs that nourish the heart are limbs of this.
8. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *"Bhakti is for the simple; knowledge is for the intelligent."* The Bhagavatam answers directly: "the bhakti cult is meant for the śūdras, vaiśyas and the less intelligent woman class. But that is not the actual fact. The bhakti cult is the topmost of all transcendental activities" (SB 1.2.7 purport).
   - *"Good work is enough."* Work that does not awaken attraction for Krishna's message is "only so much useless labor" (SB 1.2.8).
   - *"Mixed devotion is still devotion."* Service "for material gain is certainly an obstruction" (SB 1.2.6 purport); the
     definition excludes material desire, monistic knowledge and fruitive work (CC Madhya 19.167).
   - *"Liberation is bhakti's goal."* The pure devotee refuses even liberation (SB 3.29.13); liberation waits on him "with folded hands" (CC Madhya 22.21 purport).
   - *"I can get bhakti by my own effort."* It comes by the mercy of a devotee (CC Madhya 22.51); the effort is to seek and
     serve such association.
9. **Pitch for TRD.** The definition is a mirror for residents before it is a weapon for debate. Give time to the three
   exclusions as questions about one's own service, and keep the tone encouraging: the same verses promise that even
   dog-eaters are purified (SB 11.14.21) and that a sincere word of surrender frees one at once (CC Madhya 22.33).

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet the residents and say that today's session is about the one thing their whole life is made of, devotional service:
   what it exactly is, and why Krishna says it alone attains Him.
2. Ask them to write privately one service they did this week and, honestly, one motive mixed into it, if any. No sharing.
3. Read CC Madhya 19.167. Ask: what three things does the verse exclude, and what one thing does it require? Take answers.
4. Say that this verse of Rupa Gosvami is the heart of Jiva Gosvami's *Bhakti-sandarbha*, and that the Bhagavatam says the
   same in Kapila's words to his mother (SB 3.29.11–13).
5. Ask H.2 question 1 and take three answers.

**H.2 Opening questions**
1. A student says: "I do good for people and I meditate. Why do I need devotion to Krishna?" What is your first sentence?
2. Which is harder to notice in your own service: a material desire, pride of knowledge, or the feeling "this is my work"?
3. Where did your own devotional service begin: from a book, from an effort, or from a person?

## I. Core teaching points

1. **The definition.** Pure bhakti is favourable service to Krishna as He desires, "devoid of all material desires, knowledge obtained by monistic philosophy, and fruitive action" (CC Madhya 19.167); "unmotivated and uninterrupted" (SB 1.2.6); "without reason", like the Ganges to the sea, refusing even liberation (SB 3.29.11–13). *Transition:* why does Krishna say this alone reaches Him?
2. **Only bhakti attains Krishna.** "Only by practicing unalloyed devotional service with full faith in Me can one obtain Me" (SB 11.14.21); not yoga, Sankhya, pious work, Vedic study, austerity or renunciation (11.14.20); "only by devotional service" is He known as He is (BG 18.55). *Example:* the cowherd boys walked into Aghasura's mouth trusting Krishna (11.14.20 purport). *Transition:* then what of knowledge and work?
3. **Knowledge and work depend on bhakti.** "all the others are dependent on bhakti" (CC Madhya 22.17); knowledge alone cannot liberate, bhakti liberates without it (22.21); work without attraction to Krishna's message is "useless labor" (SB 1.2.8); knowledge and detachment are bhakti's fruits (SB 1.2.7). Krishna's three paths and who is fit for each (SB 11.20.6–8). *Transition:* if bhakti is so high, how does anyone get it?
4. **Bhakti comes by the mercy of a devotee.** "Unless one is favored by a pure devotee, one cannot attain the platform of devotional service" (CC Madhya 22.51); a moment's association gives all success (22.54); the one real fortune is meeting a devotee (22.45 purport). Love is in the heart already and awakens when the heart is purified by hearing and chanting (22.107). *Transition:* and how does it begin in me?
5. **It begins with surrender.** The six divisions (CC Madhya 22.100; BG 18.66); a sincere sentence frees one at once (22.33);
   "Without first taking shelter of the Lord one cannot attain perfection" (*Bhakti-sandarbha* 237). *Transition:* and then?
6. **Hearing, chanting, remembering; one rule behind all rules.** The natural characteristic of bhakti is hearing, chanting
   and remembering; the marginal one is that it awakens love (22.106). Any one of the nine processes with faith and love
   gives the result (*Bhakti-sandarbha*, before anuccheda 170). All rules serve one rule: remember Krishna, never forget Him
   (22.113). *Transition:* how does Jiva Gosvami arrange this?
7. ***Bhakti-sandarbha* arranges the abhidheya.** From the diagnosis (aversion) to the medicine (service, anuccheda 1), the
   proof that bhakti is best (170), its two kinds (235) and its first step, shelter (236–237). The order is Lord Caitanya's
   order in CC Madhya 22. *Application (preacher's):* use the definition first on yourself, then for others.

## J. Stories and illustrations

**Lord Caitanya teaches Rupa Gosvami at Prayaga** (CC Madhya 19.167–169). Teaching Rupa Gosvami, the Lord gives the
definition of first-class devotional service: free of material desire, monistic knowledge and fruitive action, "constantly serve Kṛṣṇa favorably, as Kṛṣṇa desires" (19.167), explains the three exclusions (19.168) and says: "If one renders such pure devotional service, he develops his original love for Kṛṣṇa in due course of time" (19.169). *Devotional lesson:* the verse Rupa Gosvami later placed at the head of *Bhakti-rasamrta-sindhu* was taught to him by the Lord Himself; the definition is the Lord's, and its promise is love.

**Lord Caitanya teaches Sanatana Gosvami the abhidheya** (CC Madhya 22). To Sanatana the Lord states that all Vedic literature
concludes that human activity "should be centered only on devotional service to Lord Kṛṣṇa" (22.5), that karma, jnana and yoga "are dependent on bhakti" (22.17), that bhakti comes only by a pure devotee's favour (22.51) and that "by even a moment's association with a pure devotee, one can attain all success" (22.54). He gives the words of surrender (22.33), the six divisions of surrender (22.100–101), the characteristics of bhakti (22.106–107) and the one rule behind all rules (22.113). *Devotional lesson:* the former minister who asked "who am I?" is now told what to do, in order: take shelter, hear, chant, remember, never forget.

**Kapila and the Ganges** (SB 3.29.11–13). Lord Kapila tells His mother Devahuti that unadulterated devotional service appears
when the mind "is at once attracted to hearing the transcendental name and qualities of the Supreme Personality of Godhead", and flows to the Lord "Just as the water of the Ganges flows naturally down towards the ocean", uninterrupted by any material condition (3.29.11–12); such a devotee "does not accept any kind of liberation — sālokya, sārṣṭi, sāmīpya, sārūpya or ekatva — even though they are offered" (3.29.13). *Devotional lesson:* the Ganges does not flow for a reward; pure service has no reason outside the Lord.

**The cowherd boys and Aghasura** (SB 11.14.20 purport). Srila Prabhupada's disciples write in the purport: "When the great serpent Aghāsura appeared in the precincts of Vraja, the cowherd boys, completely confident in their friendship with Lord Kṛṣṇa, fearlessly marched into the serpent's gigantic mouth. Such pure love for Kṛṣṇa brings the Lord under the control of the devotee." *Devotional lesson:* unalloyed trust, not calculation, is what controls Krishna. (The full pastime is SB 10.12; read it there before telling it.)

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. *"I serve humanity and meditate. Isn't that enough?"* Krishna says He is not controlled by yoga, pious work, study or
   austerity, only by unalloyed devotion (SB 11.14.20–21), and work that does not awaken attraction for His message is
   "useless labor" (SB 1.2.8). Service to people becomes devotional when it is done for Krishna as He desires (CC Madhya
   19.167 purport: preaching His message is the service He wants).
2. *"Isn't knowledge higher than devotion?"* "Speculative knowledge alone, without devotional service, is not able to give liberation" (CC Madhya 22.21); knowledge is a fruit of bhakti (SB 1.2.7); the idea that bhakti is for the less intelligent "is not the actual fact" (SB 1.2.7 purport).
3. *"If bhakti comes only by a devotee's mercy, what can I do?"* Seek and serve that association: "by even a moment's association with a pure devotee, one can attain all success" (CC Madhya 22.54); abide by the spiritual master's order and serve him (22.25 purport). The sincere words of surrender are always available (22.33).
4. *"Is wanting liberation a material desire?"* The pure devotee refuses even liberation (SB 3.29.13); "knowledge obtained by monistic philosophy" is one of the three exclusions (CC Madhya 19.167). Liberation comes anyway and waits on the devotee (22.21 purport).
5. *"Are the rules bhakti, or is the feeling bhakti?"* The rules "should be the servants" of remembering Krishna and never forgetting Him (CC Madhya 22.113); hearing, chanting and remembering are bhakti's natural characteristic, and awakening love its marginal one (22.106). Rules without remembrance are not yet bhakti; remembrance is sustained by the rules, above all sixteen rounds (22.113 purport).
6. *"Must I do all nine processes?"* No: "By performing any one of them, any combination of them, or all of them with faith and love one will attain the desired result" (*Bhakti-sandarbha*, passage before anuccheda 170).
7. *"How is vaidhi different from raganuga, and which am I on?"* Vaidhi follows the rules of scripture; raganuga follows the
   spontaneous love of the Lord's associates (anuccheda 235). The definition of pure bhakti is the same for both. dps-30 and
   trd-38 take this further; do not claim raganuga for oneself.

## L. Case studies

**Case 1: The service that became a career.** *Invented case for discussion.* Yadu, a full-time preacher, runs a large
college programme. He notices that he is counting attendance for his own reputation and feels hurt when another preacher's
programme is praised.
- *Dilemma:* the service continues; the motive has changed.
- *Principles:* CC Madhya 19.167–168 (material desire excluded); SB 1.2.6 purport (service "for material gain is certainly an obstruction"); CC Madhya 22.113 (all rules serve remembering Krishna).
- *Questions:* Which of the three exclusions is at work? What would "as Krishna desires" mean for Yadu's programme? What one
  practice restores the motive?
- *Analysis:* Material desire for honour has mixed in. The remedy is not to stop the programme but to return it to its owner:
  renew the six divisions of surrender (22.100), especially accepting Krishna as master, and keep sixteen rounds first
  (22.113 purport), so remembering Krishna governs the counting.
- *Takeaway:* the definition is a mirror; the surrender verses are the washing.

**Case 2: "I only do good."** *Invented case for discussion.* A doctor who runs a free clinic tells Krishnadasa, a brahmacari:
"You chant; I heal people. Surely my work pleases God more than your singing."
- *Dilemma:* honour real goodness without agreeing that it is the means.
- *Principles:* SB 11.14.20–21; SB 1.2.8; CC Madhya 22.17; CC Madhya 19.167 purport.
- *Questions:* What is true in the doctor's words? What does Krishna say pleases Him? How can his clinic become devotional
  service?
- *Analysis:* His compassion is real and is a form of piety, but Krishna says He is not controlled by pious work, only by
  unalloyed devotion (SB 11.14.20). Krishnadasa honours the work, reads SB 11.14.20–21 with him, and suggests one change: do
  the same work for Krishna as He desires, offer it to Him, and give the patients His name. Then the clinic becomes abhidheya.
- *Takeaway:* bhakti does not reject good work; it gives it its owner.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first; thank every answer)
1. What three things does pure devotional service exclude, and what does it require? *(Material desire, monistic knowledge,
   fruitive work; favourable service to Krishna as He desires; CC Madhya 19.167.)*
2. In SB 1.2.6, what two qualities must devotional service have? *(Unmotivated and uninterrupted.)*
3. Which practices does Krishna say cannot control Him? *(Mystic yoga, Sankhya, pious work, Vedic study, austerity,
   renunciation; SB 11.14.20.)*
4. Where does bhakti come from? *(The mercy of a pure devotee; CC Madhya 22.51.)*
5. What are the six divisions of surrender? *(Accept the favourable, reject the unfavourable, conviction that Krishna protects,
   accept Him as master, full self-surrender, humility; CC Madhya 22.100.)*
6. What is the one rule all rules serve? *(Always remember Krishna, never forget Him; CC Madhya 22.113.)*

**M.2 Activities**
- **The mirror (10 min, private).** Each resident writes his three main services and, beside each, honestly, which of the
  three exclusions (material desire, pride of knowledge, "my work") most threatens it, and one line from CC Madhya 22.100
  that answers it. No sharing.
- **"Is good work enough?" (12 min).** In pairs, one plays the doctor of Case 2; the other answers in three sentences with
  one verse (SB 11.14.20–21 or SB 1.2.8). Swap. Two pairs demonstrate; the group notes whether the goodness was honoured.

**M.3 Reflection** (private)
If Krishna is controlled only by unalloyed devotion, what in my service today was alloyed? What would I lose by removing it,
and what does SB 11.14.21 promise I would gain?

## P. Practical takeaway

- **Lesson:** pure bhakti is favourable service to Krishna as He desires, free of material desire, monistic knowledge and
  fruitive work; it alone attains Him; it comes by a devotee's mercy and begins with surrender (CC Madhya 19.167; SB 11.14.21;
  CC Madhya 22.51, 22.100).
- **Action this week:** each day, before your main service, read CC Madhya 19.167 and ask the three questions of the mirror
  (M.2); keep sixteen rounds before every other duty (CC Madhya 22.113 purport); and once this week answer "is good work
  enough?" with SB 11.14.20–21, honouring the goodness first.
- **Reflection:** "Which of the three exclusions do I most need to watch, and which division of surrender answers it?"
- **Next:** trd-29, *Priti-sandarbha*: prema, the highest goal. Read CC Madhya 23.1–40 and CC Adi 7.141–146 before it.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

The first four Sandarbhas told us whom we serve; the fifth, *Bhakti-sandarbha*, tells us what to do: devotional service, the abhidheya. Lord Caitanya gave Rupa Gosvami the definition: "When first-class devotional service develops, one must be devoid of all material desires, knowledge obtained by monistic philosophy, and fruitive action. The devotee must constantly serve Kṛṣṇa favorably, as Kṛṣṇa desires" (CC Madhya 19.167). Such service "is called śuddha-bhakti, pure devotional service", and it awakens one's original love for Krishna (19.169). The Bhagavatam says the same: devotional service "must be unmotivated and uninterrupted" (SB 1.2.6), and Lord Kapila compares it to the Ganges flowing to the sea, without reason and without interruption, refusing even liberation (SB 3.29.11–13).

Why is it the only means? Krishna tells Uddhava: "Only by practicing unalloyed devotional service with full faith in Me can one obtain Me" (SB 11.14.21); He is not controlled by yoga, philosophy, pious work, study, austerity or renunciation (11.14.20). In the Gita: "One can understand Me as I am, as the Supreme Personality of Godhead, only by devotional service" (BG 18.55). He gives three paths, knowledge for the disgusted, work for the attached, devotion for one who has faith in hearing His glories, and says there is no fourth (SB 11.20.6–8). Lord Caitanya concludes that karma, jnana and yoga "are dependent on bhakti" (CC Madhya 22.17), and that knowledge alone cannot liberate while bhakti liberates even without it (22.21). Knowledge and detachment come as fruits of bhakti (SB 1.2.7), and work that does not awaken attraction for Krishna's message is "useless labor" (SB 1.2.8).

Where does bhakti come from? "Unless one is favored by a pure devotee, one cannot attain the platform of devotional service" (CC Madhya 22.51), and "by even a moment's association with a pure devotee, one can attain all success" (22.54). It begins with surrender in six divisions (22.100; BG 18.66); Jiva Gosvami writes, "Without first taking shelter of the Lord one cannot attain perfection" (*Bhakti-sandarbha* 237). It is made of hearing, chanting and remembering, and it awakens love that is already in the heart (22.106–107). Any one of the nine processes, with faith and love, gives the result. And all rules serve one rule: "Kṛṣṇa... should always be remembered and never forgotten at any time" (22.113). For a temple resident the definition is first a mirror: is there material desire, pride of knowledge or "my work" in my service? The answer is surrender renewed and sixteen rounds first.

## S2. Key ideas
- Pure bhakti is favourable service to Krishna as He desires, free of material desire, monistic knowledge and fruitive work (CC Madhya 19.167).
- It is the only means that attains Krishna; the other paths depend on it (SB 11.14.20–21; BG 18.55; CC Madhya 22.17).
- It comes by the mercy of a pure devotee (CC Madhya 22.51, 22.54).
- It begins with surrender in six divisions (CC Madhya 22.100; BG 18.66).
- All rules serve one rule: remember Krishna, never forget Him (CC Madhya 22.113).

## S3. Verses to remember
- **CC Madhya 19.167:** "devoid of all material desires, knowledge obtained by monistic philosophy, and fruitive action... serve Kṛṣṇa favorably, as Kṛṣṇa desires." *For me:* the mirror for my service.
- **SB 11.14.21:** "Only by practicing unalloyed devotional service with full faith in Me can one obtain Me". *For me:* why nothing else will do.
- **CC Madhya 22.113:** Krishna "should always be remembered and never forgotten at any time. All the rules and prohibitions mentioned in the śāstras should be the servants of these two principles." *For me:* the one rule behind all my rules.

## S4. Words to know
- **Abhidheya:** what is to be done; devotional service, the second of the three subjects of scripture.
- **Anyabhilasita-sunyam:** "free of other desires"; the first words of Rupa Gosvami's definition (CC Madhya 19.167).
- **Suddha-bhakti:** pure devotional service (CC Madhya 19.169).
- **Vaidhi-bhakti / raganuga-bhakti:** devotion following the rules of scripture / devotion following the spontaneous love of Krishna's eternal associates.
- **Saranagati:** surrender; its six divisions are in CC Madhya 22.100.
- **Bhakti-sandarbha:** Jiva Gosvami's fifth Sandarbha, on devotional service as the means.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *I do good and meditate; isn't that enough?* Krishna says He is controlled only by unalloyed devotion, not by pious work or yoga (SB 11.14.20–21); work that does not awaken attraction for His message is useless labour (SB 1.2.8). Do the same good for Krishna, as He desires.
2. *Isn't knowledge higher than devotion?* Knowledge alone cannot liberate; bhakti liberates even without it (CC Madhya 22.21); knowledge is a fruit of bhakti (SB 1.2.7).
3. *If bhakti comes by mercy, what can I do?* Seek a pure devotee's association and serve under his order (CC Madhya 22.51, 22.54, 22.25); say the words of surrender sincerely (22.33).
4. *Is wanting liberation a material desire?* The pure devotee refuses even liberation (SB 3.29.13); liberation comes anyway and waits on him (CC Madhya 22.21 purport).
5. *Must I do all nine processes?* No; any one, with faith and love, gives the result (*Bhakti-sandarbha*, passage before anuccheda 170).
