# 45. Masturbation

> BSS · 120 min · drafted · SENSITIVE (sexuality and celibacy; adult men in brahmacari training; no private disclosure). Follows BIKC chapter 45, given in its own words. Builds on bss-37 (restraint, and the accident at night), bss-39 (the Krishna conscious way to deal with desire) and bss-40 (hope and the way back after a fall). This page covers one specific, very common, very private habit. It does not repeat the verses of those pages.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level
This is the most common struggle among young men who are trying to be celibate, and it is the one that people hide most. BIKC (ch. 45) says openly that "even responsible devotees who have no intentions of physically engaging in illicit sex" can become weak in this way, and that "Guilty feelings sometimes impel a devotee to hide this problem from those who care about his devotional advancement." If the subject is never named, students carry it alone. The page names it plainly, in BIKC's words, and moves the discussion to BIKC's remedy: confidential counselling and surrender to Krishna.

## C. Central message
A habit that a person repeats against his own wish is the situation Arjuna describes in the Bhagavad-gita (3.36), and Krishna names the cause: lust, "the all-devouring sinful enemy of this world", which the purport calls the soul's eternal love for Krishna transformed by contact with the mode of passion. BIKC (ch. 45) says masturbation is "a difficult problem, especially if it becomes a habit", that "A devotee with this habit will always feel guilty, and may literally go crazy", and that "The only real solution is to surrender to Kṛṣṇa" (BG 7.14), with "confidential counseling from one's guru, local authority, or any other trusted devotee". A night routine filled with Krishna and steady chanting support this.

## D. Learning outcomes
1. Explain from BG 3.36-37 and their purports what impels a person to sin "even unwillingly", and what lust is in origin.
2. State what BIKC (ch. 45) says the habit leads to if not stopped early, and describe the cycle of act, guilt and hiding and where it can be broken.
3. Name three practical parts of a night routine, and the Krishna conscious engagement under them.
4. Say whom to approach for help, and when professional help is needed.
5. Explain why marriage and brahmacarya are both honourable choices that a student decides with a senior.

## E. Scriptural foundation
Quotations are the BBT translations kept in `research/verification/bss-41.json`. Purport points were read in full on vedabase.io. Srila Prabhupada's books speak of this habit mostly through the general teaching on illicit sex and the wasting of semen. Where BIKC quotes a letter or lecture on the subject specifically, that quotation was not found on vedabase.io and is marked Requires source review.

**BG 3.36** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/36/
> "Arjuna said: O descendant of Vṛṣṇi, by what is one impelled to sinful acts, even unwillingly, as if engaged by force?"
- Context: Arjuna asks this after Krishna has taught him to follow his duty without being ruled by attraction and aversion.
- Purport point: "A living entity, as part and parcel of the Supreme, is originally spiritual, pure, and free from all material contaminations. Therefore, by nature he is not subject to the sins of the material world. But when he is in contact with the material nature, he acts in many sinful ways without hesitation, and sometimes even against his will. As such, Arjuna's question to Kṛṣṇa is very sanguine, as to the perverted nature of the living entities. Although the living entity sometimes does not want to act in sin, he is still forced to act. Sinful actions are not, however, impelled by the Supersoul within, but are due to another cause, as the Lord explains in the next verse."
- BIKC (ch. 45) cites this verse for devotees who "even against their own will (Bg. 3.36) indulge in mental fantasies and masturbation."
- Use: in the talk.

**BG 3.37** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/37/
> "The Supreme Personality of Godhead said: It is lust only, Arjuna, which is born of contact with the material mode of passion and later transformed into wrath, and which is the all-devouring sinful enemy of this world."
- Purport point: "When a living entity comes in contact with the material creation, his eternal love for Kṛṣṇa is transformed into lust, in association with the mode of passion. Or, in other words, the sense of love of God becomes transformed into lust, as milk in contact with sour tamarind is transformed into yogurt. Then again, when lust is unsatisfied, it turns into wrath; wrath is transformed into illusion, and illusion continues the material existence. Therefore, lust is the greatest enemy of the living entity, and it is lust only which induces the pure living entity to remain entangled in the material world." And: "If, therefore, lust is transformed into love for the Supreme, or transformed into Kṛṣṇa consciousness – or, in other words, desiring everything for Kṛṣṇa – then both lust and wrath can be spiritualized."
- Teaching: read the purport as it is: lust is love of God transformed, it is "the greatest enemy", and it is spiritualised by "desiring everything for Kṛṣṇa". (bss-21 uses this verse to explain what the drive is.)
- Use: in the talk.

**BG 7.14** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/7/14/
> "This divine energy of Mine, consisting of the three modes of material nature, is difficult to overcome. But those who have surrendered unto Me can easily cross beyond it."
- Purport point: the purport says that "A man bound by the hands and feet cannot free himself – he must be helped by a person who is unbound. Because the bound cannot help the bound, the rescuer must be liberated. Therefore, only Lord Kṛṣṇa, or His bona fide representative the spiritual master, can release the conditioned soul." It also says that "Devotional service, or Kṛṣṇa consciousness, can help one gain such release."
- Application (preacher's): this is the root of the advice to seek help. It is not weakness to ask. A person who is tied cannot untie himself. The help comes from Krishna and from the guru, and a trusted senior devotee leads us to them.
- Use: in the talk.

**SB 6.1.13-14** · Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/6/1/13-14/
> "To concentrate the mind, one must observe a life of celibacy and not fall down."
- Purport point: the purport says brahmacarya "has eight aspects", from not thinking of women or speaking of sex life to not engaging in sex life, so thought and speech are already part of the discipline. It also says: "To begin, however, one must control the tongue and the process of eating", and that the tongue is controlled by chanting the maha-mantra, speaking only of Krishna and tasting only what has been offered to Him; "If one can control the tongue in this way, brahmacarya and other purifying processes will automatically follow."
- Application (preacher's): this is the starting point. The first practical steps are chanting and the discipline of eating. (bss-16 uses the eight aspects to define brahmacarya; this page uses the purport for the order of practice.)
- Use: in the talk.

**SB 7.12, chapter introduction** · Read on vedabase.io · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/12/
- Statement: "Wasting semen is also illicit sex." (The context is the gṛhastha-āśrama, which "does not allow unrestricted sex life".) This is the teaching on which BIKC's chapter rests. It is a general statement and does not discuss the habit in detail. It has no verse number and is not under `references:`.

**Letter printed in BIKC (Requires source review):** BIKC (ch. 45) prints a letter it attributes to Srila Prabhupada (9 December 1968) as "addressing this problem": "such problems are not very unnatural because in the body the conditioned soul is very prone to failure... So whatever fall down has been, you should be regretful about it, but it is not so serious nor is it a permanent disqualification. But you must try to check yourself from such artificial things and take full shelter of the lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa. I think that for such checking, marriage is the only solution... It is my open advice for everyone that one who is disturbed by sex must take the responsibility of married life." The letter was not found on vedabase.io during preparation, so it is quoted as BIKC's text ("BIKC prints a letter saying"), not as Prabhupada's words, until checked. BIKC also quotes a conversation (27 June 1975), "Impossible is a word in a fool's dictionary", and refers to a lecture of 8 April 1975 (bss-39): same status. The general teaching of BG 9.30-31 and SB 11.5.42 on bss-40 stands on its own.

## F. Philosophical explanation
**F.1 BIKC chapter 45 in its own words.** "In our endeavor to become Kṛṣṇa conscious, māyā is always trying to knock us back. Even for a sincere devotee who has made considerable progress, māyā doesn't hesitate to use her lowest and grossest weapon: sex. Sometimes devotees—even responsible devotees who have no intentions of physically engaging in illicit sex—become weak. On lying down, they submit to the mad mind, and even against their own will (Bg. 3.36) indulge in mental fantasies and masturbation." Then: "This is a difficult problem, especially if it becomes a habit. If this contemplation is not stopped at an early stage, then it will likely go from thinking, to feeling, to willingly engaging in sex. Such a person usually ends up getting married, or worse, falls into illicit sex and becomes a debaucher. A devotee with this habit will always feel guilty, and may literally go crazy." And at the end: "With sincere endeavor and the help of others this degrading habit can be broken." Give these sentences as BIKC gives them, cited as BIKC (ch. 45).

**F.2 What Krishna answers.** Arjuna asks: why am I driven to do what I do not want to do (BG 3.36)? Krishna names the cause (BG 3.37) and, in the following verses, explains how to meet it (the senses are regulated and the mind is steadied by intelligence; see bss-27). The purport of BG 3.37 says lust is the soul's eternal love for Krishna "transformed into lust, in association with the mode of passion", that it is "the greatest enemy of the living entity", and that when "transformed into Kṛṣṇa consciousness – or, in other words, desiring everything for Kṛṣṇa – then both lust and wrath can be spiritualized." Teach the purport's own words.

**F.3 The cycle, and where it breaks (Application (preacher's), following BIKC).** BIKC (ch. 45) says: "Guilty feelings sometimes impel a devotee to hide this problem from those who care about his devotional advancement. However, to rectify this faulty mentality requires confidential counseling from one's guru, local authority, or any other trusted devotee. Admitting to this takes courage and can understandably be embarrassing. But if a devotee suffering from this malady is at all to overcome it, he must seek advice from a senior devotee who he can completely trust. Particularly, the spiritual master is the topmost well-wisher of the disciple, so no anarthas should be kept secret from him if the disciple really wants to weed them out for good." The preacher's drawing of this: the habit is repeated, guilt follows, hiding follows guilt, and the hidden problem is not rectified. The cycle can be broken at three places: before (a routine that protects the vulnerable times), after (not hiding), and in the middle (keeping on chanting; bss-40). Chaitanya Charan Das describes the same balance: guilt that protects is useful, guilt that paralyses is a temptation (see bss-40, F.4).

**F.4 If it grows from thoughts.** BIKC says: "If this contemplation is not stopped at an early stage, then it will likely go from thinking, to feeling, to willingly engaging in sex." This matches the eight aspects in the SB 6.1.13-14 purport and the ladder on bss-05. The earliest step, the thought, is the easiest to meet. A Krishna conscious response to a thought is quick: say the holy name aloud, stand up, wash the face, go to the company of devotees, or begin a service. (Application (preacher's).)

**F.5 The only real solution is surrender, with practical help beside it.** BIKC (ch. 45) says: "even with the best of help, it may not be easy to overcome this painful predicament. The only real solution is to surrender to Kṛṣṇa", quotes BG 7.14, and adds: "We have to take shelter of Madana-mohana" (the lecture it points to is on bss-39). The purport of BG 7.14 says why: the bound cannot free himself. The practical help is a night routine and a daily life that leaves no empty space. BIKC: "A practical tip to Kṛṣṇa-ize the mind at night is to read the Kṛṣṇa book with devotees before retiring, and thus go to sleep thinking of Kṛṣṇa's pastimes. Some devotees go to sleep listening to a cassette of Prabhupāda's transcendental voice purifying their mind." Add the following as the preacher's suggestions, none of which is a medical claim: fix a regular bedtime and rising time; keep the phone outside the sleeping area; do not stay in bed awake (get up, chant a few rounds, and return); avoid unoccupied evening hours; and let a friend or room-mate know you want to keep a good routine.

**F.6 When to see a professional.** If the habit takes up many hours, hurts the body, interferes with sleep or work, comes with anxiety or low mood, or feels out of control despite real effort, see a qualified counsellor or doctor. This is a respectable form of help, and it is compatible with devotional life. A counsellor does not replace the senior or the chanting; both can run together. In India, Tele-MANAS (14416) is free. Elsewhere use your local crisis or health service. If you have thoughts of harming yourself, tell someone near you and call a helpline at once. (Pastoral guidance.)

**F.7 Marriage.** BIKC (ch. 44) says: "if a brahmacārī persistently cannot control his mind, he had better get married before he falls down into illicit sex." BIKC (ch. 45) says of one who does not stop the contemplation early: "Such a person usually ends up getting married, or worse, falls into illicit sex and becomes a debaucher", and prints the letter (E) whose "open advice for everyone" is "that one who is disturbed by sex must take the responsibility of married life." The decision is made with a senior (Application, preacher's).

**F.8 The preacher's own conduct.** You are not a therapist. Do not ask for details, do not promise secrecy that you cannot keep if there is a risk of harm, and do not give medical advice. Say: "Thank you for telling me. Let us talk to someone who can help you." Introduce the person to a senior or a counsellor. If a minor, force or any other person is involved, it is a safeguarding matter (see bss-40, F.5).

## H. Opening and engagement
**H.1 How to start.** (1) Say in an ordinary voice: "Today's topic is a struggle that many sincere young men have and few talk about. We will read what BIKC and the Gita say about it." (2) Say: "Nobody has to say anything about himself. If you want to talk, you can come to me or to any senior, and I will help you find the right person." (3) Read BG 3.36 aloud and say that Arjuna asked this question honestly. (4) Ask H.2.

**H.2 Opening questions.**
1. Have you ever promised yourself to stop a habit and found that you did it again? What was it? (Easy; use any habit such as sleeping late or checking a phone.)
2. When you hide a mistake, does it get smaller or bigger?
3. What does Arjuna's question in BG 3.36 tell you about what kind of person he is?

## J. Stories and illustrations
**J.1 Arjuna asks (BG 3.36-37).** On the battlefield of Kurukshetra, Krishna has been teaching Arjuna, his friend and devotee, about duty and the self. Arjuna then asks: by what is one impelled to sinful acts "even unwillingly, as if engaged by force"? (BG 3.36). Krishna answers at once, naming the cause: "It is lust only… the all-devouring sinful enemy of this world" (BG 3.37). **Devotional lesson:** the Lord answers a question about our weakness with knowledge (the purport calls Arjuna's question "very sanguine, as to the perverted nature of the living entities"), and the answer in the following verses is to regulate the senses and to steady the mind by intelligence (those verses are discussed on bss-27 and bss-23). Arjuna did not hide his question. A student who asks his senior an honest question is following Arjuna.
(The story is the context of the verses only; no dialogue is added.)

**J.2 Everyday image (Application (preacher's)).** A person has a small hole in the roof of his room. He puts a bucket under it and says nothing. Every time it rains the bucket fills and must be emptied. If he tells the landlord, the roof is repaired once. The bucket is the habit; the roof is the root, and the landlord is the senior who can help with the roof. The Krishna conscious point is BG 7.14: the one who is bound cannot free himself, and the Lord and the guru free him.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections
- *Is this a sin?* Srila Prabhupada: "Wasting semen is also illicit sex" (SB 7.12 introduction). BIKC (ch. 45): if not stopped early "it will likely go from thinking, to feeling, to willingly engaging in sex"; "this degrading habit can be broken". The Gita also says that a sincere devotee who falls can return (BG 9.30-31, bss-40).
- *Why is it so difficult to stop?* BG 3.36-37 and purports: lust, "born of contact with the material mode of passion", is "the greatest enemy of the living entity", and the living entity "is still forced to act" even against his will. The purport of BG 7.14 says that a bound person cannot untie himself.
- *Does it harm my body?* Srila Prabhupada's purports on semen (SB 3.26.57, 7.11.8-12, 4.28.1) and BIKC chapter 26 are on bss-37; read them as they are there. BIKC (ch. 26): "If semen is lost, all bodily and sensory functions are weakened." If you have pain, anxiety or any illness, ask a qualified doctor.
- *I cannot tell anyone. I would die of shame.* That feeling is common and is part of the cycle (F.3). Start with one person you trust and say only a little. You may go to a counsellor first if that is easier. A senior has heard this before.
- *What if I keep failing after telling a senior?* Keep telling him. Improvement in this area is often slow, and honesty is part of the cure. See a counsellor as well (F.6), and consider with your senior whether a different arrangement of your life would help.
- *Is marriage the answer?* BIKC (ch. 44): "if a brahmacārī persistently cannot control his mind, he had better get married before he falls down into illicit sex"; the letter BIKC prints (E, Requires source review) says "one who is disturbed by sex must take the responsibility of married life" and "If you are married you can continue to practice all the items of worship and with more peace of mind." Decide with a senior (F.7).
- *Is it only about males?* BIKC's chapter speaks to men. The principles of honesty, a trusted guide and surrender apply to everyone; a woman should be guided by a senior woman devotee.

## L. Case studies
**L.1 Invented case for discussion.** *Situation:* Tarun is twenty-three and has lived in the ashram for a year. He chants his rounds and attends the morning programme, but at night, in bed, he gives in to the habit three or four times a month, and then feels so guilty that he skips the next morning's kirtana and avoids his mentor. *Dilemma:* he thinks he is a hypocrite. *Principles:* BG 3.36-37 (the cause is lust, and he is not the first to ask), BG 7.14 (a bound person needs help), BIKC (honest talk with a trusted senior). *Questions:* (a) At which three places can the cycle be broken for Tarun? (b) What would a night routine for him look like? (c) What would you want his mentor to say first? *Analysis:* the real damage is the skipped kirtana and the hiding. Tarun can (1) go to the next kirtana in any case; (2) ask his mentor for a private meeting and say, "I have a struggle at night that I would like to talk about"; (3) begin the night routine of F.5; (4) if there is no improvement after some weeks, add a counsellor. *Takeaway:* the habit is the smaller problem, and the silence is the bigger one.

## M. Discussion and reflection
**M.1 Closing check questions.**
1. What did Arjuna ask in BG 3.36? *Why is one impelled to sinful acts even unwillingly, as if by force?*
2. What was Krishna's answer in BG 3.37? *It is lust only, born of the mode of passion, the all-devouring enemy.*
3. According to the purport of BG 3.37, what is lust in origin? *Love for Krishna transformed by contact with the mode of passion.*
4. What feeds the cycle, according to BIKC? *Guilt that leads to hiding.*
5. What does the purport of BG 7.14 say about who can free a bound person? *Only Krishna or His representative the spiritual master.*
6. When is professional help advisable? *When the habit feels out of control, harms sleep or work, or comes with anxiety or low mood; Tele-MANAS 14416 in India.*

**M.2 Activities.**
(a) *Build a night routine (15 min):* in pairs, each person writes a five-line routine for the hour before sleep, with at least one Krishna conscious activity (reading, a recording of Prabhupada, a few rounds). Pairs may share ideas, not personal details.
(b) *Honest question (10 min):* each person writes on a paper one honest question about Krishna consciousness that he has never asked. The preacher collects the papers and answers some without names, following the example of Arjuna (BG 3.36).
(c) *Anonymous question box (10 min):* the preacher answers written questions from E and F only.

**M.3 Reflection.** "Whom do I trust enough to tell, and what small thing could I tell him this month?" Private.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary
BIKC (ch. 45) says: "Sometimes devotees—even responsible devotees who have no intentions of physically engaging in illicit sex—become weak. On lying down, they submit to the mad mind, and even against their own will (Bg. 3.36) indulge in mental fantasies and masturbation." Arjuna asked Krishna: by what is one impelled to sin, even unwillingly, as if by force? (BG 3.36). Krishna answered that it is lust, born from the mode of passion, "the all-devouring sinful enemy of this world" (BG 3.37). Srila Prabhupada's purport says our "eternal love for Kṛṣṇa is transformed into lust" by contact with the mode of passion, that lust "is the greatest enemy of the living entity", and that by "desiring everything for Kṛṣṇa" lust "can be spiritualized".

BIKC says: "If this contemplation is not stopped at an early stage, then it will likely go from thinking, to feeling, to willingly engaging in sex. Such a person usually ends up getting married, or worse, falls into illicit sex and becomes a debaucher. A devotee with this habit will always feel guilty, and may literally go crazy." Guilt makes a devotee hide the problem, but BIKC says the remedy "requires confidential counseling from one's guru, local authority, or any other trusted devotee", and that "The only real solution is to surrender to Kṛṣṇa" (BG 7.14). A bound person cannot untie himself, says the purport of BG 7.14. So the first practical step is to talk to a trusted senior. The second is BIKC's night routine: read Krishna book with devotees before sleep, or go to sleep hearing Srila Prabhupada's voice; keep a regular bedtime. The third is to keep chanting, even after a lapse. BIKC: "With sincere endeavor and the help of others this degrading habit can be broken."

If the habit feels out of control, takes much of your time, harms your sleep or work, or comes with anxiety or low mood, a qualified counsellor or doctor can help, and this is compatible with devotional life (Tele-MANAS 14416 in India, or your local crisis service). If you have thoughts of harming yourself, tell someone near you and call a helpline at once. On marriage, BIKC (ch. 44) says: "if a brahmacārī persistently cannot control his mind, he had better get married before he falls down into illicit sex"; decide with a senior.

## S2. Key ideas
- Arjuna asked the same question; Krishna named lust, "the all-devouring sinful enemy" (BG 3.37).
- Lust is the soul's love of Krishna transformed by the mode of passion; "desiring everything for Kṛṣṇa" spiritualises it (purport).
- BIKC: the habit, if not stopped early, leads to worse; hiding it does not rectify it. "Confidential counseling" from a trusted devotee does.
- A calm night routine with Krishna in it helps, and so does steady chanting.
- Professional help is respectable and compatible with devotion.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **BG 3.36**: Arjuna asks why one sins even unwillingly. *For me: I can ask honest questions about my weakness.*
- **BG 3.37**: it is lust, "the all-devouring sinful enemy of this world". *For me: lust is "the greatest enemy of the living entity" (purport); I turn it toward Krishna.*
- **BG 7.14**: those who surrender to Krishna can cross beyond Maya. *For me: I cannot free myself alone, so I ask for help.*

## S4. Words to know
- **Lust:** the desire for sense pleasure, here sexual.
- **Fantasy:** sexual imagining.
- **Debaucher** (BIKC's word): a person given over to sexual indulgence.
- **Counselling:** talking with a trained person about a personal difficulty.
- **Senior:** an experienced devotee you trust.
- **Surrender:** to take shelter of Krishna with trust.

## S5. Questions and answers
- *Is this a sin?* Srila Prabhupada: "Wasting semen is also illicit sex" (SB 7.12 introduction). BIKC calls it "this degrading habit" and says it "can be broken". A sincere devotee who falls can return (see bss-40).
- *Why can't I stop by willpower?* Because lust is "the greatest enemy of the living entity" (BG 3.37 purport), and a bound person cannot untie himself (BG 7.14 purport).
- *Whom should I tell?* BIKC: "one's guru, local authority, or any other trusted devotee"; or begin with a counsellor.
- *What helps at night?* BIKC: read Krishna book with devotees before sleep, or hear Prabhupada's voice; a regular bedtime; chanting if you cannot sleep.
- *Should I marry?* BIKC (ch. 44): one who "persistently cannot control his mind" had "better get married before he falls down into illicit sex". Decide with a senior.
