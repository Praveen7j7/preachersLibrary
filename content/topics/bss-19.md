# 4. Adi-rasa

> BSS (brahmacari training) · 120 min · drafted, not yet reviewed · **sensitive**: a theological topic about the origin of attraction. The SB 5.25.5 purport's own limit applies (the feelings of Radha and Krsna are discussed only by the experienced and advanced); help-seeking in K.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level
BIKC ch. 4 answers a question every serious student asks: "Where does this overwhelming urge come from?" Only after the
definition, benefits and humility of chapters 1-3 can the answer be heard without confusion.

## C. Central message
The original, pure attraction is in the spiritual world between Krsna and His devotees (adi-rasa); material
lust is its perverted reflection, as different from it as iron from gold, and the way beyond lust is to
awaken the original love, by hearing from authorised sources, not by imitation (SB 5.25.5 purport; CC Adi 4.164-165).

## D. Learning outcomes
1. State, in Prabhupada's words, the difference between lust and love (CC Adi 4.165).
2. Explain what "adi-rasa" means and where Prabhupada's purport describes it (SB 5.25.5).
3. Explain why the pastimes must not be imitated (SB 10.33.30) and how to hear them (SB 10.33.39).
4. Connect this understanding to the BIKC claim that mundane sex desire is the main obstacle (BG 3.37).

## E. Scriptural foundation

**SB 5.25.5 (purport)** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/25/5/
> Purport: "…spiritual lust and material lust are as completely different as gold and iron."
- Context: The verse describes Lord Ananta Sesa and the serpent princesses; the purport quotes Visvanatha Cakravarti on the word "adi".
- Teaching (purport): "ādi" means ādi-rasa, the original lusty feeling, born from the Supreme; only the highly elevated can understand the exchange between Radha and Krsna, and the purport says that unless one is very experienced and advanced in spiritual realisation "he is forbidden to discuss" those feelings. This is why the lecture stays at the level of the purport's principle.
- Application (preacher's): This is the exact text BIKC cites. Use the purport, not the verse text, as the base; do not describe the verse's scene.
- Use: in the talk.

**CC Adi 4.164-165** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/adi/4/164/ and /165/
> "Lust and love have different characteristics, just as iron and gold have different natures." (4.164)
> "The desire to gratify one's own senses is kāma [lust], but the desire to please the senses of Lord Kṛṣṇa is prema [love]." (4.165)
- Teaching: The difference is the object of the desire: self or Krsna.
- Application (preacher's): The brahmacari's training is to change the object, not to hate the energy.
- Use: in the talk.

**CC Adi 1.5** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/adi/1/5/
> "The loving affairs of Śrī Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa are transcendental manifestations of the Lord's internal pleasure-giving potency."
- Teaching: These affairs belong to the Lord's internal potency, not matter.
- Application (preacher's): BIKC says Krsna took the form of Lord Caitanya, in Radha's mood, to taste this. Cite this verse for that point and read its purport.
- Use: in the talk.

**BG 7.11** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/7/11/
> "I am sex life which is not contrary to religious principles."
- Teaching: Regulated procreation is also a manifestation of the Lord.
- Application (preacher's): Prevents contempt for household life.
- Use: K.

**SB 5.5.4** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/5/4/
> "When a person considers sense gratification the aim of life, he certainly becomes mad after materialistic living…"
- Teaching: BIKC (ch. 5) quotes "nunam pramattah"; here only as background on the condition from which the topic starts.
- Use: background for the preacher.

**BG 3.37** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/37/
> "It is lust only, Arjuna, which is born of contact with the material mode of passion…"
- Teaching: Lust is the all-devouring enemy in the world.
- Use: in the talk (one line, the problem).

**SB 10.33.30-31** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/10/33/30/ and /31/ (BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples)
> "One who is not a great controller should never imitate the behavior of ruling personalities, even mentally." (10.33.30)
- Teaching: Do not imitate the Lord's pastimes. The next verse says to carry out the teachings of the empowered, where consistent with their statements.
- Application (preacher's): This protects both the pastimes and the student.
- Use: in the talk.

**SB 10.33.39** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/10/33/39/ (BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples)
> "Anyone who faithfully hears or describes the Lord's playful affairs with the young gopīs of Vṛndāvana will attain the Lord's pure devotional service. Thus he will quickly become sober and conquer lust, the disease of the heart."
- Teaching: Faithful hearing, from an authorised source, cures lust.
- Application (preacher's): Read the purport on who should hear and from whom; Krsna book ch. 33 develops it.
- Use: in the talk.

**KB 29 and KB 33 (Krsna book, chapters on the rasa dance)** — verify-refs records "exists, no translation found." Use the chapters as reading, not as quotations.
- IYF's Preaching Encyclopedia (Appendix 1, "Krishna's Rasa Dance: Can We Imitate?") points to ch. 29 ("Lust and Love") and ch. 33 ("Can We Imitate Rasa Lila?" passages).

## F. Philosophical explanation
**Principle:** everything comes from the Supreme (SB 1.1.1: janmady asya yatah, as cited in the purport to 5.25.5), including
the principle of attraction; in the spiritual world it is pure and self-effacing, in matter it is a reflection that
binds. **Why it matters:** BIKC says the perversion of adi-rasa, in souls envious of Krsna's enjoyment, is the main
obstacle to progress for jnanis, yogis and neophyte devotees; the cure is to revive the original. **Connection:**
CC Adi 4.165 (object of desire). **Consequence:** the brahmacari is not an enemy of love but a student of it.
**Misunderstandings:** (1) "Then my urges are spiritual" -- no: the purport says only the very elevated can understand it;
material lust is a distortion. (2) "Then I may imitate" -- SB 10.33.30 forbids. (3) "Then rasa-lila is a private matter
of lovers" -- no: the Bhagavatam itself says faithful hearing cures lust (10.33.39). (4) "The topic is not for anyone"
-- BIKC and Prabhupada discuss it; hearing is advised from the right source.
**Pitch:** reverent and brief; the lecture is about orientation, not description.

## H. Opening and engagement
**H.1** Say calmly: "This is a delicate topic. Srila Prabhupada's purport to SB 5.25.5 says the feelings of Radha and Krsna are discussed only by the experienced and advanced, so we stay at the level of the purport. Anyone
may step out or talk to me afterwards." Ask: "Why do you think attraction is so powerful?" Take two answers. Say: "Today's
answer is: because it was originally the highest thing."
**H.2** (1) Which is more powerful: love for family or love for friends? (2) What is the difference between wanting something and wanting to give? (3) Have you seen a copy that is much worse than the original?

## I. Core teaching points
1. **The source (SB 5.25.5 purport).** Adi-rasa is the original and pure.
2. **The difference (CC Adi 4.164-165).** Iron versus gold; self versus Krsna.
3. **The perversion (BIKC).** In those envious of Krsna's enjoyment it appears as mundane sex desire, the main obstacle.
4. **Do not imitate (SB 10.33.30).**
5. **Hear (SB 10.33.39).** From authorised sources.
6. **Orientation of brahmacarya.** The student gives up the reflection by turning toward the original: hearing, chanting, service.

## J. Stories and illustrations
Source: IYF Preaching Encyclopedia, Appendix 1 (Sukadeva Gosvami and Maharaja Janaka). Retold briefly: Vyasa sent his son Sukadeva to
Janaka to learn sense control; Janaka tested him (a sugar lump on the tongue; a full cup of milk carried around the palace
among entertainers) and found him unmoved; Janaka declared Sukadeva had already conquered his senses. **Requires source review:** the
primary source of this account is not given in the Encyclopedia. Lesson: the one who narrates the rasa-lila has conquered
his senses. Devotional lesson: hear it from such a person.
No contemporary illustration is added; none improves on this.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections
1. *If attraction comes from Krsna, why avoid it?* Because the material form is a reflection that binds (CC Adi 4.164).
2. *Is rasa-lila a licence?* No. SB 10.33.30 forbids imitation.
3. *Why discuss this with brahmacaris?* BIKC: understanding the origin gives clear, unsentimental understanding for progress. If a student finds it distracting, he should step back and speak with a senior.
4. *Can I hear rasa-lila now?* Hear from a qualified devotee, in a Krsna conscious setting, with faith (10.33.39; KB 33). Ask your guru.
5. *I am struggling with desire.* Speak to a trusted senior. No shame. Tele-MANAS 14416 (India) or local services if distress is severe.

## L. Case studies
**Invented case for discussion.** Mohan hears a classmate say "Krsna's pastimes are just romance." He is unsure.
- Principles: CC Adi 4.164-165; SB 10.33.39.
- Questions: What is the difference? How should Mohan respond, calmly?
- Analysis: Say that the tradition distinguishes lust from love by the object (self vs Krsna), and offer to share the source.
- Takeaway: Answer with the text; do not argue.

## M. Discussion and reflection
**M.1** (1) What is adi-rasa? *The original, pure spiritual exchange (SB 5.25.5 purport).* (2) Gold and iron: of what? *Spiritual and material lust.* (3) What is lust, what is love? *CC Adi 4.165.* (4) Should we imitate? *No (SB 10.33.30).* (5) What cures lust? *Faithful hearing (10.33.39).*
**M.2** *Object test (10 min):* pairs state in their own words the "object of desire" in CC Adi 4.165. *Q and A (15 min):* written anonymous questions.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary
BIKC asks where the sex desire in the world comes from, and answers that its origin is in God: the original, pure attraction
is in the spiritual world, and what we feel here is its distorted reflection. Srila Prabhupada's purport to SB 5.25.5
uses the word adi-rasa for that original attraction, and says spiritual and material lust are as different
as gold and iron. Caitanya-caritamrta says lust is the desire to please one's own senses and love is the desire
to please Krsna's (CC Adi 4.165). Material desire is the reflection and the main obstacle to progress. The Bhagavatam
warns that we must not imitate the Lord's pastimes (SB 10.33.30) and says that faithful hearing of them from the right
source cures lust (10.33.39). So brahmacarya means turning toward the original, with hearing, chanting and service.

## S2. Key ideas
- Original attraction belongs to Krsna.
- Lust and love differ by their object.
- Do not imitate; hear.
- The cure is bhakti.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **CC Adi 4.165** -- lust is wanting to please my senses; love is wanting to please Krsna.
- **SB 10.33.39** -- faithful hearing helps one conquer lust.

## S4. Words to know
- *Adi-rasa* -- the original spiritual attraction. · *Kama* -- lust. · *Prema* -- love of God.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *Are my urges spiritual?* They are a reflection, as different as iron from gold.
2. *May I imitate?* No (SB 10.33.30).
3. *How do I hear this?* From a qualified devotee, with faith, guided by your guru.
4. *What if I struggle?* Speak to a senior.

## S6. Check your understanding
1. What does "adi-rasa" mean? 2. Define kama and prema. 3. What cures lust per SB 10.33.39? 4. Should the pastimes be imitated?

**Answers:** 1 The original pure attraction (SB 5.25.5 purport). 2 Desire to please own senses; desire to please Krsna (CC Adi 4.165). 3 Faithful hearing. 4 No (10.33.30).
