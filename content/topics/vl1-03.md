# ART OF MIND CONTROL

> **VL1 · 45–60 min · review: sources checked, awaiting senior review.** Some listeners may live with anxiety
> or addiction: read the care note in K before teaching. IYF has no notes for this topic
> on the materials list; the page is built from scripture and the neighbouring IYF topics.

## A. Topic overview

| | |
|---|---|
| Batch / level | VL1 (Vedic Level 1), a stand-alone talk |
| Suggested duration | 45–60 min |
| Preparation time | About 90 min, the first time |
| Difficulty | Introductory |
| Prerequisites | None |
| Audience | Students and young professionals who feel distracted, restless, anxious or "out of control" with phones, anger or worry |
| Teaching objective | Offer an honest, practical picture of the mind: it is a powerful friend or enemy, hard to control, and trainable by practice, detachment and a higher taste. Let the audience try one small experiment |

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Distraction and restlessness are among the most common experiences of young people today. This is a point
where the Gita is directly relevant and the audience needs no belief to test it: sit for one minute and watch
the mind. At VL1 the aim is **attraction through usefulness**.

How the subject grows at other levels:

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **VL1** (here) | The mind as friend or enemy; why it is hard to control; the principle of "higher taste"; a first experiment |
| VL2 | The nature of mind, intelligence and the self, argued |
| VL3 | Chanting and attention: how to hear the sound, and how to return when the mind wanders |
| SPS | Keeping attention steady across years of routine |
| DPS | Mind and the offences; steadiness over years |
| BSS | Mind and senses in brahmacari life: guarding what the mind dwells on |

**Not done here:**
- A technique that promises full control of the mind.
- Medical or psychological advice. Persistent anxiety, depression or addiction need professional help.
- Teaching meditation methods beyond a single simple trial.

## C. Central message

The mind can be our best friend or worst enemy (BG 6.5–6). It is restless and hard to control, but the Gita says
it can be brought under control by practice and detachment (BG 6.35), and the most lasting help is a higher taste.

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. State the Gita's picture of the mind as a friend or an enemy.
2. Explain why forcing the senses without changing the mind does not work (BG 3.6), and what "higher taste" means (BG 2.59).
3. Trace the chain from dwelling on an object to loss of intelligence (BG 2.62–63) in a simple everyday example.
4. Try one concrete exercise and notice the result.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** six references are enough for the talk (BG 6.5, 6.34, 6.35, 2.62–63, 2.59, 3.6). Others prepare for questions.

**BG 6.5**: Verified (exists; translation read) · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/5/
> "One must deliver himself with the help of his mind, and not degrade himself. The mind is the friend of the conditioned soul, and his enemy as well."
- **Context:** Krsna's teaching on controlling the mind in the sixth chapter.
- **Teaching:** The mind can raise or lower us; responsibility sits with us.
- **Application (preacher's):** Ask: "When has your mind been your friend this week, and when your enemy?"
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 6.6**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/6/
> "For him who has conquered the mind, the mind is the best of friends; but for one who has failed to do so, his mind will remain the greatest enemy."
- **Teaching:** The friend-or-enemy picture depends on control.
- **Use:** in the talk, with BG 6.5.

**BG 6.34**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/34/
> "The mind is restless, turbulent, obstinate and very strong, O Kṛṣṇa, and to subdue it, I think, is more difficult than controlling the wind."
- **Context:** Arjuna's honest objection. He is a warrior and says the mind is harder to control than the wind.
- **Teaching:** Scripture does not pretend this is easy. Arjuna says so, and Krsna does not dispute it.
- **Application (preacher's):** Use Arjuna's honesty to normalise the audience's own struggle: they are
  not unusually weak.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 6.35**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/35/
> "…it is undoubtedly very difficult to curb the restless mind, but it is possible by suitable practice and by detachment."
- **Teaching:** Krsna agrees it is difficult, and says it is possible through *practice* and *detachment*.
- **Purport point:** Krsna accepts Arjuna's difficulty. For this age Prabhupada names the practice as devotional
  service, first of all hearing about Krsna: "The more one hears about Kṛṣṇa, the more one becomes enlightened
  and detached from everything that draws the mind away from Kṛṣṇa."
- **Application (preacher's):** Practice = repeated, small and regular; detachment = not feeding what distracts.
- **Use:** in the talk. This is the central verse.

**BG 6.26**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/26/
> "From wherever the mind wanders due to its flickering and unsteady nature, one must certainly withdraw it and bring it back under the control of the Self."
- **Teaching:** The method is not to stop the mind from ever wandering but to bring it back, again and again.
- **Application (preacher's):** Practising return, not perfection, is the exercise in P.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 2.62–63**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/62/ (and 63)
> 2.62: "While contemplating the objects of the senses, a person develops attachment for them, and from such attachment lust develops, and from lust anger arises."
- **Teaching:** A chain. Dwelling → attachment → lust (intense desire) → anger (2.62); then delusion → bewildered memory → lost intelligence (2.63).
- **Application (preacher's):** The chain is useful for catching the early link: what we dwell on (2.62), not just the explosion.
  Invented example in L.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 2.59**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/59/
> "…ceasing such engagements by experiencing a higher taste, he is fixed in consciousness."
- **Teaching:** Restricting the senses leaves the taste; a higher taste is what lets go.
- **Purport point:** Restriction by rules is like a patient kept from certain foods: he neither likes it nor
  loses the taste. Restrictions help "until one actually has a taste for Kṛṣṇa consciousness"; then one
  "automatically loses his taste for pale things."
- **Application (preacher's):** Replace, don't only remove: a better thing to give attention to.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 3.6**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/6/
> "One who restrains the senses of action but whose mind dwells on sense objects certainly deludes himself and is called a pretender."
- **Teaching:** Outer control without inner change is self-deception.
- **Application (preacher's):** Deleting an app but still dwelling on it is not control. Use gently; the word is strong.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**For questions (K):**
- **BG 6.17** (Verified): "He who is regulated in his habits of eating, sleeping, recreation and work can
  mitigate all material pains by practicing the yoga system." Helps with "where do I start?"
- **NOI 1** (Verified): a sober person who can tolerate the urge to speak, the mind's demands, the actions of

  Gajendra, fixed his mind in his heart with perfect intelligence and chanted a mantra which he had learned in his

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** The mind is a tool of the self. It is not the self (that is VL2). Used well it lifts us; left
   unguided it drags us (BG 6.5–6). It is "restless, turbulent, obstinate" (BG 6.34).
2. **Why it matters.** Whatever we want to do (study, keep a friendship, rest) passes through attention.
   Controlling attention is controlling the day.
3. **Connections.** To happiness (VL1-01: likes vs good), to habits (VL1-04), to chanting and hearing (VL3).
4. **Consequences.** A person who learns to return attention gently is less at the mercy of mood and impulse,
   and becomes slower to anger. *(Preacher's observation, not a verse.)*
5. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - **"Control means suppress."** The Gita says the *taste* remains until a higher taste is found (BG 2.59).
   - **"A technique will fix it quickly."** Even Krsna says "practice and detachment" (BG 6.35). It is gradual.
   - **"If I can't do it, I'm weak or sinful."** Arjuna himself calls it harder than controlling the wind (BG 6.34).
   - **Spiritual bypassing.** Anxiety, depression and addiction can need professional care. The mind is not a moral failing.
6. **Pitch for VL1.** Say "mind", "attention", "friend and enemy". Define "detachment" as "not feeding it".
   No claim to authority: "Arjuna asked the same question you are asking. Here is the answer he got."

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet, give your name, and say you will run a one-minute experiment, with nothing to believe.
2. Explain the rules: eyes closed or lowered gaze, watch the breath, count each time the mind leaves.
3. Run the minute in silence; ring a bell or say "time".
4. Ask for a show of hands (more than five? more than ten?) and thank the room.
5. Say "you are in good company" and use the transition below.

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the room's own life)
1. Where does your mind go when you are meant to be studying or working?
2. What is one thing that reliably pulls your attention away?
3. When did you last do something you did not mean to do, "just for a minute"?

*Adapting:* examples work anywhere: a phone at night for a student or office worker; for a farmer or labourer, thoughts that return to worry while the hands work; for a manager, an inbox that steals an hour.

- **Opening experiment:** "Close your eyes (or lower your gaze). For one minute, try to think of nothing but
  your breath. Each time your mind goes elsewhere, silently count one." Then ask for a show of hands: how
  many counted more than five? More than ten? "You are in good company. Arjuna was too."
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** You decide to sleep early. At 11 p.m. you "just check" one message, and it is 1.30 a.m. *(Invented case for discussion.)*
- **Transition:** "Five thousand years ago, a great warrior told his teacher that the mind is harder to control than the wind."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** Exams, relationships, phones, anger, anxiety. Attention decides how
  these go.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **The mind can be friend or enemy.** BG 6.5–6. *Example:* the mind that helps you finish an assignment versus the one that replays a worry. *Transition:* why is it so hard?
2. **It really is hard.** BG 6.34. *Example:* the one-minute experiment. *Transition:* is there a way?
3. **The chain begins with what we dwell on.** BG 2.62–63. *Example:* a message you keep re-reading leads to a worry, then anger. *(Application, preacher's.)* *Transition:* so where do we intervene?
4. **Practice, return and higher taste.** BG 6.35, 6.26, 2.59. *Example:* bringing attention back, without scolding, each time. *Transition:* the experiment.
5. **Outer show without inner change is not control.** BG 3.6. *Example:* putting the phone away but staying preoccupied with it. *Transition:* to the takeaway.

## J. Stories and illustrations

- **Arjuna's question** (BG 6.33–35). Tell it as the conversation it is: Arjuna says it is hard; Krsna agrees and
  gives a way. Keep to the text: no added dialogue.
- **The one-minute experiment** (H) is the main illustration.
- **Gajendra fixes his mind** (SB 8.3.1; added 28 Oct 2026). The elephant king, caught by the crocodile and
  unable to be rescued by his herd (SB 8.2.32), "fixed his mind in his heart with perfect intelligence" and remembered a prayer from his
  previous life. Keep to the verse: the mind is steadied by intelligence, and the remembering is "by the grace of
  Kṛṣṇa". The full narrative is SB 8.2–4; read it before adding details. *(Lead: HG Devakinandan Prabhu's seminar
  "The Art of Mind Control", ISKCON Desire Tree, `research/desire-tree/…/Devakinandan_Pr_Seminars_-_The_Art_of_Mind_Control.txt`,
  which builds the whole talk on this verse with BG 6.34–35.)*
- **The husband and the newspaper** (Preaching Encyclopedia, Holy Name section, "Understanding the nature of
  mind", VOICE; the speaker credits the example to Sacinandana Swami): the wife reports the day's problems, the
  husband says "hmm, hmm" behind his newspaper and hears nothing; later he cannot repeat a word. The chapter uses
  it for inattentive chanting; at VL1 it illustrates a mind that is "present" but elsewhere. A teaching analogy,
  not scripture.
- **Contemporary:** the "just one more message" case (invented, labelled as such).

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

> **Care note:** Someone in the room may live with anxiety, panic, obsessive thoughts, depression or an
> addiction. This talk is about everyday attention, not treatment. If a participant describes serious
> difficulty, speak privately, encourage them to see a doctor or a counsellor, and say plainly that
> professional help is compatible with spiritual life *(pastoral guidance, not a scriptural claim)*. Do not
> suggest that mantra or discipline replaces treatment.

1. **"Is the mind really separate from me?"**
   - At VL1, invite observation: you can *watch* your thoughts, so who is watching? The fuller argument is for VL2.
2. **"Why can't I just stop a bad habit?"**
   - Because the taste remains (BG 2.59). Practice, detachment and a higher taste work together. See VL1-04.
3. **"Isn't suppression unhealthy?"**
   - A fair concern. The Gita itself warns against outer restraint with an unchanged mind (BG 3.6). The
     answer is to redirect, not to pretend.
4. **"Does meditation or chanting actually work?"**
   - Test it. Try the experiment. The tradition recommends chanting as the main means in this age (see
     VL3); here, invite a single trial and no commitment.
5. **"What if I fail every time?"**
   - Arjuna himself admitted the struggle openly. The practice is returning (BG 6.26), not never wandering.
6. **"I can't stop worrying."**
   - *Pastoral guidance:* thank them, speak privately, and suggest professional support.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The phone at midnight.** Kabir resolves to sleep early. He checks one message, sees a post that
irritates him, replies, and is awake at 2 a.m., angry, with an exam in the morning.
- **Principles:** BG 2.62–63 (dwelling → attachment → anger → lost judgment); BG 6.35 (practice and detachment).
- **Questions:** Where was the earliest link in the chain? What could Kabir do at that moment? What could he set up earlier in the day?
- **Suggested analysis:** The easiest place to intervene is early (not feeding the object), and by having the phone
  out of reach. This is a practical application, not a verse.
- **Takeaway:** Intervene early in the chain.

**Case 2: The pretender.** Sara deletes a social media app but spends the day thinking about what she is missing.
- **Principles:** BG 3.6; BG 2.59.
- **Questions:** Has anything changed? What higher taste could replace it?
- **Takeaway:** Control is inner as well as outer.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first; praise every try)
1. What can the mind be, according to BG 6.5-6? *A friend or an enemy.*
2. Did Arjuna say the mind is easy to control? *No; harder than the wind (BG 6.34), and Krsna agrees.*
3. What two means does BG 6.35 give? *Practice and detachment.*
4. Put in order: anger, dwelling on an object, desire, attachment. *Dwelling, attachment, desire, anger (BG 2.62).*
5. What do you do when the mind wanders in practice? *Bring it back, again and again (BG 6.26).*
6. Why does a higher taste help? *It lets the lower attraction fall away (BG 2.59), where force alone leaves the taste.*

**M.2 Activity (about 6 minutes): "Three-minute attention"**
1. 1 min: choose one object (the breath, a candle, one word) and settle.
2. 3 min: hold attention on it; each time the mind leaves, quietly return, no scolding.
3. 2 min: volunteers say what they noticed; thank each one; praise returning, not "success".

**M.3 Reflection**
- **Reflection:** Which link in the chain (dwelling, attachment, desire, anger) do you usually notice first?
- **Application (private, optional):** Pick one small distraction and decide what you will do instead for three days. *(Nobody needs to share.)*

## P. Practical takeaway

- **Main lesson:** The mind can be trained, slowly, by practice, by not feeding what disturbs it, and by finding a higher taste.
- **One action (VL1):** Do the three-minute exercise once a day for a week: sit, attend to one thing
  (the breath, or a short sound you like), and each time the mind wanders, bring it back without scolding.
  *(A simple attention exercise; a chanting trial can be offered separately and is not required.)*
- **Reflection question:** What do I give my attention to most, and does it help me?
- **Follow-up:** VL1-04 "Power of habits". Reading: *Bhagavad-gita As It Is*, Chapter 6, verses 5–6 and 33–35.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

The mind can be our best friend or our worst enemy (BG 6.5-6). It is restless and strong; even the great warrior Arjuna said it was harder to control than the wind (BG 6.34). Krsna did not say it was easy. He agreed it is difficult, and said it is possible through practice and detachment (BG 6.35). You can see it yourself: sit for one minute and count how often your mind leaves the breath.

The Gita shows how trouble grows: when we keep dwelling on something, attachment comes, then desire, then anger (BG 2.62-63). Holding back by force is not enough. A person who restrains the body but keeps dwelling on the object in the mind is pretending (BG 3.6), and the taste remains until a higher taste is found (BG 2.59). When the mind wanders, we bring it back, again and again; returning is the practice (BG 6.26).

A small daily exercise is the way to start: three minutes on one object, returning without scolding yourself. If you feel anxious most of the time, speak to a doctor or counsellor; that fits well with spiritual life.

## S2. Key ideas

- The mind can help or harm us (BG 6.5–6).
- It is hard to control, and saying so is honest (BG 6.34).
- Practice and detachment make it possible (BG 6.35).
- What we dwell on shapes what we feel and do (BG 2.62–63).
- A higher taste helps more than force alone (BG 2.59).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **BG 6.5:** "The mind is the friend of the conditioned soul, and his enemy as well." *For me:* my attention can help me or hurt me.
- **BG 6.35:** Difficult to curb, "but it is possible by suitable practice and by detachment". *For me:* small, steady steps count.
- **BG 6.26:** Wherever the mind wanders, "bring it back". *For me:* success is returning, not never wandering.

## S4. Words to know

- **Detachment:** not feeding what disturbs the mind.
- **Practice:** doing something small and regular.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Is suppression unhealthy?** The Gita warns that outer restraint with an unchanged mind is empty (BG 3.6). The answer is to redirect attention.
2. **Why can't I just stop a bad habit?** The taste remains until a higher taste is found (BG 2.59).
3. **Does meditation work?** Test it with the small exercise. Nobody is asked to believe first.
4. **What if I fail every time?** Returning is the practice (BG 6.26).
5. **What if I feel anxious most of the time?** Speak to a doctor or counsellor. Professional help fits with spiritual life.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. In BG 6.5–6, the mind can be: (a) only a friend, (b) a friend or an enemy, (c) neither.
2. What two things does BG 6.35 say will bring the mind under control?
3. True or false: Krsna says the mind is easy to control.
4. Put in order: anger, dwelling on an object, desire, attachment.
5. Why is "deleting an app but thinking about it all day" not real control (BG 3.6)?

**Answers:** 1. (b). 2. Suitable practice and detachment. 3. False; Krsna agrees it is difficult (BG 6.35). 4. Dwelling, attachment, desire, anger (BG 2.62). 5. Because the mind still dwells on the object; the Gita calls outer restraint without inner change self-deception.
