## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on dps-06 (the ten offences, listed and explained for devotees learning to chant) and trd-08 (how sixteen rounds stay
alive over years, from namabhasa toward suddha-nama). This page does not repeat either. It takes the one book Srila Bhaktivinoda
Thakura wrote on the holy name as a *system*, in the voice of Haridasa Thakura answering Lord Caitanya, and gives residents what
dps-06 does not: the remedy for each offence, the anatomy of namabhasa (its four attitudes and its two kinds, shadow and
reflection), and the Thakura's exact cure for inattentive chanting.

TRD residents chant more than anyone they teach, and teach chanting more than anyone else in the movement. Their danger is
not ignorance of the list but a chanting that has settled into a shadow of the name, with one or two offences grown quiet and
permanent. Haridasa Thakura's words to the Lord (CC Antya 3), Ajamila's story (SB 6.2) and the Thakura's book give them the
instruments to examine their own rounds honestly and to help others do the same.

## C. Central message

The holy name is Krishna Himself and acts even when uttered improperly (CC Antya 3.59), yet "simply by chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa one
does not become ecstatic" because there are offences (CC Adi 8.24). Bhaktivinoda Thakura maps every chanting onto three levels:
offensive chanting, the shadow of the name (namabhasa), and the pure name. The way up is not more rounds alone but removing
the offence that blocks the taste, each with its own remedy, and chanting attentively in the association of devotees.

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. State the three levels of chanting (nama-aparadha, namabhasa, suddha-nama) and say what each gives: hell or stagnation,
   liberation, prema (*Harinama-cintamani* ch. 3–4; CC Antya 3.61–65).
2. Name the four attitudes of namabhasa with their scriptural examples (SB 6.2.14; CC Antya 3.54; HNC ch. 3), and tell the
   shadow (chaya) from the reflection (pratibimba).
3. Give the ten offences as Srila Prabhupada lists them in the CC Adi 8.24 purport, and the Padma Purana verse behind the
   first, second, seventh, eighth, ninth and tenth.
4. Give the *Harinama-cintamani* remedy for the first offence and for inattention (ch. 4 and 12), and apply the latter to their
   own rounds this week.
5. Explain from CC Antya 4.71 and SB 1.2.17 why the offenceless name gives prema "very easily", and why that is not a shortcut.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**CC Adi 8.24 and purport** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/adi/8/24/
> "There are offenses to be considered while chanting the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra. Therefore simply by chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa one does not become ecstatic."
- **Context:** Krsnadasa Kaviraja explains why he recommends chanting *sri-krsna-caitanya prabhu-nityananda* before the
  maha-mantra: the Panca-tattva's names carry no consideration of offence.
- **Purport point:** Prabhupada gives the ten offences with the Padma Purana verses. First: "To blaspheme the great saintly
  persons who are engaged in preaching the glories of the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra is the worst offense at the lotus feet of the
  holy name." (*satāṁ nindā nāmnaḥ paramam aparādhaṁ vitanute*). Second (*śivasya śrī-viṣṇor ya iha guṇa-nāmādi-sakalaṁ*):
  separating the Lord from His name, form, qualities and pastimes, or equating Siva's name with Visnu's. Third, *guror avajñā*:
  "to consider the spiritual master to be material and therefore to envy his exalted position." Fourth, *śruti-śāstra-nindanam*.
  Fifth, *artha-vādaḥ*: "to consider the glories of the holy name to be exaggerations." Sixth, *hari-nāmni kalpanam*: to
  consider the name imaginary. Seventh (*nāmno balād yasya hi pāpa-buddhir*): sinning on the strength of the name, "the greatest
  offense at the lotus feet of hari-nāma." Eighth (*dharma-vrata-tyāga-hutādi-sarva-śubha-kriyā-sāmyam api pramādaḥ*): "It is
  offensive to consider the chanting of the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra to be a religious ritualistic ceremony." Ninth (*aśraddadhāne
  vimukhe 'py aśṛṇvati*): "It is an offense to preach the glories of the holy name among persons who have no intelligence or no
  faith in the subject matter." Tenth (*śrute 'pi nāma-māhātmye yaḥ prīti-rahito naraḥ*): hearing the glories yet continuing
  "thinking 'I am this body and everything belonging to this body is mine [ahaṁ mameti]'".
- **Teaching:** this is the authorised list; Bhaktivinoda's chapters 4–13 follow the same ten in the same order, with
  "inattention" as the ninth in his count (see F.3 for the difference in numbering).
- **Use:** in the talk, read in full once; the residents already know the list from dps-06, so the stress is on the verses.

**CC Antya 4.71 and purport** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/4/71/
> "Of the nine processes of devotional service, the most important is to always chant the holy name of the Lord. If one does so, avoiding the ten kinds of offenses, one very easily obtains the most valuable love of Godhead."
- **Context:** the Lord instructs Sanatana Gosvami.
- **Purport point:** Prabhupada quotes Jiva Gosvami's *Bhakti-sandarbha* (270): "Chanting the holy name is the chief means of
  attaining love of Godhead. This chanting or devotional service does not depend on any paraphernalia, nor on one's having taken
  birth in a good family."
- **Teaching:** the condition for prema "very easily" is *avoiding the ten offences*; the whole book of the Thakura is a
  commentary on that condition.
- **Use:** in the talk, as the thesis.

**SB 6.2.9–10, 6.2.14, 6.2.18** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/6/2/14/
> 6.2.14: "One who chants the holy name of the Lord is immediately freed from the reactions of unlimited sins, even if he chants indirectly [to indicate something else], jokingly, for musical entertainment, or even neglectfully. This is accepted by all the learned scholars of the scriptures."
> 6.2.18: "As a fire burns dry grass to ashes, so the holy name of the Lord, whether chanted knowingly or unknowingly, burns to ashes, without fail, all the reactions of one's sinful activities."
- **Context:** the Visnudutas to the Yamadutas over Ajamila, who called his son "Narayana" at death.
- **Teaching:** the four ways of 6.2.14 (*sāṅketyam*, indirectly; *pārihāsyam*, jokingly; *stobham*, for musical entertainment;
  *helanam*, neglectfully) are the four attitudes of namabhasa that Bhaktivinoda names in chapter 3. Even these free one from
  all sins (6.2.9–10: the best atonement for every sinner).
- **Purport point (6.2.18):** "Fire will act, regardless of whether handled by an innocent child or by someone well aware of its
  power."
- **Use:** in the talk, as the scriptural root of namabhasa.

**CC Antya 3.54, 3.59–65** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/3/59/
> 3.59: "The letters of the holy name have so much spiritual potency that they act even when uttered improperly."
> 3.61: "If one offenselessly utters the holy name even imperfectly, one can be freed from all the results of sinful life."
> 3.63: "Even a faint light from the holy name of the Lord can eradicate all the reactions of sinful life."
> 3.65: "Because of even the faintest rays of the effulgence of the Lord's holy name, one can attain liberation. We can see this in all the revealed scriptures. The evidence appears in the story of Ajāmila in Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam."
- **Context:** Haridasa Thakura answers the Lord's question about the yavanas who say "hā rāma, hā rāma" (3.54: "Just see
  their good fortune!").
- **Purport point (3.59):** Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati: *vyavahita* ("improperly uttered") does not mean the mundane sound of the
  letters; negligent utterance for materialistic sense gratification is not the transcendental name.
- **Teaching:** namabhasa gives liberation (3.65) but the word in 3.61 is *offenselessly*: the shadow of the name works where
  there is no offence. This is the exact hinge of the Thakura's book. *Also in trd-08* (for keeping rounds alive); here for
  the three-level map.
- **Use:** in the talk and in J.

**CC Madhya 25.30; CC Antya 20.16** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/20/16/
> 25.30: "In this Age of Kali, one cannot attain liberation without taking to the devotional service of the Lord. In this age, even if one chants the holy name of Kṛṣṇa imperfectly, he still attains liberation very easily."
> 20.16 (Siksastaka 2): "I am so unfortunate that I commit offenses while chanting the holy name, and therefore I do not achieve attachment for chanting."
- **Teaching:** 25.30 restates namabhasa's power for the preacher; 20.16 is the Lord's own diagnosis of why taste does not
  come: offences. trd-08 uses this verse for the mood of chanting; here it names the cause the Thakura treats.
- **Use:** in the talk (20.16 is the bridge from glories to offences).

**NOI 7; SB 2.3.24** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/7/
- **NOI 7:** the tongue with the jaundice of ignorance cannot taste the sugar candy of the name; the taste awakens by daily
  careful chanting. **SB 2.3.24:** "Certainly that heart is steel-framed which, in spite of one's chanting the holy name of the Lord
  with concentration, does not change when ecstasy takes place, tears fill the eyes and the hairs stand on end."
- **Teaching:** the Bhagavatam's own test of chanting: does the heart change? If not, something blocks it; the Thakura's map
  says what.
- **Use:** NOI 7 in the talk (used by dps-15 and trd-08 for other points); SB 2.3.24 for the self-check in M.

**CC Madhya 19.160; SB 1.2.17; CC Madhya 15.108; NOI 5** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/19/160/
- **19.160 purport:** "If one chants the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra while committing offenses, these unwanted creepers will grow."
  (The weeds themselves are trd-49.) **SB 1.2.17:** Krishna in the heart cleanses the desire for enjoyment from one who hears
  His words with attention. **15.108:** initiation is not required for chanting; the Lord's cult of chanting is for everyone.
  **NOI 5 purport:** the Padma Purana's offences are to be avoided by the chanter; cross-reference only.
- **Use:** 19.160 for F.4 (why offences stall the whole creeper); 1.2.17 for why attentive hearing cures (ch. 12); 15.108 and

**CC Antya 3.124, 3.241** — Verified. Haridasa's vow (ten million names a month; "As long as the vow to chant is unfulfilled, I do
not desire anything else"). **Use:** in J, as the standard of attention the Thakura's chapter 12 points to; the full story is

***Sri Harinama-cintamani* (Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura), Desire Tree edition (OCR), translator not named in the file** — read
against the file, 2 Nov 2026 (`research/books/Bhaktivinoda_Thakura_Hari_Nama_Cintamani2.txt`). The book is Haridasa Thakura's
answers to Lord Caitanya at Puri. Chapter map: 1 the glories of the name; 2 the acceptance of the name; 3 namabhasa; 4–13 the ten
offences, one chapter each; 14 offences in Deity service (sevaparadha); 15 confidential bhajana (background only).
- **The three levels (ch. 3):** "Anarthafree namabhasa chanting becomes suddhanama, which gives Krsnaprema"; "But namabhasa
  chanting can never give Krsnaprema." The chapter's own summary of namabhasa's reach: "Namabhasa is greater than the result of
  studying all the Vedas, of visiting every pilgrimage place, or of performing every possible altruistic and pious work."
- **Four attitudes (ch. 3):** "The four attitudes in namabhasa chanting are sanketa (unintentionally or coincidentally),
  parihasa (in jest or ridicule), stobha (derisively) and hela (with disregard and neglect)." Sanketa is of two kinds: chanting
  with a material conception of the name, or being reminded of the sound by another word, as the yavanas' *haram*. "Chanting in
  jest - parihasa, like Jarasandha results in liberation, as does chanting derisively (stobha, like Sisupal)."
- **Shadow and reflection (ch. 3, footnote 1):** *abhasa* is of two kinds. Chaya-namabhasa, "the faint shadowy name", comes from
  chanting "with anarthas born of" ignorance and weakness of heart, and is "rectified by continuous chanting of the Lord's holy
  name." Pratibimba-namabhasa is the name chanted with Mayavada or other "distorted philosophy"; it "appears, bequeathing
  sayujya-mukti upon the Mayavadis", and the scriptures, "while praising namabhasa, profusely condemn pratibimba namabhasa."
- **Faith changes the level (ch. 3):** "Faithful chanting that is yet afflicted by anarthas is known as sraddhanamabhasa." "Chanting
  with faith establishes one on the platform of sambandha-jnana, which results in rati or attraction for the holy name."
- **The ten offences and how they arise (ch. 4, Bhaktivinoda's introductory note):** "The nama and seva aparadhas initially arise
  from anarthas that cause namabhasa. If these aparadhas are not corrected and become intentional, they not only impede one's
  spiritual progress, but remove one from devotional service entirely, placing him in hell."
- **Remedy for the first offence (ch. 4):** "If anyone offends a devotee in a moment of delusion or madness, he must fall at the
  devotee's feet and repent bitterly and, weeping and full of contrition, beg forgiveness." "A devotee is very merciful; his heart
  will soften and he will embrace the offender, thus exonerating him from his offenses."
- **Inattention, the Thakura's ninth (ch. 12):** "Inattention is of three kinds. The first is called audasinya, or 'apathetic
  devotional service', meaning a lack of fixed resolve in sadhana. The second type is jadya, 'laziness' or even 'inertia'. The
  third kind is viskepa, which means 'distraction'". The remedy for apathy: "One must make it his daily routine to chant for an
  hour in the company of saintly Vaisnavas in a sacred, undisturbed place." "Another effective method for ridding oneself of apathy
  towards the holy name is to sit in a closed room alone and meditate on the name as did the previous sages. Or one can cover the
  head and face with a cloth and concentrate on the sound of the holy name." For laziness: associate with devotees who "never
  waste a single moment in useless talks or activities" and increase the chanting step by step. For distraction: it "breeds
  illusion", attraction "to wealth, women, position, success and the cheating propensity"; the cure is the same association and
  attention.
- **Remedy for the eighth offence, equating the name with pious ritual (ch. 11):** "Only the association of devotees can exculpate
  such offenses"; the offender serves a Vaisnava of low birth, taking his foot-dust and remnants, "to stamp out any false
  understandings of designation and caste distinctions."
- **Use:** in the talk, chapter by chapter, each remedy in one line. The Thakura adds the method; the authority for the list is
  Prabhupada's purport above.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The name is Krishna; the block is in us.** The name's letters "act even when uttered improperly" (CC Antya 3.59); the name burns sin like
   fire burning grass (SB 6.2.18). So when years of chanting bring no taste, the cause is not in the name. The Lord Himself says
   where it is: "I commit offenses while chanting the holy name, and therefore I do not achieve attachment" (CC Antya 20.16).
2. **Three levels, three fruits.** Bhaktivinoda's map: *nama-aparadha* (offensive chanting) stalls progress and, if the offences
   become intentional, ends in hell (HNC ch. 4 note); *namabhasa* (the shadow) frees from all sin and gives liberation (SB 6.2.14;
   CC Antya 3.65) "but namabhasa chanting can never give Krsnaprema" (ch. 3); *suddha-nama* gives prema (CC Antya 4.71). Most
   sincere chanting is namabhasa with faith (sraddha-namabhasa), which "clears away anarthas very easily and quickly" and becomes
   the pure name when the anarthas go.
3. **Two shadows, only one of them hopeful.** Chaya-namabhasa, the shadow from ignorance and weakness, is cured by continuing to
   chant. Pratibimba-namabhasa, the reflection distorted by Mayavada or other false philosophy, gives only the Mayavadi's
   merging and is condemned (ch. 3). This is why the Thakura treats siddhanta as part of chanting, and why trd-35 belongs beside
   this page.
4. **The offences are not only a list; they are the weeds' seed.** "If one chants the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra while committing
   offenses, these unwanted creepers will grow" (CC Madhya 19.160 purport). The first offence is the worst (Adi 8.24 purport),
   and it is the one that grows quietly among seniors (trd-50). The Thakura's remedy is concrete: fall at the devotee's feet and
   beg.
5. **Numbering.** Prabhupada's list (Adi 8.24) counts "exaggeration" and "imaginary" as the fifth and sixth; Bhaktivinoda joins
   them as his fifth and so counts inattention (pramada) as the ninth and bodily attachment as the tenth. Teach Prabhupada's
   order; say that the Thakura's chapter 12 treats inattention, which Prabhupada's eighth verse names (*pramādaḥ*).
6. **Why attention is the resident's own offence.** Three kinds (ch. 12): apathy (no fixed resolve), laziness, distraction. Each
   has a cure the resident can do today: a fixed hour of japa with devotees in a quiet holy place; chanting alone in a closed room
   or with the head covered; refusing useless talk; increasing the count step by step. SB 1.2.17 gives the reason it works: Krishna
   in the heart cleanses the one who hears with attention.
7. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *"Namabhasa is enough; I will be liberated."* Liberation is not prema (ch. 3); the Lord wants the devotee's love, not his exit.
   - *"The remedies are rituals."* They are acts of humility toward devotees, which is the opposite of the first offence.
   - *"Attention is a technique."* It is service: giving the name one's whole hearing (SB 1.2.17).
   - *"I should judge others' chanting by this map."* The map is for one's own rounds; judging another chanter is the first offence.
8. **Pitch for TRD.** Residents know the list. Spend the time on the three levels, the two shadows, the remedies, and chapter 12
   applied to their own rounds this week.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet the residents. Say: we have all chanted sixteen rounds for years; today we read the one book our acharya wrote on the
   holy name, to find out exactly what our chanting is and what stands between it and taste.
2. Say that the book is Haridasa Thakura answering Lord Caitanya, and that its map has three levels: offence, shadow, pure name.
3. Ask H.2 question 1 for private writing. Then question 2; take three answers.
4. Read CC Antya 20.16 (Siksastaka 2) aloud and say that the Lord Himself names the cause we will study.

**H.2 Opening questions**
1. Think of your most attentive japa of this month and your least. What was different: place, time, company, resolve? (Private.)
2. Which of the ten offences do you teach most often to new devotees, and which do you think about least for yourself?
3. If a guest says, "I only chant for peace of mind," which level of the Thakura's map is that, and what is its fruit?

## I. Core teaching points

1. **The letters of the name "act even when uttered improperly"** (CC Antya 3.59; SB 6.2.18). *Example:* Ajamila's "Narayana" called to his son.
   *Transition:* then why no ecstasy after years?
2. **Offences block the fruit** (CC Adi 8.24; Antya 20.16; Antya 4.71). *Transition:* so what exactly is our chanting?
3. **Three levels** (HNC ch. 3–4): offence, shadow, pure name; fruits hell/stagnation, liberation, prema. *Example:* the yavanas'
   "hā rāma" (Antya 3.54). *Transition:* the shadow has an anatomy.
4. **Four attitudes and two shadows** (SB 6.2.14; HNC ch. 3): sanketa, parihasa, stobha, hela; chaya (hopeful) and pratibimba
   (Mayavada, condemned). *Transition:* and faith changes everything.
5. **Sraddha-namabhasa becomes the pure name when anarthas go** (HNC ch. 3). *Transition:* what removes them?
6. **Each offence has a remedy** (HNC ch. 4–13): fall at the devotee's feet and beg (first); serve a low-born Vaisnava's feet
   (eighth); association of devotees in every case. *Transition:* the resident's own offence.
7. **Inattention, three kinds, three cures** (HNC ch. 12; SB 1.2.17; SB 2.3.24 as the test). *Transition:* the standard, Haridasa.

## J. Stories and illustrations

**Ajamila** (SB 6.2.9–18, in context of SB 6.1–3). The fallen brahmana calls his youngest son, Narayana, at death; the Visnudutas
stop the Yamadutas: the chanting of the holy name is the best atonement for a thief, a drunkard, a killer of a brahmana, "and for
all other sinful men" (6.2.9–10); it acts "even if he chants indirectly [to indicate something else], jokingly, for musical
entertainment, or even neglectfully" (6.2.14); as fire burns dry grass (6.2.18). *Devotional lesson:* this is namabhasa's power,
and Haridasa Thakura cites it as the proof (CC Antya 3.65). Keep to the verses; the full chapter is read in SB class.

**Haridasa Thakura and the yavanas** (CC Antya 3.49–65). The Lord asks about the yavanas who say "hā rāma"; Haridasa: "Just see their
good fortune!" (3.54), and explains: the name's letters act even uttered improperly (3.59), "If one offenselessly utters the holy
name even imperfectly, one can be freed from all the results of sinful life" (3.61), and even a faint light of the name gives
liberation (3.63, 3.65). *Devotional lesson:* the namacarya's hope for everyone, with the one condition, *offenselessly*. The
same conversation, told for keeping rounds alive, is trd-08.

**Haridasa's vow** (CC Antya 3.124, 3.241). "I have vowed to chant ten million names in a month" (3.124); to Maya herself: "As long
as the vow to chant is unfulfilled, I do not desire anything else" (3.241). *Devotional lesson:* the attention and resolve the
Thakura's chapter 12 asks for, shown by the one whose words the book records. The full narratives are dps-15 and bss-12.

**Jarasandha and Sisupala** (HNC ch. 3, citing their chanting in jest and derision as namabhasa that gave liberation). *Use:* one
sentence, as the Thakura's own examples; do not narrate beyond what he says.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"If namabhasa gives liberation, why worry about offences at all?"** Because liberation is not prema. "Namabhasa chanting can
   never give Krsnaprema" (HNC ch. 3). The Lord's promise of prema "very easily" is for chanting that avoids the ten offences
   (CC Antya 4.71). We are not here for exit; we are here for love.
2. **"Isn't the Thakura's list of ten different from Prabhupada's?"** Same ten, different numbering (F.5). Teach Prabhupada's
   order from the Adi 8.24 purport; use the Thakura's chapters for the remedies.
3. **"I offended a senior devotee years ago. Is my chanting blocked for ever?"** No. The remedy is given: fall at his feet and beg
   forgiveness with real contrition; "his heart will soften and he will embrace the offender" (HNC ch. 4). If he has passed away
   or is unreachable, ask your spiritual master or a senior Vaisnava how to make amends; do not carry it silently.
4. **"My rounds are inattentive, but I finish them. Is that an offence?"** Prabhupada's eighth verse names *pramādaḥ*, and the
   Thakura gives inattention its own chapter (12) with three kinds and cures. Finishing the count is the floor, not the ceiling.
   Start with the chapter 12 practice this week (P).
5. **"Can I chant with my head covered, as the book says? It looks strange."** The Thakura gives it as one method against apathy
   (ch. 12), to be done alone in a closed room. It is not for public japa and not a rule; the association of devotees and a fixed
   quiet hour are his first prescription.
6. **"Is chanting without initiation namabhasa?"** The Lord's cult of chanting does not require initiation (CC Madhya 15.108);
   the level depends on offences and faith, not on formal status (HNC ch. 3 on sraddha-namabhasa). Initiation then regulates and
   protects the chanting.
7. **"Should I teach the ten offences to a first-time guest?"** The ninth offence warns against instructing the glories of the name
   to the faithless; Prabhupada's purport: "they should not be instructed about the glories or the spiritual significance of the holy
   name" at first, but given the chance to hear (Adi 8.24). Let them chant; teach the offences when faith appears.
8. **"What about chapter 15?"** It describes the confidential bhajana of the name for the devotee who has reached rati. It is
   the preacher's background (research/advanced-books.md §2), not lecture content.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The quiet critic.** Govinda Prabhu, twelve years in the ashram, chants attentively but has for two years spoken
sharply, in private, about a senior preacher he thinks is proud. His japa has become dry and he blames his schedule.
- **Dilemma:** which level is his chanting, and what is the real cause?
- **Principles:** Adi 8.24 purport (first offence, the worst); HNC ch. 4 (how offences arise from anarthas and become intentional;
  the remedy); CC Madhya 19.160 purport (weeds grow when one chants while offending); Antya 20.16.
- **Questions:** Why would dryness follow criticism rather than schedule? What exactly does the Thakura tell him to do? Who can help
  him do it without a public scene?
- **Takeaway:** the first offence is the resident's own danger; the remedy is humility toward the devotee, not more rounds.

**Case 2: The philosopher.** A new bhakta chants sixteen rounds daily but reads impersonal books and says, "the name is a sound to
merge into the One."
- **Principles:** HNC ch. 3 (pratibimba-namabhasa gives only sayujya-mukti and is condemned); Adi 8.24 purport (second offence:
  separating the name from Krishna's form and qualities; fifth and sixth: exaggeration, imaginary); trd-35.
- **Questions:** Is his chanting a shadow or a reflection? What is its fruit if nothing changes? What reading and association will
  turn the reflection into the shadow and then the name?
- **Takeaway:** siddhanta is part of chanting; the remedy is devotee association and the Bhagavatam, not silence.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

The holy name is Krishna Himself. Its letters "have so much spiritual potency that they act even when uttered improperly"
(CC Antya 3.59). Ajamila called his son "Narayana" at death and was saved; the Bhagavatam says the name frees one from all sins
"even if he chants indirectly [to indicate something else], jokingly, for musical entertainment, or even neglectfully"
(SB 6.2.14), as fire burns dry
grass (SB 6.2.18). Yet the Caitanya-caritamrta also says: "simply by chanting Hare Kṛṣṇa one does not become ecstatic", because
there are offences (CC Adi 8.24). Lord Caitanya Himself prays, "I commit offenses while chanting the holy name, and therefore I do
not achieve attachment for chanting" (CC Antya 20.16). The block is never in the name; it is in us.

Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura's *Harinama-cintamani*, written as Haridasa Thakura's answers to the Lord, maps all chanting onto
three levels. Offensive chanting (nama-aparadha) stalls progress and, if the offences become wilful, leads to hell. The shadow of
the name (namabhasa), chanting without offence but with faults of ignorance and weakness, frees from all sin and gives liberation,
in four attitudes: indirectly, in jest, derisively, neglectfully (SB 6.2.14). But "namabhasa chanting can never give Krsnaprema".
When the shadow is chanted with faith, the faults (anarthas) clear "very easily and quickly" and it becomes the pure name
(suddha-nama), which gives prema. The Thakura warns of a second kind of shadow: the name chanted with Mayavada or other false
philosophy is a distorted reflection that gives only merging, and the scriptures condemn it.

The ten offences, as Srila Prabhupada gives them from the Padma Purana (CC Adi 8.24 purport), begin with the worst: blaspheming
devotees who spread the name. Then: equating Siva with Visnu or separating the name from Krishna's form and qualities; disobeying
the guru; blaspheming scripture; calling the name's glories exaggeration; calling them imaginary; sinning on the strength of the
name; equating chanting with pious ritual (and inattention, *pramada*); teaching the glories to the faithless; and hearing the
glories yet staying attached to "I and mine". The Thakura gives a remedy for each. For the first: fall at the devotee's feet and beg
forgiveness with real contrition; "his heart will soften and he will embrace the offender". For inattention, which has three kinds
(apathy, laziness, distraction): chant a fixed hour daily with devotees in a quiet holy place, or alone in a closed room with the
head covered, refuse useless talk, and increase the count step by step. Krishna in the heart cleanses the one who hears with
attention (SB 1.2.17). The honest test of chanting is the Bhagavatam's: does the heart change (SB 2.3.24)? If one chants avoiding
the ten offences, "one very easily obtains the most valuable love of Godhead" (CC Antya 4.71).

## S2. Key ideas

- The name acts even when uttered improperly (CC Antya 3.59; SB 6.2.14, 6.2.18); the block is in us (CC Adi 8.24; Antya 20.16).
- Three levels: offensive chanting, the shadow (namabhasa), the pure name; fruits: stagnation, liberation, prema (HNC ch. 3–4;
  CC Antya 4.71).
- Namabhasa has four attitudes and two kinds: the shadow of ignorance (cured by chanting) and the reflection of false philosophy
  (condemned).
- Faith turns the shadow into the pure name as anarthas clear (HNC ch. 3).
- The worst offence is blaspheming devotees; its remedy is to fall at the devotee's feet and beg (Adi 8.24 purport; HNC ch. 4).
- Inattention has three kinds and concrete cures: a fixed quiet hour with devotees, chanting alone with attention, no idle talk
  (HNC ch. 12; SB 1.2.17).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **CC Antya 3.59:** "The letters of the holy name have so much spiritual potency that they act even when uttered improperly."
  *For me:* I never doubt the name; I examine myself.
- **CC Antya 4.71:** "If one does so, avoiding the ten kinds of offenses, one very easily obtains the most valuable love of
  Godhead." *For me:* the condition for "very easily" is mine to meet.
- **CC Antya 20.16:** "I commit offenses while chanting the holy name, and therefore I do not achieve attachment for chanting."
  *For me:* when taste is missing, I look for the offence, not for a new technique.
- **SB 2.3.24:** "that heart is steel-framed which, in spite of one's chanting the holy name of the Lord with concentration, does
  not change". *For me:* the test is a changing heart.

## S4. Words to know

- **Nama-aparadha:** an offence against the holy name; ten are listed in the Padma Purana.
- **Namabhasa:** the shadow or faint appearance of the name: chanting without offence but with faults of ignorance; it gives
  liberation.
- **Chaya / pratibimba namabhasa:** the shadow (from weakness; hopeful) / the reflection (from false philosophy; condemned).
- **Suddha-nama:** the pure name, which gives prema.
- **Pramada:** inattention; its three kinds are apathy (audasinya), laziness (jadya) and distraction (viksepa).
- **Anartha:** an unwanted thing in the heart that keeps chanting at the shadow level.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **If even careless chanting gives liberation, why worry about offences?** Because liberation is not love. The shadow of the name
   "can never give Krsnaprema" (HNC ch. 3); prema comes "very easily" only when the ten offences are avoided (CC Antya 4.71).
2. **I once spoke badly of a senior devotee. Is my chanting ruined?** No, if you act. Go to that devotee, fall at his feet and
   sincerely beg forgiveness; the Thakura says his heart will soften and he will embrace you (HNC ch. 4). If you cannot reach him,
   ask your spiritual master or a senior devotee how to make amends.
3. **I finish my rounds but my mind wanders the whole time. Is that an offence?** Inattention (*pramada*) is named in Prabhupada's
   eighth verse and has its own chapter in the Thakura's book. Finishing is the floor. Begin his cure: one fixed hour of japa daily,
   in a quiet place, with devotees (HNC ch. 12).
4. **Should I explain the ten offences to someone chanting for the first time?** Not at first. Prabhupada: such people "should be
   given the chance to hear the chanting", but "in the beginning they should not be instructed about the glories" (CC Adi 8.24
   purport). Let them chant; teach the offences when faith appears.
5. **Is chanting for peace of mind wrong?** It is chanting at the shadow level, which still frees from sin (SB 6.2.14). With faith
   and devotee association it rises to the pure name. The Thakura asks only that it not be mixed with false philosophy.
6. **How do I know my chanting is improving?** By the Bhagavatam's test: the heart changes (SB 2.3.24); desires for enjoyment are
   cleansed as one hears with attention (SB 1.2.17). Look for that, not for emotions.

## S6. Check your understanding

2. True or false: the Bhagavatam says the name frees from sin only when chanted attentively.
3. Which offence does Srila Prabhupada call the worst, and what is the remedy the Thakura gives?
4. Name the three kinds of inattention.
5. What is the difference between the two kinds of namabhasa?
6. What does CC Antya 4.71 say is the condition for obtaining love of Godhead "very easily"?

**Answers:** 1. Offensive chanting (stalls; hell if wilful), namabhasa (liberation), the pure name (prema). 2. False: it acts

(SB 6.2.14). 3. Blaspheming
devotees who spread the name; fall at the devotee's feet and beg forgiveness with contrition (HNC ch. 4). 4. Apathy, laziness,

(pratibimba) comes from Mayavada or other false philosophy, gives only merging and is condemned. 6. Chanting the holy name always
while avoiding the ten offences.
