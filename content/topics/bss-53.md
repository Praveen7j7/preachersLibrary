# SB 4 canto Ch 25-29 - Puranjan Akhyana + Aila Geet (Canto 11, Ch 26, verse 1-24)

> Status line: BSS (brahmacari training) · 120 min · drafted · SENSITIVE (desire, celibacy, women spoken of in the verses; adult audience; verses and purports in their own words; no private disclosure). A study session, verse by verse through the key verses. Builds on bss-07 (association), bss-10 and bss-11 (see B).

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level
This is the allegory behind the whole BSS group. bss-07 gave the two doors of association; bss-10 and bss-54 show the forest; bss-11 shows a king's honest realisation. Here Narada Muni, speaking to a king who is busy in rituals, tells the one story that explains how a soul enters a body, becomes absorbed in the pleasures of body and family, is overtaken by old age and death, and thinks at the end of what it loved; and it ends with the introduction of the soul to its forgotten friend.

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| VL1 | The soul has an unseen friend in the heart, one verse (BG 18.61), no allegory |
| VL2 | The soul, the body as a city, and the Supersoul as friend (SB 4.28.54-64 as a short story) |
| VL3 | Thinking of the objects of desire at death and its cure by holy name and hearing (SB 4.28.28, 4.29.38) |
| SPS | The full allegory with Narada's explanation (SB 4.29.1-30) |
| DPS | Allegory and the rest of the Pracetas narrative (SB 4.30-31) |
| BSS | This page: the soul's fall into absorption, time's arrival, the remembrance at death, the swan friend, and the song of Pururava as a warning from one who lived it |

**Builds on bss-07** (SB 5.5.2: association is the door; SB 4.25.62's purport quotes this verse). **Builds on bss-10** and **bss-54** (the forest of enjoyment: not repeated here; this page keeps to Puranjana's city, his wife, time and the friend). **Builds on bss-11** (Yayati's honest conclusion about desire, SB 9.19.14; here the second king, Pururava, gives a different angle: how a man who is learned and powerful is still robbed of his attention, and what he does with the grief). The BIKC chapters on sex desire and falldown (bss-21 to bss-24) own the practical teaching; this page does not repeat them.

Not done here: the full chapter by chapter text of SB 4.25-29 (about 270 verses), the detailed list of the nine gates and their cities (SB 4.25.46-53 and the key in SB 4.29.8-15, kept in the extension), the debates on karma and rebirth in SB 4.29.58-78 (kept to R), and the BIKC detail on celibacy practice.

## C. Central message
Every soul is like King Puranjana: it comes into a body, builds its life around the pleasures of that body and family, and forgets the friend who has been sitting beside it all along. Time comes, and what the heart is fixed on at that moment decides what comes next. The way out is not a clever technique but to hear that friend and turn back to Him, through hearing about Krishna and the association of those who know Him.

## D. Learning outcomes
By the end, participants can:
1. Retell the Puranjana story in six steps and name what each main figure stands for according to SB 4.29 (soul, friend, city, intelligence, time, old age, death).
2. Explain from SB 4.25.4 and 4.25.12 why endless activity for enjoyment does not reach its goal.
3. Explain why Puranjana failed to remember his friend (SB 4.28.25) and what he remembered instead (SB 4.28.28 with BG 8.6).
4. State what the two swans of SB 4.28.54-64 teach about the soul's position and why this is the cure.
5. Read the Aila-gita (SB 11.26) as Pururava's own confession, noting that he blames himself and not Urvasi (SB 11.26.17), and choose one concrete protection for the week.

## E. Scriptural foundation
Use about twelve verses in the talk, one by one (marked "in the talk"). The rest are background.

SB 4.25 to 4.29 sets: Maitreya tells Vidura what Narada told King Pracinabarhisat (Prachinabarhi), who was performing animal sacrifices. This page keeps the ordinary spellings Puranjana, Pracinabarhi.

### Part 1: The story of Puranjana (SB 4.25-29)

**SB 4.25.4**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/25/4/
> "The chief aim of life is to get rid of all miseries and enjoy happiness, but these two things cannot be realized by fruitive activity."
- **Context:** Narada, seeing King Pracinabarhi engaged in costly ritual sacrifices, asks him what he wants to gain.
- **Teaching:** The aim of life is freedom from misery and enjoyment of happiness; the verse says fruitive activity cannot give either.
- **Purport point:** The purport says one who works for material benefit does not see that time will not allow permanent enjoyment, and that if one wants to enjoy something one must suffer for something else. It adds that even promotion to a heavenly planet does not remove birth, old age, disease and death, and that for a neophyte devotee routine service may be painful, but he has the hope of the highest happiness, which a fruitive worker does not have (the purport cites BG 4.9).
- **Application (preacher's):** Narada asks a respectful question that applies to everyone who works hard: what is all this work for?
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 4.25.10**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/25/10/
> "He had a friend named Avijñāta [“the unknown one”]. No one could understand the activities of Avijñāta."
- **Context:** Narada begins the story of King Puranjana ("one who enjoys in a body").
- **Teaching:** The king has a friend named Avijnata, "the unknown one".
- **Purport point:** The purport explains that every living entity is a puranjana; the soul is eternal (it quotes BG 2.24) and acts under the direction of its friend the Supersoul, who sits in the heart (it quotes BG 15.15). The living entity is called avijnata-sakha, "one who has an unknown friend", because he thinks himself independent of God and of the laws of nature.
- **Application (preacher's):** The whole page depends on this verse. It says that we have a friend, and that we do not know him.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 4.25.12**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/25/12/
> "Unfortunately he found a feeling of insufficiency everywhere."
- **Teaching:** Puranjana, full of desires, travels the world looking for a place where all of them can be fulfilled, and finds a feeling of insufficiency everywhere.
- **Purport point:** The purport compares material enjoyment with society, friendship and love to a drop of water on a desert, and says the soul, as part of the Lord, seeks complete enjoyment, which cannot be had apart from the Lord. It gives the deer (hunter's music), the fish (the bait) and the elephant as examples of an animal captured by one sense; in the human form all senses can be tried, "but the result is that he becomes so harassed in his attempted sense gratification that he ultimately becomes morose".
- **Application (preacher's):** One sentence on bss-10: the forest of enjoyment is the same picture. Do not tell it again here.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 4.25.62**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/25/62/
> "In this way, King Purañjana was captivated by his nice wife and was thus cheated."
- **Context:** The end of Chapter 25. The king has found a city with nine gates and a wife (SB 4.25.13-61); the verse says where this leaves him.
- **Teaching:** The king is captivated and cheated; against his own wish he stays under the control of his wife, like a pet animal.
- **Purport point:** The purport explains the word *vipralabdha* (cheated) as: the king got the queen to satisfy his desires, and so was cheated by material existence. It then quotes SB 5.5.2 (service of saints opens liberation; association with women and those addicted to them opens bondage), says the order of sannyasa trains one to avoid illicit sex, and says the attachment to one's wife means attachment to the material qualities. It ends by saying that devotional service gives knowledge and renunciation without separate effort (it quotes SB 1.2.7).
- **Application (preacher's):** The queen of the story is explained later as material intelligence (SB 4.29.5). Read the purport's quotation of SB 5.5.2 as it is: association with women and with those addicted to them opens the door to bondage.
- **Use:** in the talk, with SB 4.29.5 to follow.

**SB 4.27.5**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/27/5/
> "his new life and youth expired in half a moment."
- **Context:** Puranjana has many sons and daughters (SB 4.27.6-7), and the city of Pancala becomes crowded (SB 4.27.9).
- **Teaching:** With his heart full of lust and sinful reactions, his youth passed in half a moment.
- **Purport (exact, the parts used):** "In youth a person becomes very lusty to enjoy all kinds of sense objects. The sense objects are form, taste, smell, touch and sound. The modern scientific method, or advancement of scientific civilization, encourages the enjoyment of these five senses. The younger generation is very pleased to see a beautiful form, to hear radio messages of material news and sense gratificatory songs, to smell nice scents and nice flowers, and to touch the soft body or breasts of a young woman and gradually touch the sex organs. All of this is also very pleasing to the animals; therefore in human society there are restrictions in the enjoyment of the five sense objects. If one does not follow, he becomes exactly like an animal." On youth: "The words navaṁ vayaḥ ... indicate the period of youth from age sixteen to thirty. These thirteen or fifteen years of life are years in which one can very strongly enjoy the senses ... But 'time and tide wait for no man' — the span of youth expires very quickly. One who wastes his life simply by committing sinful activities in youth immediately becomes disappointed and disillusioned when the brief period of youth is over." On the law for human beings: "The animals do not violate the laws of nature. For example, the sex impulse in animals is very strong during certain months of the year. The lion is very powerful. He is a flesh-eater and is very strong, but he enjoys sex only once in a year. Similarly, according to religious injunctions a man is restricted to enjoying sex only once in a month, after the menstrual period of the wife, and if the wife is pregnant, he is not allowed sex life at all. That is the law for human beings. A man is allowed to keep more than one wife because he cannot enjoy sex when the wife is pregnant. If he wants to enjoy sex at such a time, he may go to another wife who is not pregnant. These are laws mentioned in the Manu-saṁhitā and other scriptures." Conclusion: "unrestricted sense enjoyment means sinful activities. Illicit sex is sex that violates the laws given in the scriptures ... Our real function is to change our consciousness from kaśmala, sinful consciousness, to Kṛṣṇa, the supreme pure", the process of ceto-darpana-marjanam.
- **Application (preacher's):** The purport names the sense objects and the law for human beings plainly; read them as they are. The strong years of youth are short, so use them in service.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 4.28.17**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/28/17/
> "King Purañjana was overly attached to his family and conceptions of “I” and “mine.”"
- **Context:** Old age (Kalakanya) and Yavana-raja (death's king, "Fear") have attacked the city; Puranjana's family turns against him and he is about to leave (SB 4.28.1-16).
- **Teaching:** At the time of separation he is very sorry because of his attachment to family and to "I" and "mine".
- **Purport point:** The purport says that thoughts of material enjoyment do not go away at death; the soul is carried by the subtle body (mind, intelligence, ego) to another gross body. It adds that attachment to family is most prominent at death, when a man may call a son and give him charge of the family without knowing his own destination.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 4.28.25**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/28/25/
> "out of his gross ignorance he still could not remember his friend and well-wisher, the Supersoul."
- **Teaching:** Dragged away by Yavana, Puranjana still cannot remember his friend.
- **Purport point:** The purport quotes BG 5.29 (the Lord is the enjoyer, the proprietor and the friend of all) and BG 18.61 and says the living entity and the Supersoul sit side by side in the same tree, yet the foolish living entity does not turn to the Lord for protection. It says that "despite all our offenses" the Lord, like a father or mother, will give relief if we surrender (it cites BG 7.14, *mam eva ye prapadyante*). It adds that bad association and attachment to sense gratification are why we do not remember our best friend.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 4.28.28**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/28/28/
> "King Purañjana gave up his body while remembering his wife, and consequently in his next life he became a very beautiful and well-situated woman."
- **Teaching:** Puranjana, thinking of his wife at death, takes birth as the daughter of King Vidarbha.
- **Purport point:** The purport quotes BG 8.6 (what one remembers when quitting the body decides the next state) and says one will think at death of the subject that occupied one's life awake, lightly sleeping or deeply sleeping. It says he got a family of a king because of his earlier pious activities, and that even a devotee who falls from yoga gets a chance in a good home to resume service (it cites BG 6.41).
- **Application (preacher's):** Teach what the verse and purport say: the thought at death (BG 8.6) decided his next body. SB 4.28.61 (below) says the identity of man, woman or eunuch is of the body.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 4.28.54**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/28/54/
> "both you and I are exactly like two swans. We live together in the same heart"
- **Context:** The widow, the former Puranjana, is about to enter the fire after her husband Malayadhvaja's death (SB 4.28.29-50). A brahmana, an old friend, comes and speaks (SB 4.28.51-53).
- **Teaching:** The brahmana says they are like two swans sharing the same heart as their lake, together for thousands of years, yet still far from their original home.
- **Purport point:** The purport says the original home of the Lord and the soul is the spiritual world where both live peacefully; when the soul wants to enjoy himself he falls into the material world; even then the Lord stays as Supersoul, but the soul in forgetfulness does not recognise Him.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 4.28.61**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/28/61/
> "Sometimes you think yourself a man, sometimes a chaste woman and sometimes a neutral eunuch."
- **Teaching:** These identities are of the body, which is made by the illusory energy; "both of us — you and I — are pure spiritual identities".
- **Purport point:** The purport says the living entity forgets that he is the eternal part and parcel of the Lord, and that the way to return is bhakti-yoga, also called sanatana-dharma; as long as society works on false bodily identification, advancement in science and philosophy only misleads ("the blind lead the blind").
- **Application (preacher's):** Every person in the story and every listener is a soul in a body made by the illusory energy.
- **Use:** in the talk, in one sentence.

**SB 4.28.62**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/28/62/
> "I, the Supersoul, and you, the individual soul, are not different in quality, for we are both spiritual."
- **Teaching:** The soul and the Lord are alike in quality (both spiritual).
- **Purport point:** The purport says that those who are learned (*kavayah*) know the soul is one in quality with the Lord yet falls under maya's control, while the Lord controls maya; the Mayavadi philosophers who cannot distinguish controller and controlled are "again and again defeated by the illusory energy".
- **Application (preacher's):** Always read this verse together with SB 4.28.64. "Alike in quality" does not mean "equal in power", because the soul is controlled by maya and the Lord controls maya.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 4.28.64**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/28/64/
> "When the one swan is instructed by the other, he is situated in his constitutional position."
- **Teaching:** One swan instructs the other; the instructed swan is situated in his constitutional position and regains the original Krishna consciousness lost because of material attraction.
- **Purport point:** The purport says one swan (the Lord) is superior and the instructor; the soul must learn Bhagavad-gita from a realized soul (it quotes BG 4.34); the soul becomes conditioned when he declines to remain subordinate and tries to be an enjoyer, and he is liberated when he gives up the attitude of ownership. It says the verses of Narada are spoken "to revive our consciousness".
- **Application (preacher's):** For a brahmacari this is the practical meaning of hearing from the guru and from senior devotees.
- **Use:** in the talk.

### Part 2: Narada's explanation (SB 4.29)

**SB 4.29.3**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/29/3/
> "The person I have described as unknown is the Supreme Personality of Godhead, the master and eternal friend of the living entity."
- **Teaching:** Avijnata is the Lord; He remains unknown to the conditioned soul because material names, activities and qualities cannot reveal Him.
- **Purport point:** The purport explains that nirakara, avijnata and *avan-manasa-gocara* mean that He is not perceived by materialistic people, not that He has no form. It quotes BG 18.55 (He can be known only by devotional service), says the Lord has specific names such as Rama, Krishna, Govinda, Narayana, Vishnu and Adhokshaja, and that He reveals Himself to the devotee whose tongue is engaged in service (it quotes the Padma Purana verse *atah sri-krsna-namadi*).
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 4.29.5**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/29/5/
> "The word pramadā mentioned in this regard refers to material intelligence, or ignorance."
- **Teaching:** The queen is material intelligence; one who takes shelter of it identifies with the body, thinks "I" and "mine", and is entrapped.
- **Purport point:** The purport says that material intelligence is ignorance; when it is dovetailed with Krishna's desire it becomes buddhi-yoga (it quotes BG 10.10). It describes the false claim "I am the monarch of all I survey", says nothing belongs to the living entity, and quotes Lord Chaitanya's *ceto-darpana-marjanam*.
- **Application (preacher's):** This verse is the key to the whole story. Read it aloud again just before the break so that everyone can use it in the second half.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 4.29.22**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/29/22/
> "What was described as Kālakanyā should be understood as old age."
- **Teaching:** Kalakanya is old age, whom Yavanesvara (Yamaraja, death) accepts as his sister.
- **Purport point:** The purport says one is subjected to Yamaraja and his sister Jara because of impious activity, and those engaged in devotional service under Narada's instructions are not; if one is Krishna conscious he conquers death and, after leaving the body, returns to Godhead (it cites BG 4.9).
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 4.29.38**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/29/38/
> "very soon becomes eligible to see the Supreme Personality of Godhead face to face."
- **Teaching:** One who is faithful, always hearing the glories of the Lord and engaged in Krishna consciousness, soon becomes eligible to see Him face to face.
- **Purport point:** The purport says constant engagement in the service of Vasudeva means constantly hearing the glories of the Lord, and it lists the nine processes (*sravanam kirtanam visnoh smaranam pada-sevanam* and the rest) as the only means of perfection.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 4.29.50**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/29/50/
> "He is also our best friend, and everyone should take shelter at His lotus feet."
- **Teaching:** Sri Hari, the Supersoul, guides and controls all; He is our best friend; taking shelter of Him makes life auspicious.
- **Purport point:** The purport quotes BG 18.61 and 15.15 (He directs from the heart), says "the better part of valor is to take His direction and be happy", and that the Supersoul speaks only to pure devotees who constantly serve Him (BG 10.10). It adds from Chaitanya-bhagavata that the best education is fixing the mind on Krishna's lotus feet.
- **Use:** in the talk.

### Part 3: The song of Pururava (Aila-gita, SB 11.26)

*SB 11.26 is in Canto 11: BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples. Where a verse has no purport on Vedabase this is said below.*

**SB 11.26.1**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/26/1/
> "Having achieved this human form of life, which affords one the opportunity to realize Me"
- **Context:** Lord Krishna begins the Aila-gita for Uddhava. It is Krishna speaking.
- **Teaching:** In the human form, situated in devotional service, one can achieve the Lord, "the reservoir of all pleasure".
- **Purport point:** The purport says that even liberated persons may fall because of bad association, that association with women is especially dangerous within the material world, and that the Aila-gita is spoken to prevent such a falldown; one is saved from sex attraction by the association of saintly persons.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 11.26.5**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/26/5/
> "When she was leaving him, even though he was naked he ran after her just like a madman and called out in great distress, “O my wife, O terrible lady! Please stop!”"
- **Purport (exact):** "As his beloved wife was leaving him, the shocked king was crying out, “My dear wife, please think for a moment. Just stop! You terrible lady, can’t you stop? Why don’t we speak together for a while? Why are you killing me?” Thus lamenting, he followed her."
- **Use:** in the talk (J.2), as the verse tells it.

**SB 11.26.3**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/26/3/
> "One should never associate with materialists, those dedicated to gratifying their genitals and bellies."
- **Teaching:** By following them one falls into the deepest pit of darkness, like a blind man following another blind man. (The page shows no separate purport.)
- **Application (preacher's):** This is the same teaching as SB 5.5.2 in bss-07, spoken by a different person. Tell the audience this in one sentence and go on to the next verse.
- **Use:** in the talk, one minute.

**SB 11.26.12**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/26/12/
> "What is the use of a big education or the practice of austerities and renunciation"
- **Context:** Pururava (Aila) after Urvasi has left him (SB 11.26.4-11). Narrated also in Canto 9, the purport of 11.26.4 says.
- **Teaching:** Education, austerity, renunciation, scripture study, solitude and silence are useless if the mind is stolen by a woman.
- **Purport (exact):** "All of the above-mentioned processes are useless if one’s heart and mind are stolen by an insignificant woman. One who hankers after a woman’s association certainly spoils his spiritual progress." It adds that Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura says one who worships the example of the liberated gopis, who accepted Krishna as their paramour, can free his mental activities from the contamination of lust.
- **Application (preacher's):** It is a king's confession, and the purport states it as a rule for everyone; read both.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 11.26.14**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/26/14/
> "my lusty desires kept rising again and again within my heart and were never satisfied, just like a fire that can never be extinguished by the oblations of ghee poured into its flames."
- **Teaching:** Years of enjoyment did not end the desire. (No separate purport on Vedabase.)
- **Application (preacher's):** Say "Builds on bss-11": Yayati said the same in SB 9.19.14; the two kings never met on this page.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 11.26.15**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/26/15/
> "Who but the Supreme Personality of Godhead, who lies beyond material perception and is the Lord of self-satisfied sages, can possibly save my consciousness"
- **Teaching:** Only the Lord can rescue the consciousness. (No separate purport.)
- **Application (preacher's):** This verse is where the song changes. Pururava first complains that his mind was stolen, and then he asks who can save it. His answer is the Lord. A brahmacari can make the same prayer.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 11.26.17**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/26/17/
> "How can I blame her for my trouble when I myself am ignorant of my real, spiritual nature?"
- **Teaching:** Pururava does not blame Urvasi; he says he did not control his senses and was like a man who sees a harmless rope as a snake.
- **Purport point:** The purport says the one who thinks the illusory energy exists for his own sense gratification will bring on himself an avalanche of fear and anxiety, and that Pururava frankly admits Urvasi is not to blame: he himself was the offender for trying to exploit her external form.
- **Application (preacher's):** Read it with 11.26.1's purport ("the association of women is especially dangerous") and 11.26.24 ("never let his senses associate freely with women"): the king blames his own uncontrolled senses, and the song still tells him whom not to associate with.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 11.26.24**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/26/24/
> "Even those who are highly learned cannot trust the six enemies of the mind"
- **Teaching:** One should not let the senses associate freely with women or with men attached to women; if even the learned cannot trust the six enemies of the mind, how much less foolish persons like Pururava. (No separate purport.)
- **Application (preacher's):** Pururava calls himself foolish here. The lesson is humility: a senior brahmacari needs the same care with his senses as a new one. Which "six enemies" the text means is not defined by the verse as shown; if asked, say "Requires source review" and look it up with the purport of SB 11.26.24 or the Sanskrit commentary before answering.
- **Use:** in the talk, as the close.

### Background verses quoted in the purports above
**BG 8.6**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/8/6/
> "Whatever state of being one remembers when he quits his body, O son of Kuntī, that state he will attain without fail."
- **Use:** with SB 4.28.28; the verse the purport itself cites.

**BG 18.61**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/61/
> "The Supreme Lord is situated in everyone’s heart, O Arjuna"
- **Use:** with SB 4.25.10, 4.28.25 and 4.29.50; the friend is in the heart.

## F. Philosophical explanation
**Principle.** The soul is eternal and spiritual (SB 4.28.61, 4.28.62). It wanted to enjoy on its own (SB 4.28.64 purport), came into a body, and the Lord came with it as its friend (SB 4.28.54 purport, 4.25.10 purport). In the body, material intelligence (pramada, SB 4.29.5) leads the soul to say "I" and "mine" about body, home and family. Time (SB 4.29.21-22: Chandavega and the days and nights, old age, death) takes everything the soul claimed. At the end the soul carries only its thoughts (SB 4.28.17 purport, 4.28.28).
**Why it matters.** The brahmacari has chosen to step out of the pattern. This story shows him that the pattern is a story about the whole soul, not just a rule about conduct, and that the pattern ends when he hears from the friend.
**Connections.** Association (SB 5.5.2, bss-07) and the senses (bss-24) are the *means* of the story; SB 4.28.28 with BG 8.6 is the *consequence*; SB 4.28.64 is the *cure*; Pururava's song (SB 11.26) is a *second, shorter* witness.
**Consequences.** The story is both a warning (SB 11.26.1 purport: the Aila-gita is spoken "to prevent such a falldown") and an introduction to a friend who is already there (SB 4.28.54).
**Common misunderstandings.**
1. "The story is only about women." The queen is material intelligence (SB 4.29.5), and Pururava blames his own senses (SB 11.26.17); at the same time the purports say "the association of women is especially dangerous" (11.26.1) and the song says "never let his senses associate freely with women or with men attached to women" (11.26.24). Teach both as they are.
2. "The story says the Lord is separate and we are nothing." SB 4.28.62 says the soul and the Lord are alike in quality; the purport adds that the soul is controlled by maya while the Lord controls maya (that is the difference).
3. "Puranjana's next birth was arbitrary." The verse and purport say he remembered his wife at death (BG 8.6) and was born in a king's family because of earlier pious deeds (SB 4.28.28 purport).
4. "A brahmacari who feels pulled is like Puranjana." The story shows how a heart is bound and how it is freed: by hearing from the friend (SB 4.28.64). Help is available (see K and M.3).
**How to pitch it.** Tell the story first, as the verses tell it. Give Narada's key (SB 4.29) after the story. End with the friend in the heart.

## H. Opening and engagement
**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes).**
1. Offer a short prayer or one round of kirtana; say the title once.
2. Say: "Tonight there is one story, told by Narada, a great devotee, to a king who was busy with rituals. It has a king, a city, a queen, a friend nobody knows, and two visitors who come to every city. By the end we will know who each one is."
3. Read SB 4.25.10 slowly in English only. Pause three seconds.
4. Say: "Keep one question in your mind: who is the friend?"
**H.2 Opening questions.**
1. "When you were small, did you ever have an invisible friend, or a hero you talked to in your mind? What did you tell him?" (Take two or three answers; do not comment.)
2. "When you hear 'a story that is really about me', what do you expect?"
3. "What do you think a person thinks about at the end of a long life?" (This leads to SB 4.28.28.)

## I. Core teaching points
**I.1 The wanderer and the city (SB 4.25.4, 4.25.10, 4.25.12, 4.25.62).** Narada asks the king the right question (what are you trying to get?). Then he tells of Puranjana, who wanders restlessly, finds a city and a wife, and is "cheated" (*vipralabdha*, SB 4.25.62 purport). Explain *vipralabdha* simply: he got what he asked for, and it was not what he needed. Illustration (preacher's, labelled): a person works for years to win a contract or buy a home and, a year later, is no happier. The point is that the soul wants a joy that only the Lord holds. Devotional point: the desire was for a joy that only the Lord holds (SB 4.25.12 purport). Transition: what does time do to this?
**I.2 Time arrives (SB 4.27.5, 4.29.22, 4.28.17).** Youth goes in half a moment; read the 4.27.5 purport as it is (E): the five sense objects the young are pleased by, the lion who enjoys sex once a year, and the law for human beings; old age comes as Kalakanya, the daughter of Time, whom no one in the story would accept (SB 4.27.19-20), and death accepts her as a sister (SB 4.29.22). The city is attacked, the family turns away, the king is sorry only about his family (SB 4.28.17). Conclusion: the king cannot take anything he called "mine" out of the city with him. Transition: what does he take with him?
**I.3 The last thought (SB 4.28.25, 4.28.28, BG 8.6).** He cannot remember the friend (SB 4.28.25); he remembers his wife; the next life follows (SB 4.28.28). Explain BG 8.6 plainly: the thought at the end comes from the thoughts of a lifetime, awake or asleep. Devotional point: the training of a devotee is to make the Name the habitual thought; the purport adds that a fallen devotee still gets a chance to resume service (SB 4.28.28 purport citing BG 6.41). Transition: who was the friend?
**I.4 The key and the swans (SB 4.29.3, 4.29.5, 4.28.54, 4.28.61-64).** Say what each figure is, using Narada's words: Puranjana is the soul (SB 4.25.10 purport), the unknown friend is the Lord (SB 4.29.3), the queen is material intelligence (SB 4.29.5), Kalakanya is old age and Yavana-raja is death (SB 4.29.22). Then the brahmana appears to the grieving widow and speaks: "We are two swans in one heart" (SB 4.28.54). The friend does not scold her. He gently reminds her who she is and who he is. SB 4.28.61: man, woman, eunuch are bodily names; SB 4.28.64: the instructed swan regains Krishna consciousness. Devotional point: the Lord's instruction comes through the guru and the Gita (the purport quotes BG 4.34).
**I.5 One who lived it: Pururava (SB 11.26).** The Lord Himself (Krishna) tells Uddhava the song of King Aila so that no one falls through bad association (SB 11.26.1 purport). When Urvasi left, "even though he was naked he ran after her just like a madman" (SB 11.26.5). His grief turns into honest thought about himself (read SB 11.26.12-15), he refuses to blame the woman (SB 11.26.17), and his conclusion is to ask for the Lord (SB 11.26.15) and to guard the senses (SB 11.26.24). Devotional point: honest repentance turns into prayer; the song ends with a plan. Builds on bss-11.

## J. Stories and illustrations
**J.1 King Puranjana (SB 4.25-29; key verse 4.28.28).** Retell in order, as in the verses, with no invented dialogue.
1. Narada shows King Pracinabarhi the animals he has killed in sacrifice and tells him the old history of Puranjana (SB 4.25.7-9).
2. Puranjana has a friend, Avijnata; he travels, finds nothing to satisfy him, then sees a city of nine gates and a woman there (SB 4.25.10-13, 20-21). She tells him she has built the city for his enjoyment for a hundred years (SB 4.25.37).
3. They live together; he follows the gates of the city to different places with different friends (SB 4.25.43-56).
4. One day he hunts without telling his wife; she is angry; he pacifies her and the pleasure is renewed (SB 4.26.4, 4.26.18-4.27.3). Tell this in two sentences.
5. He has many children and grandchildren; he loses the youth that passed in half a moment (SB 4.27.5-9).
6. Chandavega's soldiers (days and nights) and then Kalakanya (old age) and the King of the Yavanas (Fear, death) attack; the city burns; the family deserts him; he is dragged away (SB 4.27.13-4.28.24).
7. He thinks of his wife and is reborn as the daughter of King Vidarbha (SB 4.28.28); she marries Malayadhvaja, a devotee who goes to Kulacala to worship Krishna and attains devotion (SB 4.28.29-42); she serves him; when he leaves his body she prepares to follow him (SB 4.28.43-50).
8. A brahmana, her old friend, speaks to her and reminds her who she is (SB 4.28.51-64).
9. Narada tells the king that this is an instruction for self-realization (SB 4.28.65); Pracinabarhi leaves home, practises austerities at Kapilasrama and attains the Lord (SB 4.29.81-82).
Devotional lesson: the soul's real friend never leaves, and the story ends with the friend speaking and the listener turning to Him.
**J.2 Pururava and Urvasi (SB 11.26.4-11; told in Canto 9 also, per SB 11.26.4 purport; bss-38 gives SB 9.14).** The emperor, bereft of Urvasi, "even though he was naked ... ran after her just like a madman and called out in great distress, 'O my wife, O terrible lady! Please stop!'" (SB 11.26.5; the purport: "Why don’t we speak together for a while? Why are you killing me?"). For many years he "had enjoyed sex pleasure in the evening hours" and was never satisfied; he did not notice how the nights came and went (SB 11.26.6), and he says "That lady cheated me so much that I did not even see the rising or setting of the sun" (SB 11.26.8; the purport: he "forgot his devotional service to the Supreme Lord and became more concerned with pleasing this beautiful young lady"). Tell it as the verses tell it. Devotional lesson: a man at the height of power can still lose his attention; the cure is to ask the Lord to keep the consciousness.
**J.3 Invented illustration (labelled):** a village road with a stranger who walks beside a traveller all the way to town, never speaking, never leaving. At the last bend the traveller sees him and says, "Have you been here all along?" The point is SB 4.28.54 and 4.25.10: the friend is present and unrecognised. The traveller's first words to the friend are the beginning of prayer: the soul speaking to the Lord.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections
1. **"The story seems to blame women."** The queen is a figure for material intelligence (SB 4.29.5). Pururava says he cannot blame Urvasi (SB 11.26.17, and the purport says he himself was the offender). The same song says "never let his senses associate freely with women or with men attached to women" (SB 11.26.24), and the 11.26.1 purport says "the association of women is especially dangerous". SB 4.28.61 says identities of man and woman are bodily and the soul is spiritual. Give all of it as it is.
2. **"Is it true that a man becomes a woman because of attachment?"** SB 4.28.28 says Puranjana did, and the purport relates it to BG 8.6: the thought at death. Say what the verse and purport say.
3. **"I'm young. Why should I think of death?"** SB 4.27.5 and its purport: youth is "the period ... from age sixteen to thirty", "the span of youth expires very quickly", and "one who wastes his life simply by committing sinful activities in youth immediately becomes disappointed and disillusioned when the brief period of youth is over."
4. **"What if I feel pulled the way Puranjana was?"** Everyone's heart can be captured, as Pururava, a great emperor, says (SB 11.26.12). He turns to the Lord (SB 11.26.15). Speak to a trusted senior devotee; a qualified counsellor is a good help too. If you are in distress or cannot cope, call Tele-MANAS 14416 (India) or your local crisis service. Nothing in this session needs private disclosure.
5. **"Why do we need a guru if the Lord is in the heart?"** SB 4.29.50 purport: the Supersoul speaks only to pure devotees who constantly serve Him; SB 4.28.64 purport: learn Gita from a realized soul (BG 4.34).
6. **"Are the nine gates and all the cities important to remember?"** No. SB 4.29.8-15 gives the key to each; they belong to the extension, not the talk.

## L. Case studies
**L.1 Invented case for discussion.** A young man joins the ashram full of faith, then discovers that his mind runs to a future of comfort and a family life as soon as he is tired or lonely. He is ashamed and thinks of leaving. *Dilemma:* is the pull proof that he is not fit? *Principles:* SB 4.25.12 (the soul seeks complete enjoyment; only the Lord can supply it), SB 11.26.24 (even the learned cannot trust the senses), SB 4.28.64 (hear from the friend). *Questions:* What does the story tell him is happening in his heart? Who can he talk to without shame? What could he do the next evening he feels tired and lonely? *Analysis:* The verses do not say he is unfit; they say that it is the nature of the heart, that the cure is hearing and service, and that company matters. *Takeaway:* he should speak to a senior devotee, keep chanting and serving, and take the pull as a reminder to turn to the Lord, his friend in the heart.
**L.2 Invented case for discussion (extension).** A devotee who has served for twenty years feels he has no time to chant attentively and worries about how he will be remembered. *Principles:* SB 4.28.17 (concern at the time of death), SB 4.28.28 with BG 8.6. *Questions:* What is he thinking of? What could daily practice change? *Takeaway:* what we think of in life is what we think of at the end, so the practice is the preparation.

## M. Discussion and reflection
**M.1 Closing check questions (with expected answers).**
1. *Who is Puranjana?* The living entity, the soul in a body (SB 4.25.10 purport).
2. *Who is the unknown friend?* The Supreme Lord, the Supersoul (SB 4.29.3).
3. *What is the queen?* Material intelligence, or ignorance (SB 4.29.5).
4. *Who is Kalakanya and what does she bring?* Old age, accepted by death as his sister (SB 4.29.22).
5. *Why does Puranjana take a woman's body in his next life?* He gave up his body remembering his wife (SB 4.28.28); BG 8.6.
6. *What do the two swans teach?* The Lord and the soul live together in the heart; when the soul hears the instruction of the Lord it is situated again in Krishna consciousness (SB 4.28.54, 4.28.64).
**M.2 Activities.**
a) *Key matching (10 min).* In pairs, match six cards (soul, Lord, intelligence, old age, death, body) to six story figures. Check with SB 4.29.3, 4.29.5, 4.29.22 and the verses.
b) *Swan conversation (10 min).* In threes, read SB 4.28.54 and 4.28.64. One person says, in his own words, what the first swan says to the other; another says what it would sound like if the friend were to speak to him tonight. Not shared unless volunteered.
c) *Short drama (15 min, extension).* Roles: narrator, the grieving widow, the brahmana. Script: SB 4.28.52-54 and 4.28.62-64 in the characters' own words (labelled as dramatisation, without adding story). Debrief: what is the first thing the brahmana does? (He speaks as a friend.)
**M.3 Reflection.** "If the friend were sitting beside me this week, what would I want to tell Him?" Private; nothing is shared. If a participant is distressed afterwards, point to a trusted senior devotee, a professional counsellor, or Tele-MANAS 14416 (India) or the local crisis service.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary
Narada Muni once told a king named Pracinabarhi a story to help him see what his life was really about. The story is about King Puranjana. He had a friend called Avijnata, "the unknown one", whose doings nobody could understand (SB 4.25.10). Puranjana wandered the world, found nothing that satisfied him (SB 4.25.12), and then found a city with nine gates and a queen. He lived there in pleasure, and in the end he was "cheated" (SB 4.25.62). His youth passed in half a moment (SB 4.27.5). Old age and death arrived, his family turned away, and he thought only of his family at his last moment and could not remember his friend (SB 4.28.17, 4.28.25). Because he remembered his wife at death, he took a woman's body in the next life (SB 4.28.28; compare BG 8.6).
In his new life he married a devotee. When her husband died, an old friend, a brahmana, came and said: "We are like two swans living in the same heart" (SB 4.28.54). He told her that man, woman or neutral are only bodily names, and that both she and he are spiritual (SB 4.28.61). By hearing from him she could come back to Krishna consciousness (SB 4.28.64). Narada explained the key: Puranjana is the soul, Avijnata is the Lord, the queen is material intelligence, and Kalakanya is old age whom death accepts as his sister (SB 4.29.3, 4.29.5, 4.29.22). The way out is to hear the glories of the Lord and take shelter of Him (SB 4.29.38, 4.29.50).
The Lord later told the same lesson through the song of King Pururava (Aila). He was a great king who lost his mind to desire: when Urvasi left, "even though he was naked he ran after her just like a madman" (SB 11.26.5). The purport to SB 4.27.5 says the young are pleased "to touch the soft body or breasts of a young woman and gradually touch the sex organs", that this "is also very pleasing to the animals", and that "if one does not follow" the restrictions for human society "he becomes exactly like an animal". Pururava said: "How can I blame her for my trouble when I myself am ignorant of my real, spiritual nature?" He asked who but the Lord could save his consciousness, and he warned that one should never let the senses associate freely with women or with men attached to women, since even the learned cannot trust the six enemies of the mind (SB 11.26.17, 11.26.15, 11.26.24). For a brahmacari this means: remember the friend, hear from devotees, and guard the senses with humility.

## S2. Key ideas
- You are a soul with a friend in your heart, the Lord, who is never absent (SB 4.25.10, BG 18.61).
- Absorption in body and family never gives full satisfaction (SB 4.25.12) and time ends it (SB 4.27.5).
- What we remember at the end follows from what we thought of in life (SB 4.28.28, BG 8.6).
- Hearing from the Lord and from devotees brings us back (SB 4.28.64, 4.29.38).
- Pururava blamed himself, not the woman (SB 11.26.17), and concluded: never let the senses associate freely with women (SB 11.26.24).

## S3. Verses to remember
- **SB 4.28.54**: "both you and I are exactly like two swans. We live together in the same heart." *For me:* I am not alone.
- **SB 4.28.28**: Puranjana gave up his body remembering his wife. *For me:* my daily thoughts are my preparation for the end.
- **SB 11.26.17**: "How can I blame her for my trouble when I myself am ignorant of my real, spiritual nature?" *For me:* I take responsibility for my senses.

## S4. Words to know
- **Allegory:** a story whose figures stand for something else.
- **Avijnata:** "the unknown one": the Lord as the soul's unseen friend.
- **Supersoul (Paramatma):** the Lord in the heart of every living being.
- **Material intelligence:** thinking that takes the body and its things as "I" and "mine".
- **Kalakanya:** the daughter of Time, old age.
- **Aila-gita:** the song of King Aila (Pururava), SB 11.26.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *Does the story blame women?* The queen stands for material intelligence (SB 4.29.5), and Pururava blames himself (SB 11.26.17); he also says never to let the senses associate freely with women (SB 11.26.24). Man, woman and neutral are bodily names (SB 4.28.61).
2. *Who is the friend?* The Lord in the heart (SB 4.29.3, BG 18.61).
3. *Why did Puranjana forget him?* Absorbed in family and "mine" (SB 4.28.17, 4.28.25).
4. *What happens at death?* We attain the state we remember (BG 8.6; SB 4.28.28).
5. *What if I feel pulled?* Everyone can be, even a great king (SB 11.26.12). Speak to a trusted senior devotee, hear and chant, ask the Lord (SB 11.26.15); if you cannot cope, call Tele-MANAS 14416 (India) or your local crisis service.

## S6. Check your understanding
1. Who is Avijnata?
2. According to SB 4.29.5, what is the queen?
3. What is Kalakanya?
4. Why did Puranjana become a woman in his next life?
5. In SB 4.28.54, who are the two swans?
6. In SB 11.26.17, whom does Pururava blame?

**Answers:** 1. The Lord, the unknown friend of the soul (SB 4.29.3). 2. Material intelligence, or ignorance (SB 4.29.5). 3. Old age (SB 4.29.22). 4. He gave up his body remembering his wife (SB 4.28.28; BG 8.6). 5. The soul and the Lord (SB 4.28.54). 6. Himself, not Urvasi (SB 11.26.17).
