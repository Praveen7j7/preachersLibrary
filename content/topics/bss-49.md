# 25. Health

> Status line: BSS (brahmacari training) · 120 min · drafted · sensitive (body, food, illness, mental health; the story of Sanatana Gosvami mentions a wish to end one's life, so help-seeking guidance is included).

## A. Topic overview
| | |
|---|---|
| Batch / level | BSS, brahmacari training (Bhakti Siddhanta Sabha). A brahmacari is a celibate student who lives simply, serves his guru and studies scripture. |
| Suggested duration | 120 minutes (lecture, with an "if time allows" extension) |
| Preparation time | About 2 hours: read the verses in E with their purports, read the BIKC chapter "Health" once, then this page |
| Difficulty | Intermediate |
| Prerequisites | None. It helps to have heard bss-04 (balance in austerity) |
| Audience | Young men living or preparing to live as brahmacaris. The page is also useful to any devotee. |
| Teaching objective | The audience understands that the body belongs to Krishna and is His instrument. Because of this, caring for it with simple, regular habits and with medical help when needed is a part of service. It is neither a goal in itself nor something to ignore. |

BIKC means *Brahmacarya in Krishna consciousness* by Bhakti Vikasa Swami. This page follows its chapter 25, "Health" (summary and brief quotation only; the book is copyrighted).

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level
A brahmacari gives most of his time to service and keeps a strict daily routine. This needs a body and a mind that work. Two mistakes are common. Some devotees neglect the body and say "I am not this body". Others become so interested in diet and fitness that the body becomes the main concern. This page gives the middle path that Srila Prabhupada teaches: the body is Krishna's, so we keep it fit for His service and we do not make it our goal.

**Builds on bss-04** (Lord Caitanya and the milk-drinking brahmacari), which teaches that austerity done with pride is useless and gives the balance of BG 6.16-17. **Builds on bss-08** (control of the tongue) and **bss-01** (why we do not imitate Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami's extreme fasting). Those pages are not repeated here. This page uses BG 6.16-17 for a different purpose: practical habits for food, sleep and work, with research on exercise.

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| VL1 | A simple talk on keeping a healthy daily routine for the sake of chanting and service |
| VL2 | Why the body is not the self, and why it still needs care (BG 2.13-14 in its own topic) |
| VL3 | Health habits that protect the morning program and sadhana |
| SPS | Regulated living (yukta-vairagya) and avoiding both neglect and obsession |
| DPS | Health and service for those who hold responsibility: pacing, rest, asking for help |
| BSS | This page: the body as Krishna's property and instrument, with practical care for food, sleep, movement, cleanliness, sickness and mental health |

**Not done here:** sexual health and the chapter "Retention of semen" (later BSS pages); medical advice for any illness (a qualified doctor gives that); weight loss or muscle building as a goal; praise of fasting or of sleeping very little.

## C. Central message
The body does not belong to us. It belongs to Krishna, and He uses it as an instrument for His service. So a devotee looks after it with simple, regular habits and goes to a doctor when ill. He does this to serve better. He does not do it to enjoy the body, and he does not ruin it to show how renounced he is.

## D. Learning outcomes
By the end, participants can:
1. Explain, using CC Antya 4.76 and 4.78, why Lord Caitanya called Sanatana Gosvami's body "My property" and "My instrument".
2. State in their own words what BG 6.16-17 and NOI 2 say about eating, sleeping and over-strictness.
3. Describe three practical habits for food, sleep and cleanliness that support service.
4. Say what to do when they are ill, very tired or low in mood: tell a mentor, see a doctor or counsellor, and keep chanting what they can.
5. Choose one health habit to start this week.

## E. Scriptural foundation
Use these in the talk: SB 7.6.5, CC Antya 4 (the verses listed), BG 6.16, BG 6.17, NOI 2 and BG 3.13. The others are for questions and background.

**SB 7.6.5**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/6/5/
> "…must endeavor to achieve the highest goal of life as long as the body is stout and strong…"
- **Context:** Prahlada Maharaja is teaching his schoolmates in the demons' school.
- **Teaching:** A person who can tell right from wrong should work for the highest goal of life while the body is strong and not yet weakened.
- **Purport point:** The purport ends by saying that we should live so that we keep ourselves "always healthy and strong in mind and intelligence" in order to tell the goal of life from a life full of problems. It also says that the highest goal can be achieved as long as the body is stout and strong.
- **Application (preacher's):** Health is a way to protect the years in which we can serve most. The verse does not say that health is the goal.
- **Use:** in the talk, as the opening verse.

**CC Antya 4.20 and 4.21**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/4/20/ · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/4/21/
> "…Besides that, I have infections on my body." (4.20, Sanatana speaking)
> "Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, however, embraced Sanātana Gosvāmī by force. Thus the moisture oozing from the itching sores touched the transcendental body of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu." (4.21)
- **Context:** Sanatana Gosvami came to Jagannatha Puri with a body covered in itching sores. He asked the Lord not to touch him.
- **Teaching:** The Lord embraced him anyway.
- **Application (preacher's):** Illness does not make a devotee less dear to Krishna. A sick devotee should never be treated with disgust or shame.
- **Use:** in the talk (J.1).

**CC Antya 4.55 and 4.56**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/4/55/ · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/4/56/
> "You should know that one cannot attain Kṛṣṇa simply by giving up the body. Kṛṣṇa is attainable by devotional service. There is no other means for attaining Him." (4.56)
- **Teaching:** The Lord told Sanatana that if Krishna could be reached by giving up the body, He would give up millions of bodies at once (4.55). But Krishna is reached only by devotional service (4.56).
- **Use:** in the talk (J.1). Present this with care; see the guidance in H.1 and K.5.

**CC Antya 4.72**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/4/72/
> "…“My decision to commit suicide has not been greatly appreciated by Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu.”"
- **Teaching:** This verse tells us that Sanatana had decided to end his life. The Lord had just forbidden it.
- **Use:** in the talk. Do not describe how he planned to do it, and do not go into detail.

**CC Antya 4.76**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/4/76/
> "“Your body is My property. You have already surrendered unto Me. Therefore you no longer have any claim to your body."
- **Teaching:** The body of a surrendered devotee is the Lord's property.
- **Use:** in the talk. This is the central verse of the page.

**CC Antya 4.78 and 4.83**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/4/78/ · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/4/83/
> "“Your body is My principal instrument for executing many necessary functions. By your body I shall carry out many tasks." (4.78)
> "“I have to do all this work through your body, but you want to give it up. How can I tolerate this?”" (4.83)
- **Teaching:** The Lord has work to do through Sanatana's body: to explain devotional service, to establish centres, and to teach the renounced order (4.79-81).
- **Application (preacher's):** Every devotee's body is also an instrument for some service. Caring for it protects the Lord's work.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 6.16**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/16/
> "There is no possibility of one’s becoming a yogī, O Arjuna, if one eats too much or eats too little, sleeps too much or does not sleep enough."
- **Teaching:** The verse names four faults: eating too much, eating too little, sleeping too much and sleeping too little.
- **Purport point:** The purport says that too much eating means more than is needed to keep body and soul together, and that one should not sleep more than six hours a day. It also says that a person who "artificially abstains from eating, manufacturing his own personal process of fasting" cannot practise yoga. It recommends eating the remnants of food offered to Krishna.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 6.17**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/17/
> "He who is regulated in his habits of eating, sleeping, recreation and work can mitigate all material pains by practicing the yoga system."
- **Purport point:** The purport says that extravagance in eating, sleeping, defending and mating can block progress, and that a Krishna conscious person's sleep is kept to a minimum because he cannot bear to pass a minute without service. It names Rupa Gosvami and Haridasa Thakura as the ideal. It says also that because he is regulated in all his work, speech, sleep and wakefulness, there is no material misery for him.
- **Application (preacher's):** Rupa Gosvami and Haridasa Thakura are shown as the ideal and not as a rule for us. Sleeping two hours is not a plan for a beginner. See K.2.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**NOI 2**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/2/
> "…eating more than necessary or collecting more funds than required…"
> "…practicing the scriptural rules and regulations only for the sake of following them and not for the sake of spiritual advancement, or rejecting the rules and regulations of the scriptures and working independently or whimsically…"
- **Teaching:** Devotional service is spoiled when we become too entangled in six activities. Two are in the lines above: eating more than necessary, and following rules only for their own sake or rejecting the rules whimsically.
- **Application (preacher's):** The second line shows the balance. Health habits are good when they serve spiritual advancement. They become a problem when they are done only to follow a rule, or when they are dropped because we feel like it.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 3.13**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/13/
> "The devotees of the Lord are released from all kinds of sins because they eat food which is offered first for sacrifice."
- **Teaching:** Food offered first to the Lord frees the eater from sin; food cooked only for one's own enjoyment is "only sin".
- **Application (preacher's):** For a brahmacari, taking only prasadam is the first and simplest health habit. It also controls the tongue (see bss-08).
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 17.8**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/17/8/
> "Foods dear to those in the mode of goodness increase the duration of life, purify one’s existence and give strength, health, happiness and satisfaction."
- **Purport point:** The verse has no purport of its own on Vedabase. The purport of BG 17.10, which discusses the same group of verses, says that the purpose of food is to increase the length of life, purify the mind and aid bodily strength, and that milk products, sugar, rice, wheat, fruits and vegetables were chosen by earlier authorities for this purpose.
- **Use:** for questions about what to eat.

**BG 17.10**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/17/10/
- **Use:** background for BG 17.8 only (see above).

**BG 17.14**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/17/14/
> "Austerity of the body consists in worship of the Supreme Lord, the brāhmaṇas, the spiritual master, and superiors like the father and mother, and in cleanliness, simplicity, celibacy and nonviolence."
- **Teaching:** Cleanliness and simplicity are listed as austerity of the body.
- **Use:** in the talk, for the part on cleanliness.

**SB 6.1.13-14**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/6/1/13-14/
> "…control the mind and senses, give charity, be truthful, clean and nonviolent, follow the regulative principles and regularly chant the holy name of the Lord."
- **Purport point:** The purport says that, to begin, one must control the tongue and the process of eating. In devotional service this is done by chanting the Hare Krishna mantra, speaking only of Krishna and tasting only what is offered to Krishna.
- **Use:** background; BIKC uses the purport in the same way.

**BG 2.14**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/14/
> "…and one must learn to tolerate them without being disturbed."
- **Purport point:** The purport gives the examples of bathing early on a cold morning and of a woman cooking in the heat of summer, to show that duty is done in spite of weather.
- **Application (preacher's):** This verse is about tolerating ordinary discomfort while doing our duty. It does not tell us to ignore a real illness or to refuse treatment.
- **Use:** for questions.

**SB 1.9.27**: Verified (purport) · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/9/27/
- **Purport point:** In a list of how to overcome faults, the purport says, "Disturbances from various diseases can be avoided by regulated diets." Bhishma is teaching Yudhisthira here.
- **Application (preacher's):** The purport states a general principle. It does not say that every illness comes from diet. BIKC cites this line.
- **Use:** background.

**SB 4.28.36**: Verified (purport) · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/28/35-36/
- **Context:** King Malayadhvaja practised severe austerity and became thin.
- **Purport point:** The purport says that a person who wants to advance should not eat very much, that we should eat only prasadam and no more than needed, and that fasts on Ekadasi and on appearance days help prevent too much sleep and laziness.
- **Application (preacher's):** The purport also says that being too fat shows a lack of progress. Never use this line to comment on another person's body. Speak only about eating no more than we need.
- **Use:** background.

**SB 8.6.12**: Verified (purport) · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/8/6/12/
- **Purport point:** The purport recommends protecting cows and says that "one should take ample milk, and thus one can prolong one's life, develop his brain, execute devotional service." The verse itself is about bhakti-yoga being the way to the Lord's favour. BIKC quotes the purport sentence about milk.
- **Use:** background. See bss-04 for the balance on milk.

**SB 12.3.33**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/12/3/33/
> "The brahmacārīs will fail to execute their vows and become generally unclean…"
- **Teaching:** This is listed among the signs of Kali-yuga. The word translated "unclean" is a failure of care that BIKC takes up in its section on cleanliness.
- **Use:** background. SB 12.3.33 is from Canto 12: BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples.

## F. Philosophical explanation
**Principle.** The body is made of matter, and the soul is the person inside it (BG 2.13-14, covered in other topics). A devotee also knows a second fact: the body has been given to him by Krishna and still belongs to Krishna. Lord Caitanya says this directly to Sanatana Gosvami: "Your body is My property" (CC Antya 4.76). A surrendered devotee does not own his body, so he does not have the right to neglect it or to destroy it.

**Why it matters.** There are two opposite mistakes. One is to enjoy the body, to make it comfortable, beautiful or strong for its own sake. The second is to say "I am not this body" and treat the body with contempt. BIKC compares the second attitude to the Mayavadi attitude and gives the Vaisnava thought instead: "This body belongs to Krishna, and I have to serve Krishna with it. It must be kept fit enough to serve Krishna." (summary of BIKC chapter 25). Lord Caitanya's words to Sanatana teach the same thing in a story.

**Connections.** SB 7.6.5 gives the reason to keep healthy: the highest goal is reached while the body is strong. BG 6.16-17 gives the method: neither too much nor too little in eating, sleeping, recreation and work. NOI 2 gives the warning: eating more than necessary spoils devotion, and so does following rules only for the sake of the rules. BG 3.13 and SB 6.1.13-14 give the centre of the method: prasadam, the holy name and the control of the tongue.

**Consequences.** If the body is treated as Krishna's tool, then tiredness, sickness and low mood are problems to be handled with help, and they are not signs that we have failed. If the body is treated as our own, we become either anxious about our comfort or careless about our health, and in both cases service suffers.

**Common misunderstandings.**
1. "A good devotee never gets sick." Sanatana Gosvami was among the greatest devotees, and he had sores on his body (CC Antya 4.20-21). Illness comes to the body, not to the devotion.
2. "More austerity means more advancement." BG 6.16 says that eating too little or sleeping too little also prevents yoga. NOI 2 warns against following rules only for the sake of the rules.
3. "Health is material, so it is not our business." SB 7.6.5 and CC Antya 4.78 show that a healthy body is an instrument for the Lord's work.
4. "If I am unwell in mind, I should only chant more." Chanting and association help and are the heart of our life. They do not replace a doctor or counsellor when we need one. See K.4.

**How to pitch it.** Speak warmly. Many in the audience have given up comforts and are tired or homesick. Begin with Sanatana's story, because it shows that Krishna is tender with a sick devotee.

## H. Opening and engagement
**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes).**
1. Begin with a short prayer or one round of kirtana. Say the title once.
2. Say: "Tonight we will talk about the body. Many of us are tired by the end of the day, and some of us are unwell. This session is not about fitness. It is about a question: whose body is it, and what does Krishna want from us concerning it?"
3. Say this clearly before the story: "Later we will hear about a great devotee who was very ill and who felt that his life was without value. If anyone here ever has such thoughts about themselves, please speak to a trusted senior devotee or a qualified counsellor. In India you can call Tele-MANAS on 14416. In other countries, please contact your local crisis service. You will not be judged, and I will stay after the session if you want to talk."
4. Say: "We will begin with that story."

**H.2 Opening questions.**
1. "What is one habit of your day that helps you chant or serve better?" (Take three answers. Say "thank you" to each. Do not correct.)
2. "What is one thing that makes it hard for you to serve with a fresh mind, for example sleep, food, noise or worry?"
3. "If a friend were ill, would you say that his illness shows a spiritual problem? Why or why not?" (This leads to the story.)

## I. Core teaching points
**I.1 The body is Krishna's property and instrument (SB 7.6.5, CC Antya 4.76, 4.78, 4.83).** Prahlada says that a wise person works for the goal of life while the body is strong. Lord Caitanya goes further and tells Sanatana that his body is the Lord's own property and that the Lord will do many tasks through it. So health is about service. An illustration (preacher's, labelled): a farmer who borrows a bullock cart from a neighbour does not leave it in the rain, and he does not overload it until it breaks. He looks after it because it is the neighbour's and he must return it in good order. Our body is on loan from Krishna in the same way. Ending: ask "What does Krishna want to do through my body this week?" Transition: how do we look after it?

**I.2 Food: prasadam, enough, and not too much (BG 3.13, 6.16, 17.8, NOI 2, SB 6.1.13-14).**
1. Eat only food offered to Krishna (BG 3.13). This keeps the diet simple and makes eating a part of worship.
2. Eat neither too much nor too little (BG 6.16). The purport says "too much" means more than is needed to keep body and soul together. Eating more than necessary is the first of the six faults in NOI 2.
3. Choose foods that support health and a clear mind (BG 17.8, with the purport of BG 17.10). The purport names milk products, grains, fruits and vegetables.
4. Do not turn rules into a burden. NOI 2 warns against following rules only for their own sake. BIKC says that a devotee who cannot control his tongue at feasts should not despair, because taking only prasadam already restrains the tongue, and patient service will help over time. Lord Caitanya also encouraged devotees to take prasadam happily (summary of BIKC).
5. Do not copy extreme fasting. Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami and the other Gosvamis ate very little because they were absorbed in service, and BIKC says we should not imitate them (see bss-01).
Transition: food is only one part of the day. What about sleep?

**I.3 Sleep and movement (BG 6.16-17; research in O).** BG 6.16 warns against too much sleep and too little sleep. The purport says one should not sleep more than six hours a day, and the BG 6.17 purport says a Krishna conscious person keeps sleep to a minimum because he cannot bear to pass a minute without service. This is the measure we teach: the spiritual purpose sets the amount, and the body's needs are met inside it. The same verse also warns against not sleeping enough, so the way to keep the measure is regularity: go to bed early, rise for the morning program, and keep the same hours every day. Material research on sleep (O) is not the standard for a devotee. Say plainly: "Srila Prabhupada gives the measure: not more than six hours, at regular times, so that we rise fresh for service. If you keep regular hours and are still always tired, tell your mentor and see a doctor, because constant tiredness can be a sign of illness." BIKC (summary) notes that some devotees manage on five hours, some need six or more, and that going to bed early is better than struggling through the morning program half asleep. For movement, BIKC says that dancing in kirtana and walking while chanting japa already give exercise, and that a few minutes of exercise are acceptable. The World Health Organization's guidelines for adults suggest 150 to 300 minutes of moderate activity a week (Bull et al. 2020; see O). Walking briskly while chanting japa is one way to move; each person should adapt the amount to his age and health. Transition: one more area, the daily care of the body.

**I.4 Cleanliness, illness and mental health (BG 17.14, SB 12.3.33; CC Antya 4).**
1. Cleanliness is austerity of the body (BG 17.14). A clean body, clean clothes and a clean room show care for the service we are doing.
2. When ill, tell your mentor and see a doctor. Do not hide it and do not try to "bear it" in silence. Sanatana Gosvami did not hide his sores from the Lord (CC Antya 4.20). BG 2.14 asks us to tolerate ordinary heat, cold and discomfort. It does not ask us to ignore real illness.
3. If you feel very low, anxious or hopeless, or you have thoughts of harming yourself, that is a health matter and not a failure of faith. Speak to a trusted senior devotee, and also see a qualified counsellor or doctor. In India call Tele-MANAS on 14416. In other countries contact the local crisis service. Chanting, hearing and the company of devotees are a great support in recovery. They work together with proper care and do not replace it. Never stop a medicine without asking the doctor who prescribed it. (Application, preacher's; general safety guidance.)
Ending: a healthy and cared-for body is a sign that we remember whose it is.

## J. Stories and illustrations
**J.1 Sanatana Gosvami at Jagannatha Puri (CC Antya 4.15-4.83).** Tell it in order, using only what the verses say.
1. Sanatana Gosvami reached Puri with itching sores on his body. He met Haridasa Thakura, and the Lord came to see them (4.15-4.18).
2. When the Lord came to embrace him, Sanatana stepped back. He said he was low-born and had infections on his body (4.19-4.20).
3. The Lord embraced him by force, and the fluid from the sores touched the Lord's body (4.21).
4. Later the Lord spoke to him about giving up the body. Sanatana had decided to end his life, and the Lord told him that one cannot reach Krishna by giving up the body (4.55-4.56). Say only this and do not describe any method. Sanatana understood that the Lord had forbidden it (4.72-4.73).
5. Sanatana said he was the lowest of men and asked what use his life was (4.75). The Lord answered, "Your body is My property" (4.76). He said that the body was His instrument for many tasks, and He named them: to explain the principles of devotional service, to establish centres, to rediscover lost places of pilgrimage and to teach the renounced order (4.78-4.80). Then He said, "I have to do all this work through your body, but you want to give it up. How can I tolerate this?" (4.83).
**Devotional lesson:** Krishna is tender with a sick devotee. A body that looks useless to its owner is, for the Lord, an instrument for service. Our worth does not depend on how healthy we feel. It depends on our relationship with Krishna.
**Note for the preacher:** after this story, stop for a moment. Say once more that anyone who has such thoughts should speak to someone, and give the helpline again.

**J.2 Rupa Gosvami and Haridasa Thakura (BG 6.17 purport).** The purport of BG 6.17 says Rupa Gosvami slept no more than two hours a day, and sometimes not even that. It says Haridasa Thakura would not take prasadam or sleep before completing his daily chanting of three hundred thousand names. The purport presents them as ideals of service. **Lesson:** their lives show what absorption in service can do. They are not instructions for beginners. We follow their footsteps by serving as our guru directs (see bss-01).

**J.3 Invented illustration (labelled).** A young man who has just joined an ashram decides to sleep only four hours and to skip lunch on three days a week to "become strict". After three weeks he is falling asleep during the morning class and has a headache. His mentor asks him to keep the measure Srila Prabhupada gives in the BG 6.16 purport, regular sleep of up to six hours with an early bedtime, and to eat regular prasadam. His chanting becomes better, not worse. The point: BG 6.16 warns about too little as well as too much, and NOI 2 says rules followed only for show spoil devotion.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections
1. **"Isn't it bodily consciousness to care for the body?"** Bodily consciousness is thinking "I am this body". Caring for the body as the Lord's property and instrument is service. Lord Caitanya called Sanatana's body His property and told him not to destroy it (CC Antya 4.76).
2. **"Prabhupada says we should sleep very little. Should I?"** The BG 6.16 purport gives the measure: not more than six hours. The BG 6.17 purport shows Rupa Gosvami, who slept two hours or less, as the ideal of absorption in service, not as a rule for a beginner. The same verse warns against sleeping too little. So keep regular hours within the six-hour measure, go to bed early, and let your service grow. Do not cut your sleep further by yourself; if you are always tired on a regular schedule, tell your mentor and see a doctor.
3. **"What should I eat?"** Take prasadam, in a moderate amount, with foods in the mode of goodness (BG 17.8-10). For particular diets, such as for a medical condition, follow your doctor. BIKC cautions against health-food fads and special diets that become a preoccupation.
4. **"I am sad all the time, or anxious. Is it just maya?"** It may be a health problem and not a lack of faith. Speak to a senior devotee you trust, and see a doctor or counsellor. In India, Tele-MANAS is 14416. Elsewhere, contact the local crisis service. Keep chanting what you can and stay in the company of devotees.
5. **"Why did the Lord stop Sanatana from giving up his body?"** The Lord said it does not lead to Krishna (CC Antya 4.56), that "measures like suicide are causes for sin" (CC Antya 4.60) and that the body is His property with work still to do (4.76-4.83). Anyone with such thoughts about themselves should speak to someone today. Help is available.
6. **"Is exercise needed?"** BIKC says a few minutes are acceptable, that kirtana and walking japa already move the body, and that heavy exercise is not recommended for brahmacaris. The WHO guideline gives a general target for adults (O). Adapt it to your age and health.
7. **"Can fasting cure disease?"** No claim is made here. Fasting on Ekadasi is a devotional practice (SB 4.28.36 purport). Any medical use of fasting needs a doctor's advice.

## L. Case studies
**L.1 Invented case for discussion.** Karan, 20, is a new brahmacari. He is always tired, he nods off during the morning class, and he feels guilty. He thinks that his tiredness proves he is not serious. He sleeps five hours because he has heard that "real devotees sleep little".
- *Dilemma:* Should he try to sleep even less?
- *Principles:* BG 6.16-17; CC Antya 4.76 and 4.78; SB 7.6.5.
- *Questions:* What does BG 6.16 say about sleeping too little? Whom could he talk to? What would be a gentle first step?
- *Analysis:* He should tell his mentor, plan an earlier bedtime for two weeks and watch how he feels. If he is still very tired, he should see a doctor. He should not judge himself. His body is the Lord's instrument and needs care.
- *Takeaway:* tiredness is information to share with someone. It is not a proof of failure.

**L.2 Invented case for discussion (extension).** Dev, 23, begins to exercise two hours a day and to count his protein. He is proud of his shape and compares himself with others.
- *Principles:* NOI 2 (over-endeavouring for mundane things), BG 17.14 (simplicity), SB 7.6.5 (the body is a means).
- *Questions:* What has changed in his purpose? How could he keep what is useful and turn it back toward service?
- *Takeaway:* a fit body is good when it serves. When it becomes a goal, it joins the list of things that spoil devotion in NOI 2.

## M. Discussion and reflection

1. *Whose body is it, according to Lord Caitanya?* The Lord's property and instrument (CC Antya 4.76, 4.78).
2. *Why did the Lord not want Sanatana to give up his body?* Krishna is not reached by giving up the body, and the Lord had work to do through it (CC Antya 4.56, 4.83).
3. *What does BG 6.16 say about eating and sleeping?* Not too much and not too little.
4. *What does NOI 2 say about rules?* Following them only for their own sake, or rejecting them whimsically, spoils devotion.
5. *What should you do if you are very low in mood or have thoughts of harming yourself?* Speak to a trusted senior devotee and see a doctor or counsellor. In India call Tele-MANAS on 14416; elsewhere, use the local crisis service.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary
The body does not belong to us. It belongs to Krishna, and He uses it as an instrument. We learn this from the story of Sanatana Gosvami. He came to Jagannatha Puri with itching sores on his body. He felt unworthy and had decided to end his life. Lord Caitanya embraced him, told him that one cannot reach Krishna by giving up the body, and said, "Your body is My property" (CC Antya 4.76). The Lord told him that He had many tasks to do through his body (CC Antya 4.78).
Because the body is Krishna's, we look after it with simple habits so that we can serve. We take only prasadam, in a moderate amount (BG 3.13, BG 6.16). We neither sleep too much nor too little (BG 6.16-17). We follow the rules to advance in devotion and not just for their own sake (NOI 2). We keep ourselves and our rooms clean (BG 17.14). Srila Prabhupada gives the measure for sleep: not more than six hours, at regular times, so that we rise fresh for the morning program (BG 6.16 purport). For movement, the World Health Organization suggests 150 to 300 minutes of activity a week for adults; walking while chanting japa is one way. If we are always tired or unwell, a doctor can advise on our own case.
If we are ill, tired or very low in mood, we tell a mentor and see a doctor or counsellor. We keep chanting and stay in devotees' company. In India, Tele-MANAS (14416) gives free help. In other countries, call the local crisis service. If you ever feel that your life has no value, please speak to someone today.

## S2. Key ideas
- The body is Krishna's property and His instrument (CC Antya 4.76, 4.78).
- We keep it fit for service. Health is not the goal.
- Eat only prasadam, not too much and not too little. Do not sleep too much or too little.
- Do not follow rules only for show, and do not drop them whimsically (NOI 2).
- When unwell in body or mind, ask for help. This is wise and not weak.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **CC Antya 4.76**: "Your body is My property. You have already surrendered unto Me." *For me:* I look after my body because it is the Lord's.
- **BG 6.17**: A person regulated in eating, sleeping, recreation and work can mitigate material pains by yoga. *For me:* I keep a steady daily routine.
- **SB 7.6.5**: Work for the highest goal while the body is strong. *For me:* I use my healthy years for service.

## S4. Words to know
- **Brahmacari:** a celibate student who lives simply and serves his guru.
- **Prasadam:** food offered to Krishna.
- **Mentor:** a senior devotee who guides you in daily life.
- **Austerity (tapasya):** voluntary simplicity that purifies the heart.
- **Mode of goodness:** the quality of nature that brings clarity and health.
- **Ekadasi:** a fasting day on the eleventh day after each new and full moon.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *Is caring for my body a sign of attachment?* No. The Lord called Sanatana's body His property and told him not to destroy it. Caring for it is service (CC Antya 4.76).
2. *Does illness mean I am a bad devotee?* No. Sanatana Gosvami had sores, and the Lord embraced him (CC Antya 4.20-21).
3. *How much should I sleep?* The BG 6.16 purport says not more than six hours, and the verse warns against sleeping too little. Keep regular hours, go to bed early and rise for the morning program. If you are always tired, tell your mentor and see a doctor.
4. *What if I feel very sad or anxious?* Tell a senior devotee you trust, and see a doctor or counsellor. In India call Tele-MANAS 14416. Elsewhere, use the local crisis service.
5. *Should I fast a lot to become advanced?* No. BG 6.16 warns against eating too little. NOI 2 warns against following rules only for their own sake.

**Answers:** 1. The Lord's (CC Antya 4.76). 2. One cannot reach Krishna by giving up the body, and the Lord had work to do through it (CC Antya 4.56, 4.83). 3. Eating too much and too little; sleeping too much and too little (BG 6.16). 4. Eating more than necessary spoils devotional service (NOI 2). 5. A mentor or trusted senior devotee, and a doctor or counsellor; in India Tele-MANAS 14416.
