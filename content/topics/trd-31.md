# Laghu-bhagavatamrta: Krishna and His avataras

> **TRD · up to 180 min · review: drafted.** Group F, advanced Gaudiya siddhanta. Rupa Gosvami's Laghu-bhagavatamrta ("the
> lesser nectar of the Bhagavata", written as a companion to his brother Sanatana's Brhad-bhagavatamrta, trd-30) sets out in
> order the forms and descents of the Lord: which form is original, which are expansions, which are incarnations, and of how
> many kinds. Lord Caitanya gave the same order to Sanatana Gosvami at Varanasi (CC Madhya 20), and Srila Prabhupada's
> purports there cite the Laghu-bhagavatamrta directly. **Source note:** the file `research/books/Rupa_Goswami_Sri_Laghu_Bhagavatamrta.txt`
> and Visvanatha Cakravarti's summary `Visvanath_Cakravarti_Thakura_Sri_Bhagavatamrta_Kanika.txt` are font-scrambled PDF
> extractions and could not be read in this session. The book is quoted only where Srila Prabhupada quotes it (CC Madhya 20.165
> purport); its own structure is marked "Requires source review".

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on trd-27: that page establishes that Krishna is the original Personality of Godhead (SB 1.3.28; Bs 5.1) and the source of
all avataras. This page does not re-prove it; it lays out the ordered list of what comes from Him. Builds on trd-30: there the
realms were graded; here the Lord's forms are graded, from svayam-rupa down to the empowered living entity.

A preacher meets the avatara question in every form: the villager who asks whether Krishna is "one of the ten avataras", the
scholar who treats all incarnations as equal, the modern teacher who claims to be one. Lord Caitanya anticipated all of this when
He taught Sanatana Gosvami, and Rupa Gosvami then wrote the Laghu-bhagavatamrta to give the same teaching with the Puranas' evidence
in order. Srila Prabhupada's purports to CC Madhya 20.244-246 give the complete lists. No lower batch learns the classification
as a subject; without it a resident answers from memory of pictures, not from siddhanta.

## C. Central message

Krishna is the one original form; every other form of Godhead is His expansion, and an expansion that descends into the material
world is called an avatara (CC Madhya 20.263-264). Lord Caitanya counts six kinds of avataras and says they are innumerable, "like
rivulets flowing from inexhaustible sources of water" (SB 1.3.26). Rupa Gosvami's Laghu-bhagavatamrta gives this order with the
scriptures' evidence, so that a devotee never mistakes a river for the source.

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Define avatara from CC Madhya 20.263-264 and distinguish "expansion" from "incarnation".
2. Name the three principal forms of the Lord (svayam-rupa, tad-ekatma-rupa, avesa-rupa: CC Madhya 20.165) and explain
   prabhava and vaibhava (20.167-168).
3. List the six kinds of avataras (CC Madhya 20.245-246) and give the principal members of each from Srila Prabhupada's purports.
4. Explain why the Lord descends and why His body is transcendental (BG 4.6-8; CC Madhya 20.248 purport), and the fruit of knowing
   this (BG 4.9).
5. Say what the Laghu-bhagavatamrta is, where Srila Prabhupada cites it, and what we have and have not read of it.

**CC Madhya 20.165 (with purport)** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/20/165/
> "The Supreme Personality of Godhead exists in three principal forms — svayaṁ-rūpa, tad-ekātma-rūpa and āveśa-rūpa."
- **Context:** Lord Caitanya to Sanatana Gosvami at Varanasi, beginning the sambandha teaching on the Lord's forms.
- **Purport point:** Prabhupada quotes the Laghu-bhagavatamrta: "Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī has described the svayaṁ-rūpa in his Laghu-bhāgavatāmṛta, Pūrva-khaṇḍa, verse 12: ananyāpekṣi yad rūpaṁ svayaṁ-rūpaḥ sa ucyate." His translation: "The form of the Supreme Personality of Godhead that does not depend on other forms is called the svayaṁ-rūpa, the original form." He adds SB 1.3.28 and Bs 5.1 as confirmation.
- **Application (preacher's):** this is the one place where we hold the Laghu-bhagavatamrta's words in Prabhupada's own citation. Quote
  it as he does, with the book, division and verse number.
- **Use:** in the talk; the starting definition.

**CC Madhya 20.166-168** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/20/166/
> 20.166: "The original form of the Lord [svayaṁ-rūpa] is exhibited in two forms — svayaṁ-rūpa and svayaṁ-prakāśa. In His original form as svayaṁ-rūpa, Kṛṣṇa is observed as a cowherd boy in Vṛndāvana."
- **Teaching (20.167-168):** in His original form Krishna manifests prabhava and vaibhava; "He expands His one original form into many, as He did during the rāsa-līlā dance" (167); the forms at the rasa dance and the 16,108 forms at Dvaraka are prabhava-prakasa (168).
- **Use:** in the talk; the room should see that "expansion" begins inside Vrindavana itself.

**CC Madhya 20.244-246 (with purports)** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/20/245/
> 20.245: "There are six types of incarnations [avatāras] of Kṛṣṇa. One comprises the incarnations of Viṣṇu [puruṣa-avatāras], and another comprises the incarnations meant for the performance of pastimes [līlā-avatāras]."
> 20.246: "There are incarnations that control the material qualities [guṇa-avatāras], incarnations who appear during the reign of each Manu [manvantara-avatāras], incarnations in different millenniums [yuga-avatāras] and incarnations of empowered living entities [śaktyāveśa-avatāras]."
- **Context (20.244):** "The first personal expansion is Saṅkarṣaṇa, and the others are incarnations like the fish incarnation."
- **Purport point (20.244):** the three purusa-avataras named: "Kāraṇodakaśāyī Viṣṇu, Garbhodakaśāyī Viṣṇu and Kṣīrodakaśāyī Viṣṇu". Then the twenty-five lila-avataras, from "(1) Catuḥsana, or the four Kumāras, (2) Nārada, (3) Varāha, (4) Matsya" to "(23) Kṛṣṇa, (24) Buddha and (25) Kalki", called kalpa-avataras because they appear in each day of Brahma; some are counted among the prabhava and some among the vaibhava avataras.
- **Purport point (20.246):** "The guṇa-avatāras are three — Lord Brahmā, Lord Śiva and Lord Viṣṇu"; fourteen manvantara-avataras listed from Yajna to Brhadbhanu, "sometimes called vaibhava-avatāras"; the four yuga-avataras by colour, white, red, dark blue and "generally kṛṣṇa (black) but in special cases pīta (yellow) as Caitanya Mahāprabhu in Kali-yuga"; the saktyavesa-avataras divided into divine absorption (bhagavad-avesa) and the seven empowered with a particular sakti, from Sesa "empowered for the personal service of the Supreme Lord ( sva-sevana-śakti )" to Parasurama "empowered to cut down rogues and demons ( duṣṭa-damana-śakti )".
- **Use:** in the talk; these two purports are the backbone of N and of the handout table.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** There is one original form, svayam-rupa, "that does not depend on other forms" (Laghu-bhagavatamrta, Purva-khanda 12,
   as Prabhupada cites it); that form is Krishna, the cowherd boy of Vrindavana (CC Madhya 20.166; SB 1.3.28). Every other form of
   Godhead is either His own appearance in another feature (tad-ekatma-rupa) or a living entity He has empowered (avesa-rupa) (20.165).
2. **Expansion is not incarnation.** All expansions live in the spiritual world; the same expansion, when it descends to the material
   world, is called an avatara, "one who descends" (CC Madhya 20.263-264; SB 1.3.28 purport). So the question "is Krishna an avatara?" is
   answered in two steps: as the source He is not one of the rivers; yet when He Himself descends, He is counted among the avataras
   "because out of His causeless mercy the Lord descends" (SB 1.3.28 purport).
3. **The six kinds** (CC Madhya 20.245-246, with the purports' lists):
   - *Purusa-avataras:* Karanodakasayi, Garbhodakasayi and Ksirodakasayi Visnu, the Lords of creation.
   - *Lila-avataras:* twenty-five pastime incarnations, from the four Kumaras to Kalki, innumerable in truth (20.297-298).
   - *Guna-avataras:* Brahma, Visnu and Siva, who control the modes; Siva accepts maya's association, Visnu is beyond it (20.311-312).
   - *Manvantara-avataras:* fourteen, one for each Manu, from Yajna to Brhadbhanu.
   - *Yuga-avataras:* white, red, black and yellow for the four ages (20.330); the yellow one is Lord Caitanya (20.246 purport; trd-32).
   - *Saktyavesa-avataras:* empowered living entities, primary (avatara) and secondary (vibhuti) (20.368); the chief seven named with their saktis (20.246 purport).
4. **Why He descends.** BG 4.7-8: decline of religion, rise of irreligion; to deliver the pious, destroy the miscreants and re-establish
   dharma. Prabhupada's purport adds that the killing could be done by His agents; "the Lord especially descends to appease His unalloyed devotees". The avatara is for the devotee.
5. **How He descends.** Not by taking a material body: "there is no creation of the Lord's form or body, since all of the forms are
   eternally existent" (BG 4.7 purport); the avataras are not within this world but "visible by the causeless mercy of the Lord"
   (CC Madhya 20.248 purport). Hence the moon-in-the-branches example: it looks placed in the tree and is far beyond it.
6. **The fruit of knowing this.** "One who knows the transcendental nature of My appearance and activities… attains My eternal abode"
   (BG 4.9). Avatara-tattva is not scholarship; it is a path to Goloka (trd-30).
7. **Why Rupa Gosvami wrote it.** Lord Caitanya gave Sanatana the order orally (CC Madhya 20); Rupa Gosvami, who received the Lord's
   instruction at Prayaga, set it down with the scriptures' evidence, as Prabhupada's citation of "Pūrva-khaṇḍa, verse 12" shows. The
   book is a reference work for exactly the questions a preacher is asked.
8. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *"Krishna is the eighth of Vishnu's ten avataras."* The Dasavatara is a selection; the list is innumerable (SB 1.3.26); and Krishna is the source of the list (SB 1.3.28; trd-27).
   - *"All incarnations are equal; choose any."* The kinds differ in nature and function (CC Madhya 20.245-246); Siva and Brahma are guna-avataras, not Visnu-tattva in the same sense (20.311).
   - *"A great saint is an avatara."* A saint may be a saktyavesa-avatara only if directly empowered; otherwise at most vibhuti (20.368). Modern self-declared incarnations fit neither; Prabhupada's test is scripture (SB 1.3.26 purport lists them by name).
   - *"The avatara's body is born and dies."* It is manifested, not created (BG 4.6-7 purport).
9. **Pitch for TRD.** Give the lists exactly as Prabhupada's purports give them; do not improvise members. Name the Laghu-bhagavatamrta
   as the source and say what we have read of it (one verse, through Prabhupada).

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet the devotees and say that today's subject is the Lord's forms and descents, in order, from Lord Caitanya's own teaching.
2. Say: "Every one of us has been asked at the door: which God do you worship, and is Krishna one of the ten avataras? Today we
   answer from siddhanta, not from memory."
3. Ask H.2 question 1; take three answers and write the words used (avatara, expansion, incarnation, form) on the board.
4. Ask H.2 question 2 for a show of hands.
5. Say that Rupa Gosvami wrote a whole book to answer these questions and that Lord Caitanya taught its content to Sanatana; begin with CC Madhya 20.165.

**H.2 Opening questions**
1. When someone says "Krishna is an avatara of Vishnu", what exactly do they mean, and what do you answer?
2. How many incarnations are there: ten, twenty-four, or more? (show of hands)
3. What is the difference, in your own words, between an expansion and an incarnation?

## I. Core teaching points

1. **One original form.** Svayam-rupa is the form that depends on no other (Laghu-bhagavatamrta, Purva-khanda 12, in CC Madhya 20.165
   purport); it is Krishna in Vrindavana (20.166). *Transition:* and yet He is many.
2. **He expands without ceasing to be one.** Prabhava and vaibhava; the rasa dance and Dvaraka (20.167-168). *Example:* one lamp lighting
   many lamps (Prabhupada's frequent comparison; not a verse). *Transition:* when do we call an expansion an avatara?
3. **Avatara means "one who descends".** An expansion in the spiritual world that descends to the material world (20.263-264; SB 1.3.28 purport).
   *Transition:* how many kinds descend?
4. **Six kinds, all from Krishna.** Purusa, lila, guna, manvantara, yuga, saktyavesa (20.245-246), with the purports' lists. *Transition:* why does He come?
5. **For His devotees.** BG 4.7-8 and purport: the demons could be killed by others; He comes to relieve His devotees. *Transition:* in what kind of body?
6. **In His own eternal form, by His own mercy.** BG 4.6-7 purport; CC Madhya 20.248 purport (the moon and the branches). *Transition:* and the result of knowing it?
7. **Knowing His descent, one goes to His abode.** BG 4.9. *Transition:* the yellow avatara of our own age, trd-32.

## J. Stories and illustrations

**1. Lord Caitanya teaches Sanatana the kinds of avataras (CC Madhya 20.244-368).** Not a narrative with events but the scene itself:
Sanatana Gosvami, having escaped prison and reached Varanasi, sits before the Lord for two months, and the Lord, as a teacher, lays out
the Lord's forms in order, pausing to say that the incarnations cannot be counted and that He will name the chief ones (20.297). Tell
the room where in the chapter each kind is given, so that they can read it themselves. *Lesson:* the order itself is mercy; a disciple
who has the order can answer any question.

**2. The moon and the branches (CC Madhya 20.248 and purport).** The Lord's own example: the avataras cannot be counted; "We can simply
indicate them by giving the example of the moon and the branches of a tree." Prabhupada: the moon seems to sit in the branches but is
far away; so the avataras appear in the material world but are not of it. *Lesson:* never speak of the Lord's descent as if He took a
body like ours (BG 4.6-7 purport).

**3. Krishna counted among the avataras though He is the source (SB 1.3.28 and purport).** Suta Gosvami lists the incarnations and then
says all are portions or portions of portions "but Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa is the original Personality of Godhead." Prabhupada: He is counted among
the avataras "because out of His causeless mercy the Lord descends". *Lesson:* the two-step answer to the villager's question; the full
proof is trd-27.

**4. The Kali-yuga avatara "garbed as a devotee" (SB 1.3.26 purport).** Prabhupada cites Prahlada's prayer that the Lord appears according
to the yugas and "In the Kali-yuga You have incarnated garbed as a devotee", and adds: "This incarnation of the Lord in the Kali-yuga is Lord Caitanya Mahāprabhu."
*Lesson:* the list we teach ends at our own age; the next page takes it up.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"Is Krishna an avatara or not? You seem to say both."** Both are true in order: He is the source, not one of the rivers (SB 1.3.28);
   when the source Himself descends, He is counted among the avataras by His mercy (1.3.28 purport; CC Madhya 20.264).
2. **"Are Brahma and Siva incarnations of God?"** They are guna-avataras who control the modes (CC Madhya 20.246 purport). Siva "accepts
   māyā's association" and is in his constitutional form a maha-bhagavata; Visnu is beyond maya (20.311 purport). Give Prabhupada's words as they are.
3. **"Buddha is in the list; so is Buddhism Vedic?"** Buddha is counted as the twenty-fourth lila-avatara (20.244 purport). What He taught and
   why is a separate topic (the Bhagavatam's own account in SB 1.3.24); do not answer beyond the verse.
4. **"Can a living saint be an avatara?"** Only as a saktyavesa-avatara if directly empowered by the Lord; otherwise at most vibhuti (20.368).
   The scriptures name the avataras in advance (SB 1.3.26 purport); a claim with no scriptural basis is not accepted.
5. **"Why so many incarnations? Isn't one enough?"** Because the Lord is unlimited and acts for His devotees in every age and planet
   (SB 1.3.26; BG 4.8 purport). The variety is His mercy, not a weakness of the system.
6. **"We cannot read the Laghu-bhagavatamrta; why name it?"** Because Prabhupada cites it as the source of the definition (CC Madhya 20.165
   purport) and lists it among Rupa Gosvami's works (CC Adi 10.84 purport). We say what we have read.

## L. Case studies

**Case 1 (Invented case for discussion).** At a house programme the host, a devout Vaisnava of another sampradaya, says: "We worship
Vishnu; Krishna is His eighth avatara, so we are already worshipping Krishna." *Dilemma:* agree politely, or correct? *Principles:*
SB 1.3.28 and purport; CC Madhya 20.263-264; Bs 5.39. *Questions:* What is true in the host's statement? Where does it stop? How does
the preacher honour Vishnu and still speak the siddhanta? *Analysis:* begin by agreeing that Krishna descends and is counted among the
avataras; then read SB 1.3.28; then Bs 5.39, where Rama and the others appear from Vaikuntha and Krishna from Goloka. *Takeaway:*
siddhanta is spoken with respect, in order, from the verses.

**Case 2 (Invented case for discussion).** A college student says his family follows a living teacher who is "an avatara of Krishna for
this age". *Dilemma:* how to answer without insulting the family? *Principles:* CC Madhya 20.367-368; SB 1.3.26 purport (the avataras are
named in scripture); BG 4.6 purport (false claims to be God). *Questions:* What would make someone a saktyavesa-avatara? Who decides?
*Analysis:* explain the category honestly; the Lord's empowered agents are named in scripture, and the avatara of this age is also named
(SB 1.3.26 purport). *Takeaway:* teach the category; let the scripture do the judging.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Rupa Gosvami wrote the Laghu-bhagavatamrta to set out, in order, the forms of the Lord and His descents. Lord Caitanya had taught
the same order to Sanatana Gosvami at Varanasi, and Srila Prabhupada quotes the book in his purport: the form of the Lord "that does
not depend on other forms is called the svayaṁ-rūpa, the original form" (CC Madhya 20.165 purport). That form is Krishna, seen as a
cowherd boy in Vrindavana (CC Madhya 20.166). Krishna expands Himself into many forms, as at the rasa dance and in Dvaraka, yet remains
one (20.167-168). Our library's copy of the Laghu-bhagavatamrta is unreadable, so the book is taught through Prabhupada's citation
and Lord Caitanya's teaching.

An expansion of the Lord lives in the spiritual world; when it descends into the material world, it is called an avatara, "one who
descends" (CC Madhya 20.263-264; SB 1.3.28 purport). Lord Caitanya names six kinds (20.245-246). Purusa-avataras: the three Visnus who
create and maintain the universes. Lila-avataras: pastime incarnations such as Matsya, Kurma, Varaha, Nrsimha, Vamana and Rama; the
purport lists twenty-five. Guna-avataras: Brahma, Visnu and Siva, who control the modes of nature; Siva accepts the association of
maya, Visnu is beyond it (20.311). Manvantara-avataras: fourteen, one for each Manu. Yuga-avataras: white, red, black and yellow in the
four ages (20.330); the yellow one of Kali-yuga is Lord Caitanya (20.246 purport). Saktyavesa-avataras: living entities empowered by the
Lord, primary and secondary (20.368). In truth the avataras cannot be counted; they are "like rivulets flowing from inexhaustible
sources of water" (SB 1.3.26), and Krishna is "the original Personality of Godhead" from whom they all come (SB 1.3.28).

Why does He come? "Whenever and wherever there is a decline in religious practice… at that time I descend Myself" (BG 4.7), "to deliver
the pious and to annihilate the miscreants, as well as to reestablish the principles of religion" (BG 4.8). He comes in His own eternal
form, not in a created body (BG 4.6; 4.7 purport), and though He appears in this world He is not of it, like the moon that seems to sit
in the branches of a tree (CC Madhya 20.248 and purport). One who understands His descent "attains My eternal abode" (BG 4.9).

## S2. Key ideas
- Krishna is the svayam-rupa, the one form that depends on no other (CC Madhya 20.165 purport).
- An expansion that descends to the material world is called an avatara (CC Madhya 20.264).
- There are six kinds of avataras: purusa, lila, guna, manvantara, yuga, saktyavesa (20.245-246).
- The avataras are innumerable, and Krishna is their source (SB 1.3.26, 1.3.28).
- He descends for His devotees, in His eternal form; knowing this, one attains His abode (BG 4.7-9).

## S3. Verses to remember
- **CC Madhya 20.264:** "All the expansions of Lord Kṛṣṇa are actually residents of the spiritual world. But when they descend into the material world, they are called incarnations [avatāras]." For me: I know what the word means.
- **SB 1.3.26:** "the incarnations of the Lord are innumerable, like rivulets flowing from inexhaustible sources of water." For me: I never say "only ten".
- **BG 4.9:** one who knows the transcendental nature of His appearance "attains My eternal abode". For me: this study is itself a way home.

## S4. Words to know
- **Svayam-rupa:** the Lord's original form, Krishna, which depends on no other form.
- **Tad-ekatma-rupa / avesa-rupa:** the Lord's own forms in other features; living entities He empowers (CC Madhya 20.165).
- **Prabhava, vaibhava:** the Lord's expansions of Himself into many forms, as at the rasa dance (20.167-168).
- **Avatara:** "one who descends"; an expansion of the Lord appearing in the material world.
- **Purusa, lila, guna, manvantara, yuga, saktyavesa:** the six kinds of avataras.
- **Vibhuti:** a secondary, indirectly empowered living entity (20.368).

## S5. Questions and answers
1. **Is Krishna one of Vishnu's avataras?** Krishna is the source of all avataras (SB 1.3.28); when He Himself descends, He is counted among them by His mercy (1.3.28 purport).
2. **How many avataras are there?** Innumerable (SB 1.3.26); the lists name the principal ones.
3. **Are Brahma and Siva God?** They are guna-avataras who control the modes; Siva accepts maya's association, Visnu is beyond it (CC Madhya 20.246 purport; 20.311).
4. **Can a person today be an avatara?** Only if directly empowered by the Lord as a saktyavesa-avatara, as the scriptures describe; otherwise not (20.368; SB 1.3.26 purport).
5. **Does the Lord take a material body when He comes?** No. His forms are eternal and He manifests Himself (BG 4.6; 4.7 purport).

deliver the pious, annihilate the miscreants, re-establish religion (BG 4.8).
