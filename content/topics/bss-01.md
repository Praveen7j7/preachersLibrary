# Renunciation & surrender of Raghunath Das Goswami

> **BSS · 2 hours · review: drafted · sensitive (celibacy, austerity, food).** Written for aspiring and vowed brahmacaris.
> The warning in K about imitating extreme austerity is part of the lesson, not an extra.

## A. Topic overview

| | |
|---|---|
| Batch / level | BSS (Bhakti Siddhanta Sabha: devotees preparing for, or living, brahmacari life) |
| Suggested duration | 120 minutes (plan in G) |
| Preparation time | About 2 hours, plus listening to one IYF lecture (O) |
| Difficulty | Advanced |
| Prerequisites | Having read the story once (CC Antya 6; summary in J) |
| Audience | Aspiring and practising brahmacaris; works for any audience that has met the Goswamis |
| Teaching objective | Participants see that real renunciation (vairagya) is the by-product of attachment to Krishna, copy its principles (not its extremes), and know the difference from showy renunciation |

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

The BSS member has chosen, or is choosing, to give up household life and sense enjoyment. The first danger is that renunciation becomes
a performance or a grim contest. Raghunatha dasa Gosvami is the model the Lord Himself held up, so this is the first
page of the batch: it sets the standard (renunciation flowing from love) that the later pages on celibacy, discipline and falldown assume.

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| VL1 | Not taught; at most "the Goswamis gave up great wealth" in one line |
| VL2 | Why a person might give up pleasures for a higher taste |
| VL3 | Regulation and the first sense-control practices |
| SPS | The Goswamis as teachers of steady daily bhajana |
| DPS | Detachment while carrying responsibility |
| BSS | This page: renunciation as fruit of attachment, and its misuse |

**Not done here:** praising hunger or sleeplessness as proof of advancement; ranking devotees; telling anyone to leave family duties without guidance; sexual detail (that is for later BSS pages, starting with the BIKC group).

## C. Central message

Raghunatha dasa did not renounce in order to become great at renunciation. His heart was taken by Krishna, so everything
else fell away. We follow his direction (chant, simple food, no gossip, inner service to Radha-Krishna), not his extreme level, and we ask for the same attachment.

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants will be able to:
1. Explain, with SB 1.2.7 and SB 11.2.42, why detachment follows devotion and not the reverse.
2. Tell the story of Raghunatha dasa in order and say what each stage shows about surrender.
3. Distinguish false renunciation (markata-vairagya) from renunciation connected to Krishna.
4. Recite and apply the Lord's two-line instruction (CC Antya 6.236-237) to a brahmacari day.
5. Say why Srila Prabhupada warned against imitating him.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**SB 1.2.7** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/7/
> "By rendering devotional service unto the Personality of Godhead, Śrī Kṛṣṇa, one immediately acquires causeless knowledge and detachment from the world."
- Context: Suta Gosvami summarises what bhakti brings.
- Teaching: Detachment is a result of bhakti, not a separate project.
- Application (preacher's): A brahmacari's first question each day is "Where is my service and chanting?" and not "What am I giving up?"
- Use: in the talk

**BG 2.59** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/59/
> "Though the embodied soul may be restricted from sense enjoyment, the taste for sense objects remains."
- Context: Krishna on the one whose senses are withdrawn.
- Teaching: Restriction alone leaves the taste; the verse goes on to say that a higher taste ends it.
- Purport point: Srila Prabhupada compares rules without Krishna consciousness to diet restrictions for a patient who still likes the foods. Only a higher taste (Krishna consciousness) removes the old taste.
- Application (preacher's): Raghunatha's higher taste is what made his austerity natural.
- Use: in the talk

**CC Madhya 16.238** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/16/238/
> "You should not make yourself a showbottle devotee and become a false renunciant."
- Context: Lord Caitanya's advice to Raghunatha dasa when he first wanted to leave home; the Lord asked him to stay and wait for the right time.
- Purport point: The purport explains markata-vairagya (monkey-like renunciation) as outward show while inwardly enjoying.
- Application (preacher's): Check motives for any outward step: is it for display, or for service?
- Use: in the talk

**CC Antya 6.221-223, 226-227** — Verified
> "Raghunātha dāsa has done well,"
- Context: Raghunatha stopped taking prasadam at the Lord's residence and began begging at the Simha-dvara (the main gate of Jagannatha's temple). The Lord was pleased (6.221-222).
> "A person in the renounced order should always chant the holy name of the Lord."
> "The duty of a person in the renounced order is to chant the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra always."
> "One who is subservient to the tongue and who thus goes here and there, devoted to the genitals and the belly, cannot attain Kṛṣṇa."
- Teaching: The Lord names what renunciation is for: constant chanting; the food, begging and simplicity serve that.
- Application (preacher's): The tongue and the genitals are the two fastest-growing appetites for a young man; the Lord links them, and the cure is the name on the tongue.
- Use: in the talk

**CC Antya 6.236-237** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/6/236/
> "Do not talk like people in general or hear what they say."
> "Always chant the holy name of Lord Kṛṣṇa, and within your mind render service to Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa in Vṛndāvana."
- Context: The Lord's instruction to Raghunatha, entrusted to Svarupa Damodara.
- Purport point: Bhaktivinoda Thakura's gloss, quoted in the purport, takes ordinary talk (gramya-katha) as talk of family life (marriage, children and their entanglements); the renunciant neither hears nor speaks it.
- Application (preacher's): For a brahmacari today this includes the feed and the chat group: what goes in through the ears.
- Use: in the talk

**CC Antya 6.278** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/6/278/
> "When one eats food offered by a materialistic man, one’s mind becomes contaminated"
- Context: Raghunatha stopped accepting invitations (the Lord's warning is at the start of the passage).
- Purport point: The purport gives Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati's note that a casual sahajiya (so-called Vaisnava) is also a "materialist" for this purpose.
- Use: background for the preacher (on association and food)

**CC Antya 6.293, 6.296** — Verified
> "For three years He kept the stone and garland."
- Context: The Lord gave Raghunatha the Govardhana stone (the Lord's own worship for three years) and gunja garland, and taught the simple worship with a jug of water and tulasi leaves (6.296).
- Teaching: The goal of renunciation is service: Govardhana-sila is Krishna; the garland is Radharani's gift.
- Use: in the talk (closing)

## F. Philosophical explanation

**Principle.** *Yukta-vairagya* (renunciation by engaging things in Krishna's service) versus *phalgu-vairagya* (dry rejection).
BG 2.59 and SB 1.2.7 together say that the taste of the soul must be fed; a hungry soul cannot be satisfied by a list of prohibitions.

**Why it matters for BSS.** Brahmacari life can become competitive austerity or empty rule-keeping. Both end in the hunger BG 2.59 describes.

**Connections.** Sadhana (bss-44 and later) and discipline (BIKC pages) are the how; Raghunatha is the why.

**Consequences.** If renunciation is the result of attachment, we pursue attachment (chanting, hearing, service, association) and the renunciation grows; if we pursue renunciation directly, we need constant force.

**Misunderstandings.**
1. "He fasted, so fasting is the path." The Lord asked him to eat simply, not to starve; his austerity was his own spontaneous mood (see K).
2. "Renunciation is for those who failed in the world." Raghunatha left a family of great wealth (per the story index, data/stories.json; details in J).
3. "Renunciation means hating the body." The body is a servant; he used it for service for many years.

**Pitch.** Tell the story slowly and let the verses answer one question: "What did Raghunatha gain that made all this easy?"

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start.** Welcome them. Say: "Today's life is about the most famous young man in the Goswami line who gave up a great fortune. Before we hear
what he gave up, I want us to guess what he gained." Put the question on the board: "What would have to be so attractive that you would happily
give up your favourite food, your phone, and a comfortable home?" Take three answers without comment; thank each.

**H.2 Opening questions.**
1. When did you last give something up gladly? What made it easy? (Expect: love for a person or goal.)
2. Which is harder for you, to give something up or to keep doing something small every day? Why?
3. Have you seen a person who looks very renounced but is not happy? What was missing? (Return to this at M.1.)

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Renunciation is not the aim; Krishna is.** SB 1.2.7. Example: J.4 (the Govardhana stone). Transition: "How did that begin?"
2. **Real attempts, repeated.** Raghunatha did not succeed at once; the Lord twice (J.2) asked him to wait (CC Madhya 16.238). Show patience and obedience to a guide.
3. **The standard is chanting.** CC Antya 6.223, 226. Everything else is for the name.
4. **Guard the ear and the tongue.** CC Antya 6.227, 236. Gossip, news, rich food, comfort.
5. **Attachment inside:** "within your mind render service to Radha and Krishna" (6.237). Vraja-seva is the target of the heart; the body serves where it is.
6. **Do not imitate; follow the direction.** K.1.

## J. Stories and illustrations

*Source for all: CC Antya 6 (the Lord's meeting and instruction) and the story index (data/stories.json). Details marked "Requires source review" are not checked here.*

**J.1 A wealthy boy.** Raghunatha dasa was the son of a very wealthy man (story index). Lesson: the pull of wealth, and the greater pull.
**J.2 Home and escape.** He left family repeatedly until he reached the Lord at Puri (story index). The Lord told him to wait and not become a showy renunciant (CC Madhya 16.238). *Requires source review* for dates and the order of events; hear the IYF lecture first (O).
**J.3 Under Svarupa Damodara.** The Lord placed Raghunatha in the care of Svarupa Damodara (CC Antya 6 context).
**J.4 The simplicity.** He stopped taking prasadam at the Lord's house and begged at Simha-dvara (6.221); the Lord praised it (6.222-227). Later the Lord gave him the Govardhana stone and garland (6.291-296).
**J.5 Radha-kunda.** He lived at Radha-kunda in great austerity (story index; *Requires source review* for details).
**Devotional lesson.** The Lord gave Raghunatha His own worshipped Govardhana: renunciation is the fruit of attachment to Krishna, who is pleased to give Himself to the renunciant who wants only Him.
**Contemporary illustration (preacher's).** A student who stops all entertainment but has nothing in its place feels empty; the same student who joins the morning kirtan cares less for what he gave up. Always end: the replacement is hearing and chanting.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"Shall I fast and sleep little as he did?"** No. Srila Prabhupada, quoted in BIKC (Path of Perfection, ch. 4), warned that imitating Raghunatha immediately leads to failure. The Goswamis acted from absorption; follow the Lord's instruction instead.
2. **"What if I have no such attachment?"** Pray for it, hear, chant, serve; SB 11.2.42 says detachment comes together with devotion.
3. **"Is renunciation harmful to the body or mind?"** Regulated eating and sleep are part of the path; see BG 6.16-17 on the next pages. If anyone in the room is skipping meals or sleep to the point of weakness, or feels low, speak to a senior devotee and a doctor; in India call Tele-MANAS 14416, elsewhere your local crisis or health service. Renunciation is never a competition.
4. **"Does this mean no family duties?"** Not for the unprepared. The Lord asked Raghunatha to stay and wait before leaving. BSS members consult guides before major steps.
5. **"Is it fanatic to restrict food and talk?"** The rule has a reason: CC Antya 6.278 on food, 6.236 on talk. The mind takes colour from what it receives.

## L. Case studies

**Invented case for discussion.** Arun, 22, joins the ashram. In two weeks he is fasting on three extra days and sleeping four hours, and tells younger boys they are "soft". He feels proud and then ill.
- Dilemma: his zeal is real; his method is imitation.
- Principles: Lord's instruction (chant, simple prasadam); Prabhupada's caution (K.1); BG 2.59 (higher taste).
- Questions: What would Raghunatha's Lord say to him? What should the temple president do first?
- Analysis: Appreciate the zeal, reduce to regulated eating and sleep, increase hearing and chanting, ask a senior to guide; the pride is the first thing to bring to Krishna in prayer.
- Takeaway: austerity serves chanting; if it cuts into chanting, it is wrong for now.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check (with answers).**
1. What does SB 1.2.7 say comes from devotional service? *Knowledge and detachment.*
2. What is false renunciation? *Showbottle devotee (CC Madhya 16.238).*
3. What is the "duty" of the renounced person in CC Antya 6.226? *Chanting Hare Krishna always.*
4. Which two appetites does 6.227 name together? *Belly and genitals (and the tongue).*
5. What was the Lord's inner instruction in 6.237? *Within the mind serve Radha and Krishna in Vrindavana.*
6. Why shouldn't we copy his austerities? *They came from absorption; imitation fails (Prabhupada).*

**M.2 Activities.**
- **Fit the instruction to the day (10 min, pairs).** Write down a brahmacari's day in 8 steps. Mark where "gramya-katha" enters (phone, meals, free time) and one concrete protection for each (for example: switch the phone off after 9 pm; chant first rounds before breakfast).
- **Story relay (10 min, groups of 4).** Each tells one part of the story in 90 seconds; the group says what Krishna is shown to be like in that part.
- **Q and A round** (see K).

**M.3 Reflection.** "What in my day currently comes before the name? What one thing could I offer to Krishna this week instead of removing it?" Private; no sharing needed.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Raghunatha dasa Gosvami was the son of a very rich man, yet he left home again and again until he reached Lord Caitanya at Puri. The Lord first told a young Raghunatha not to become a showbottle devotee, but to act properly and wait. Later Raghunatha lived with very little. He stopped taking food at the Lord's house and begged at the temple gate. The Lord was pleased and said that the renounced person should chant Hare Krishna always and eat simply (CC Antya 6.221-227).

The Lord gave him a short instruction: do not talk or listen to ordinary talk, do not eat very rich food or dress showily, chant the holy name always, and within the mind serve Radha and Krishna in Vrindavana (6.236-237). Later the Lord gave him the Govardhana stone and garland, which He had worshipped for three years.

The lesson is that renunciation is the result of attachment to Krishna (SB 1.2.7). We do not copy his extreme austerity; Srila Prabhupada said imitation would lead to failure. We take his direction: chant, keep simple, guard the ears and tongue, and keep service to Krishna in the mind.

## S2. Key ideas
- Detachment follows devotion (SB 1.2.7).
- Showbottle renunciation is not what the Lord wants (CC Madhya 16.238).
- The renunciant's duty is chanting the holy name always (CC Antya 6.226).
- Follow his direction, not his extreme level.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **SB 1.2.7** — devotional service gives knowledge and detachment. For me: start with service.
- **CC Antya 6.226** — chant Hare Krishna always. For me: my first rounds come first.
- **CC Antya 6.237** — chant and serve Radha-Krishna in the mind. For me: keep Krishna in the mind during the day.

## S4. Words to know
- *Vairagya*: detachment, giving up what is not helpful to Krishna.
- *Markata-vairagya*: "monkey renunciation," show without inner taste.
- *Gramya-katha*: ordinary talk about worldly matters.
- *Simha-dvara*: the lion gate of the Jagannatha temple in Puri.
- *Prasadam*: food offered to Krishna.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. **Why did the Lord say "wait" to Raghunatha?** To avoid showy renunciation (CC Madhya 16.238).
2. **Should I fast like him?** No; imitation fails. Follow the instruction (S1).
3. **How can I become detached?** Through devotional service (SB 1.2.7).
4. **What is "ordinary talk"?** Talk of worldly and family life (CC Antya 6.236-237 purport).

## S6. Check your understanding
1. What does SB 1.2.7 say comes from devotional service?
2. What does "showbottle devotee" mean?
3. What did the Lord say a renunciant should do always?
4. Where did Raghunatha beg?
5. Why should we not imitate his austerities?

**Answers:** 1. Knowledge and detachment (SB 1.2.7). 2. A false renunciant who shows outwardly (CC Madhya 16.238). 3. Chant Hare Krishna (CC Antya 6.226). 4. At the Simha-dvara (CC Antya 6.221). 5. They came from absorption; imitation leads to failure (S1).

## S7. Try this week
Before you remove or reduce anything, add one thing for Krishna (for example, 10 more minutes of attentive japa). Private reflection: "What would I most like to give Krishna?"
