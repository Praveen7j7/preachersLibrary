## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on DPS-02 (revealing the heart in confidence): that page teaches NOI 4 as a loving exchange between devotees.
This page takes the same verse into the ashram, where the stakes are higher. A resident wears the dress of
renunciation; if his private life differs from it, the Bhagavatam calls him a pretender (*mithyacara*, BG 3.6) and a
"renegade" to be rejected or re-taught (SB 7.15.38–39), and Lord Caitanya would not see the face of such a person
(CC Antya 2.117). The protection is not fear but honesty: with one's authority, a senior devotee and oneself.

## C. Central message

A resident's dress, title and schedule are public; his mind is not. The Bhagavatam condemns the one whose private life
contradicts his public vows, and Lord Caitanya enforced it without compromise. The Vaisnava protection is to have no
second life: to reveal the mind in confidence to an experienced devotee before a weakness becomes a secret.

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Explain *guhyam akhyati prcchati* (NOI 4) as Prabhupada's purport explains it: "An experienced devotee explains, and an inexperienced devotee learns from him."
2. State what SB 7.15.36–39 say about a renunciant who returns to what he renounced, in the text's own words (*vantasi*; "the lowest renegade"), and the two responses the text allows (reject, or teach with compassion).
3. Tell the Chota Haridasa narration exactly as CC Antya 2 tells it, and give the lesson the text itself states (2.143–144, 2.168).
4. Name the three parts of truthful speech in BG 17.15 and the meaning of *satyam* in BG 16.1–3's purport ("not distort the truth for some personal interest").
5. Identify one matter they have never told their authority, and plan the conversation (privately).

## E. Scriptural foundation

**NOI 4** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/4/

- **Purport point:** "(3) opening one's mind to the devotees, (4) inquiring from them about the confidential service of the Lord… An experienced devotee explains, and an inexperienced devotee learns from him. This is guhyam ākhyāti pṛcchati."

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** Renunciation is a public vow; the mind is private. When the two separate, the Gita's word is
   *mithyacara*, pretender, "the greatest cheater, even though he sometimes speaks of philosophy" (BG 3.6 purport). The
   Bhagavatam's words for the renunciant who goes back are harsher still: *vantasi*, one who eats his own vomit; "the
   lowest renegade" (SB 7.15.36, 38–39). These words are read as they are. They describe a condition, and the same
   verses say what to do about it.
2. **Why the double life forms.** Not usually by a decision. A small weakness is hidden to protect one's standing
   (*pratistha*, trd-15); the hiding requires a little diplomacy (*kutinati*, CC Madhya 19.159); the diplomacy makes the
   mind "always suspicious" (19.159 purport); and the gap widens. Kali's method is exactly this: "flourishing by deceit"
   (SB 1.17.25).
3. **The protection.** NOI 4 is usually taught as friendship. In the ashram it is also safety: "an experienced devotee
   explains, and an inexperienced devotee learns from him." The resident who reveals his mind *before* a weakness becomes
   a secret has no second life to maintain. SB 7.15.38–39 give the authority two responses, rejection or compassionate
   teaching; honesty makes the second one possible, and makes it possible early.
4. **Truthfulness is more than not lying.** *Satyam* in BG 16.1–3's purport is "not distort[ing] the truth for some
   personal interest": selective reports, favourable framing and silence count. BG 17.15 gives the manner: truthful,
   pleasing, beneficial, not agitating. And *apaisunam* (BG 16.1–3) balances it: this page is about one's own honesty,
   not about exposing others; "to call a thief a thief is not faultfinding," but neither is it this page's subject.
5. **What the Lord's conduct shows.** With Sanatana, complete openness was met with correction *and* healing (CC Antya
   4). With Chota Haridasa, a renunciant who begged rice from a woman and spoke intimately with her was excluded for life
   (CC Antya 2.113–117), and the text says why: "to reestablish the principles of religion" (2.143), so that "they all
   stopped talking with women, even in dreams" (2.144). The chapter also records that the devotees concluded he
   "must have ultimately attained shelter at the lotus feet of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu" (2.166). Both the severity and
   the mercy are in the text; neither is added or removed.
6. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *"Revealing my mind means telling everyone everything."* No: in confidence, to an experienced devotee (NOI 4).
   - *"If I confess, I will be thrown out."* SB 7.15.38–39 allow compassionate teaching, and Prabhupada's purport asks
     for it where there is strength. The hidden fault, discovered, leaves less room.
   - *"Honesty means harshness."* BG 17.15: truthful *and* pleasing *and* beneficial.
   - *"This topic is about catching others."* It is about having no second life oneself (*apaisunam*).

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet the residents. Say: "Everyone here lives a public life. People see our dress, our schedule and our seat in
   class. Nobody sees our mind. Today is about keeping the two the same."
2. Say: "Nothing today is said to shame anyone. Nothing private will be asked aloud. The scriptures today use very
   strong words, and we will read them as they are."
3. Write on the board: *guhyam akhyati prcchati*, "revealing one's mind in confidence, inquiring confidentially" (NOI 4).
4. Ask H.2 question 1 for private writing (two minutes).
5. Ask question 2 aloud; take two or three answers; thank each by name; begin with BG 3.6.

**H.2 Opening questions**
1. (Private.) Is there anything about my inner life that my authority would be surprised to learn? Write yes or no,
   and nothing more.
2. Why do people hide small faults rather than large ones? (Expected: small ones seem not worth the embarrassment; they
   grow.)
3. Whom in this temple could you tell anything? If no one comes to mind, what would it take?

## I. Core teaching points

1. **The double life begins in the mind.** BG 3.6: restraining the hands while the mind dwells on sense objects is
   pretence; "the greatest cheater, even though he sometimes speaks of philosophy." *Example:* a resident who gives a
   class on sense control that evening and scrolls for an hour that night. *Transition:* what does the Bhagavatam call
   the renunciant who goes back?
2. **The Bhagavatam's words, as they are.** SB 7.15.36: *vantasi*; 7.15.37: "the greatest rascals"; 7.15.38–39: "the
   lowest renegade," and the two responses: reject, or "taking compassion upon him, teach him." *Transition:* which
   response one receives depends largely on honesty.
3. **Truthfulness is the last leg, and deceit is Kali's tool.** SB 1.17.24–25. *Satyam*: not distorting truth for
   personal interest (BG 16.1–3 purport); diplomacy is a weed and makes the mind "always suspicious" (CC Madhya 19.159
   purport). *Transition:* how does a Vaisnava stay truthful?
4. **Reveal the mind in confidence, early.** NOI 4: the inexperienced devotee tells, the experienced one explains. The
   Lord is "the benefactor of the truthful devotee" (SB 1.2.17). *Example:* Sanatana told the Lord everything,
   including who had advised him (J.1). *Transition:* and the Lord's standard for a renunciant.
5. **The Lord's standard, shown once for all.** Chota Haridasa (J.2). The chapter's own lesson: 2.143–144, 2.168.
   *Transition:* how, then, to speak a hard truth: BG 17.15.

## J. Stories and illustrations

**J.1 Sanatana Gosvami hides nothing (CC Antya 4.136–170).** Sanatana, distressed that the Lord embraced his diseased
body, "submitted to Jagadānanda Paṇḍita the cause of his distress" (4.136). Jagadananda advised him to return to
Vrndavana after Ratha-yatra (4.141). When the Lord asked, Sanatana told Him plainly: "I have consulted Jagadānanda
Paṇḍita for his opinion, and he has also advised me to return to Vṛndāvana" (4.156). The Lord chastised Jagadananda
(4.157–160), then told Sanatana that He was never inconvenienced by his body, embraced him again, and his disease
left him (chapter summary; 4.166–170 for the Lord's words to Sanatana). *Connection:* Sanatana's complete openness,
even where it exposed his adviser, brought correction to one and healing to the other. *Use:* fifteen minutes.

**J.2 Chota Haridasa (CC Antya 2.101–168).** Told as the chapter tells it. Bhagavan Acarya, preparing to feed the
Lord, sent Chota (Junior) Haridasa, who "used to sing for Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu," to beg fine rice from
Madhavi-devi, "an elderly lady who always performed austerities… very advanced in devotional service," whom the Lord
counted among His three and a half intimate devotees (2.102–106). The Lord ate, praised the rice, asked who had begged
it, and on returning home ordered Govinda: "From this day forward, do not allow Choṭa Haridāsa to come here" (2.113).
Haridasa fasted three days. When Svarupa Damodara and others asked, the Lord said: "I cannot tolerate seeing the face
of a person who has accepted the renounced order of life but who still talks intimately with a woman" (2.117); "a
wooden statue of a woman attracts the mind of even a great saintly person" (2.118); "There are many persons with
little in their possession who accept the renounced order of life like monkeys" (2.120). The devotees appealed:
"Haridāsa has committed a small offense" (2.123); the Lord replied, "My mind is not under My control. It does not like
to see anyone in the renounced order who talks intimately with women… If you speak this way again, I shall go away"
(2.124–125). Paramananda Puri pleaded; the Lord prepared to leave for Alalanatha rather than yield (2.129–134).
Svarupa Damodara told Haridasa the Lord "will surely be merciful, for at heart He is very kind" (2.139). A year
passed "but still there was not a sign of Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu's mercy toward him" (2.145). Then Haridasa, "after
offering Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu his respectful obeisances, departed for Prayāga without saying anything to anyone"
(2.146), "entered deep into the water at Triveṇī… and in this way gave up his life" (2.147). "Immediately after
committing suicide in this way, he went in his spiritual body to Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu and received the mercy of the
Lord. However, he still remained invisible" (2.148); he sang at night for the Lord (2.149). When devotees asked where
he was, the Lord said, "A person is sure to achieve the results of his fruitive activities" (2.163), and, hearing the
details, "smiled in a pleased mood and said, 'If with sensual intentions one looks at women, this is the only process
of atonement'" (2.165). The devotees concluded he "must have ultimately attained shelter at the lotus feet of Śrī
Caitanya Mahāprabhu" (2.166).
- *The chapter's own lesson (not ours):* "When He chastises His dear devotees, He certainly does so to reestablish the
  principles of religion or duty" (2.143). "After all the devotees saw this example, a mentality of fear grew among
  them. Therefore they all stopped talking with women, even in dreams" (2.144). "This incident manifests the mercy of
  Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, His teaching that a sannyāsī should remain in the renounced order, and the deep attachment
  to Him felt by His faithful devotees" (2.168).
- *Application (preacher's), stated separately from the narration:* the Lord's standard for a renunciant admits no
  private exception, and the text says this was mercy. *Safety note (preacher's, outside the narration):* Haridasa's
  death is narrated, not recommended; a resident in despair over a fault speaks to his authority the same day, and if
  he has thoughts of ending his life he also calls Tele-MANAS 14416 (India) or the local emergency number. The text's
  own hope is in 2.139: "He will surely be merciful."
- *Use:* twenty-five minutes; every detail as in the verses; the chapter's conclusions in its own words.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"If I tell my authority a weakness, won't it end my service?"**
   - SB 7.15.38–39 give the authority two responses, and the purport asks for compassion and teaching where there is
     strength. Honesty early makes that possible; a secret discovered late leaves less room. Sanatana's openness brought
     healing (J.1).
2. **"The Chota Haridasa story seems too severe for a small fault. Should we teach it?"**
   - Yes, as the text tells it, including the devotees' own words "a small offense" (2.123) and the Lord's reply (2.124).
     The chapter itself states the purpose (2.143–144, 2.168). We add no softening and no extra severity.
3. **"Isn't a report about another resident's fault also honesty?"**
   - This page is about one's own life. *Apaisunam* forbids finding fault "unnecessarily"; "to call a thief a thief is
     not faultfinding" (BG 16.1–3 purport). Where another's conduct endangers the mission or a person, tell the
     authority, truthfully and without agitation (BG 17.15), and nothing to anyone else. Conflicts between peers:
     trd-19.
4. **"My authority is very busy. Whom do I open my mind to?"**
   - NOI 4 says "an experienced devotee"; arrange with your authority who that person is for you. What matters is
     that someone responsible knows (SB 1.7.49 purport: "actions should be taken with full knowledge").
5. **"I have hidden something for years. Is it too late?"**
   - The Lord is "the benefactor of the truthful devotee" (SB 1.2.17). Speak this week; the length of the silence is a
     reason to end it, not to continue it.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real people.*

**Case 1: The phone.** Gauranga, four years in the ashram, leads the evening programme. For two months he has been
watching films on his phone late at night. His sadhana sheet shows full rounds. He tells himself it will stop by itself.
- **Dilemma:** Nothing is "broken" outwardly. What is his position in the texts, and what should he do this week?
- **Principles:** BG 3.6 (the pretender's mind); SB 7.15.38–39 (the two responses); NOI 4 (reveal the mind, early);
  CC Madhya 19.159 purport (diplomacy makes the mind suspicious).
- **Questions:** What is the double life here, exactly? Why does the sadhana sheet make it worse rather than better?
  To whom, and in which words (BG 17.15), should he speak?
- **Analysis:** The sheet is true in rounds and false in spirit: "distorting the truth for some personal interest"
  (BG 16.1–3 purport). Speaking now invites the compassionate response; waiting invites the other.
- **Takeaway:** The first truthful sentence to an authority is the end of the double life, whatever follows.

**Case 2: The pleasant report.** Keshava, a department head, reports monthly to the temple president. He mentions
successes fully and problems "later, when they're solved."
- **Principles and analysis:** *Satyam* means not distorting for personal interest (BG 16.1–3 purport); SB 1.7.49
  purport: "actions should be taken with full knowledge," which his authority cannot have. BG 17.15 gives the way to
  report a problem: truthful, beneficial, not agitating. *Takeaway:* leaving out is a form of the double life.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first; thank each answer)
1. What does *guhyam akhyati prcchati* mean? *Expected:* revealing one's mind in confidence and inquiring confidentially (NOI 4).
2. In BG 3.6, who is called a pretender? *Expected:* one who restrains the senses outwardly while the mind dwells on sense objects.
3. What two responses do SB 7.15.38–39 allow toward a pretender? *Expected:* reject him from any position, or with compassion teach him to resume his position.
4. What does *satyam* mean in the purport to BG 16.1–3? *Expected:* not to distort the truth for some personal interest.
5. In the chapter's own words, why did the Lord chastise Chota Haridasa? *Expected:* "to reestablish the principles of religion or duty" (CC Antya 2.143); so that the devotees "stopped talking with women, even in dreams" (2.144).
6. What are the four marks of truthful speech in BG 17.15? *Expected:* truthful, pleasing, beneficial, not agitating.

**M.2 Activities**
- **Option 1: The one conversation (10 min, private).** Each resident writes: the name of the devotee he will speak to,
  the day this week, and the first sentence he will say (checked against BG 17.15). The paper is kept, not shared.
- **Option 2: Hard truth, right manner (20 min).** Pairs take Case L.2 and draft Keshava's three-sentence report of a
  real problem using BG 17.15's four marks; two pairs read theirs aloud; the room says which mark each sentence meets.

**M.3 Reflection**
- Why does the Lord's standard for the renounced order admit no private exception (CC Antya 2.117, 2.124)? What does
  that say about how I treat my own "small" exceptions?

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

A temple resident's dress, title and schedule are public; his mind is private. The Gita calls the person who restrains
his hands while his mind "dwells on sense objects" a pretender, and Prabhupada's purport calls him "the greatest
cheater, even though he sometimes speaks of philosophy" (BG 3.6). The Bhagavatam uses its strongest words for a
renunciant who returns to what he gave up: *vantasi*, "one who eats his own vomit" (SB 7.15.36); "the greatest
rascals" (7.15.37); "the lowest renegade" (7.15.38–39). The same verses say what should be done with such a person:
"either reject him from any position, or taking compassion upon him, teach him, if possible, to resume his original
position."

Such a double life forms slowly: a weakness is hidden to protect one's standing; hiding needs a little diplomacy; and
diplomacy, Prabhupada writes, makes the mind "always suspicious," so "our dealings should always be straightforward"
(CC Madhya 19.159 purport). In this age truthfulness is the one remaining leg of religion, and Kali flourishes "by
deceit" (SB 1.17.24–25). *Satyam* means "not distort[ing] the truth for some personal interest" (BG 16.1–3 purport),
and truthful speech is "truthful, pleasing, beneficial, and not agitating" (BG 17.15).

The Vaisnava protection is in NOI 4: "revealing one's mind in confidence, inquiring confidentially." "An experienced
devotee explains, and an inexperienced devotee learns from him." Sanatana Gosvami told Lord Caitanya everything,
including who had advised him, and received correction for the adviser and healing for himself (CC Antya 4.136–170).
Chota Haridasa, a renunciant who begged rice from a woman and spoke intimately with her, was excluded by the Lord for
life; a year later he gave up his life at Prayaga, and the chapter says the Lord acted "to reestablish the principles
of religion" and that the devotees concluded he attained the Lord's shelter (CC Antya 2.113–168). The lesson the
session drew: have no second life; tell an experienced devotee early.

## S2. Key ideas

- Outward restraint with an inward second life is pretence, "the greatest cheater" (BG 3.6 and purport).
- The Bhagavatam condemns the renunciant who goes back in the strongest words, and allows two responses: rejection or compassionate teaching (SB 7.15.36–39).
- Deceit is Kali's weapon; truthfulness is the last leg of religion (SB 1.17.24–25). *Satyam*: no distortion for personal interest (BG 16.1–3 purport).
- Protection: reveal the mind in confidence to an experienced devotee, early (NOI 4); speak truthfully, pleasingly, beneficially, without agitation (BG 17.15).
- Lord Caitanya's standard for the renounced order admitted no private exception (CC Antya 2.117, 2.143–144).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **NOI 4:** "revealing one's mind in confidence, inquiring confidentially." *For me:* one devotee should know my mind.
- **BG 3.6:** one "whose mind dwells on sense objects… is called a pretender." *For me:* the double life starts inside.
- **SB 7.15.38–39:** "one should either reject him from any position, or taking compassion upon him, teach him." *For me:* honesty early invites the second response.
- **BG 17.15:** speech "truthful, pleasing, beneficial, and not agitating." *For me:* how to say a hard thing.

## S4. Words to know

- **Guhyam akhyati prcchati:** revealing one's mind in confidence and inquiring confidentially (NOI 4).
- **Mithyacara:** pretender; outward restraint with the mind on sense objects (BG 3.6).
- **Vantasi:** "one who eats his own vomit": a renunciant who returns to what he renounced (SB 7.15.36).
- **Kutinati:** diplomatic or duplicitous behaviour, a weed beside devotion (CC Madhya 19.159).
- **Satyam:** truthfulness; not distorting the truth for personal interest (BG 16.1–3 purport).

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **If I tell my authority a weakness, will I lose my service?** The texts allow rejection or compassionate teaching (SB 7.15.38–39), and Prabhupada asks for compassion where there is strength. Telling early makes the second possible.
2. **Was Chota Haridasa's fault not "small"?** The devotees said so (CC Antya 2.123); the Lord replied that His mind "does not like to see anyone in the renounced order who talks intimately with women" (2.124). The chapter says the purpose was to re-establish religious principles (2.143).
3. **Should I report other residents' faults?** This topic is about one's own honesty. "To call a thief a thief is not faultfinding," but faultfinding "unnecessarily" is forbidden (BG 16.1–3 purport). Where someone is endangered, tell the authority only, in the manner of BG 17.15.
4. **I have kept something hidden for years. Is it too late?** No. Krishna is "the benefactor of the truthful devotee" (SB 1.2.17). Speak this week.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. Who is a "pretender" according to BG 3.6?
2. What word does SB 7.15.36 use for a renunciant who returns to materialistic life, and what does it mean?
3. What two responses do SB 7.15.38–39 allow toward such a person?
4. According to SB 1.17.25, which leg of religion remains in Kali-yuga, and by what does Kali flourish?
5. What does *satyam* mean in the purport to BG 16.1–3?
6. In its own words, why does CC Antya 2.143 say the Lord chastised Chota Haridasa?

**Answers:** 1. One who restrains the senses of action while the mind dwells on sense objects (BG 3.6). 2. *Vantasi*,
"one who eats his own vomit" (SB 7.15.36). 3. Reject him from any position, or with compassion teach him to resume his
original position (SB 7.15.38–39). 4. Truthfulness; "by deceit" (SB 1.17.25). 5. Not to distort the truth for some
personal interest (BG 16.1–3 purport). 6. "To reestablish the principles of religion or duty" (CC Antya 2.143).
