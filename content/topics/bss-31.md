# 35. Dealings with Women Devotees

> BSS (brahmacari training) · 120 min · drafted · SENSITIVE (celibacy and relations between men and women; no private disclosure). Follows BIKC chapter 35. Builds on bss-03 (Chota Haridasa) and bss-16 (the eight aspects of celibacy).

| | |
|---|---|

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level
A brahmacari lives and serves in a temple community where women devotees are fellow servants. Srila Prabhupada did not turn
women away, and he also warned the brahmacaris to be steady and careful. The IYF motive for BSS is "following the principles
of brahmachari life" (research/batch-criteria.md, section 6). This page gives both sides: respect for women devotees, and the brahmacari's own protection.

## C. Central message
Women devotees are devotees, and every devotee deserves respect and a chance to serve Krishna. A brahmacari protects his vow by simple habits: respectful distance, service-only conversation, no private meetings, and honest reporting to his mentor. In SB 11.26.17 King Pururava says, "How can I blame her for my trouble when I myself am ignorant of my real, spiritual nature?"

## D. Learning outcomes
By the end, participants can:
1. Explain, from BG 9.32 and its purport, why women devotees are equally eligible for the highest destination, and why Srila Prabhupada would not turn them away.
2. Explain the point of SB 7.12.7, 7.12.9, 7.12.10 and 9.19.17: the senses are strong, even in advanced persons, so rules protect the one who is still conditioned.
3. State what SB 3.31.42's purport says: "not only is woman the gateway to hell for man, but man is also the gateway to hell for woman. It is a question of attachment."
4. List five practical habits of respectful conduct (section I.4).
5. Say where to go for help: a mentor, the temple authority, a counsellor, Tele-MANAS 14416.

## E. Scriptural foundation
Use BG 9.32, SB 7.12.9, SB 9.19.17 and SB 11.26.17 in the talk. Use SB 7.12.7 and 7.12.10 as support. SB 3.31.40, 3.31.42, 11.26.12 and CC Antya 2.117 are for questions and for I.2 and I.4. All verses and purports were read on Vedabase. SB 11.26 is Canto 11, so the translation is the BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples.

**BG 9.32**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/9/32/
> "…can attain the supreme destination."
- **Context:** Krishna says that those who take shelter of Him, whatever their birth, including women, merchants and workers, reach the supreme destination.
- **Purport point:** The purport says clearly that in devotional service there is no distinction between lower and higher classes of people; everyone is eligible, and the guidance of a pure devotee can purify anyone who takes complete shelter of Krishna.
- **Application (preacher's):** This is the starting point of the whole page. A woman devotee is a soul who has taken shelter of Krishna. A brahmacari who looks down on her, or who is rude, contradicts this verse.
- **Use:** in the talk (I.1).

**BG 5.18**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/5/18/
- **Purport point:** The purport says a Krishna conscious person makes no distinction of species or caste. All bodies are different material productions, but the soul and the Supersoul in each are of the same spiritual quality. SB 7.12.10's purport quotes this verse to say that equal vision is the test of self-realization.
- **Use:** background; for the point that until we have this vision, rules protect us.

**SB 7.12.7**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/12/7/
> "A brahmacārī, or one who has not accepted the gṛhastha-āśrama [family life], must rigidly avoid talking with women or about women, for the senses are so powerful that they may agitate even the mind of a sannyāsī, a member of the renounced order of life."
- **Context:** Narada instructs Yudhisthira on the life of a brahmacari in the gurukula.
- **Purport point:** The purport says brahmacarya means the vow not to marry but to observe strict celibacy, and: "A brahmacārī or sannyāsī should avoid talking with women or reading literature concerning talks between man and woman. The injunction restricting association with women is the basic principle of spiritual life. Associating or talking with women is never advised in any of the Vedic literatures."
- **Application (preacher's):** The reason the verse gives is the power of the senses. Say this in the lecture.
- **Use:** in the talk, in support (I.2).

**SB 7.12.9**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/12/9/
> "Woman is compared to fire, and man is compared to a butter pot. Therefore a man should avoid associating even with his own daughter in a secluded place. Similarly, he should also avoid association with other women. One should associate with women only for important business and not otherwise."
- **Purport point:** The purport explains the picture: when butter and fire are kept together, the butter melts. "However advanced one may be in restraining the senses, it is almost impossible for a man to keep himself controlled in the presence of a woman, even if she is his own daughter, mother or sister. Indeed, his mind is agitated even if one is in the renounced order of life. Therefore, Vedic civilization carefully restricts mingling between men and women. If one cannot understand the basic principle of restraining association between man and woman, he is to be considered an animal. That is the purport of this verse."
- **Application (preacher's):** Explain the image in the purport's words: butter kept near fire melts, so the man keeps away from the heat.
- **Use:** in the talk (I.2). BIKC's "fire and butter principle" is this verse.

**SB 7.12.10**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/12/10/
> "…there is every chance that he will fall down because his intelligence is bewildered."
- **Purport point:** The purport says that until a person is fully self-realized and free from identifying with the body, the duality of man and woman continues, so one should be extremely careful. It quotes BG 5.18 for the vision of a person who is self-realized, and it says no one should think himself perfect and forget the scriptural caution.
- **Use:** in the talk (I.2); it explains why the rule is for everyone still in the conditioned stage.

**SB 9.19.17**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/9/19/17/
> "One should not allow oneself to sit on the same seat even with one's own mother, sister or daughter, for the senses are so strong that even though one is very advanced in knowledge, he may be attracted by sex."
- **Purport point:** "Learning the etiquette of how to deal with women does not free one from sexual attraction." Such attraction "is possible even with one's mother, sister or daughter"; "This is a psychological fact." "Therefore, one should be extremely careful in dealings with women." Lord Caitanya "was most strict in such dealings, especially after He accepted the sannyāsa order. Indeed, no woman could come near Him to offer Him respect." "A brahmacārī is forbidden even to see the wife of his spiritual master if she happens to be young."
- **Use:** in the talk (I.2). This verse is also used in bss-11; here it is used only for the practical rule of caution.

**SB 3.31.40**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/31/40/
> "The woman, created by the Lord, is the representation of māyā, and one who associates with such māyā by accepting services must certainly know that this is the way of death, just like a blind well covered with grass."
- **Context:** Lord Kapila teaches His mother Devahuti about the conditioned soul's entanglement.
- **Purport point:** "Similarly, association with a woman begins when one accepts service from her, because woman is especially created by the Lord to give service to man. By accepting her service, a man is entrapped. If he is not intelligent enough to know that she is the gateway to hellish life, he may indulge in her association very liberally. This is restricted for those who aspire to ascend to the transcendental platform." The purport describes the custom of fifty years ago in Hindu society: a wife could not see her husband during the daytime, and houses had internal quarters for the woman and external quarters for the man. It ends: "Acceptance of service rendered by a woman may appear very pleasing, but one should be very cautious in accepting such service because it is clearly said that woman is the gateway to death, or forgetfulness of one's self. She blocks the path of spiritual realization."
- **Application (preacher's):** BIKC (ch. 35) draws its rule from this verse: "A brahmacārī should be very cautious if a woman is being 'too nice' to him (e.g. keeps giving him mahā-prasāda). Service by a woman is a trap for a man. (SB 3.31.40)"
- **Use:** in the talk (I.4).

**SB 3.31.42**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/31/42/
- **Purport point:** The purport says Lord Kapila's teaching shows that not only is a woman the gateway to hell for a man, but a man is also the gateway to hell for a woman. It says this is a question of attachment: as long as either is attached to the other for material enjoyment, each is dangerous to the other. If the attachment is transferred to Krishna, both become Krishna conscious.
- **Application (preacher's):** Read the purport's own sentences: "As long as either is attached to the other for such material enjoyment, the woman is dangerous for the man, and the man is also dangerous for the woman. But if the attachment is transferred to Kṛṣṇa, both of them become Kṛṣṇa conscious, and then marriage is very nice."
- **Use:** in the talk (I.1).

**SB 11.26.17**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/26/17/
> "How can I blame her for my trouble when I myself am ignorant of my real, spiritual nature? I did not control my senses, and so I am like a person who mistakenly sees a harmless rope as a snake."
- **Context:** King Pururava, who lost his wife Urvasi, laments. The full story is told in bss-32.
- **Purport point:** The purport says the king frankly admits that Urvasi is not to blame. He had mistakenly thought the Lord's material energy exists for his own enjoyment, and so he suffered. It says he himself was the offender for trying to exploit her external form.
- **Application (preacher's):** The purport's sentence: "Purūravā himself was the offender for trying to exploit the external form of Urvaśī."
- **Use:** in the talk (I.1).

**CC Antya 2.117**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/2/117/
> "I cannot tolerate seeing the face of a person who has accepted the renounced order of life but who still talks intimately with a woman."
- **Context and use:** The Lord's words to Chota Haridasa's case. The story is told in full in bss-03; here it is one paragraph (J.1). The purport quotes Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura: simplicity is the first qualification of a Vaishnava, and duplicity is a great offence; so the lesson is non-duplicity as well as caution.

**SB 9.23.7-10**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/9/23/7-10/
> "…after being brought from the forest by the allurement of prostitutes, who danced, staged theatrical performances accompanied by music, and embraced and worshiped him."
- **Teaching:** The text says Rsyasrnga, who had grown up in the forest, was appointed priest for a sacrifice when the demigods did not send rain. He was brought out by the allurement of prostitutes, and rain fell after he came. He married Santa, whom King Romapada had accepted as his daughter, and he performed a sacrifice that gave King Dasaratha sons.
- **Use:** in J.2, one sentence; the story is told in full on bss-51. No purport is printed on Vedabase for this passage.

**SB 11.26.12**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/26/12/
> "What is the use of a big education or the practice of austerities and renunciation, and what is the use of studying religious scriptures, of living in solitude and silence, if, after all that, one's mind is stolen by a woman?"
- **Context:** King Pururava's song, in the same chapter as 11.26.17.
- **Purport point:** "All of the above-mentioned processes are useless if one's heart and mind are stolen by an insignificant woman. One who hankers after a woman's association certainly spoils his spiritual progress." The purport adds Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura's statement that one who worships the example of the liberated gopis of Vrindavan, who accepted Lord Krishna as their paramour, can free his mental activities from the contamination of lust.
- **Use:** in the talk (I.2), with SB 9.19.17.

## F. Philosophical explanation
**Principle.** In Srila Prabhupada's purports the cause of the danger is attachment in a conditioned person, and the remedy is Krishna consciousness. BG 9.32 says that women who take shelter of Krishna attain the supreme destination; SB 3.31.42's purport says "the woman is dangerous for the man, and the man is also dangerous for the woman" as long as there is attachment. SB 7.12.7, 7.12.9, 7.12.10, 9.19.17 and 11.26.12 restrict association and say why: the senses are strong even in the advanced, and a mind stolen by a woman spoils everything else.
**BIKC's guidance, in summary (BIKC ch. 35; see O).**
1. *Respect, from a distance.* BIKC (ch. 35) says: "Women in ISKCON are all devotees and therefore glorious. Some of them are clearly advanced from their previous lives. They should be regarded with all due respect—from a distance. If they are serious devotees, they will respect your strictness."
2. *Patience.* BIKC says: "Due to lack of training or attachment to women's lib ideas, women devotees may sometimes act improperly with you. Better let it pass, and not make a scene out of it." And: "We men also may still be influenced by the lusty exploitative mentality in our dealings with women. So we may also be at fault. If it becomes necessary, have a word with the temple authorities."
3. *Srila Prabhupada's example.* BIKC reports that when a devotee suggested not accepting women as full-time devotees, Srila Prabhupada replied that they had come to take shelter of Krishna and could not be turned away (reported by Danavira Gosvami). It quotes his letter of 29 Sep 1975: "If our male members, the brahmacārīs and the sannyāsīs, become steady in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, there is no problem. It is the duty of the male members to be very steady and cautious. This can be done by regular chanting like Haridāsa Ṭhākura did. Whenever there is a young woman, we should remember Haridāsa Ṭhākura and beg his mercy to protect us, and we should think that these beautiful gopīs are meant for the enjoyment of Kṛṣṇa. It is a dilemma for our society that we cannot deny these girls, and at the same time they are a great dangerous allurement to the young boys." It also reports that when told some brahmacaris felt disturbed by women in the temple, he "sarcastically suggested that the brahmacārīs go to the forest" (letter, 3 Dec 1972). It also reports that in 1967 he said not to see women devotees as objects of sense gratification but as associates of Krishna, and that in Mayapur in 1976 he said men should address women as "my dear mother" and women should see the men as "my dear son". It reports a Seattle conversation in 1968: "Girls should not be taken as inferior. Sometimes, of course, in scripture we say that woman is the cause of bondage. So, that should not be aggravated that women are inferior. The girls who come, we should treat them nicely." (These are BIKC's reports and quotations of letters and recollections. *Requires source review* against the originals before quoting them as Srila Prabhupada's words.)
4. *Practical caution, in BIKC's words.* "For a brahmacārī, young women mean trouble. However sincere young women devotees may be, when in contact with brahmacārīs a kind of energy is produced that is not conducive for devotional advancement. Those who have regular contact with young women devotees, even innocently or for the sake of service, are almost certain to get worn down. They may not even notice the effect of such association, but nevertheless it is like radioactivity: slow, subtle, and irreversible." "Even though brahmacārīs may preach to anyone, it is better that women preach to women." "If a brahmacārī who wants to stay a brahmacārī finds that a young woman is becoming friendly towards him, alarm bells should go off in his head and he should extricate himself from that situation. If that is not possible, the next best response is not to respond. To remain polite but cold in the face of advances, and to consistently show disinterest, soon convinces women to direct their conjugal aspirations elsewhere. There is no room for sentiment in such dealings." If she persists, BIKC gives the words: "Mātājī, I already gave many lifetimes to many women like yourself. Please give me your blessings that I can give this life fully to Kṛṣṇa, without unnecessary disturbances or entanglements." Where men and women must serve side by side: "Brahmacārīs should maintain Vaiṣṇava respect towards devotee women, without becoming overly familiar or loose, or developing friendships with them."
5. *Care in public speaking (BIKC's own advice).* "There are certain points in Prabhupāda's books concerning women which unless one is a very self-controlled devotee and expert preacher, one should be cautious about repeating in public and in classes, especially if women devotees or guests are present (e.g., quotes stating women to be less intelligent than men, or nine times as lusty, etc.)." Its reason: "Everyone in this age is low born. Men or women, we are all running on Lord Caitanya's and Śrīla Prabhupāda's mercy. We don't want to discourage women who are already devotees, nor those who are potential devotees. Nor do we want to make the brahmacārīs artificially proud. Sensitive topics need to be handled by competent devotees."
**Connections.** bss-16 (celibacy as service); bss-21 to bss-23 (attraction and its results); bss-12 (Haridasa Thakura's steadiness); bss-32 (women who are not devotees).
**Consequences.** BIKC names both failures: "Brahmacārīs often have a tendency to reject women, but Śrīla Prabhupāda never did that because he was above attraction and repulsion and simply wanted to engage everyone in Kṛṣṇa's service"; and the brahmacari in regular contact with young women devotees is "almost certain to get worn down".
**Common misunderstandings.**
1. "Send the women devotees away." BIKC reports Srila Prabhupada's reply to that suggestion: "They have come to take shelter of Kṛṣṇa. We cannot turn them away." SB 11.26.17: Pururava blames himself, not Urvasi.
2. "Respect means friendship." BIKC says respect, but not familiarity.
3. "If a woman devotee seems friendly, she is at fault." BIKC says men may be at fault too, and it advises letting things pass and involving authorities if needed.
4. "Rules are for the weak." SB 9.19.17 says even the advanced can be attracted.
**How to pitch it.** Speak as a senior. Read the verses, purports and BIKC as they are. Put the practice of chanting first, as the letter BIKC quotes does.

## H. Opening and engagement
**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes).**
1. One round of kirtana or a short prayer; say the title.
2. Say: "This is a talk between brothers. Its subject is how we deal with women devotees in service, and how we protect our own vow. Nobody will be asked about private matters."
3. Read BG 9.32 slowly. Say: "This is the base of everything we say today."
**H.2 Opening questions.**
1. "Name a woman devotee whose service or example has inspired you, and say in one sentence what you learned from her." (Take three answers; thank each speaker.)
2. "Why do you think Srila Prabhupada did not turn women away from the movement?"
3. "In your own words, what is the difference between respecting someone and being close friends with them?"

## I. Core teaching points
**I.1 Women devotees are devotees (BG 9.32, SB 3.31.42, SB 11.26.17).** Krishna says that all who take shelter of Him reach the supreme destination, and the purport says there is no distinction in devotional service. The purport to SB 3.31.42 says: "not only is woman the gateway to hell for man, but man is also the gateway to hell for woman. It is a question of attachment." SB 11.26.17: Pururava says, "How can I blame her for my trouble when I myself am ignorant of my real, spiritual nature?" Transition: "So why any caution at all?"
**I.2 The caution protects the conditioned person (SB 7.12.7, 7.12.9, 7.12.10, 9.19.17, 11.26.12).** Read the translations in full. SB 7.12.7: a brahmacari "must rigidly avoid talking with women or about women, for the senses are so powerful that they may agitate even the mind of a sannyāsī". SB 7.12.9: woman is fire and man a butter pot, so "a man should avoid associating even with his own daughter in a secluded place"; read the purport's closing sentences as they are. SB 9.19.17: not even the same seat with one's own mother, sister or daughter, "for the senses are so strong that even though one is very advanced in knowledge, he may be attracted by sex." SB 11.26.12: education, austerity, scripture, solitude and silence are of no use "if, after all that, one's mind is stolen by a woman". The purport to SB 7.12.10 says that no one should consider himself above the rule.
**I.3 Srila Prabhupada's mood (BIKC reports).** He would not turn women away, and he asked the male devotees to be steady and cautious and to chant regularly. He taught them to see women devotees as associates of Krishna and as mothers. The brahmacari's attitude is therefore respect and gratitude, together with firm self-protection.
**I.4 Five practical habits (Application, preacher's, based on BIKC).**
1. Speak to women devotees only when service needs it, in an open place, briefly and politely.
2. Do not meet alone behind closed doors. Choose a place where others can see you.
3. Do not develop personal friendship or exchange personal messages. Keep messages short and about service.
4. If a woman devotee begins to give you much personal attention or many favours, BIKC says "alarm bells should go off in his head"; extricate yourself, or if that is not possible, "remain polite but cold" and "consistently show disinterest". Tell your mentor early.
5. If you feel disturbed in mind, do not hide it. Tell your mentor or a trusted senior, increase your chanting and hearing, and ask the temple authority to adjust your service if needed. Tele-MANAS 14416 (India) or a qualified counsellor can also help. Nobody will be shamed.
If anyone, of any gender, is being harassed or pressed, the temple authority should be told, and the person who reports should not be blamed.

## J. Stories and illustrations
**J.1 Chota Haridasa (CC Antya 2.102-155; key verse 2.117). Builds on bss-03, where it is told in full.** Chota Haridasa, a singer in Lord Caitanya's circle, went to an elderly woman devotee to ask for rice, at the request of Bhagavan Acarya. The Lord heard that a renunciant had spoken with a woman, and said He could not tolerate the sight of such a person (2.117). The full story is told on bss-03. **Devotional lesson:** the Lord is strict with those close to Him because He wishes their devotion to be pure and honest. The lesson for us is to keep our conduct simple and honest, and to depend on His mercy.
**J.2 Rsyasrnga (SB 9.23.7-10). Told in full on bss-51; here one sentence only.** The text says that Rsyasrnga, brought up in the forest, was brought out of it "by the allurement of prostitutes, who danced, staged theatrical performances accompanied by music, and embraced and worshiped him". **Application (preacher's):** the story shows that a celibate's purity has great power, and that being unaware of the world is no protection. It is better to be trained and guarded than to be innocent. **Devotional lesson:** the Lord uses a pure brahmacari's sacrifice for the welfare of many.
**Illustration (preacher's, labelled).** A cook who carries a pot of ghee past an open stove walks carefully. He walks with attention because he wants to bring the ghee to the offering. The brahmacari's care is the same: it is for the offering of his life to Krishna.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections
1. **"Doesn't this treat women as dangerous?"** Give the sources' own words. SB 3.31.42 purport: "As long as either is attached to the other for such material enjoyment, the woman is dangerous for the man, and the man is also dangerous for the woman." SB 11.26.17 purport: "Purūravā himself was the offender." BIKC (ch. 35): "For a brahmacārī, young women mean trouble", and in the same chapter women devotees "should be regarded with all due respect—from a distance."
2. **"Srila Prabhupada said some hard things about women. How do I explain them?"** Read the purport yourself and quote it as it is. BIKC's own advice (F, point 5) is that such points be repeated cautiously in public by one who is self-controlled and an expert preacher, and its reason is that "Everyone in this age is low born" and all run on the mercy of Lord Caitanya and Srila Prabhupada. BIKC also reports Srila Prabhupada's words in Seattle, 1968: "Sometimes, of course, in scripture we say that woman is the cause of bondage. So, that should not be aggravated that women are inferior." (Requires source review for the conversation.)
3. **"What if a woman devotee is persistently friendly?"** BIKC: "alarm bells should go off in his head and he should extricate himself from that situation. If that is not possible, the next best response is not to respond. To remain polite but cold in the face of advances, and to consistently show disinterest, soon convinces women to direct their conjugal aspirations elsewhere." If she persists, BIKC gives the words in F, point 4. Tell your mentor and the temple authority.
4. **"Is it wrong for a woman devotee to hope to marry a good brahmacari?"** No. BIKC reports a recollection (told by Giriraja Swami; *Requires source review*) that Srila Prabhupada said the managers should see that the brahmacaris stay brahmacaris and the women get married, and that this is the managers' concern. BIKC adds: "Naturally, women devotees want to marry the best men devotees", and a brahmacari who wants to stay brahmacari "has to be free from material compassion for women looking for husbands."
5. **"What if I feel strongly disturbed?"** That is not a sin or a reason for shame. Talk to your mentor, increase chanting and hearing, adjust service if needed, and consider a qualified counsellor. Tele-MANAS 14416 (India) or your local crisis service is there if distress is heavy.

## L. Case studies
**L.1 Invented case for discussion.** A brahmacari works with a woman devotee on a festival team. They have to meet often. After some weeks she brings him prasadam every day and asks his opinion about personal matters. *Dilemma:* he wants to be polite, and he sees that he is starting to look forward to the meetings. *Principles:* SB 7.12.9 (association only for important business), SB 3.31.40 (personal service creates closeness), SB 11.26.17 (the work is on his own mind). *Questions:* What does he say to her, kindly? Whom does he tell? How can the team arrange work so that they are not alone? *Analysis:* he tells his mentor, thanks her politely, moves their communication to a group setting, and asks the team leader to adjust duties. *Takeaway:* early honest reporting is a kindness to both.
**L.2 Invented case for discussion (extension).** A brahmacari hears a classmate say that women should not be allowed in the temple. *Principles:* BG 9.32, SB 3.31.42. *Questions:* How can he answer from the sources? *Takeaway:* BIKC reports Srila Prabhupada's reply to the same suggestion: "They have come to take shelter of Kṛṣṇa. We cannot turn them away."

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary
Women devotees are devotees. In Bhagavad-gita 9.32 Krishna says that those who take shelter of Him, including women, can reach the supreme destination, and Srila Prabhupada's purport says that in devotional service there is no distinction between higher and lower people. Srila Prabhupada, as reported in the book *Brahmacarya in Krishna consciousness* (BIKC), did not turn women away from the movement: "They have come to take shelter of Kṛṣṇa. We cannot turn them away." BIKC quotes his letter: "It is the duty of the male members to be very steady and cautious. This can be done by regular chanting like Haridāsa Ṭhākura did."
The scriptures also say that the senses are strong. Srimad-Bhagavatam 7.12.7 says a brahmacari "must rigidly avoid talking with women or about women, for the senses are so powerful that they may agitate even the mind of a sannyāsī". SB 9.19.17 says not to sit on the same seat even with one's own mother, sister or daughter, because even a man advanced in knowledge "may be attracted by sex". SB 7.12.9 compares a woman to fire and a man to a butter pot: butter melts near fire, so "One should associate with women only for important business and not otherwise." SB 11.26.12 says education, austerity, scripture, solitude and silence are of no use "if, after all that, one's mind is stolen by a woman". The purport to SB 3.31.42 says: "not only is woman the gateway to hell for man, but man is also the gateway to hell for woman. It is a question of attachment." In SB 11.26.17 King Pururava says, "How can I blame her for my trouble when I myself am ignorant of my real, spiritual nature?"
So a brahmacari does the following. He speaks to women devotees only for service, in open places, briefly and politely. He does not meet alone behind closed doors and does not make personal friendships. If a woman devotee begins to give him much personal attention, BIKC says "alarm bells should go off in his head"; he stays "polite but cold" and tells his mentor early. If he is disturbed, he chants and hears more, tells a trusted senior, and asks for help; a qualified counsellor or Tele-MANAS 14416 (India) are also available. Nobody is blamed or shamed.

## S2. Key ideas
- Women devotees "should be regarded with all due respect—from a distance" (BIKC).
- "It is a question of attachment": each is dangerous to the other as long as there is attachment (SB 3.31.42 purport).
- The rules restrict association because the senses are strong even in the advanced (SB 7.12.9, 9.19.17).
- Pururava blames himself, not Urvasi (SB 11.26.17).
- Honest, early talk with a mentor and regular chanting are the main protection.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **BG 9.32**: Those who take shelter of Krishna "can attain the supreme destination." *For me:* a woman devotee has taken shelter of Krishna.
- **SB 7.12.9**: "One should associate with women only for important business and not otherwise." *For me:* I keep my contact short and about service.
- **SB 11.26.17**: "How can I blame her for my trouble when I myself am ignorant of my real, spiritual nature?" *For me:* I work on my own mind.

## S4. Words to know
- **Brahmacari:** a celibate student living simply and serving his guru.
- **Mataji:** a respectful way of addressing a woman devotee, meaning "mother".
- **Purport:** Srila Prabhupada's explanation of a verse.
- **Sannyasi:** a person in the renounced order.
- **Mentor:** a senior devotee who guides and cares for you.
- **Temple authority:** the person or group who manages the temple community.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *Does Krishna consciousness treat women as lower?* No. BG 9.32 and its purport say that there is no distinction in devotional service.
2. *Why the cautions?* "The senses are so strong that even though one is very advanced in knowledge, he may be attracted by sex" (SB 9.19.17).
3. *What if a woman devotee is too friendly?* BIKC: extricate yourself; if that is not possible, "remain polite but cold" and "consistently show disinterest". Tell your mentor or the temple authority.
4. *What if I feel disturbed?* Tell a mentor or trusted senior, chant and hear more, and seek a counsellor if needed. Tele-MANAS 14416 (India) or your local crisis service can help. No shame.
