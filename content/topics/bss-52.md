# Movement of Living Entity - SB 3.31.32 to 42

> Status line: BSS (brahmacari training) · 120 min · drafted · sensitive (sexual attraction, strong wording about women in the verses and purports, read as they are; help-seeking guidance in H.1 and K.6).

## A. Topic overview
| | |
|---|---|
| Batch / level | BSS, brahmacari training (Bhakti Siddhanta Sabha). A brahmacari is a celibate student who lives simply, serves his guru and studies scripture. |
| Suggested duration | 120 minutes (study session: verse by verse, with an "if time allows" extension) |

| Difficulty | Intermediate |

| Teaching objective | The audience follows Lord Kapila's own order in SB 3.31: how a soul in the human form is pulled back by attachment and by the company of those absorbed in it (verses 32 to 42), how the soul then moves from body to body (verses 43 to 46), and the way out (verses 47 and 48) |

SB 3.31 is the chapter entitled "Lord Kapila's Instructions on the Movements of the Living Entities". The curriculum title picks out verses 32 to 42. This page goes through those verses and then through the last six verses of the chapter, which complete the thought.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level
In bss-22 and bss-14 the audience met a few verses of Lord Kapila's teaching. This page takes SB 3.31 in its own order. Lord Kapila describes the suffering of the soul in the womb and at birth (verses 1 to 31, which this page does not cover). He then asks what happens when the soul, with a human body again, chooses the company and the attractions that brought it down before. Verses 32 to 42 are some of the strongest words about sexual attraction in the Bhagavatam; they are read as they are, with Srila Prabhupada's purports. Verses 43 to 48 then show the larger picture and the way out.

**Builds on bss-14** (Kapila's teachings in SB 3.25, which are not repeated) **and bss-22** (which quotes SB 3.31.35 for the point about association; here it is read in its chapter). See also bss-12 (Haridasa Thakura and the higher taste, which SB 3.31.38 refers to) and bss-53 (King Puranjana, which describes the same movement as a story).

**Not done here:** the description of the womb and the child's suffering (verses 1 to 31); the philosophy of SB 3.25 (bss-14); the full list of verses on sexual desire (bss-21 to bss-23, bss-39); comments on the modern roles of women or men.

## C. Central message
Lord Kapila says that a person in the human form who goes back to the company and the attractions of sense gratification falls again. He also says that men and women are both bound by attachment to each other, that the attachment is cut by attachment to Krishna, and that a person who sees his true position does not fear death or exaggerate the body's pleasures, but moves about in the world free from attachment, engaged in the Lord's service.

## D. Learning outcomes
By the end, participants can:
1. Explain in their own words what SB 3.31.32 says happens to the soul that returns to the company of the sensually minded.
2. Say what SB 3.31.33 lists as lost, and what SB 3.31.38 gives as the higher attraction.
3. Explain why SB 3.31.41-42 and their purports apply to men and women alike, and what the purport says is the real danger.
4. Describe how SB 3.31.43-46 explains birth, death and movement from one body to another.
5. State the practical advice of SB 3.31.47-48 and choose one step for the coming week.

## E. Scriptural foundation
The verses are taken in order. Use SB 3.31.32, 33, 38, 41, 42, 44, 47 and 48 in the talk. The others are for questions and for the extension.

**SB 3.31.30**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/31/30/
> "By such ignorance the living entity accepts the material body, which is made of five elements, as himself."
- **Context:** Lord Kapila is speaking to His mother Devahuti about the soul that has come out of the womb.
- **Teaching:** Through ignorance the living being takes the body as himself and takes impermanent things as his own.
- **Purport point:** The purport says that the first ignorance is to take the body as the self and the second is to accept something as one's own because of a bodily connection.
- **Use:** background, the root of what follows.

**SB 3.31.31**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/31/31/
> "For the sake of the body, which is a source of constant trouble to him and which follows him because he is bound by ties of ignorance and fruitive activities, he performs various actions which cause him to be subjected to repeated birth and death."
- **Purport point:** The purport says that work not done to satisfy Yajna (Visnu) is a cause of bondage, and that the conditioned soul takes his kinsmen and country as his own through the body.
- **Use:** in the talk, in one sentence.

**SB 3.31.32**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/31/32/
> "If, therefore, the living entity again associates with the path of unrighteousness, influenced by sensually minded people engaged in the pursuit of sexual enjoyment and the gratification of the palate, he again goes to hell as before."
- **Teaching:** If the soul again joins the path of those who pursue sex and the pleasure of the tongue, it again goes to a hellish condition.
- **Purport point:** The purport says that if the person gets another chance in a human body and wastes it in the association of those concerned with sex life and palatable dishes, he glides down again. It says a person who wants to advance must be very careful about such association, and should mix with saintly persons. It also says that a person can be officially very religious and still slide down if he keeps this association.
- **Application (preacher's):** The verse is about whom we choose to spend our time with and listen to.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 3.31.33**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/31/33/
> "He becomes devoid of truthfulness, cleanliness, mercy, gravity, spiritual intelligence, shyness, austerity, fame, forgiveness, control of the mind, control of the senses, fortune and all such opportunities."
- **Teaching:** The verse lists what a person loses: truthfulness, cleanliness, mercy, seriousness, spiritual intelligence, modesty, austerity, fame, forgiveness, control of mind and senses, fortune.
- **Purport point:** The purport says that those too addicted to sex life cannot understand the purpose of the Absolute Truth, and that the ultimate goal is Krishna.
- **Use:** in the talk, as the list of what is at stake.

**SB 3.31.34**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/31/34/
> "One should not associate with a coarse fool who is bereft of the knowledge of self-realization and who is no more than a dancing dog in the hands of a woman."
- **Purport point:** The purport says that this restriction "is especially meant for those who are in the line of advancement in Kṛṣṇa consciousness", and that the qualities listed in the previous verse are to appear with progress, "but if one associates with a śūdra, a foolish person who is like a dancing dog in the hands of a woman, then he cannot make any progress. Lord Caitanya has advised that any person who is engaged in Kṛṣṇa consciousness and who desires to pass beyond material nescience must not associate himself with women or with persons interested in material enjoyment. For a person seeking advancement in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, such association is more dangerous than suicide."
- **Use:** in the talk (I.2), read in full.

**SB 3.31.35**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/31/35/
- **Use:** background only. The verse and its purport are used in bss-22 and are not repeated here.

**SB 3.31.36**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/31/36/
> "…Brahmā was bewildered by her charms…"
- **Purport point:** The purport says that even great demigods like Brahma and Siva are captivated, so ordinary souls should be careful, and that it is best to practise controlling the senses by bhakti-yoga, engaging in the service of Madana-mohana, Krishna, who can subdue Cupid. It says that otherwise attempts to control the senses will fail.
- **Purport point (continued):** "Therefore, everyone is advised that one should not freely mix even with one's daughter, mother or sister because the senses are so strong that when one becomes infatuated the senses do not consider the relationship of daughter, mother or sister."
- **Use:** in the talk. The story of Brahma is in SB 3.12 and is told on bss-39.

**SB 3.31.37**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/31/37/
> "Amongst all kinds of living entities begotten by Brahmā, namely men, demigods and animals, none but the sage Nārāyaṇa is immune to the attraction of māyā in the form of woman."
- **Purport point:** "Throughout the entire material world, beginning from Brahmā down to the small, insignificant creatures like the ant, everyone is attracted by sex life. That is the basic principle of this material world. Lord Brahmā's being attracted by his daughter is the vivid example that no one is exempt from sexual attraction to woman. Therefore woman, is the wonderful creation of māyā to keep the conditioned soul in shackles."
- **Use:** for questions.

**SB 3.31.38**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/31/38/
> "Just try to understand the mighty strength of My māyā in the shape of woman, who by the mere movement of her eyebrows can keep even the greatest conquerors of the world under her grip."
- **Teaching:** Lord Kapila calls the attraction a strength of His own māyā that can hold even the greatest conquerors.
- **Purport point:** The purport says that this power of attraction exists first in the Supreme Lord and is reflected in a perverted way in this world. The Lord's attraction is to Radharani, the manifestation of His pleasure potency. A person attracted to Radha and Krishna is no longer attracted by material beauty ("param drstva nivartate"). The purport gives Haridasa Thakura, who turned a prostitute into a great devotee, as an example, and quotes Yamunacarya, who says that after he became attracted to Radha and Krishna the thought of material enjoyment became distasteful to him.
- **Application (preacher's):** This is the cure. The remedy is a higher attraction, not only restraint. See bss-12 and bss-17.
- **Use:** in the talk. This is the key verse.

**SB 3.31.39**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/31/39/
> "One who aspires to reach the culmination of yoga and has realized his self by rendering service unto Me should never associate with an attractive woman, for such a woman is declared in the scripture to be the gateway to hell for the advancing devotee."
- **Teaching:** A person who aspires to the culmination of yoga should never associate with an attractive woman.
- **Purport point:** "In no standard yoga system is the association of women accepted. It is clearly stated here that the association of women is the gateway to hellish life. The association of woman is very much restricted in the Vedic civilization. Out of the four social divisions, the brahmacārī, vānaprastha and the sannyāsī — three orders — are strictly prohibited from the association of women; only the gṛhasthas, or householders, are given license to have an intimate relationship with a woman, and that relationship is also restricted for begetting nice children. If, however, one wants to stick to continued existence in the material world, he may indulge in female association unrestrictedly."
- **Application (preacher's):** Read it with SB 3.31.41-42 and their purports, which give the same teaching to the woman about the man.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 3.31.40**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/31/40/
> "The woman, created by the Lord, is the representation of māyā, and one who associates with such māyā by accepting services must certainly know that this is the way of death, just like a blind well covered with grass."
- **Purport point:** The purport explains the image: an abandoned well covered with grass is not seen by a traveller, who falls in and dies. "Similarly, association with a woman begins when one accepts service from her, because woman is especially created by the Lord to give service to man. By accepting her service, a man is entrapped. If he is not intelligent enough to know that she is the gateway to hellish life, he may indulge in her association very liberally. This is restricted for those who aspire to ascend to the transcendental platform." It describes the custom of fifty years ago in Hindu society (a wife could not see her husband during the daytime; internal quarters for the woman, external for the man), and ends: "it is clearly said that woman is the gateway to death, or forgetfulness of one's self. She blocks the path of spiritual realization."
- **Application (preacher's):** BIKC (ch. 35) draws its rule from this verse: "Service by a woman is a trap for a man. (SB 3.31.40)" The practical rules are in bss-31 and bss-55.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 3.31.41**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/31/41/
> "A living entity who, as a result of attachment to a woman in his previous life, has been endowed with the form of a woman, foolishly looks upon māyā in the form of a man, her husband, as the bestower of wealth, progeny, house and other material assets."
- **Teaching:** The same attachment binds a woman to a man and to the things he provides.
- **Purport point:** "From this verse it appears that a woman is also supposed to have been a man in his (her) previous life, and due to his attachment to his wife, he now has the body of a woman." The purport says a person gets the next birth according to what he thinks of at death: "A woman's attachment to her husband may elevate her to the body of a man in her next life, but a man's attachment to a woman will degrade him, and in his next life he will get the body of a woman." "To be either a woman or a man only involves one's bodily dress." "In the body of a man there is a greater opportunity to get out of the material clutches; there is less opportunity in the body of a woman." "The relationship between man and woman is very complicated, but the substance is that one who aspires to ascend to the transcendental stage of spiritual realization should be very careful in accepting the association of a woman. In the stage of Kṛṣṇa consciousness, however, such restriction of association may be slackened because if a man's and woman's attachment is not to each other but to Kṛṣṇa, then both of them are equally eligible to get out of the material entanglement." "A man should not be attached to a woman, nor should a woman be attached to a man. Both man and woman should be attached to the service of the Lord. Then there is the possibility of liberation from material entanglement for both of them."
- **Application (preacher's):** Read the purport as it is, in this order: the previous life, the bodily dress, the opportunity, the caution, and the attachment to Krishna that makes both equally eligible.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 3.31.42**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/31/42/
> "A woman, therefore, should consider her husband, her house and her children to be the arrangement of the external energy of the Lord for her death, just as the sweet singing of the hunter is death for the deer."
- **Purport point:** The purport says, "not only is woman the gateway to hell for man, but man is also the gateway to hell for woman. It is a question of attachment. A man becomes attached to a woman because of her service, her beauty and many other assets, and similarly a woman becomes attached to a man for his giving her a nice place to live, ornaments, dress and children." "As long as either is attached to the other for such material enjoyment, the woman is dangerous for the man, and the man is also dangerous for the woman. But if the attachment is transferred to Kṛṣṇa, both of them become Kṛṣṇa conscious, and then marriage is very nice." It quotes Rupa Gosvami's definition of yukta-vairagya (Bhakti-rasamrta-sindhu 1.2.255): to use things as far as needed for Krishna's service, without attachment. It says that man and woman can live together as householders in relationship with Krishna, and engage the children, wife and husband in Krishna conscious duties.
- **Application (preacher's):** This is the interpretive key to verses 35 to 41: the danger is attachment, and it works in both directions.
- **Use:** in the talk. This is the second key verse.

**SB 3.31.43**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/31/43/
> "Due to his particular type of body, the materialistic living entity wanders from one planet to another, following fruitive activities."
- **Purport point:** The purport says that a living entity in a material body is called jiva-bhuta, and when free from it, brahma-bhuta. It says that by good fortune a person may meet a bona fide spiritual master and receive the seed of devotional service, which grows when watered by hearing and chanting. Unless a person gets this association, there is no liberation from the cycle.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 3.31.44**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/31/44/
> "When the reaction of his particular activity comes to an end, that end is called death, and when a particular type of reaction begins, that beginning is called birth."
- **Purport point:** The purport says that the soul has travelled through species and planets from time immemorial, that one life is a flash in a long film of actions and reactions, and that different bodies are given for different types of activity. It gives as an example Narada Muni, who in one millennium was the son of a maidservant and in the next a great sage.
- **Use:** in the talk. For Narada's boyhood in full, see bss-55 (J.1).

**SB 3.31.45-46**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/31/45-46/
> "When one begins to view the physical body as one’s very self, that is called birth."
- **Teaching:** Death is the body losing its power to perceive, as the eye loses sight when its nerve is damaged, and the seer remains. Birth is taking the body as oneself.
- **Purport point:** The purport says that the living being has neither birth nor death but is eternal (the purport quotes Bhagavad-gita: "na hanyate hanyamane sarire").
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 3.31.47**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/31/47/
> "Therefore, one should not view death with horror, nor have recourse to defining the body as soul, nor give way to exaggeration in enjoying the bodily necessities of life. Realizing the true nature of the living entity, one should move about in the world free from attachment and steadfast in purpose."
- **Teaching:** Three things to avoid (fear of death, taking the body as the self, exaggerated enjoyment) and one to do (move about free from attachment, steadfast).
- **Purport point:** The purport says that a sane person upset by the sufferings of the womb should look for the remedy, which is found by associating with liberated persons, and that engagement in devotional service in any of the Lord's forms is liberation. It says that real renunciation is giving up the false idea that we can lord it over material nature, that giving up a palace for a forest is not renunciation because both belong to the Lord, and that something that can be used in the Lord's service but is rejected is called phalgu-vairagya (false renunciation). It ends by saying that we should be satisfied with what Krishna supplies and spend our time in devotional service.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 3.31.48**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/31/48/
> "Endowed with right vision and strengthened by devotional service and a pessimistic attitude towards material identity, one should relegate his body to this illusory world through his reason."
- **Purport point:** The purport says one associates with liberated persons through philosophy, by understanding, and that a devotee's attitude toward material existence is detachment, while he does his devotional service and at the end enters the kingdom of God. It also says that the chapter is entitled "Lord Kapila's Instructions on the Movements of the Living Entities".
- **Use:** in the talk, as the closing verse.

## F. Philosophical explanation
**Principle.** Lord Kapila describes a cycle. A soul takes a body by ignorance and works for the body (verses 30 and 31). It suffers in the womb and is born. In the human form it gets another chance. If it returns to the company of those absorbed in sex and taste, it falls again (verse 32). The cause of the falling is attachment: attachment to the body, and the attachment between men and women, which Lord Kapila calls the strength of His māyā (verse 38). The soul then moves from body to body according to its activities (verses 43 to 46). The way out is to see the true position of the soul, to live free of attachment and fear, engaged in devotional service, and to take the company of those who are liberated (verses 47 and 48).

**Why it matters.** A brahmacari has chosen to live without sexual relationships. Verses 32 to 42 describe the thing he is giving up and the danger he is guarding against. The remedy named in verse 38 and its purport is the positive part of his life: a stronger attraction to Radha and Krishna. Verses 43 to 48 place his daily effort inside a bigger frame. He is a soul passing through a long journey, and the human form is the chance to end it.

**Connections.** The verses connect to bss-07 (SB 5.5.8, the attraction between male and female as the basic principle of material life), to bss-22 (SB 3.31.35), to bss-12 and bss-17 (the higher taste), to bss-53 (Puranjana, the story of the same cycle) and to bss-05 (Bharata and the deer).

**Consequences.** Read the verses with the purports, in full. Purport 39: "the association of women is the gateway to hellish life". Purport 42: "man is also the gateway to hell for woman. It is a question of attachment." Purport 41: being a woman or a man is a bodily dress, and both should be attached to the service of the Lord. The chapter ends with advice to live free of fear and attachment, with devotional service.

**Common misunderstandings.**
1. "The purports say only that women are dangerous." They say both: "the woman is dangerous for the man, and the man is also dangerous for the woman" as long as there is attachment, and the cure is attachment to Krishna (purports 38, 41, 42).
2. "A brahmacari must fear the world." SB 3.31.47 says not to view death with horror, and to move about free from attachment and steadfast in purpose. The goal is freedom, not fear.
3. "Renunciation means rejecting everything." The purport of 3.31.47 says that rejecting what could be used for the Lord's service is false renunciation.
4. "The verses are about other people." The warning of 3.31.32 applies to anyone with a human body, including the speaker. The preacher says "we", not "they".

**How to pitch it.** Begin with the remedy, SB 3.31.38 and 3.31.42, and then read the harder verses as the reasons for it, in full and with the purports. Read the verses as Lord Kapila's advice to His own mother.

## I. Core teaching points
**I.1 The root: taking the body as the self (SB 3.31.30-31).** Lord Kapila says that through ignorance the soul accepts the body as himself and works for it, which brings repeated birth and death. The purport says that the first ignorance is to take the body as the self and the second is to take as "mine" whatever is connected with the body. Everything in the rest of the chapter grows from this. Ending: ask the audience what they treat as "mine" because of the body. Transition: what happens when the soul gets a human body again?

**I.2 Association decides (SB 3.31.32-34).** Verse 32 says that if the soul again keeps the company of those engaged in sex and taste, it falls as before. The purport adds that a person can be officially religious and still fall by this association, and that a person who wants to advance should mix with saintly persons. Verse 33 lists what is lost: truthfulness, cleanliness, mercy, seriousness, spiritual intelligence, modesty, austerity, fame, forgiveness, control of the mind and senses, fortune. Ask the audience which of these they would least like to lose. Verse 34 gives the instruction, read it in full: "One should not associate with a coarse fool who is bereft of the knowledge of self-realization and who is no more than a dancing dog in the hands of a woman." The purport quotes Lord Caitanya: one who desires to pass beyond material nescience "must not associate himself with women or with persons interested in material enjoyment. For a person seeking advancement in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, such association is more dangerous than suicide." **Illustration (preacher's, labelled):** a person who wants to be a good runner trains with runners, not because other people are bad, but because his legs follow his company. Ending: choose the company that carries you forward. Transition: why is the pull so strong?

**I.3 The strength of māyā and the cure (SB 3.31.36-38).** Verse 36 gives the example of Brahma. Verse 37 says that none of the beings begotten by Brahma, except the sage Narayana, is immune. Verse 38 calls the attraction the strength of the Lord's own māyā. The purport answers the question "where does this power come from?" It says the attraction exists first in the Lord and in the spiritual world, where it is directed to Radharani, the Lord's pleasure potency, and that here it is reflected in a perverted way. The cure is not to be without attraction. It is to turn the attraction to Radha and Krishna. Haridasa Thakura, whom the purport names, was unmoved because he was absorbed in the holy name. Ending: the remedy is a higher attraction (bss-17). Transition: the next four verses speak about both men and women.

**I.4 Attachment on both sides (SB 3.31.39-42).** Verse 39 tells the advancing devotee that he "should never associate with an attractive woman, for such a woman is declared in the scripture to be the gateway to hell", and the purport gives the rule for the four orders of life. Verse 40: "The woman, created by the Lord, is the representation of māyā", and accepting her services "is the way of death, just like a blind well covered with grass." Explain the image as the purport does: a traveller falls into the well he cannot see, and "association with a woman begins when one accepts service from her". Verses 41 and 42 then say the same about a woman and her attachment to a man and to the things he provides; purport 41 says the man attached to a woman gets a woman's body next life, and that both bodies are dresses. The purport of verse 42 says, "not only is woman the gateway to hell for man, but man is also the gateway to hell for woman. It is a question of attachment." It goes on to say that if the attachment is transferred to Krishna, marriage is very nice, and that Rupa Gosvami's yukta-vairagya is the standard: use things for Krishna without attachment. **Application (preacher's):** a brahmacari follows the restriction given to his order in purport 39, and knows from purport 42 that the same teaching binds the woman. Ending: "It is a question of attachment", and Krishna is the cure. Transition: where does all this lead?

**I.5 The movement and the way out (SB 3.31.43-48).** The soul moves from body to body and from planet to planet according to its activities (43). Death is the end of one set of reactions and birth the beginning of another (44). Death is the body losing its power to perceive, and birth is the soul beginning to see the body as himself (45-46). The purport says that the soul itself has neither birth nor death. Then comes the advice (47): do not view death with horror, do not take the body as the self, do not exaggerate the enjoyment of its needs. Move about in the world free from attachment and steady in purpose. The purport adds that the remedy is found by associating with liberated persons and by engaging in devotional service. Real renunciation is to give up the false idea that we are the controller. Verse 48 closes the chapter. **Application (preacher's):** a brahmacari may sometimes feel that his life is a long list of "do nots". This ending is a list of "do's": see correctly, serve, associate with the liberated, and be steady. Ending: end with SB 3.31.47 read aloud, slowly.

## J. Stories and illustrations
**J.1 Narada's two lives (SB 3.31.44 purport; the full story in SB 1.5-1.6).** The purport of SB 3.31.44 says that Narada Muni was the son of a maidservant in one millennium and a great sage in the next. In bss-55 the story of his boyhood is told in full. **Devotional lesson:** the same soul can rise as well as fall. Association with devotees (see bss-55 for his boyhood) changed his course.

**J.2 Bharata and the deer (SB 5.8; told in full in bss-05).** Maharaja Bharata, who had retired to the forest to worship the Lord, grew attached to an orphaned deer, and at death thought of the deer and took birth as one. **Devotional lesson:** even a sincere renunciant moves to a new body according to what he is attached to at the end. This is verse 44 in a story. (The full story, with the verses, is on bss-05.)

**J.3 Invented illustration (labelled).** A young man in a village walks each evening along a path by a field. One day grass is cut and left on an old, open well. His elder brother, who has lived there for years, says, "Stay on the stones." The younger one asks, "Why?" The elder points to the well, and the young man steps back. The point: SB 3.31.40 uses the same picture. The danger is covered. A person who knows the ground can warn another, and a student who asks and listens is saved (BG 4.34).

## K. Anticipated questions and objections
1. **"Does this verse say that women are the gateway to hell?"** Yes: verse 39 says it, and purport 39 says "the association of women is the gateway to hellish life". Purport 42 adds that "man is also the gateway to hell for woman. It is a question of attachment." Purport 41 says that both man and woman should be attached to the service of the Lord.
2. **"Why such strong words?"** Give the purports' own reason. Purport 36: "even great demigods like Brahmā and Lord Śiva, what to speak of the ordinary conditioned soul, are captivated by the beauty of woman." Purport 37: sex attraction "is the basic principle of this material world". Purport 40: "This is restricted for those who aspire to ascend to the transcendental platform."
3. **"Is a brahmacari not allowed to speak to women?"** The purport of verse 39 says the three orders of brahmacari, vanaprastha and sannyasi are restricted from association with women, and the householder is licensed under restrictions. How a brahmacari conducts himself with women devotees, in service and in the ashram, is the subject of bss-31 and bss-55. Ask your temple's senior for the local practice.
4. **"I am afraid of death after hearing about the womb and about rebirth."** Verse 47 says not to view death with horror. The purport says a sane person looks for the remedy, which is found in the company of liberated persons and in devotional service. Chant, hear and serve, and speak with a senior devotee.
5. **"Does this mean that we should hate our body?"** No. Verse 47 says not to define the body as the soul and also not to exaggerate its enjoyment. The purport says phalgu-vairagya, rejecting what can be used in the Lord's service, is false renunciation. See bss-49 on health.
6. **"I am struggling with attraction and feel guilty."** This is common and it is not a reason for shame. Verse 38's purport gives the cure: a higher attraction, by hearing and chanting and by serving devotees. Talk to a trusted senior devotee or your mentor. If you feel low, anxious or hopeless, also see a qualified counsellor or doctor. In India call Tele-MANAS on 14416; elsewhere use the local crisis service.
7. **"What does purport 41 say about being reborn as a woman?"** Read it as it is: "a man's attachment to a woman will degrade him, and in his next life he will get the body of a woman"; "To be either a woman or a man only involves one's bodily dress"; "In the body of a man there is a greater opportunity to get out of the material clutches; there is less opportunity in the body of a woman"; and if the attachment is to Krishna, "both of them are equally eligible to get out of the material entanglement".

## L. Case studies
**L.1 Invented case for discussion.** Rohan, 20, is a brahmacari. A colleague at his part-time job is friendly and kind. They begin to chat about their days for a long time. He finds he is thinking about her at night.
- *Dilemma:* He does not want to be rude or unkind, and he does not want to be caught.
- *Principles:* SB 3.31.38 (the cure is a higher attraction), SB 3.31.40 (the covered well), SB 3.31.42 purport (the danger is attachment on both sides).
- *Questions:* What is the first thing he could do? Who could he talk to? What could he do in the evening that gives him a higher taste?
- *Analysis:* He should keep his conversation at work to work, be courteous and short, speak to his mentor soon and honestly, and increase his hearing and chanting.
- *Takeaway:* the covered well is seen by the person who is told about it. Tell someone early.

**L.2 Invented case for discussion (extension).** A student says, "SB 3.31.39 is sexist. I cannot teach it."
- *Principles:* SB 3.31.41-42 and their purports; honesty.
- *Questions:* How would you answer without hiding the verse?
- *Analysis:* Read verses 39, 41 and 42 with their purports together. Say that the purport restricts the brahmacari, vanaprastha and sannyasi from the association of women, that the same teaching is given to women about men, and that the cure is Krishna in both cases. Offer to read the purports together.
- *Takeaway:* read the whole teaching, not one line.

## M. Discussion and reflection
**M.1 Closing check questions (with expected answers).**
1. *What does SB 3.31.32 say happens if the soul returns to the company of those absorbed in sex and the tongue?* It again goes to a hellish condition.
2. *Name three things SB 3.31.33 says are lost.* For example: truthfulness, cleanliness, mercy, control of the mind and senses.
3. *What does the purport of SB 3.31.38 give as the cure?* Attraction to Radha and Krishna, which makes material attraction fall away.
4. *According to the purport of SB 3.31.42, who is the danger for whom?* Each can be a danger for the other when there is attachment. The cure is attachment to Krishna.
5. *What is death, according to SB 3.31.44?* The end of one set of reactions; birth is the beginning of another.
6. *What three things does SB 3.31.47 say not to do, and what to do?* Not to view death with horror, not to define the body as the soul, not to exaggerate the enjoyment of the body; to move about free from attachment and steadfast in purpose.

**M.2 Activities.**
a) *Verse in a sentence (10 min).* In pairs, each person takes one of verses 32, 33, 38, 42 and 47 and says it in one plain sentence. The partner says whether it is clear. Then each pair says the one sentence they find most helpful.

**M.3 Reflection.** "What do I think about most when I am alone, and what would I like to think about?" Private; nothing is shared.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary
In the 31st chapter of the Third Canto of the Bhagavatam, Lord Kapila tells His mother Devahuti how the soul moves from body to body. By ignorance the soul takes the body as himself and works for it, and this leads to repeated birth and death (SB 3.31.30-31). If the soul is given a human body again and goes back to the company of those absorbed in sex and the pleasure of the tongue, it falls again (SB 3.31.32). Many good qualities are lost this way, such as truthfulness, cleanliness, mercy and control of the mind and senses (SB 3.31.33).
Lord Kapila calls the attraction between men and women the strength of His own māyā. Even Brahma was bewildered by it (SB 3.31.36-38). The remedy is a higher attraction. Srila Prabhupada explains that the attraction first exists in the Lord and in the spiritual world, and that a person attracted to Radha and Krishna loses interest in material attractions (purport to SB 3.31.38). Lord Kapila then says that one who aspires to the culmination of yoga "should never associate with an attractive woman, for such a woman is declared in the scripture to be the gateway to hell for the advancing devotee" (SB 3.31.39), and that accepting a woman's services "is the way of death, just like a blind well covered with grass" (SB 3.31.40). The verses that follow say the same thing about a woman and a man. The purport says, "not only is woman the gateway to hell for man, but man is also the gateway to hell for woman. It is a question of attachment" (purport to SB 3.31.42). If the attachment is turned to Krishna, both are freed.
Death is the end of one set of actions and reactions, and birth is the start of another (SB 3.31.44). The soul itself does not die. So we are not to fear death, nor to take the body as the self, nor to exaggerate the enjoyment of the body. We should move about in the world free from attachment and steady in purpose, in devotional service (SB 3.31.47-48). If you feel pulled or low, speak to a trusted senior devotee or a counsellor. In India call Tele-MANAS on 14416.

## S2. Key ideas
- The soul moves from body to body by attachment and ignorance.
- Close company with those absorbed in sex and taste pulls us down (SB 3.31.32).
- The cure is a higher attraction, to Radha and Krishna (SB 3.31.38).
- An attractive woman is "the gateway to hell for the advancing devotee" (SB 3.31.39), and "man is also the gateway to hell for woman. It is a question of attachment" (SB 3.31.42 purport).
- Do not fear death. Live free from attachment, in service (SB 3.31.47).

## S3. Verses to remember
- **SB 3.31.38**: The Lord's māyā in the form of attraction can bind even the greatest conquerors. *For me:* I need a higher attraction, not only willpower.
- **SB 3.31.42** (purport): "not only is woman the gateway to hell for man, but man is also the gateway to hell for woman. It is a question of attachment." *For me:* I move my attachment to Krishna.
- **SB 3.31.47**: Do not fear death; move about free from attachment, steady in purpose. *For me:* I live with confidence.

## S4. Words to know
- **Brahmacari:** a celibate student who lives simply and serves his guru.
- **Māyā:** the Lord's illusory energy that makes the soul forget Him.
- **Attachment:** a tie of the heart to a person or thing as "mine".
- **Fruitive activities:** actions done for their results, which bring a new body.
- **Renunciation:** giving up. Real renunciation is giving up the idea that we are the controller.
- **Yukta-vairagya:** using things for Krishna's service, without attachment.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *What does SB 3.31 say about women?* Verse 39 says an attractive woman is "the gateway to hell for the advancing devotee", and verse 40 that accepting her services "is the way of death". Srila Prabhupada's purport to verse 42 says that "man is also the gateway to hell for woman. It is a question of attachment", and that both should be attached to the service of the Lord (SB 3.31.41-42).
2. *How can I resist attraction?* By a higher taste: hearing and chanting, service and the company of devotees (purport to SB 3.31.38).
3. *Should I be afraid of death?* No. SB 3.31.47 says not to view death with horror.
4. *What if I feel guilty or low?* Speak to a trusted senior devotee or a counsellor. In India call Tele-MANAS on 14416.
5. *What is false renunciation?* Giving up something that could be used in the Lord's service and not using it for that (purport to SB 3.31.47).

4. What does SB 3.31.44 call death?

**Answers:** 1. It again goes to a hellish condition (SB 3.31.32). 2. Attraction to Radha and Krishna (purport to SB 3.31.38). 3. Not to view death with horror, not to define the body as the soul, not to exaggerate the enjoyment of the body (SB 3.31.47).
