# Revealing Heart in Confidence - Art of one to one meeting with facilitator

> **VL3 · 180 min · review: senior-review · sensitive: true** (it covers disclosures of self-harm and abuse, F.6–8 and
> L Case 4; Stage 2 §2.5: the head reviews it before use). IYF's material is one short deck, *Revealing the Heart in Confidence with Honesty
> and Trust* (`research/iyf-materials/iyf-decks/revealing the heart in confidence.ppt.txt`; the same file sits under
> dps-02, where the topic returns at DPS). Its outline (definition, need, problems if not revealed, examples, obstacles,
> remedies) is sound and is kept. Its one Prabhupada quotation is unsourced and is flagged (O). This page adds what the
> deck lacks and VL3 most needs: **what the student may expect from the facilitator**, especially confidentiality, and
> **how a facilitator should respond** when a heart is opened to him. The last part is guidance for the preacher and
> facilitators (an FTP matter), labelled as such; it is not a scriptural claim.

## A. Topic overview

| Difficulty | Introductory |

| Teaching objective | Establish the one-to-one meeting as a real exchange: the student brings his heart (sadhana, doubts, struggles, successes) and the facilitator receives it in confidence and guides him; teach both the student's part and, for the preacher, the facilitator's part |

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on vl3-07: the week of home practice is what the student has to report; this page does not re-teach that practice.

IYF's own KRAs for every batch include "personal meeting with the facilitator, at least every 15 days" (System tab);
the VL3 KRAs add "Meeting with facilitators" and the Standards tab asks IYF to "ensure one to one facilitation of
everyone" for VL2 and VL3 (`research/batch-criteria.md` §0, §3). The topic exists to make those meetings worth having.
VL3 is also where the student first meets real difficulty in practice (rounds missed, family questions, doubts), and
where a habit of hiding or of opening up is formed.

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| VL1 | Not covered; at most an invitation to meet a mentor |
| VL2 | "Meet your facilitator every week" as a rule (VL2 form) |
| **VL3** (here) | Why and how to open the heart; what to bring; what confidentiality means; what to expect back |
| SPS | Relationships, connectivity and dealings among devotees (sps-04, sps-18, sps-21) |
| DPS | The same quality at depth (dps-02): guidance before decisions of life (ashrama, career, initiation); Sanatana Gosvami and Sri Caitanya as the model of confidential inquiry |
| BSS | The brahmacari (celibate student) and his senior: honesty in ashram life |

**Not done here:**
- Guru-tattva. The facilitator is a senior friend and guide, not the student's spiritual master; the page never
  blurs this. The verses on approaching a spiritual master (BG 2.7, 4.34, SB 3.25.8) are used for the *attitude* of
  inquiry, and each use is labelled.
- Public confession. Nothing in the session asks anyone to disclose anything in the group (M).
- Counselling or therapy. A facilitator is neither; the page says when to refer (F.8).

## C. Central message

Opening one's mind in confidence and inquiring confidentially are two of the six exchanges of love between devotees
(NOI 4); Arjuna's "Now I am Your disciple… Please instruct me" (BG 2.7) shows how the heart is revealed, and Krsna's
"Because you are My very dear friend, I am speaking to you… the most confidential knowledge" (BG 18.64) shows the trust
that answers it. A facilitator who keeps that trust, and a student who uses it, make every other practice easier.

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. Name the six loving exchanges of NOI 4 and explain why "revealing one's mind in confidence" and "inquiring
   confidentially" belong among them.
2. Describe, from BG 2.7 and 4.34, the attitude in which the heart is opened: honest about confusion, submissive,
   inquiring, not demanding.
3. List what to bring to a one-to-one meeting (sadhana, doubts, struggles, successes, plans) and what makes it
   hard (the deck's "false ego" list), with one remedy each.
4. State what confidentiality they may expect from a facilitator and where its limits lie (safety).
5. (Preachers and facilitators) Describe how to receive a revealed heart: listen, keep it, encourage, guide from
   scripture, refer when needed (BG 17.15; BG 18.63).

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** NOI 4, BG 2.7 and BG 18.64 carry the talk; BG 4.34 and 17.15 for attitude on both sides; the Bhagavatam
examples in J.

**NOI 4** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/4/
> "Offering gifts in charity, accepting charitable gifts, revealing one's mind in confidence, inquiring confidentially, accepting prasāda and offering prasāda are the six symptoms of love shared by one devotee and another."
- **Context:** Rupa Gosvami's *Upadesamrta*; verse 3 told us to keep devotees' company; verse 4 says what that company
  consists of.
- **Teaching:** Six exchanges make up loving dealings between devotees; two of the six are confidential speech.
- **Purport point:** "(3) opening one's mind to the devotees, (4) inquiring from them about the confidential service
  of the Lord… An experienced devotee explains, and an inexperienced devotee learns from him. This is guhyam ākhyāti
  pṛcchati." "Even in ordinary social activities, these six types of dealings between two loving friends are
  absolutely necessary." ISKCON "has been established to facilitate these six kinds of loving exchanges".
- **Application (preacher's):** The one-to-one meeting is NOI 4 items 3 and 4 given a time and a place. The purport's
  own example of two businessmen over a meal shows the exchanges are natural, not artificial; IYF simply makes sure
  they happen.
- **Use:** in the talk; the verse to remember.

**BG 2.7** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/7/
> "Now I am confused about my duty and have lost all composure because of miserly weakness. In this condition I am asking You to tell me for certain what is best for me. Now I am Your disciple, and a soul surrendered unto You. Please instruct me."
- **Context:** Arjuna, having argued his case for an entire chapter, stops arguing and opens his heart.
- **Teaching:** Honest admission of confusion plus a request for instruction is the start of guidance.
- **Purport point:** "In every step there is perplexity, and therefore it behooves one to approach a bona fide
  spiritual master who can give one proper guidance." Arjuna "could understand that his affection for family members…
  were the causes of his perplexities."
- **Application (preacher's):** Arjuna names the real problem ("confused… lost all composure… miserly weakness"), not
  a polished version of it. That honesty is the model. **Label:** Arjuna approaches Krsna as guru; the facilitator is
  not the student's guru. The *attitude* transfers; the relationship does not.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 4.34** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/4/34/
> "Just try to learn the truth by approaching a spiritual master. Inquire from him submissively and render service unto him. The self-realized souls can impart knowledge unto you because they have seen the truth."
- **Purport point:** "Inquiries and submission constitute the proper combination for spiritual understanding."
  "Both blind following and absurd inquiries are condemned." "A bona fide spiritual master is by nature very kind
  toward the disciple."
- **Application (preacher's):** For the student: inquire, do not only report. For the facilitator (the purport's last
  sentence): kindness is the nature of one who guides. Same label as BG 2.7: the verse is about the guru; the attitude
  of submissive inquiry is what VL3 borrows.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 10.9** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/10/9/
> "The thoughts of My pure devotees dwell in Me, their lives are fully devoted to My service, and they derive great satisfaction and bliss from always enlightening one another and conversing about Me."
- **Purport point:** Devotees "take pleasure in discussing Him with other devotees"; the seed of devotion grows by
  "regular watering" of hearing and chanting.
- **Application (preacher's):** "Enlightening one another" (*bodhayantaḥ parasparam*) is mutual. The facilitator also
  learns from the student's realisations; the deck's point that positive realisations too should be shared rests here.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 17.15** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/17/15/
> "Austerity of speech consists in speaking words that are truthful, pleasing, beneficial, and not agitating to others, and also in regularly reciting Vedic literature."
- **Purport point:** "When a teacher speaks, he can speak the truth for the instruction of his students"; one should
  "quote from scriptural authority to back up what he is saying", and "such talk should be very pleasurable to the ear."
- **Application (preacher's):** The facilitator's four tests for his reply: true, pleasing, beneficial, not agitating.
  The student's four tests for what he says about others in the meeting (no gossip dressed as disclosure).
- **Use:** in the talk, for the facilitator's part.

**BG 18.63** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/63/
> "Thus I have explained to you knowledge still more confidential. Deliberate on this fully, and then do what you wish to do."
- **Purport point:** "God does not interfere with the little independence of the living entity… Before surrendering,
  one is free to deliberate on this subject as far as the intelligence goes." Such instruction "comes also through
  the spiritual master, the bona fide representative of Kṛṣṇa."
- **Application (preacher's):** Even Krsna, after the most confidential instruction, leaves Arjuna free to decide. A
  facilitator advises and leaves the choice with the student. This rules out pressure and control.
- **Use:** in the talk, for the facilitator's part.

**BG 18.64** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/64/
> "Because you are My very dear friend, I am speaking to you My supreme instruction, the most confidential knowledge of all. Hear this from Me, for it is for your benefit."
- **Purport point:** The most confidential knowledge is understood "by one who is actually very dear to Kṛṣṇa"; it
  is given "for your benefit".
- **Application (preacher's):** Confidence flows both ways: Arjuna opened his heart (2.7); Krsna gives what is "most
  confidential" to "My very dear friend". Confidentiality in a one-to-one is the human echo of this: what is said
  because of friendship is kept because of friendship.
- **Use:** in the talk; the second verse to remember.

**BG 6.5** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/5/
> "One must deliver himself with the help of his mind, and not degrade himself. The mind is the friend of the conditioned soul, and his enemy as well."
- **Application (preacher's):** Why a second person is needed: the mind that is to be trained is the same mind doing
  the judging. The deck's list of why hearts stay closed ("don't want to be evaluated", "image conscious") describes
  the mind as enemy.
- **Use:** background.

**SB 1.19.37** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/19/37/
> "You are the spiritual master of great saints and devotees. I am therefore begging you to show the way of perfection for all persons, and especially for one who is about to die."
- **Purport point:** "Unless one is perfectly anxious to inquire about the way of perfection, there is no necessity
  of approaching a spiritual master… Mahārāja Parīkṣit is the right type of disciple because he puts forward questions
  vital to the interest of all men."
- **Application (preacher's):** Pariksit (the deck's example) shows *inquiring confidentially*: a real question, about
  his real situation, to one who can answer.
- **Use:** in J.

**SB 3.25.7** and **SB 3.25.8** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/25/7/
> 3.25.7: "Devahūti said: I am very sick of the disturbance caused by my material senses, for because of this sense disturbance, my Lord, I have fallen into the abyss of ignorance."
- **Purport point (3.25.7):** "Unless one becomes tired of material sense gratification, there is no opportunity to
  hear transcendental messages from a person like Kapila. Devahūti expressed that she was tired."
- **Purport point (3.25.8):** The guru is "one who can take the disciple to the other side" (*pāragam*).
- **Application (preacher's):** Devahuti (the deck's example) reveals her heart without decoration: "I am very sick…
  I have fallen." Her honesty is what makes Kapila's instruction possible. **Label:** Kapila is the Lord and her son;
  the facilitator is neither. The pattern of honest disclosure is what transfers.
- **Use:** in J.

**SB 5.10.15** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/10/15/
> "…He immediately descended from his palanquin and fell flat on the ground with his head at the lotus feet of Jaḍa Bharata in such a way that he might be excused for his insulting words against the great brāhmaṇa."
- **Purport point:** Rahugana "regretted his insulting words… and immediately descended… in order to be excused and
  to hear from him further".
- **Application (preacher's):** Rahugana (the deck's example) shows the hardest disclosure: admitting a mistake to
  the person one wronged, then asking to learn. The deck's "don't want to accept our mistake" obstacle has this as its
  cure.
- **Use:** in J.

**"Any decision however important should be confirmed by some authority, that makes the matter perfect" (deck,
attributed to Srila Prabhupada)** — Requires source review. No date, place or letter is given. Do not quote it as
Prabhupada's until found; the principle is adequately stated by BG 4.34 and 18.63.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The practice has a scriptural name.** *Guhyam ākhyāti pṛcchati*: "revealing one's mind in confidence,
   inquiring confidentially" (NOI 4). It is not an IYF management tool; it is one of the six symptoms of love between
   devotees that Rupa Gosvami lists, and the purport says ISKCON exists to facilitate exactly these exchanges. The
   fortnightly meeting is where VL3 practises items 3 and 4.
2. **What is revealed.** The deck's definition is good and is kept: one's "personal sadhana, devotee relationships,
   philosophical doubts, successful experiences, plans for preaching and other realizations both positive and
   negative". Four of these are easy to forget: *doubts* (the deck: "gradually doubts would increase" if not spoken),
   *successes* (BG 10.9: enlightening one another; the deck: unrevealed success breeds "subtle pride"), *relationships
   with other devotees* (friction kept inside becomes offence), and *plans* (a plan checked before it is acted on). The
   deck's phrase "each and every thought present in the mind" is too strong for VL3; say "whatever is actually shaping
   your practice".
3. **How it is revealed: Arjuna's way.** BG 2.7: he names the state ("confused… lost all composure"), the cause
   ("miserly weakness") and the request ("tell me for certain what is best… Please instruct me"). Three parts: what is
   happening, what I think is behind it, what I am asking. Honest, specific, asking. Not a performance of humility and
   not a demand for approval. BG 4.34: inquiry *and* submission; the purport condemns both "blind following and absurd
   inquiries".
4. **Why hearts stay closed.** The deck's list, under "false ego", holds up and should be read out: tendency to lord;
   not wanting to be evaluated; not wanting a reality check; image-consciousness; not wanting to admit a mistake; more
   faith in one's own efforts than in guidance; not knowing the importance of revealing; not knowing how. BG 6.5
   explains the root: the mind is both friend and enemy, and the enemy-mind guards its image. The deck's two images
   are useful if labelled as the course's illustrations: a "constipated heart" (something taken in and never let out)
   and the servant who carried a burden alone until it became resentment.
5. **What happens when it stays closed.** The deck's consequences, in order: independent mentality; doubts grow;
   service becomes a burden; subtle pride ("kartā aham iti manyate", BG 3.27's bewildered doer); mental offences
   against devotees; and "as time would increase it would become difficult to reveal". The last is the practical
   point for VL3: the first meeting is the easiest one to be honest in.
6. **What may be expected back: confidentiality.** This is the part the deck omits and students most need to hear.
   *Application (preacher's) and IYF practice:* what a student says to his facilitator about himself stays with the
   facilitator. It is not repeated to other students, not used as an example in a class, not passed to the student's
   family, not reported upward except as the facilitator's own general assessment, and never used to shame. BG 18.64
   is the scriptural echo: the most confidential knowledge is spoken "because you are My very dear friend"; what
   friendship opens, friendship keeps. The IYF deck's own definition, "the right person", presupposes this. **Limits
   (pastoral guidance, labelled):** if what is disclosed involves risk to the student's safety or someone else's
   (self-harm, abuse, a crime against a person), the facilitator must involve a senior and, where needed, professional
   help; he tells the student he is doing so. This is the one exception, and it protects the student.
7. **How a facilitator responds (guidance for preachers and facilitators).** Five steps, each with its text.
   (a) *Listen first.* NOI 1 (not cited here; see dps-32) begins with tolerating the urge to speak; let the student
   finish. (b) *Keep it.* F.6. (c) *Encourage.* BG 4.34 purport: the guide "is by nature very kind". A struggle
   disclosed is a sign of trust, never an occasion for displeasure; the student who is scolded for a missed week
   learns to hide the next one. (d) *Guide from scripture, in the Gita's manner of speech.* BG 17.15: true, pleasing,
   beneficial, not agitating; "quote from scriptural authority". Point to the verse and the practice, not to your
   opinion. (e) *Leave the choice with him.* BG 18.63: "deliberate on this fully, and then do what you wish to do."
   Advice, not orders; never pressure about family, career or vows at VL3 (vl3-13; dps-56).
8. **When to refer (pastoral guidance, labelled).** A facilitator is not a counsellor. If a student describes
   persistent low mood, hopelessness, panic, self-harm or thoughts of ending his life, or any abuse, the facilitator
   listens, stays with him, tells him that professional help is fully compatible with spiritual life, involves a
   senior, and gives the free 24×7 Tele-MANAS helpline, 14416 (Government of India). Chanting continues; it does not
   replace care. No shame language, ever.
9. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - "Revealing the heart means telling the facilitator everything about everyone." No: about *oneself*. Grievances
     about other devotees are raised to resolve them, not to be relieved of them; BG 17.15 applies to what is said.
   - "The facilitator is my guru." He is a senior friend who guides. Guru-tattva comes at DPS.
   - "If I disclose a struggle I'll be judged or dropped." The opposite (BG 9.30–31 in dps-01; here F.7c).
   - "A good meeting is a long one." Fifteen honest minutes beat an hour of attendance-reporting.
10. **Pitch for VL3.** Practice terms (facilitator, one-to-one, sadhana card). One practice to establish: the next
    meeting prepared on paper, with one honest item in each of the four boxes (P). Encouraging throughout; the mood
    of NOI 4 is love, not audit.

## G. Lecture flow

**Main plan (about 3 hours: 90 min lecture + 90 min engagement)**

| Time | Segment | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0-8 | Opening: "When did you last tell anyone something that mattered?" (H) | No answers required aloud |
| 8-20 | NOI 4: the six exchanges; two are confidential speech; ISKCON exists for these | The verse to remember |
| 20-35 | Arjuna's way (BG 2.7; 4.34): what, why, what I'm asking | Three-part model on the board |
| 35-50 | Why hearts stay closed and what it costs (F.4-5; BG 6.5) | Read the deck's list; students tick silently |
| 50-65 | What you may expect back: confidentiality and its one limit (F.6; BG 18.64) | Say it plainly; this is what they came for |
| 65-80 | Examples: Pariksit, Devahuti, Rahugana (J) | Three kinds of disclosure |
| 80-90 | Prepare your next meeting (P) | The four boxes |
| 90-170 | Engagement block: two activities from M.2, about 35 min each | Everything invented or private; no one discloses a real matter |
| 170-180 | Closing check (M.1) and reflection (M.3) | Praise good answers |

**Alternative: up to a 2-hour lecture, activities skipped.** Spend the extra time on F.6 and the cases in L (read aloud, discussed as invented), take M.1 orally, and give the four boxes (M.2 Option 2) as homework.

**30-minute cut:** drop J to one example (Devahuti) and F.5 to one line; keep NOI 4, BG 2.7, F.6 and P.

**Alternative structure (facilitators' session):** when the audience is facilitators (FTP), invert the order: open with
F.7 (how to respond) and F.6 (confidentiality), work through the cases in L from the facilitator's chair, and end with
NOI 4 as the standard they are serving.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet, say your name, and say clearly that nobody will be asked to share anything personal today.
2. Define the words: a facilitator is a senior devotee who guides you one-to-one; "confidence" means trust and privacy.
3. Ask H.2 question 1 silently: students think, no one answers aloud.
4. Ask H.2 question 2; take two or three general answers.
5. Say what you will do: learn how to use a one-to-one meeting well, and what you can expect back.

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the room's own life)
1. Think of the last time you told someone something that mattered. Who was it? (Silent.)
2. What makes a person safe to talk to: what do they do, and what do they never do?
3. Why can a doctor treat only what you tell them?

- **Opening question (no answers aloud):** "Think of the last time you told someone something that actually mattered
  to you. Who was it, and why them?" Pause. "Whatever made you choose that person is what this topic is about."
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** Aman has met his facilitator six times. Each time: "Rounds? Four. Reading? Yes.
  Class? Yes." Both leave satisfied. Aman has had the same doubt about Deity worship for three months and has told no
  one.
- **Transition:** "Rupa Gosvami says that devotees' friendship has six parts, and two of them are exactly what Aman
  is not doing."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** VL3 is where practice meets friction. Whether a student learns to speak or to
  hide this year decides much of what follows.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Two of the six exchanges are confidential speech.** NOI 4. *Example:* the purport's two businessmen. *Transition:*
   how does one actually do it?
2. **Arjuna's three parts.** BG 2.7: what is happening, what is behind it, what I ask; BG 4.34: inquiry with
   submission. *Example:* "I missed my rounds four days this week; I think it's because I sleep after midnight; what
   should I change?" *Transition:* why is this so hard?
3. **The mind guards its image.** BG 6.5; the deck's list. *Example:* "subtle pride" from unrevealed success.
   *Transition:* what do I get back?
4. **Confidence is kept, and it flows both ways.** BG 18.64; F.6 with its one limit. *Example:* what is and is not
   done with what you say. *Transition:* three who did it.
5. **Pariksit, Devahuti, Rahugana.** SB 1.19.37; 3.25.7–8; 5.10.15: a real question, an honest state, an admitted
   wrong. *Transition:* to P.

## J. Stories and illustrations

- **Arjuna** (BG 1–2): a whole chapter of arguments, then BG 2.7. The shift from arguing to asking is the story.
- **Maharaja Pariksit** (SB 1.19.37 and purport): seven days to live; asks the question that matters to all. *Inquiring
  confidentially*: a real question to one who can answer.
- **Devahuti** (SB 3.25.7–8): "I am very sick… I have fallen into the abyss of ignorance." *Revealing the mind*: an
  honest state, no decoration. The purport: she had to be tired of sense gratification before she could hear.
- **Maharaja Rahugana** (SB 5.10.15): descends from the palanquin, falls at Jada Bharata's feet, asks to be excused and
  to hear more. *Admitting a wrong* to the one wronged. (The deck lists him; read SB 5.10 before telling.)
- **Dhruva Maharaja** (the deck lists him; SB 4.8): insulted by his stepmother, he goes to his mother Suniti and tells
  her, and she directs him to the Lord; later Narada guides him. **Partially verified:** read SB 4.8.9–25 before telling;
  do not add dialogue beyond the text.
- **Contemporary image (the preacher's):** a doctor can only treat the symptom you describe. The one you hide is the
  one that grows.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

> **Before you teach:** be ready to say, in one sentence, what confidentiality means in IYF and what its one limit
> is (F.6). Students will test you on it.

1. **"Will what I say be kept private?"**
   - Yes, as F.6 describes: not repeated to other students, not used as an example, not passed to family, not used to
     shame. The one limit is safety (self-harm, abuse, harm to others), and even then the facilitator tells you he is
     involving a senior. Say this plainly; if your temple's practice differs, say what it actually is.
2. **"Why can't I just sort things out myself with Krsna?"**
   - You should, and you also need a second pair of eyes: the mind that needs training is the mind doing the
     judging (BG 6.5). Arjuna had Krsna in his chariot and still had to say "Please instruct me" (BG 2.7). Rupa Gosvami
     makes the exchange a *symptom of love* (NOI 4), not a sign of weakness.
3. **"What if my facilitator is only a few years older than me?"**
   - NOI 4 purport: "An experienced devotee explains, and an inexperienced devotee learns from him." Experience in the
     practice is what counts. If a question is beyond him, a good facilitator says so and asks a senior, with your
     permission.
4. **"Isn't talking about my doubts disloyal?"**
   - The opposite. The deck warns that unspoken doubts grow. BG 4.34 purport condemns blind following as much as
     absurd inquiry. A doubt spoken to the right person becomes a question; hidden, it becomes a wall.
5. **"I did something I'm ashamed of. Will I be dropped from the batch?"**
   - No. The facilitator's response to a disclosed struggle is kindness and guidance (BG 4.34 purport), and the Gita
     forbids deriding a devotee for a lapse (BG 9.30–31, taught at dps-01). Rahugana's admission (SB 5.10.15) was the
     beginning of his learning, not the end.
6. **"Should I also tell him about problems with other devotees?"**
   - Yes, to resolve them, and in the Gita's manner (BG 17.15: true, pleasing, beneficial, not agitating). Not to
     unload. The facilitator will help you speak to the person, not speak for you.
7. **(Facilitators) "A student told me something I'm not qualified to handle."**
   - Say so, thank him for trusting you, and bring in a senior with his knowledge. If it concerns his safety or mental
     health, F.8: stay with him, involve a senior, give Tele-MANAS 14416, and never treat chanting as a substitute for
     care.
8. **(Facilitators) "How much should I tell him about my own struggles?"**
   - Enough to show that struggle is normal; not so much that the meeting becomes about you. BG 10.9: devotees
     enlighten *one another*, but the meeting has a direction.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The attendance report.** Aman (H) reports "four rounds, reading yes" for the sixth time while carrying a
three-month doubt about Deity worship.
- **Principles:** NOI 4 (inquiring confidentially); BG 2.7 (name the real thing); the deck's "doubts would increase".
- **Questions:** Why does he not ask? What is the smallest honest sentence he could say next time? What should the
  facilitator do differently to make the question possible?
- **Takeaway:** The meeting is for the doubt, not the numbers.

**Case 2: The borrowed example.** A facilitator, teaching a class, says "one of my students told me last week that he
had stopped chanting because…" and gives enough detail that two students recognise who it is.
- **Principles:** F.6 (confidentiality); BG 18.64 (what friendship opens, friendship keeps); BG 17.15.
- **Questions:** What has been lost, and for whom? What will that student do next time? How should the facilitator
  repair it?
- **Takeaway:** Nothing disclosed one-to-one is teaching material, even anonymised, without permission.

**Case 3: The missed month.** Rohit missed his rounds for most of a month during exams and now avoids his facilitator,
sure he will be scolded.
- **Principles:** BG 4.34 purport (kindness); BG 9.30–31 (no derision for a lapse; dps-01); the deck's "as time
  would increase it would become difficult to reveal".
- **Questions:** What is Rohit's mind telling him (BG 6.5)? What should the facilitator's first sentence be when
  Rohit finally comes? What would make it safe to come sooner next time?
- **Takeaway:** The first meeting after a lapse is the most important one; make it easy.

**Case 4 (facilitators): The disclosure that is bigger than you.** In a one-to-one, a student says he has not wanted to
be alive for some weeks.
- **Principles:** F.8 (pastoral guidance): stay, listen, do not minimise, do not moralise; involve a senior; give
  Tele-MANAS 14416; professional help is compatible with spiritual life.
- **Questions:** What do you say in the first minute? Whom do you tell, and how do you tell the student you are
  doing so? What do you *not* say ("just chant more", "this is maya")?
- **Takeaway:** Confidentiality's one limit is safety, and it is exercised *with* the student, not behind him.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Rupa Gosvami lists six exchanges that make up love between devotees: giving and accepting charity, revealing one's mind in confidence and inquiring confidentially, and accepting and offering prasadam (food offered to Krsna) (NOI 4). Your one-to-one meeting with your facilitator, a senior devotee who guides you and is a friend, not your spiritual master, is where you practise the middle two. Many people attend such meetings and report only "rounds four, reading yes"; real revealing means bringing a doubt, a struggle, a success and a plan.

Arjuna shows how. In BG 2.7 he says what is happening (he is confused), what is behind it (a miserly weakness) and what he asks (instruct me). He inquires with submission: BG 4.34 condemns blind following and absurd inquiries, and asks for sincere questions. Hearts stay closed because of false ego (identifying with an image we must protect): we do not want to be judged, to admit a mistake, or to lose face. Hidden things grow, and the longer they stay hidden the harder they are to say. The remedy is to say the hardest item first.

What you say about yourself stays with the facilitator; the one limit is safety, yours or someone else's, and even then he tells you he is involving a senior. What comes back is kindness, guidance from scripture, and a free choice: "deliberate on this fully, and then do what you wish to do" (BG 18.63). Pariksit asked the question that mattered, Devahuti described her state honestly, and Rahugana admitted his wrong (SB 5.10.15); each opened a path. Before your next meeting, fill four boxes on paper: sadhana as it is, one doubt, one struggle, one success or plan, and bring them.

## S2. Key ideas

- Two of the six loving exchanges between devotees are confidential speech (NOI 4).
- Arjuna's way: what is happening, what is behind it, what I am asking (BG 2.7).
- Inquire with submission; neither blind following nor argument (BG 4.34).
- What friendship opens, friendship keeps (BG 18.64); the one limit is safety.
- Advice, not orders: "deliberate on this fully, and then do what you wish to do" (BG 18.63).
- Doubts, struggles, successes and plans all belong in the meeting; hidden doubts grow.

## S3. Verses to remember

- **NOI 4:** "…revealing one's mind in confidence, inquiring confidentially… are the six symptoms of love shared by one devotee and another." *For me:* opening up is not weakness; it is one of the signs of love.
- **BG 2.7:** "Now I am confused about my duty… I am asking You to tell me for certain what is best for me. Now I am Your disciple, and a soul surrendered unto You. Please instruct me." *For me:* say the real thing, then ask.
- **BG 18.64:** "Because you are My very dear friend, I am speaking to you My supreme instruction, the most confidential knowledge of all…" *For me:* confidence flows both ways between friends.

## S4. Words to know

- **Facilitator:** the senior devotee who guides you one-to-one; a friend and guide, not your spiritual master.
- **Guhyam akhyati prcchati:** "reveals the mind in confidence, inquires confidentially" (NOI 4).
- **Sadhana:** your daily practice (rounds, reading, hearing, principles), recorded on the sadhana card.
- **False ego:** identifying with a self-image that must be protected; the reason hearts stay closed.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Will what I tell my facilitator stay private?** Yes: not repeated to other students, not used as an example, not passed to your family, never used to shame you. The one limit is safety (self-harm, abuse, harm to someone), and even then he will tell you he is involving a senior.
2. **Can't I sort things out with Krsna directly?** Do; and also use a second pair of eyes. The mind that needs training is the mind doing the judging (BG 6.5). Arjuna had Krsna beside him and still asked (BG 2.7).
3. **Isn't speaking about my doubts disloyal?** No. Hidden doubts grow; spoken to the right person they become questions. BG 4.34 condemns blind following as much as argument.
4. **I did something I'm ashamed of. Will I be dropped?** No. The response to a disclosed struggle is kindness and guidance (BG 4.34 purport). Rahugana's admission of his mistake (SB 5.10.15) began his learning.
5. **Should I mention problems with other devotees?** Yes, to resolve them, speaking truthfully, pleasingly and without agitation (BG 17.15); not to unload.

## S6. Check your understanding

2. What three things does Arjuna say in BG 2.7?
3. According to BG 4.34, what two things are condemned?
4. What does confidentiality in a one-to-one cover, and what is its one limit?
5. In BG 18.63, what does Krsna tell Arjuna to do after hearing the confidential knowledge?
6. Give one reason from the "why hearts stay closed" list and its remedy.

**Answers:** 1. Giving charity, accepting charity, revealing the mind in confidence, inquiring confidentially, accepting prasadam, offering prasadam (NOI 4). 2. What is happening (confused, lost composure), what is behind it (miserly weakness), what he asks (instruct me) (BG 2.7). 3. Blind following and absurd inquiries (BG 4.34 purport). 4. Everything you say about yourself; the limit is safety (yours or another's). 5. "Deliberate on this fully, and then do what you wish to do" (BG 18.63). 6. Any from the list (e.g. not wanting to admit a mistake); remedy: say the hardest item first at the next meeting.
