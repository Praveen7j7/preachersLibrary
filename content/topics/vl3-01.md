# Art of Self Management

> **VL3 · 180 min · review: sources-checked.** The same title is taught at VL1 as [VL1-09](vl1-09.md). This page **builds on that page** and
> teaches only what is new at VL3: self-management *inside a devotional routine* (four rounds, reading, hearing, the four principles,
> a weekly sadhana card and a facilitator). IYF's material for this row is the VOICE deck *Self Management* (Radheshyam Das;
> `research/iyf-materials/vl3-01/`); its scriptural core (BG 6.17, NOI 1, "mind: friend or enemy", mantra meditation) is used; its
> management frameworks are labelled as the deck's, and its unsourced slides are left out (O). No special sensitivity.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

At VL1 this title is a bridge talk: a corporate subject with the Gita's picture of the mind, and no demand for practice. At VL3 the
listeners already chant and read a little, and they have met the real problem: *they know the routine and still miss it*. The Gita's
sixth chapter was written for exactly this stage. Arjuna's complaint (BG 6.34) comes **after** Krsna has described the practice
(6.10–32), and Krsna's reply (6.35) names the two things that work: suitable practice and detachment. The purport to 6.35 says what
that practice is in this age: hearing about Krsna, which detaches the mind from everything else. So VL3's "self-management" is not a
new set of tips; it is learning to run one's sadhana as the management system itself.

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|

| VL2 | Mind, senses and the four regulated areas met as ideas; first experiments with chanting and reading; no accountability tools yet |
| **VL3-01** (here) | **What is new:** (1) the Gita's own "suitable practice" is devotional: hearing and chanting (6.35 purport; 9.14; 12.9); (2) regulation is anchored in prasadam and sadhana, not in diet rules (6.17 purport); (3) *bringing the mind back* (6.26) as a skill learnt in japa and used all day; (4) the six urges (NOI 1) as the VL3 checklist; (5) higher taste, not suppression (2.59); (6) accountability: the sadhana card and the facilitator as the "manager’s report" |
| SPS | Steadiness when practice gets hard; keeping standards without drifting |

**Not done here:**
- Repeating VL1-09's full exposition. Recap in ten minutes (G), then move on.
- A diet, sleep or productivity programme. The VOICE deck's sleep arithmetic ("9 pm to 12 = 6 hrs"), "fill the stomach 50% food,
  25% water, 25% air" and food lists are not scripture; the stomach rule may be given as a *traditional saying*, nothing more (O).
- Testimonials and brain-wave claims about chanting (deck slides 47–51). Unsourced; left out (O).
- Teaching the ten offences or the details of japa technique: that is VL3-04.
- Moral verdicts on "today’s youth" (the Preaching Encyclopedia chapter opens with them). Teach regulation as a skill.

## C. Central message

The mind can be trained to be a friend, and the Gita's training method for this age is a daily practice of hearing and chanting
inside regulated habits (BG 6.35 and purport; 6.17; 9.14). Self-management at VL3 means running that routine honestly: fix a few
things, do them daily, bring the mind back when it wanders (BG 6.26), and report to someone (the sadhana card, the facilitator).

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. Restate VL1-09's core in two minutes (BG 6.5–6; 3.42; 6.16–17; 6.34–35) and say what VL3 adds.
2. Explain why the Gita calls hearing and chanting the "suitable practice" for controlling the mind (BG 6.35 purport; 12.9), and
   why a higher taste works where suppression fails (BG 2.59).
3. Describe the skill of BG 6.26 (notice the mind has wandered, bring it back) and where in their day they already practise it.
4. Name the six urges of NOI 1 and identify the one that most disturbs their own routine.
5. Write a one-week routine with three fixed points (rounds, reading or hearing, one regulated habit) and name who will see their
   sadhana card.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** BG 6.5–6 (recap), BG 6.35 with purport, BG 6.26, BG 6.17 with purport, BG 2.59, NOI 1 and BG 12.9 carry the talk. The rest
answer questions.

**BG 6.5**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/5/
> "One must deliver himself with the help of his mind, and not degrade himself. The mind is the friend of the conditioned soul, and his enemy as well."
- **Teaching:** The same mind lifts or lowers; the responsibility to use it well is ours.
- **Use:** recap (VL1-09).

**BG 6.6**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/6/
> "For him who has conquered the mind, the mind is the best of friends; but for one who has failed to do so, his mind will remain the greatest enemy."
- **Purport point:** The purpose of yoga practice is to control the mind so that it becomes a friend; as long as the mind is an
  unconquered enemy "one has to serve the dictations of lust, anger, avarice, illusion, etc."; when it is conquered one agrees to
  follow the Lord within; for one who takes to Krsna consciousness directly this follows "automatically".
- **Application (preacher's):** The purport already points past willpower to devotional practice. That is the VL3 bridge.
- **Use:** recap and bridge.

**BG 3.42**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/42/
> "The working senses are superior to dull matter; mind is higher than the senses; intelligence is still higher than the mind; and he [the soul] is even higher than the intelligence."
- **Use:** recap (the chain of command diagram of VL1-09 N).

**BG 6.16–17**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/16/ and /17/
> 6.16: "There is no possibility of one’s becoming a yogī, O Arjuna, if one eats too much or eats too little, sleeps too much or does not sleep enough."
> 6.17: "He who is regulated in his habits of eating, sleeping, recreation and work can mitigate all material pains by practicing the yoga system."
- **Purport point (6.17):** Eating "can be regulated only when one is practiced to take and accept prasādam"; sleeping is kept to a
  minimum because a Krsna conscious person "cannot bear to pass a minute of his life without being engaged in the service of the
  Lord" (the examples are Rupa Gosvami and Haridasa Thakura); work is regulated because it is connected with Krsna's interest.
- **Application (preacher's):** At VL1 this verse was "balance". At VL3 the purport shows *what* anchors the balance: prasadam
  regulates eating, sadhana regulates sleep (a fixed rising time for japa), service regulates work. Do not present Rupa Gosvami's
  two hours of sleep as a target for students; present it as the direction, not the dose.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 6.26**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/26/
> "From wherever the mind wanders due to its flickering and unsteady nature, one must certainly withdraw it and bring it back under the control of the Self."
- **Purport point:** The mind is flickering by nature; the one who controls it is a *gosvāmī*, the one controlled by it a *go-dāsa*;
  the way to full control is engaging purified senses in Krsna's service.
- **Teaching:** The instruction is not "never wander" but "bring it back", every time.
- **Application (preacher's):** This is the single most useful skill of the whole topic, and japa is its daily gym: notice, return;
  notice, return. A person who does this for four rounds every morning is practising the same movement they need at a study desk
  or when a phone lights up. (VL3-04 teaches the japa side in detail.)
- **Use:** in the talk; the core skill.

**BG 6.34–35**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/34/ and /35/
> 6.34: "The mind is restless, turbulent, obstinate and very strong, O Kṛṣṇa, and to subdue it, I think, is more difficult than controlling the wind."
> 6.35: "Lord Śrī Kṛṣṇa said: O mighty-armed son of Kuntī, it is undoubtedly very difficult to curb the restless mind, but it is possible by suitable practice and by detachment."
- **Purport point (6.35):** Krsna accepts the difficulty. "What is that practice?" In this age the strict yoga rules cannot be
  followed, but "by the practice of Kṛṣṇa consciousness" one engages in nine kinds of devotional service, "the first and foremost"
  being hearing about Krsna, "a very powerful transcendental method for purging the mind of all misgivings"; the more one hears, the
  more one is detached from what draws the mind away; the purport compares it to a hungry man's satisfaction with each morsel and
  to curing a disease "by expert treatment and appropriate diet".
- **Application (preacher's):** This purport is the hinge of the VL3 page: the Gita's own "suitable practice" is the routine the
  participants have begun. Say it plainly: "Your reading and hearing time *is* the mind-control programme."
- **Use:** in the talk; the hinge.

**BG 2.59**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/59/
> "Though the embodied soul may be restricted from sense enjoyment, the taste for sense objects remains. But, ceasing such engagements by experiencing a higher taste, he is fixed in consciousness."
- **Purport point:** Restriction by rules is like a patient's diet: he obeys but does not lose the taste; such restrictions "are only
  good until one actually has a taste for Kṛṣṇa consciousness", after which one "automatically loses his taste for pale things".
- **Application (preacher's):** Explains why VL1-style regulation alone frays after a few weeks and why the routine must include
  things one can come to *enjoy* (kirtan, reading, prasadam, association), not only things one gives up.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 2.64–65**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/64/ and /65/
> 2.64: "But a person free from all attachment and aversion and able to control his senses through regulative principles of freedom can obtain the complete mercy of the Lord."
> 2.65: "For one thus satisfied [in Kṛṣṇa consciousness], the threefold miseries of material existence exist no longer; in such satisfied consciousness, one’s intelligence is soon well established."
- **Teaching:** "Regulative principles of freedom" is the Gita's own phrase for regulation (the next topic, VL3-02, takes its title
  from it); the fruit is satisfaction and a steady intelligence.
- **Use:** in the talk, as the bridge to VL3-02.

**BG 12.9–10**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/12/9/ and /10/
> 12.9: "My dear Arjuna, O winner of wealth, if you cannot fix your mind upon Me without deviation, then follow the regulative principles of bhakti-yoga. In this way develop a desire to attain Me."
> 12.10: "If you cannot practice the regulations of bhakti-yoga, then just try to work for Me, because by working for Me you will come to the perfect stage."
- **Purport point (12.9):** Two processes: for one who already has attachment to Krsna, and for one who has not, who follows
  "prescribed rules and regulations" to reach that attachment; "bhakti-yoga is the purification of the senses"; the regulations are
  followed "under the guidance of an expert spiritual master" and begin with rising early.
- **Application (preacher's):** The Gita itself grades the practice to the person. A VL3 participant is at 12.9: not yet absorbed,
  so following a routine under guidance. 12.10 (work for Krsna) is the honest fallback for a week when the routine collapses: take
  one service.
- **Use:** in the talk and for K.4.

**BG 9.14**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/9/14/
> "Always chanting My glories, endeavoring with great determination, bowing down before Me, these great souls perpetually worship Me with devotion."
- **Purport point:** The mahatma is always engaged in glorifying the Lord, "hearing and chanting about Viṣṇu"; "firm determination"
  is named; the regulated observances such as Ekadasi are "offered by the great ācāryas for those who are actually interested".
- **Use:** in the talk: determination plus chanting, the two words of the VL3 KRA "increment in the chanting rounds".

**BG 2.41**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/41/
> "Those who are on this path are resolute in purpose, and their aim is one. O beloved child of the Kurus, the intelligence of those who are irresolute is many-branched."
- **Purport point:** *Vyavasāyātmikā* intelligence is "a strong faith that by Kṛṣṇa consciousness one will be elevated to the
  highest perfection"; the purport cites CC Madhya 22.62 on faith; service "is best practiced under the able guidance of a spiritual
  master".
- **Application (preacher's):** "Many-branched" intelligence is the student with five apps, three plans and no routine. One aim,
  one routine.
- **Use:** in the talk (H) and M.

**NOI 1**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/1/
> "A sober person who can tolerate the urge to speak, the mind’s demands, the actions of anger and the urges of the tongue, belly and genitals is qualified to make disciples all over the world."
- **Context:** Rupa Gosvami's first instruction; the deck's slide 24 lists these six urges.
- **Purport point:** The purport opens with Pariksit's question (SB 6.1.9–10): why do people atone and sin again, like an elephant
  that bathes and then throws dust on itself? Experience is gathered by hearing (the intelligent) or by seeing, for example seeing
  a thief punished (the less intelligent).
- **Application (preacher's):** The six urges are the VL3 self-audit: which one breaks my routine (speech: late-night chats; belly:
  heavy dinners that kill the morning; anger: a quarrel at home that eats the evening)? The verse's "qualified to make disciples" is
  the goal statement; at VL3 teach the six urges as the checklist and leave the qualification as the horizon.
- **Use:** in the talk and in the workshop (G).

**BG 17.15–16**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/17/15/ and /16/
> 17.15: "Austerity of speech consists in speaking words that are truthful, pleasing, beneficial, and not agitating to others, and also in regularly reciting Vedic literature."
> 17.16: "And satisfaction, simplicity, gravity, self-control and purification of one’s existence are the austerities of the mind."
- **Note:** The VOICE deck (slide 20) prints 17.15's text under the number "BG 17.14". Cite it as 17.15.
- **Application (preacher's):** "Regularly reciting Vedic literature" puts daily reading inside austerity of speech; the two verses
  cover the "speech" and "mind" urges of NOI 1.
- **Use:** background and K.

**BG 6.18–19**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/18/ and /19/
> 6.19: "As a lamp in a windless place does not waver, so the transcendentalist, whose mind is controlled, remains always steady in his meditation on the transcendent Self."
- **Use:** the closing image (N); the goal, stated without pretending anyone in the room is there.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The problem has moved.** At VL1 the problem was "I don’t know how to manage myself". At VL3 it is "I know what to do each
   morning and still don't do it". The Gita places Arjuna's protest (6.34) after the method (6.10–32), which means the method is
   expected to be hard to keep. Krsna's reply does not lower the method; it names two supports: *abhyāsa* (suitable practice) and
   *vairāgya* (detachment).
2. **What the practice is.** The 6.35 purport says it: in this age, the nine processes of devotion, first hearing about Krsna. The
   same purport explains the mechanism: hearing detaches the mind from what pulls it elsewhere, "just like the feeling of satisfaction
   a hungry man has". So the VL3 routine (rounds, reading, hearing) is not a religious add-on to self-management; it is the Gita's
   self-management method. The VOICE deck reaches the same point at slides 27 and 44 ("mantra meditation for managing the mind").
3. **Why willpower alone frays.** BG 2.59: rules restrict the act, not the taste. A restricted taste returns under pressure (exams,
   loneliness, a free evening). Only a higher taste displaces it. Hence the routine must contain things the participant can come to
   relish: kirtan, a chapter that moves them, prasadam, Sunday association. A routine of pure prohibitions is a VL1 routine.
4. **The one skill that transfers.** BG 6.26: the mind wanders; bring it back. The verse assumes wandering. A chanter who returns the
   mind to the sound a hundred times in four rounds has done a hundred repetitions of the exact movement needed at a desk. This makes
   japa the training ground and the rest of the day the field. (How to chant is VL3-04; here only the transfer is taught.)
5. **Anchored regulation.** The 6.17 purport anchors three of the four areas in devotion: prasadam for eating, service for work, and
   sleep kept minimal because the day is full of service (Haridasa Thakura would not sleep before finishing his rounds). It says
   nothing specific about recreation. *Application (preacher's):* for VL3 that means: fix a rising time because the rounds need it; eat what has been offered because it changes
   what one wants to eat; let recreation be partly kirtan and association. Regulation stops being a list and becomes a shape of the day.
6. **Graded, not all-or-nothing.** BG 12.9–10 grades the practice: if you cannot fix the mind, follow the regulations; if you cannot
   follow the regulations this week, work for Krsna. A participant who missed every round in exam week has not failed; 12.10 gives
   them a floor: one service, then climb back.
7. **The six urges as the audit.** NOI 1 names speech, mind, anger, tongue, belly, genitals. Each participant can find the one that
   breaks their routine most often. The purport's elephant (bathing, then dust) is a kind description of every "I’ll restart Monday".
8. **Accountability is part of the method.** The purports to 12.9 and 2.41 both say the practice is best done "under the guidance" of
   someone. IYF's structure supplies it: the weekly sadhana card and the meetings with a facilitator (batch-criteria §3). A card
   is not surveillance; it is the manager's own report. *Application (preacher's):* a routine that nobody sees is a routine that
   dissolves quietly.
9. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - "VL3 self-management means more rules." It means a *different kind* of practice (hearing, chanting), with a few rules as scaffolding.
   - "If I had more willpower I’d be fine." The Gita assumes the mind is strong (6.34) and prescribes practice, not heroics.
   - "Rupa Gosvami slept two hours, so I should sleep less." The purport gives him as an ideal of engagement, not a prescription for
     students. BG 6.16 forbids sleeping too little.
   - "The sadhana card is a score." It is a mirror. Honest zeros are more useful than flattering fours (VL3-08 covers confidence).
   - "Spiritual routine replaces medical help." Sleep disorders, anxiety and appetite problems can have medical causes; encourage
     professional help alongside sadhana (pastoral guidance, not scripture).
10. **Pitch for VL3.** Practical and encouraging. Use practice terms (*japa*, *sadhana*, *prasadam*, *abhyāsa*, *vairāgya*) and
    define each once. Scripture is instruction for practice, not a debate. Every participant should leave with something they will do
    tomorrow morning.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet the group, say your name, and say today is a practical session: no one is asked to report anything they do not choose to share.
2. Write "First 30 minutes of my day" on the board.
3. Ask H.2 question 1; take two or three answers and thank each by name.
4. Say that nearly everyone knows what they meant to do; the session is about the gap.
5. Give the transition line below and begin the recap.

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the group's own lives)
1. What did you do in the first thirty minutes of your day yesterday: phone first, or something you planned first?
2. Think of one good habit you kept for a month. What made it possible: a person, a place, a time?
3. When you plan to rise early or practise something and miss it, what usually gets in the way?

- **Opening question:** "Describe the first thirty minutes of your day yesterday. Phone first, or beads first?" Take two or three
  answers without comment. "Almost everyone here knows what they *meant* to do. Today is about the gap, and the Gita has a lot to say
  about it."
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** Ritesh, VL3, chants four rounds on Sundays and Mondays, two on Tuesday, none by Thursday, and
  writes "4" on the card anyway. He is not lazy; his routine has no anchor and no witness.
- **Transition:** "Arjuna made the same complaint (6.34) after hearing the whole method. Krsna did not lower the method. He named two
  supports."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** exams and placements, hostel nights, family pressure, and a real wish to chant that keeps
  losing to a phone. The topic gives them a method, not a scolding.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **The mind is trainable (recap).** BG 6.5–6; 3.42. *Example:* the same mind that scrolls at 1 am can hold a mantra at 6 am; it has
   been trained one way. *Transition:* what trains it the other way?
2. **The Gita's practice is devotional.** BG 6.35 purport; 12.9; 9.14. *Example:* a hungry man's satisfaction with each morsel (the
   purport's own image): each day of hearing gives a little more taste. *Transition:* why not just rules?
3. **Taste beats restriction.** BG 2.59. *Example:* the patient on a diet still wants the sweets; the person who found better food
   stops wanting them. *Transition:* but the mind will still wander.
4. **Bring it back.** BG 6.26. *Example:* in four rounds the mind leaves a hundred times; a hundred returns is a hundred repetitions of
   the skill. *Transition:* what breaks the routine most?
5. **Audit the six urges.** NOI 1; 17.15–16. *Example:* a 1 am chat (speech) or a heavy late dinner (belly) kills the 6 am round.
   *Transition:* and anchor the four areas.
6. **Anchor, don't just balance.** BG 6.17 purport (prasadam, rising for japa, service). *Example:* "I eat prasadam" changes the
   question from "what do I avoid?" to "what do I offer?" *Transition:* who sees it?
7. **Report.** BG 12.9 and 2.41 purports ("under guidance"); the sadhana card; the facilitator. *Example:* a routine nobody sees
   dissolves quietly. *Transition:* to the takeaway (P) and the "regulative principles of freedom" (2.64; VL3-02).

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"I know all this from VL1. What’s different?"**
   - Honest answer: the verses are the same, the practice is new. At VL1 we said "regulate". Now the Gita's own purport says *what*
     the practice is: hearing and chanting (6.35 purport). Your rounds are the method, not an extra.
2. **"I chant, and my mind still wanders the whole time. Isn’t that failure?"**
   - BG 6.26 assumes it wanders. The instruction is to bring it back. Each return is the practice. VL3-04 will give the method in
     detail. *Application (preacher's):* count returns, not failures.
3. **"Why do I need a sadhana card? Isn’t that for the facilitator’s statistics?"**
   - The purports to 12.9 and 2.41 say regulated practice is best done under guidance. The card is how guidance sees the week. Write
     honest numbers; a true zero helps your facilitator help you, a false four helps no one (VL3-08).
4. **"Exam week destroyed my routine. Do I start from zero?"**
   - BG 12.10 is Krsna's own fallback: if you cannot keep the regulations this week, work for Me. Take one service, keep one round,
     climb back. (VL2-02 made the same point from BG 6.41–45: nothing is lost.)
5. **"Isn’t a fixed early rising time just another rule? You said taste matters more."**
   - Both are in the Gita: rules as scaffolding (12.9), taste as the real cure (2.59). The 6.17 purport ties sleep to service and
     chanting; *application (preacher's):* you rise early *for* something you come to enjoy. If the rounds are joyless, the rising time will not last; fix the taste too
     (kirtan, a book you love, association).
6. **"Rupa Gosvami slept two hours. Should I?"**
   - No. BG 6.16 forbids too little sleep. The purport gives him as an ideal of engagement, not a prescription for students with
     exams. Keep a fixed, sufficient sleep.
7. **"I struggle with anxiety or insomnia. Will sadhana fix it?"**
   - Sadhana helps many people, and it is compatible with professional help. If sleep, mood or appetite are seriously disturbed,
     see a doctor or counsellor as well; the free national helpline Tele-MANAS is 14416. This is pastoral guidance, not scripture.
8. **"The deck says chanting puts the brain in an 'alpha zone' and quotes industrialists. Can I use that?"**
   - Not without sources. The deck gives none. The Gita's own case (6.35 purport; 2.59) does not need them.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The Thursday collapse.** Priya chants four rounds on Sunday and Monday, two on Tuesday, none on Thursday and Friday; the
pattern repeats every week. She reads only on Sunday. She says, "I’m just not disciplined."
- **Principles:** BG 6.26 (bring it back, not "never slip"); 6.17 purport (anchor rising in japa); NOI 1 (which urge: late-night
  calls on Wednesday); 12.9 purport (under guidance).
- **Questions:** What happens on Wednesday night? Which of the four areas is unanchored? What would three *fixed* points look like
  for her? Who sees her card?
- **Suggested analysis:** The problem is structural, not moral: a late Wednesday (speech urge) removes Thursday's morning. Fix the
  night to fix the morning; tell the facilitator the real numbers.
- **Takeaway:** Diagnose the urge, anchor the habit, report honestly.

**Case 2: The all-or-nothing chanter.** Arjun set a target of eight rounds after Sankalpa camp, managed it for nine days, missed one
day, and has not chanted for three weeks "because what’s the point of two rounds?"
- **Principles:** BG 2.41 (one aim, not many branches: his aim became the number, not Krsna); 12.9–10 (graded practice); 6.35 (practice
  and detachment, including detachment from one's own record).
- **Questions:** What was his real aim? What does 12.10 offer him this week? What number would he actually keep for a month?
- **Takeaway:** Choose a number you will keep, and keep it; increase from stability (the VL3 KRA is *increment*, not a jump).

**M.2 Activities** (engagement block; pick two or three)

*Option A: Anchor grid in threes (30 min)*
1. 3 min: form groups of three; each takes the N grid.
2. 10 min: each person names (a) the urge that most often breaks their routine, (b) one anchor from the 6.17 purport they will use. Sharing is optional.
3. 8 min: each writes three fixed points for next week (a time, a place, a quantity).
4. 5 min: pairs exchange cards: "I'll ask you next Sunday."
5. 4 min: debrief with 6.35 and 6.26.

*Option B: Notice-and-return drill (20 min)*
1. 2 min: explain the loop: wander, notice, return (N diagram).
2. 5 min: everyone chants quietly; each raises a finger every time they notice the mind has gone, then returns.
3. 5 min: pairs say what pulled the mind (no judgement; a pull is information).
4. 8 min: the leader draws the most common pulls on the board; the group picks one change for the next japa (move the phone, change the seat, chant earlier).

*Option C: Honest-card circle (25 min)*
1. 5 min: the leader shows an invented card (Ritesh in H: "4" written on days he chanted two) and asks what the card could show instead.
2. 10 min: in threes, each says one number they would put on a truthful card for last week, or passes.
3. 10 min: the group lists three reasons a facilitator wants a truthful number (to help, to see patterns, to not be surprised). Thank each contribution.

**M.3 Reflection**
- **Reflection:** Which urge most often breaks my routine? Which of the four areas (eating, sleeping, recreation, work) has no anchor in my sadhana?
- **Application (private, optional):** The three fixed points of my next week, and the person who will see my card.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

At VL1 we learnt that the mind can be a friend or an enemy (BG 6.5–6) and that regulating the four areas of life (eating, sleeping, recreation and work) helps (BG 6.17). Sleep too little or too much and the practice suffers (BG 6.16), so keep a fixed, sufficient sleep and a fixed rising time. Now that you chant (repeat the Hare Krsna mantra on beads, called japa) and read a little every day, the Gita's next lesson is that the practice that trains the mind is not willpower but hearing and chanting about Krsna, done daily (BG 6.35 and its purport). Rules restrict an act but not the taste for it; a higher taste is what makes a change last (BG 2.59).

The mind will wander; the instruction is to bring it back every time (BG 6.26), and japa is where you practise that. Check which of the six urges (NOI 1: speech, the mind's demands, anger, and the tongue, belly and genitals) breaks your routine, and anchor your habits in your sadhana (daily devotional practice).

A sadhana card is a weekly record of your practice. Practice is best done under guidance (BG 12.9 purport), so let your facilitator, an experienced devotee who supports you, see an honest card. If a week is too hard to follow in full, BG 12.10 gives a floor: work for Krsna in some small way. You have not failed; you climb back.

## S2. Key ideas

- Your daily reading, hearing and chanting *are* the Gita's mind-management method (BG 6.35 purport).
- Taste beats restriction: build in things you can come to enjoy (BG 2.59).
- The mind wanders; bring it back. Each return is the practice (BG 6.26).
- Six urges to watch: speech, the mind's demands, anger, tongue, belly, genitals (NOI 1).
- Anchor the four areas: prasadam, a rising time for japa, kirtan and reading, one service (BG 6.17 purport).
- A routine someone sees lasts longer: the sadhana card and your facilitator.

## S3. Verses to remember

- **BG 6.35:** It is "very difficult to curb the restless mind, but it is possible by suitable practice and by detachment". *For me:* difficulty is expected; practice is the answer.
- **BG 6.26:** "From wherever the mind wanders… one must certainly withdraw it and bring it back under the control of the Self." *For me:* don't count failures, count returns.
- **BG 2.59:** "…ceasing such engagements by experiencing a higher taste, he is fixed in consciousness." *For me:* add something I enjoy, not only something I give up.
- **NOI 1:** A sober person tolerates "the urge to speak, the mind’s demands, the actions of anger and the urges of the tongue, belly and genitals". *For me:* find my one weak urge.

## S4. Words to know

- **Sadhana:** daily spiritual practice (chanting, reading, hearing, following the principles).
- **Japa:** chanting the Hare Krsna mantra softly on beads; one round is 108 mantras.
- **Abhyasa / vairagya:** practice / detachment, the two supports Krsna names in BG 6.35.
- **Prasadam:** food offered to Krsna before eating.
- **Sadhana card:** your weekly record of rounds, reading, hearing and principles, shared with your facilitator.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **I heard this at VL1. What's new?** The verses are the same; the practice is new. The Gita's own "suitable practice" is hearing and chanting (BG 6.35 purport). Your rounds are the method, not an extra.
2. **My mind wanders during japa. Am I failing?** No. BG 6.26 expects it to wander and tells you to bring it back. Each return is a repetition of the skill.
3. **Why fill a sadhana card?** Practice is best done under guidance (BG 12.9 purport). The card lets your facilitator see your real week. Honest numbers help; flattering ones don't.
4. **Exam week wiped out my routine. Start from zero?** No. BG 12.10 gives a floor: if you can't keep the regulations this week, do one service for Krsna and keep one round. Then climb back.
5. **Should I sleep two hours like Rupa Gosvami?** No. BG 6.16 says too little sleep stops yoga. Keep a fixed, sufficient sleep and a fixed rising time.
6. **I have anxiety or trouble sleeping. Will chanting fix it?** Chanting helps many people and goes well with professional help. If sleep or mood is seriously disturbed, see a doctor or counsellor too (Tele-MANAS: 14416).

## S6. Check your understanding

3. What does BG 6.26 tell you to do when the mind wanders?

**Answers:** 1. (b) (BG 6.35). 2. Hearing about Krsna (BG 6.35 purport). 3. Withdraw it and bring it back under control (BG 6.26). 4. The urge to

higher taste removes it (BG 2.59). 6. False: it says "just try to work for Me" (BG 12.10).
