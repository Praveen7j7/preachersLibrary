# Sermons of Avadhuta brahmana to Prahlada maharaj

> Status line: BSS · 120 min · drafted · not sensitive. Builds on bss-08 (which names the avadhuta's gurus but leaves the python and the bee to this page).

## A. Topic overview
| | |
|---|---|
| Batch / level | BSS, brahmacari training |
| Suggested duration | 120 minutes |
| Preparation time | About 2 hours (read SB 7.13 once in full) |
| Difficulty | Intermediate |
| Prerequisites | bss-08 |
| Audience | Brahmacaris, and anyone ready to hear about renunciation. *Python-like* = living on whatever comes without effort, as a python lies still until food arrives |
| Teaching objective | Hear the avadhuta's own account of why he stopped chasing material happiness, and see how a brahmacari takes that attitude in a way suited to a student: simple, dependent on Krsna, and ruled by the guru |

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level
The brahmacari sees a standard of detachment ahead of him. SB 7.13 shows it through the mouth of a perfected soul, and
Prahlada, a great devotee, honours him. This page keeps the brahmacari from two errors: despising the standard, and
copying its outward form before the heart is ready.

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| VL1 | One image: the deer and the covered well (SB 7.13.29) |
| VL2 | Why material happiness fails (SB 7.13.26-30) |
| VL3 | Sannyasa rules in SB 7.13.1-10 as background |
| SPS | The sermon as a whole and Narada's setting |
| DPS | The relation to SB 11.7-9 (bss-08) |
| BSS | This page: the avadhuta's story of his own change, the python and the bee, and the brahmacari's way to follow the spirit |

Not done here: the sannyasa rules in detail (SB 7.13.1-9), the full list of gurus (bss-08).

## C. Central message
The avadhuta stopped running after happiness outside because he saw that real happiness is within the soul; a
brahmacari follows by simplicity, contentment with what Krsna sends, and obedience, not by copying the avadhuta's outward
behaviour.

## D. Learning outcomes
Participants can:
1. Retell the meeting of Prahlada and the avadhuta in order.
2. Explain the avadhuta's reason for stopping material endeavour (SB 7.13.26-27).
3. Explain the deer-and-well image and apply it (SB 7.13.29) to the search for happiness.
4. Explain what the python and the bee each teach (SB 7.13.35-37).
5. State how a brahmacari lives the spirit without imitating the form.

## E. Scriptural foundation
**SB 7.13.1**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/13/1/
> "A person able to cultivate spiritual knowledge should renounce all material connections…"
- **Context:** Narada Muni is instructing Maharaja Yudhisthira on the behavior of one in the renounced order; he then gives a historical example (7.13.11).
- **Teaching:** Merely keeping the body inhabitable, he should travel from place to place, one night in each village.
- **Use:** background.

**SB 7.13.10**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/13/10/
> "Although a saintly person may not expose himself to the vision of human society, by his behavior his purpose is disclosed."
- **Teaching:** He may present himself like a restless child, and although he is a great speaker, like a dumb man.
- **Use:** background; explains the avadhuta's unusual appearance.

**SB 7.13.14**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/13/14/
> "Neither by that saintly person’s activities, by his bodily features, by his words nor by the symptoms of his varṇāśrama status could people understand whether he was the same person they had known."
- **Use:** in the talk; warns against judging a saint by appearance.

**SB 7.13.15**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/13/15/
> "The advanced devotee Prahlāda Mahārāja duly worshiped and offered obeisances to the saintly person who had adopted a python’s means of livelihood."
- **Teaching:** Prahlada touched his head to the saint's lotus feet and then inquired.
- **Application (preacher's):** An advanced devotee asks with respect. A brahmacari's questions to seniors are made in the same mood.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 7.13.24**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/13/24/
> "Because of insatiable material desires, I was being carried away by the waves of material nature’s laws…"
- **Teaching:** The avadhuta describes his past: struggling for existence in many forms of life.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 7.13.26**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/13/26/
> "In this human form of life, men and women unite for the sensual pleasure of sex, but by actual experience we have observed that none of them are happy."
- **Teaching:** He therefore stopped taking part in materialistic activities.
- **Purport point:** The purport quotes Prahlada's words that the happiness of sex life and household life is trifling; married persons are happy for some time, but dissension and separation follow.
- **Purport point (continued):** "Marriage is recommended to give men and women a concession for restricted sex life"; "Unfortunately, especially in this age, men and women unite for unrestricted sexual enjoyment. Thus they are victimized, being obliged to take rebirth in the forms of animals to fulfill their animalistic propensities."
- **Application (preacher's):** The saint speaks from experience ("by actual experience we have observed"); for a brahmacari it supports the choice already made.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 7.13.27**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/13/27/
> "Material sense enjoyment is simply an imagination."
- **Teaching:** The soul's real form of life is spiritual happiness, reached by stopping materialistic activities.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 7.13.29**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/13/29/
> "Just as a deer, because of ignorance, cannot see the water within a well covered by grass, but runs after water elsewhere"
- **Teaching:** The living entity, covered by a material body, does not see the happiness within himself and runs after happiness in the material world.
- **Purport point:** Understanding one's real identity as spirit leads to understanding Krsna and the real happiness exchanged between Krsna and oneself.
- **Use:** in the talk; the central picture.

**SB 7.13.30**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/13/30/
> "The living entity tries to achieve happiness and rid himself of the causes of distress"
- **Teaching:** Because bodies are under the control of material nature, his plans are in the end baffled.
- **Use:** background.

**SB 7.13.33**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/13/33/
> "Those who are considered materially powerful and rich are always full of anxieties"
- **Teaching:** Anxieties come from governments, thieves, enemies, family members, time and even themselves.
- **Use:** background; answers "isn't wealth safety?"

**SB 7.13.35**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/13/35/
> "The bee and the python are two excellent spiritual masters"
- **Teaching:** They teach satisfaction with collecting only a little and staying in one place.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 7.13.36**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/13/36/
> "From the bumblebee I have learned to be unattached to accumulating money"
- **Teaching:** Money is as good as honey, and anyone can kill its owner and take it.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 7.13.37**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/13/37/
> "I do not endeavor to get anything, but am satisfied with whatever is achieved in its own way."
- **Teaching:** If he gets nothing, he lies patient and unagitated like a python for many days.
- **Application (preacher's):** For a brahmacari, "without endeavour" is not idleness; it is trust in Krsna while following the guru's order for service and begging, with prasadam as the daily test.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 7.13.45**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/13/45/
> "Prahlāda Mahārāja, you are certainly a self-realized soul and a devotee of the Supreme Lord."
- **Use:** background; the avadhuta speaks to a fit hearer.

**SB 7.13.46**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/13/46/
> "he understood the occupational duties of a perfect person [paramahaṁsa]"
- **Teaching:** Prahlada worshiped the saint, took permission and went home.
- **Use:** closing.

## F. Philosophical explanation
**Principle.** The soul's constitutional position is happy (SB 7.13.27); material endeavour is a search in the wrong place (SB 7.13.29). **Why it matters.** The brahmacari is told to give up what the world values. This sermon gives the reason from a man who knew it. **Connections.** SB 5.5.1 (bss-07) gave the aim; SB 11.8.44 (bss-08) gave Pingala's freedom; here is the same truth told in the first person. **Consequences.** A student who hears only "give up" will feel deprived; a student who hears "the water is within" will look within, through chanting and hearing. **Misunderstandings.** (1) That the python's conduct is a model for a brahmacari: it is the stage of a self-realised sannyasi, and the brahmacari follows his guru's instructions, which include working for the mission. (2) That the sermon despises family: it is a statement of one soul's experience; the householder is also to serve Krsna. (3) That "no endeavour" means no devotional discipline. **Pitch.** Slow and personal; leave silence after SB 7.13.29.

## H. Opening and engagement
**H.1 How to start.** Place a covered bowl of water on a table with a cloth on it. Say: "A deer is thirsty. Where does it go?" Let someone answer, then uncover the bowl. Read SB 7.13.29. Say: "A saint once told a king that we are that deer. Today we will hear who he was."
**H.2 Opening questions.** (1) "What is the thing you thought would make you happy and did not?" (answers heard, not discussed) (2) "What does a person look like who has everything he needs and wants nothing?" (3) "Would you recognise a saint if he did not look like one?"

## I. Core teaching points
**I.1 The saint's account (SB 7.13.24-27).** He was carried by insatiable desire through many lives. In human form he looked at men and women uniting for pleasure and saw, by experience, that none are happy; he therefore stopped. End: he does not say "I am stronger than you", he says "I looked and saw."
**I.2 The deer and the well (SB 7.13.29-31).** The deer runs for water; the water is under the grass at its feet. The soul runs for happiness in the body and world; it is within, in its relationship with Krsna, as the purport says. The plans of every body are baffled (7.13.30). End: the way to uncover the well is chanting and hearing, not stronger search elsewhere.
**I.3 The python and the bee (SB 7.13.35-37).** The bee gathers honey that is taken from it, so the sage does not hoard; the python lies where it is and takes what comes, patient when nothing does. Present as attitudes of contentment and dependence on the Lord. Illustration (preacher's, labelled): a student who begins each day with a plan and ends in worry if it fails; the lesson is to plan for service and accept the result as Krsna's arrangement.
**I.4 How a brahmacari follows.** Not by copying the saint's appearance (SB 7.13.14 says people could not even tell), but by the spirit: simplicity, contentment, respect for seniors as Prahlada shows (SB 7.13.15), and obedience to the guru's service order.

## J. Stories and illustrations
**J.1 Prahlada meets the python-like saint (SB 7.13.11-46; key verse 7.13.29).** Narada tells Yudhisthira of an old conversation. Prahlada, touring with his associates to study the nature of saints, comes to the bank of the Kaveri near Mount Sahya and finds a saint lying like a python. He worships him and asks: why do you lie here, stout, without work, though you are learned? The saint replies: from insatiable desire I wandered in many lives; in this human form I saw that none who pursue sexual enjoyment are happy, so I stopped; the soul's real happiness is spiritual. He mentions the bee and python. Prahlada hears, worships him and leaves. Devotional lesson: Prahlada, himself a pure devotee, receives instruction with humility and honors the saint, showing how Vaishnavas listen to one another.
**J.2 Invented illustration (labelled):** a farmer in a dry district keeps digging new wells on the far side of his field while his own field has a spring under the stones. The farmer is the deer. When a visitor tells him, he starts with one stone at a time. Lesson: the discipline of chanting, which opens the heart's spring.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections
1. **Is this teaching that I should do nothing?** No. SB 7.13.37 describes one in the renounced order at a particular stage; the brahmacari follows his guru's service order.
2. **Isn't this against marriage?** The purport to SB 7.13.26: "Marriage is recommended to give men and women a concession for restricted sex life, which is also recommended in Bhagavad-gītā by the Supreme Personality of Godhead." The verse's point is that unrestricted enjoyment makes no one happy.
3. **Why is the saint lying like a python?** SB 7.13.10 explains that saints may present themselves like a child or a dumb man; the saint was a paramahamsa.
4. **How can I be satisfied with little when I am a student and need money?** The attitude is dependence on Krsna while doing one's duty. See SB 7.13.36 on hoarding.
5. **Is renunciation without devotion enough?** The avadhuta's sermon ends in a devotee's honour; he speaks to Prahlada, a devotee (7.13.45).

## L. Case studies
**L.1 Invented case for discussion.** A brahmacari is offered an internship with a good salary at a company before his decision to join the temple full-time. *Principles:* SB 7.13.29, 7.13.36. *Questions:* what is the covered well here? What would the bee suggest? *Analysis:* ask guide and guru; the vote is not from the avadhuta's example but from the guru's instruction. *Takeaway:* decisions are made in service.
**L.2 Invented case.** A young man sees a sannyasi who does not seem to "do" much and criticises him. *Principles:* SB 7.13.14, 7.13.15. *Questions:* what would Prahlada do? *Takeaway:* approach with respect and questions.

## M. Discussion and reflection
**M.1 Closing check questions.**
1. *Who found the python-like saint?* Prahlada Maharaja (SB 7.13.12-15).
2. *Why did the saint stop material endeavour?* By experience he saw that none are happy in sensual pleasure (SB 7.13.26).
3. *What is the deer's mistake?* Not seeing the water within the covered well and running elsewhere (SB 7.13.29).
4. *What does the bee teach?* Not to accumulate money, which others can take (SB 7.13.36).
5. *What does the python teach?* Satisfaction with what comes and patience (SB 7.13.37).
6. *How does a brahmacari follow?* In the spirit, under the guru's order, not by copying the form.
**M.2 Activities.** (a) *The covered well (10 min):* each draws a well with grass over it and writes on the grass the thing they chase; then writes the one practice (chanting, hearing, service) that clears it. (b) *Prahlada's questions (10 min):* pairs write three respectful questions they would ask a senior devotee. (c) *Role-play (15 min, extension):* Prahlada and the saint; Prahlada asks SB 7.13.16-19, the saint answers with 7.13.26-27; debrief on tone.
**M.3 Reflection.** "What am I running toward that I could find inside?"

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary
Narada Muni told King Yudhisthira about a talk between Prahlada and a saint who lay like a python. Prahlada was touring to study saints. He found one by the river Kaveri who did no work, and he worshiped him and asked why he was so content (SB 7.13.11-20).
The saint answered that he had once been carried by endless desire through many lives. In the human form he saw by experience that no one who seeks sensual pleasure is happy, so he stopped (SB 7.13.24-26). He explained that real happiness is spiritual (SB 7.13.27) and that we are like a deer that runs for water while it stands over a well covered by grass (SB 7.13.29). He said the bee teaches not to hoard (SB 7.13.36) and the python teaches contentment (SB 7.13.37). Prahlada worshiped him and returned home (SB 7.13.46). A brahmacari takes the spirit: simplicity, trust in Krsna and respect for seniors, while serving as the guru asks.

## S2. Key ideas
- Happiness is within, in relation to Krsna.
- Material enjoyment is imagination, not satisfaction.
- The bee teaches not to hoard; the python, contentment.
- Do not judge a saint by appearance.
- Follow the spirit; obey the guru.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **SB 7.13.29**: the deer cannot see the water in the covered well. *For me:* look within through chanting.
- **SB 7.13.36**: be unattached to accumulating money. *For me:* keep life simple.
- **SB 7.13.15**: Prahlada worshiped the saint before asking. *For me:* ask with respect.

## S4. Words to know
- **Avadhuta:** wandering saint.
- **Python-like:** living on what comes.
- **Paramahamsa:** a perfect person.
- **Hoard:** to store up.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *Should I do nothing?* No; follow the guru's service order.
2. *Is the saint against marriage?* He speaks from his own experience.
3. *Why a python?* To show contentment.
4. *Can I copy him?* Take the spirit, not the form.

## S6. Check your understanding
1. Who met the saint? 2. Why did the saint stop? 3. What is the deer's mistake? 4. What does the bee teach? 5. Where is real happiness?

**Answers:** 1. Prahlada (SB 7.13.12-15). 2. He saw none are happy in sensual pleasure (SB 7.13.26). 3. Running elsewhere while the water is under the grass (SB 7.13.29). 4. Not to hoard (SB 7.13.36). 5. In the soul's spiritual life with Krsna (SB 7.13.27, 7.13.29 purport).
