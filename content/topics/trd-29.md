# Priti-sandarbha: prema, the highest goal

> **TRD · up to 180 min · review: drafted.** Group F, advanced Gaudiya siddhanta. The sixth and last of Jiva Gosvami's
> Sat-sandarbhas is the Priti-sandarbha, the "essay on love". It answers the last of the three questions of trd-23: what is
> the goal (prayojana)? The answer, from Srila Prabhupada's books first, is love of God (prema), which stands above
> liberation. **Source note:** the Priti-sandarbha text in `research/books/Jiva_Goswami_Sri_Priti_Sandarbha.txt` is a
> font-scrambled PDF extraction (private-use glyph codes) and could not be read in this session; the book is therefore
> cited only as Srila Prabhupada describes it (CC Madhya 1.43 purport). Any statement about its internal order of
> anucchedas is marked "Requires source review".

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on trd-23 (the map: sambandha, abhidheya, prayojana) and trd-28 (pure bhakti as the only means). Those pages
set the goal in one line; this page studies the goal itself. Builds on dps-18 (bhava and prema as the last two stages):
dps-18 describes how prema appears in the devotee; this page asks why prema is the goal at all, and why liberation,
which the whole of India's religious culture treats as the summit, is not.

A temple resident has given his life for this goal. If he thinks the goal is "to go back to Godhead" in the sense of
escaping the world, his bhajana will quietly become a search for relief, and his preaching will present Krishna
consciousness as one more path to liberation. The Priti-sandarbha, as Srila Prabhupada summarises it, is the acharyas'
systematic answer: the goal is a person, Krishna, and love for Him; liberation is a by-product that a pure devotee does not
even ask for (SB 3.29.13; SB 9.4.67).

trd-48); Goloka as the destination (trd-30, trd-48); the Bhakti-sandarbha's account of the means (trd-28).

## C. Central message

Love of Krishna (prema) is the fifth and final goal of human life, above religiosity, wealth, enjoyment and even
liberation (CC Adi 7.144). It is not a reward for service; it is the service itself, matured into a bond that nothing can
break (CC Madhya 23.7). A pure devotee does not ask for liberation, because liberation already waits on him as a servant
(SB 9.4.67 purport), and he wants only to keep serving, life after life (CC Antya 20.29).

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Explain, from CC Adi 7.141-146, how Lord Caitanya moved Prakasananda's assembly from Brahman to prema as the fifth goal.
2. State Rupa Gosvami's definition of prema (CC Madhya 23.7) and Narada-pancaratra's definition of bhakti (CC Madhya 23.8) in
   their own words, and tell them apart from lust (CC Adi 4.165).
3. Name the five kinds of liberation and show from SB 3.29.13 and SB 9.4.67 why a pure devotee refuses them.
4. Describe what the Priti-sandarbha covers, using only Srila Prabhupada's summary, and say what the book adds to a preacher.
5. Apply the siddhanta to their own life: recognise when bhajana or preaching has slipped into seeking relief, and correct it
   by the Lord's own standard (SB 9.4.63-68).

## E. Scriptural foundation

**CC Adi 7.144** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/adi/7/144/
> "Love of Godhead is so exalted that it is considered to be the fifth goal of human life. By awakening one's love of Godhead, one can attain the platform of conjugal love, tasting it even during the present span of life."
- **Context:** Lord Caitanya at Varanasi, explaining the Vedanta-sutra to Prakasananda Sarasvati and his followers. He has just
  explained sambandha (7.141) and abhidheya (7.142); this verse gives prayojana.
- **Teaching:** The four goals everyone knows (dharma, artha, kama, moksa) are not the end. Prema is a fifth goal above them.
- **Purport point:** "The Māyāvādī philosophers consider the highest goal of perfection to be liberation ( mukti ), which is the fourth perfectional platform." Devotional service "is situated on the platform above liberation". Prabhupada quotes the Lord's
  words to Rupa Gosvami: out of millions of liberated persons, one may become a devotee of Krishna.
- **Use:** in the talk; this is the thesis verse.

**CC Adi 7.141-143, 7.145-146** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/adi/7/141/
- **Teaching (7.141):** One approaches the Lord only by devotional service beginning with hearing. **(7.142):** by regulated
  practice under the spiritual master, dormant love awakens; this is abhidheya. **(7.143):** as love develops, attachment to
  everything else goes. **(7.145):** the Lord becomes submissive to an insignificant devotee; "In reciprocal devotional activities with the Lord, the devotee actually enjoys the transcendental mellow of devotional service." **(7.146):** relationship,
  activity and goal are the subject of every aphorism of the Vedanta-sutra.
- **Purport point (7.145):** Prabhupada cites Bilvamangala Thakura: mukti stands before the devotee prepared to render service.
  "The Māyāvādī philosophers' standard of mukti is very insignificant for a devotee, for by devotional service even the Supreme Personality of Godhead becomes subordinate to him." Example: Krishna drove Arjuna's chariot.
- **Purport point (7.142):** the three steps named: sambandha, abhidheya and "prayojana-siddhi, or fulfillment of the ultimate goal of one's life".
- **Use:** in the talk; the five verses read as one argument (G, segment 2).

**CC Madhya 20.142** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/20/142/
> "The goal of love of Godhead is not to become materially rich or free from material bondage. The real goal is to be situated in devotional service to the Lord and to enjoy transcendental bliss."
- **Context:** Lord Caitanya to Sanatana Gosvami at Varanasi, inside the teaching on the Vedic literature's three subjects.
- **Purport point:** "The goal of devotional service is to be eternally situated in the loving service of the Lord and to enjoy spiritual bliss from that service." Liberation and opulence come by themselves; "One does not have to endeavor separately for opulence."
- **Also in:** CC Madhya 20.143 (Krishna, His service and love of Krishna are "the three great riches of life"); trd-23 uses this verse for the map, so cite it here only in passing.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**CC Madhya 23.7 and 23.8** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/23/7/
> 23.7: "When that bhāva softens the heart completely, becomes endowed with a great feeling of possessiveness in relation to the Lord and becomes very much condensed and intensified, it is called prema [love of Godhead] by learned scholars."
- **Context:** Lord Caitanya to Sanatana, quoting Bhakti-rasamrta-sindhu 1.4.1 (the purport says so) and then Narada-pancaratra
  through BRS 1.4.2 (23.8): bhakti is "an unflinching sense of ownership or possessiveness in relation to Lord Viṣṇu", when one
  "thinks Viṣṇu and no one else to be the only object of love".
- **Teaching:** prema is defined by two marks: the heart is fully softened, and there is mamata, a sense of "He is mine", that has
  become dense. Nothing in the definition is about escape from the world.
- **Use:** in the talk; write both definitions on the board.

**CC Madhya 23.14-15** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/23/14-15/
- **Teaching:** the road to prema: faith, association, practice, freedom from unwanted habits, steadiness, taste, attachment,

- **Use:** one line in the talk.

- **Purport point:** Prabhupada quotes Siksastaka 4 and notes the words "in life after life": "a devotee does not even desire the cessation of birth and death". Merging into the effulgence "he considers worse than hellish". "If one is engaged in pure transcendental loving service to the Lord, it is understood that he has already achieved the five kinds of liberation."

**SB 9.4.63-68** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/9/4/63/
> 9.4.63: "I am completely under the control of My devotees. Indeed, I am not at all independent. Because My devotees are completely devoid of material desires, I sit only within the cores of their hearts."
> 9.4.67: "My devotees, who are always satisfied to be engaged in My loving service, are not interested even in the four principles of liberation [sālokya, sārūpya, sāmīpya and sārṣṭi], although these are automatically achieved by their service."

- **Purport point (9.4.67):** Bilvamangala's verse again: "mukti stands before him with folded hands to offer all kinds of service".
  "The pure devotee automatically achieves liberation, even without desiring it."
- **Use:** in the talk; the Lord's own statement of what prema does to Him.

- **Context:** Nrsimhadeva offers Prahlada any benediction; Prahlada refuses (story in J).

- **Purport point (7.10.7):** Prabhupada quotes Siksastaka 4 and adds: "Devotees are always on the positive platform, in contrast to the Māyāvādīs, who want to make everything impersonal or void."

**CC Adi 4.165; CC Adi 4.200-201** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/adi/4/165/

- **Purport point (4.165):** the revealed scriptures' definition of pure love: a bond that is not broken even when there is ample

- **Teaching (4.200-201):** "Whenever there is unselfish love, that is its style. The reservoir of love derives pleasure when the lovable object is pleased." The gopis' happiness "was dependent on the pleasure of Kṛṣṇa".

- **Teaching (1.2.6):** the supreme dharma is that by which one attains loving devotional service; it "must be unmotivated and uninterrupted to completely satisfy the self". **(1.7.10):** even the atmaramas, "though freed from all kinds of material bondage, desire to render unalloyed devotional service", because the Lord's qualities attract even the liberated.
- **Use:** background and one line each in the talk.

- **Teaching:** Ramananda Raya's answer that Prabhupada records in the purport: "Ecstatic love for the Supreme Personality of Godhead is the essence of all perfection." The catuh-sloki holds the same three subjects (25.102; see trd-23).
- **Use:** background; one line.

**CC Madhya 1.43 (purport): Srila Prabhupada's summary of the Priti-sandarbha** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/1/43/
- **Teaching:** "The sixth Sandarbha is called Prīti-sandarbha, a thesis on love of Godhead." Prabhupada lists its contents: through love of
  Godhead "one becomes perfectly liberated and attains the highest goal of life"; the liberated condition of a personalist is
  distinguished from that of an impersonalist; liberation in loving service "is described as the most exalted, and meeting the Supreme Personality of Godhead face to face is shown to be the highest perfection of life"; salokya, samipya and sarupya are compared and "Devotional service is considered to be liberation with greater facilities"; then the state after reaching the devotional platform, "the marginal symptoms of transcendental love, and how it is awakened; the distinction between so-called love and transcendental love"; the gopis' love, the residents of Gokula, and finally "the superexcellence of the love of the gopīs and that of Śrīmatī Rādhārāṇī".
- **Application (preacher's):** this is the whole page in one paragraph by Prabhupada. Read it aloud in the talk; it is the most
  reliable description of the book available to us.
- **Use:** in the talk (segment 5).

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** The Vedic literature has one subject in three parts (CC Adi 7.146; trd-23). The third part, prayojana, is
   prema. Lord Caitanya names it "the fifth goal of human life" (CC Adi 7.144). The four goals everyone knows end in moksa,
   liberation; prema begins where moksa ends (BG 18.54-55).
2. **Why liberation is not the end.** Liberation removes the disease; it does not give the life. Prabhupada's purports to CC Adi
   7.144 and BG 18.54 say the same: for the Mayavadi, brahma-bhuta is the last word; for the devotee it is the starting line of
   service. Kapila's verse (SB 3.29.13) states that even when the Lord Himself offers the five liberations, the pure devotee does
   not take them; the Lord's own verse (SB 9.4.67) says they are reached anyway, as a by-product.
3. **What prema is.** Rupa Gosvami defines it by density of feeling and by mamata, "He is mine" (CC Madhya 23.7). The test is in
   CC Adi 4.165 and 4.200-201: prema wants Krishna's pleasure, lust wants one's own. The desire for liberation is a refined form of
   wanting one's own relief (CC Adi 4.165 purport lists "liberation from material bondage" among the subdivisions of sense gratification).
   So the desire for mukti is not simply a smaller goal; it is a different kind of goal, pointed at oneself.
4. **What prema does to the Lord.** The infinite becomes submissive to the infinitesimal (CC Adi 7.145). He tells Durvasa He is
   "completely under the control of My devotees" (SB 9.4.63). He easily gives liberation but rarely gives direct service (SB 5.6.18).
   This is why prema is called the highest: it is the one thing that moves the Supreme.
5. **Why the devotee alone is peaceful.** Karmis, jnanis and yogis all want something, so they are kami, lusty (CC Madhya 19.149).
   The nārāyaṇa-parāyaṇa wants only service, so heaven, hell and liberation are equal to him (SB 6.17.28). A resident who still
   measures his life by how peaceful he feels has not yet understood the goal; peace is a symptom of prema, not the aim.
6. **What the Priti-sandarbha adds.** Prabhupada's summary (CC Madhya 1.43) shows the book's method: it does not dismiss liberation;
   it classifies it. Liberation while living and after death, immediate and gradual, Brahman realisation and meeting the Lord,
   salokya, samipya, sarupya, and then "liberation with greater facilities", devotional service itself. It then describes prema's
   symptoms, its awakening, its difference from so-called love, and its highest form in the gopis and Radharani. A preacher who
   knows this order can answer the Vedantist on his own ground and still end at Vrindavana.
7. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *"Back to Godhead" means escape.* No: it means service in the Lord's company (CC Madhya 20.142). The devotee prays "life after life" (CC Antya 20.29).
   - *Prema is an emotion to be felt.* It is defined by possessiveness toward Krishna and a softened heart, shown in service, not by tears on demand (CC Madhya 23.7; dps-18).
   - *Prema is the reward for sadhana.* Sadhana and prema are the same service at two stages (CC Madhya 23.14-15); the reward is not separate from the service.
   - *A devotee should at least want liberation.* Wanting it blocks pure love (CC Madhya 19.175).
8. **Pitch for TRD.** Use the Sanskrit terms freely; the room knows them. Keep the argument in Prabhupada's purports; name the
   Priti-sandarbha as the acharyas' systematic treatment and say honestly that we teach it through Prabhupada until a readable
   edition is checked.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet the devotees and say that today's subject is the goal: not the practices, not the stages, but the thing itself.
2. Say: "Everyone here has given up a great deal for Krishna consciousness. Today we check what we gave it up for."
3. Ask H.2 question 1 for private writing (one line, not shared).
4. Ask H.2 question 2; take three answers from the room and write them on the board without comment.
5. Say that Lord Caitanya answered this question in front of the greatest Vedantists of His time, and begin with CC Adi 7.141.

**H.2 Opening questions**
1. (Private) When you pray at the end of japa, what do you most often ask for? Write one line.
2. If Krishna offered you, today, release from birth and death with no further condition, would you take it? Why or why not?
3. What is the difference between a servant who loves his master and a servant who wants to be free of his master?

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Prema is the fifth goal.** Above dharma, artha, kama and moksa (CC Adi 7.144). *Example:* the four goals are the four
   floors everyone knows; Lord Caitanya shows a fifth floor the Vedantists had not climbed. *Transition:* what is on that floor?
2. **Prema is defined, not vague.** A fully softened heart and dense "He is mine" (CC Madhya 23.7); Vishnu as the only object of
   love (23.8). *Example:* a mother does not "feel like" caring for her child; the child is hers. *Transition:* then what is lust?
3. **The test: whose pleasure?** Lust seeks one's own; prema seeks Krishna's (CC Adi 4.165). The desire for liberation seeks
   one's own relief and so belongs to the first list. *Transition:* so what does the pure devotee do with liberation?
4. **He does not take it, and he gets it anyway.** SB 3.29.13; SB 9.4.67; SB 5.6.18. *Example:* Ambarisa fasting while Durvasa
   runs through the universe (J). *Transition:* why does the Lord speak like this?
5. **Prema controls the Lord.** SB 9.4.63-68; CC Adi 7.145. *Transition:* the Priti-sandarbha systematises all this.
6. **The Priti-sandarbha's order.** Liberation classified, then surpassed; prema described to its highest form (CC Madhya 1.43 purport).
   *Transition:* what does a resident ask for now? Siksastaka 4.

## J. Stories and illustrations

**1. Prahlada refuses Nrsimhadeva's benediction (SB 7.10.4-7).** Tell it as the verses tell it. After Hiranyakasipu is killed,
the Lord offers Prahlada any benediction. Prahlada replies that a devotee who wants material benefit for service "is no better
than a merchant who wants profit in exchange for service" (7.10.4), that a servant who desires profit is not a pure devotee and a
master who gives rewards to keep his position is not a pure master (7.10.5), that he and the Lord are simply servant and master,
"We have no other relationship" (7.10.6), and that if the Lord insists on giving a benediction, let it be that no material desire
remain in his heart (7.10.7). *Lesson:* the goal is the relationship itself. Prabhupada's purport links Prahlada's prayer to
Siksastaka 4 (CC Antya 20.29).

**2. Durvasa Muni and Maharaja Ambarisa (SB 9.4; the Lord's words in 9.4.63-68).** Ambarisa, having fasted on Ekadasi, breaks the
fast at the right moment with water while waiting for his guest Durvasa. Durvasa, offended, creates a demon to kill him; the Lord's
Sudarsana cakra burns the demon and chases Durvasa through the universe, to Brahma, to Siva and finally to Vaikuntha. There the Lord
tells him He cannot help: He is "completely under the control of My devotees" (9.4.63); without such devotees He does not wish to
enjoy His own bliss (9.4.64); they give up everything for Him, so He cannot give them up (9.4.65); they control Him as chaste wives
control gentle husbands (9.4.66); they do not want even the four liberations, which come to them anyway (9.4.67); He is in their
hearts and they in His (9.4.68). Durvasa must return to Ambarisa, who has waited a year without eating, and ask his forgiveness.
*Lesson:* the Lord Himself defines the goal: a devotee who wants nothing has Him entirely. Prabhupada's purport to 9.4.65 contrasts
the self-interested yogi who fled with the devotee who stayed fixed and prayed.

**3. Lord Caitanya and the Varanasi sannyasis (CC Adi 7.141-146).** The Lord, invited by the Maharashtrian brahmana, sits among
Prakasananda's followers and explains the Vedanta-sutra. He builds from hearing (7.141), through regulated practice (7.142) and
growing detachment (7.143), to love as the fifth goal (7.144), the Lord's submission to His devotee (7.145), and the three
subjects of every sutra (7.146). *Lesson:* the goal can be established before the strongest opponents from their own scripture.
(The full narrative is in dps and sps pages on Lord Caitanya's life; here only these verses are used.)

**Illustration (writer's, not scriptural):** a man rescued from a flood may want only dry ground, or he may want to reach the one
who threw the rope. Liberation is dry ground; prema is the person. The Bhagavatam says the pure devotee runs past the ground to
the person (SB 3.29.13).

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"The Gita says the brahma-bhuta stage is the goal (BG 18.54)."** Read 18.54 to the end: "In that state he attains pure devotional
   service unto Me." The purport says the impersonalist stops there and the devotee goes further. 18.55 gives the entry into the kingdom
   by devotion alone.
2. **"Isn't rejecting liberation just pride?"** No. Liberation is not rejected as worthless; it is received as a by-product and not
   made the aim (SB 9.4.67). Prahlada does not refuse the Lord; he refuses to be a merchant (SB 7.10.4).
3. **"If prema is so rare (CC Madhya 19.150), why aim for it?"** Because it is the only goal that satisfies (19.150 purport), and
   because the road is open to anyone who hears and chants (CC Adi 7.141-142). Rarity is a reason for seriousness, not despair.
4. **"Why does the pure devotee call merging 'worse than hellish'?"** Because in hell the soul still exists as an individual who can
   serve later; in merging, the opportunity for service is lost. Prabhupada's words in the SB 3.29.13 purport are to be given as they are.
5. **"We cannot read the Priti-sandarbha; why teach it?"** We teach what Prabhupada wrote about it (CC Madhya 1.43). Tell the room
   plainly what we have and have not read. That honesty is itself part of the siddhanta of this page.
6. **"Does wanting Goloka count as wanting liberation?"** Wanting Goloka to serve Krishna there is prema's own direction (CC Madhya 20.142);
   wanting Goloka as a comfortable place is the salokya the pure devotee declines (SB 3.29.13). The motive decides. See trd-30 and trd-48.

## L. Case studies

**Case 1 (Invented case for discussion).** A brahmacari of six years tells his counsellor that he is tired and that he keeps
himself going by thinking "at least I will not come back to this world". *Dilemma:* is this a healthy motive? *Principles:* CC
Madhya 19.175; CC Madhya 20.142; SB 6.17.28. *Questions:* What is he actually hoping for? What would Prahlada say? What practice
restores the right aim? *Analysis:* his goal has become relief. The remedy is not scolding but hearing: SB 9.4.63-68 read with him,
so that the person of Krishna returns to the centre. *Takeaway:* when bhajana aims at escape, the heart dries; when it aims at
Krishna's pleasure, even tiredness becomes service.

**Case 2 (Invented case for discussion).** A preacher's college programme is built on "freedom from stress and the cycle of birth
and death". Attendance is good. *Dilemma:* is the message complete? *Principles:* CC Adi 7.144; BG 18.54-55. *Questions:* Where
does the audience think the road ends? How can the fifth goal be introduced without losing them? *Analysis:* start where they are,
as Lord Caitanya did with the sannyasis, but never end on liberation; end on the person who gives it. *Takeaway:* a programme
that stops at moksa has preached the fourth goal.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Everyone agrees that human life has goals. The scriptures of India name four: religious duty (dharma), wealth (artha), enjoyment
(kama) and liberation (moksa), which means release from birth and death. Most religious people think liberation is the last word.
When Lord Caitanya taught the Vedanta-sutra to the great sannyasis of Varanasi, He showed that there is a fifth goal above
liberation: love of God, prema (CC Adi 7.144). The whole Vedic literature, He said, teaches three things: our relationship with
Krishna, our activity in that relationship, and the goal, love of Krishna (CC Adi 7.146).

What is prema? Rupa Gosvami defines it: when devotional emotion fully softens the heart and becomes a dense feeling of "Krishna is
mine", learned scholars call it prema (CC Madhya 23.7). The test that separates love from lust is simple: lust wants my own
pleasure, love wants Krishna's pleasure (CC Adi 4.165). Even the wish for liberation is a wish for my own relief, so it belongs on
the side of self-interest, not of love.

The Bhagavatam shows what a pure devotee does with liberation: he does not accept it, even when the Lord offers it (SB 3.29.13).
The Lord Himself told the sage Durvasa that He is completely under the control of His devotees, that they do not want even the
four kinds of liberation, and that those liberations come to them anyway (SB 9.4.63-68). He gives liberation easily but direct
service rarely (SB 5.6.18). Prahlada, offered any gift by Nrsimhadeva, asked only that no material desire remain in his heart
(SB 7.10.7). Lord Caitanya prayed the same: not wealth, followers, a beautiful wife or poetic language, but unmotivated service,
life after life (CC Antya 20.29).

Jiva Gosvami's Priti-sandarbha is the acharyas' systematic book on this goal. Srila Prabhupada summarises it (CC Madhya 1.43
purport): it compares the kinds of liberation, shows that loving service is "liberation with greater facilities", and then
describes love of God up to its highest form in the gopis and Srimati Radharani.

## S2. Key ideas
- Prema, love of Krishna, is the fifth goal of life, above liberation (CC Adi 7.144).
- Prema means a softened heart and a dense sense of "Krishna is mine" (CC Madhya 23.7).
- Love seeks Krishna's pleasure; lust, and the wish for relief, seek my own (CC Adi 4.165).
- A pure devotee does not ask for liberation; it comes anyway, as a by-product (SB 3.29.13; SB 9.4.67).
- Prema controls the Lord: "I am completely under the control of My devotees" (SB 9.4.63).

## S3. Verses to remember
- **CC Adi 7.144:** "Love of Godhead is so exalted that it is considered to be the fifth goal of human life." For me: my aim is higher than freedom; it is love.
- **SB 9.4.67:** "My devotees, who are always satisfied to be engaged in My loving service, are not interested even in the four principles of liberation…" For me: I need not ask for what service already brings.
- **CC Antya 20.29:** "All I want, life after life, is unmotivated devotional service to You." For me: this is my prayer after japa.

## S4. Words to know
- **Prayojana:** the goal of life; in Gaudiya siddhanta, prema.
- **Prema:** love of God; devotional emotion grown dense, with a sense that Krishna is mine.
- **Mukti / moksa:** liberation from birth and death. Five kinds are named: salokya (living on the Lord's planet), sarsti (equal opulence), samipya (nearness), sarupya (similar form) and ekatva (merging).
- **Bhukti, mukti, siddhi:** material enjoyment, liberation and mystic power; the three desires that keep a person restless (CC Madhya 19.149).
- **Priti-sandarbha:** Jiva Gosvami's sixth Sandarbha, "the essay on love", on prema as the highest goal.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. **Is liberation bad?** No. It is real and the Lord gives it. But it is not the goal; love of God is (CC Adi 7.144).
2. **Why does a devotee refuse liberation?** Because he wants only to serve; liberation comes anyway (SB 3.29.13; SB 9.4.67).
3. **How do I know if my devotion is love or self-interest?** Ask whose pleasure I want: Krishna's or my own (CC Adi 4.165).
4. **Is wanting to go to Goloka a selfish desire?** Not if I want to go there to serve Krishna; it is, if I want it as a comfortable place (CC Madhya 20.142).
5. **Can I read the Priti-sandarbha?** The copy we have is unreadable; for now we learn it from Srila Prabhupada's summary (CC Madhya 1.43 purport).

## S6. Check your understanding
1. Which is the fifth goal of life according to Lord Caitanya? (a) moksa (b) prema (c) dharma
2. Complete Rupa Gosvami's definition: prema is bhava that softens the heart and becomes a dense feeling of ______.
3. True or false: a pure devotee accepts liberation gladly when the Lord offers it.
4. What did the Lord tell Durvasa about His independence?
5. Prahlada asked Nrsimhadeva for one benediction. What was it?
6. In Prabhupada's summary, what does the Priti-sandarbha call devotional service in comparison with the kinds of liberation?

**Answers:** 1 (b) (CC Adi 7.144). 2 "Krishna is mine", possessiveness toward the Lord (CC Madhya 23.7). 3 False (SB 3.29.13).
4 He is not at all independent; He is completely under the control of His devotees (SB 9.4.63). 5 That no material desire remain in
his heart (SB 7.10.7). 6 "Liberation with greater facilities" (CC Madhya 1.43 purport).
