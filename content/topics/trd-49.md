# The weeds beside the creeper: labha, puja, pratistha, kutinati, jiva-himsa

> **TRD · up to 180 min · review: drafted (30 Oct 2026).** Group H (blockers for senior devotees), first of six.
> For residents whose years and positions have made the subtle weeds possible. Verses checked on vedabase
> (research/verification/trd-49.json). Builds on trd-15 (pratistha) and dps-14 (anartha-nivrtti); the mad-elephant
> offence (CC Madhya 19.156-157) is trd-50.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on dps-14 (the stage of anartha-nivrtti) and trd-15 (pratistha). dps-14 gives the general map of anarthas; trd-15
takes one weed, name and fame, in the preacher. This page takes the whole list in CC Madhya 19.158-161 as Lord Caitanya
gave it to Rupa Gosvami, with Prabhupada's purports, and asks the senior devotee's question: which of these are growing
beside a creeper that has been watered for years?

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| VL1 | Not covered; the invitation to begin watering the creeper |
| VL2 | The seed and the gardener (CC Madhya 19.151-152) as the picture of spiritual life |
| VL3 | Watering by hearing and chanting; what starves the creeper (irregular practice) |
| SPS | The four regulative principles as the fence; nisiddhacara explained |
| DPS | Anartha-nivrtti in the stage sequence (dps-14); the general list of anarthas |
| BSS | The brahmacari's weeds: adoration, sense gratification in the ashram, diplomacy with authorities |
| **TRD** (here) | The upasakhas as they grow with years and position: profit, adoration, importance, diplomacy, envy; Bhaktisiddhanta's expanded list in the 19.160 purport; the senior's self-audit |

**Not done here:** the mad-elephant offence (trd-50); pratistha in depth (trd-15); the mission's money (trd-17);
envy as a psychological subject (dps-20).

## C. Central message

"Sometimes unwanted creepers… grow along with the creeper of devotional service" (CC Madhya 19.158). When they are
watered with the same hearing and chanting, "the other creepers are nourished while the bhakti creeper is curtailed"
(19.160). The intelligent devotee "must cut it down instantly" (19.161). Years of service do not kill these weeds;
they give them room.

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Recite the list in CC Madhya 19.159 and explain each item as Prabhupada's purport explains it.
2. Explain from 19.160 why chanting and hearing can nourish the weeds, and give Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati's expanded
   list of how the so-called devotee is "victimized by unwanted creepers".
3. Distinguish between cutting a weed and cutting the creeper: what "instantly" means and does not mean (19.161).
4. Connect the cleansing of the Gundica temple (CC Madhya 12.135 purport) to the cleansing of the heart of "straw,
   grains of sand, weeds or dust".
5. Identify, privately, which upasakha has the most room in their own life and name one action against it.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**CC Madhya 19.151**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/19/151/
> "…Out of many millions of wandering living entities, one who is very fortunate gets an opportunity to associate with a bona fide spiritual master by the grace of Kṛṣṇa. By the mercy of both Kṛṣṇa and the spiritual master, such a person receives the seed of the creeper of devotional service."
- **Context:** Lord Caitanya instructs Rupa Gosvami at Prayaga. Verses 151-155 give the creeper's growth, 156-157
  the mad elephant (trd-50), 158-161 the unwanted creepers.
- **Use:** in the talk, the opening of the passage.

**CC Madhya 19.152-155**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/19/152/ (and /153/, /154/, /155/)
> 19.152: "When a person receives the seed of devotional service, he should take care of it by becoming a gardener and sowing the seed in his heart. If he waters the seed gradually by the process of śravaṇa and kīrtana [hearing and chanting], the seed will begin to sprout."
> 19.155: "The creeper greatly expands in the Goloka Vṛndāvana planet, and there it produces the fruit of love for Kṛṣṇa. Although remaining in the material world, the gardener regularly sprinkles the creeper with the water of hearing and chanting."
- **Teaching:** the creeper grows beyond the universe and the Brahman effulgence to Goloka (19.153-154) and bears
  prema (19.155), while the gardener goes on watering from here.
- **Use:** in the talk: the picture must be whole before the weeds make sense.

**CC Madhya 19.158**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/19/158/
> "Sometimes unwanted creepers, such as the creepers of desires for material enjoyment and liberation from the material world, grow along with the creeper of devotional service. The varieties of such unwanted creepers are unlimited."
- **Teaching:** the two root weeds are *bhukti* (enjoyment) and *mukti* (liberation); their varieties are unlimited.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**CC Madhya 19.159**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/19/159/
> "Some unnecessary creepers growing with the bhakti creeper are the creepers of behavior unacceptable for those trying to attain perfection, diplomatic behavior, animal-killing, mundane profiteering, mundane adoration and mundane importance. All these are unwanted creepers."
- **The Bengali list, as the 19.160 purport repeats it:** *nisiddhacara* (forbidden behaviour), *kutinati*
  (diplomacy), *jiva-himsana* (violence or envy toward living beings), *labha* (profit), *puja* (adoration),
  *pratistha* (importance, position).
- **Purport point:** "Kuṭīnāṭī, or diplomatic behavior, cannot satisfy the ātmā, the soul. It cannot even satisfy
  the body or the mind. The culprit mind is always suspicious; therefore our dealings should always be straightforward
  and approved by Vedic authorities. If we treat people diplomatically or duplicitously, our spiritual advancement is
  obstructed." And: "Jīva-hiṁsana refers to the killing of animals or to envy of other living entities." Also:
  "whatever material profit one has must be abandoned at the time of death… Similarly, adoration by mundane people is
  valueless because after death one has to accept another body."
- **Use:** in the talk, the central verse; put the six items on a slide.

**CC Madhya 19.160**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/19/160/
> "If one does not distinguish between the bhakti creeper and the other creepers, the sprinkling of water is misused because the other creepers are nourished while the bhakti creeper is curtailed."
- **Purport point:** "If one chants the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra while committing offenses, these unwanted creepers will grow.
  One should not take advantage of chanting the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra for some material profit." Prabhupada then gives
  Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura's description of the unwanted creepers: one who hears and chants "without
  trying to give up offenses" becomes attached to sense gratification; may desire liberation or yogic powers
  (*siddhi-lobhi*); "may also be victimized by diplomatic or crooked behavior"; may associate with women for illicit
  sex; "may make a show of devotional service like the prākṛta-sahajiyās"; may claim "a monopoly on spiritual
  advancement" through caste or dynasty and so "become a pseudo guru"; may consider a Vaisnava to belong to a mundane
  caste ("this is a Hindu Vaiṣṇava, and this is a European Vaiṣṇava"); "may also try to carry out a professional
  business by means of chanting the Hare Kṛṣṇa mantra or reading Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam"; may "become a cheap Vaiṣṇava by
  trying to chant in a secluded place for material adoration"; may "desire mundane reputation by making compromises
  with nondevotees concerning one's philosophy or spiritual life". "All these are pitfalls of personal sense
  gratification. Just to cheat some innocent people, one makes a show of advanced spiritual life and becomes known as
  a sādhu, mahātmā or religious person."
- **Application (preacher's):** every item in this list is a senior's temptation, not a newcomer's. A newcomer cannot
  run a professional business on the Bhagavatam or compromise the philosophy for reputation; a senior can.
- **Use:** in the talk; read the purport's list aloud in Prabhupada's words.

**CC Madhya 19.161**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/19/161/
> "As soon as an intelligent devotee sees an unwanted creeper growing beside the original creeper, he must cut it down instantly. Then the real creeper, the bhakti-latā, grows nicely, returns home, back to Godhead, and seeks shelter under the lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa."
- **Purport point:** "If one is misled by unwanted creepers and is victimized, he cannot make progress back to
  Godhead. Rather, he remains within the material world… Such a person may be elevated to the higher planetary systems,
  but because he remains within the material world, he is subjected to the threefold material miseries."
- **Use:** in the talk, the instruction.

**CC Madhya 12.135**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/12/135/
> "Outside the gateway of the temple, all the roads were also cleansed, and no one could tell exactly how this was done."
- **Context:** the cleansing of the Gundica temple before Ratha-yatra.
- **Purport point:** Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati says the Lord "as the world leader, was personally giving instructions
  on how one should receive Lord Kṛṣṇa… within one's cleansed and pacified heart." Prabhupada: "If the heart is filled
  with straw, grains of sand, weeds or dust (in other words, anyābhilāṣa-pūrṇa), one cannot enthrone the Supreme
  Personality of Godhead there. The heart must be cleansed of all material motives… The heart must be cleansed without
  ulterior motive."
- **Use:** in the talk (J): the Lord's own demonstration of weeding.

**SB 1.2.6**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/6/
> "…Such devotional service must be unmotivated and uninterrupted to completely satisfy the self."
- **Purport point:** "adoption of devotional service for material gain is certainly an obstruction to the progressive
  path of renunciation." trd-21 used this verse for happiness; here the point is that *labha, puja, pratistha* are
  precisely the motives that make service motivated.
- **Use:** in the talk, one reading.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** The bhakti creeper and the weeds drink the same water. Hearing and chanting done "while
   committing offenses" or "for some material profit" nourish the weeds (CC Madhya 19.160 purport). That is why a
   devotee can chant for twenty years and find his desire for position larger than it was at five: the water went to
   the wrong plant.
2. **Why the senior is more exposed.** Each of the six weeds in 19.159 needs soil that a newcomer does not have.
   *Labha*: access to funds and donors. *Puja*: people who honour you. *Pratistha*: a position to protect. *Kutinati*:
   relationships complex enough to manage diplomatically. *Jiva-himsa* as envy: peers whose success is measurable
   against yours. *Nisiddhacara*: the private freedom a senior has and a bhakta does not. The purport's list of
   "pitfalls" (professional business on the Bhagavatam, compromising philosophy for reputation, chanting in seclusion
   for adoration, pseudo-guru claims) is a list of senior temptations.
3. **How the weeds hide.** A weed near a creeper looks like growth. The temple grows, the collections grow, the
   following grows, and no one asks whether it is the creeper or the weed that grew. The 19.160 purport describes "a show
   of advanced spiritual life". The 12.135 purport names the material in the heart as "straw, grains of sand, weeds or
   dust", that is, as *anyabhilasa*, other desires.
4. **What "cut it down instantly" means.** Not cutting the service (the creeper), not cutting the person (envy
   wearing the mask of reform), but cutting the motive, at once, when it is seen. Lord Caitanya cleaned the Gundica
   temple Himself, so thoroughly that "no one could tell exactly how this was done" (12.135): weeding is quiet work,
   done by the owner of the heart.
5. **Connections.** The weeds grow where the fence of 19.157 is weak (trd-50). Pratistha has its own page (trd-15).
   Labha and the mission's money is trd-17. Diplomacy against honesty is trd-16. Envy of a peer that becomes an offence
   is trd-50. Comfort and career is trd-53.
6. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *These weeds are for beginners; a senior has passed them.* 19.158: "the varieties of such unwanted creepers are
     unlimited", and the purport's list requires seniority to be possible.
   - *Being direct is kutinati's opposite, so I may be harsh.* The purport asks for dealings that are "straightforward
     and approved by Vedic authorities"; harshness toward devotees is jiva-himsa, the next weed in the same verse.
   - *Weeding means I must give up my service or post.* The post is not the weed; the attachment to it is. Cutting
     the attachment may or may not mean leaving the post; ask your authority (dps-02, trd-16).

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes).** Say: "Lord Caitanya gave Rupa Gosvami a picture of our life: a seed, a
gardener, water, a creeper that grows to Goloka. Then He said something that every one of us who has been here for years
needs to hear: weeds grow beside the creeper, and they drink the same water. Tonight we read the list He gave, and
Prabhupada's purports, and we ask one question honestly: which of these weeds has the most room in my life now that I
have a position, a service, a name?" Read CC Madhya 19.158 and 19.159 aloud. Then: "Everything else tonight explains
these two verses."

**H.2 Opening questions:**
1. "Which of these six words do you hear most often applied to someone else, and least often to yourself: profit,
   adoration, importance, diplomacy, envy, forbidden behaviour?"
2. "What can a senior devotee do that a new bhakta cannot? Make a list of five things; we will see which of them are
   soil for weeds."
3. "Lord Caitanya says 'cut it down instantly'. What is the last thing you cut instantly in your own life?"

## I. Core teaching points

1. **The creeper and the weeds drink the same water.** CC Madhya 19.160 and purport. Plain explanation: chanting with
   a motive feeds the motive. Example (preacher's application): the kirtana leader whose rounds improve on days he is
   praised. Transition: what the weeds are.
2. **The six weeds, defined by Prabhupada.** 19.159 and purport. Example for each, in the senior's life (F.2).
   Transition: the acharya's longer list.
3. **Bhaktisiddhanta's pitfalls are a senior's list.** 19.160 purport. Example: "a professional business by means of
   chanting… or reading Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam" is possible only for one who can draw an audience. Transition: what to do.
4. **Cut the weed, not the creeper, not the person.** 19.161 and F.4. Example: a department head who, noticing he
   enjoys the deference of juniors, starts offering obeisances first and asking a junior's advice weekly; he does not
   abandon the department. Transition: the Lord's demonstration.
5. **The Lord cleaned the Gundica temple Himself.** 12.135 purport. Example: weeding is private; no announcement.
   Transition: the result.
6. **A weeded creeper "returns home".** 19.161; SB 1.2.6 (unmotivated service satisfies). Transition to M.

## J. Stories and illustrations

- **The cleansing of the Gundica temple (CC Madhya 12.135 and purport).** Before Ratha-yatra the Lord and His
  devotees washed the Gundica temple; the verse says even the roads outside the gate were cleaned and "no one could tell
  exactly how this was done". Devotional lesson (the purport's own): the Lord "as the world leader" showed how to clean
  the heart of "straw, grains of sand, weeds or dust", that is, of every motive other than Krishna, so that Krishna can
  be enthroned there. The whole chapter (CC Madhya 12) gives the details; read it before teaching, and tell only what
  the text tells.
- **Rupa Gosvami receives the teaching (CC Madhya 19.151-161).** The passage itself is the story's setting: the Lord
  at Prayaga, Rupa Gosvami at His feet. Devotional lesson: the teaching on weeds was given to the devotee who would
  write the science of bhakti; it is for the most advanced, not only for beginners.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. *"I have chanted for fifteen years. How can weeds still be there?"* 19.160: if the water went to the weeds, they
   grew with the years. The question is not how long one watered but what one watered. The remedy is in the same
   passage: chant without offences and without seeking profit (purport), and cut what is seen (19.161).
2. *"Is wanting the temple to grow and collect more a weed (labha)?"* Wanting Krishna's temple to prosper for His
   service is service; wanting the growth for one's own profit, credit or security is labha. The purport's test is
   whether the gain is "for some material profit". trd-17 treats the handling of the mission's money.
3. *"My position requires diplomacy."* The purport asks for dealings "straightforward and approved by Vedic
   authorities". Tact and timing are not kutinati; concealment and double-dealing are. trd-16 treats honesty with
   authorities.
4. *"Isn't noticing weeds in myself discouraging?"* 19.161 calls the one who sees them "intelligent". Noticing is the
   beginning of cutting.
5. *"May I point out a weed in a godbrother?"* Only as the Vaisnava way allows (dps-22, NOI 4's loving exchanges), and
   never from envy, which is itself jiva-himsa (19.159 purport). Seeing a senior's faults as a mad elephant offence is
   trd-50.

## L. Case studies

**Case 1 (Invented case for discussion).** A twelve-year resident runs the temple's most successful outreach. Donors
know him by name; his talks are recorded; younger devotees copy his style. Lately he feels irritated when another
preacher is praised, and he has begun to soften certain points of philosophy for influential guests. *Principles:*
19.159 (puja, pratistha, jiva-himsa as envy); 19.160 purport ("compromises with nondevotees concerning one's
philosophy" for "mundane reputation"). *Questions:* Which weeds are visible? What is the creeper here, and must it be
cut? *Analysis:* the preaching is the creeper; the enjoyment of adoration and the compromise are weeds. *Takeaway:*
cut instantly and quietly: restore the compromised points in the next talk, and praise the other preacher by name in
public.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions (expected answers in brackets):**
1. Name the six weeds of CC Madhya 19.159. [Forbidden behaviour, diplomacy, violence or envy toward living beings,
   profit, adoration, importance.]
2. What are the two root weeds in 19.158? [Desire for material enjoyment and desire for liberation.]
3. Why can chanting nourish weeds (19.160)? [If one chants while committing offences or for material profit, the water
   goes to the unwanted creepers.]
4. What must the intelligent devotee do on seeing a weed (19.161)? [Cut it down instantly.]
5. What does Prabhupada say diplomatic behaviour cannot do (19.159 purport)? [Satisfy the soul; it cannot even satisfy
   the body or the mind.]
6. What did Lord Caitanya demonstrate by cleaning the Gundica temple (12.135 purport)? [How to cleanse the heart of all
   material motives so that Krishna can be enthroned there.]

**M.2 Activity (25 min): the senior's audit.** Each resident writes privately: (a) the five things his position allows
him that a new bhakta cannot do (H.2 question 2); (b) beside each, which of the six weeds it could feed; (c) one weed
to cut this week and the exact action. Pairs then discuss only the method of cutting, never the private content.

**M.3 Reflection.** "If Lord Caitanya cleaned my heart as He cleaned the Gundica temple, what would He sweep out first?"

## R. Adapting for another level

### R.1 Deeper verses in reserve
*For a more advanced audience; not for this level's talk or handout.*
- **CC Madhya 19.153-154:** the creeper passing the Viraja, the Brahman effulgence and reaching Goloka; use it to show
  that *mukti* (the Brahman effulgence) is a stage the creeper passes through, which is why desiring it is a weed.
- **SB 1.2.6 purport:** "temporary cessation of misery is falsely called happiness"; use it with trd-21.

### R.2 VL2 and VL3
Teach the seed and the gardener (CC Madhya 19.151-152) as the picture of spiritual life: the seed comes by mercy, the

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

At Prayaga, Lord Caitanya taught Rupa Gosvami with a picture. By the mercy of Krishna and the spiritual master a fortunate
soul "receives the seed of the creeper of devotional service" (CC Madhya 19.151). He becomes a gardener, plants it in
his heart and waters it "by the process of sravana and kirtana" (19.152). The creeper grows past the walls of the
universe and the Brahman effulgence to Goloka Vrindavana and bears "the fruit of love for Krsna", while the gardener
goes on watering from here (19.153-155).

Then the Lord warned: "Sometimes unwanted creepers, such as the creepers of desires for material enjoyment and
liberation from the material world, grow along with the creeper of devotional service. The varieties of such unwanted
creepers are unlimited" (19.158). He named some: "behavior unacceptable for those trying to attain perfection,
diplomatic behavior, animal-killing, mundane profiteering, mundane adoration and mundane importance" (19.159): in
Bengali, *nisiddhacara, kutinati, jiva-himsa, labha, puja, pratistha*. Srila Prabhupada explains that diplomatic
behaviour "cannot satisfy the atma, the soul. It cannot even satisfy the body or the mind", that *jiva-himsa* means
killing animals or "envy of other living entities", and that profit and adoration are worthless because they are left
behind at death.

The danger is that the weeds drink the same water. "If one does not distinguish between the bhakti creeper and the
other creepers, the sprinkling of water is misused because the other creepers are nourished while the bhakti creeper is
curtailed" (19.160). Prabhupada's purport adds Srila Bhaktisiddhanta's list of what happens to one who chants without
giving up offences: he may make "a show of devotional service", run "a professional business by means of chanting the
Hare Krsna mantra or reading Srimad-Bhagavatam", chant in seclusion "for material adoration", or make "compromises with
nondevotees concerning one's philosophy" for reputation. These are the temptations of experienced devotees, because
only they have the position to do them. The Lord's instruction is simple: "As soon as an intelligent devotee sees an
unwanted creeper growing beside the original creeper, he must cut it down instantly" (19.161). He showed it Himself
when He cleaned the Gundica temple so thoroughly that "no one could tell exactly how this was done" (12.135); the
purport says the heart must be cleansed of "straw, grains of sand, weeds or dust", every motive other than Krishna.

## S2. Key ideas
- The creeper and the weeds drink the same water of hearing and chanting (CC Madhya 19.160).
- Six named weeds: forbidden behaviour, diplomacy, violence or envy, profit, adoration, importance (19.159).
- The weeds need soil that only years and position give; seniors are more exposed, not less.
- The intelligent devotee sees a weed and cuts it instantly, quietly, without cutting his service or another person
  (19.161).
- Lord Caitanya demonstrated the cleansing of the heart at the Gundica temple (12.135 purport).

## S3. Verses to remember
- **CC Madhya 19.158**: "unwanted creepers… grow along with the creeper of devotional service." For me: growth is not
  proof that the creeper grew.
- **CC Madhya 19.160**: "the other creepers are nourished while the bhakti creeper is curtailed." For me: what am I
  watering when I chant?
- **CC Madhya 19.161**: "he must cut it down instantly." For me: noticing is the beginning; delay is watering.

## S4. Words to know
- **Bhakti-lata-bija:** the seed of the creeper of devotional service.
- **Upasakha:** an unwanted side-creeper, a weed.
- **Labha, puja, pratistha:** profit, adoration, importance (position).
- **Kutinati:** diplomacy, duplicity.
- **Jiva-himsa:** violence toward living beings, including envy.
- **Nisiddhacara:** behaviour forbidden for those seeking perfection.
- **Anyabhilasa:** any desire other than to serve Krishna.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *I have chanted for years. How can weeds still be there?* If the water went to the weeds, they grew with the years
   (19.160). Chant without offences and without seeking profit, and cut what you see (19.161).
2. *Is wanting the temple to grow a weed?* Wanting Krishna's service to prosper is service; wanting the growth for your
   own profit, credit or security is *labha* (19.159 purport).
3. *My service needs diplomacy.* Tact is not *kutinati*; concealment and double-dealing are. Prabhupada asks for
   dealings "straightforward and approved by Vedic authorities".
4. *Isn't noticing my weeds discouraging?* The Lord calls the one who sees them "intelligent" (19.161).

## S6. Check your understanding
1. Name the two root weeds in CC Madhya 19.158.
2. Which of these is NOT in the list of 19.159: diplomacy, adoration, sleeping too much, profit?
3. Why can chanting nourish the weeds (19.160)?
4. What does Prabhupada say diplomatic behaviour cannot satisfy?
5. What must the devotee do when he sees a weed (19.161), and what did the Lord demonstrate at the Gundica temple?

**Answers:** 1. Desire for material enjoyment and desire for liberation (19.158). 2. Sleeping too much (the list is
forbidden behaviour, diplomacy, violence or envy, profit, adoration, importance). 3. Chanting while committing offences
or for material profit sends the water to the unwanted creepers (19.160 purport). 4. The soul, and not even the body
or the mind (19.159 purport). 5. Cut it down instantly (19.161); the cleansing of the heart of every motive other than
Krishna (12.135 purport).

that "no one could tell exactly how"). Private reflection: "What did my position let me do this week that fed a weed?"
