# How to deal with Parents, relatives & friends

> **VL3 · 180 min · review: sources-checked.** IYF's material is one deck, *Art of Smart Dealings: Friends, Relatives & Parents*
> (`research/iyf-materials/iyf-decks/Topic-8 Art of Smart Dealing - Parent_ Relatives_ Friends _ IYF.txt`), with a
> matching lecture by HG Sundar Gopal Prabhu (O). The deck's frame is excellent and is kept: *"not getting affected by
> materialistic philosophy and association, at the same time not getting alienated"*; three categories of people;
> five stages of an observer; parents' NICE (need, interest, concern, expectation). Two of its lines are adjusted
> (F.6, O). The rule of this page, from the head: **respectful, and no family conflict encouraged.** Nothing here
> teaches a student to defy, deceive or distance his parents. Prahlada and Hiranyakasipu are not used as a model.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on vl3-09 (what to say about ISKCON) and vl3-11 (how to argue): this page does not repeat the objection answers or the debate arguments; it is about how to live with the people who ask.

IYF's VL3 motive: "well known in challenges from society, friends and relatives and appropriate dealing with them"
(KRA tab; `research/batch-criteria.md` §3). VL1 names "relationship problems" among youth's troubles; VL2's 2015
syllabus had this very session and "Overcoming social pressures". At VL3 the student's practice is now visible at
home (he chants, he eats prasadam, he is out on Sundays), and the family's reaction decides whether the practice
survives the year. This is the last VL3 topic on the site; it gathers VL3-09 (what to say) and VL3-11 (how to argue)
into *how to live with people*.

| VL2 | "How to deal with parents, relatives and friends"; "Overcoming social pressures" (2015 syllabus): the student's own steadiness |
| **VL3** (here) | Keeping practice *and* relationships: respect, speech, example, duty; sorting observers; parents' NICE; no conflict |

**Not done here:**
- Any instruction to leave home, defy parents, hide practice, or treat family as "bad association" to be cut. NOI 2's
  *jana-saṅga* is applied, at VL3, to adopting worldly *values*, not to avoiding one's parents (F.5).
- Marriage, in-laws and children: BVS (left out for now).
- Preaching technique to strangers: DPS ("art of approaching new people").
- Conflict that has become serious (threats, violence, being thrown out): that is a one-to-one matter (vl3-08) and a
  seniors' matter; the page says so (K.8).

## C. Central message

Respect for father and mother is itself an austerity Krsna prescribes (BG 17.14), and the devotee is "a kind friend
to all living entities" by whom "no one is put into difficulty" (BG 12.13–15). So the devotee keeps his practice
without a fight: he speaks so as not to agitate (BG 17.15), teaches by example rather than argument (BG 3.21; 3.26;
3.29), does his duties better than before (BG 3.8), and leaves others their freedom as Krsna left Arjuna his (BG 18.63).

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. State the scriptural ground for respecting parents (BG 17.14) and for not agitating others (BG 17.15; 3.26; 3.29).
2. Use the deck's three categories and five stages to decide, for a given person, whether to leave them alone, serve
   them patiently or invite them.
3. Describe parents' NICE (need, interest, concern, expectation) for their own parents and name one way to meet each.
4. Explain why the devotee is the best family member (BG 12.13–15; BG 3.8; SB 7.9.43's compassion), and why example
   beats argument (BG 3.21).
5. Choose one act this week that will make a parent *less* worried about their practice, and know where to take a real
   conflict (vl3-08).

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** BG 17.14–15, 3.21, 3.26/3.29 and 12.13–15 carry the talk; BG 18.63, 18.67–68 for whom to speak to; the
Bhagavatam verses in J.

**BG 17.14** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/17/14/
> "Austerity of the body consists in worship of the Supreme Lord, the brāhmaṇas, the spiritual master, and superiors like the father and mother, and in cleanliness, simplicity, celibacy and nonviolence."
- **Context:** Krsna lists austerities of body, speech and mind (17.14–16).
- **Teaching:** Respect for father and mother is a prescribed austerity, in the same verse as worship of the Lord.
- **Purport point:** "One should offer, or learn to offer, respect to God or to the demigods, the perfect, qualified
  brāhmaṇas and the spiritual master and superiors like father, mother or any person who is conversant with Vedic
  knowledge. These should be given proper respect."
- **Application (preacher's):** The verse that settles the tone of the whole topic. A devotee who disrespects his
  parents in the name of Krsna has failed an austerity Krsna Himself named. Say it first.
- **Use:** in the talk; the verse to remember.

**BG 17.15** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/17/15/
> "Austerity of speech consists in speaking words that are truthful, pleasing, beneficial, and not agitating to others, and also in regularly reciting Vedic literature."
- **Purport point:** "a teacher should not speak to those who are not his students if he will agitate their minds."
- **Application (preacher's):** Four tests for every sentence at the dinner table: true, pleasing, beneficial, not
  agitating. Most family arguments fail the last two. *Truthful* also rules out deceiving parents about where one goes
  (F.6).
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 3.8** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/8/
> "Perform your prescribed duty, for doing so is better than not working. One cannot even maintain one's physical body without work."
- **Purport point:** "So-called renunciation for the purpose of maintenance is never approved by the Lord."
- **Application (preacher's):** Parents' first fear is studies and career. The Gita answers it: duty is to be done.
  The deck's practical line, show them your improved grades, is this verse made visible.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 3.21** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/21/
> "Whatever action a great man performs, common men follow. And whatever standards he sets by exemplary acts, all the world pursues."
- **Purport point:** "A leader cannot teach the public to stop smoking if he himself smokes. Lord Caitanya said that
  a teacher should behave properly before he begins teaching."
- **Application (preacher's):** At home the student is not a preacher; he is an example or nothing. The deck's "your
  conviction, behaviour and stability" is this verse.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 3.26** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/26/
> "So as not to disrupt the minds of ignorant men attached to the fruitive results of prescribed duties, a learned person should not induce them to stop work. Rather, by working in the spirit of devotion, he should engage them in all sorts of activities [for the gradual development of Kṛṣṇa consciousness]."
- **Purport point:** "a realized soul in Kṛṣṇa consciousness should not disturb others in their activities or
  understanding, but he should act by showing how the results of all work can be dedicated to the service of Kṛṣṇa."
- **Application (preacher's):** The Gita's own instruction not to unsettle people who are not asking. Parents who
  are not asking are not to be lectured. Show, do not disrupt.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 3.29** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/29/
> "Bewildered by the modes of material nature, the ignorant fully engage themselves in material activities and become attached. But the wise should not unsettle them, although these duties are inferior due to the performers' lack of knowledge."
- **Purport point:** "Those who are enlightened in spiritual life, however, should not try to agitate such materially
  engrossed persons. Better to prosecute one's own spiritual activities silently." And: "the devotees of the Lord are
  more kind than the Lord… they undertake all kinds of risks, even to the point of approaching ignorant men".
- **Application (preacher's):** Both halves matter: "silently" at home; "approaching" is for those who show
  readiness (the deck's third category). **Caution:** the verse's word "ignorant" is a technical term for those without
  spiritual knowledge; never use it of anyone's parents in class.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 18.63** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/63/
> "Thus I have explained to you knowledge still more confidential. Deliberate on this fully, and then do what you wish to do."
- **Purport point:** "God does not interfere with the little independence of the living entity."
- **Application (preacher's):** If Krsna leaves Arjuna free after the Gita, the student leaves his parents and friends
  free after one conversation. No pressure, no repeated campaigns.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 18.67** and **BG 18.68** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/67/
> 18.67: "This confidential knowledge may never be explained to those who are not austere, or devoted, or engaged in devotional service, nor to one who is envious of Me."
> 18.68: "For one who explains this supreme secret to the devotees, pure devotional service is guaranteed, and at the end he will come back to Me."
- **Purport point (18.68):** "Bhagavad-gītā should be explained to persons who are ready to accept Kṛṣṇa."
- **Application (preacher's):** The scriptural basis for the deck's sorting: speak of Krsna to those ready to hear;
  do not force it on the hostile. Readiness, not relationship, decides.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 12.13-14** and **BG 12.15** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/12/13-14/
> 12.13–14: "One who is not envious but is a kind friend to all living entities, who does not think himself a proprietor and is free from false ego, who is equal in both happiness and distress, who is tolerant, always satisfied, self-controlled…"
> 12.15: "He by whom no one is put into difficulty and who is not disturbed by anyone, who is equipoised in happiness and distress, fear and anxiety, is very dear to Me."
- **Purport point (12.13–14):** "Nor does a devotee become his enemy's enemy"; "A devotee is also always kind to
  everyone, even to his enemy." (12.15): "Since a devotee is kind to everyone, he does not act in such a way as to put
  others into anxiety."
- **Application (preacher's):** The portrait of the devotee as family member: kind to all, puts no one into anxiety,
  not disturbed by mockery. A parent should find the son *easier* to live with after VL3, not harder.
- **Use:** in the talk; the second verse to remember.

**BG 2.14** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/14/
> "…one must learn to tolerate them without being disturbed."
- **Application (preacher's):** For the mocking friend: tolerance is a skill, not a defeat.
- **Use:** in K.

**SB 3.25.20** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/3/25/20/
> "Every learned man knows very well that attachment for the material is the greatest entanglement of the spirit soul. But that same attachment, when applied to the self-realized devotees, opens the door of liberation."
- **Purport point:** "Attachment cannot be killed; it has simply to be transferred."
- **Application (preacher's):** Why keeping devotee association matters while living at home: the heart needs an
  attachment that lifts. **Caution:** this verse is not a mandate to withdraw affection from family; it is about where
  the heart's deepest attachment rests. At VL3 the application is "keep coming to class", not "love your parents less".
- **Use:** in the talk, with the caution.

**NOI 2** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/2/
> "…(5) associating with worldly-minded persons who are not interested in Kṛṣṇa consciousness…"
- **Purport point:** "Jana-saṅga refers to associating with persons not interested in Kṛṣṇa consciousness. One should
  strictly avoid such association"; Narottama dasa Thakura's advice to live in the association of devotees.
- **Application (preacher's):** The purport is strict; do not soften its words. But a VL3 student lives with his
  parents, and BG 17.14 makes respecting them an austerity, so avoiding *association* cannot mean shunning or
  neglecting them. Apply it as: do not take up worldly values, talk and entertainments as your own, and keep regular
  devotee association (class, facilitator, a devotee friend). Present this as the application, every time, or the
  verse will be misused (F.5).
- **Use:** in the talk, defined.

**SB 4.8.11** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/8/11/
> "Queen Suruci told Dhruva Mahārāja: My dear child, you do not deserve to sit on the throne or on the lap of the King. Surely you are also the son of the King, but because you did not take your birth from my womb, you are not qualified to sit on your father's lap."
- **Context:** The insult that sends the child Dhruva weeping to his mother Suniti, who directs him to the Lord
  (4.8.13–24); Narada then guides him (4.8.25 onward).
- **Application (preacher's):** The scriptural picture of a family wound met by a mother's wise counsel and a saint's
  guidance, not by retaliation. Dhruva does not fight his stepmother; on his mother's counsel he turns to the Lord.
  **Caution:** Dhruva then leaves for the forest (4.8.25 onward); that is not the VL3 point and is never told as a
  model for leaving home (K.8). Read the chapter before telling it.
- **Use:** in J.

**SB 7.9.43** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/9/43/
> "…My concern is only for the fools and rascals who are making elaborate plans for material happiness and maintaining their families, societies and countries. I am simply concerned with love for them."
- **Application (preacher's):** Prahlada's attitude to those absorbed in worldly plans is *concern out of love*. Take
  only that: the devotee's response to relatives who do not understand is compassion, not contempt. **Caution:** the
  verse's strong words ("fools and rascals") are Prahlada's about worldly planners in general and are never to be
  applied to a student's own parents; quote the last sentence, and read the first as the Bhagavatam's language.
- **Use:** in F.4, with the caution.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The frame (the deck's, kept).** "The art of not getting affected by materialistic philosophy and association,
   at the same time not getting alienated." Two failures: the student who absorbs the family's anxieties and drifts
   from practice; and the student who becomes a stranger in his own home. The Gita rules out both: keep your practice
   (BG 3.8, 3.21) *and* put no one in difficulty (BG 12.15).
2. **Respect first.** BG 17.14 lists respect for father and mother as a bodily austerity alongside worship of the Lord.
   This is the student's first duty at home, before any explaining. Practically: greet them, eat with them, help with
   what they need, keep your word to them. A parent whose son has become more respectful will tolerate a great deal of
   Sunday class.
3. **Why people react as they do (the deck's two tools).** (a) *Three categories:* those who will never understand;
   those who will understand after some time; those who will want to become like you. (b) *Five stages of an
   observer:* criticise, mock, neutral, favour, follow. People move up the stages as they watch the student's
   "conviction, behaviour and stability" over time (BG 3.21: example sets the standard). The deck's key explanation
   for parents, kept in its own words: "People may be good but they may not know the real purpose of life… They have
   good heart for you but they may not understand importance of spiritual life. Not because they are demons but
   because they have never got informed." This is the whole tone.
4. **Dealing by category (the deck, adjusted to the texts).**
   - *Those who will never understand (critics):* BG 3.29, "should not unsettle them… prosecute one's own spiritual
     activities silently"; BG 18.67, do not press the confidential knowledge on the hostile; BG 2.14, tolerate; BG
     12.13–14, "nor does a devotee become his enemy's enemy". The deck says "boldly and logically defeat them, avoid
     them, don't become inimical"; keep "don't become inimical", and replace "defeat them" with "answer once, briefly,
     with a source (vl3-09), then stop". Winning an argument at home loses the home.
   - *Those who will understand after some time (mockers, neutrals):* the deck: "don't criticise or debate with them;
     help them (*suhṛdam*); prasadam". BG 3.26: engage them, do not disrupt them; SB 7.9.43: concern out of love. Take
     prasadam home; help with their work; let time and example (BG 3.21) do what argument cannot.
   - *Those who want to become like you (favour, follow):* BG 18.68, "explains this supreme secret to the devotees";
     the deck: "bring them to classes". Invite, once, warmly; leave the choice (BG 18.63).
5. **Association, defined carefully.** NOI 2's *jana-saṅga* and SB 3.25.20's "attachment… transferred" are often
   misread at this age as "stay away from my family". At VL3, apply them as (E, NOI 2): do not adopt worldly *values*
   and *talk* as your own,
   and keep your heart's deepest attachment where it lifts you (devotees, class, chanting). A student who cites these
   verses to skip his father's hospital visit has inverted them; BG 17.14 and 12.15 govern. Say this plainly.
6. **Parents: NICE (the deck).** *Need* (help in the house, their health, their dignity), *Interest* (your studies,
   your future, your safety), *Concern* (that you will neglect your career, leave home, become a monk), *Expectation*
   (that you will be present at family occasions, respectful, honest). Meet each: serve their need; report what
   interests them (the deck: "you have to tell how you have improved your grades – they are interested in that"); answer
   the concern with BG 3.8 lived (grades, punctuality) and with vl3-09's one-liners; fulfil the expectation (attend the
   wedding; keep the promise). The deck's "see things from their perspective"; "work with head, not with heart only";
   "don't accept all proposals, don't fight also". **One adjustment:** the deck says "what method you are adopting and
   how you are spending every moment you don't have to tell". Read it as: you need not narrate every detail, and you may
   choose your moment; but BG 17.15 begins with *truthful*. Never let a parent discover that their son has been
   deceiving them about where he goes; nothing destroys trust faster, and nothing is less Krsna conscious.
7. **Friends.** The same categories. Friends who mock test BG 2.14 and 12.15 ("not disturbed by anyone"). Friends who
   drift away are not to be chased or resented. The deck's observation that the student will be misunderstood at first
   is realistic; BG 3.21 says what changes it: time and example. One devotee friend (NOI 4, vl3-08) steadies the rest.
8. **What the devotee brings to a family.** BG 12.13–15: kind to all, free from envy, puts no one in difficulty,
   undisturbed. BG 3.8: duty done. BG 17.14: respect. SB 7.9.43: love even for those who plan without Krsna. A family
   that gains such a son has gained; the preacher's task is to make the student *that* son, and let the family notice.
9. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - "Family is maya; detach." SB 3.25.20 is about where the deepest attachment rests, not about withdrawing care.
     BG 17.14 commands respect; BG 12.15 forbids causing anxiety.
   - "I must convince my parents." BG 18.63: even Krsna left Arjuna free. BG 3.29: do not unsettle. Live it; they will see.
   - "If they oppose, I should leave home." Not at VL3, not alone, not from a class. Any serious conflict goes to the
     facilitator and seniors (K.8).
   - "I may hide my practice to keep peace." BG 17.15: truthful first. Discretion about details, never deception.
10. **Pitch for VL3.** Practical, encouraging, warm about parents. Terms: *suhṛdam* (well-wisher), *jana-saṅga*
    (defined), NICE. One practice: one act this week that makes a parent less worried.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet, say your name, and say this session is about keeping both your practice and your family close.
2. Say that nobody is asked to share anything about their own family; all cases are invented.
3. Ask H.2 question 1; collect two or three light answers on the board.
4. Ask H.2 question 2 silently for private thought.
5. Say that if anyone has a serious difficulty at home, they can speak to the facilitator one-to-one afterwards (K.8).

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the room's own life)
1. When you first told family or friends what you were doing, what was their first reaction?
2. Who in your family or village or town do you most want to understand you? (Private.)
3. What does a person in your life do that makes you feel respected?

- **Opening question:** "When you first came home from a class and said where you'd been, what did your mother say?
  Your friends?" Collect reactions on the board; sort them into the five stages later.
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** Karan's mother says, "You've become very serious. You don't laugh with us any
  more." Karan says, "You wouldn't understand." Ask: which of the two failures (F.1) is this, and what did it cost?
- **Transition:** "Krsna has a verse for Karan's mother and a verse for Karan."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** the family's reaction over the next year decides more about whether they keep
  chanting than any lecture does.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Respect for parents is a prescribed austerity.** BG 17.14. *Example:* the same son, more respectful.
   *Transition:* what does Krsna say the devotee is like at home?
2. **Kind to all; puts no one in difficulty.** BG 12.13–15. *Example:* Karan's mother. *Transition:* then why do people
   react badly?
3. **Three categories, five stages; example moves people.** BG 3.21; the deck. *Example:* the uncle who mocked for two
   years and now asks for prasadam. *Transition:* how to deal with each.
4. **Silence for the hostile, service for the slow, invitation for the ready.** BG 3.29/18.67; BG 3.26/SB 7.9.43; BG
   18.68/18.63. *Example:* "answer once, then stop". *Transition:* and parents specifically?
5. **NICE, met; truthful always.** The deck; BG 3.8; BG 17.15. *Example:* grades shown, promises kept. *Transition:* to P.

## J. Stories and illustrations

- **Dhruva and Suniti** (SB 4.8.9–25): the stepmother's insult (4.8.11), the child's tears, the mother's counsel to
  seek the Lord, Narada's guidance. The family wound is real; the response is not retaliation but turning to the Lord
  with a guide. Read the chapter; tell only what it says.
- **Arjuna's family** (BG 1): the Gita itself opens with a man torn between duty and relatives, and Krsna does not
  tell him to stop loving them; He tells him what to do. One line.
- **Prahlada's love for those who do not understand** (SB 7.9.43, with the caution in E): one line on compassion as
  the devotee's stance. *Not* the Prahlada–Hiranyakasipu conflict, which is not a model for any home.
- **Srila Prabhupada and his own family:** he lived as a householder for decades, maintaining his family while
  serving his guru's order, and took sannyasa only late in life. *Srila Prabhupada-lilamrta* has the account;
  **Partially verified**: read it before giving details; tell as Satsvarupa dasa Goswami's account.
- **Contemporary image (the preacher's):** a lamp in a room. It does not argue with the dark; it is lit, and people
  gradually move towards it.
- **Traditional saying (the deck's "elephant and dog"):** the elephant walks on while dogs bark. Label it as a saying;
  it illustrates BG 12.15's "not disturbed by anyone". *(The deck's Hindi line "Bola to meet khane laga" is not
  understood from the extract and is left out.)*

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

> **Before you teach:** decide what you will say to a student in real conflict at home (K.8), and have your
> facilitator's and a senior's names ready. Do not improvise advice to leave home or defy parents; you will not give it.

1. **"My parents think ISKCON will make me leave studies and become a monk."**
   - BG 3.8: the Gita tells you to do your duty; show them the marks (the deck). Give the one-liner from vl3-09 and
     invite them to meet your facilitator. Then let a semester of results speak (BG 3.21).
2. **"My father mocks me every time I chant."**
   - BG 2.14 and 12.15: tolerate, undisturbed. Chant where it does not provoke; never answer mockery with argument
     (BG 3.29: "silently"). In the deck's five stages, mockers often move to neutral when the son is more helpful and
     more cheerful.
3. **"Should I stop eating what my mother cooks?"**
   - Not abruptly, and never with contempt. Help her cook; offer the food together; eat with the family. The four
     principles (vl3-02) are about what you eat, not about rejecting her kitchen. If diet becomes a flashpoint, bring it to
     your facilitator; there is almost always a quiet arrangement.
4. **"Isn't my family 'bad association' (NOI 2)?"**
   - Not in the sense you fear. NOI 2's purport says to avoid the company of those not interested in Krsna
     consciousness; for you, living at home, that means not adopting worldly values and talk as your own. It never
     means shunning your parents: BG
     17.14 commands respect for them; BG 12.15 forbids causing them anxiety. Keep devotee association *as well*, not
     *instead*.
5. **"My friends have drifted away since I started."**
   - Some will (the deck's first category); don't chase or resent them. Keep one devotee friend (NOI 4, vl3-08). Stay
     kind to the old friends (BG 12.13–14); a few will come back to the third category when they see you steady.
6. **"Can I just not tell my parents where I go on Sundays?"**
   - No. BG 17.15 begins with *truthful*. You may choose your moment and need not narrate every detail, but never let
     them discover a deception. Nothing damages your standing, or Krsna's in their eyes, more.
7. **"They want me at a family wedding on the day of the camp."**
   - Go to the wedding, cheerfully (parents' *expectation*; BG 17.14). Camps recur. A son who skips family occasions
     for class confirms every fear; one who attends them well disarms it.
8. **"My father has said he will throw me out if I keep going."**
   - Stop here and take it to your facilitator this week (vl3-08), who will bring in a senior. Do not decide alone and
     do not escalate. In the meantime: respect, duty, patience (BG 17.14; 3.8; 2.14). No class will tell you to leave
     home; anyone who does is not speaking for IYF. *(Pastoral guidance, labelled:)* if there is violence or the
     student is not safe, the facilitator involves a senior at once and, where needed, professional or legal help
     (vl3-08, F.8).

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The Sunday argument.** Karan (H) misses a cousin's birthday for class; his mother says he loves "those people"
more than his family; he replies that she "wouldn't understand".
- **Principles:** BG 17.14 (respect); BG 17.15 (not agitating); BG 12.15 (no one put in difficulty); parents'
  *expectation* (F.6).
- **Questions:** Which failure in F.1 is this? What should Karan have done about the birthday? What one sentence
  repairs "you wouldn't understand"?
- **Takeaway:** Attend the birthday; the class will be there next week; so will the lamp.

**Case 2: The slow uncle.** An uncle mocked Riya's "Hare Krishna phase" for two years. Last Diwali he asked her to bring
"that sweet from the temple" and asked what she reads.
- **Principles:** the deck's second category moving to the fourth stage; BG 3.21; BG 3.26; BG 18.68 (now ready).
- **Questions:** What moved him? What should Riya do now (bring prasadam; answer the question briefly; invite once)?
  What should she not do (overwhelm him)?
- **Takeaway:** Time and example did the work; now one warm invitation, then freedom (BG 18.63).

**Case 3: The hidden Sundays.** Nitin tells his parents he is at "coaching" on Sundays. They find out from a neighbour.
- **Principles:** BG 17.15 (truthful first); F.6's adjustment of the deck; BG 17.14.
- **Questions:** What has Nitin lost that honesty would have kept? How does he repair it (full honesty now, an apology,
  an invitation to meet the facilitator)? What should he have said six months ago?
- **Takeaway:** Discretion about details, never deception about the fact.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Krsna lists respect for father and mother as an austerity, in the same verse as worship of the Lord, the brahmanas (learned teachers) and the spiritual master (BG 17.14), together with cleanliness, simplicity, celibacy and nonviolence. The devotee He describes is "a kind friend to all living entities" by whom "no one is put into difficulty" (BG 12.13-15). So dealing with family and friends begins with respect and with speech that is "truthful, pleasing, beneficial, and not agitating" (BG 17.15).

People react in stages, from criticising and mocking to neutral, favouring and following, and what moves them is the example of a great person's actions (BG 3.21): your conviction, behaviour and stability over time, not argument. For those who will not understand: answer once, then keep your practice quietly (BG 3.29). For those who will understand in time: serve them, bring prasadam (food offered to Krsna), do not debate (BG 3.26). For those who want what you have: invite them once, and leave the choice (BG 18.68; 18.63).

Parents have a Need, an Interest, a Concern and an Expectation (NICE); meet them, especially by doing your duties well (BG 3.8) and never by deceiving them. Your family is not "bad association": NOI 2 warns against the company of the worldly-minded, and at home you apply it by not adopting worldly values and talk, while BG 17.14 makes respecting your parents a duty. Keep devotee friends as well, not instead. A serious conflict at home goes to your facilitator, never to a decision made alone.

## S2. Key ideas

- Respect for parents is a prescribed austerity (BG 17.14).
- Speech: truthful, pleasing, beneficial, not agitating (BG 17.15).
- Example moves people; argument does not (BG 3.21). Do not unsettle those who are not asking (BG 3.26; 3.29).
- Five stages: criticise, mock, neutral, favour, follow. Act by stage.
- Parents' NICE: Need, Interest, Concern, Expectation. Meet each; show your results (BG 3.8).
- Family is not "bad association"; at home, avoiding it means not adopting worldly values (NOI 2, applied).
- Leave others free, as Krsna left Arjuna (BG 18.63). No one put in difficulty (BG 12.15).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **BG 17.14:** "Austerity of the body consists in worship of the Supreme Lord, the brāhmaṇas, the spiritual master, and superiors like the father and mother…" *For me:* respecting my parents is part of my sadhana.
- **BG 17.15:** "Austerity of speech consists in speaking words that are truthful, pleasing, beneficial, and not agitating to others…" *For me:* four tests before I speak at the dinner table.
- **BG 12.15:** "He by whom no one is put into difficulty and who is not disturbed by anyone… is very dear to Me." *For me:* my family should find me easier to live with, not harder.

## S4. Words to know

- **Suhrdam:** a well-wisher; the devotee's stance toward those who do not yet understand.
- **Jana-sanga:** association with persons not interested in Krsna consciousness, which NOI 2 says to avoid; for a student at home it means not taking up worldly values and talk as his own, never shunning his parents (BG 17.14).
- **NICE:** Need, Interest, Concern, Expectation: what your parents have about you (the IYF framework).
- **Five stages:** criticise, mock, neutral, favour, follow: how observers move as they watch you.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **My parents fear I'll leave studies and become a monk.** Do your duty (BG 3.8) and show them the marks. Give the one-line answer from VL3-09 and invite them to meet your facilitator. Let a semester speak.
2. **My father mocks my chanting.** Tolerate, undisturbed (BG 2.14; 12.15). Chant where it doesn't provoke. Never argue with mockery; be more helpful and more cheerful.
3. **Is my family "bad association"?** No. Living at home, you avoid bad association by not adopting worldly values as your own. BG 17.14 commands respect for parents. Keep devotee friends as well, not instead.
4. **Can I just not tell them where I go on Sundays?** No. "Truthful" comes first in BG 17.15. Choose your moment; never deceive.
5. **They want me at a wedding on the camp day.** Go, cheerfully. Camps recur; a son who attends family occasions well disarms every fear.
6. **My father threatens to throw me out.** Take it to your facilitator this week; do not decide alone. No class will tell you to leave home.
