# Dhrtarastra quits home

> BSS (Bhakti Siddhanta Sabha, brahmacari training) · 120 min · drafted, not yet reviewed · not sensitive.

## A. Topic overview
| | |
|---|---|
| Batch / level | BSS (brahmacari training), narrative from Srimad-Bhagavatam |
| Suggested duration | 120 min lecture, with an "if time allows" extension |
| Preparation time | About 2 hours (read SB 1.13 in full, with purports) |
| Difficulty | Intermediate |
| Prerequisites | None; the story is told from the beginning |
| Audience | Young men training as brahmacaris (celibate students), at any stage of schooling or work |
| Teaching objective | Participants see, through one old king's life, how attachment blinds a person to time, and why a student who learns detachment early is fortunate. They leave with one concrete practice of "reading time" in their own day. |

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level
A brahmacari is in training for the whole of life. Dhrtarastra shows what the untrained life looks like at its end: a
learned, powerful man who sees the end only when it is forced on him. The page is a story with a single lesson (time
and attachment), so it sits early in the BSS sequence, before the technical chapters on celibacy.

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| VL1 | A five-minute story about "the one thing everyone postpones". |
| VL2 | SB 1.13 as evidence that the body is temporary (reasoning from the verses). |
| VL3 | Time (kala) as the Lord's energy and its place in daily sadhana. |
| SPS | Householder duty versus renunciation; the three types of transcendentalist in SB 1.13.27 purport. |
| DPS | Vidura as a guru-figure and how a senior gives unwelcome advice. |
| BSS | This page. |

Not done here: the full Mahabharata background of Dhrtarastra's sons and the war (only what SB 1.13 itself tells);
the technical rules of celibacy (see the BIKC pages bss-16 to bss-19).

## C. Central message
Time is the Lord's own energy and it does not negotiate; the wise person leaves his attachments willingly, early, and
depends on the Lord, instead of being stripped of them at the end.

## D. Learning outcomes
After the session participants can:
1. Retell, in order, how Vidura persuaded Dhrtarastra and what Dhrtarastra did (SB 1.13.17-29).
2. Identify the three things Vidura named: eternal time, helplessness before it, and the misery of clinging (1.13.17-25).
3. Explain the difference between the three kinds of leaving home in the purport to SB 1.13.27.
4. Apply the lesson to a brahmacari's day: one habit that "reads time" instead of denying it.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**SB 1.13.1** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/13/1/
> "…returned to Hastināpura."
- Context: Sūta Gosvāmī opens the chapter; Vidura has been on pilgrimage and has received knowledge from the sage Maitreya.
- Teaching: Vidura returns with realised knowledge, not merely information; this is why his advice has weight.
- Purport point: Not needed for the talk.
- Application (preacher's): Advice from someone who has first practised it carries a different weight; a leader of a brahmacari group should speak from practice.
- Use: in the talk (one sentence).

**SB 1.13.17** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/13/17/
> "Insurmountable eternal time imperceptibly overcomes those who are too much attached to family affairs…"
- Context: Vidura begins his counsel to Dhrtarastra.
- Teaching: Time acts without being noticed; the person who is deeply attached does not notice.
- Purport point: Prabhupada gives the inner voice of the attached householder ("I am now happy; I have everything in order…") and says he is "blind to the passing of eternal time."
- Application (preacher's): For a student the same voice sounds like "after the exams," "after I settle," "after this season." The verse is the cure for "later."
- Use: in the talk.

**SB 1.13.19** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/13/19/
> "…it is the Supreme Personality of Godhead as eternal time [kāla] that has approached us all."
- Context: Vidura says no one in the world can remedy this situation.
- Teaching: Time is not an impersonal clock; it is the Lord Himself in the form that removes everything.
- Purport point: No superior power can check death; old age is "the notice of the arrival of death."
- Application (preacher's): This is why fear is a poor response and surrender a good one: one cannot defeat the Lord, but one can take shelter of Him.
- Use: in the talk. Compare BG 11.32 ("Time I am…") in the extension.

**SB 1.13.20** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/13/20/
> "…must surrender his most dear life, and what to speak of other things, such as wealth, honor, children, land and home."
- Context: Vidura lists what time takes.
- Teaching: If life itself is surrendered, nothing smaller is safe.
- Purport point: Prabhupada recalls a busy planner called away on his way to a meeting; time comes in the middle of plans.
- Application (preacher's): A brahmacari can ask which of his possessions he would carry out; only his service and knowledge go with him.
- Use: in the talk.

**SB 1.13.21-22** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/13/21/ and /22/
> "…you are living in the home of another." (1.13.21) · "You have been blind from your very birth…" (1.13.22)
- Context: Vidura lists the facts: father, brother, sons gone; age; failing body.
- Teaching: Time has already taken most of what Dhrtarastra loved; the evidence is in front of him.
- Purport point (1.13.22): the signs of old age are warnings that death is near, yet he was "foolishly carefree."
- Application (preacher's): Respectful honesty: Vidura names facts, not insults.
- Use: in the talk.

**SB 1.13.25** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/13/25/
> "…your miserly body will certainly dwindle and deteriorate like an old garment."
- Context: Dhrtarastra wants to live on, even at the cost of honor.
- Teaching: Clinging to life for its own sake is called miserliness (krpana).
- Purport point: Two classes of men, the krpana (miser) and the brahmana; the brahmana values the body rightly and uses it for realisation.
- Application (preacher's): A brahmacari is training to be a "brahmana" in this sense: to value the body as an instrument.
- Use: in the talk.

**SB 1.13.26-27** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/13/26/ and /27/
> "He is called undisturbed who goes to an unknown, remote place and, freed from all obligations, quits his material body when it has become useless." (1.13.26)
> "He is certainly a first-class man who awakens and understands…the falsity and misery of this material world and thus leaves home and depends fully on the Personality of Godhead…" (1.13.27)
- Context: Vidura describes two kinds of wise people.
- Teaching: The first-class person awakens by himself or from others and leaves home to depend on the Lord in the heart.
- Purport point (1.13.27): three classes: the dhira (undisturbed by separation from family), the sannyasi "by frustrated sentiment," and the sincere devotee who awakens God consciousness by hearing and chanting.
- Application (preacher's): "Frustrated sentiment" is a warning to a young renunciate: leaving because of disappointment is not the same as leaving because of awakening.
- Use: in the talk.

**SB 1.13.28-29** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/13/28/ and /29/
> "…leave for the North immediately, without letting your relatives know…" (1.13.28)
> "…broke at once the strong network of familial affection by his resolute determination." (1.13.29)
- Context: Vidura's urgent request, and Dhrtarastra's response, the same night.
- Teaching: Resolute determination, informed by introspective knowledge (prajna), cuts attachment.
- Purport point: Prabhupada notes the approach of Kali-yuga as a reason for urgency.
- Application (preacher's): Note both halves: Vidura's counsel and Dhrtarastra's acceptance. Counsel works only when it is also received.
- Use: in the talk.

**SB 1.13.55 and 1.13.57** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/13/55/ and /57/
> "…he will quit his body, most probably on the fifth day from today. And his body will turn to ashes." (1.13.57)
- Context: Narada Muni tells Yudhisthira how the end will come for his uncle.
- Teaching: The leaving was not an escape; it was the beginning of practice (yoga), and the end was peaceful.
- Application (preacher's): For the talk say only what Narada says; do not add details of the ending.
- Use: background for the preacher; one sentence if time allows.

**BG 11.32** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/11/32/
> "Time I am, the great destroyer of the worlds…"
- Context: Krsna speaks to Arjuna on the battlefield of Kuruksetra, the same war that destroyed Dhrtarastra's sons.
- Teaching: The same Kala that Vidura names speaks in the Gita.
- Application (preacher's): Ties the story to the Gita they already know. Use: in the talk (extension).

**SB 2.1.4** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/2/1/4/
> "Persons devoid of ātma-tattva do not inquire into the problems of life, being too attached to the fallible soldiers like the body, children and wife."
- Teaching: The general principle behind Dhrtarastra's case.
- Use: extension.

**BG 2.14** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/14/
> "…one must learn to tolerate them without being disturbed."
- Use: K (the question "should I not enjoy anything?").

## F. Philosophical explanation
**Principle.** Time (kala) is a feature of the Lord's energy (BG 11.32; SB 1.13.19). Every material relationship has an
expiry date. **Why it matters.** The student years are the cheapest time to learn what Dhrtarastra learned at the end.
**Connection.** SB 1.13 is the scriptural base for later BSS themes: detachment, discipline, simplicity. **Consequence.**
Without training, attachment grows with age (the king is old, blind, hard of hearing, and still attached). **Common
misunderstandings.** (1) "Leaving home means abandoning duty." Dhrtarastra's duty to his family had been
discharged to the extent possible; Vidura's advice concerns one who lives only by another's charity. (2) "A brahmacari
should leave his parents without a word." The story is about an elderly king; for a young person, guru's
instruction and parents' welfare are part of the training (see bss-16 on submission to guru). (3) "Fear is the
motive." Vidura names facts to induce awakening (prajna), not panic.
**How to pitch it.** Tell it as a story of a respectful brother and a stubborn brother. Let the verses carry the point.

## H. Opening and engagement
**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes).** Say: "Raise a hand if you have ever said 'I will start properly after…'
(exams, next month, next year)." Count the hands out loud, thank them for honesty, and say: "Today we meet a king who
said it for sixty or seventy years, and his younger brother who finally told him the truth." Then write on the board:
**TIME** and ask what colour it is. Take two answers. Move on.
**H.2 Opening questions.**
1. What is one thing you keep postponing? (Everyone can answer; write answers on the board without comment.)
2. Who in your life tells you the truth even when it is not pleasant? Why do you listen to them?
3. If you knew you had only one year left, what would you stop doing today?

## I. Core teaching points
1. **Time works unnoticed (SB 1.13.17).** The attached person does not feel it passing. *Example:* a student scrolling
   through a phone for "five minutes" and finding an evening gone. *Transition:* Vidura does not argue; he lists facts.
2. **The facts speak (SB 1.13.21-22).** Vidura names who is gone and what has failed in the body. *Example:* Prabhupada
   says old age is a notice served by time. *Transition:* What is the response?
3. **Time is the Lord (SB 1.13.19; BG 11.32).** Fear has nowhere to go; shelter does. *Transition:* Shelter requires
   leaving something.
4. **Two kinds of leaving (SB 1.13.26-27).** One leaves in awakening; another leaves from frustration. The Bhagavatam
   praises the first and its purport warns of the second. *Transition:* Dhrtarastra decides.
5. **Application (preacher's) for a brahmacari: practise now what Dhrtarastra did at the end.** Detachment is not
   dramatic: it is a daily set of small renunciations (the schedule in bss-44 and bss-45 gives the form). The fortunate
   student learns it while his senses are strong, from a Vidura-like guide, not from a failing body.

## J. Stories and illustrations
Source: SB 1.13 (data/stories.json, id "dhrtarastra"; key verse SB 1.13.20).
**The story, in order (all from SB 1.13):**
1. Vidura, after pilgrimage and instruction from Maitreya, returns to Hastinapura (1.13.1). The Pandavas, led by
   Yudhisthira, are ruling; Dhrtarastra and his wife Gandhari live in the palace.
2. Vidura speaks to Dhrtarastra: leave now, do not delay (1.13.18). He lists the facts (1.13.17-25) and describes the
   first-class person (1.13.26-27).
3. Dhrtarastra, convinced by introspective knowledge, cuts the "network of familial affection" and leaves that very
   night, with Gandhari following him toward the Himalayas (1.13.28-30).
4. In the morning Yudhisthira searches for his uncle; Narada arrives and explains (1.13.32 onwards); Dhrtarastra will
   leave his body on the fifth day (1.13.57).
**Telling tips.** Name Vidura's tone (calm, factual). Do not add dialogue beyond what the text says.
**Contemporary illustration (preacher's, labelled).** A man who has run a family business for forty years is asked
"what next?" and has no answer: all his identity was the business. Ask the group which parts of their own identity
are borrowed from things time will take, and end with the devotional point: the one identity time cannot take is
servant of Krsna (CC Madhya 20.108), nourished by hearing and chanting in the company of devotees.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections
1. *Isn't it cruel to leave a blind old man's family?* Dhrtarastra left, with Gandhari's company, after Vidura
   reminded him that he was living in another's home (1.13.21); the text does not frame it as cruelty. A young student's
   duties are different: serve guru, family welfare, and training.
2. *Should I not enjoy anything?* BG 2.14: sense-pleasures come and go; learn to tolerate. The point is not joylessness
   but not being owned by it.
3. *Why did Dhrtarastra not realise earlier?* SB 2.1.4: attachment prevents inquiry. The purport to 1.13.17 gives the
   inner voice. Inquiry needs association, which is why Vidura mattered.
4. *Is leaving home necessary for everyone?* No: SB 1.13.27's purport distinguishes types; the page does not claim it
   as an order for householders. Requires source review for any wider claim.
5. *What if I fear death?* Fear is natural (1.13.25: "unwillingness to die"); the remedy shown is dependence on the Lord
   within the heart (1.13.27).

## L. Case studies
**Invented case for discussion.** Arun is 19 and in the second year of a brahmacari programme. He enjoys the daily
schedule but keeps saying, "In a few years, when I am settled, I will be serious." His friend Dev notices that Arun
has said this every term.
- Dilemma: How does Dev help Arun without lecturing?
- Principles: SB 1.13.17 (time unnoticed), 1.13.27 (awakening from others), Vidura's tone.
- Questions: (1) What would Vidura do first? (2) What facts could Dev state? (3) What would be unhelpful?
- Analysis: Facts, not insults; one concrete step; invite, never force.
- Takeaway: "Later" is the form attachment takes in youth; the Krsna conscious answer is to begin hearing and chanting
  today in devotees' association (SB 1.13.27), not to rely on willpower alone.

## M. Discussion and reflection
**M.1 Closing check questions (with expected answers).**
1. Who advised Dhrtarastra? *Vidura, his younger brother (1.13.18).*
2. According to Vidura, what had overtaken Dhrtarastra? *Eternal time, the Lord Himself as kala (1.13.19).*
3. When did Dhrtarastra leave? *That very night, with Gandhari (1.13.28-30).*
4. What does "krpana" mean here? *A miser, one who values the body wrongly (purport to 1.13.25).*
5. Name the "first-class" person in 1.13.27. *One who awakens and leaves home depending on the Lord within.*
6. What is a brahmacari's version of "leaving home"? *Daily small detachments learned early; discuss.*
**M.2 Activities.**
- *The two lists (15 min, pairs).* Each writes two lists: "What time will take from me" and "What I keep after." Compare
  with 1.13.20. Share one item voluntarily.
- *Vidura's counsel (20 min, groups of 3).* Roles: Vidura, Dhrtarastra, narrator. Read SB 1.13.17-29 aloud in turn. Then
  discuss: what did Vidura do that made counsel acceptable?
- *Reading time (5 min).* Everyone writes one devotional act (extra rounds, hearing, service to the guru) they will not postpone today.
**M.3 Reflection.** Private: "What am I protecting that time will take anyway?" No sharing required.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary
Dhrtarastra was an old, blind king who lived in the palace of his nephews. His younger brother Vidura, who had just
returned from pilgrimage with real knowledge, told him plainly: time, which is the Lord Himself, has come for you; your
father, brother and sons are gone, you live in another's home, and your body is failing (SB 1.13.17-22). No one can
stop this (1.13.19). Vidura described the first-class person, who awakens to the falsity of material life and depends on
the Lord (1.13.27). Dhrtarastra accepted, cut his family attachment and left that night with his wife Gandhari
(1.13.28-30). Narada later told Yudhisthira that Dhrtarastra would leave his body on the fifth day (1.13.57). For a
brahmacari the lesson is to learn detachment now, while strong, instead of waiting for time to force it.

## S2. Key ideas
- Time is the Lord Himself, and it acts unnoticed.
- Attachment blinds even a wise, experienced person.
- Good advice states facts respectfully, and good listeners accept it.
- Learn detachment early.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **SB 1.13.17** — "Insurmountable eternal time imperceptibly overcomes…" Time passes while I feel busy.
- **SB 1.13.27** — The first-class person "leaves home and depends fully on the Personality of Godhead." Awakening, not frustration, is the right reason.

## S4. Words to know
- *Kala* — time; here, the Lord's form as the remover of all things.
- *Krpana* — a miser; one who values the body wrongly.
- *Dhira* — one who is undisturbed.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *Who was Vidura?* Dhrtarastra's younger brother, who returned from pilgrimage with knowledge (1.13.1).
2. *Why did Dhrtarastra leave?* He accepted that time was upon him and cut his attachment (1.13.29).
3. *Does a brahmacari leave home like this?* No; the story teaches the principle: learn detachment early, under guru.
4. *Is it wrong to enjoy?* Pleasure comes and goes; learn to tolerate it (BG 2.14), and do not be owned by it.

## S6. Check your understanding
1. Who is "eternal time" in 1.13.19? (a) A planet (b) the Supreme Lord (c) the king
2. Name two facts Vidura told Dhrtarastra.
3. When did Dhrtarastra leave?
4. What does the purport to 1.13.25 say about the krpana?
5. Why is leaving from "frustrated sentiment" different from awakening?

**Answers:** 1 (b), 1.13.19. 2 Father, brother and sons dead; living in another's home; body failing (1.13.21-22). 3 That
night (1.13.28-30). 4 He values the body wrongly and clings to it. 5 Awakening comes from understanding; frustration
comes from disappointment (purport to 1.13.27).

## S7. Try this week
Each morning name one act of service, hearing or chanting you will not postpone, and do it first. Private reflection: "What would I do
differently if I believed 'later' might not come?"
