# The twenty four gurus of Avadhuta brahmana

> Status line: BSS · 120 min · drafted · not sensitive. SB 11.7-11.9 is BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples. Builds on bss-07; the python and the honeybee are left to bss-09.

## A. Topic overview
| | |
|---|---|
| Batch / level | BSS, brahmacari training |
| Suggested duration | 120 minutes |
| Preparation time | About 2 hours (read SB 11.7.31 to 11.9.31 in one sitting) |
| Difficulty | Intermediate |
| Prerequisites | bss-07 (the human form and association) |
| Audience | Brahmacaris and seekers. *Avadhuta* = a saint who has renounced all conventional marks of status and wanders freely |
| Teaching objective | Learn how a humble student takes lessons in tolerance, service, sense control and solitude from the world around him and carries them to one guru, one Lord |

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level
A brahmacari lives by observation and service. The avadhuta shows an attitude of learning: every created thing speaks of
the Lord to a humble heart. The page picks the lessons that matter to a brahmacari and leaves the rest to R.

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| VL1 | One image (earth or tree) as a lesson in tolerance (see vl1-09) |
| VL2 | The twenty-four named together and the idea of learning from everything |
| VL3 | Detachment through the elephant, deer and fish; sense control |
| SPS | The chapters in sequence, with Sridhara Swami's comment on 11.9.31 |
| DPS | The Uddhava Gita context and the guru-sishya line |
| BSS | This page: tolerance, service, controlling the senses (especially the tongue), solitude and the choice of one guru |

Not repeated here: the python and honeybee (bss-09 owns them), the Pingala sermon's theology of hope (kept brief).

## C. Central message
A humble seeker can learn from everything, but the learning must end in taking shelter of the Lord through one
bona fide guru; for a brahmacari, the lessons are tolerance, service, control of the senses and a preference for Krsna's
company over the crowd.

## D. Learning outcomes
Participants can:
1. Tell the setting: King Yadu asks why the young brahmana is so peaceful (SB 11.7.31-32).
2. Name at least six of the twenty-four gurus and the lesson of each.
3. Explain how the elephant, deer, fish and Pingala each teach sense control.
4. Explain why SB 11.9.31 says one may not get complete knowledge from one guru, and how Srila Prabhupada's purport (as quoted from Sridhara Swami) reconciles it with accepting one guru.
5. Choose one "guru" to learn from this week, and offer the lesson to Krsna.

## E. Scriptural foundation
**SB 11.7.32**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/7/32/
> "My dear King, with my intelligence I have taken shelter of many spiritual masters."
- **Context:** The avadhuta brahmana, wandering, meets King Yadu (here addressed as son of Yayati) who asks about his peace.
- **Teaching:** Having gained transcendental understanding from them, he wanders the earth liberated.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 11.7.33**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/7/33-35/
> "O King, I have taken shelter of twenty-four gurus, who are the following: the earth, air, sky, water, fire, moon, sun, pigeon and python…"
- **Teaching:** The list continues: the sea, moth, honeybee, elephant, honey thief, deer, fish, the prostitute Pingala, the kurara bird and the child, and further names (read the full verse aloud).
- **Use:** in the talk; put the list on a board.

**SB 11.7.36**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/7/36/
> "Please listen, O son of Mahārāja Yayāti, O tiger among men, as I explain to you what I have learned from each of these gurus."
- **Use:** background.

**SB 11.7.37**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/7/37/
> "A sober person, even when harassed by other living beings, should understand that his aggressors are acting helplessly under the control of God, and thus he should never be distracted from progress on his own path."
- **Purport point:** The earth is the symbol of tolerance; despite digging, pollution and war, she remains tolerant.
- **Application (preacher's):** For a brahmacari, the first trial is often another person's rudeness. The verse does not say the person is right; it says do not leave your path.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 11.7.38**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/7/38/
> "A saintly person should learn from the mountain to devote all his efforts to the service of others and to make the welfare of others the sole reason for his existence."
- **Teaching:** The tree teaches the same dedication to others.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 11.8.13**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/8/13/
> "A saintly person should never touch a young girl."
- **Teaching:** The verse continues that he should not even let his foot touch a wooden doll shaped like a woman; by such contact he is captured by illusion, as the elephant is captured by the she-elephant.
- **Purport point:** "The lesson to be learned from the elephant is that the desire to relish the touch sensation is certainly the cause of ruining one’s life." "Therefore, somehow or other one should avoid being cheated by allurement to the sensuous form of woman. One should not allow one’s mind to be lost in lusty dreams of sex pleasure." "Somehow or other one should remain aloof from sense gratification in the form of sex pleasure; otherwise, there is no possibility of understanding the spiritual world."
- **Application (preacher's):** BIKC (chapter "Restricting Association with Women") says: "Brahmacārīs should avoid physically touching women, for to do so even accidentally will agitate the mind."
- **Use:** in the talk, briefly, with the next verse.

**SB 11.8.14**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/8/14/
> "A man possessing intelligent discrimination should not under any circumstances try to exploit the beautiful form of a woman for his sense gratification."
- **Teaching:** Just as an elephant trying to enjoy a she-elephant is killed by rival bull elephants, such enjoyment brings ruin.
- **Purport point:** "Just as one is enchanted by the beautiful form of a woman, many other men will also be enchanted, and there is danger that they will be stronger than oneself and may even jealously kill one. Crimes of passion are extremely common."
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 11.8.21**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/8/21/
> "However, if one is able to control the tongue, then one is understood to be in full control of all the senses."
- **Teaching:** The preceding sentence: as long as the tongue is not conquered one cannot be said to have controlled the senses.
- **Purport point:** By eating, one gives energy to all the senses; if the tongue is uncontrolled, all the senses are dragged down. Fasting weakens the other senses, but the tongue becomes greedier.
- **Application (preacher's):** The BIKC quotes Srila Prabhupada that the tongue is controlled by chanting, speaking only of Krsna and tasting only prasadam (summary of a passage there; see O). Prasadam and chanting are the tongue's service, not its enemy.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 11.8.22**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/8/22/
> "O son of kings, previously in the city of Videha there dwelled a prostitute named Piṅgalā."
- **Use:** introduction to Pingala; the verse continues: "Now please hear what I have learned from that lady."

**SB 11.8.23, 11.8.38**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/8/23/ and /38/
> "Once that prostitute, desiring to bring a lover into her house, stood outside in the doorway at night showing her beautiful form." (11.8.23)
> "A person who has developed detachment can give up the bondage of material society, friendship and love, and a person who undergoes great suffering gradually becomes, out of hopelessness, detached and indifferent to the material world. Thus, due to my great suffering, such detachment awoke in my heart; yet how could I have undergone such merciful suffering if I were actually unfortunate? Therefore, I am in fact fortunate and have received the mercy of the Lord. He must somehow or other be pleased with me." (11.8.38)
- **Teaching:** Read 11.8.24-37 (her waiting, her disappointment and her words) before the talk, and tell them as the verses tell them.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 11.8.44**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/8/44/
> "Material desire is undoubtedly the cause of the greatest unhappiness, and freedom from such desire is the cause of the greatest happiness."
- **Teaching:** Cutting off her desire to enjoy so-called lovers, Pingala went peacefully to sleep.
- **Application (preacher's):** Her lesson is the verse's own words: freedom from material desire is the cause of the greatest happiness.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 11.9.10**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/9/10/
> "When many people live together in one place there will undoubtedly be quarreling."
- **Teaching:** Even two living together have frivolous talk and disagreement; so one should live alone, as learned from the young girl's bracelet.
- **Application (preacher's):** For the temple-living brahmacari, solitude means time apart for japa and reading within a community; it is not isolation from service.
- **Use:** background.

**SB 11.9.29**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/9/29/
> "After many, many births and deaths one achieves the rare human form of life…"
- **Teaching:** A sober person should quickly endeavor for the ultimate perfection of life.
- **Purport point:** Human life enables one to understand the Absolute Truth and is full of grave responsibility; even houseflies have a busy sex life.
- **Use:** closing; it echoes bss-07's SB 5.5.1.

**SB 11.9.31**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/9/31/
> "Although the Absolute Truth is one without a second, the sages have described Him in many different ways. Therefore one may not be able to acquire very firm or complete knowledge from one spiritual master."
- **Purport point:** The purport quotes Sridhara Svami: practically all great saintly persons of the past did not take shelter of many spiritual masters but accepted one; the words about many sages show the personal and impersonal descriptions.
- **Application (preacher's):** Read together with the purport: learn from many, take shelter of one. The Guru-Tattva booklet in the IYF Drive is a ready reference for guru selection (see O).
- **Use:** in the talk, with care.

## F. Philosophical explanation
**Principle.** Humility is the student's condition. The avadhuta's twenty-four "gurus" are not initiating gurus but instructors whose conduct reminds of the Lord's arrangement. **Why it matters.** The brahmacari is surrounded by teachers: the weather, the tongue, a crowd, a friend. Without this verse's attitude he sees irritation; with it, he sees instruction. **Connections.** Tolerance (earth) protects the path; service (mountain, tree) is the goal of the path; sense control (elephant, deer, fish, tongue) is the practice; Pingala shows that freedom from desire is happiness (SB 11.8.44), not a loss. **Consequences.** Without a single guru the student collects opinions; with one, he collects realisation. **Misunderstandings.** (1) That the avadhuta rejects a guru; his lessons end in shelter of the Lord. (2) That one can pick teachers at will; SB 11.9.31's purport quotes Sridhara Svami to reconcile. (3) That Pingala's story is told as scandal; the avadhuta names her as his guru: "Now please hear what I have learned from that lady" (SB 11.8.22). **Pitch.** Light and visual: bring one object per guru.

## H. Opening and engagement
**H.1 How to start.** Bring three simple objects (a stone or soil in a pot, a leaf, a small bell). Hold up the soil: "What would this teach a student?" After two answers, read SB 11.7.33. Say: "A saint once named twenty-four teachers. Today we will meet some of them and ask what each gives a brahmacari."
**H.2 Opening questions.** (1) "Who has taught you the most without ever giving a lesson?" (2) "What is the hardest sense to control, in your experience?" (answers are heard without comment) (3) "If you could keep only one teacher, why?"

## I. Core teaching points
**I.1 Tolerance and service (SB 11.7.37-38).** Earth is dug, polluted, fought on, and stays a base for all. A brahmacari's day has small injuries: noise, remarks, hard seva. The point is not that he becomes a doormat but that he refuses to leave the path; service to others (tree, mountain) completes it. End: offer the day's small injuries to Krsna in japa.
**I.2 Sense control (SB 11.8.13-14, 11.8.21).** The elephant is captured by its own attraction; the deer by music; the fish by the hook of the tongue. Make three points: (a) senses are strong; (b) the purport to 11.8.13: "one should remain aloof from sense gratification in the form of sex pleasure; otherwise, there is no possibility of understanding the spiritual world"; (c) the tongue is the gate, controlled by chanting and prasadam. BIKC (chapter "Restricting Association with Women"): "For those interested in spiritual advancement, association with women must be restricted to the minimum." Transition: what if someone has already struggled?
**I.3 Pingala (SB 11.8.22-44).** Retell as the verses tell it: a prostitute of Videha who, "desiring to bring a lover into her house, stood outside in the doorway at night showing her beautiful form" (SB 11.8.23). After much disappointment her suffering turned to detachment, which she calls the mercy of the Lord (SB 11.8.38); "completely cutting off her desire to enjoy so-called lovers, Piṅgalā very happily went to sleep" (SB 11.8.44). Devotional lesson: freedom from desire is happiness, and the Lord's mercy is open to everyone.
**I.4 Solitude and one guru (SB 11.9.10, 11.9.31).** Time apart helps a devotee avoid quarrel and distraction; take shelter of one guru; learn from the world. Link to bss-07's SB 5.5.18.

## J. Stories and illustrations
**J.1 The avadhuta and King Yadu (SB 11.7-9; key verse 11.7.33).** Retell: King Yadu sees a young brahmana wandering fearlessly and happily, and asks the cause. The brahmana answers that his intelligence has led him to twenty-four gurus; he then explains one by one. Devotional lesson: a humble seeker is taught by everything because he sees everything in relation to the Lord, and that is why he is peaceful.
**J.2 Invented illustration (labelled):** a student cleaning a stone floor every morning notices that however many footsteps the floor receives, it stays a place for the deity's visitors. Lesson: SB 11.7.37; offering one's own tolerance to Krsna.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections
1. **Is the avadhuta's list a way to avoid a guru?** No; he teaches the king, and the purport to 11.9.31 quotes Sridhara Svami that great saints took shelter of one.
2. **Why is a prostitute a guru?** The avadhuta says: "Now please hear what I have learned from that lady" (SB 11.8.22); SB 11.8.44 gives her lesson.
3. **What does SB 11.8.13 ask of a saintly person?** The verse: he "should never touch a young girl" and should "not even let his foot touch a wooden doll in the shape of a woman"; the purport: "somehow or other one should avoid being cheated by allurement to the sensuous form of woman."
4. **How do I control the tongue?** Chanting, speaking only of Krsna and tasting only prasadam (BIKC quoting Srila Prabhupada).
5. **Is solitude better than temple life?** SB 11.9.10 is a principle; the temple brahmacari practises it through quiet japa and reading.

## L. Case studies
**L.1 Invented case for discussion.** A new brahmacari's roommate snores and criticises him. *Principles:* SB 11.7.37. *Questions:* what would the earth do? *Analysis:* stay on his path, speak politely, take the roommate's remarks as instruction in tolerance; offer the matter to the Lord in japa. *Takeaway:* tolerance as service.
**L.2 Invented case.** A brahmacari finds that he is eating more than he needs at every festival. *Principles:* SB 11.8.21. *Questions:* what would controlling the tongue mean at the next feast? *Analysis:* offer food with devotion, take prasadam in moderation, and chant. *Takeaway:* the tongue is trained by service.

## M. Discussion and reflection
**M.1 Closing check questions.**
1. *How many gurus did the avadhuta name?* Twenty-four (SB 11.7.33).
2. *What does the earth teach?* Tolerance (SB 11.7.37).
3. *What does the mountain/tree teach?* Service of others (SB 11.7.38).
4. *Which sense is hardest to control?* The tongue (SB 11.8.21).
5. *What did Pingala learn?* That freedom from material desire is the cause of the greatest happiness (SB 11.8.44).
6. *May we learn only from one person?* Learn from many; take shelter of one guru (SB 11.9.31 with purport).
**M.2 Activities.** (a) *Object lessons (10 min):* in pairs, each picks an object and names its lesson in one line; one pair presents. (b) *Pingala retold (10 min):* groups of three retell it as the verses tell it; the preacher checks against SB 11.8.22-44. (c) *Tongue plan (10 min):* each writes one change in how they will take prasadam this week.
**M.3 Reflection.** "Which teacher around me am I not listening to?"

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary
A wise young brahmana was wandering with no worries. King Yadu asked his secret. The brahmana said he had taken shelter of twenty-four gurus: earth, air, sky, water, fire, moon, sun, pigeon, python, sea, moth, honeybee, elephant, honey thief, deer, fish, Pingala, the kurara bird, a child and others (SB 11.7.33).
From the earth he learned tolerance: even when harassed, do not leave your path (SB 11.7.37). From mountain and tree he learned to live for others (SB 11.7.38). From the elephant, the deer and the fish he learned how the senses capture a person, and that if the tongue is controlled all the senses are controlled (SB 11.8.14, 11.8.21). Pingala, a prostitute of Videha who stood in her doorway at night hoping for a lover (SB 11.8.22-23), learned after much disappointment that freedom from material desire brings the greatest happiness (SB 11.8.44), and she slept peacefully.
He ended by saying that human life is rare and should be used quickly for perfection (SB 11.9.29), and that great saints took shelter of one guru while learning from many (SB 11.9.31 and purport). For a brahmacari, every lesson ends in service of Krsna.

## S2. Key ideas
- Learn from everything with humility.
- Tolerance and service protect the path.
- Control the tongue by chanting and prasadam.
- Desire brings unhappiness; freedom from it brings happiness.
- Learn from many; take shelter of one guru.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **SB 11.7.37**: harassed by others, do not leave your path. *For me:* patience is progress.
- **SB 11.8.21**: control the tongue and you control all senses. *For me:* take only prasadam, in moderation.
- **SB 11.8.44**: freedom from material desire is the cause of the greatest happiness. *For me:* there is hope.

## S4. Words to know
- **Avadhuta:** a wandering saint beyond social marks.
- **Guru:** teacher; here a person or thing that teaches.
- **Prasadam:** food offered to Krsna.
- **Tolerance:** staying on your path when disturbed.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *Is the avadhuta against having a guru?* No; he explains that great saints took shelter of one (SB 11.9.31 purport).
2. *How can I control my tongue?* Chant, speak of Krsna, take prasadam.
3. *Why is Pingala a guru?* The avadhuta learned from her change (SB 11.8.22, 11.8.44).
4. *Why is the earth a guru?* Tolerance (SB 11.7.37).

## S6. Check your understanding
1. How many gurus did the avadhuta name? 2. What does the earth teach? 3. Which sense is the key one? 4. What did Pingala gain? 5. What does SB 11.9.31 say about learning?

**Answers:** 1. Twenty-four (SB 11.7.33). 2. Tolerance (SB 11.7.37). 3. The tongue (SB 11.8.21). 4. Peace through freedom from desire (SB 11.8.44). 5. Learn from many sages; take shelter of one guru (SB 11.9.31 and purport).

## S7. Try this week
Offer one small irritation each day to Krsna in your japa. Private reflection: "What did I learn from a person I did not expect to learn from?"
