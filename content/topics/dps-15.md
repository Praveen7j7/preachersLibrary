## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on dps-12, dps-13 and dps-14 (the earlier stages of the same series): this page does not re-teach them, it locates niṣṭhā on the map they draw.

DPS participants already *do* the practices. Their real question is why practice still wobbles after
months or years, and what changes that. The series gives the map (CC Madhya 23.14–15). This session
covers the turning point between *working to practise* and *practising from a steadied heart*.

## C. Central message

*Niṣṭhā* is firm, unwavering devotion. It is established when regular hearing and service have almost
completely destroyed what troubles the heart (SB 1.2.18). It is less a feat of willpower than the
stability of a cleaner heart. Its sign is that practice no longer depends on mood or circumstance.

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Place niṣṭhā in Rupa Gosvami's sequence (CC Madhya 23.14–15) and say what comes before and after it.
2. Explain the Bhagavatam's sequence: hearing → cleansing → fixed devotion → passion and ignorance
   subside → satisfaction (SB 1.2.17–19).
3. Distinguish niṣṭhā from external regularity, from stubbornness, and from emotional highs.
4. Name, privately, what most often makes their own practice waver, and connect it to anartha-nivrtti.
5. Apply the supports Rupa Gosvami gives (enthusiasm, confidence, patience: NOI 3) and the assurance
   given to those who slip (BG 9.30–31; SB 1.5.17).

## E. Scriptural foundation

**CC Madhya 23.14–15**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/23/14-15/
> "In the beginning there must be faith. Then one becomes interested in associating with pure devotees. Thereafter one is initiated by the spiritual master and executes the regulative principles under his orders. Thus one is freed from all unwanted habits and becomes firmly fixed in devotional service. Thereafter, one develops taste and attachment…"
- **Context:** Caitanya Mahaprabhu instructing Sanatana Gosvami, quoting Rupa Gosvami's
  *Bhakti-rasamrta-sindhu* (1.4.15–16). The verse has no purport of its own.
- **Teaching:** Niṣṭhā ("firmly fixed in devotional service") follows freedom from unwanted habits and
  precedes taste.
- **Use:** in the talk, as the map. Have it on a slide.

**SB 1.2.17–18**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/18/
> 1.2.18: "By regular attendance in classes on the Bhāgavatam and by rendering of service to the pure devotee, all that is troublesome to the heart is almost completely destroyed, and loving service unto the Personality of Godhead, who is praised with transcendental songs, is established as an irrevocable fact."
- **Context:** Suta Gosvami to the sages at Naimisaranya, describing how devotion develops.
- **Teaching:** The cause of fixed devotion (*naiṣṭhikī bhakti*) is daily hearing of the Bhagavatam and
  service to the devotee-Bhagavata. Krsna Himself, as Supersoul, cleanses the heart of one who has the urge
  to hear (1.2.17).
- **Purport point (1.2.18):** There are two Bhagavatas, the book and the devotee. "By such sincere
  association of the Bhāgavatas… he becomes fixed in the devotional service of the Lord. The more progress
  is made in devotional service under the guidance of the Bhāgavatas, the more one becomes fixed."
- **Lecture (Calcutta, 26 Sep 1974):** Srila Prabhupada glosses *naiṣṭhikī*: "Niṣṭhā means 'firmly fixed
  up'", and links it to CC Madhya 23.14–15: "This niṣṭhā stage comes when one is freed from anartha."
  https://vedabase.io/en/library/transcripts/740926sbcal/ (Verified)
- **Application (preacher's):** Steadiness is grown through the right input, not forced.
- **Use:** in the talk. This is the central reference.

**SB 1.2.19–21**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/1/2/19/
> 1.2.19: "As soon as irrevocable loving service is established in the heart, the effects of nature's modes of passion and ignorance, such as lust, desire and hankering, disappear from the heart. Then the devotee is established in goodness, and he becomes completely happy."
- **Teaching:** The fruits of niṣṭhā: lust and hankering subside, goodness and happiness follow (1.2.19),
  then knowledge of the Lord (1.2.20), and the knot of the heart is cut (1.2.21).
- **Purport point (1.2.19):** Devotional service "is not inactivity, but the unalloyed activity of the
  soul"; the effects of passion and ignorance are eliminated by it.
- **Application (preacher's):** If lust and hankering still pull hard, that is not a reason for despair.
  It shows the stage one is at.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**NOI 3**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/3/
- **Teaching:** Six principles favourable to pure devotional service: enthusiasm, endeavour with
  confidence, patience, following the regulative principles, giving up the association of nondevotees,
  following the previous acaryas.
- **Purport point:** "One should not be impatient in discharging devotional service but should take
  instructions from the spiritual master and execute them with patience, depending on the mercy of guru and
  Kṛṣṇa." Prabhupada cites the movement's own beginning: there was no response at first, but devotional
  activities were continued with patience.
- **Use:** in the talk (practical supports).

**NOI 7**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/7/
- **Teaching:** The tongue afflicted by the "jaundice" of ignorance cannot taste the sweetness of the holy
  name, but by careful, daily chanting a natural taste awakens.
- **Purport point:** Recovery comes "by the regular cultivation of Kṛṣṇa consciousness". The bitterness is
  the disease, not the medicine.
- **Use:** as the bridge to the next session (ruci).

**BG 2.41** with purport: Verified. Resolute intelligence has one aim; the irresolute mind is
many-branched. The purport defines that resolution as strong faith, citing CC Madhya 22.62: faith "means
unflinching trust in something sublime". *Use:* link back to DPS-12 (sraddha).

**BG 9.14**: Verified. Great souls worship "endeavoring with great determination" (*dṛḍha-vratāḥ*).
*Use:* determination is part of the practice that leads to niṣṭhā.

**BG 6.26, 6.35**: Verified. Bring the wandering mind back each time it wanders; the mind is curbed "by
suitable practice and by detachment". *Use:* practical method in the talk.

**BG 9.30–31; SB 1.5.17**: Verified. A devotee who commits even an abominable act but is properly
situated in determination is to be considered saintly and quickly becomes righteous (BG 9.30–31). One who
falls while immature in devotion faces no danger of being unsuccessful (SB 1.5.17). The SB 1.5.17 purport
also warns that one who surrenders "by some temporary sentiment" may fall through undesirable association.
*Use:* questions (K); prevents despair and complacency alike.

**SB 11.2.42**: Verified · *BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples.* Devotion,
experience of the Lord and detachment occur simultaneously and increase together. *Use:* honest
self-check: steadiness without growing detachment is not yet niṣṭhā.

**CC Antya 3.121, 3.124, 3.130; 3.241**: Verified. Haridasa Thakura's vow of chanting (story in J).

**CC Antya 20.21**: Verified. Humility and tolerance as the qualification for always chanting. *Use:* the
mood that protects niṣṭhā from pride.

***Mādhurya-kādambinī* (Śrīla Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura)**: acarya's book, approved by the head (28 Oct 2026).
Edition cited: *Mādhurya-kādambinī*, with the *Bhāvānuvāda* and *Pīyūṣa-varṣiṇī-vṛtti* commentary of Bhaktivedānta
Nārāyaṇa Gosvāmī Mahārāja, English, first edition, Gaudiya Vedanta Publications, August 2018 (ISBN 978-1-63316-169-6);
text in `research/books/`. Verified against the edition text (accuracy review, 28 Oct 2026). Supporting source for the *structure* of the stage; the principle itself rests on SB 1.2.17–19.
- **Unsteady practice before niṣṭhā (Second Shower of Nectar, texts 5–11).** Bhajana-kriyā "is of two types: aniṣṭhitā
  (unsteady) and niṣṭhitā (steady)". Unsteady practice has six kinds, "in the following sequence: (1) utsāha-mayī,
  (2) ghana-taralā, (3) vyūḍha-vikalpā, (4) viṣaya-saṅgarā, (5) niyamākṣamā and finally (6) taraṅga-raṅgiṇī" (text 5).
  In the book's own glosses: *utsāha-mayī*, "filled with short-lived enthusiasm" (the new student who feels himself
  already a scholar); *ghana-taralā*, "'thick and thin', or irresolute, endeavour" (sometimes intense, sometimes slack);
  *vyūḍha-vikalpā*, "extensive indecision" (should I stay at home or renounce? chant only, or hear, or worship?);
  *viṣaya-saṅgarā*, "battling attachment to the objects of the senses" (he resolves to give them up, "yet, even after
  resolving to renounce enjoying sense objects, from time to time he is cheated by them"); *niyamākṣamā*, "the inability
  to uphold vows" (he vows daily "I will chant the holy name this number of times…" and "is unable to keep them for
  long"); *taraṅga-raṅgiṇī*, "delighting in the mundane benefits afforded by bhakti" (frolicking in the "waves" of
  wealth, worship and fame that come when bhakti appears in a person).
- **Where niṣṭhā begins (Fourth Shower, text 1).** The author places niṣṭhā *after* anartha-nivṛtti by quoting
  SB 1.2.17–18: "It is then that unflinching devotion (naiṣṭhikī-bhakti) to Bhagavān Śrī Kṛṣṇa… awakens within the
  heart." This agrees with Prabhupada's 1974 gloss above.
- **The sign of niṣṭhā (Fourth Shower, text 2).** "Niṣṭhā means 'without wavering'." Even one who wants to practise daily
  cannot be steady while five obstacles remain: *laya* (drowsiness, "increasingly oppressive… while chanting, more while
  hearing, and even more while remembering"), *vikṣepa* (distraction by mundane topics during practice), *apratipatti*
  (being "simply unable to properly hear, chant or remember" even without the first two), *kaṣāya* (impressions of
  "anger, greed and false ego" surfacing during practice) and *rasāsvāda* (inability to absorb the mind in kīrtana "when
  the opportunity for material sense pleasure arises"). "The absence of these five obstacles is symptomatic of niṣṭhā."
- **Kinds of niṣṭhā (Fourth Shower, text 3a).** "Niṣṭhā is also of two types: (1) resting in bhakti herself,
  sākṣād-bhakti-vartinī… and (2) resting in that which is favourable to bhakti, tad-anukūla-vastu-vartinī." The first has
  three main divisions, kāyikī (body: hearing kathā, serving Tulasī, Deity worship, cleaning the temple), vācikī (speech:
  kīrtana, prayer, reciting narrations) and mānasī (mind: remembering the Lord's names, forms, qualities and pastimes).
  Some say niṣṭhā comes first in the body, then speech, then mind; others that "there is no fixed sequence", since it
  follows each sādhaka's saṁskāras. The second kind is steadiness in qualities favourable to bhakti: "not desiring respect
  from others, yet always offering others respect, friendship and compassion". The book adds at once (texts 3b–3c) that
  such qualities do not by themselves show niṣṭhā: a self-controlled devotee may have them without niṣṭhā in bhakti, and
  one with niṣṭhā in bhakti may lack them. "Intense enthusiasm to perform the limbs of bhakti, such as hearing and
  chanting, is the actual benchmark of steadiness (niṣṭhā) in bhakti" (text 3c).
- **Application (preacher's):** the six kinds are a mirror for honest self-location, never labels for others. Teach them
  as the book gives them, with the edition named; don't improvise a seventh.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** In Rupa Gosvami's sequence, niṣṭhā is the fifth step (CC Madhya 23.14–15):
   - sraddha
   - sadhu-sanga
   - bhajana-kriya
   - anartha-nivrtti
   - **nistha**
   - ruci, asakti, bhava, prema

   The Bhagavatam describes the same development (SB 1.2.17–18). Regular hearing and service to the
   Bhagavata, with Krsna Himself cleansing the heart, lead to devotion that is *naiṣṭhikī*: fixed,
   irrevocable.
2. **Why it matters.** Before niṣṭhā, practice depends on supports: mood, company, schedule, enthusiasm.
   When these fail, practice falls. Niṣṭhā is the point at which devotion holds when supports fall away.
   Without understanding this, devotees either blame themselves ("I have no willpower") or lower the goal
   ("this is as good as it gets").
3. **Connections.**
   - Niṣṭhā is the *fruit* of anartha-nivrtti (DPS-14): "This niṣṭhā stage comes when one is freed from
     anartha" (lecture, 1974).
   - It is the *ground* for taste (DPS-16; NOI 7).
   - It is nourished by the six principles of NOI 3.
   - It is protected by humility (CC Antya 20.21). *Mādhurya-kādambinī* (Fourth Shower, 3a) makes humility itself an
     object of niṣṭhā: steadiness in "not desiring respect from others, yet always offering others respect". It also warns
     (3b–3c) that such qualities alone do not prove niṣṭhā; the benchmark is intense enthusiasm for hearing and chanting.
   - Its sign, says the same book (Fourth Shower, 2), is the near-absence of the five obstacles (drowsiness, distraction,
     incapacity, surfacing anger/greed/pride, and pull towards sense pleasure) *during* hearing, chanting and remembering.
4. **Practical consequences.** If steadiness comes from purification, the remedy for unsteadiness is more
   and better *hearing* and *service to devotees* (SB 1.2.18). Gritting one's teeth harder is not the
   remedy. Discipline still matters (BG 9.14, BG 6.26, 6.35), as the means that keeps one hearing and
   serving.
5. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *Niṣṭhā = never missing a round.* External regularity is a symptom and a support, but niṣṭhā describes
     the heart (SB 1.2.19: lust and hankering subside).
   - *Niṣṭhā = rigidity.* Fixedness in devotion is not stubbornness in opinions or harshness to others.
     Humility is its companion (CC Antya 20.21).
   - *Niṣṭhā = feeling inspired.* Inspiration comes and goes; niṣṭhā holds when it goes.
   - *"I slipped, so I never had niṣṭhā and never will."* The scriptures address the struggling devotee
     with assurance (BG 9.30–31; SB 1.5.17), and the stages are a direction of growth.
   - *Claiming the stage.* Locating oneself on the map is for self-examination, not self-advertisement.
6. **Pitch for DPS.** Use the Sanskrit names of the stages; participants know them from DPS-12…14. Keep
   the session reflective rather than technical. The aim is understanding one's own practice, not passing
   a test.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet, say your name, and say the session is about steadiness, not about grading anyone.
2. Define the term: niṣṭhā (steadiness, being firmly fixed) is the stage of devotion after unwanted habits are cleared.
3. Ask H.2 question 1 for private writing (no sharing).
4. Ask H.2 question 2; take two or three general answers.
5. Say that the Bhagavatam names a cause for steadiness, and that it is not willpower; begin the recap.

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the room's own life)
1. Think of your steadiest week of practice and your least steady. What was different? (Private.)
2. What support, such as a person, a place or a routine, quietly held your practice up?
3. What happens to your practice when that support goes?

- **Opening question (written privately):** "Think of the steadiest week of your spiritual life and the
  least steady. What was different?"
- **Scenario:** a devotee who chanted enthusiastically for a year finds that a new job, exams or a
  disappointment in the community quietly erodes the morning programme. Nothing dramatic happened.
  Practice simply depended on supports that disappeared.
- **Transition:** "Rupa Gosvami describes a stage where devotion stops depending on such supports. The
  Bhagavatam tells us how it comes about, and it is not what most of us try first."
- **Why it matters:** every devotee here will meet the week when supports fail. Knowing what niṣṭhā is
  changes what we do in that week.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Niṣṭhā is a stage on a map.** It follows anartha-nivrtti and precedes taste (CC Madhya 23.14–15).
   *Example:* the map on the board. *Transition:* what produces it?
2. **It grows from hearing and service.** SB 1.2.18: regular hearing of the Bhagavatam and service to the
   pure devotee almost destroy what troubles the heart, and devotion becomes irrevocable. Prabhupada:
   "Niṣṭhā means firmly fixed up… This niṣṭhā stage comes when one is freed from anartha." *Example:* a
   plant becomes steady when its roots are established, not when it is tied more tightly to a stick.
   *(Illustration, not a scriptural image.)* *Transition:* how do we know it is present?
3. **It shows in the heart, not only the schedule.** Lust, desire and hankering subside; the devotee
   becomes satisfied (SB 1.2.19). Devotion, experience of the Lord and detachment grow together
   (SB 11.2.42). *Transition:* so what about all the effort we make?
4. **Effort keeps us within reach of the cause.** Determination (BG 9.14), bringing the mind back
   (BG 6.26), practice and detachment (BG 6.35), and enthusiasm, confidence and patience (NOI 3) keep us
   hearing and serving. *Transition:* what does this look like in a great devotee?
5. **Steadiness protected by humility.** Haridasa Thakura's steadiness was never arrogance. Mahaprabhu's
   standard: lower than the grass, more tolerant than a tree (CC Antya 20.21). *Transition:* the next
   stage, taste (NOI 7).

## J. Stories and illustrations

**Haridasa Thakura and the prostitute** (CC Antya 3.104–144). Keep to the text:
- Ramacandra Khan sends a young prostitute to discredit Haridasa (3.104–109).
- He asks her to sit and hear the chanting "until my regular chanting is finished" (3.121).
- He tells her: "I have vowed to chant ten million names in a month" (3.124).
- Night after night the chanting continues. "By his association the mind of the prostitute had changed"
  (3.130), and she becomes a celebrated devotee (3.142).
- *Connection:* his steadiness did not depend on circumstances. More than that, it *transformed* the
  circumstance. Note the connection to SB 1.2.18: she was purified by hearing and by the association of a
  pure devotee.
- *Caution:* the same story is BSS-12 ("Haridas Thakur and the prostitute"). At DPS use it for niṣṭhā; the
  BSS session draws its brahmacari lessons.

**Haridasa and Mayadevi** (CC Antya 3.240–249). Optional second illustration: "As long as the vow to
chant is unfulfilled, I do not desire anything else" (3.241).

**Srila Prabhupada's patience** (NOI 3 purport). The movement had no response at first, but devotional
activities were continued with patience.

**Haridasa beaten in twenty-two marketplaces** (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Ādi-līlā, chapter 16; English text in
`research/books/Caitanya Bhagavata.txt`, translator not named; **narrative only**, checked 28 Oct 2026). Before the
Nawab, Haridasa says that "even if my body is cut into pieces and I loose [sic] my life, I will never give up chanting the
Lord's holy name"; the Qazi orders him lashed "in twenty two market places untill he dies"; dragged from market to market,
"he faithfully chanted Lord Krishna's holy name"; his one prayer is for the men beating him. *Connection:* the same
steadiness as in CC Antya 3, under a different test; practice that did not depend on circumstance. *Use:* one minute,
as the earlier chapter of the same life, if the prostitute story is already known to the group. Cite the book, not
"tradition says".

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"I've chanted 16 rounds for three years and still feel unsteady. Am I doing something wrong?"**
   - Not necessarily. The stages describe purification, which takes the time it takes; NOI 3 asks for
     patience. Ask honestly: am I *hearing* (SB 1.2.18)? Am I *serving devotees*? Is my chanting attentive
     (NOI 7)?
   - Suggest one change with a mentor's help (VL3-08, DPS-02).
2. **"Isn't niṣṭhā just discipline?"**
   - Discipline is the means; niṣṭhā is the result in the heart (SB 1.2.18–19). Someone can be disciplined
     and still full of hankering. That is regularity, not yet niṣṭhā.
3. **"If I fall down, do I go back to the beginning?"**
   - The Gita's assurance is strong: one determined in devotion is to be considered saintly and quickly
     becomes righteous (BG 9.30–31); falling while immature carries no danger of being unsuccessful
     (SB 1.5.17).
   - Neither verse is a licence: the SB 1.5.17 purport warns about falling through "undesirable
     association". Return quickly, with guidance.
4. **"How do I know which stage I'm at?"**
   - Use the map for direction, not for rank. SB 11.2.42 gives a self-check (are devotion, experience and
     detachment growing together?). Leave assessment to those who guide you.
   - Claiming a stage is a warning sign (CC Antya 20.21).
5. **"Is it grace or my effort?"**
   - Both. Krsna Himself cleanses the heart of one with the urge to hear (SB 1.2.17), and our part is
     regular hearing and service (SB 1.2.18) with determination (BG 9.14). *Proposed:* a full advanced
     session on grace and endeavour (Stage 2 §7.1 #7).
6. **"What about the six kinds of unsteadiness I've heard in classes?"**
   - They are real and come from *Mādhurya-kādambinī*, Second Shower of Nectar, texts 5–11 (Gaudiya Vedanta
     Publications edition, 2018; see E). Viśvanātha Cakravartī divides bhajana-kriyā into *aniṣṭhitā* (unsteady) and
     *niṣṭhitā* (steady), and names six kinds of unsteady practice in sequence: short-lived enthusiasm (*utsāha-mayī*),
     thick-and-thin effort (*ghana-taralā*), extensive indecision (*vyūḍha-vikalpā*), battling sense attachment
     (*viṣaya-saṅgarā*), inability to keep vows (*niyamākṣamā*) and delighting in the side-benefits of bhakti such as
     respect and fame (*taraṅga-raṅgiṇī*).
   - The book says these "allow that devotee to identify his own specific stage": they are for self-location, not for
     rating others. Steadiness itself (Fourth Shower, text 2) is recognised by the near-absence of five obstacles during
     practice: drowsiness, distraction, incapacity, surfacing anger/greed/pride, and the pull of sense pleasure.
   - If asked which kind one is in, suggest reading the Second Shower with a senior devotee rather than deciding in the
     room.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The new job.** Arjun, a DPS devotee, kept a full morning programme for two years. Since starting
work six months ago he chants at night, misses class and feels dry. He still finishes 16 rounds.
- **Dilemma:** Is he steady or not? What should change?
- **Principles:** SB 1.2.18 (hearing + service as cause); NOI 7 (taste through attentive chanting); NOI 3
  (patience, regulative principles).
- **Questions:**
  - What has Arjun kept, and what has he lost?
  - Which input from SB 1.2.18 has dropped out?
  - What is one realistic change?
- **Analysis:** Rounds remain (good); hearing and association have gone. That is the cause of dryness.
  For example: twenty minutes of Bhagavatam on the commute, and one weekly service with devotees.
- **Takeaway:** Protect the cause (hearing, service), not only the symptom (rounds).

**Case 2: The steady critic.** Meera never misses mangala-arati and is quick to point out others'
lateness. Newer devotees avoid her.
- **Principles:** CC Antya 20.21 (humility as the condition of chanting); SB 1.2.19 (niṣṭhā shows as
  freedom from hankering and as satisfaction); SB 11.2.42 (growth together).
- **Questions:**
  - Is regularity with harshness niṣṭhā?
  - What might her steadiness still depend on (recognition, control)?
- **Takeaway:** Regularity is not the whole of it: the scriptures pair fixed devotion with hankering subsiding
  (SB 1.2.19) and with humility (CC Antya 20.21). *Application (preacher's):* use the case for self-examination, not
  to rate Meera; *Mādhurya-kādambinī* (Fourth Shower, 3b) warns that the presence or absence of such qualities does not
  by itself show whether someone has niṣṭhā.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Most of us have had a steady month and a shaky one. Rupa Gosvami's map of devotional life (CC Madhya 23.14-15) has a name for the point where practice stops wobbling: *niṣṭhā* (firmness), being "firmly fixed in devotional service". It comes after we are freed from unwanted habits (*anartha-nivrtti*) and before taste (*ruci*) awakens. The Bhagavatam says how it comes about: by regularly hearing the Bhagavatam and serving the devotees, Krsna Himself cleanses the heart, "all that is troublesome to the heart is almost completely destroyed", and devotion becomes irrevocable (SB 1.2.17-18). Steadiness is less a feat of willpower than the stability of a cleaner heart. Its signs are inside: lust and hankering subside, and the devotee becomes satisfied (SB 1.2.19); devotion and detachment grow together (SB 11.2.42). Disciplined practice alone is not yet niṣṭhā, since a person can keep a schedule and still be full of hankering.

Effort still matters, because it keeps us within reach of the cause. NOI 3 names six supports: enthusiasm, endeavour with confidence, patience, following the regulative principles, giving up the association of nondevotees, and following the previous teachers. When the mind wanders, we withdraw it and bring it back under the self's control (BG 6.26). Haridasa Thakura's steadiness under pressure (CC Antya 3) was never arrogance; humility protects it (CC Antya 20.21). A sincere devotee who slips is not lost (BG 9.30-31; SB 1.5.17), though this is never a licence for carelessness.

Viśvanātha Cakravartī's *Mādhurya-kādambinī* lists six kinds of unsteady practice, to help us locate ourselves honestly and never to grade others: short-lived enthusiasm, thick-and-thin effort, extensive indecision, battling sense attachment, inability to keep vows, and delighting in the fame that bhakti can bring. Ask which input (hearing, service, attentive chanting) has been weakest in your least steady weeks, and strengthen that one.

## S2. Key ideas

- Niṣṭhā is a stage on a map: after anartha-nivrtti, before ruci (CC Madhya 23.14–15).
- It grows from two inputs: regular hearing of the Bhagavatam and service to devotees (SB 1.2.18).
- It shows in the heart, not only in the schedule: lust and hankering subside; satisfaction comes (SB 1.2.19);
  devotion, experience of the Lord and detachment grow together (SB 11.2.42).
- Effort still matters: determination (BG 9.14), bringing the mind back (BG 6.26, 6.35), enthusiasm, confidence and
  patience (NOI 3) keep us hearing and serving.
- Unsteady practice has recognisable kinds (*Mādhurya-kādambinī*, Second Shower): the list is a mirror for ourselves,
  not a label for others.
- A slip is not the end: the Gita and Bhagavatam reassure the sincere devotee (BG 9.30–31; SB 1.5.17), without
  licensing carelessness.
- Steadiness is protected by humility (CC Antya 20.21).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **CC Madhya 23.14–15:** "Thus one is freed from all unwanted habits and becomes firmly fixed in devotional service.
  Thereafter, one develops taste and attachment." *For me:* I can say where I am on the map without despair or pride.
- **SB 1.2.18:** "By regular attendance in classes on the Bhāgavatam and by rendering of service to the pure devotee,
  all that is troublesome to the heart is almost completely destroyed, and loving service… is established as an
  irrevocable fact." *For me:* when practice wobbles, check my hearing and my service first.
- **SB 1.2.19:** "As soon as irrevocable loving service is established in the heart, the effects of nature's modes of
  passion and ignorance, such as lust, desire and hankering, disappear from the heart." *For me:* the sign is inside.
- **NOI 3:** six principles favourable to devotional service: enthusiasm, confidence, patience, following the regulative
  principles, giving up the association of nondevotees, following the previous acaryas. *For me:* patience is a
  principle, not a failure.

## S4. Words to know

- **Niṣṭhā:** firm, unwavering devotion; "without wavering" (*Mādhurya-kādambinī*, Fourth Shower, 2).
- **Naiṣṭhikī bhakti:** devotion that has become fixed and irrevocable (SB 1.2.18).
- **Anartha-nivrtti:** the clearing of unwanted things from the heart; the stage before niṣṭhā.
- **Aniṣṭhitā / niṣṭhitā bhajana-kriyā:** unsteady / steady practice (*Mādhurya-kādambinī*, Second and Fourth Showers).
- **Ruci:** taste; the stage after niṣṭhā (NOI 7 describes how it awakens).

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **I have chanted for years and still feel unsteady. Is something wrong with me?**
   Not necessarily. Purification takes the time it takes, and NOI 3 asks for patience. Ask honestly: am I *hearing*
   the Bhagavatam regularly, and am I *serving devotees* (SB 1.2.18)? Those are the inputs the text names.
2. **Isn't steadiness just discipline?**
   Discipline is the means; niṣṭhā is the result in the heart (SB 1.2.18–19). A person can be disciplined and still full
   of hankering. That is regularity, not yet niṣṭhā.
3. **If I fall down, do I start again from zero?**
   The texts reassure the sincere: one who is "properly situated in his determination" is to be considered saintly and
   "quickly becomes righteous" (BG 9.30–31); one who falls while immature faces no danger of being unsuccessful
   (SB 1.5.17). Return quickly and take guidance; the assurance is not a licence.
4. **What are the "kinds of unsteadiness" senior devotees mention?**
   *Mādhurya-kādambinī* (Second Shower, texts 5–11) lists six, in order: short-lived enthusiasm, thick-and-thin effort,
   extensive indecision, battling sense attachment, inability to keep vows, and delighting in the respect and fame that
   bhakti brings. They are for locating oneself, never for grading others.
5. **How would I know niṣṭhā has come?**
   Not by claiming it. The Bhagavatam's sign is that lust and hankering subside and satisfaction grows (SB 1.2.19), and
   that devotion and detachment grow together (SB 11.2.42). *Mādhurya-kādambinī* adds: the five obstacles (drowsiness,
   distraction, incapacity, surfacing anger/greed/pride, pull of sense pleasure) have almost left one's hearing and
   chanting. Leave assessment to those who guide you.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. In CC Madhya 23.14–15, what comes immediately before niṣṭhā and what immediately after?
2. According to SB 1.2.18, what two things produce fixed devotion?
3. True or false: SB 1.2.19 says that at niṣṭhā one chants more rounds.
4. Name three of the six principles in NOI 3.
5. From *Mādhurya-kādambinī*: name two of the six kinds of unsteady practice, in your own words.
6. What does BG 6.26 tell us to do each time the mind wanders?

**Answers:** 1. Before: freedom from unwanted habits (anartha-nivrtti); after: taste (ruci) and attachment
(CC Madhya 23.14–15). 2. Regular hearing of the Bhagavatam and service to the pure devotee (SB 1.2.18). 3. False: it
says the effects of passion and ignorance, "such as lust, desire and hankering, disappear from the heart" and the
devotee becomes completely happy (SB 1.2.19). 4. Any three of: enthusiasm, endeavour with confidence, patience,
following the regulative principles, giving up the association of nondevotees, following the previous acaryas (NOI 3).
5. Any two of: short-lived enthusiasm (utsāha-mayī), thick-and-thin effort (ghana-taralā), extensive indecision
(vyūḍha-vikalpā), battling sense attachment (viṣaya-saṅgarā), inability to keep vows (niyamākṣamā), delighting in
bhakti's side-benefits such as fame (taraṅga-raṅgiṇī) (*Mādhurya-kādambinī*, Second Shower). 6. Withdraw it and
bring it back under the control of the self (BG 6.26).
