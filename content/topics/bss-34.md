# 38. Dealings with Family and Friends

> BSS (brahmacari training) · 120 min · drafted · not sensitive (a note on grief appears in J.2). Follows BIKC chapter 38. Builds on bss-07 (SB 5.5.18, a guide or parent must help others escape birth and death), bss-13 (Dhrtarastra leaves home) and bss-06 (Prahlada's firmness with his father).

## A. Topic overview
| | |
|---|---|
| Batch / level | BSS, brahmacari training (Bhakti Siddhanta Sabha). Brahmacari = a celibate student who lives simply, serves the guru and studies scripture |
| Suggested duration | 120 minutes (lecture, with an "if time allows" extension) |
| Preparation time | About 2 hours: read BIKC ch. 38 (about one and a half pages), CC Madhya 3.165-190 with the purport to 3.181, SB 11.2.46 with its purport, then this page |
| Difficulty | Intermediate |

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level
A young man who is preparing to join the ashram, or already has, often meets worry or opposition at home. IYF's own
deck "Art of Smart Dealings: Friends, Relatives and Parents" states the aim as "not getting affected by materialistic philosophy and
materialistic association, at the same time not getting alienated". The BSS motive is "preparing the plan to join K.C as full time
brahmachari" (research/batch-criteria.md, section 6). A brahmacari who is kind and steady keeps his family's goodwill.

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| VL1 | Not taught as a topic; the IYF deck for new students is used in some programmes |
| VL2 | Why family worries; how to answer questions simply |

Not done here: dealing with nondevotees in general (bss-35); marriage; the details of a family's grief or legal issues. If a family situation involves danger or abuse, speak to a trusted senior and a qualified professional.

## C. Central message
A brahmacari loves his family and friends by being kind, patient and honest, by serving them when they need help, by sharing prasadam, and by never giving up his practice. He gives family and friends what is best for them, a connection with Krishna, slowly and in a way they can accept.

## D. Learning outcomes
By the end, participants can:
1. Name the four kinds of persons in SB 11.2.46 and say how a devotee treats each.
2. Retell how Lord Caitanya and His mother Sacimata together arranged for His life as a sannyasi while keeping contact (CC Madhya 3).
3. Give five practical ways to keep goodwill with parents (the IYF deck and BIKC).
4. Explain why the scripture says relations of the body are not permanent (SB 6.16.5) without becoming cold.
5. Write one kind action for a family member or friend this week.

## E. Scriptural foundation
Use SB 11.2.46, CC Madhya 3.176, 3.177, 3.181, 3.182 and 3.185, and SB 7.9.43 in the talk. The rest are for questions. All verses and purports were read on Vedabase. SB 11.2.46 is in Canto 11, so its translation is the BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples.

**SB 11.2.46**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/2/46/
> "…is a sincere friend to all the devotees of the Lord, shows mercy to ignorant people who are innocent and disregards those who are envious of the Supreme Personality of Godhead."
- **Context:** Sage Havir describes the three kinds of devotee to King Nimi. This verse describes the intermediate devotee, the madhyama-adhikari, who relates to four kinds: the Lord, devotees, the innocent and the envious.
- **Purport point:** The purport says the second-class devotee is eager to preach to the innocent but should avoid the atheistic class so that he is not disturbed or polluted by them. It says a Vaishnava preacher who meets one envious of him should stay at a distance, but he may meditate on ways to save the envious class; Srila Jiva Gosvami gives Prahlada as an example.
- **Application (preacher's):** BIKC uses these four kinds for family and friends. Most relatives are in the third group, the innocent, who do not know what a devotee's life is. The verse tells us to show them mercy. Mercy means patience, prasadam and a good example.
- **Use:** in the talk (I.1).

**CC Madhya 3.176 and 3.177**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/3/176/ (and /177/)
> "…as long as I remain manifest, I shall never give you up. Nor shall I be able to give up My mother." (3.176)
> "…it is not the duty of a sannyāsī to remain at his birthplace, encircled by relatives." (3.177)
- **Context:** Lord Caitanya, as a sannyasi, has come to Advaita Acarya's house in Santipura. His mother Sacimata has come from Navadvipa, cooks for Him, and begs the devotees that she alone may serve Him while He is there (3.160, 3.169-171). He gathers the devotees and explains His situation (3.173-178).
- **Teaching:** He declares He cannot give up the devotees or His mother, and also that a sannyasi should not live at his birthplace among relatives.
- **Use:** in the talk (I.2, J.1).

**CC Madhya 3.178, 3.181, 3.182**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/3/181/ (and /178/, /182/)
> "…if Nimāi remains at Jagannātha Purī, He may not leave any one of us and at the same time can remain aloof as a sannyāsī." (3.182)
- **Teaching:** He asks the devotees to make an arrangement so that He does not leave them and is not blamed for staying with relatives (3.178). Sacimata says she would be happy if He stayed, but would be unhappy if people blamed Him (3.181), and proposes Jagannatha Puri. It is close to Navadvipa, so news can travel (3.182-184).
- **Purport point (3.181):** The purport says a mother is naturally happy if her son stays home, but if he does not search for Krishna, saintly persons blame him, which hurts her. A real mother who wants her son to progress spiritually allows him to search for Krishna. By letting Him go, Sacimata instructs all mothers. If a mother does not allow it, the purport says she is "ma", which indicates maya. It quotes SB 5.5.18 (Builds on bss-07): no one should become a guru, relative, father, mother, Deity or husband if he cannot help a person escape death. It says the highest perfection for a family member is to help others return to Godhead, and notes that Sacimata arranged to receive news of her son.
- **Application (preacher's):** Note the pattern. Neither the son nor the mother treats the separation as a quarrel. They talk, find a solution together, and keep up news. A brahmacari can do the same with his parents. Be careful not to use the word "maya" about a living mother in front of anyone; read the purport for yourself.
- **Use:** in the talk (I.2).

**CC Madhya 3.185**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/3/185/
> "I do not care for my personal happiness or unhappiness, but only for His happiness."
- **Teaching:** Sacimata's statement about her son. The text gives her words; the loving attitude is hers. It is the model for a parent who understands.
- **Use:** in the talk, with J.1.

**SB 6.16.5 and 6.16.8**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/6/16/5/ (and /8/)
> "…no one is permanently related." (6.16.5)
- **Context:** After the death of his young son, King Citraketu hears from the sage Narada that the child's soul, brought back into the body for a short time, answers that he has been in many bodies and many relationships (6.16.4).
- **Purport point (6.16.5):** The purport says a friend today may be an enemy tomorrow; our relationships as friends or enemies, family men or outsiders, come from our different dealings. **Purport point (6.16.8):** The purport says the soul's relationship with his body and with his so-called father and mother is an illusory conception, which continues as long as one is not enlightened about the position of the living entity.
- **Application (preacher's):** These verses are about the soul's real position, not about being unkind to family. The same purports say that the right response is to be free from grief and false possessiveness. Then we can serve our families more clearly, because we want their lasting good.
- **Use:** background, with care (J.2). Do not use these verses to comfort a person in fresh grief.

**SB 6.17.28**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/6/17/28/
> "Devotees solely engaged in the devotional service of the Supreme Personality of Godhead, Nārāyaṇa, never fear any condition of life."
- **Purport point:** The purport says devotees do not mind reverses in life because in the Lord's service they have learned to tolerate hardships, and that all devotees who have taken shelter as eternal servants are glorious.
- **Use:** in the talk, closing (J.2): the fearlessness that helps a brahmacari stay kind under family pressure.

**SB 7.9.43**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/9/43/
> "I am simply concerned with love for them."
- **Context:** Prahlada tells Lord Nrsimha that he himself is not afraid of material existence, because he is absorbed in the Lord's glories, but he is concerned about people making plans for material happiness for their families and countries.
- **Purport point:** The purport says these people are called fools (vimudha) because they try to make the material world a place of happiness, but nature acts in her own way, and this cannot be done without the Lord's mercy.
- **Application (preacher's):** Prahlada's mood towards those who are absorbed in families is love and concern, not contempt. The same mood is right for our own relatives.
- **Use:** in the talk (I.3).

**BG 17.15**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/17/15/
> "…speaking words that are truthful, pleasing, beneficial, and not agitating to others"
- **Purport point:** The purport says one should not speak so as to agitate others' minds, one should not talk nonsense, and talk should be pleasing to the ear and supported by scripture.
- **Application (preacher's):** At home, the first austerity is gentle, true and pleasant speech. Builds on bss-26, where BG 17.14-16 are used for austerity.
- **Use:** in the talk (I.3).

**SB 5.5.18**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/5/5/18/
- Builds on bss-07, where the verse is read in full. Here it is used only as quoted in the purport to CC Madhya 3.181. **Use:** reference only.

## F. Philosophical explanation
**Principle.** In the Vaishnava teaching, the real relationship with every person is through Krishna. A devotee wishes the lasting good of his family and friends, which is Krishna consciousness. He gives it by the means that suit the person: patience and service for the innocent, closeness for devotees, distance from the envious (SB 11.2.46).
**BIKC's guidance (BIKC ch. 38), in summary (see O).** A Krishna conscious person cannot be totally insensitive to those near and dear from before. Parents often cannot understand why "their boy" has changed his life. Family members may be favourable, neutral or hostile. If dealt with considerately, they usually adjust gradually and sometimes become devotees. So be patient and do not antagonize. If they make a fuss, point out the positive aspects of your involvement that they can appreciate, such as that you are happy and living a clean life, and give them prasadam as much as possible. If they remain persistently antagonistic, BIKC says there may be no alternative to politely but firmly cutting off contact until they are ready to change their outlook, and ends: "ultimately it's your life". BIKC quotes a letter of Srila Prabhupada of 29 March 1968 which advises treating four kinds of people in four ways (love God and devotees; make friendship with devotees; enlighten the innocent; reject the opposed), giving parents proper respect according to social custom while not accepting un-Godly instructions, and, to avoid misunderstanding, remaining silent without affirming or rejecting their instructions, while being careful not to let them know disapproval so that they do not feel sad. (BIKC's quotation of the letter; *Requires source review* against the original letter.)
**IYF's deck (research/iyf-materials/iyf-decks/Topic-8 Art of Smart Dealing - Parent_ Relatives_ Friends _ IYF.txt).** The deck sorts observers into three groups: those who will never understand, those who will understand after some time, and those who will want to become like you. It advises: for the first group, avoid them and do not become inimical; for the second, do not criticize or debate, help them, and give prasadam; for the third, bring them to classes. For parents it advises: understand their need, interest, concern and expectation and reciprocate; see things from their perspective; talk regularly; tell them how your studies have improved because they care about that; you do not have to make them understand everything; work with the head and not with the heart only; do not accept every proposal and do not fight; if they are in need, go and serve them. (The deck is a slide outline; the lecture itself was not transcribed for this page. The deck also uses two Hindi sayings that are not explained in the text; they are not used here.)
**Connections.** bss-06 (Prahlada with his father); bss-07 (SB 5.5.18); bss-13 (leaving home); bss-30 (attitudes to householders); bss-35 (dealing with nondevotees in general).
**Consequences.** Harshness hardens opposition and shuts the door to the family's future interest. Patience and service often win their trust.
**Common misunderstandings.**
1. "Devotees should break off from family." BIKC says to be patient and considerate and only in the case of persistent antagonism to limit contact, as a last step.
2. "Being kind means agreeing with everything." The letter says to remain silent without affirming un-Godly instructions, and to keep to the principles.
3. "Relations are only illusion, so I need not care." The purports of SB 6.16.5 and 6.16.8 speak of false possessiveness, not of neglecting duty. Lord Caitanya cared for His mother (CC Madhya 3).
4. "Friends from before are all bad association." Use SB 11.2.46 to sort: some friends are innocent and can be helped.
**How to pitch it.** Speak warmly, with examples of kindness. Many in the room will carry worry about their family.

## H. Opening and engagement
**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes).**
1. One round of kirtana or a short prayer; say the title.
2. Say: "Almost everyone who takes to Krishna consciousness seriously meets questions from family or old friends. Today we learn how Lord Caitanya Himself handled it with His mother."
3. Read CC Madhya 3.176 slowly. Say: "He says He can give up neither the devotees nor His mother. Let us see how He and His mother solved it."
**H.2 Opening questions.**
1. "What is one thing your parents or old friends worry about when they see your spiritual life?" (Take three answers; do not comment.)
2. "What is one thing they appreciate?"
3. "If you had five minutes to help them feel comfortable, what would you say or do?"

## I. Core teaching points
**I.1 Four kinds of persons (SB 11.2.46).** The intermediate devotee loves the Lord, befriends devotees, shows mercy to the innocent and distances himself from the envious. Most family members and old friends belong to the third group. They are not enemies; they do not know. The IYF deck says the same in three groups and advises that for those who will understand in time one should not criticize or debate but help them and give prasadam. Transition: "What does this look like in a real family? Lord Caitanya shows us."
**I.2 Lord Caitanya and Sacimata (CC Madhya 3.175-185).** The Lord says He will never be indifferent to the devotees and cannot give up His mother, but a sannyasi should not live encircled by relatives. His mother says that she wants His happiness more than her own, and proposes that He live at Jagannatha Puri, close enough for news to travel. The purport says a mother who understands lets her son search for Krishna. Notice the method: the family speaks openly, finds a solution together, and keeps contact. Transition: "How do we do this at home?"
**I.3 Practical ways to keep goodwill (BIKC; IYF deck; BG 17.15; SB 7.9.43).** (a) Speak gently, truthfully and pleasantly (BG 17.15). (b) Give prasadam often. (c) Talk with them regularly and tell them about the good changes they can see, such as better studies, health or peace; the deck says parents care about these. (d) Do not try to make them understand everything; do not argue. (e) When they have a need, serve them. (f) Follow the letter's advice: respect them, do not accept un-Godly instructions, and avoid a debate. (g) Keep Prahlada's mood: love for them (SB 7.9.43).
**I.4 When a family stays hostile, and the truth about relations (BIKC; SB 6.16.5; SB 6.17.28).** If they stay hostile, BIKC says that the last step is to limit contact politely but firmly until they are ready to change. Before that step, speak to your mentor. The scriptures tell us that bodily relations change (SB 6.16.5), so we should not cling, and devotees are fearless (6.17.28). Yet the same Lord who said this arranged for His mother's news. So firmness and kindness go together.

## J. Stories and illustrations
**J.1 Lord Caitanya and Sacimata (CC Madhya 3.150-190).** After taking sannyasa, the Lord came to Advaita Acarya's house in Santipura. Sacimata arrived and began to cook for Him (3.160). When He fell to the ground in kirtana, she prayed that He should feel no pain (3.163-166). Other devotees wished to feed Him, but she asked that she alone supply His food while He stayed (3.169-171). The Lord told the devotees that He could not give up them or His mother, and that people would blame Him if a sannyasi stayed among relatives, and He asked them to make an arrangement (3.176-178). Sacimata proposed Jagannatha Puri, from where news would reach her (3.182-184), and said she cared only for His happiness (3.185). **Devotional lesson:** love for the Lord, shown by the mother, makes room for His service; and the Lord returns that love with care for her. This is the model for a family that lets its son serve Krishna.
**J.2 Citraketu (SB 6.14-17; key verse 6.17.28). A note first:** this story is about the death of a child. Do not tell it in a way that could hurt anyone who has lost a family member. If the audience includes someone in fresh grief, leave it out. King Citraketu, who had no child, received a son by the grace of the sage Angira. The child died, apparently poisoned by a jealous co-wife. Narada, by his power, let the soul speak. The soul said that no one is permanently related (6.16.5), and that bodily relations are an illusion for one who is not enlightened (6.16.8). The relatives gave up their lamentation (6.16.12-13), and Citraketu came out of "the dark well of family life" (6.16.15). Later Lord Siva says that devotees of Narayana never fear any condition of life (6.17.28). **Devotional lesson:** Narada's mercy freed the whole family from grief and gave Citraketu devotion to the Lord. Real kindness to a family is to give it a connection with Krishna.
**Illustration (preacher's, labelled).** A person who is crossing a river on a boat with his relatives does not argue with them about the boat. He helps each person to sit safely and keeps rowing to the other shore. The boat is the human form, the other shore is Krishna's service, and the family are fellow travellers whom he serves along the way.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections
1. **"My parents say I should marry and work. What do I say?"** Listen, thank them, and say you are thinking carefully and taking advice from your mentor (see the IYF deck: work with head, not heart only). Do not argue.
2. **"They criticise my guru or the movement."** BIKC advises patience and showing positive results. The letter advises silence without affirmation or negation of instructions, and respect. Do not debate.
3. **"Is it wrong to cut off contact?"** BIKC says it is the last option for persistent hostility, "until they are ready to change their outlook". Discuss it with your mentor first. Keep praying for them, as SB 7.9.43 shows.
4. **"My old friends tease me. What do I do?"** Use SB 11.2.46: some are innocent and need mercy, and some are persistently hostile. Be cheerful, share prasadam, invite them to a programme, and keep your distance from those who mock.
5. **"I feel guilty about leaving my family."** Lord Caitanya's example shows that care can continue. Talk to your mentor. If you feel very anxious or low, a counsellor or Tele-MANAS 14416 (India) can help.

## L. Case studies
**L.1 Invented case for discussion.** A brahmacari's father says he is wasting his education. The brahmacari feels that he must prove him wrong. *Dilemma:* argue, or remain silent and risk looking weak. *Principles:* BG 17.15 (pleasing, beneficial speech), the letter (respect; silence without argument), IYF deck (tell them what they care about). *Questions:* What can the brahmacari say that is true, kind and brief? What can he do for his father this week? *Analysis:* he thanks his father, shares the exam result or the job offer if he has one, offers prasadam, and helps with a household task. *Takeaway:* kindness in action speaks more than argument.
**L.2 Invented case for discussion (extension).** A group of old friends invite a brahmacari to a late-night party. *Principles:* SB 11.2.46, bss-30, CC Madhya 3.177. *Questions:* How can he refuse with warmth? How can he stay a friend? *Takeaway:* he says thank you, invites them to a festival feast or kirtana, and keeps in touch by a phone call.

## M. Discussion and reflection
**M.1 Closing check questions (with expected answers).**
1. *What four kinds of person does SB 11.2.46 name?* The Lord, the devotees, the innocent, and those envious of the Lord.
2. *How does a devotee treat the innocent?* With mercy: patience, a good example, prasadam.
3. *What did Lord Caitanya say about His mother (CC Madhya 3.176)?* He could not give her up.
4. *What did Sacimata propose?* That He live at Jagannatha Puri so that news could travel.
5. *What does BIKC advise if parents make a fuss?* Be patient, point to the positive results, give prasadam.
6. *When does BIKC say contact may be cut?* Only if hostility persists, politely and firmly, and after talking to your mentor.
**M.2 Activities.**
a) *Prasadam plan (10 min, pairs).* Each pair plans how and when to give prasadam to a family member or friend this month.
b) *Three sentences (10 min, pairs).* Each writes three sentences for a parent: a thank you, a positive change, and an invitation (for example, to a festival). The partner checks that the words are true, pleasant and beneficial (BG 17.15).
c) *Short drama (15 min, extension).* Roles: Lord Caitanya, Sacimata, Advaita Acarya. Script of six lines using CC Madhya 3.176-185 only (label as dramatisation). Debrief: how did they solve the problem together?
**M.3 Reflection.** "What would my family say they see in me that they like?" Private.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary
Many devotees find that parents or old friends do not understand their new life. The book *Brahmacarya in Krishna consciousness* says we cannot be insensitive to those near and dear from before. Parents often cannot understand why "their boy" has changed. Family members may be favourable, neutral or hostile. If treated with consideration, they usually adjust, and some become devotees.
Srimad-Bhagavatam 11.2.46 describes the intermediate devotee, who offers love to the Lord, is a sincere friend to devotees, shows mercy to the innocent, and keeps away from those who are envious of the Lord. Most relatives and old friends are innocent. They deserve patience, a good example and prasadam. IYF's deck advises that we do not argue with them, that we talk regularly, that we tell them about the good changes they care about, such as studies and health, and that we serve them when they are in need.
Lord Caitanya shows how to do it. When He met His mother Sacimata after taking sannyasa, He said He could not give her up, and also that a sannyasi should not live among relatives. Together with His mother and the devotees He found a solution: He would live at Jagannatha Puri, where news could travel (CC Madhya 3.176-185). Srila Prabhupada's purport says a mother who wants her son's real progress lets him search for Krishna. If a family stays hostile, the book says the last step may be to limit contact, politely and firmly, after speaking with your mentor. Throughout, the aim is to give family and friends the best gift, a connection with Krishna, gently.

## S2. Key ideas
- Most relatives and old friends are innocent, and deserve mercy.
- Speak gently and truthfully, give prasadam, and serve them when they need help.
- Do not argue; do not give up your practice.
- Lord Caitanya and His mother solved the problem by talking and agreeing.
- Limiting contact is a last step, taken after talking to a mentor.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **SB 11.2.46**: the devotee "shows mercy to ignorant people who are innocent and disregards those who are envious of the Supreme Personality of Godhead." *For me:* I treat different people in different ways.
- **CC Madhya 3.185**: "I do not care for my personal happiness or unhappiness, but only for His happiness." *For me:* a parent's love lets the child serve Krishna.
- **BG 17.15**: speaking "words that are truthful, pleasing, beneficial, and not agitating to others". *For me:* I speak gently at home.

## S4. Words to know
- **Brahmacari:** a celibate student living simply and serving his guru.
- **Madhyama-adhikari:** an intermediate devotee, who relates in four ways (SB 11.2.46).
- **Sannyasi:** a person in the renounced order.
- **Prasadam:** food offered to Krishna.
- **Innocent:** a person who does not know about spiritual life but has no ill will.
- **Mentor:** a senior devotee who guides and cares for you.

## S5. Questions and answers
1. *What do I say when my parents worry?* Listen, thank them, tell them the good changes they can see, and share prasadam. Do not argue.
2. *What if they criticise?* Stay respectful and calm. Remain silent without agreeing to un-Godly instructions (BIKC, quoting Srila Prabhupada's letter).
3. *Must I cut off contact?* Only as a last step if hostility persists, politely, and after you have talked to your mentor.
4. *How do I treat old friends?* With mercy for the innocent, distance from those who mock, and an invitation to a programme.
