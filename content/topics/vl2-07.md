# Identifying the divine guide — how ISKCON is not comparable to other so-called spiritual organisations

> **VL2 · 180 min · review: sources-checked · controversy topic, sensitive.** IYF's angle (site subtitle): *"establishing
> ISKCON as the hope for the current human civilisation."* The page handles the comparison by **tests**, applied
> equally to every teacher and institution including ISKCON, and names no other organisation negatively (F.5, K).
> IYF notes: `research/iyf-materials/vl2-07/` (EBG-5 lecture notes and the slide text "Identifying the Divine Guide").

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on [DYS-2](dys-01-s2.md) (we learn what lies beyond the senses by hearing a reliable source, and BG 4.34 asks for inquiry, not blind following) and [VL2-06](vl2-06.md) (why the teaching needs re-establishing). Neither argument is repeated; this page's own angle is **how to recognise a reliable teacher or institution**, by tests anyone can apply.

VL2-05 and VL2-06 argued from texts. The sceptic's best reply came up in both: "Texts pass through human hands;
who decides what they mean?" This topic is the tradition's answer: a qualified guide in an unbroken line, checked
against scripture and the saints. At VL2 the case is reasoned: we accept guides everywhere in life; the Gita's
model student asks for one (BG 2.7); the texts give tests, and tests cut both ways. IYF's subtitle asks for a
comparison with other organisations. A reasoned-faith talk makes that comparison by **criteria the listener can
apply**, never by naming others' faults.

**IYF's VL2 frame** (IYF's own documents, `research/batch-criteria.md` §2 and §8 point 2): course name "Technical Science
for Spiritual Value", mood *questioning, reasoned*; motive "developing strong faith in practicing spirituality" and "removing all
misconceptions about ISKCON and spirituality"; KRAs "Start doing chanting", "Coming to regular classes", "Meeting with
facilitators". The 2015 VL2 lesson plan runs DYS 1–6 first and then the EBG study-sessions; this page mirrors the EBG session "Identifying the Divine Guide" (Encyclopedia transcript, O). Practice is
expected to *begin* during VL2 (by its end: 2–4 rounds, 15 min of Prabhupada's books and 15–30 min of kirtan hearing a day), so
this page may carry a first practical step without breaking IYF's level (P).

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **VL2** (here) | Why a guide (BG 2.7, 4.34, SB 11.3.21); tests of a genuine guide (parampara, realisation, scripture, sense control, example, no caste bar); warning signs the texts give (SB 7.5.31, BG 16.23); how to apply the tests to any institution; what ISKCON claims and how to check it |
| VL1 | A short talk on asking for help when confused (BG 2.7 only) |

**Not done here:**
- Naming any living teacher or organisation as fraudulent. The texts give criteria; the listener applies them.
- Claiming ISKCON has never had a failure of leadership. It has, and says so in its own child-protection and governance documents (K.5; *Requires source review* for specifics).
- Asking anyone to accept a guru today. VL2 ends with "know the tests".

## C. Central message

Life's perplexities arrive uninvited, "like a forest fire" (BG 2.7 purport), and the Gita's own model student
responds by asking for a guide. Scripture then tells us how to recognise a genuine one: he has heard in an
unbroken line (BG 4.2, 4.34), has realised the conclusions of scripture and can convince others (SB 11.3.21),
controls his senses (NOI 1), teaches by example, and is judged by knowledge of Krsna rather than birth (CC Madhya
8.128). These tests apply to every teacher and institution, including ours.

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. Explain why the Gita says a guide is needed, using Arjuna's own request (BG 2.7) and Krsna's instruction (BG 4.34).
2. List five scriptural tests of a genuine guide with a verse for each.
3. Name the warning signs the texts themselves give (SB 7.5.31, BG 16.23, SB 5.5.18).
4. Describe the "checks and balances" of guru, sadhu and sastra and say why no one of them stands alone.
5. Apply the tests to an institution's claims, including ISKCON's, and say what evidence they would look for.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** seven references carry the talk (BG 2.7, 4.34, 4.2, SB 11.3.21, NOI 1, CC Madhya 8.128, SB 7.5.31). The rest prepare you for K.

**BG 2.7**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/2/7/
> "Now I am confused about my duty and have lost all composure because of miserly weakness. In this condition I am asking You to tell me for certain what is best for me. Now I am Your disciple, and a soul surrendered unto You. Please instruct me."
- **Context:** Arjuna, overwhelmed before the battle, stops arguing and asks for instruction.
- **Teaching:** Confusion about duty; a request for certain guidance; acceptance of discipleship.
- **Purport point:** Perplexities "are like a forest fire that somehow blazes without being set by anyone"; "one must approach a spiritual master who is in the disciplic succession". The purport quotes Brhad-aranyaka Upanisad 3.8.10: the *krpana* (miser) "quits this world like the cats and dogs, without understanding the science of self-realization"; the *brahmana* uses the body "to solve all the problems of life". Arjuna "offers himself to Kṛṣṇa as a disciple."
- **Application (preacher's):** IYF's notes open with the two types of men from this purport. It is the honest starting point: not "you must have a guru" but "here is what the Gita's own hero did when he was stuck".
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 4.34**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/4/34/
> "Just try to learn the truth by approaching a spiritual master. Inquire from him submissively and render service unto him. The self-realized souls can impart knowledge unto you because they have seen the truth."
- **Teaching:** Approach a spiritual master; inquire submissively and serve; the self-realised can impart knowledge because they have seen the truth.
- **Purport point:** "No one can be a bona fide spiritual master without following this principle of disciplic succession"; "mental speculation or dry arguments cannot help"; "In this verse, both blind following and absurd inquiries are condemned"; "Inquiries and submission constitute the proper combination for spiritual understanding."
- **Application (preacher's):** The purport's sentence condemning blind following is the one a VL2 audience needs to hear from the preacher's mouth. Reasoned faith is the text's own standard.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 4.2, 4.3**: Verified. The science is received "through the chain of disciplic succession" and the succession can break (4.2); Krsna speaks it again to a devotee and friend (4.3). **Use:** test 1 (parampara).

**SB 11.3.21, 11.3.22**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/3/21/ · *BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples.*
> 11.3.21: "Therefore any person who seriously desires real happiness must seek a bona fide spiritual master and take shelter of him by initiation. The qualification of the bona fide guru is that he has realized the conclusions of the scriptures by deliberation and is able to convince others of these conclusions. Such great personalities, who have taken shelter of the Supreme Godhead, leaving aside all material considerations, should be understood to be bona fide spiritual masters."
- **Teaching:** The genuine guru has realised scripture's conclusions by deliberation, can convince others, and has left aside material considerations.
- **Purport point:** The guru must be *nisnatam*, "deeply experienced in authorized Vedic literatures and in practical understanding of the Supreme Personality of Godhead"; "Without scriptural knowledge and practical realization… a so-called guru will be unable to dissipate the doubts of his disciples"; *upasamasrayam*: one "who has retired from the glittering illusions of materialistic society, friendship and love."
- **Application (preacher's):** Tests 2 and 3 (realisation; scripture) and the warning about material attachment. The disciple learns pure devotional service from such a guru (11.3.22).
- **Use:** in the talk.

**NOI 1, NOI 5**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/1/
> NOI 1: "A sober person who can tolerate the urge to speak, the mind's demands, the actions of anger and the urges of the tongue, belly and genitals is qualified to make disciples all over the world."
- **Teaching:** Control of six urges is the qualification to make disciples.
- **Purport point (NOI 1):** "One who is less intelligent gathers experience by seeing, and one who is more intelligent gathers experience by hearing"; atonement without sense control is like "an elephant's bathing"; "real prāyaścitta, atonement, is the awakening of our dormant Kṛṣṇa consciousness."
- **Application (preacher's):** Test 4 (sense control) is Rupa Gosvami's first instruction. IYF's notes: "one whose senses are not in control cannot be trusted." NOI 5 grades one's dealings by the devotee's level; **use:** background.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**CC Madhya 8.128**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/8/128/
> "Whether one is a brāhmaṇa, a sannyāsī or a śūdra — regardless of what he is — he can become a spiritual master if he knows the science of Kṛṣṇa."
- **Teaching:** Knowledge of the science of Krsna, not social position, qualifies a spiritual master.
- **Purport point:** "knowledge of Kṛṣṇa consciousness is more important than caste"; three kinds of guru: vartma-pradarsaka (first information), diksa (initiation), siksa (instruction); a brahmana "cannot become a spiritual master unless he is a Vaiṣṇava."
- **Application (preacher's):** Test 5 (no caste or nationality bar), and the answer to K.3 on caste.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 7.5.31, SB 5.5.18, BG 16.23, 16.24**: Verified.
> SB 7.5.31: "…who have therefore accepted as their leader or guru a similar blind man attached to external sense objects… As blind men guided by another blind man miss the right path and fall into a ditch…"
> SB 5.5.18: "One who cannot deliver his dependents from the path of repeated birth and death should never become a spiritual master…"
- **Teaching:** A guru attached to sense objects leads followers into a ditch; one who cannot deliver dependants should not become a guru; acting against scripture brings no perfection (16.23); duty is learnt from scripture (16.24).
- **Purport point (16.23):** "A person who knowingly violates the rules acts in lust."
- **Application (preacher's):** The texts' own warning signs. IYF's list (fees for mantras, licence to abandon austerities, yoga as gymnastics, "I am God") maps onto these verses; present the verses, not names.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**CC Adi 1.46, 1.47**: Verified. "One should know the ācārya as Myself and never disrespect him" (1.46, Krsna's words quoted from SB 11.17.27); the instructing spiritual master is Krsna's manifestation (1.47). **Use:** K.6, with care: this is the position of a *genuine* acarya; it is never a reason to suspend the tests.

**BG 13.8–12, 4.39, 4.40, 9.3, 4.11, 18.75, CC Madhya 19.151**: Verified.
- 13.8–12 lists "approaching a bona fide spiritual master" among the items of knowledge. 4.39–40 and 9.3: faith is needed to gain knowledge; the faithless doubter finds no happiness. 4.11: all who surrender are rewarded accordingly (for K.2). 18.75: Sanjaya heard "by the mercy of Vyāsa", a teacher. CC Madhya 19.151: by the mercy of Krsna one gets a bona fide guru, and from both the seed of devotion. **Use:** K and F.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** [DYS-2](dys-01-s2.md) showed that we hear from a reliable source and inquire (BG 4.34), in a line that preserves the message (4.2). This page asks only whom to hear. The Gita's model student asks when stuck (2.7), and Prahlada says the alternative is the blind leading the blind (SB 7.5.31).
2. **The tests.** Scripture does not say "trust anyone in robes". It gives criteria, each with a verse:
   1. *Heard in succession* (BG 4.2, 4.34 purport): no concocted philosophy.
   2. *Realised the conclusions and can convince others* (SB 11.3.21): not only a scholar, not only a mystic.
   3. *Teaches by scripture, not against it* (BG 16.23–24): what he says can be checked.
   4. *Controls the senses* (NOI 1; SB 11.3.21 *upasamasrayam*): not after wealth, women or followers.
   5. *Judged by knowledge of Krsna, not by birth, caste or nationality* (CC Madhya 8.128).
   6. *Gives spiritual knowledge, not material boons* (SB 11.3.22; IYF: "guru is not an ashirwad-baba").
   7. *Teaches by example* (acarya; BG 3.21 in VL2-06).
3. **Checks and balances.** Guru, sadhu and sastra confirm one another. A teacher who contradicts scripture fails test 3 however charismatic; a reading of scripture no saint ever held should be doubted; the saints are recognised by scripture's own criteria. IYF's notes call this the "check and balance system". Say plainly: this is why tradition protects against both the lone guru and the lone reader.
4. **Applying the tests to institutions, including ours.** An organisation is tested the same way: Is its message the parampara text or a new one (test 1, BG 4.2 purport)? Are its teachers trained in scripture and expected to live it (tests 2–4)? Does it teach anyone regardless of birth (test 5)? Does it promise liberation or boons (test 6)? Does it correct leaders who fail (test 4)? Prabhupada's claim for ISKCON rests on test 1 (the Gita "as it is") and on the purposes written in 1966 (VL2-06). The listener should hear that ISKCON invites the tests, not that it is exempt from them.
5. **The comparison IYF asks for, done fairly.** "Not comparable to so-called spiritual organisations" is a strong phrase. The honest VL2 form is: *the texts give tests; here they are; apply them to everyone.* Say nothing about any named organisation. Where a practice is described (fees for secret mantras, teaching yoga only for the body, claiming "I am God"), cite the verse it fails (BG 16.23; SB 7.5.31; BG 4.34 purport on manufacturing a process) and leave the listener to apply it. Chaitanya Charan's "inclusivist" frame (https://www.thespiritualscientist.com/do-all-religions-lead-to-god/, linked from chaitanyacharan.com) is useful here: many paths exist; the test is whether a path moves a person towards God.
6. **"The hope for civilisation."** Prabhupada's claim, in CC Adi 9.41 purport (VL2-06) and the seven purposes, is that a society educated in these principles becomes peaceful and God-conscious. At VL2 present it as his claim and ask what evidence would count: changed lives the listener can meet, communities that live the four principles, books anyone can read. Not "we are the hope" but "this is the hope he offered; test it".
7. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - **"Having a guru means switching off my mind."** BG 4.34 purport condemns blind following in the same sentence as absurd inquiry.
   - **"All gurus are frauds because some were."** SB 7.5.31 and SB 5.5.18 are the texts' own warnings; the existence of tests presupposes that some fail them.
   - **"ISKCON passes automatically."** No institution does. ISKCON's own history includes leaders who failed test 4; its governance and child-protection reforms are its response (K.5).
   - **"Guru is God."** CC Adi 1.46 says to honour the acarya as the Lord's representative; the purport context is a genuine acarya. It is never a licence to abandon tests 1–4.
8. **Pitch for VL2.** Define *guru*, *parampara*, *acarya*, *sadhu*, *sastra* once each. Argue from everyday trust, Arjuna's request, and the tests. End with "know the tests", not "take initiation".

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Welcome by name; say today is about how to judge any teacher or organisation, not about accepting one.
2. Ask H.2 question 1; take three answers and thank each. Do not ask for details of anyone's pain.
3. Say: "Everyone here has acted on someone's advice. The question is how we decide whom to believe."
4. Write "how do I check?" on the board.
5. Promise: we will use tests from the texts, apply them to everyone including ourselves, and name no one.

**H.2 Opening questions**
- Think of advice you followed that turned out wrong. What could you have checked beforehand?
- What makes you trust a doctor, a mechanic or a village elder you have never tested?
- What would you want to know about a teacher before you listened to them for years?

**Adapting for your audience:** any trade works for the trust question: a farmer choosing a seed seller, a patient choosing a doctor, an investor choosing an adviser. Avoid the room's own religious leaders as examples.

**More material for the opening**

- **Opening question:** H.2 question 1 (the older "whom did you trust today" question repeated DYS-2's everyday-trust argument and is dropped).
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** A student buys a "diamond" online because the seller said it was one. Her friend asks, "Did you check with a jeweller?" Then the friend asks: "And your meditation teacher? Did you check him?" (IYF's notes use the same image.)
- **Transition:** "The Gita's hero was not ashamed to ask for a guide (BG 2.7). The texts were also not naive about guides: they give tests. Today we learn both."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** scandals have made "guru" a suspect word; loyalty to a family guru has made it an untouchable one. The tests free the listener from both.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Perplexity arrives uninvited; the wise ask for guidance.** BG 2.7 and purport (forest fire; krpana vs brahmana). *Example:* the exam question nobody taught you. *Transition:* but whom to ask?
2. **One who has seen the truth, in a line that preserved it.** BG 4.34, 4.2. *Example:* electricity reaches your socket only through connected poles (IYF's image). *Transition:* and how do I recognise such a person?
3. **Scripture gives tests.** Succession; realisation and ability to convince (SB 11.3.21); scripture (BG 16.23–24); sense control (NOI 1); knowledge of Krsna over birth (CC Madhya 8.128); spiritual not material gifts (SB 11.3.22); example. *Transition:* and tests can fail.
4. **Scripture gives warnings too.** Blind leading blind (SB 7.5.31); one who cannot deliver should not be guru (SB 5.5.18). *Example:* the texts' own list, not newspaper names. *Transition:* three checks.
5. **Guru, sadhu, sastra.** No one alone. *Transition:* the same for institutions.
6. **Apply the tests to every institution, including ours.** What ISKCON claims (test 1; seven purposes), what it has got wrong and reformed, what you can check. *Transition:* to the hope and to P.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

> **Before you teach:** someone in the room has been hurt by a religious leader, or loves one you might be tempted to criticise. Give the tests; name no one. If a listener raises a specific case, acknowledge the pain, do not adjudicate in public, offer to talk after.

1. **"Why do I need a guru? I have the Gita and the internet."**
   - BG 4.34 purport: "Nor by independent study of books of knowledge can one progress in spiritual life." The Gita itself was received through a teacher (BG 18.75), and its own hero asked for one (2.7). Honest question: answer with the everyday-trust argument, then the verses.
2. **"My family has a guru. Are you saying he is false?"**
   - No. The texts give tests (F.2); apply them yourself, with respect. BG 4.11: all who sincerely surrender are rewarded accordingly. Nothing here asks anyone to reject a family guru tonight.
3. **"Isn't guru-ism how caste kept power?"**
   - Hereditary priesthood did. CC Madhya 8.128 is the tradition's own correction: knowledge of Krsna, not birth, qualifies a guru; the purport says a brahmana who is not a Vaisnava cannot be one.
4. **"After all the scandals, how can you ask for faith in any teacher?"**
   - SB 7.5.31 and SB 5.5.18 are the texts' own warnings; the tests exist because some fail them. Faith is asked for a teacher who passes them, and even then "blind following" is condemned (BG 4.34 purport). Objection answered by agreeing with its premise.
5. **"ISKCON has had its own scandals."**
   - Yes. Leaders have failed test 4 (sense control) and worse; ISKCON has set up governance and child-protection structures in response. Say this plainly. Details and dates: **Requires source review** against ISKCON's own published documents before quoting. The point for VL2: the tests apply to us, and an institution is judged partly by how it corrects.
6. **"If the guru is 'as good as God' (CC Adi 1.46), how can I test him?"**
   - The verse describes how to honour a genuine acarya; the whole chapter assumes the tests have been met. Honour follows recognition; it never replaces it.
7. **"What makes ISKCON 'not comparable' to others?"**
   - Reframe: the texts give criteria; apply them to everyone. ISKCON's distinctive claims are an unaltered parampara text (BG 4.2 purport) and the stated purposes of 1966. Whether it meets its own tests is for the listener to examine. Never run down another organisation.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The weekend retreat.** Nikhil is invited to a retreat where a teacher gives "personalised mantras" for a fee and says scripture is "for beginners; advanced seekers follow intuition."
- **Principles:** BG 4.34 purport (manufacturing one's own process); BG 16.23 (acting against scripture); SB 11.3.21 (realised the conclusions *of the scriptures*).
- **Questions:** Which tests does Nikhil have enough information to apply? What would he need to ask? Is the fee itself the problem, or what it is for?
- **Takeaway:** The texts' tests give words for a discomfort Nikhil already felt.

**Case 2: The devoted uncle.** Priyanka's uncle follows a guru who gives blessings for business success and asks for nothing. Priyanka admires his sincerity but hears nothing of scripture in the satsang.
- **Principles:** SB 11.3.22 (spiritual knowledge, not boons); BG 4.11 (sincerity rewarded); CC Madhya 19.151 (the seed of devotion comes through a bona fide guru).
- **Questions:** What does Priyanka respect here? Which test is unaddressed? How can she raise it without insulting her uncle?
- **Takeaway:** Respect for sincerity and clarity about tests can coexist.

**Case 3: The ISKCON mentor.** Arjun's IYF mentor is kind and learned, and Arjun has started treating his every opinion, including on Arjun's career, as scripture.
- **Principles:** BG 4.34 purport (blind following condemned); guru–sadhu–sastra; NOI 5 (dealings graded by level).
- **Questions:** What has Arjun confused? What would healthy inquiry look like? How would a genuine mentor respond to being questioned?
- **Takeaway:** The tests protect the relationship as much as the seeker.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first)
1. In BG 2.7, what does Arjuna do when he is perplexed? *Expected:* he asks Krsna to be his teacher and becomes a disciple.
2. Name two of the seven tests. *Expected:* any two, for example heard in succession; realised and able to convince; teaches by scripture; controls the senses.
3. What does CC Madhya 8.128 say about birth? *Expected:* knowledge of Krsna, not birth, qualifies a teacher.
4. What are the three checks that lock together? *Expected:* guru, sadhu, sastra.
5. Do the tests apply to ISKCON? *Expected:* Yes, to every teacher and institution; that is the point.

- **B. Build your own checklist (30 min).** Pairs write five things they would check before trusting an adviser they will follow for years (a doctor, a mentor, a spiritual teacher); then match each to the seven tests and note any test they missed (BG 4.34 asks for inquiry). Share the best three.
- **C. Role-play the first meeting (25 min).** One plays a respectful newcomer with three prepared questions, one plays a teacher answering from the texts; swap after ten minutes. Debrief: which question was best?

**M.3 Reflection (private, optional).** Whom have I trusted in spiritual matters, and by which of the seven tests? Which one test would I apply this month to the voices I already listen to? Nobody needs to share.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

When we are confused about life's deepest questions, whom do we ask? The Bhagavad-gita (an ancient Sanskrit scripture, here in Srila Prabhupada's translation) shows its hero Arjuna doing something brave: he admits he is perplexed, asks Krsna for a guide and becomes a disciple (BG 2.7). Krsna then says how knowledge comes: approach one who has seen the truth, inquire respectfully and serve (BG 4.34), in an unbroken line of teachers that has kept the message intact (BG 4.2). The same verse's purport warns against blind following as much as against arrogant questioning.

But the texts were not naive about teachers. They warn that a blind leader leads followers into a ditch (SB 7.5.31) and give tests. A genuine guru, a spiritual teacher, has realised the conclusions of scripture and can convince others (SB 11.3.21); controls his senses (NOI 1, the Nectar of Instruction); teaches according to scripture (BG 16.24); gives spiritual knowledge rather than material favours (SB 11.3.22); is qualified by knowledge of Krsna, not by birth or nationality (CC Madhya 8.128); and teaches by example. Guru, sadhu (saintly persons) and sastra (scripture) confirm one another, so none stands alone.

These tests apply to every teacher and every organisation, including ISKCON, whose founder claimed to present the same teaching without change and whose own history includes leaders who failed and reforms made in response. You do not have to accept a guru today; the step is to know the tests, apply them respectfully and keep inquiring.

## S2. Key ideas

- Perplexity comes uninvited; the wise ask for a guide (BG 2.7).
- Knowledge of the truth comes by hearing from one who has seen it, in succession (BG 4.34, 4.2).
- Scripture gives tests for a genuine guide; blind following is condemned (BG 4.34 purport).
- Scripture also gives warnings (SB 7.5.31, SB 5.5.18).
- Guru, sadhu and sastra check one another; none stands alone.
- The same tests apply to institutions, including ours.

## S3. Verses to remember

- **BG 4.34:** "Just try to learn the truth by approaching a spiritual master. Inquire from him submissively and render service unto him. The self-realized souls can impart knowledge unto you because they have seen the truth." *For me:* inquiry is part of the method.
- **SB 11.3.21:** the genuine guru "has realized the conclusions of the scriptures by deliberation and is able to convince others of these conclusions." *For me:* I can test what I am taught against scripture.
- **CC Madhya 8.128:** "regardless of what he is — he can become a spiritual master if he knows the science of Kṛṣṇa." *For me:* birth and status are not the test.

## S4. Words to know

- **Guru / spiritual master:** one who has seen the truth and can teach it (BG 4.34).
- **Parampara:** the chain of teachers through which the message is preserved (BG 4.2).
- **Acarya:** one who teaches by personal example.
- **Sadhu, sastra:** saintly persons and scripture; with the guru, the three checks on one another.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Why can't I learn from the book alone?** The Gita itself came through a teacher (BG 18.75), and its purport to 4.34 says independent study does not carry one forward. Books plus a qualified guide, with your questions intact.
2. **Does having a guru mean switching off my mind?** No. BG 4.34 purport condemns blind following as much as absurd inquiry.
3. **Is my family's guru false?** Nobody said so. Apply the tests yourself, with respect.
4. **After the scandals, why trust anyone?** The texts themselves warn about false guides (SB 7.5.31). That is why they give tests.
5. **Has ISKCON failed its own tests?** Some of its leaders have, and ISKCON has set up structures in response. The tests apply to us too.
6. **Do I have to accept a guru now?** No. Today's step is to know the tests.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. In BG 2.7, what does Arjuna call himself?
2. Name two of the three things BG 4.34 asks a student to do.
3. What two qualifications does SB 11.3.21 give for a genuine guru?
4. True or false: CC Madhya 8.128 says only a brahmana can be a spiritual master.
5. What image does SB 7.5.31 use for following an unqualified leader?

**Answers:** 1. "Your disciple, and a soul surrendered unto You" (BG 2.7). 2. Approach a spiritual master, inquire submissively, render service (BG 4.34). 3. He has realised the conclusions of the scriptures by deliberation, and he is able to convince others of them (SB 11.3.21). 4. False: anyone "regardless of what he is" who knows the science of Krsna (CC Madhya 8.128). 5. Blind men guided by a blind man, who fall into a ditch (SB 7.5.31).
