# Hari-bhakti-vilasa: why the Vaisnava rules matter

> **TRD · up to 180 min · review: drafted.** Group F, advanced Gaudiya siddhanta. The Hari-bhakti-vilasa is the Vaisnava smrti: the
> book of rules for Vaisnava life and Deity worship, compiled by Sanatana Gosvami at Lord Caitanya's order (CC Madhya 24.329-345) and
> arranged in twenty vilasas (CC Madhya 1.35 purport). Every standard a temple resident lives by, from initiation and tilaka to Ekadasi
> and arati, has its root here. This page teaches what the book is, why the Lord wanted it, how Srila Prabhupada used it, and where he
> drew its limits. **Source note:** the file `research/books/Sanatana_Goswami_Hari_Bhakti_vilasa.txt` (fetched 30 Oct 2026 from the
> Desire Tree catalogue) is a font-scrambled PDF extraction and could not be read; so is Gopala Bhatta Gosvami's Sat-kriya-sara-dipika.
> The Hari-bhakti-vilasa is quoted only where Srila Prabhupada quotes it, with his vilasa and verse numbers.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on dps-08: that page teaches Vaisnava etiquette as practice; this page gives the book behind the practice and the order of the Lord
that produced it. Builds on trd-10: Deity worship as a relationship is the spirit; the Hari-bhakti-vilasa is the body of rules in which the
spirit is kept. Builds on trd-28: pure bhakti is the only means; the rules exist to carry a devotee to it, and Srila Prabhupada's purports
say plainly where the rule serves and where the essence rules the rule.

A temple resident is the keeper of standards. If he knows the rules without their source, he either enforces them as custom or drops them
when inconvenient. Lord Caitanya did not leave the standards to custom: He told Sanatana Gosvami what the Vaisnava smrti must contain
(CC Madhya 24.329-345), and Sanatana wrote it (CC Madhya 1.35). Prabhupada quotes the book on initiation (CC Madhya 15.108 purport), on who
may receive a mantra (24.331 purport), on the renounced order's duties (Antya 6.223 purport), and he also records Bhaktisiddhanta
Sarasvati's opinion on the compilation (1.35 purport) and his own rule that the acharya adjusts details to time, place and candidate while
keeping the essence (23.105 purport). No lower batch studies the smrti itself.

## C. Central message

Lord Caitanya Himself ordered the Vaisnava smrti and dictated its contents, from taking shelter of a bona fide guru to Ekadasi and temple
building (CC Madhya 24.329-345); Sanatana Gosvami wrote it, and "Kṛṣṇa will help you by spiritually awakening you" (24.345). The rules are
not customs but the Lord's own direction for a life of devotion, to be followed "without argument" (BG 16.24 purport); and the acharya who
applies them "has to consider time, candidate and country" while never giving up the essence (CC Madhya 23.105 purport).

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Tell how the Hari-bhakti-vilasa came to be written, from Sanatana's request to the Lord's blessing (CC Madhya 24.325-328, 24.345).
2. List the contents of the Lord's synopsis in order (CC Madhya 24.329-344) and name the twenty vilasas in outline (CC Madhya 1.35 purport).
3. Quote the Hari-bhakti-vilasa rules Srila Prabhupada quotes: on initiation (2.3-4, 2.6, 2.10, 2.12), on who may receive a mantra (1.194), on
   householders and renunciants (20.366, 379, 382), with his sources.
4. Explain from BG 16.23-24 and SB 7.5.23-24 why regulation is the path and not a cage.
5. State and apply Prabhupada's limits: the acharya adjusts to time, place and candidate (CC Madhya 23.105); some rules are for those dependent
   on the social order (8.128); Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati's view of the compilation (1.35); the holy name needs no initiation to deliver (15.108).

**CC Madhya 24.325-328** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/24/326/
> 24.326: Sanatana Gosvami then requested the Lord, "Please personally tell me how I can write this difficult book about Vaiṣṇava behavior. Please manifest Yourself in my heart."
> 24.328: Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu replied, "Whatever you want to do you will be able to do correctly by Lord Kṛṣṇa's favor. He will manifest the real purport."
- **Context:** Varanasi; the end of the Lord's two months of teaching Sanatana. Sanatana calls himself lowborn and ignorant of good behaviour (24.325) and asks the Lord to direct him from within (24.327).
- **Purport point (24.325):** Sanatana "submitted himself as a fallen, lowborn person because he had served in the Muslim government."
- **Purport point (24.326):** "Vaiṣṇava literatures are not mental concoctions. They are all authorized literatures meant to guide those who are going to be Vaiṣṇavas." "Unless one is fully qualified in Vaiṣṇava behavior and authorized by superior authority (the Supreme Personality of Godhead), one cannot write Vaiṣṇava literatures".
- **Use:** in the talk (J story 1).

**CC Madhya 24.329-345: the Lord's synopsis** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/24/329/
> 24.329: "Because you asked Me for a synopsis, please hear these few indications. In the beginning describe how one must take shelter of a bona fide spiritual master."
> 24.345: "I have thus given a synopsis of the Vaiṣṇava regulative principles. I have given this in brief just to give you a little direction. When you write on this subject, Kṛṣṇa will help you by spiritually awakening you."
- **Teaching, in order:** the characteristics of guru and disciple, Krishna as the worshipable object, the bija-mantras (330); qualifications for a
  mantra, its perfection and purification, initiation, morning duties, remembrance, cleanliness (331); brushing the teeth, bathing, prayers,
  obeisances and service to the guru, tilaka in twelve places, stamping the body with the Lord's names and symbols (332); gopicandana, neck beads,
  tulasi leaves, cleaning cloth, altar and house, the temple bell (333); Deity worship with food offered at least five times daily, putting the
  Lord to bed, arati, worship with five, sixteen or fifty ingredients (334); the Deities and the salagrama-sila, visiting temples and holy places
  (335); the holy name's glory and its offences, the Vaisnava's symptoms, giving up seva-aparadha (336); the items of worship, soft chanting,
  prayers, circumambulation, obeisances (337); purascarana, taking prasadam, giving up unoffered food, not blaspheming devotees (338); the symptoms
  of a devotee, association, service to devotees, giving up nondevotees, hearing the Bhagavatam regularly (339); daily, fortnightly and monthly
  duties, Ekadasi, Janmastami, Rama-navami, Nrsimha-caturdasi (340-341); pure and mixed Ekadasi and the fault of not observing it (342); evidence
  from the Puranas for everything (343); what is to be done and not done, "described as regulations and etiquette" (344).
- **Purport point (24.330):** the Padma Purana's definition of the guru is quoted; "The guru must be situated on the topmost platform of devotional service."
- **Purport point (24.331):** "The following injunction is given in the Hari-bhakti-vilāsa (1.194) regarding mantra-adhikārī, the qualification for receiving mantra initiation" and the translation: "Śūdras and women who are chaste and sincerely interested in understanding the Absolute Truth are qualified to be initiated with the pāñcarātrika-mantras." Prabhupada confirms it with BG 9.32.
- **Purport point (24.334):** the five and the sixteen ingredients listed; the eleventh vilasa is referred to.
- **Purport point (24.337):** "All these are mentioned in the Hari-bhakti-vilāsa. The eighth vilāsa of that book should be consulted as far as possible."
- **Purport point (24.336):** "If a devotee very strictly follows the methods of Deity worship, he will naturally and quickly become a pure Vaiṣṇava."
- **Purport point (24.345):** "One cannot write on spiritual matters without being blessed by Kṛṣṇa and the disciplic succession of gurus. The blessings of the authorities are one's power of attorney."
- **Use:** in the talk; read 24.329-345 straight through (segment 3).

**CC Madhya 1.35 (with purport): the twenty vilasas** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/1/35/
> "Some of the books compiled by Śrīla Sanātana Gosvāmī were the Hari-bhakti-vilāsa, Bṛhad-bhāgavatāmṛta, Daśama-ṭippanī and Daśama-carita."
- **Purport point (contents):** "The subject matter of the Hari-bhakti-vilāsa, by Śrī Sanātana Gosvāmī, was collected by Śrīla Gopāla Bhaṭṭa Gosvāmī and is known as a vaiṣṇava-smṛti. This vaiṣṇava-smṛti-grantha was finished in twenty chapters, known as vilāsas." Prabhupada then summarises each: guru and disciple and mantras (1); initiation (2); Vaisnava behaviour, cleanliness, remembrance, chanting the mantras (3); samskara, tilaka, mudra, mala, guru-puja (4); the sitting place, breathing, meditation, salagrama worship (5); inviting and bathing the Lord (6); collecting flowers (7); the Deity, incense, lamps, offerings, music, garlands, prayers, counteracting offences (8); tulasi, oblations to forefathers, offering food (9); the devotees (10); Deity worship and the glories of the holy name, offences in chanting and their relief, surrender (11); Ekadasi (12); fasting and Maha-dvadasi (13); monthly duties (14); Ekadasi without water, branding, Caturmasya, Janmastami and other festivals (15); Karttika, Govardhana-puja, Ratha-yatra (16); preparations for worship, the maha-mantra and japa (17); the forms of Visnu (18); establishing the Deity and bathing before installation (19); "the construction of temples, referring to those constructed by the great devotees" (20).
- **Purport point (the compilation):** "According to Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura, the regulative principles of devotional service compiled by Gopāla Bhaṭṭa Gosvāmī do not strictly follow our Vaiṣṇava principles. Actually, Gopāla Bhaṭṭa Gosvāmī collected only a summary of the elaborate descriptions of Vaiṣṇava regulative principles from the Hari-bhakti-vilāsa. It is Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Gosvāmī's opinion, however, that to follow the Hari-bhakti-vilāsa strictly is to actually follow the Vaiṣṇava rituals in perfect order." The smarta-samaja influenced portions of the compilation; "It is better to consult the commentary made by Sanātana Gosvāmī himself for the Hari-bhakti-vilāsa under the name of Dig-darśinī-ṭīkā."
- **Application (preacher's):** this is Prabhupada's own account of the book and its limits; give it as he gives it, including Bhaktisiddhanta's caution.
- **Use:** in the talk (segments 2 and 7).

**CC Madhya 15.108 (with purport)** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/15/108/
> "One does not have to undergo initiation or execute the activities required before initiation. One simply has to vibrate the holy name with his lips. Thus even a man in the lowest class [caṇḍāla] can be delivered."
- **Context:** Lord Caitanya to Haridasa Thakura, on the glory of the holy name.
- **Purport point:** Prabhupada defines diksa from the Bhakti-sandarbha (283) and then: "The regulative principles of dīkṣā are explained in the Hari-bhakti-vilāsa (2.3-4) and the Bhakti-sandarbha (283)." He quotes HBV 2.6 from the Visnu-yamala: "Unless one is initiated by a bona fide spiritual master, all his devotional activities are useless. A person who is not properly initiated can descend again into the animal species." And HBV 2.10: "It is the duty of every human being to surrender to a bona fide spiritual master. Giving him everything — body, mind and intelligence — one must take Vaiṣṇava initiation from him." And: "According to the Vaiṣṇava regulative principles, one must be initiated as a brāhmaṇa."
- **Application (preacher's):** the verse says the holy name delivers even without initiation; the purport gives the smrti's rule that one must be
  initiated. Both are Prabhupada's; teach both, in his order: the name's power first, then the duty.
- **Also in:** trd-08 uses this verse for chanting.
- **Use:** in the talk (segment 5).

**CC Madhya 23.105 (purport)** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/madhya/23/105/
- **Teaching:** the Lord taught Sanatana proper renunciation and "forbade dry renunciation and speculative knowledge in all respects."
- **Purport point:** "To broadcast the cult of Kṛṣṇa consciousness, one has to learn the possibility of renunciation in terms of country, time and candidate." "The teacher ( ācārya ) has to consider time, candidate and country. He must avoid the principle of niyamāgraha — that is, he should not try to perform the impossible." "The essence of devotional service must be taken into consideration, and not the outward paraphernalia. **CC Antya 6.223 (purport)** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/6/223/

- **Context:** Lord Caitanya's instructions to Raghunatha dasa Gosvami.
- **Purport point:** "As stated in the Hari-bhakti-vilāsa at the end of the twentieth vilāsa (366, 379, 382)": the rules written are mostly for

Then the Bhakti-sandarbha (283): "It is Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam's opinion that the process of Deity worship is not actually necessary".
- **Application (preacher's):** the smrti itself says for whom its rules are written. A resident who lives by the holy name is in the book's
  own exception, not outside the book.
- **Use:** in the talk (segment 7).

**NOD 6; NOD 8** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/nod/6/

**BG 16.23-24** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/16/24/
> 16.23: "He who discards scriptural injunctions and acts according to his own whims attains neither perfection, nor happiness, nor the supreme destination."
> 16.24: "One should therefore understand what is duty and what is not duty by the regulations of the scriptures. Knowing such rules and regulations, one should act so that he may gradually be elevated."
- **Purport point (16.23):** "A person who knowingly violates the rules acts in lust. He knows that this is forbidden, but still he acts. This is called acting whimsically."
- **Purport point (16.24):** sastra is free of the four defects, "imperfect senses, the propensity for cheating, certainty of committing mistakes, and certainty of being illusioned", and "Without following the principles of the scriptures, one cannot elevate himself to the perfectional stage." ## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** A devotee acts by scripture, not by whim (BG 16.23-24). Scripture is trustworthy because it is free of the four defects
   that disqualify any conditioned soul from making rules (16.24 purport). For Vaisnavas the scripture of conduct is the smrti, and Lord Caitanya
   Himself commissioned the Vaisnava smrti and dictated its outline (CC Madhya 24.329-345).
2. **What the book is.** Twenty vilasas, from the guru-disciple relationship and initiation through daily conduct, Deity worship, the holy name,
   Ekadasi and festivals, to the forms of Visnu, Deity installation and temple construction (CC Madhya 1.35 purport). It rests on Puranic
   evidence for every point (24.343). The Lord's own synopsis runs from "take shelter of a bona fide spiritual master" (24.329) to "regulations
   and etiquette" (24.344).
3. **Why the Lord wanted it.** Sanatana, called lowborn by himself, asked the Lord to manifest in his heart (24.326-327). The Lord's reply, and
   Prabhupada's purport, give the principle of all Vaisnava writing: "The blessings of the authorities are one's power of attorney" (24.345 purport).
   The standards of a temple are not an institution's rules but the Lord's direction received through Sanatana Gosvami.
4. **The rule within the rules.** The nine processes (SB 7.5.23-24) are the frame; arcana with sixteen ingredients is one of them. Rupa Gosvami took
   the Hari-bhakti-vilasa's "basic principles, not details" into the sixty-four items (NOD 6). Prabhupada's purport to 24.336: "If a devotee very strictly follows the methods of Deity worship, he will naturally and quickly become a pure Vaiṣṇava." The detail serves the devotion.
5. **How Prabhupada used it.** He quoted it where it establishes a Vaisnava standard: initiation is necessary and makes a brahmana of anyone
   (15.108 and 23.105 purports: HBV 2.6, 2.10, 2.12); women and sudras who are chaste and sincere may receive the pancaratrika mantras (24.331
   purport: HBV 1.194); the renounced live by the holy name (Antya 6.223 purport: HBV 20.366-382). He also quoted it when he wished to limit a rule:
   the preference for a brahmana initiator "is meant for those who are overly dependent on the mundane social order" (8.128 purport).
6. **The limits he drew.** (a) The holy name delivers even the uninitiated (15.108); the smrti's rule of initiation stands beside that, not above it.
   (b) The acharya "has to consider time, candidate and country" and avoid niyamagraha, "he should not try to perform the impossible"; "The essence
   of devotional service must be taken into consideration, and not the outward paraphernalia"; the book "was specifically meant for India" in the
   age of smarta-vidhi (23.105 purport). (c) Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati's caution: Gopala Bhatta's compilation was influenced by the smarta-samaja;
   consult Sanatana's own Dig-darsini-tika (1.35 purport). (d) The book itself says its rules are mostly for wealthy householders, and the
   renounced "should have no occupation other than chanting the holy name" (Antya 6.223 purport). (e) The Bhagavatam's own position: Deity worship
   is not absolutely necessary; surrender by any limb gives success (Bhakti-sandarbha 283 in the same purport).
7. **Neither rigidity nor laxity.** The two errors a resident meets are the same error facing two ways: treating a detail as the essence (niyamagraha,
   23.105 purport) or dropping the standard because "the essence is what matters". Prabhupada's practice was both: he gave his disciples the
   Hari-bhakti-vilasa's standards of initiation, tilaka, Ekadasi and arcana, and he adjusted details for the world. The resident does the same: he
   keeps the standard and asks the acharya's question about any adjustment.
8. **Misunderstandings.**
   - *The rules are brahminical culture, not bhakti.* The Lord dictated them as "Vaiṣṇava regulative principles" (24.345); the Hari-bhakti-vilasa makes a brahmana of anyone by initiation (23.105 purport).
   - *If the holy name is enough, the rules are optional.* Prabhupada puts both in one purport (15.108): the name's power and the duty of initiation.
   - *Every rule in the compilation is binding everywhere.* Bhaktisiddhanta's caution and the book's own words about householders say otherwise (1.35; Antya 6.223 purports).
   - *Adjustment means I decide.* The acharya adjusts (23.105 purport); the resident follows his authority and asks.
9. **Pitch for TRD.** Read the Lord's synopsis aloud; give the twenty vilasas as Prabhupada lists them; quote only what he quotes; and spend real time
   on the limits, which are also his words.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet the devotees and say that today's subject is the book behind the standards they keep every day.
2. Say: "Every one of us put on tilaka this morning in twelve places and will fast on Ekadasi next week. Today we learn who ordered these things and why."
3. Ask H.2 question 1; take three answers and write the rules named on the board.
4. Ask H.2 question 2 for private thought.
5. Say that Lord Caitanya Himself dictated the outline of the Vaisnava smrti to Sanatana Gosvami, and begin with BG 16.23.

**H.2 Opening questions**
1. Name one rule of temple life you keep without knowing where it is written.
2. (Private) Is there a rule you keep only because others are watching, or one you have quietly dropped?
3. What is the difference between a custom and a scriptural regulation?

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Scripture, not whim.** BG 16.23-24: one who discards the injunctions attains neither perfection nor happiness; sastra is free of the four defects.
   *Transition:* which scripture governs a Vaisnava's conduct?
2. **The Lord ordered the smrti.** Sanatana asked; the Lord promised Krishna's help and dictated the outline (CC Madhya 24.325-345). *Transition:* what is in it?
3. **From guru to temple.** The synopsis (24.329-344) and the twenty vilasas (1.35 purport). *Transition:* what did Rupa Gosvami take from it?
4. **Principles, not details.** NOD 6 (the sixty-four items) and NOD 8 (the thirty-two offences). *Transition:* and Prabhupada?
5. **Prabhupada quoted it for the standard.** Initiation makes a brahmana of anyone (HBV 2.12); the sincere of any birth may receive the mantra (1.194);
   the renounced live by the name (20.366-382). *Transition:* and he drew the limits.
6. **The acharya considers time, place and candidate.** 23.105 purport; 8.128 purport; Bhaktisiddhanta's caution (1.35 purport); the name delivers all (15.108).
   *Transition:* so how does a resident hold the rules?

## J. Stories and illustrations

**1. Sanatana Gosvami receives the order (CC Madhya 24.325-328, 24.345).** Tell it as the verses tell it. At the end of the Lord's teaching at
Varanasi, Sanatana says, "I am a most lowborn person. I have no knowledge of good behavior. How is it possible for me to write authorized directions about Vaiṣṇava activities?" (24.325). He asks the Lord to tell him personally how to write "this difficult book about Vaiṣṇava behavior" and to manifest Himself in his heart (24.326); if the Lord directs him from within, "although I am lowborn, I may hope to be able to write it" (24.327). The Lord replies that whatever he wants to do he will do correctly by Krishna's favour: "He will manifest the real purport" (24.328). Then the Lord gives the synopsis, and ends: "When you write on this subject, Kṛṣṇa will help you by spiritually awakening you" (24.345). *Lesson:* the Vaisnava smrti was written by a devotee who felt unfit, under the Lord's direct order and with His promise; its authority is the Lord's.

**2. The book's own exception (CC Antya 6.223 and purport).** When the Lord instructed Raghunatha dasa Gosvami to always chant the holy name and live on alms, Prabhupada's purport turns to the end of the Hari-bhakti-vilasa's twentieth vilasa: the rules written are mostly for wealthy householders; the renounced who have surrendered everything "should have no occupation other than chanting the holy name of the Lord." *Lesson:* the smrti knows its own scope; a resident who lives by the name fulfils the book, not escapes it.

**3. The candala and the name (CC Madhya 15.108 and purport).** The Lord tells Haridasa Thakura that one need not undergo initiation; vibrating the holy name delivers even a candala. In the same breath Prabhupada quotes the Hari-bhakti-vilasa: without initiation one's devotional activities are useless and one may fall to the animal species (HBV 2.6). *Lesson:* both are true, in Prabhupada's order: the name's power is unlimited, and the duty of initiation is the Vaisnava's regulative principle. A preacher gives both.

**Illustration (writer's, not scriptural):** a hospital has protocols for every hour of a patient's day. A nurse who knows only the protocol becomes a machine; one who knows the healing behind it keeps the protocol with care and asks the doctor before changing anything. The acharya is the doctor (CC Madhya 23.105 purport).

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"If the holy name delivers without initiation (CC Madhya 15.108), why insist on rules?"** Because Prabhupada insists in the same purport, quoting
   the Hari-bhakti-vilasa (2.6, 2.10): the name's power and the Vaisnava's duty stand together. The rule helps the chanter chant purely.
2. **"Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati said the compilation does not strictly follow our principles. So can we ignore it?"** He said both things: that
   Gopala Bhatta's summary was influenced by the smarta-samaja, and that "to follow the Hari-bhakti-vilāsa strictly is to actually follow the Vaiṣṇava rituals in perfect order"; consult Sanatana's own commentary (CC Madhya 1.35 purport). The answer is care, not neglect.
3. **"Prabhupada changed details (women on the altar, Western dress). Doesn't that break the smrti?"** He applied the smrti's own principle: the acharya
   considers time, candidate and country and keeps the essence (CC Madhya 23.105 purport). The adjustment is the acharya's, not ours.
4. **"Is the Hari-bhakti-vilasa only for pujaris?"** Its first vilasas are about guru, disciple, initiation, daily conduct, cleanliness, tilaka and the
   holy name (1.35 purport; 24.329-333): every resident's morning.
5. **"The book says take initiation from a brahmana if one is available. Our gurus are not born brahmanas."** Prabhupada: that rule "is meant for those
   who are overly dependent on the mundane social order" (CC Madhya 8.128 purport); and the same book says initiation makes a brahmana (HBV 2.12, in 23.105 purport).
6. **"Why can we not read the book itself?"** Our copy is unreadable (font-scrambled). Until a readable edition is checked, quote only what Prabhupada quotes, with his numbers.

## L. Case studies

**Case 1 (Invented case for discussion).** A new resident is told "we fast from grains on Ekadasi" and asks why. The senior answers, "it is our tradition."
*Dilemma:* is that answer enough? *Principles:* CC Madhya 24.340-342 (the Lord Himself orders Ekadasi described, with its fault if neglected); 1.35 purport
(twelfth, thirteenth and fifteenth vilasas). *Questions:* What changes when the resident knows the Lord ordered it? *Analysis:* "tradition" invites
negotiation; "the Lord told Sanatana to write it, and the Hari-bhakti-vilasa gives three vilasas to it" invites devotion. *Takeaway:* teach the source, not the custom.

**Case 2 (Invented case for discussion).** A preacher in a country where tilaka draws hostility asks whether to stop wearing it on the street. *Dilemma:* who
decides, and on what basis? *Principles:* CC Madhya 24.332 (tilaka in the Lord's synopsis); CC Madhya 23.105 purport (the acharya considers time, candidate
and country; essence over paraphernalia; avoid the impossible). *Questions:* Is this an adjustment the preacher may make himself? *Analysis:* the standard
is the Lord's; the adjustment is the acharya's. He asks his authority and follows the answer; he does not quietly decide. *Takeaway:* adjustment is
received from above, never taken from below.

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

The Bhagavad-gita says that one who discards the scriptures' injunctions and acts by his own whims "attains neither perfection, nor happiness, nor
the supreme destination" (BG 16.23), and that one should learn duty from the scriptures and act so as to be elevated (BG 16.24). Scripture is trusted
because it is free of the four defects every conditioned soul has (16.24 purport). For Vaisnavas the scripture of daily conduct is the Hari-bhakti-vilasa,
the Vaisnava smrti.

It was written at Lord Caitanya's order. At Varanasi, Sanatana Gosvami, calling himself lowborn and ignorant of good behaviour, asked the Lord how he
could write such a book and begged Him to manifest in his heart (CC Madhya 24.325-327). The Lord promised Krishna's help and then gave a synopsis
(24.328-345): take shelter of a bona fide guru; the marks of guru and disciple; Krishna as the object of worship and the mantras; initiation; the morning
duties, bathing, prayers, service to the guru, tilaka in twelve places, neck beads, tulasi, cleanliness, the temple bell; Deity worship with food offered
at least five times a day and arati; the holy name and its offences; prasadam; the devotees' association and hearing the Bhagavatam; Ekadasi and the
festivals; and everything proved from the Puranas and described "as regulations and etiquette" (24.344). "When you write on this subject, Kṛṣṇa will help
you by spiritually awakening you" (24.345).

Srila Prabhupada describes the finished book: twenty vilasas, from guru and initiation through conduct, worship, the holy name, Ekadasi and festivals to
Deity installation and temple building (CC Madhya 1.35 purport). Rupa Gosvami took its basic principles into the sixty-four items of devotional service
(NOD 6), and the thirty-two offences in Deity service are listed in NOD 8. Prabhupada quotes the book for the standard: initiation is necessary and makes a
brahmana of anyone (HBV 2.6, 2.10, 2.12 in the 15.108 and 23.105 purports); chaste and sincere women and sudras may receive the mantras (HBV 1.194 in the
24.331 purport); the renounced live by the holy name (HBV 20.366-382 in the Antya 6.223 purport). He also gives the limits: the acharya "has to consider time,
candidate and country", keeps the essence over the paraphernalia and avoids the impossible (23.105 purport); some rules are for those dependent on the
social order (8.128 purport); Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati cautioned that the compilation needs care (1.35 purport); and the holy name delivers even the
uninitiated (15.108). So a resident keeps the standards as the Lord's direction and leaves adjustments to the acharya.

## S2. Key ideas
- Act by scripture, not whim; scripture is free of the four defects (BG 16.23-24).
- Lord Caitanya ordered the Vaisnava smrti and dictated its outline; Sanatana Gosvami wrote it (CC Madhya 24.329-345).
- Twenty vilasas cover guru, initiation, conduct, worship, the holy name, Ekadasi, festivals and temples (CC Madhya 1.35 purport).
- Prabhupada quotes the book for the standard and also states its limits (CC Madhya 15.108, 23.105, 8.128, Antya 6.223 purports).
- The acharya adjusts to time, place and candidate; the resident keeps the standard and asks.

## S3. Verses to remember
- **BG 16.23:** "He who discards scriptural injunctions and acts according to his own whims attains neither perfection, nor happiness, nor the supreme destination." For me: my standards are not my preferences.
- **CC Madhya 24.329:** "In the beginning describe how one must take shelter of a bona fide spiritual master." For me: every rule begins with my guru.
- **CC Madhya 24.345:** "When you write on this subject, Kṛṣṇa will help you by spiritually awakening you." For me: the rules I keep came with Krishna's help.

## S4. Words to know
- **Smrti:** scripture of conduct and ritual; the Hari-bhakti-vilasa is the Vaisnava smrti.
- **Vilasa:** a chapter of the Hari-bhakti-vilasa; there are twenty.
- **Diksa:** initiation, which awakens transcendental knowledge and destroys sin (CC Madhya 15.108 purport).
- **Arcana:** Deity worship, one of the nine processes (SB 7.5.23-24).
- **Niyamagraha:** clinging to rules while missing the purpose, or trying to perform the impossible (CC Madhya 23.105 purport).
- **Dig-darsini-tika:** Sanatana Gosvami's own commentary on the Hari-bhakti-vilasa (CC Madhya 1.35 purport).

## S5. Questions and answers
1. **Are temple rules just tradition?** No. Lord Caitanya dictated their outline and Sanatana Gosvami wrote them at His order (CC Madhya 24.329-345).
2. **If chanting alone delivers, why initiation and rules?** Prabhupada teaches both in one purport: the name's power and the duty of initiation (CC Madhya 15.108 and purport).
3. **Can a non-brahmana be initiated and worship the Deity?** Yes; initiation makes a brahmana of anyone, as the Hari-bhakti-vilasa itself says (HBV 2.12 in the 23.105 purport).
4. **Did Prabhupada change the rules?** He applied the acharya's principle of time, place and candidate while keeping the essence (CC Madhya 23.105 purport). That adjustment is the acharya's, not ours.
5. **Can I read the Hari-bhakti-vilasa?** Our copy is unreadable; quote only what Prabhupada quotes until a readable edition is checked.
