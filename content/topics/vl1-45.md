# Unity is the strength

> **VL1 · 45–60 min · review: sources checked, awaiting senior review.** Scripture light but exact. IYF holds one deck (six slides) and three
> recordings (Govardhan, 1 Oct 2013). The deck's flow is kept: basis of unity → why we fail to unite → what
> keeps integrity intact. Its historical examples (Jinnah, Maharana Pratap and Shakti Singh, Haldighati) and
> the Dadhici/Vrtrasura episode are left out or flagged (see J). Not a sensitive topic, but the deck's
> political examples are not used, so that no community in the room is characterised.

## A. Topic overview

| | |
|---|---|
| Batch / level | VL1 (Vedic Level 1), a stand-alone talk |
| Suggested duration | 45–60 min (G gives a 30-minute cut) |
| Preparation time | About 90 min, the first time |
| Difficulty | Introductory |
| Prerequisites | None (VL1-44 "Power of association" gives the raw material: company) |
| Audience | Students and young professionals; group projects, hostel committees, start-up teams, families, friend circles that fell apart over "petty things" |
| Teaching objective | Show that unity is not sameness or compromise but a shared goal above everyone's ego; name the three things that break teams (envy, position-hunting, politics) and the two that hold them (a common centre, loving exchange); give one practice for the week |

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on vl1-44: that talk shows how company shapes the mind; this one asks what holds a group together once it exists. Do not repeat vl1-44's stories (Mrgari, Dhruva, Bharata); refer back to them in one line.

Everyone in the room has been in a group that worked and a group that collapsed, and they usually blame
personalities. The tradition offers something sharper: groups hold when their members serve one goal above
themselves and break when "petty things" (IYF's phrase) become the goal. The Bhagavatam gives a clean case, the
ten Pracetas, whom the Lord praises for being "engaged in one occupation" (SB 4.30.8), and the purport names
the United Nations as the counter-example. At VL1 the talk stays with everyday teams and asks one thing: find
the centre.

How the subject grows at other levels:

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **VL1** (here) | Unity as a shared centre; what breaks teams; what holds them; one practice |
| VL2 | Why a *spiritual* centre unites where material goals cannot (BG 5.29; the root-and-branches logic of SB 4.31.14) |
| SPS | The six loving exchanges (NOI 4) as the life of a devotee community; disagreement over service as "spiritual" (SB 4.30.8 purport) |
| DPS | Leading a team: *puja, labha, pratistha* (worship, gain, prestige) as the leader's own temptation |
| BSS | Cooperation in an ashrama; chastisement from the guru (the deck's last slide) |

**Not done here:**
- Historical and political examples from the deck (partition-era leaders, Rajput history). A VL1 room is mixed;
  no community is to be characterised, and the examples are unsourced.
- The Dadhici and Vrtrasura episode (SB Canto 6): advanced, and its point ("Does Krsna do politics?") is a
  DPS discussion.
- Guru-tattva (BG 4.34) beyond one line: "a team needs someone who defines the goal".

## C. Central message

A group is strong exactly as far as its members serve something higher than themselves together. Unity is not
everyone agreeing or everyone being alike; it is everyone facing the same centre. Envy, position-hunting and
politics are what happens when the centre is lost.

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. Define unity as a shared centre, not sameness or compromise, using SB 4.30.8.
2. Explain the root-and-branches logic (SB 4.31.14): why serving the centre serves every part.
3. Name three things that break groups (envy, desire for position, politics) and recognise one in a group they
   belong to.
4. Describe the six exchanges of NOI 4 as practical ways a team builds trust.
5. Choose one practice (give, receive, confide, ask, share) to try in one of their groups this week.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** SB 4.30.8 and 4.31.14 carry the talk; NOI 4 gives the practice; CC Antya 20.29 names what disunites.
The rest prepare you for questions.

**SB 4.30.8**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/30/8/
> "My dear sons of the King, I am very much pleased by the friendly relationships among you. All of you are engaged in one occupation — devotional service. I am so pleased with your mutual friendship that I wish you all good fortune."
- **Context:** The ten sons of King Pracinabarhi (the Pracetas) practised austerities together for ten thousand
  years; the Lord appears and speaks first of their unity.
- **Teaching:** The Lord is pleased by friendship among those who serve one goal together.
- **Purport point:** "The unity of the individual souls attempting to satisfy the Supreme Lord or rendering
  service to the Lord is real unity. In the material world such unity is not possible. Even though people may
  officially unite, they all have different interests. In the United Nations, for instance, all the nations have
  their particular national ambitions, and consequently they cannot be united." And: "If there is some
  disagreement over service, such disagreement is to be taken as spiritual."
- **Application (preacher's):** Note that the Lord praises their friendship *before* granting anything: the
  unity itself is the achievement. Note too that the purport does not expect devotees to have no disagreements;
  it tells them what kind of disagreement is healthy.
- **Use:** in the talk (the spine).

**SB 4.31.14**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/4/31/14/
> "As pouring water on the root of a tree energizes the trunk, branches, twigs and everything else, and as supplying food to the stomach enlivens the senses and limbs of the body, simply worshiping the Supreme Personality of Godhead through devotional service automatically satisfies the demigods, who are parts of that Supreme Personality."
- **Context:** Narada instructs the Pracetas.
- **Teaching:** As watering the root nourishes the whole tree and feeding the stomach the whole body,
  worshipping the Supreme Lord through devotional service satisfies the demigods, who are His parts.
- **Purport point:** "If one tries to feed his eyes or ears independently, the result is only havoc. Simply by
  supplying food to the stomach, we satisfy all of the senses."
- **Application (preacher's):** A team whose members each feed their own "limb" (credit, comfort, visibility)
  produces havoc; a team that feeds the shared purpose finds every member nourished. The verse is about God; the
  team is the analogy, and I say so.
- **Use:** in the talk (the picture).

**BG 5.29**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/5/29/
> "A person in full consciousness of Me, knowing Me to be the ultimate beneficiary of all sacrifices and austerities, the Supreme Lord of all planets and demigods, and the benefactor and well-wisher of all living entities, attains peace…"
- **Teaching:** Peace follows from knowing who the real beneficiary and well-wisher of everyone is.
- **Purport point:** "Unless one understands these bare facts, it is not possible to achieve peace in the world
  either individually or collectively."
- **Application (preacher's):** IYF's slide uses this verse for "Krsna is the well-wisher of all". At VL1 use it
  as the reason a spiritual centre can unite people who have nothing else in common: it is a centre that wishes
  everyone well.
- **Use:** in the talk, one line.

**NOI 4**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/4/
> "Offering gifts in charity, accepting charitable gifts, revealing one's mind in confidence, inquiring confidentially, accepting prasāda and offering prasāda are the six symptoms of love shared by one devotee and another."
- **Context:** Rupa Gosvami, having listed what spoils and what favours devotional service (texts 2–3),
  describes how devotees deal with one another.
- **Teaching:** Love between devotees shows in six exchanges: give, receive, confide, inquire, accept food,
  offer food.
- **Purport point:** "Even in ordinary social activities, these six types of dealings between two loving friends
  are absolutely necessary." The purport adds: "An experienced devotee explains, and an inexperienced devotee
  learns from him."
- **Application (preacher's):** This is the page's practice list. The purport itself extends the six exchanges
  to ordinary friendship, so at VL1 they can be offered as "how trust is built in any team".
- **Use:** in the talk (the practice).

**BG 4.34**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/4/34/
> "Just try to learn the truth by approaching a spiritual master. Inquire from him submissively and render service unto him…"
- **Teaching:** Truth is learnt by approaching one who has seen it, with inquiry and service.
- **Purport point:** "In this verse, both blind following and absurd inquiries are condemned."
- **Application (preacher's):** IYF's deck quotes this on its last slide ("Goal is defined by Guru Parampara").
  At VL1 one line: every united team has someone who defines the goal, and the tradition is careful about who
  that is. Do not teach guru-tattva here.
- **Use:** background; one line in the talk.

**CC Antya 20.29**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/20/29/
> "O Lord of the universe, I do not desire material wealth, materialistic followers, a beautiful wife or fruitive activities described in flowery language. All I want, life after life, is unmotivated devotional service to You."
- **Context:** The fourth verse of Sri Caitanya Mahaprabhu's *Siksastaka*.
- **Teaching:** The devotee desires neither wealth, followers, a beautiful wife nor fruitive activities
  described in flowery language, only unmotivated devotional service, life after life.
- **Application (preacher's):** IYF's slide reads this as "four things will disunite us". That is an
  application, not the verse's statement. Used as such: when a team member starts wanting the money, the
  following or the show for himself, the centre is lost.
- **Use:** in the talk (what disunites), labelled as application.

**CC Antya 20.32**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/cc/antya/20/32/
> "O My Lord, O Kṛṣṇa, son of Mahārāja Nanda, I am Your eternal servant, but because of My own fruitive acts I have fallen into this horrible ocean of nescience. Now please be causelessly merciful to Me. Consider Me a particle of dust at Your lotus feet."
- **Context:** The fifth verse of the *Siksastaka*; IYF's slide quotes its opening (*ayi nanda-tanuja*).
- **Teaching:** The mood of the servant: a particle of dust, asking for mercy.
- **Application (preacher's):** The deck pairs it with "Simplicity is required. Sincerity is required. Pray."
  At VL1 use only the image: a team of people who each think of themselves as dust at the feet of the goal has
  nothing to fight over.
- **Use:** in the talk, one line.

**BG 10.9**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/10/9/
> "…they derive great satisfaction and bliss from always enlightening one another and conversing about Me."
- **Teaching:** Devotees find joy in enlightening one another.
- **Application (preacher's):** The positive picture of a united group: not uniformity but mutual enlightening.
  Links back to VL1-44.
- **Use:** in the talk, one line.

**BG 3.21**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/3/21/
> "Whatever action a great man performs, common men follow. And whatever standards he sets by exemplary acts, all the world pursues."
- **Teaching:** Leaders set the standard others follow.
- **Application (preacher's):** IYF's slide: "If a common man envies, it doesn't create much disturbance. If a
  leader is envious, it can create havoc." This verse is the scriptural ground for that observation; the
  historical example on the slide is not used.
- **Use:** in the talk, one line (for K and Case 2).

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** Real unity is many individuals serving one centre (SB 4.30.8 purport: "real unity").
   Each Praceta remained an individual; they were united in whom they served. When the centre is served, every
   part is nourished (SB 4.31.14); when the parts feed themselves, the result is "havoc". The centre that can
   unite strangers is one that is the well-wisher of all (BG 5.29).
2. **Why it matters.** Teams, families and friend groups break over "petty things" (IYF) because, without a
   centre above the members, each member becomes his own centre. The tradition does not ask people to be less
   individual; it asks them to find what they all face.
3. **Connections.** Grows out of VL1-44: association gives you people; a centre makes them a team. Envy (VL1-20
   on greed; VL1-17 on anger) is the emotion that breaks the centre. At SPS/DPS the six exchanges (NOI 4) become
   the daily life of a devotee community, and disagreement about *how* to serve is treated as healthy (SB 4.30.8
   purport).
4. **Consequences.** Someone who understands this stops asking "why can't these people get along?" and asks
   "what are we actually serving, each of us?" They practise one of the six exchanges, usually confiding or
   asking, and notice trust rise.
5. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - **"Unity means we all agree."** The purport expects disagreement; it distinguishes disagreement *over the
     service* (healthy) from disunity *over interests* (SB 4.30.8 purport).
   - **"Unity means compromise: everyone gives up a bit."** The United Nations is the purport's example of
     exactly that, and of why it fails. Unity is not meeting in the middle; it is all facing the same way.
   - **"Strong leaders create unity."** A leader sets the standard (BG 3.21), for good or ill; an envious leader
     creates havoc (IYF's observation). Unity comes from the centre, which the leader must also serve.
   - **"Spiritual people are automatically united."** The purport says plainly that even in a society of Krsna
     consciousness members "sometimes appear disunited due to their having different opinions and leaning toward
     material things". Honesty here wins trust.
6. **Pitch for VL1.** Say "centre" and "goal"; say "envy", "position" and "politics" in plain English (IYF's
   *puja, labha, pratistha* can be mentioned once as "worship, gain, prestige"). No history, no current affairs.
   The Pracetas and the tree do the work.

**Alternative structure (case-first):** open with Case 1 (L), let the group name what went wrong, then introduce

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet, say your name, and say the talk is about teams of any size: friends, family, a work crew.
2. Write "Unity is the strength" on the board and ask someone to say what "unity" means to them.
3. Ask H.2 question 1 as private recall; no names.
4. Take one or two volunteers who say only what the group was for, not who was to blame.
5. Say the transition line below and go to the ten brothers.

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the room's own life)
1. Think of a group that fell apart. Did it fall apart over too many differences, or because it lost a common aim?
2. Think of a group that worked well (a family harvest, a football side, a shift crew, a project team). What did everyone in it care about more than themselves?
3. When you are in a team, what is your first reaction when someone else gets the credit?

- **Opening question:** "Think of a group you were part of that fell apart: a project team, a band, a friend
  circle, a committee. Don't say names. Just answer privately: did it fall apart because you had too many
  differences, or because you stopped having something in common?" Pause. "Most groups die of the second."
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** Four friends start a college fest committee. In week one they are a team.
  By week four, two are fighting over whose name goes first on the poster, one has stopped replying, and the
  fourth is doing all the work and resenting it. Nothing external changed. What did?
- **Transition:** "The Bhagavatam describes ten brothers who stayed united for ten thousand years, and it tells
  us exactly why."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** Their careers and friendships will be decided less by talent than by
  their ability to stay in a team without becoming its centre.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Unity is one centre, not one opinion.** SB 4.30.8 and purport: the Pracetas were individuals "engaged in
   one occupation"; the United Nations unites officially and fails because each member keeps its own ambition.
   *Example:* the fest committee had four centres by week four. *Transition:* why does a centre work?
2. **Serve the root and every branch is fed.** SB 4.31.14. *Example:* a team that feeds its purpose finds every
   member nourished; a team where each feeds his own limb produces "havoc". *Transition:* what kind of centre
   can unite strangers?
3. **A centre that wishes everyone well.** BG 5.29: the Lord as "benefactor and well-wisher of all living
   entities". *Example:* the Pracetas' centre had no favourites. *Transition:* so what breaks groups?
4. **Envy, position, politics.** IYF's diagnosis; BG 3.21 on why a leader's envy spreads; CC Antya 20.29
   (deeper verse; the talk uses only its plain meaning, as application) on wanting the wealth, the followers and the show. *Example:* "whose name goes first".
   *Transition:* and what holds them?
5. **Six exchanges build trust.** NOI 4: give, receive, confide, inquire, accept food, offer food; the purport
   calls these "absolutely necessary" between any two loving friends. *Example:* the team that eats together
   and confides survives the poster argument. *Transition:* to one practice.

## J. Stories and illustrations

- **The Pracetas** (SB Canto 4, chapters 24–31; verified verses 4.30.8 and 4.31.14). Ten brothers, sent by
  their father to perform austerities, worship Lord Visnu together for ten thousand years under the water. When
  the Lord appears, His first words praise their mutual friendship and their single occupation (4.30.8). Later
  Narada instructs them with the root-and-branches example (4.31.14). *Connection:* unity as a shared centre.
  Tell it from the text; do not add dialogue.
- **The United Nations** (SB 4.30.8 purport): Srila Prabhupada's own example of official unity without a common
  centre. *Connection:* compromise is not unity. Say it as his example, not as a current-affairs comment.
- **Left out from IYF's deck, and why:**
  - Jinnah, Maharana Pratap and Shakti Singh, Haldighati: historical claims without sources, and a mixed VL1
    room should hear no community characterised. *Requires source review* if a preacher wants them elsewhere.
  - Srngi cursing Pariksit (SB 1.18): the deck's point is "one should have *adhikara* for a post". True but
    advanced; the story is better told in its own right at VL3+.
  - Dadhici and Vrtrasura (SB Canto 6): "Does Krsna do politics?" is a DPS discussion.
  - Hiranyakasipu as "political mentality": SB Canto 7 is used at VL1-48; here it would crowd the talk.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"Unity sounds like conformity. I don't want to lose my individuality."**
   - Each Praceta stayed an individual soul (SB 4.30.8 purport). Unity in the tradition is not being alike; it
     is facing the same centre. The purport even expects disagreement, as long as it is about *how* to serve.
2. **"Every group thinks its goal is the 'higher centre'. Gangs are united too."**
   - Fair. The test is BG 5.29: is the centre the well-wisher of *all* living entities, or of us against them?
     A centre that excludes is just a bigger ego. The Pracetas' goal had no enemies.
3. **"My team's problem is one toxic person, not a missing centre."**
   - Sometimes. BG 3.21 says a leader's example spreads, and IYF's note that an envious leader "can create
     havoc" is sound. But ask what that person is serving and what the rest of you are serving; a team with a
     centre survives a difficult member, a team without one is destroyed by him.
4. **"Can a non-religious team use this?"**
   - Yes, up to a point. Any shared purpose above the members unites them; the purport of NOI 4 says the six
     exchanges are necessary "even in ordinary social activities". The tradition's further claim, that only a
     spiritual centre unites *permanently*, is for VL2; here offer the general principle and invite the test.
5. **"Why do religious groups fight so much, then?"**
   - Because the centre is lost there too. The purport admits that even in a society of Krsna consciousness
     members sometimes appear disunited "due to their having different opinions and leaning toward material
     things" (SB 4.30.8). The teaching is a standard, not a boast.
6. **"Isn't 'confiding' and 'inquiring' (NOI 4) risky in a competitive team?"**
   - It is, which is why trust is built in steps: start by giving and sharing food, which cost little, and
     confide as trust grows. The purport's own example, two businessmen, begins with a shared meal.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The fest committee.** Four friends; by week four two fight over the poster credit, one has gone quiet,
one does everything and resents it.
- **Dilemma:** Nothing external changed. What did?
- **Principles:** SB 4.30.8 (one occupation vs four interests); SB 4.31.14 (feeding limbs vs the root); NOI 4
  (which exchanges stopped?).
- **Questions:** What was the shared centre in week one? When did each member become his own centre? Which of
  the six exchanges could restart the team (a shared meal? one honest conversation)?
- **Takeaway:** Teams die of lost centres, not of differences.

**Case 2: The quiet envy.** Meera leads a student start-up. A junior member is better at pitching than she is.
She starts scheduling pitches when he is away.
- **Principles:** BG 3.21 (the leader's example spreads); CC Antya 20.29 as application (wanting the prestige
  for oneself); SB 4.30.8 purport (disagreement over service is fine; competing interests are not).
- **Questions:** What is Meera serving right now? What will the team learn from her? What would "serving the
  centre" look like in the next pitch?
- **Takeaway:** A leader's envy is the team's envy within a month.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first; praise every try)
1. Does unity mean everyone thinks alike? *No: the ten brothers stayed individuals and were united in whom they served (SB 4.30.8).*
2. Why was the Lord pleased with the Pracetas? *They were engaged in one occupation, in friendship (SB 4.30.8).*
3. What happens if each limb is fed separately (SB 4.31.14)? *Havoc; feeding the stomach feeds all the limbs.*
4. Name the three things that break a group. *Envy, hunger for position, politics.*
5. Name three of the six exchanges (NOI 4). *Any of: giving, accepting, confiding, inquiring, accepting food, offering food.*
6. According to BG 3.21, whose example do common people follow? *A great person's.*

**M.2 Activity (about 8 minutes): "The shared aim"**
1. 1 min: groups of 4 name a real or imagined group they all know (a family, a sports side, a work crew).
2. 3 min: each person writes, silently, the group's aim in one line, then they compare.
3. 3 min: discuss: did all four lines match? If not, which of envy, position or politics is the risk? Share only the group, never a person.
4. 1 min: each picks one of the six exchanges to try this week. Praise every honest answer.

**M.3 Reflection**
- **Comprehension:** What happens when you feed the limbs instead of the stomach (SB 4.31.14)?
- **Reflection:** In one group I belong to, what are we actually serving, each of us? Where have I been feeding
  my own limb?
- **Application (private):** One of the six exchanges I will try this week, with whom. *(Nobody needs to
  share.)*

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Most groups do not break because the members are too different; they break because they stop having something
in common above themselves. The Bhagavatam tells of ten brothers who worshipped the Lord together for ten
thousand years. When the Lord appeared, His first words were about their friendship: "All of you are engaged
in one occupation" (SB 4.30.8). Real unity is many individuals facing one centre. Pour water on a tree's root
and every branch is fed; feed each branch separately and you get havoc (SB 4.31.14). Groups fall apart when
members start serving themselves, through envy, hunger for position, or politics, and they hold when members
build trust by giving, receiving, confiding, asking, and sharing food (NOI 4). Unity does not mean losing your individuality: the brothers stayed individuals and were united in whom they served. A centre that unites strangers is one that wishes everyone well (BG 5.29); a centre that excludes is just a bigger ego, which is why religious groups can fight too when they lose the centre.

A leader's example is followed by others (BG 3.21), so envy at the top spreads quickly, but a team with a real centre can survive one difficult member. Any purpose above the members, not only a religious one, can unite a group, and the six exchanges of NOI 4 are needed even in ordinary social life. The question for any group is not "why can't we get along?" but "what are we each actually serving?"

## S2. Key ideas

- Unity is one centre, not one opinion (SB 4.30.8). Disagreement about *how* to serve is healthy.
- Serve the root and every part is fed; feed the parts separately and the result is havoc (SB 4.31.14).
- A centre that can unite strangers is one that wishes everyone well (BG 5.29).
- Groups break through envy, hunger for position, and politics; a leader's example spreads fastest (BG 3.21).
- Trust is built by six exchanges: give, receive, confide, inquire, accept food, offer food (NOI 4).
- A united group enlightens one another (BG 10.9); it does not make everyone the same.

## S3. Verses to remember

- **SB 4.30.8:** "I am very much pleased by the friendly relationships among you. All of you are engaged in one
  occupation…" *For me:* unity is facing the same way, not being the same.
- **SB 4.31.14:** "As pouring water on the root of a tree energizes the trunk, branches, twigs and everything
  else…" *For me:* serve the centre and I am fed too.
- **NOI 4:** giving, accepting, "revealing one's mind in confidence, inquiring confidentially", accepting and
  offering prasada. *For me:* six ways to build trust this week.

## S4. Words to know

- **Pracetas:** the ten brothers of SB Canto 4 whom the Lord praised for their unity.
- **Prasada:** food offered to the Lord and shared; in NOI 4, sharing it is a sign of love between friends.
- **Puja, labha, pratistha:** worship, gain, prestige: the three cravings that turn a team member into his own
  centre.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Does unity mean I lose my individuality?**
   No. Each of the ten brothers stayed an individual; they were united in whom they served (SB 4.30.8 purport).
2. **Gangs are united too. What's the difference?**
   The test is the centre: does it wish *all* living beings well (BG 5.29), or us against them? A centre that
   excludes is just a bigger ego.
3. **My team has one difficult person. Isn't that the problem?**
   Partly; a leader's example spreads (BG 3.21). But a team with a real centre survives a difficult member; a
   team without one is destroyed by him.
4. **Can a non-religious team use this?**
   Yes: any purpose above the members unites them, and the six exchanges are needed "even in ordinary social
   activities" (NOI 4 purport). Whether only a spiritual centre unites *permanently* is a question for a later
   session.
5. **Why do religious groups fight, then?**
   Because they lose the centre too. The tradition admits it openly (SB 4.30.8 purport). The teaching is a
   standard, not a boast.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. Why was the Lord pleased with the Pracetas (SB 4.30.8)? (a) they were all alike, (b) they were engaged in
   one occupation together, (c) they never disagreed.
2. True or false: SB 4.31.14 says you should feed each limb of the body separately to keep it healthy.
3. Name three of the six exchanges in NOI 4.
4. According to BG 3.21, whose example do common people follow?
5. In your own words: what is the difference between compromise and unity?

**Answers:** 1. (b) (SB 4.30.8). 2. False: feeding the stomach enlivens all the limbs; feeding them separately is
havoc (SB 4.31.14 and purport). 3. Any three of: giving gifts, accepting gifts, revealing one's mind in
confidence, inquiring confidentially, accepting prasada, offering prasada (NOI 4). 4. A great man's (BG 3.21).
5. Compromise is everyone giving up a bit and keeping their own centre (the purport's United Nations example);
unity is everyone facing one centre (SB 4.30.8).
