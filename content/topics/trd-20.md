# Sustainable full-time service: health, rest, study, inspiration

> **TRD · up to 180 min · review: drafted · sensitive: true** (mental strain; referral guidance in O). Group D, third page.
> Builds on BIKC chapter 25, "Health" (bss-49), which gives the brahmacari's rules; this page is about serving for a
> lifetime, not burning out. Long-term loss of enthusiasm is trd-52.

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

Builds on bss-49 (BIKC "Health"): the rules are there. A resident's danger is different: not ignorance of the rules but
a pace that cannot last. Many services, late nights, missed hearing, and the belief that collapse is dedication. The
Gita's regulation (*yukta-ahara-vihara*, BG 6.17) is given *for yogis*; the Bhagavatam calls the body a boat with the guru
as captain (SB 11.20.17) and tells us to reach the goal "as long as the body is stout and strong" (SB 7.6.5). This page
teaches the scriptural measure and the Krishna conscious reason for keeping it.

## C. Central message

The body is Krishna's boat for crossing this world, with the guru as captain (SB 11.20.17); it is kept fit *for* service,
by the Gita's measure: regulated eating, sleep "not more than six hours," recreation and work (BG 6.16–17). Over-endeavour
spoils bhakti (NOI 2); patience and enthusiasm build it (NOI 3). A resident serves for a lifetime by keeping the measure
his acaryas gave, not by collapsing for them.

## D. Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to:
1. Quote BG 6.16–17 and state the measures their purports give (eat only prasada, not more than needed; "not more than six hours" of sleep; regulated work).
2. Explain SB 11.20.17 and SB 7.6.5: why the body is maintained, and for how long the opportunity lasts.
3. Distinguish NOI 2's *prayasa* (over-endeavour) from NOI 3's *utsaha* (enthusiasm) with *dhairya* (patience).
4. Name the measure Prabhupada gives against the material-level research in O, and say which is the standard.
5. Write a weekly plan for their own service with hearing, rounds, rest and study at fixed times, and show it to their authority.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**BG 6.16** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/16/
> "There is no possibility of one's becoming a yogī, O Arjuna, if one eats too much or eats too little, sleeps too much or does not sleep enough."
- **Purport points:** "Too much eating means eating more than is required to keep the body and soul together." "He does not fast or eat more than is required." "One who eats more than required will dream very much while sleeping, and he must consequently sleep more than is required. One should not sleep more than six hours daily. One who sleeps more than six hours out of twenty-four is certainly influenced by the mode of ignorance."
- **Use:** in the talk, the measure. Note the verse's two sides: too much *and* too little.

**BG 6.17** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/17/
> "He who is regulated in his habits of eating, sleeping, recreation and work can mitigate all material pains by practicing the yoga system."
- **Purport points:** "any unnecessary time spent sleeping is considered a great loss… Therefore, his sleeping is kept to a minimum. His ideal in this respect is Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī, who… could not sleep more than two hours a day." "Because he is regulated in all his work, speech, sleep, wakefulness and all other bodily activities, there is no material misery for him."
- **Application (preacher's):** The purport names Rupa Gosvami as the *ideal*; the measure for us is the six hours of the previous purport. BIKC says the same: "There is no question of our imitating these great ācāryas."
- **Use:** in the talk.

**SB 11.20.17** — Verified · *BBT translation, completed by Srila Prabhupada's disciples* · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/20/17/
> "This human body can be compared to a perfectly constructed boat having the spiritual master as the captain and the instructions of the Personality of Godhead as favorable winds impelling it on its course. Considering all these advantages, a human being who does not utilize his human life to cross the ocean of material existence must be considered the killer of his own soul."
- **Use:** in the talk: the body is kept because it is the boat.

**SB 7.6.5** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/7/6/5/
> "…a person fully competent to distinguish wrong from right must endeavor to achieve the highest goal of life as long as the body is stout and strong and is not embarrassed by dwindling."
- **Purport point:** such a person "should not waste his energy and valuable human lifetime simply working like a cat or dog." BIKC opens its "Health" chapter with this verse.
- **Use:** in the talk: strength is for the goal, and it does not last.

**SB 1.2.10** — Verified. "One should desire only a healthy life, or self-preservation, since a human being is meant for inquiry about the Absolute Truth." *Use:* the one bodily desire the Bhagavatam permits.

**BG 3.8** — Verified. "One cannot even maintain one's physical body without work"; purport: the Lord "did not want Arjuna to become a pretender." *Use:* rest is not idleness; work is prescribed.

**BG 6.5** — Verified. "One must deliver himself with the help of his mind, and not degrade himself." *Use:* the mind that drives one to collapse is not a friend.

**NOI 2** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/2/
> "(1) eating more than necessary… (2) over-endeavoring for mundane things that are very difficult to obtain… (4) practicing the scriptural rules and regulations only for the sake of following them and not for the sake of spiritual advancement, or rejecting the rules…"
- **Use:** in the talk: *atyahara* and *prayasa*; and *niyamagraha* on both sides (fanatical rules, or none).

**NOI 3** — Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/noi/3/
> "…by enthusiasm, by confidence, by patience, by following the regulative principles…"
- **Purport point:** "Devotional service is not a matter of sentimental speculation or imaginative ecstasy. Its substance is practical activity." (The patience passage: dps-15 E.)
- **Use:** in the talk: enthusiasm *with* patience is the sustainable pair.

**SB 1.2.18** — Verified. Regular hearing of the Bhagavatam and service to the devotee establish devotion irrevocably. *Use:* hearing is the daily inspiration that cannot be dropped for "more service."

**BIKC, chapter 25, "Health"** (*Brahmacarya in Krishna consciousness*, Bhakti Vikasa Swami; text in
`research/iyf-materials/iyf-drive-4/`; the chapter is taught in full on bss-49). Cited as BIKC, in its own words:
"The Vaiṣṇava thinks, 'This body is not mine. It belongs to Kṛṣṇa, and I have to serve Kṛṣṇa with it. It must be kept fit
enough to serve Kṛṣṇa.'" "A balanced approach is needed." "Eighteen wide-awake hours are better than twenty hours half
awake." "Regulation, rising and resting at fixed times, helps to control sleep." "Sleeping in a secluded place is not
good, especially for neophyte devotees." "Devotees are advised to exercise moderately; otherwise bodily consciousness will
increase." BIKC quotes letters of Prabhupada (e.g. "A brahmacārī should not have any complaint of bodily disease," Letter,
17/02/69; "Eating should be minimized. Too much eating leads to too much sleeping, and then sex desire," Letter, 09/01/75):
cite these as BIKC's quotations, not as independently verified.

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** The body is not mine and not nothing: it is Krishna's boat, with the guru as captain (SB 11.20.17).
   It is kept fit *for* the crossing, and the time to cross is "as long as the body is stout and strong" (SB 7.6.5). So
   the one bodily desire permitted is "a healthy life" for the sake of inquiry (SB 1.2.10). Neglect is the Mayavadi's
   error ("I am not this body, so it does not matter"), as BIKC says; preoccupation is the materialist's.
2. **The measure.** Krishna gives it for yogis: regulated eating, sleeping, recreation and work (BG 6.17), and He names the
   two failures on each side: too much and too little (BG 6.16). Prabhupada fixes the numbers: eat prasada, not more than
   needed; sleep "not more than six hours"; work regulated, "connected with Kṛṣṇa's interest." The head's rule for this
   library (30 Oct 2026): where Prabhupada gives a measure, that measure is taught; research is background only.
3. **Too little is also a failure.** The verse says "does not sleep enough" and "eats too little" are equally disqualifying.
   The purport's ideal, Rupa Gosvami's two hours, is an ideal, not a rule, and BIKC adds: "There is no question of our
   imitating these great ācāryas." A resident who sleeps four hours to serve more and falls asleep in Bhagavatam class has
   broken BG 6.16 from the other side.
4. **Over-endeavour and patience.** NOI 2's *prayasa* spoils bhakti; NOI 3's *utsaha* with *dhairya* builds it. The
   difference is not the amount of work but its source and rhythm: enthusiasm fed by hearing (SB 1.2.18) and paced by
   patience lasts; endeavour driven by anxiety or the wish to be seen (trd-15) collapses. NOI 2's *niyamagraha* warns both
   ways: rules kept fanatically without their purpose, and rules rejected.
5. **Inspiration is an input, not a mood.** SB 1.2.18 names the inputs: daily hearing of the Bhagavatam and service to the
   devotee. The first service dropped by a tired resident is usually the morning class; it is the one that feeds the rest.
6. **Misunderstandings.** *"Collapsing for Krishna is surrender":* BG 6.16 calls the extreme a disqualification. *"Rest
   is laziness":* sleep beyond six hours is ignorance (6.16 purport); sleep within it is regulation (6.17). *"Health talk
   is bodily consciousness":* the body is the boat (SB 11.20.17); BIKC: "It must be kept fit enough to serve Kṛṣṇa."

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet the residents. Say: "We have given our lives; the question today is how to give all of it, for fifty years, and
   not only the first three. Krishna gives a measure for that, and the acaryas kept it."
2. Say: "Nobody's health will be discussed in public. If anyone is always unwell, the first step is a doctor; the number
   for anyone under strain is in the handout."
3. Write on the board: *yuktahara-viharasya*, "regulated in eating, sleeping, recreation and work" (BG 6.17).
4. Ask H.2 question 1 for private writing.
5. Ask question 2 aloud; take two or three answers; thank each; begin with SB 11.20.17.

**H.2 Opening questions**
1. (Private.) How many hours did you sleep on average this week, and how many Bhagavatam classes did you hear fully awake?
2. Think of a senior devotee who has served full-time for thirty years or more. What do you notice about his daily
   routine?
3. Which goes first when you are overloaded: rounds, hearing, sleep or service? (Expected: hearing or sleep; both are inputs.)

## I. Core teaching points

1. **The body is Krishna's boat; keep it fit for the crossing.** SB 11.20.17; SB 7.6.5 ("as long as the body is stout and
   strong"); SB 1.2.10 (desire only a healthy life). BIKC: "It must be kept fit enough to serve Kṛṣṇa." *Transition:* fit by
   what measure?
2. **The Gita's measure, both sides.** BG 6.16–17 with purports: prasada only, not more than needed; not more than six hours'
   sleep; too little is also a disqualification; regulated work. *Transition:* but the acaryas slept two hours.
3. **Ideal and rule.** Rupa Gosvami's two hours is the ideal the purport names; BIKC: "no question of our imitating." The
   rule for us is the six hours. *Transition:* so where does over-work come from?
4. **Prayasa or utsaha.** NOI 2: over-endeavour spoils; NOI 3: enthusiasm with patience builds. BG 6.5: the mind can be the
   enemy that drives collapse. *Application (preacher's):* ask of a new service, "can I still do it in year ten?"
   *Transition:* what keeps enthusiasm alive?
5. **Hearing is the input never dropped.** SB 1.2.18. *Application (preacher's):* the morning class is protected before
   any other service is accepted. Then the weekly plan (M.2).

## J. Stories and illustrations

**Prahlada's instruction (SB 7.6.1–5).** Prahlada Maharaja, a five-year-old, told his schoolmates that intelligent people
practise bhagavata-dharma from childhood (7.6.1) and that one must reach the goal "as long as the body is stout and strong
and is not embarrassed by dwindling" (7.6.5). *Connection:* strength is given for the goal and runs out; the resident's
healthy years are the window.

**Rupa Gosvami and Haridasa Thakura (BG 6.17 purport).** Prabhupada names the ideal: Rupa Gosvami "could not sleep more
than two hours a day, and sometimes not even that"; Haridasa Thakura "would not even accept prasādam nor even sleep for a
moment without finishing his daily routine of chanting… three hundred thousand names." *Connection:* the acaryas were
absorbed, not driven; we follow their footsteps at the measure given to us, as BIKC says.

**Invented illustration (labelled):** a village farmer and a city surgeon both work long hours; the farmer rests when the
sun sets and the surgeon when the roster says; both know that a collapsed worker helps no one. *Krishna conscious point:*
the resident's roster is given by Krishna in BG 6.17, and the reason to keep it is that the boat must reach the shore.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"Prabhupada slept two or three hours and worked constantly. Why should I sleep six?"**
   - The purport to BG 6.17 names Rupa Gosvami's two hours as the *ideal* of a liberated acarya; the purport to 6.16 gives
     *us* the rule, "not more than six hours." BIKC: "There is no question of our imitating these great ācāryas." Follow in
     their footsteps at the measure given.
2. **"Service is urgent. If I rest, the programme suffers."**
   - BG 6.16: one who "does not sleep enough" cannot be a yogi; BIKC: "eighteen wide-awake hours are better than twenty
     hours half awake." Over-endeavour (*prayasa*) spoils bhakti (NOI 2). Plan the service inside the measure, with the
     authority.
3. **"Isn't caring for the body materialistic?"**
   - The body is Krishna's boat (SB 11.20.17); the Bhagavatam permits the desire for "a healthy life" for inquiry
     (SB 1.2.10). Preoccupation with the body is materialistic; keeping the boat fit is service (BIKC).
4. **"I am always tired and have lost taste. What do I do?"**
   - First, see a doctor if you are always unwell. Then check the inputs: sleep within the measure, prasada not more than
     needed (BG 6.16), and daily hearing (SB 1.2.18). Tell your authority (trd-16). If strain continues, the referral in O.
     Lost enthusiasm after many years: trd-52.

## L. Case studies

*Invented case for discussion. Not real people.*

**Case 1: Three services.** Yadu, two years a resident, cooks for the morning offering, leads a college programme and
manages the book table. He sleeps four to five hours, has stopped attending Bhagavatam class to prepare his talks, eats
standing in the kitchen, and has had a cold for six weeks. He says, "This is what surrender means."
- **Dilemma:** His dedication is real. What has he misread?
- **Principles:** BG 6.16 (too little sleep disqualifies); BG 6.17 (regulated work); SB 1.2.18 (hearing dropped); NOI 2
  (*prayasa*); SB 11.20.17 (the boat); BIKC ("if always unwell, see a doctor" is the plain safety rule).
- **Questions:** Which input has he dropped first? Which verse does his "surrender" sentence contradict? What does his
  authority need to know, and what plan fits the measure?
- **Analysis:** He has dropped hearing and sleep, the two inputs; the body is signalling. The plan: see a doctor; restore
  the class and six hours; ask the authority which of the three services to hand over for now. Surrender is doing what
  Krishna says in BG 6.16–17, not what the mind demands (BG 6.5).
- **Takeaway:** The measure is the surrender.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first; thank each answer)
1. What four things does BG 6.17 say to regulate? *Expected:* eating, sleeping, recreation and work.
2. How much sleep does the BG 6.16 purport allow? *Expected:* not more than six hours.
3. Does BG 6.16 condemn only too much eating and sleeping? *Expected:* no; too little as well.
4. What is the body compared to in SB 11.20.17, and who is the captain? *Expected:* a boat; the spiritual master.
5. What is the difference between *prayasa* (NOI 2) and *utsaha* with *dhairya* (NOI 3)? *Expected:* over-endeavour that spoils bhakti; enthusiasm with patience that builds it.
6. Which daily input does SB 1.2.18 name that must not be dropped? *Expected:* regular hearing of the Bhagavatam (and service to the devotee).

**M.2 Activities**
- **Option 1: The weekly plan (25 min).** Each resident writes a seven-day grid: rising, rounds, class, services, prasada
  times, rest (within six hours), study, one weekly recreation that is Krishna conscious (kirtana, a walk chanting). A
  partner checks it against BG 6.16–17 and SB 1.2.18. The plan goes to the authority.
- **Option 2: Ideal and rule (15 min).** Groups list three things the acaryas did that we follow in footsteps (not imitate)
  and three measures given to us; each reports.

**M.3 Reflection**
- Why does Krishna give the *same* warning for too little as for too much (BG 6.16)? What does my pattern of
  over-endeavour protect or prove, and to whom?

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

The Bhagavatam compares the human body to "a perfectly constructed boat having the spiritual master as the captain and
the instructions of the Personality of Godhead as favorable winds"; one who does not use it to cross the ocean of material
existence "must be considered the killer of his own soul" (SB 11.20.17). Prahlada Maharaja says the goal must be reached
"as long as the body is stout and strong" (SB 7.6.5). So the one bodily desire the Bhagavatam allows is "a healthy life"
for the sake of inquiry (SB 1.2.10). BIKC puts it plainly: "This body is not mine. It belongs to Kṛṣṇa… It must be kept
fit enough to serve Kṛṣṇa."

Krishna gives the measure: "There is no possibility of one's becoming a yogī… if one eats too much or eats too little,
sleeps too much or does not sleep enough" (BG 6.16); "he who is regulated in his habits of eating, sleeping, recreation and
work can mitigate all material pains" (BG 6.17). Prabhupada's purports fix it: eat only prasada and not more than needed;
"one should not sleep more than six hours daily"; work regulated for Krishna. Too little is also a failure. The purport
names Rupa Gosvami, who slept two hours, as the ideal; BIKC adds, "There is no question of our imitating these great
ācāryas."

Over-endeavour (*prayasa*) spoils devotion (NOI 2); enthusiasm with patience builds it (NOI 3). The input that keeps
enthusiasm alive is daily hearing of the Bhagavatam and service to devotees (SB 1.2.18), the first thing a tired devotee
usually drops. The session ended with each resident writing a weekly plan within the measure, to show his authority. If
you are always unwell, see a doctor; if under strain, tell your authority.

## S2. Key ideas

- The body is Krishna's boat; keep it fit for the crossing (SB 11.20.17; SB 7.6.5; SB 1.2.10).
- The measure: prasada, not more than needed; sleep not more than six hours; regulated work and recreation (BG 6.16–17 purports).
- Too little sleep or food disqualifies as surely as too much (BG 6.16).
- The acaryas' two hours is the ideal to follow in footsteps, not imitate (BG 6.17 purport; BIKC).
- Over-endeavour spoils bhakti; enthusiasm with patience and daily hearing sustain it (NOI 2–3; SB 1.2.18).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **BG 6.17:** "regulated in his habits of eating, sleeping, recreation and work." *For me:* regulation is Krishna's own instruction.
- **BG 6.16:** "eats too much or eats too little, sleeps too much or does not sleep enough." *For me:* collapse is not surrender.
- **SB 11.20.17:** the body is a boat "having the spiritual master as the captain." *For me:* I keep the boat for the crossing.

## S4. Words to know

- **Yukta-ahara-vihara:** regulated eating and recreation (BG 6.17).
- **Prayasa:** over-endeavour for things hard to obtain; spoils devotion (NOI 2).
- **Utsaha, dhairya:** enthusiasm and patience, two of six things that help devotion (NOI 3).
- **Niyamagraha:** following rules for their own sake, or rejecting them (NOI 2).

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Prabhupada and the acaryas slept very little. Why should I sleep six hours?** The BG 6.17 purport names Rupa Gosvami's two hours as the ideal; the 6.16 purport gives us the rule, six hours. BIKC: "no question of our imitating."
2. **If I rest, the service suffers.** One who "does not sleep enough" cannot be a yogi (BG 6.16); BIKC: eighteen wide-awake hours beat twenty half awake. Plan the service inside the measure with your authority.
3. **Isn't caring for the body materialistic?** Keeping Krishna's boat fit is service (SB 11.20.17); the Bhagavatam permits the desire for a healthy life (SB 1.2.10).
4. **I am always tired. What do I do?** See a doctor if always unwell; restore sleep, prasada and daily hearing (BG 6.16; SB 1.2.18); tell your authority; if under strain, Tele-MANAS 14416.
