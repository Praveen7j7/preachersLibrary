# Art of Smart Work

> **VL1 · 45–60 min · review: sources checked, awaiting senior review.** A relevance talk for students and young professionals. Scripture light but
> exact: the three modes of nature (BG 14) and the three kinds of worker (BG 18.26–28) carry the talk. IYF's deck
> ("Art of relaxed and efficient working": book of life, personality type by mode, time-manager self-test, ABCDE items
> that raise goodness, attitude and behaviour) gives the spine; its brain-wave and hormone slides are dropped (see O).

## A. Topic overview

| | |
|---|---|
| Batch / level | VL1 (Vedic Level 1), a stand-alone talk |
| Suggested duration | 45–60 min (G gives the 60-minute flow and a 30-minute cut) |
| Preparation time | About 90 min, the first time |
| Difficulty | Introductory |
| Prerequisites | None (VL1-10 and VL1-24 help) |
| Audience | Students told to "work smart, not hard" without being told how; young professionals who work long hours with poor results; many with no settled faith |
| Teaching objective | Replace "smart work" as a slogan with the Gita's model: the quality of work follows the mode the worker is in; three modes, three kinds of worker; and five everyday inputs that shift a person toward the mode of goodness, where smart work actually happens |

## B. Why this topic belongs at this level

"Work smart, not hard" is advice everyone has received and nobody has been taught. The Gita's answer is unexpected
and testable: the smartness of work is not a technique but a *state* of the worker. Chapter 14 names three states
(goodness, passion, ignorance) and chapter 18 describes the worker in each. A VL1 room can recognise all three in
one afternoon of their own week. The aim is **attraction through a usable self-diagnosis**: the listener should be
able to name their mode on a given day and know which input would move it.

IYF's deck has three "tips": write your book of life (innate and acquired qualities; the chariot of the body);
know your personality type by mode (with long trait lists and the cap-seller-and-monkeys exercise); and a
time-manager self-test (plan / do / interact; the urgent/important matrix). It then gives the ABCDE items that boost
goodness (association, books, chanting, diet, environment), diet by mode (BG 17.8–10), and attitude and behaviour
changes. This page keeps **modes → workers → inputs**, keeps the cap-seller exercise and the ABCDE list (labelled as
IYF's method), and drops the brain-wave and hormone slides, which have no source.

*Builds on vl1-10 (BG and art of work: BG 2.47, effort not result) and vl1-24 (Time management: regulation, BG 6.17): here BG 2.47 and 2.50 get one sentence each as the horizon, and BG 6.17 is used only as an input that shifts the mode. This talk's own material is the three modes as work-states and the three workers.*

How the subject grows at other levels:

| Level | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **VL1** (here) | Three modes as work-states (BG 14.5–8, 11–13); three workers (BG 18.26–28); inputs that raise goodness (BG 17.8–10; BG 6.17); "yoga is the art of all work" (BG 2.50) as the horizon |
| VL2 | The modes as a full theory of nature and of the self's conditioning; why even goodness binds (BG 14.6 purport) |
| VL3 | Working above the modes: work as devotional service; the inputs become *sadhana* (daily spiritual practice) |
| SPS | Keeping work in goodness while the practice grows; avoiding strain |
| DPS | Work as service for those who lead and support others; keeping goodness under pressure |
| BSS | The student-ashram day as a deliberate shift of inputs toward goodness |

**Not done here:**
- Personality typing as identity. Modes are states that shift daily; nobody is "a tamasic person".
- Prescribing diet or chanting. BG 17.8–10 is read as the Gita's classification; the ABCDE items are what
  practitioners use.
- Pseudo-science about meditation and brain waves (O).

## C. Central message

Smart work is not a trick; it is a state. The Gita says nature has three modes, and the same task done in goodness,
passion or ignorance produces a different worker and a different result (BG 14.5; BG 18.26–28). Smart work is work in
the mode of goodness: clear, regulated, enthusiastic, even in success and failure. And the mode can be shifted by what
we take in: company, reading, food, environment, and a quiet practice (BG 17.8–10; BG 6.17).

## D. Learning outcomes

By the end, participants should be able to:
1. Name the three modes and their everyday symptoms (BG 14.6–8, 11–13): clarity and calm; craving, restlessness and
   strain; dullness, delay and confusion.
2. Describe the three workers of BG 18.26–28 and identify which one they were on a specific recent day.
3. Explain why "smart work" in the Gita's sense means working in goodness, and connect it to "yoga is the art of all
   work" (BG 2.50) and to effort without anxiety about results (BG 2.47).
4. Choose one input (association, reading, food, environment, practice) to change this week and predict its effect.

## E. Scriptural foundation

**Use:** six references carry the talk (14.5; 18.26–28; 17.8–10 read together; 6.17). The rest prepare you.

**BG 14.5**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/14/5/
> "Material nature consists of three modes – goodness, passion and ignorance. When the eternal living entity comes in contact with nature, O mighty-armed Arjuna, he becomes conditioned by these modes."
- **Teaching:** Three modes condition everyone in contact with nature.
- **Purport point:** Living entities "are induced to act according to that nature. This is the cause of the varieties of
  happiness and distress."
- **Application (preacher's):** The talk's premise: the quality of my work follows the mode I am in. Present it as a
  model to test against one's own week.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 18.26**, **BG 18.27**, **BG 18.28**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/18/26/ (and 27, 28)
> 18.26: "One who performs his duty without association with the modes of material nature, without false ego, with great determination and enthusiasm, and without wavering in success or failure is said to be a worker in the mode of goodness."
> 18.27: "The worker who is attached to work and the fruits of work, desiring to enjoy those fruits, and who is greedy, always envious, impure, and moved by joy and sorrow, is said to be in the mode of passion."
> 18.28: "The worker who is always engaged in work against the injunctions of the scripture, who is materialistic, obstinate, cheating and expert in insulting others, and who is lazy, always morose and procrastinating is said to be a worker in the mode of ignorance."
- **Teaching:** Three portraits of a worker.
- **Purport point (18.26):** the good worker "is always enthusiastic till the completion of such work… He does not care
  for success or failure". **(18.27):** the passionate worker "is very happy if his work is successful and very much
  distressed when his work is not successful". **(18.28):** "anything which can be done in an hour they drag on for
  years."
- **Application (preacher's):** These three are "smart work", "hard-and-anxious work" and "no work". The 18.28
  purport's line about dragging an hour's work into years is the Gita's own word on procrastination.
- **Use:** in the talk; the anchor passage.

**BG 14.6**, **BG 14.7**, **BG 14.8**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/14/6/ (and 7, 8)
> 14.6: "…the mode of goodness, being purer than the others, is illuminating, and it frees one from all sinful reactions. Those situated in that mode become conditioned by a sense of happiness and knowledge."
> 14.7: "The mode of passion is born of unlimited desires and longings, O son of Kuntī, and because of this the embodied living entity is bound to material fruitive actions."
> 14.8: "…the mode of darkness, born of ignorance, is the delusion of all embodied living entities. The results of this mode are madness, indolence and sleep, which bind the conditioned soul."
- **Purport point (14.7):** "As long as one is hankering after these things, he has to work very hard." **(14.8):** in
  ignorance "one cannot understand a thing as it is"; such a person "sleeps more than is required… appears to be
  always dejected".
- **Application (preacher's):** The three modes as three work-states: clear; driven and strained; dull and delayed.
  The purport to 14.6 adds that even goodness binds, through pride in knowledge; at VL1 mention this in one sentence
  so goodness is not sold as the goal.
- **Use:** in the talk (with the table in N).

**BG 14.11**, **BG 14.12**, **BG 14.13** (symptoms): Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/14/11/ (and 12, 13)
> 14.11: "The manifestation of the mode of goodness can be experienced when all the gates of the body are illuminated by knowledge."
> 14.12: "…when there is an increase in the mode of passion the symptoms of great attachment, fruitive activity, intense endeavor, and uncontrollable desire and hankering develop."
> 14.13: "When there is an increase in the mode of ignorance, O son of Kuru, darkness, inertia, madness and illusion are manifested."
- **Purport point (14.11):** in goodness "one can see things in the right position". **(14.13):** one in ignorance
  "wants to act whimsically, for no purpose. Even though he has the capacity to work, he makes no endeavor."
- **Application (preacher's):** The diagnostic checklist for "which mode am I in today?"
- **Use:** in the talk.

**BG 17.8**, **BG 17.9**, **BG 17.10** (diet by mode): Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/17/8/ (and 9, 10)
> 17.8: "Foods dear to those in the mode of goodness increase the duration of life, purify one's existence and give strength, health, happiness and satisfaction. Such foods are juicy, fatty, wholesome, and pleasing to the heart."
> 17.9: "Foods that are too bitter, too sour, salty, hot, pungent, dry and burning are dear to those in the mode of passion. Such foods cause distress, misery and disease."
> 17.10: "Food prepared more than three hours before being eaten, food that is tasteless, decomposed and putrid, and food consisting of remnants and untouchable things is dear to those in the mode of darkness."
- **Purport point (17.10, which covers all three):** "The purpose of food is to increase the duration of life, purify
  the mind and aid bodily strength." Foods in goodness: "milk products, sugar, rice, wheat, fruits and vegetables."
- **Application (preacher's):** IYF's "D for diet". At VL1, read the classification as the Gita's and let the room
  notice how food affects their afternoon; no dietary rule is set.
- **Use:** in the talk, briefly.

**BG 6.17**: Verified · https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/6/17/
> "He who is regulated in his habits of eating, sleeping, recreation and work can mitigate all material pains by practicing the yoga system."
- **Purport point:** extravagance in eating, sleeping, defending and mating "can block advancement"; a regulated person
  has "no material misery".
- **Application (preacher's):** Regulation is the single most reliable input toward goodness; it is also VL1-24's
  tool.
- **Use:** in the talk.

**For questions (K):**
- **BG 2.50** (Verified): "strive for yoga, which is the art of all work." The phrase behind the topic's title (see
  VL1-10, where it is the anchor). Purport: Arjuna is advised "to act in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, the purifying process of
  resultant action." The horizon beyond the modes.
- **BG 2.47** (Verified): "You have a right to perform your prescribed duty, but you are not entitled to the fruits of
  action." The good worker's evenness (18.26) rests here. For "how do I stop being moved by joy and sorrow about
  results?"

## F. Philosophical explanation

1. **The principle.** Nature works in three modes, and everyone in contact with nature is conditioned by them
   (BG 14.5). Each mode has symptoms (BG 14.6–8, 11–13) and produces a characteristic worker (BG 18.26–28): in
   goodness, determined, enthusiastic and even; in passion, attached, greedy, moved by joy and sorrow; in ignorance,
   lazy, morose and procrastinating. "Smart work" is work in goodness. Modes are states, not identities, and they are
   shifted by inputs: food (BG 17.8–10), regulation of the basics (BG 6.17), and, in IYF's formulation, association,
   books, chanting and environment. Beyond the modes, the Gita holds out work as yoga, "the art of all work"
   (BG 2.50), done without claim on the result (BG 2.47).
2. **Why it matters.** Students are told to work smart and given no model. This one is concrete: notice the state,
   change the input, watch the work change. It explains why the same person is brilliant on Tuesday and useless on
   Thursday.
3. **Connections.** VL1-10 (effort and result) is the good worker's evenness; VL1-24 (time) is regulation; VL1-20
   (greed) is the passionate worker's engine; VL1-21 (pleasure) is the ignorant worker's escape.
4. **Consequences.** A person who manages their mode produces better work in fewer hours, recovers faster from
   failure, and is less exhausting to work with. A claim about tendency, not a promise.
5. **Misunderstandings to prevent.**
   - **Typing people.** IYF's deck lists traits by mode; use them as a mirror for one's own day, never as labels for
     others. BG 14.10 (Verified) says that sometimes goodness, sometimes passion, sometimes ignorance becomes
     prominent, and "there is always competition for supremacy"; its purport adds that "if one wants, he can
     develop, by practice, the mode of goodness". State this plainly.
   - **Goodness as the goal.** The purport to BG 14.6 warns that goodness binds through pride in knowledge. The Gita's
     goal is above the modes (BG 2.50); at VL1 one sentence.
   - **Diet as rule.** BG 17.8–10 is read, not imposed.
   - **"Smart" as "less".** The good worker has "great determination and enthusiasm" (BG 18.26). Smart is not lazy;
     ignorance is lazy (BG 18.28).
6. **Pitch for VL1.** Say "three states", "clear, driven, dull", "inputs". *Sattva, rajas, tamas* may be said once
   each because IYF's deck uses them. Present Krsna as the Gita's speaker without arguing His divinity.

## G. Lecture flow

**Main plan (60 min; 45 min if you skip the activity in M.2)**

| Time | Segment | Notes |

| 5–12 | Three modes, three states (BG 14.5–8); the cap-seller exercise (J) | How would each mode react? Keep it light |
| 12–22 | Three workers (BG 18.26–28) read slowly; the room identifies their day | Table in N; the procrastination line from the 18.28 purport |
| 22–28 | Symptoms checklist (BG 14.11–13): "which mode am I in today?" | Self-diagnosis, not labelling |
| 28–38 | Inputs that shift the mode: food (BG 17.8–10), regulation (BG 6.17), IYF's ABCDE | Label ABCDE as IYF's method; what practitioners use |
| 38–41 | Beyond the modes: "yoga is the art of all work" (BG 2.50); effort not result (BG 2.47) | One sentence each; the horizon |

| 51–55 | Closing check questions (M.1) | Praise every try |

**30-minute cut:** drop the symptoms checklist (fold two symptoms into the workers segment) and the BG 2.50 horizon.

**Alternative structure (IYF's self-tests):** run IYF's "plan / do / interact" self-test (day-dreamer, workaholic,
delightful-but-incompetent) as an ice-breaker, then map each failure type onto a mode (planning without doing =
ignorance's inertia; doing without planning = passion's strain) and teach BG 18.26–28 and the inputs.

## H. Opening and engagement

**H.1 How to start (first 5 minutes)**
1. Greet the room, say your name, and say the talk is about how work feels from the inside.
2. Write on the board: "Work smart, not hard".
3. Ask H.2 question 1 by show of hands; thank the room.
4. Ask question 2; take two or three answers and thank each speaker.
5. Give the transition line below and go to the three modes.

**H.2 Opening questions** (about the room's own life)
1. Think of your best working day this month and your worst. What was different (sleep, food, company, mood)?
2. Has anyone told you to "work smart"? Did they tell you how?
3. What work do you do (study, a job, a farm, a shop, a home) and when in the day is it at its best?

- **Opening question:** "Think of your best working day this month and your worst. Same person, same skills. Raise
  your hand if you can say what was different." Pause. "The Gita says the difference has a name, and it is not
  'motivation'."
- **Relatable scenario (invented):** Zoya has a report due. Monday she eats a heavy late lunch, scrolls, naps, and
  stares at a blank page (dull). Tuesday she panics, works till 3 a.m. on coffee, snaps at her flatmate, and produces
  twelve messy pages (driven). Wednesday she wakes early, eats lightly, works two quiet hours, and finishes a clean
  report by noon (clear). Which day was "smart work"? What was different?
- **Transition:** "Chapter 14 of the Gita calls these three states goodness, passion and ignorance, and chapter 18
  describes the worker in each."
- **Why it matters to this audience:** Everyone has been told to work smart. Nobody has been told that smartness is a
  state they can shift.

## I. Core teaching points

1. **Three modes, three states.** BG 14.5–8. *Plain:* goodness is clear and calm; passion is craving and strain;
   ignorance is dullness and delay. *Example:* Zoya's three days (H). *Transition:* the Gita describes the worker in
   each.
2. **Three workers.** BG 18.26–28. *Plain:* determined-and-even; attached-and-anxious; lazy-and-postponing.
   *Example:* "anything which can be done in an hour they drag on for years" (18.28 purport). *Transition:* how do I
   know which one I am today?
3. **The symptoms checklist.** BG 14.11–13. *Plain:* clarity in all the senses; hankering and restlessness; darkness
   and inertia. *Example:* the 4 p.m. slump versus the 7 a.m. clarity. *Transition:* can the state be changed?
4. **Inputs change the state.** BG 17.8–10; BG 6.17; IYF's ABCDE. *Plain:* what I eat, when I sleep, whom I sit with,
   what I read and where I work set my mode. *Example:* the same task after a heavy meal and after a light one.
   *Transition:* and smart work has a horizon.
5. **Beyond the modes.** BG 2.50; BG 2.47. *Plain:* the Gita's final word on work is yoga: full effort, no claim on
   the result. *Example:* the craftsman absorbed in the work, not the applause (VL1-10). *Transition:* to the one
   input to change.

## J. Stories and illustrations

- **The cap-seller and the monkeys** (IYF's exercise, a traditional story): a tired cap-seller sleeps under a tree;
  monkeys take his caps and climb up wearing them. Ask: how would he react in each mode? (Ignorance: despair or sleep
  on; passion: rage, throw stones; goodness: observe, think, throw his own cap down so they imitate him.) Label it
  "a traditional story used as an exercise"; the goodness ending is the room's to invent.
- **Zoya's three days** (invented sketch, H). Label it invented.
- **IYF's self-test types** (day-dreamer, workaholic, "delightful but incompetent"): teaching categories from the
  deck, not scripture.
- **Not used:** IYF's deck claims that mantra meditation puts the brain in an "alpha zone" (with a frequency chart) and
  that screen time blocks four named hormones. Neither has a source on the deck. **Requires source review**; do not
  read them out as facts. The Benson "relaxation response" slide has a real source (O) and may be used with its
  limits stated.

## K. Anticipated questions and objections

1. **"Isn't this just personality typing? I'm obviously 'passion'."**
   - The Gita describes modes as states that rise and fall; BG 14.11–13 speaks of their *increase*, and IYF's own deck
     uses the cap-seller to show one person reacting three ways. Nobody is a mode. Ask instead: which mode was I in
     at 4 p.m. yesterday, and what had I eaten, read and sat with?
2. **"Smart work sounds like an excuse for working less."**
   - The good worker of BG 18.26 has "great determination and enthusiasm" and works "till the completion". It is the
     *ignorant* worker who is "lazy, always morose and procrastinating" (BG 18.28). Smart means clear and
     regulated, not minimal.
3. **"Hard work got me here. Why change?"**
   - The passionate worker of BG 18.27 also gets results, at the cost of being "moved by joy and sorrow" and greedy for
     fruits. The question is not whether hard work works but what it costs and whether it is sustainable. BG 2.47 offers
     the same effort without the anxiety.
4. **"Are you telling us what to eat?"**
   - No. BG 17.8–10 is the Gita's classification of foods by their effects; it is read so you can test it against your
     own afternoons. Practitioners choose foods in goodness; the choice is yours.
5. **"Is goodness the goal then?"**
   - Not finally. The purport to BG 14.6 warns that even goodness binds, through pride in knowledge. The Gita's goal
     is work as yoga, "the art of all work" (BG 2.50), which is above the modes. Goodness is the platform from which
     that becomes possible.
   - The Preaching Encyclopedia (VOICE, Mantra Meditation Circle, "Is Work Worship I–II") puts the same point as a test:
     work is always worship, the question is *whose*, and the object of worship shows in the goal of the work (the
     senses, the ego, or God). Its conclusion: "work become worship only when it is done in divine consciousness",
     built on a regulated morning practice before the working day; such work is "actual yoga, which according to the
     Bhagavad-gita is 'the art of all work'". Supporting; the verse is BG 2.50 above.
6. **"What is the fastest input to change?"**
   - IYF's deck ranks association first ("where do we get our scripts from?") and practitioners agree: the company we
     keep sets the mode faster than anything. The Gita's own list for regulation is eating, sleeping, recreation and
     work (BG 6.17). Pick the one you can change tomorrow morning.

## L. Case studies

*Invented cases for discussion. Not real incidents.*

**Case 1: The 3 a.m. achiever.** Farhan is known for heroics: all-nighters, last-minute brilliance, and a temper. His
work is good; his health, friendships and mornings are not. He says this is just how he works.
- **Dilemma:** He gets results. What is the problem?
- **Principles:** BG 18.27 (the passionate worker: attached, moved by joy and sorrow); BG 14.12 (symptoms of
  increased passion); BG 6.17 (regulation).
- **Questions:**
  - Which worker of BG 18.26–28 is Farhan, and what is the evidence?
  - What inputs keep him in that mode (food, sleep, company, environment)?
  - What one change toward BG 6.17 would he accept?
- **Suggested analysis:** His results are real and his mode is unsustainable. The Gita does not ask him to care less
  about work; it asks him to shift the state in which he does it, starting with one regulated basic.
- **Takeaway:** Brilliance in passion costs what brilliance in goodness does not.

**Case 2: The stuck one.** Megha has a semester's work pending. She wakes late, eats whatever is around, scrolls for
hours, feels dull and guilty, and says she "lacks motivation".
- **Principles:** BG 14.8 and 14.13 (ignorance: inertia, delusion); BG 18.28 (procrastination); BG 17.10 (foods in
  darkness); BG 6.17 (regulation).
- **Questions:**
  - Is "lack of motivation" the cause or the symptom?
  - Which inputs are feeding the mode she is in?
  - What is the smallest regulated change that would shift one morning?
- **Suggested analysis:** Motivation is downstream of mode. One fixed waking time, one light breakfast and one hour
  in company that works (association) shift the state; the work follows. Guilt is not an input.
- **Takeaway:** You do not need motivation to change your inputs; you need inputs to change your motivation.

## M. Discussion and reflection

**M.1 Closing check questions** (easy first; praise every try)
1. Is smart work a trick, according to the Gita? *No; it is a state of the worker.*
2. Name the three modes. *Goodness, passion, ignorance (BG 14.5).*
3. Describe the worker in goodness. *Determined, enthusiastic, even in success and failure (BG 18.26).*
4. Describe the worker in passion. *Attached to results, moved by joy and sorrow (BG 18.27).*
5. Describe the worker in ignorance. *Lazy, morose and procrastinating (BG 18.28).*
6. Name one input that can shift the mode. *Food (BG 17.8–10), regulated habits (BG 6.17), company, reading, environment.*

**M.2 Activity (about 10 minutes): "Name the mode"**
1. 2 min: each person recalls one working day from this month and writes which mode it was and one clue (food, sleep, mood).
2. 4 min, in pairs: each names the input that came just before the shift (the day itself stays private).
3. 3 min: volunteers share one input. Thank each volunteer.
4. 1 min: link to the one input to change in P.

**M.3 Reflection**
- **Reflection:** Replay yesterday hour by hour. Where did your mode change, and what happened just before?
- **Application (private, optional):** Which single input (food, sleep, company, reading, environment, a quiet
  practice) could you change tomorrow? What do you predict will happen to your work? *(Nobody needs to share.)*

# Part 2: For students

## S1. Summary

Everyone is told to "work smart, not hard", but nobody says how. The Bhagavad-gita (an ancient Sanskrit scripture, a conversation between Krsna and Arjuna) answers that smart work is a state, not a trick. Nature has three modes, goodness, passion and ignorance, and we are conditioned by them (BG 14.5). In goodness we are clear and calm; in passion, craving and strained; in ignorance, dull and delayed (BG 14.6–8). You can recognise all three in your own week: the clear day, the frantic day, the foggy day.

The Gita describes the worker in each: determined, enthusiastic and even in success and failure (BG 18.26); attached to results and "moved by joy and sorrow" (BG 18.27); "lazy, always morose and procrastinating" (BG 18.28). Smart work is work in goodness.

The mode can be shifted by what we take in: food (BG 17.8–10), regulated habits of eating, sleeping, recreation and work (BG 6.17), and, as practitioners add, the company we keep, what we read and where we work. Beyond the modes, the Gita calls work done as yoga "the art of all work" (BG 2.50). This week, change one input and watch your work.

## S2. Key ideas

- Smart work is a state of the worker, not a technique.
- Three modes, three states: clear, driven, dull (BG 14.6–8).
- Three workers: even and enthusiastic; anxious and attached; lazy and postponing (BG 18.26–28).
- Modes are states that shift daily, not labels for people.
- Inputs change the state: food, sleep, company, reading, environment, a quiet practice (BG 17.8–10; BG 6.17).

## S3. Verses to remember

- **BG 18.26:** the good worker acts "with great determination and enthusiasm, and without wavering in success or
  failure". *For me:* smart does not mean less; it means clear and steady.
- **BG 18.28:** the worker in ignorance is "lazy, always morose and procrastinating". *For me:* when I postpone, I can
  ask what state I am in and what fed it, instead of blaming "motivation".
- **BG 6.17:** "He who is regulated in his habits of eating, sleeping, recreation and work can mitigate all material
  pains…" *For me:* regulation is the most reliable way to shift my mode.

## S4. Words to know

- **Modes of nature (gunas):** goodness (sattva), passion (rajas), ignorance (tamas): three states that condition how
  we think, feel and work (BG 14.5).
- **Worker in goodness:** determined, enthusiastic, even (BG 18.26). The Gita's picture of smart work.
- **Inputs:** what shifts a mode: food, sleep, company, reading, environment, practice.

## S5. Questions and answers

1. **Isn't this personality typing?**
   No. The Gita speaks of modes increasing and decreasing (BG 14.11–13); the same person is in different modes on
   different days. Ask "which mode was I in at 4 p.m.?" not "which mode am I?"
2. **Doesn't "smart work" mean working less?**
   The good worker has "great determination and enthusiasm" (BG 18.26). It is the worker in ignorance who is lazy
   (BG 18.28). Smart means clear and regulated.
3. **Hard, anxious work got me here. Why change?**
   It works, at a cost: being "moved by joy and sorrow" and greedy for results (BG 18.27). The Gita offers the same
   effort without the anxiety (BG 2.47).
4. **Are you telling me what to eat?**
   No. BG 17.8–10 classifies foods by their effects so you can test it against your own afternoons.
5. **Is goodness the goal?**
   It is the platform. The Gita's final word on work is yoga, "the art of all work" (BG 2.50), above the modes.

## S6. Check your understanding

1. Which worker is "lazy, always morose and procrastinating" (BG 18.28): goodness, passion or ignorance?
2. The worker in passion is "moved by joy and sorrow". What is he attached to (BG 18.27)?
3. Name two qualities of the worker in goodness (BG 18.26).
4. According to BG 17.8, what do foods in goodness give?
5. In your own words: why does the talk say "smart work is a state, not a trick"?

**Answers:** 1. Ignorance (BG 18.28). 2. Work and the fruits of work (BG 18.27). 3. Any two of: without false ego,
great determination, enthusiasm, unwavering in success or failure (BG 18.26). 4. Longer life, purification, strength,
health, happiness and satisfaction (BG 17.8). 5. Because the quality of work follows the mode the worker is in
(BG 14.5; 18.26–28), and the mode is shifted by inputs, not by techniques alone.
